Project Summary

In Somalia a huge percentage of the population still relies on humanitarian health assistance with the number increasing. About 40% of Somalia’s population needs life saving humanitarian assistance (OCHA,2019 ). Majority of women, girls,boys and men in target area need access to emergency health care. Neonatal, infant and child mortality remain high with communicable diseases as leading cause. AWD/cholera is prevalent enhanced by poor WASH (REACH,2018; Health cluster 2019). High maternal mortality rate contributed by limited antenatal care (1/4 of women), low proportion of births attended by skilled attendants (1/3), limited facilities for emergency maternal care. Insecurity, poor health seeking practices, poor functioning referral systems, limited access to health services and underlying malnutrition pose major challenges to control and prevention of diseases (UNICEF 2018; MoH 2018 ).The influx of IDPs continues to increase their vulnerably, reduce access to health services, decrease their resilience and increasing need for protection interventions.
Kismayo has a rapidly growing population due to immigration in search of aid . Therefore Health services, especially in IDP,are limited. With concerted efforts,AWD/cholera is prevalence has significantly reduced but prevailing poor WASH status threatens to fuel increased prevalence in case of shock. Though great improvement has been noted, few women deliver with skilled assistance due to cost, ignorance and culture. Acute malnutrition rates among children under 5 remains high in all locations especially among IDP. Dhankenley(Kahda) hosts the most vulnerable and protracted IDP in Mogadishu, most facing multiple displacement (OCHA, 2019). In both Kismayo and Kahda IDP are increasing due to in-migration straining limited service. In Kahda ,health seeking behavior remains poor though services are available,vaccination coverage is low and in Kahda SAM rates are highest in the country.
Hobyo and Jariban have poor coverage of functional health services, and high morbidity rates. Nutrition situation is critical, vaccination coverage low, WASH status very poor,rural areas are hard to reach with some communities marginalized.
In all proposed areas, sexual violations including rape is rampant, highest cases reported in Hobyo. This is further fueled by the distance covered by women and children as they seek health services. This often exposes then to hazards and vulnerabilities that can be easily avoided by scaling up services to hard to reach areas , or improving the same through use of well equipped and trained mobile teams.
SAF UK intends to provide lifesaving assistance and strengthen community level resilience in all these areas. The proposed program will also seek to integrate interventions with those of Nutrition, WASH , food security and protection. The program will consider Gender, protection and accountability to affected population as key cross cutting issues that will have to be mainstreamed. the organisation, has in all areas, set up community governance structures that support sustainability of health programs and will seek to strengthen these and integrate to other structures by other clusters, like the community water boards. Participation of community members and groups for sustainability will also be key.
The program will contribute to achievement of various government health plans and in line with government policies.
All staff and community volunteers will have their capacity well built.

Mitigation Measures

For IDP the main risk is displacement. for this the organisation has maintained adatabase for all bene􀀀ciaries with telephone contacts and that of relatives. For thosein rural Mudug the risk is spontaneous clan clashes. While it is difficult to deal with this,we have ensured that services are provided for the different clans in their areasequitably. Interclan clashes therefore have minimal effect our programs. in all locationthe organisation will seek to provide safety nets by implementing BSFP . Victims ofGBV will also be provided with an organisational GBV focal point who will linksurvivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensure IDp have access tobasic services in an equitable and digni􀀀ed manner with equality also observed in joballocation in projects serving IDP population. Among IDP the risk of exclusion basedon tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certain community who found it hardto access services in the recent past based on their tribe and skin color. Theorganisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actually reaches out to allcommunity members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is not based on tribe butprofessional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid of cultures who havelearned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved to the targetcommunities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs and women sothat equity is achieved.All gender will be awarded opportunity to benefit in equitably. Women and youth whoare mostly left out in recruitment will be considered in all projects and positions will beawarded equitably. Needs assessment will include all community groups so that an all inclusive, agreeable and fair assessment is reached. Both men and women leaderswith an additional youth representative will be part of community health board as perorganisational policy. This will ensure decisions made are all inclusive. SAF UKpolicies have sought to put men at the helm of needs affecting their wives, daughtersand female relatives. The organisation's APP framework seeks to ensure menparticipate through affirmative action for men ensuring that men are not excluded frominterventions targeting women beneficiaries. Women , especially in IDP face multipleprotection related risks, some whose effects are hard to reverse. The organisation has recently amended relevant policies to put protection at the center of our interventions. The organisation proposed to bring integrated services closer to beneficiaries, especially women and children, so that they do not have to walk far to access services exposing them to multiple hazards. A clear referral pathway forreferring cases of GBV has been established in out APP framework, with a GBV focal point set in every program to facilitate this. Children who are often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries but as part of the community. Their views on service delivery and need will occasionally be sought by a professional child specialist so that the whole community participates in program design and implementation. Community diagnosis,community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will seek to include all community groups to ensure all are reached.
