Project Summary

This project aim at promoting Nutrition for vulnerable groups (children 0 – 59 Months, PLWs and caregivers of children less than 2 years) among IDPs and affected communities in the NW and SW regions of Cameroon. The project is going to: Improve access to quality lifesaving services for management of acute malnutrition for children through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acute malnutrition; Improve access to services preventing under-nutrition for the most vulnerable groups (0 – 59 Months, PLWs and caregivers of children less than 2 years). The project will also adopt integrated interventions to improve the nutrition of the most vulnerable children in the targeted health districts in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon. The project will increase access, coverage and utilization of lifesaving nutrition services and to enhance protection of the nutrition status, with a package of treatment/curative and prevention services delivered through mobile clinics, community platforms and health facilities when possible. Activities will be implemented by a network of volunteers (CHW, Community Action Group, women associations, etc.). Our Strategies and approaches for quality and inclusive programming will include:
• Building the capacity of CHW to set-up CMAM (early identification, referral & treatment), optimal IYCF-E and nutrition assessment.
• Establish safe spaces for feeding and responsive care and promote linkages with Child Protection
• Systematically engage with communities to implement preparedness, preventive and response activities at community level, including the promotion of positive practices such as optimal infant and young child feeding, access to and adoption of healthy diets, routine immunization and micronutrient supplementation, and early detection and treatment of severe wasting and other forms of life-threatening acute malnutrition
• Ensuring that all children aged under five years in affected areas are screened regularly for the early detection of severe wasting and other forms of life‑threatening acute malnutrition and are referred as appropriate for treatment services.
• Work with GBV actors to reduce risks of GBV related to nutrition programmes. If there are no GBV actors available, train nutrition staff on the GBV Pocket Guide
The project will promote the family-MUAC” participatory approach aims to train Families on MUAC measurement and self-referral for care. It is critical for early detection and therefore rapid care limiting the occurrence of complications and consequently deaths. The program also includes support to pregnant and breastfeeding Women to increase attendance in ante-natal/post-natal consultation and skilled
The project location is North West, the project will be implemented in the following health districts and health areas; Bamenda (Mankon, mendankwe, Nkwen rural, Azire), Benakuma (Befang, Baworo, Benakuma), Fundong (belo, Mentang, Fundong urban), Kumbo east (Jakiri urban, tatum, Mbah, Mbam, sop, Nkar, Shisong), Kumbo west (Kumbo urban, Kumbo CMA, Buh, BBH, Melim, Kai), Ndop ( Babessi, Bamali, Bamunka urban, Bamunka rural), Nkambe (Nkambe urban, mesaje, Fonfuka), and Wum district (Wum urban, st. martins, Bafmeng, Furu awa)

1 - Besoins / Needs

The socio political crisis that started since 2016 in the two English speaking regions of Cameroon escalated into an armed conflict and insecurity in 2017 has rendered many people displaced from their localities. The increasing tension and hostilities has promoted insecurity and significant internal displacements. While humanitarian needs, joblessness, crime and scarcity of food are on the increase, most farmers have fled from the villages into bushes, forest and a large number made it to nearby cities. Consequently many have become vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. The causes of malnutrition basically in the SW and Northwest region especially during this season have been increased by the scarcity of food due to the continuous displacement of people due to the sociopolitical crisis. North West (NW) and South West (SW) regions of Cameroon are characterized by a relatively low prevalence of acute malnutrition but high prevalence of chronic malnutrition, micronutrient deficiency and poor indicators related to infant and young child feeding practices. However, deterioration of nutrition situation is foreseen since the ongoing conflict continue to inflict immense pressure on the underlying factors to malnutritionChildren under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLWG’s) are among the most vulnerable population group affected by humanitarian crises, being particularly susceptible to nutrition deprivation as a result of physiological vulnerability due to increased nutrient requirements. Populations living in rural remote/hard to reach areas, internally displaced persons (IDPs) in urban/peri urban and households/ communities hosting IDPs are more vulnerable to malnutrition since they are more exposed to underlying factors of malnutrition (food insecurity, limited access to water, poor sanitary conditions and hygiene services, and morbidity due to overstretching of basic social services). Elderly, persons with disabilities, orphaned, separated or unaccompanied children are also at a higher risk of malnutrition as they have limited access to services and humanitarian assistance. These different vulnerabilities are related to their differing nutritional requirements, location (rural remote/urban/hard to reach remote locations) and their status (IDPs/Host population)The nutrition situation in NW and SW regions remains unclear due to absence of a comprehensive nutrition anthropometric survey using SMART methodology. Nutrition screenings integrated in rapid emergency food security assessments (EFSA) revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 5.1% and 7.1% in NW and SW regions respectively. Analysis from Mid Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening data showed that NW region is more affected showing a proxy GAM prevalence of 5.0% and 1.3% in NW and SW regions respectively. However, these findings are not conclusive of the nutrition situation as there could be locations of high nutrition vulnerability that are not yet identified or inaccessible humanitarian assistance. Prior to the crisis, the regions presented vulnerabilities to malnutrition with high rates of stunting of 36% and 28% in NW and SW respectively. Furthermore, poor indicators on Infant and young child feeding practices were reported with 42.5% and 58.3% of exclusive breast-feeding rates for NW and SW region respectively. Complementary feeding also appears compromised, with only 30 per cent of children 6-23 months consuming the minimum acceptable diet and over 40% (44.3% NW and 41.9% SW) of children aged 6-59months were reported to have anemiaThe drivers of malnutrition which includes high food insecurity, lack of access to health services, poor sanitary conditions, morbidity (malaria, respiratory infections, and cholera), poor sanitary conditions and repeated internal displacement remained accelerated in 2020.
