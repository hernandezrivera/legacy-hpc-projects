Project Summary

Lebanon is suffering from multiple crises negatively impacting locals’ food security and resilience. The severe currency devaluation (estimated at approximately 7% of its initial value as of August 2021), has led to severe reduction of national purchasing power, and as such, access to basic needs such as food. As per WFP, food prices have quadrupled throughout 2020, leading to vulnerable Lebanese to resort to severe coping measures to meet their food needs.
RMF aims to distribute 736 hot meals daily to 736 vulnerable beneficiaries in Batroun, Koura, Bsharri, and Zgharta.
This is based on RMF’s experience in food distribution and livelihood:
- RMF distributes 2,500 food boxes on a monthly basis in North-Lebanon
- 9,094 Food Parcels were distributed in North-Lebanon in partnership with Plan International under the SAWA Campaign.
- Distribution of 100 food kits to 400 people affected by the Beirut blast.
- 500 food boxes were distributed in Beirut in partnership with Plan International
- Food for Asset / Training funded by BMZ (2019-2020), and through the EU MADAD Fund (2021) in partnership with WFP.
- Agriculture farmer’s development and livelihoods project/ AFDAL 2, led by Care International (2020 – 2021), and funded by BMZ in partnership with WFP
- RMF is an active member in the North-Lebanon Food Security and Agriculture sector working group.
RMF will outreach the most vulnerable. RMF developed and manages a vulnerability assessment form, hosted on online servers, through which field staff are able to gather beneficiaries’ data. Gathered applications are graded based on an established vulnerability criteria, through which, the most vulnerable beneficiaries will be selected. Segments with the highest vulnerability scores will benefit from hot meals distribution on a daily basis throughout the 11 month initiative.
RMF operates an industrial kitchen in North-Lebanon able to produce 1500 meals on a daily basis. Each hot meal is comprised of a main course, a salad, and a dessert. Meals are produced based on a developed weekly menu that can be shared with targeted beneficiaries. Ingredients are stored with respect to national food safety standards. The kitchen and specialized distribution trucks are subject to constant quality control by certified chefs. The kitchen will allow for 10 vulnerable and trained youths at a time to work in hot meals preparation, ensuring temporary employment. RMF manages a technical institute where youths are trained on food preparation. Youths will be working on a rotational basis, benefitting from hands on experience in food preparation. To note that 94,203 ready to eat meals were delivered till January 2021 to vulnerable Lebanese families in Zgharta, Mennieh, Tripoli, and Bsharri in partnership with UNICEF.
Food ingredients will be procured, and sorted within RMF campus in Mejdlaya, which includes packaging, cold storage, and dairy facilities, with a state of the art warehouse and a distribution center coupled with trucks and trained teams serving the North network, while being strategically located in proximity of all regions in the North. All facilities are subject to periodic disinfection, and all RMF staff are equipped with covid-19 prevention equipment. RMF will prioritize purchasing fresh food ingredients from local producers and farmers, to enhance local economic development. RMF is already collaborating with farmers and producers in the area, and is able to mobilize procurement efficiently.
Hot meals will be delivered directly to each beneficiary’s household through a routing system. RMF staff are already well acquainted with the targeted areas though previous and current projects. The routing schedule will be shared to beneficiaries, to be present at the household on the day of delivery. RMF has ensured access to fuel and good connections with suppliers ensuring constant supply. RMF’s campus and warehouse also benefit from increased power supply through renewable energy.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

RMF will distribute 736 daily hot meals for vulnerable communities in Batroun, Koura, Bsharri, and Zgharta through: - Expansion of RMF’s existing vehicle routing system and delivery schedule, to be shared among targeted beneficiaries within the targeted areas- Outreach by field staff, and selection of 736 extremely vulnerable individuals to benefit from distribution through RMF’s online vulnerability assessment application, and related vulnerability scoring sheet.- Development of a weekly menu meeting beneficiaries’ nutrition needs- Procurement of recommended food ingredients through open competitive tendering. Preference will be made for locally produced items to support local economic development. Vegetables will be procured from local farmers and cooperatives, where RMF is currently leading several agriculture projects, at reasonable prices supporting farmers livelihood- Monitoring of trained youths’ food preparation output quality- Logistics management through disinfection of RMF’s warehouse, trucks, and kitchen, as well as provision of PPEs for distribution, and hot meals preparation staff. RMF kitchen and hot meals will be subject to quality control from a certified chef- Distribution of hot meals to vulnerable individuals in-line with best available distribution standard operating procedures- Collection of M&E data such as beneficiaries’ signatures and photographic evidence, ensuring proper reporting, and hotline management for community queries and feedback.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protection, gender equality, and social inclusion are mainstreamed in RMF’s response to ensure that the needs of women, men, girls and boys are achieved. Given the significant risk fragile communities face, RMF initiates community dialogue with key stakeholders, and municipalities to identify risks and suggest solutions to address rights violations to prevent and mitigate risks. RMF will ensure prevention and response to SEA and consider / integrate other forms of misconduct in the response, as well as undertake hot meals distribution without causing any negative distribution effect, as per the Do No Harm approach, for example ensuring that distribution of hot meals takes place in daylight hours, at each beneficiary’s household, at a pre-determined date shared with the beneficiaries. RMF staff are trained to receive input from marginalized communities suffering from SEA related risks, and will ensure proper referral to RMF’s internal case management unit for proper case handling.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Setting-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints is a key contributor for ensuring accountability for affected populations. An appropriate feedback/complaint mechanisms will be put in place to make sure that affected populations are able to reflect their feedback as well as report on any type of complaint. A dedicated hotline will be activated for this initiative, managed by a trained hotline respondent able to provide logistical feedback, as well as ensure proper referral if needed. The timing in which hot meals distribution will be undertaken in each village will be shared with each village’s residents. RMF will also consider avoiding distribution during community events (for example, weekly prayer) in the route planning. Finally feedback is solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during the implementation of the activities.
