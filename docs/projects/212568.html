Project Summary

The project aims to support the resilience of communities through food security and agriculture Response in NW Syria to address the identified needs of the most vulnerable population of IDPs and Host Communities in NW Syria Aleppo The intervention will be designed in line with FSL Cluster guidelines, and the strategic objectives of the sector in HRP 2024 with considering the lessons learnt from similar projects that have been implemented by Bonyan.
Syria's humanitarian landscape in 2024 presents a grim picture, with 16.7 million individuals requiring assistance amidst ongoing crises. The devastating earthquake further compounded the situation, displacing hundreds of thousands, causing nearly 5,900 deaths, and injuring over 12,800 people. The crisis has spawned a relentless cycle of vulnerability, with a disproportionate effect on the most needy, including people with disabilities. The escalating cost of living, exemplified by the quadrupling price of food baskets over two years, underscores the acute need for food assistance and agricultural services, affecting 15.4 million people across the nation.
To address these needs, the project establishes a comprehensive assistance intervention targeting 24,500 individuals (11,000 men and 13,500 women). This multifaceted approach includes:
[1] daily distribution of bread bundles to ensure basic nutritional needs are met.
[2] Provision of food vouchers to offer flexibility in food choices and support local markets for 8 distribution rounds.
[3] Distributing of agricultural inputs for farmers
[4] Establishment of income-generating activities, with a focus on vocational training for youth and women. This initiative aims to empower them to create sustainable businesses, contributing to economic resilience.
[5] Rehabilitation of critical infrastructure, related to bread supply chain and irrigation systems, to improve food production and distribution efficiencies.
Community engagement forms the backbone of the project's strategy. By establishing committees comprised of IDPs, host community members, agricultural association representatives, and local council members, the project ensures an inclusive and participatory approach to beneficiary selection and verification. These committees are tasked with facilitating communication, overseeing the distribution process, and ensuring transparency and fairness in aid delivery.
For 2025 Bonyan plans to expand its activities by 30% to reach total of 32,000 individuals with a total cost of 2.3M USD
The project also addresses various protection risks, including the potential spread of COVID-19 and Cholera, by implementing stringent preventive measures and adapting program activities to minimize health risks. Challenges in accessing distribution points, especially for vulnerable groups, are mitigated through thoughtful planning and community-driven solutions.
PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) measures are a critical component, with appointed focal persons to handle allegations and promote awareness. The project emphasizes survivor-centered approaches, ensuring immediate support and access to information for all participants.
Risks related to fraud, aid diversion, and the mishandling of supplies are proactively managed through comprehensive complaint mechanisms and community engagement strategies. Efforts to prevent crowding at distribution points and reduce the risk of harassment include partnering with multiple bakeries and considering the proximity of distribution points to beneficiaries.
This expanded overview underscores the project's holistic approach to addressing the multifaceted challenges faced by communities in Northwest Syria. Through targeted support, community engagement, and a robust framework for managing risks, the initiative seeks to make a significant and sustainable impact on the lives of those most in need.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Bonyan Organization will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct MSNA to collect most recent data about FSA needs of IDPs, and HCs (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary data Bonyan will assess the market system’s functionality and capacity to absorb increased demand for goods and services, and potential impact on markets to apply the "DO NO HARM" aspects Then, BO will select beneficiaries following transparent processes based on clear vulnerability and eligibility criteria to ensure provision of support to the most vulnerable. All activity will be documented, lessons learnt will be collected to measure impact of the activities and the community initiatives. The project contributes to Scale-up and sustain minimum food and diversified consumption needs by distributing: (1) Bread bundles directly to households on daily bases (1.8 Kg of bread) targeting 2500 HHs for 3 months (78 days), and (2) Provide the assessed food insecure people with monthly in-kind food assistance targeting 800 HHs for 8 rounds following the standards of FSA cluster. On the other hand, the project will Support the self-reliance of affected households by: (1) distributing of agricultural inputs for one round targeting 400 HHs, the inputs will include seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, and equipment; coupled with related training. (2) provision of small livestock, animal feed distribution, artificial insemination, as well as a related training targeting 200 HHs of vulnerable small-scale breeders. And (3) provide a tailored and well adopted vocational training targeting 1000 individuals. Lastly, the project will support the communities through the rehabilitation of 4 productive and economic infrastructures including 2 infrastructures related to bread production and supply chain, and 2 infrastructures related irrigation systems. During the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of Bonyan will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation. Monitoring and quality control will be in more focus on the distribution of bread activities where Periodic field visits to the identified bakeries to monitor and assess the production process, and to ensure that the bakeries are following the standards set (hygiene, bread specification, etc.), also the weight of the bundle will be assessed in the distribution points. Also MEAL team will conduct Interviews and surveys with beneficiaries to measure their satisfaction and taking their feedback after receiving the service.
