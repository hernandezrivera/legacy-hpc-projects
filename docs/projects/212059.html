Project Summary

The crisis in Syria has resulted in over 70% of families being unable to afford basic needs due to worsening economic conditions, ongoing conflict, and poor access to services. In 2023, 77% of households had insufficient income to cover basic needs, with households in different areas requiring income increases of up to 67%. The depreciation of the Syrian Pound, rising costs, and the gap between income and expenditure have negatively impacted food security and reported needs for food and nutrition assistance.
Access to livelihoods, basic needs, infrastructure, and services has deteriorated, threatening the resilience and social cohesion of households. Regarding access to electricity, 73% of households have electricity for less than one-third of the day. The lack of electricity affects the repair and production capacity of key services and local businesses, hindering the potential expansion of local production of essential items and services. Approximately 90% of households in Syria experience a severe income-expenditure gap.
In 2024, based on the HNO an estimated 16.7 million people in Syria need humanitarian assistance, an increase from 15.3 million in 2023, due to high inflation, currency depreciation, increased commodity prices, and earthquakes causing further displacement, deaths, injuries, and damage to infrastructure. Active conflict, military operations, and attacks on civilian infrastructure continue to hinder humanitarian access, exacerbating the crisis.
Before the civil war, Aleppo Governorate was an active hub of industry and commerce, known as the heartbeat of Syrian economic activity. Aleppo governorate is characterized by the largest number of people affected by the ongoing protracted crises and the recent catastrophic earthquake crisis with 64% of the total population of 4,226,203 being unable to meet their basic needs.
Dorcas began providing relief in Aleppo in 2016, and since then, has implemented various projects, including livelihood initiatives that started in 2018. Despite occasional rocket attacks and the impact of the Idleb situation, Aleppo's situation has been relatively stable. However, the financial burden of daily life expenses and displacement from rural Aleppo and other cities have made it difficult for households to meet their increased needs.
Dorcas has continued its Livelihood Project in Aleppo, aiming to support and empower vulnerable families by providing professional skills and developing various enterprises. The project aims to restore urban and rural livelihoods in conflict-affected areas, rejuvenate local markets, and provide awareness sessions for entrepreneurs, farmers, and livestock owners.
The project will support 180 business owners in scaling up their operations and providing new equipment, raw materials, and maintenance for their shops. By doing so, the project aims to stimulate purchasing power and kickstart the micro-economy in the area. Additionally, the project will help beneficiaries supplement their household incomes, making their livelihoods more resilient to shocks and crises.
Dorcas has reached beneficiaries through various strategies, including community centers, direct contacts, market and shop visits, churches, and local NGOs. Despite the successful interventions and positive feedback from previous years, the needs remain enormous, and the challenges continue to grow, with the deterioration of the economic situation and unexpected issues like the cholera outbreak, the Ukraine war, and the February 2023 earthquake, further complicating the situation.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The situation continues to worsen financially & economically, and the daily-life expenses are a burden, in addition to the displacement movement from many rural Aleppo areas and other cities to Aleppo. Job opportunities and financial capacities were not enough to cover the increased needs. Based on all of the mentioned above, the continuous monitoring of the market, the unstable rate of the Syrian pound versus $, the sanctions, the decrease of the export & import activities, the unstable availability of raw materials, Dorcas long waiting lists of beneficiaries requesting support to their businesses, stakeholders, communities committees, the increase of prices in addition to Dorcas participation to the sector meetings... reveal that the needs are still huge and the response is insufficient to support entrepreneurs and allow them to generate income and to live with dignity. So Dorcas will continue the intervention to support 180 small entrepreneurs and empower them to ensure sustainability. The implementation of the activities always starts with a set of criteria: The beneficiaries of this project are working men & women in Aleppo Syria, trying to survive the Syrian economic crisis and civil war by working to generate incomes to support themselves and their dependents, they are equally men & women, married, single, widowed or divorced aged between 18 & 64 years, preferably having (or a member of the dependent) medical conditions and at least 5% of the total beneficiaries are PWD, beneficiaries are willing to work and have sufficient skills in the business. The process starts with the data collection from Dorcas community centers, stakeholders, partners, outreach, and communities' committees... then followed by individual interviews, field visits, studies of the social files, 1st selection, feasibility studies, and beneficiaries' knowledge and skills, offers. Furthermore, the evaluation of the projects by the livelihood team jointly with the beneficiaries, followed by the evaluation of an impartial committee to ensure transparency and objectivity. The execution phase is done jointly by Dorcas' livelihood team and the beneficiary keeping a critical eye on the market and prices to ensure the maximum benefit for the beneficiaries. The execution is not the final step in the implementation activities, it is followed by a 6 months follow-up by Dorcas expert team to ensure the continuity of the small business, without neglecting the continuous presence of the monitoring and evaluation team to ensure the proper follow-up of the process and the respect of CHS.
