Project Summary

This program will address priority needs of approximately 10,500 people in the target location inhabited by IDPs, host community, and returnees. The project will prioritize prompt protection services for women, men, boys and girls as well as provision of quality case management for vulnerable children, paying special attention to UASC and those with other protection concerns. It will provide psychosocial services (PSS) to children whose lives have been severely interrupted by emergency and those at risk of gender based violence (GBV). WOCO will use preventive mechanisms and mitigate risks constantly faced by the community, treating each case as a priority. The project will work closely with other actors to create atmosphere of trust, assuring target population of an environment of free service delivery. Regular monitoring and assessments will be done to find out gaps that need immediate attention, sharing the same with other actors at sub- cluster and cluster levels. 1 - Needs

Upper Nile is currently classified in IPC 4 level with several unmet needs negatively impacting Protection and welfare of women and children as the worst affected. The situation needs urgent intervention by Child protection and Gender Based Violence mitigation actors. Increasing returnees from Sudan to Fashoda County has also witnessed the community scrambling for the meagre resources available especially when FSL main in food distribution partner, WFP is reducing the quantity of food ratio per HH coupled with annual flood that has created stress on the existing humanitarian services. According to IRNA joint assessment conducted on the 3rdof September 2020 by implementing partners in Aburoc IDP settlement (IMA, IMC, WOCO, SI, DANIDA, DRC, NRDC and WVI) to identify the needs of the host community residing in the surrounding villages of the Aburoc, Lul affected by flood, the assessment showed that 133 HH were displaced. The situation required immediate humanitarian response which included provision of life-saving assistance, food, NFI kits (plastic sheets, blankets, clothes for children and mosquito nets), WASH NFI kits distribution and health services. An estimate of 798 children were affected by cold resulting from lack of proper shelter, leading to some children contracting diseases such pneumonia, diarrhoea and other water born diseases. Even as residents scrambled for life-saving services, sexual harassment and domestic violence were raised as a serious concern during the discussion with women and girls. COVID-19 outbreak in South Sudan early last year has further exacerbated the inequality between women, men, girls and boys. Women and girls who make up the majority of frontline health workers, careers at home, community volunteers and mobilisers stand to be overwhelmed with more health and domestic responsibilities with the advent of COVID-19 and bear the brunt of gender based violence. In the wake of COVID-19, the vulnerabilities of women and girls to child marriage and teenage pregnancies have increased manifold. The rate of teenage pregnancies and child marriages have increased across the state as schools are closed and girls are engaged in activities that are exposing them to GBV risk. In South Sudan, several reports from partners suggest that during the COVID-19 pandemic, there have been increase in teenage pregnancies and child marriage, particularly from May 2020 to date. The COVID-19 pandemic has disrupted usual operation of schools, higher learning institutions, routine health services and community-based services. The formal education, where many young people spend most of their time has been severely impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic since the government issued an order to temporarily close down all learning institutions, from pre-schools up to universities. Since March 20, 2020. Further, suspension of social-cultural activities and opportunities for leisure deprives young people of the opportunities for identity development, coping skills, as well as stress reduction, and can lead to mental health problems. In addition, if caregivers are infected, quarantined, or pass away, protection and psychosocial issues for adolescents may arise. Besides, Upper Nile is a top priority county in the HRP COVID-19 infection. Children continue to be forcibly recruited into armed groups while also suffering from other grave child rights violations. CAAFAG reported many such cases in Fashoda inn 2020. Some of these children reintegrated themselves voluntarily into the community without undergoing through comprehensive reintegration process, though there was were changes observed in their behavior. There is a need to change the negative mentality and provide a sustainable reintegration program for these children. Comprehensive approaches are required to identify and release those who reported to be in military barracks. Above all, returnees are likely to be exposed to ERWs and UXOs, which are prevalent in the locations the

2 - Project activities

1) Capacity building of actors on CPiE for WOCO staff and community for 15 frontline staff.2) Facilitate FTR for UASC that are currently in WOCO caseload.3) Conduct training on CP approaches and COVID 19 risk communication for 25 Community represented including members of the child protection committee.4) Case management for vulnerable and at risk children.5) Reach 10,000 children (6000 girls and 4000 boys) with home based/CFS/small group/ psychosocial support activities including safe games and recreational activities.6) Reach 400 children and young people (220 girls and 180 boys) with home based/small group life skills and age-appropriate psychosocial support.7) Provision of MHPSS for children and caregivers including parental support session and mainstreaming COVID 19 risk communication for 180 children (220 boys and 180 girls).8) Provide parents and caregivers (100 women and 80 men) with COVID 19 sensitive parenting skills and PSS.9) Establish 1 child friendly space in Aburoc IDPs settlement to accommodate 300 boys and girls.10) Establish /Strengthen 1 Child Projection referral pathway.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All the WOCO’s Staff especially newly recruited will be trained and regularly mentored on PSEA and code of conduct and will ensure that staff have sign all related safe guarding policies before engagement in any WOCO activities. WOCO will ensure that there is a clear communication strategy and information flow, it will as well ensure that benificaries consents is considered before collecting any information regarding their situation and needs, beneficiaries information will be kept in closed and confidential place, information will only be shared with the relevant service provider on a need to know basis and for the purpose services provision, any information send to the third party will be protected and pass words will only be share with the concern persons. The organization will be committed and accountable to the affected populations by ensuring that there is an internal control mechanism (policies and bi-laws) to guide the project staff and ensure that they are empowered to implement activities in the projects coverage areas. Staff will also be briefed on the Humanitarian Code of Conduct to ensure commitment to standards and appropriate interaction with beneficiaries. Transparency, Feedback and complaints: The organization will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and its affected populations over information provision. The Organization will consult the beneficiaries for both IDPs and host community (10 men and 10 women ; 7 boys and 7 girls) on a regular basis to seek their views on how to improve policies and practice in programming, ensuring information sharing, recognizing communication and reporting channels and that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined to express their views on the project implementation. Participation, design, monitoring and evaluation: The organization will engage affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes and will establish clear guidelines and practices to engage beneficiaries and ensure that the most marginalized population (People with disability) are represented and exercise influence. Persons with disabilities are more likely to experience adverse socioeconomic outcomes such as less education, poorer health outcomes, lower levels of employment, and higher poverty rates.
