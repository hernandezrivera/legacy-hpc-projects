Project Summary

DRC will build the resilience of 21,973 conflict affected and displacement-affected people in the BAY states. This will enable them to regain their economic well-being thus from meeting their basic needs in emergency contexts to recovering and / or strengthening sustainable and resilient livelihoods.
The concept of graduation underpins DRC’s approach to economic recovery. Across all activities, DRC aims at graduating people of concern from assistance to self-reliance and resilience or, in other words, to being able to cover their needs independently of external assistance and in a dignified and sustainable manner.
Using conditional cash transfers for 2,067 individuals, DRC will 39 construct/ rehabilitate community assets to increase access to basic infrastructure and basic social services. DRC will also restore livelihoods such as agriculture, petty businesses for 7,663 individuals and also create new livelihoods like vocational skills for 5,354 individuals in the BAY states through entrepreneurship and small business cash grants. DRC will promote social cohesion amongst communities through community reconciliation and peace dialogues as well as strengthening community level infrastructure for conflict prevention and reconciliation for 6,850 individuals.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in north-east Nigeria which is now in its eleventh year has witnessed an increase violence in 2021 in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states (BAY states). The insecurity has resulted in mass population movement and increased humanitarian needs. The main drivers of the conflict include poverty, unemployment, socio-economic imbalances, marginalization, governance deficits, rapid population growth and climate shocks, all of which remain unaddressed. Solutions to the crisis remain elusive despite government’s offer of amnesty to repentant Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) operatives. Throughout 2021, attacks against civilians and military installations continued resulting in displacement, destruction of infrastructure like roads, hospitals plus destruction of livelihoods. This has resulted in increase of needs and continued reliance on humanitarian aid. According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 8.7 million people (2.1 million from Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector) are currently in need in the BAY states, an increase of 10% compared to 2020. This is coupled with the COVID-19 pandemic and drop in oil prices which could have led the worst recession of the Nigerian economy in four decades. Despite being the largest source of rural employment and livelihood, the agriculture sector has severely been affected with Adamawa and Yobe reporting 38.4% while Borno is at 33% unemployment. The limited inclusion of women and girl’s perspectives policy-making decisions, resource allocation, and implementation in economic and social sectors continues to challenge the advancement of gender equality in north-east Nigeria. As a result, 70% of women live below the poverty line. The situation has even been made worse by NSAG activities and prevailing humanitarian crisis. The COVID-19 pandemic has strained the already weak services and further exacerbated existing needs, impacting movement, livelihoods, access to services, especially for the most vulnerable populations. For example, a study by iMMAP and Data Friendly Spaces (DFS) done between February 2020 and July 2021 on the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the food security, nutrition, and livelihood situation across the BAY states showed that, 44% of individuals in Borno who were in full-time employment before the pandemic became part-time employees as a result of the crisis, while 34% were temporarily laid off, leading to a severe loss of income. At the time of the study, 39% of businesses had to close, while 43% of respondents said that they knew of a business that had failed to reopen due to the impact of COVID-19. Spending also decreased by 43%. Even after restrictions were relaxed, 48% and 24% of respondents indicated that their income had either reduced or completely stopped, respectively. 38% of people did not receive any form of support, and started adopting negative coping strategies, including a reduction in the number of meals consumed per day (16%). 19% of respondents indicated that they no longer had savings as a result of the pandemic, while 26% said they were unable to afford basic goods and services although not specified, and could not afford three meals a day.

2 - Activities

DRC's potential donors for the selected activities are USAID Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance (BHA), the European Union and the Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Activities are planned to be implemented in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states. Activities for the project will include; Activity 1 – Rehabilitation of community assets and improving access to income through Cash for Work activities: This activity will focus on rehabilitating and constructing basic community and social infrastructure in areas of return. Workers will receive cash payments for completed works on a monthly basis. This activity will target 413 households representing 2,067 individualst in BAY states. Activity 2 - Vocational Skills Trainings for Youth: DRC will train 1,071 youth representing 5,354 individuals in new marketable vocational skills identified by labor market assessments. This activity will strengthen capacity of youth and create off-farm income generating activities mainly in Yobe and Borno states. Activity 3 – Small business management training and distribution of Business Grants for Small Scale Business Start-up: A total of 1,533 households (representing 7,663 individuals - women and youth) will be supported with small business management trainings and business grants to improve their skills and increase their access to safer livelihood options for income opportunities and future recovery. Beneficiaries will engage in the viable small businesses identified by DRC Livelihood Market Assessments for Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. DRC will also provide business advisory and monitoring for women and youth assisted with income generation activities. Activity 4 – Support formation of Village Savings and Lending Associations (VSLA) groups to enhance access to financial services that include credit/loans, savings and fund growth for 462 women and men (representing 2309 individuals) in Yobe and Borno states. Activity 5 – Strengthen community level infrastructures for peace and strengthen community level conditions for: conflict prevention, social cohesion, reconciliation and peace building. This will take place in IDP camp settings, transitionary settings, in host communities and other locations with the BAY states. The activity will target 540 households representing 2,700 individuals. Activity 6 – Implement community reconciliation and Peace-building dialogues/ meetings/ events to support co-existence in areas of return. This will be done through multi-stakeholder dialogue sessions that will be done across the BAY states. The activity will reach 830 households representing 4,150 individuals.Activity 7 - This activity will focus on rehabilitating and constructing basic community and social infrastructure. This will increase access to infrastructure and basic social services in areas of return. DRC has already commenced a participatory Community Based Planning (CBP) process focused on identifying the community assets for rehabilitation or construction utilizing donor guidelines that may include water sources, abattoirs, market sheds, innovative food, schools, security related assets etc, which will determine the exact nature of work. Workers will receive cash payments for completed works on a monthly basis. This activity will target 39 infrastructures.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The project uses a graduated approach. It has livelihood restoration interventions that are aiming at enabling communities restart or expand their original livelihood. It also aims at equipping people with new skills such as vocational skills. There is an aspect of financial inclusion through saving and lending groups. The projects also aims to build connections between community groups, LGA officials, and State officials for greater co-management of grievances and greater trust using multi-stakeholder dialogues. The project sequenced and integrated with other DRC emergency programs in the same or nearby LGAs. It is also implemented in coordination with other partners in the ERL.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

DRC in all proposed locations works with Protection staff that will mainstream protection issues before and during project implementation. DRC has Protection Mainstream strategy for all sectors to ensure protection issues are well mainstreamed. Assessments and community consultations will consider all age, gender and marginalized/minority groups during programme design and planning to ensure that they influence the decisionmaking process for the project. DRC will ensure implementing a do no harm approach to its programming to minimize disrupting social cohesion and peaceful existence. DRC also has a complaints and response mechanism in place to facilitate the report of sexual exploitation and abuse also complemented by regular Protection Monitoring and Screening conducted by Protection staff.
