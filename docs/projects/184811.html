Project Summary

Ensuring life-saving and comprehensive health-care services, including non-communicable disease (NCD) care, as well as essential health screening, treatment, and referral services.
The primary goals are to enhance NCD care delivery (hypertension, diabetes, asthma, anemia, gestational diabetes, pre-eclamptic hypertension, malnutrition, and so on), as well as to operate a NCD based pathological diagnostic laboratory for better screening and treatment with greater accuracy and precision, and to detect and treat precancerous lesions for cervical cancer (CC) using a ‘see-and-treat' module in conjunction with family planning activities, with an expansion of a paperless NIROG digital clinical record (EMR) system to strengthen coordination among service providers in the FDMN camps for better information management and monitoring both for FDMN and host-community. Planning to implement CC screening, with inclusion of family planning activities, and improve overall service accuracy and precision by utilizing NIROG EMR, and operating a pathological diagnostic lab.
HAEFA will place a greater emphasis on incorporating communicable disease screening, e.g. Tuberculosis, strengthening post COVID complications attentions, malnutrition, and GBV assistance and referral, and a greater emphasis on ANC and PNC care, developing a focus section for transgender in NIROG EMR for more precise patient history and follow-up, building capacity in various settings for people with disabilities, a more effective referral system with ambulance stationing, increasing space for establishing a triage center, flu corner, patient waiting area, and breast feeding corner. Engagement with SRH-WG, CHWG, and in collaboration, to deploy FP activities more dedicatedly and deploying community awareness mobilizers. In terms of accountability, HAEFA will strengthen efficient feedback mechanism (exit satisfaction poll, anonymous phone line) aimed at improving health well-being for both FDMN and host-community members, through which HAEFA aims to ensure quality and effective essential service package in both camp 1W and 9 HPs.
Since 2017, HAEFA's medical teams have given healthcare to nearly 170,000 Rohingya refugees in camps.
In 2018-2019, HAEFA received the Grand Challenges Canada's 'Stars in Global Health' award for RMG healthcare services such as EMR-based NCD (HTN, DM, Pregnancy with gestational DM, hypertension in pregnancy/PET) and provided "see-and-treat" cervical cancer(CC) screening using mobile colposcopy ODT and treatment by hand-held thermocoagualtor to more than 10,000 RMG workers at their workplace. As successful expansion of this CC screening, HAEFA expanded this facility to nine sub-districts in northern part of the Bangladesh and six sub-districts in Cox's Bazar by providing treatment to more than 37,000 women (30-60 years) in 2020-2021.
HAEFA also pioneered a COVID-19 Competency Training of Trainers program in collaboration with Brown University in 2020. The training was aimed at the healthcare professionals at all levels and institutions including physicians, nurses, midwives, public health workers, ER/lab/ICU/OR, hospital directors, and policy makers in the camps and in Bangladesh. In 2021, HAEFA developed an Advanced COVID-19 Clinical Management Certification Course in collaboration with the Bangladesh Ministry of Health, the DGHS, and USAID, which adheres to the ninth version of the National Guideline of Covid-19 Management for BMDC Certified Clinicians for effectively managing Covid cases where 3,000 BMDC Certified Clinicians received the training. Currently, HAEFA is providing Mental Health Support and Resilience TOT in collaboration with the DGHS for the healthcare workers of Bangladesh. Additionally, as part of Emergency Preparedness, HAEFA with Project HOPE, provided and is ready to provide medicines and medical supplies to the medical teams for treating health problems of the population impacted by Cyclone Amphan in May 2020.

1 - Identified Needs

1. Needs of FDMN (Rohingya): Ensuring adequate, respectful application of minimal requirements for services in health facilities is a constant problem for the FDMN (Rohingya) population. Access to health services is becoming increasingly difficult for persons with disabilities, women, children, and transgender people. Sexual, reproductive, and maternal health services, for example, have been reduced in recent years. Current confinement measures have made it more difficult for them to access services such as menstrual hygiene management (MHM), sexual reproductive health, women and girl friendly environments, and access to protection and justice for survivors of GBV. Such uncertainty has fostered emotions of insecurity and stress, which have led to widespread mental health issues among all people, particularly men and boys. This increased the necessity for community monitoring, rapid efforts to address fears and misconceptions about isolation facilities triage system, specialized treatment and referral channels to tackle the issue and stigmatization. There are shortages in Non- Communicable disease (NCD) management, laboratory diagnostic capacity, screening for diseases among the survivors such as cervical cancer, and specialized services (skin & gestational diabetes and hypertension in pregnancy, and other health care services for people with palliative needs). A laboratory will be able to address better understanding regarding the NCD treatment. The lab tests such as Serum Electrolytes (Na+,K+,Cl-),Serum Creatinine, Serum Alkaline Phosphate (ALP),Total Cholesterol, Triglycerides, Serum Albumin, Anemic Patients, Hb% test etc. will help determine the treatment of the patients better. Providing treatment for the NCD patients and keeping them under control will provide long term benefits to the FDMN population. The NCD patient need constant monitoring (BP, RBG, FBG, malnourishments) and surveillance for their stability to be maintained. A record of the changing statistics needs to be tracked and recorded for the betterment of the patient. Furthermore, there is a need to increase the availability of family planning services to everyone, as well as the number of door-to-door community health worker volunteers who ensure gender balance and representation across diverse communities in raising awareness for cervical cancer screening and family planning initiatives. Gender sensitivity and protection training for health care practitioners, as well as making all essential services and materials for all forms of sexual and reproductive health care available to all, are critical. Awareness and knowledge must be spread to both genders, and contraceptive methods must be implemented. If assistance is required, the FDMN leaders should be consulted. Counselors must instill trust in the FDMN population and reduce their phobia about contraception. 2) Needs of FDMN (Host Community):Many of the same needs are observed in the host community particularly regarding health care, NCD management, laboratory diagnostic facility, and reproductive healthcare, moreover, host communities reported greater challenges to accessing health facilities, with only 40% reporting safe and easy access to health facilities.In light of NCD, cervical cancer screening and family planning, the gap in knowledge amplified the fear and stigmas surrounding contraceptives immensely affects the host community as well. They are not willing to consult a medical professionals regarding complications faced sexually as they are taboo topics and refrain from discussion until and unless it becomes serious in fear of being judged or shamed. Door to door counselling needs to be implemented across all communities without being judged or harassed for seeking cervical cancer screening. The misconceptions that exist in the society needs to be eradicated for overall benefit of the wellbeing of the patients.

2 - Activities or outputs

1.NCD Treatment: Ensuring Screening and Management for cardiovascular risk factors including hypertension and High Blood Sugar, COPD, Asthma along with Gestational diabetes and Pre-eclamptic hypertension. 2.Diagnostic Lab: Strengthening health care services, particularly laboratory and diagnostic capability, for Rohingya refugees and the Bangladeshi host community in the event of the health post essential service package. 3.Cervical Cancer Screening and Family Planning in coordination with the SRH-WG (Reproductive Health): Planning to using Mobile-ODT and Thermo-coagulator, in collaboration with Brown University, OGSB, DGHS, and an oncologist, to provide cervical cancer screening for female patients aged 30-65 years. In addition to screening, health posts delivers: Family planning awareness initiatives through FP counselling and delivering contemporary contraceptive techniques to consenting people and patients requiring long-term contraception are referred with counseling to SRH-WG. 4.NIROG (Electronic Medical Record System): Utilizing [NIROG]'s digital EMR system to electronically register and track all patients , allowing chronic and recurrent patients to receive more efficient and accurate treatment. 5.Essential Health Screening, Treatment and Referral: Working to ensure ANC and PNC facilities with monitoring and follow-up. Facilitating the diagnosis of common communicable disease such as tuberculosis, as well as their management and referral as needed. Providing Oral Rehydration treatment point for AWD and Outpatient curative care. Providing psychological first aid according to the identification and referring to the concerning working group. An efficient referral system consisting of patients with malnutrition, pregnant women and GBV survivors will be referred to women friendly spaces and trauma clinics with accordance to an ambulance and Medical Assistant. 6.Post COVID Preparedness: Health Posts forecasts a large increase in lung disorders, respiration cases, and arthritic and different organ (renal, hepatic) difficulties following Covid-19 and HAEFA has been given precise care and medical attention and will continue to provide in future. Triage facility were established to screen symptoms, oxygen assist settings are in located and an ambulance will be stationed for immediate medical response and referral. HAEFA shall continue to distribute "Protective Health and Hygiene Packages". 7.Host Community Health Access: (NCD, CDC, Cervical Cancer, and FP): HAEFA has been delivering NCD, communicable disease, and reproductive healthcare to the local host populations in the adjacent unions of camps. HAEFA will promote health awareness education, with the goal of decreasing stigma and misinformation among host communities. 8.Equitable Health Services: HAEFA will continue to ensure equal and comprehensive health access. Separate latrines for men and women are in effect, but plans for a separate latrine for transgender also ramp in all settings for disable persons are in the building process along with triage center, flu corner, patient waiting space, and breast feeding corning. 9.Community Awareness Mobilizer: HAEFA CAM makes regular phone calls and visits to patients, focused on presumptive covid-19 cases. Furthermore, will conduct regular door-to-door awareness campaigns focusing on mental health issues in conjunction with FP counseling and maternal health care, promoting antenatal and post-natal checkups in accordance with CHWG.10.Child-friendly Feedback Mechanism: HAEFA has and will continue to have a child and adolescent-friendly, gender-responsive, inclusive, and confidential feedback and complaint procedure. Negative complaints will be rewarded with Feedback candy. 11.Publicizing Health-wellbeing: HAEFA HPs will continue to be decorated with posters depicting symptoms and treatments for various ailments. Tele Awareness Videos, Posters, Banners, Leaflets, and Signboards will aid in bringing persons' health to the notice.
