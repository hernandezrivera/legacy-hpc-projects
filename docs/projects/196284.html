Project Summary

This project delivers a strategic and integrated set of interventions to restore local economy in the aftermath of protracted conflict, and deteriorated economic situation. Its main objective is to create income generating opportunities for the most vulnerable IDPs, and host community members. The project proposes an integrated protection and livelihood intervention where the most vulnerable households (HHs) (those being referred from child protection case management actors and GBV case management actors, as well as PwDs) will be directly targeted to enhance their resilience and promote their access to employment and entrepreneurship opportunities.
By pursuing the referred main objective, the project aims to achieve the following outcomes: 1. Essential socioeconomic services are revived and restored through the creation of short-term work opportunities and rehabilitation work ( markets, Schools) and roads gravelling and repair that linked markets with IDPs sites 2. Access to inclusive livelihoods and entrepreneurship support is enhanced through vocational training (VT) 3. Local small businesses and entrepreneurs are supported 4. Social cohesion among IDPs and host communities is enhanced. For this purpose, a set of interlinked activities will be conducted in coordination with FSL, WASH, Shelter and Protection clusters.
The added value of SARD is anchored around our experience in both ERL and Protection activities, which will be a key to ensure the smooth transitioning between protection activities to livelihood activities for the most vulnerable
The project aligns itself and contributes to the objective of strengthening access to livelihoods by creating inclusive income generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery. Failing income, widespread unemployment and diminished purchasing power have been leading millions of Syrians to poverty over the past years. Structural changes in the labour market -caused by the fragile security situation- have increased challenges to accessing any income sources.
The growing dependence on more uncertain sources of income, such as through self-employment, remittances and humanitarian aid, combined with the deteriorating purchasing power of local currency has forced many households to develop different coping strategies to cover their basic needs. This situation has only been aggravated by the protracted conflict. For the individuals whose businesses were affected or damaged and based on each’s circumstances and evaluation, targeting will be focused on providing the main instruments/equipment/machinery to make them capable of revitalizing the business once again. For the HH who are in need of sustainable livelihood, assistance based on vocational training, cash for work support and the access to business grants will be provided. Moreover, the intervention will target the most vulnerable of HHs in NWS, those where cases of children vulnerability are being managed by child protection case management, as well as those where cases of GBV are being managed by GBV case management actors.
The amount of cash disbursed on a monthly basis is determined and adjusted based on prevailing labor rates, to ensure that CfW payments are lower than competitive market wages.
SARD will conduct comprehensive labor market assessment to identify the needs, demands, and gaps of careers, jobs, and vocations in the targeted locations, especially in floods affected areas and nearby host communities in cooperation and coordination with the SEECC. Thought out the process, SARD will ensure that women and PwDs are well represented in the data collection process (be it FGDs, KII, Surveys) which will reflect the actual needs from their own perspectives and in their words. All project data will be disaggregated by Gender and disability. This includes specifying a target for women and target for PwD.

3 - Needs

The draft 2023 HNO for ERL indicated: As socioeconomic deterioration continues, Early Recovery and Livelihoods needs are expected to continue rising. A further increase in the cost of living – due to, among other factors, the reduction of subsidies, general price increases, volatility of the exchange rate, and the global economic outlook affecting remittances and return rates –, is likely and will result in a further deterioration in terms of real income and purchasing power across all population groups. The sector foresees the income/expenditure gap to continue increasing or remain high in the coming year across all population groups. Without significant investments in key sectors to boost economic growth, thereby increasing the productivity of inputs, driving profits up and stimulating a higher demand for workforce, as well as measures to raise wages while controlling and reducing inflation, the livelihood interventions of humanitarian actors are expected to be insufficient to significantly change the negative income/expenditure gap in the short term. Considering the current global economic outlook, a period of further economic contraction is to be expected for 2023, with reduced production, additional currency devaluation, severity of poverty, and further socio-economic deterioration affecting IDPs, residents, and returnees.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project proposes a comprehensive, livelihood intervention where the most vulnerable households (HHs) (those referred from Protection actors and PwDs) will be directly targeted to enhance their resilience and promote their access to employment. The project formulation is based on needs identified in RNA/REACH/HNAP data. Consultations conducted with stakeholders have informed the design of thisproposal. The project aims to achieve the following:Equitable access to basic services are improved through the rehabilitation of basic economic and educational infrastructures in Suran sub-district linked with thecreation of short-term livelihood opportunities.Access to inclusive livelihoods and entrepreneurship support is enhanced through an innovative and unique three-phase scheme (one-month vocational training,one-month apprenticeship, and four-month job placement).Local workshops owners are supported to enhance their capacities to train and host the three-phase scheme applicants (VT, apprenticeship, and job placement)as well as increase their production to sustain longer-term employment and enhance the economic cycle.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Under the current context, the most vulnerable categories, such as women headed HH, PwD, may be excluded from accessing the service due to the socio-cultural barriers.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures. a) SARD increase visibility for self-referral to ensure inclusion of the most vulnerable individuals. b) SARD applies a scoring system to all selected beneficiaries in order to categories them according to their vulnerabilities. c) PWD friendly tools such as ramps and handrail will be provided, complaint response mechanism (CRM). PWD will be considered by designing tools and conducting focus group discussions FGDsProtection Risk: The high target of the project may lead in some cases to reduce the quality of the service.Likelihood: low, Impact: medium.Mitigation Measures: a) Prioritization of safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: in IDP sites/camps, the program and MEL teams constantly monitors the quality of the assistance delivered, ensuring that only quality activities are provided. b) SARD has a strong CRM in place that is regularly advertised to local communities.Protection Risk: the services provider contracted by SARD, may employ children to work on sites.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD’s MEL and Project team conduct spot checks and regular visits to work sites, b) SARD included the PSEA, CSG, and CoC in the signed contract, c) SARD clearly specify the discipline measures in case of any breach.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Due the nature of the project, household visits are required to assess the needs and deliver the services which may expose the most vulnerable individual to the sexual exploitation and abuse.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: Through SARD's a) MEL team, and established accountability and complaint mechanism. B) constantly check the efficiency of the reporting pathways; c) SARD run regular refreshing training on PSEA; d) awareness raising activities to the beneficiaries of the program.Risk: SARD works with contractors to deliver services, the latter may abuse their power or violate PSEA, CSG and CoC.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD included the PSEA, CSG, and CoC in the signed contract with the contractors, b) SARD clearly specify the discipline measures in case of any breach by the contractors of the policies mentioned above, c) awareness raising for beneficiaries on the mean to set a complaint.Risk: The socio-cultural context in the area may impact the relationship between SARD employees which may results in abuse of power or sexual harassment.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD value a divers and transparent working environment, therefore, it ensure all staff are aware of the PSEA and CoC; b) review the CoC and PSEA on regular basis and updates them accordingly; c) review the complaint mechanism and ensure all staff are aware of them by running refreshing training on regular basis.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions & RisksAssumptions: - Identification and willingness by targeted individuals to participate in work activities - Other IDPs accepting selected individuals in conducting short-term work without exacerbating possible pre-existent social tensions. - Security situation is stable - Women are included in the activity - Local labor market and local communities will not be affected negatively because of the CFW activity. - Work environment secure against SEA and CSG issues. - Participants are well trained and equipped to perform their tasks appropriately. Risks -Persistency/return to negative coping mechanisms, such as the employment of children, during the short-term work opportunity or at the end of it. Mitigation: the project will purposefully target households at risk of child labor or where child labor cases are present, with vocational training, CfW and business support. Moreover, the child labor cases will benefit from protection support in the context of the project. -Local community discourages women participation in CFW. Mitigation: Selection criteria will be followed and well explained to local community , and the project will enroll women in culturally accepted tasks. Risk: Smaller USD denominations, such as 5, 10, and 20 USD banknotes, are unavailable or are poor quality. Mitigation: SARD will provide the TRY as equivalents of smaller USD denominations when the latter are not available.
