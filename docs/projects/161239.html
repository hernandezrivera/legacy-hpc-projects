Project Summary

Years of conflict, displacement and underdevelopment have limited people’s livelihood opportunities, marginalized women’s formal employment opportunities, and weakened families’ ability to cope with the protracted crisis and sudden shocks, like the death leading to psycho-social distress and mental health conditions. Five years of the most recent conflict has forced nearly 4.2 million people to flee their homes in search of safety, nearly 2 million of them within and 2.2 million outside the country. While the intensity of conflict may have reduced recently, and clashes contained to certain regions, vulnerable children and families will continue to experience the impacts of the conflict through 2019 and beyond. United Nations reports indicate that all parties to the conflict have repeatedly violated international humanitarian law and perpetrated serious human rights abuses, including gang rape, abductions, sexual slavery of women and girls, and recruitment of children, both girls and boys. People affected by the conflict, including the more than 300,000 refugees in South Sudan, repeatedly identify security among their primary needs (HNO 2019).
Approximately 61% of the affected population are children, of which 1.9 million boys and girls face acute and severe protection risks. Boys and girls continue to be exposed to threats of recruitment, psycho-social distress, family separation, abuse, neglect, exploitation, and sexual and physical violence. Since 2013, over 100,000 children in South Sudan have been affected by 3,500 verified Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism incidents, of which killing and maiming accounted for 12%; denial of humanitarian access 10%; rape and other grave sexual violence accounted for 8%; abduction 8 %; and attacks on schools and hospitals 7%. Over 20,000 children are estimated to have been recruited to armed forces and armed groups and more than 1 million children have been affected by psycho-social distress and at risk of developing mental health conditions. Needs remain high particularly in former Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Lakes states which are also locations prioritized in this project. Among the IDP population, women and children more vulnerable due to gender roles and dynamics in the communities and the protection risks and further affecting the protective environment of children and support networks (HNO 2019).
In response to the above needs, Save the Children will support the most vulnerable children and their families with the provision of comprehensive case management services to 4,000 affected children (boys: 2,120, girls: 1,880); provision of resilience building psycho-social support (PSS) activities to 22,151 beneficiaries (boys: 9,200, girls: 12,951); community mobilization and engagement; training and facilitation of community based structures to develop and implement child protection community action plans; structured awareness and sensitization on child protection and cross cutting issues to 47,200 people (boys: 10,624, girls: 11,876, men: 11,754, women: 12,946); advocacy and mainstreaming child protection in other sectors in line with CPSC, revised CPMS standards and IASC Centrality of Protection principles among other key quality standards. The project will be implemented in Jonglei (Bor South, Akobo and Nyirol), Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East, South & North, Magwi and Lafon) Lakes (Rumbek Centre and Rumbek East) and Northern Bahr el Ghazal (Aweil East) States, building on SCI's long-term presence and ongoing programs in these areas.
The proposed activities will contribute to CO1: Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach areas;CO2: Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced resilience and CP Objective 3: Strengthen the capacity and resilience of children and community mechanism.

1 - Needs

Akobo is located in former Jonglei State. Its a point of trade and transit between South Sudan and Ethiopia. Since 2013, South Sudanese heading to or returning from Ethiopia pass through the route. REACH assessment in June 2019 recorded arrivals and departures on a daily basis since May 2015. The situation puts children at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse neglect and thus the need for SCI to provide lifesaving CP services to enhance protection of vulnerable children and families. In July 2019, beneficiaries highlighted need to strengthen and expand lifesaving CP services. In Lakes State, Insecurity and protection concerns persisted in the first quarter of 2019. Nearly half (49%) of assessed settlements in March reported population were feeling unsafe, comparable to 53% in December 2018. Protection concerns were driven by insecurity across the region .Fear of cattle raiding is increasing and percentage of assessed settlements reporting cattle raiding as the main protection concern for men and boys rose from 8% in January to 37% in March. Key protection concerns highlighted by the population are cattle raiding (20%), violence between neighbors (11%), communal violence (16%), sexual violence (6%) among other issues. Girls are at risk of early marriage reported by 29% of assessed settlements. Violence between neighbors was reported in 11% of settlements pausing risks of family separation, psychosocial distress, risk of abuse, exploitation and neglect, (Situation Overview: Lakes State South Sudan January - March 2019). In Aweil East, boys and girls are at risk of recruitment into armed groups and forces. SCI observed presence of IDPs and returnees in remote locations and areas proximity to the Khartoum border and they are in need of lifesaving Child Protection services. On 3rd September 2019, some CAAFAG were captured in the recent fighting between the SSPDF and rebel group of former Army Chief of general staff Paul Malong Awan. 21 boys were released through coordinated support from Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare, NDDR and UNICEF. More are expected to be released in South Sudan. There is also need to strengthen MHPSS and CAAFAG reintegration, Case management/ FTR services, provision of reintegration kits, education, medical, MHPSS services, food and nutrition, WASH among other holistic support (SCI Report, June 2019). Assessment conducted in Kapoeta locations in August 2019 in locations CP activities were expanded in 2018 highlighted Child abuse, exploitation, and violence are major risks. Others include internal displacement, poverty in the areas exposes children to risks of abuse, exploitation and violence. Cattle raids, child abductions and young children herding cattle and working in the cattle kraals hence risks of child labor and exploitation. UASC WG in South Sudan conducted capacity building needs in early 2019 for all UASC partners. Key constrains highlighted are inadequate or lack of funds for continuity of FTR activities, insecurity, lack of humanitarian access, transport and logistical challenges, communication challenges, need for ongoing technical support and mentorship. They recommended support to strengthen their capacity, resource mobilization for continuity of FTR and CM management, supervision and CPIMS+ training. Active caseload of 8000 UASC are still in need of FTR/reintegration services. In Maban, the host community is 67,000 individual and150,138 refugees (UNHCR, Bunj Maban Situational Report: October 2019).Relations with host communities are constrained. Presence of armed actors remains a major risk to physical safety of humanitarian and civilian .CP issues risks for UASC child marriage, FGM, teenage pregnancies,denial of education opportunities, discriminatory practices including lack of women/girls participation. 400 beneficiaries (40 per location) comprising of 200 boys and girls 200 men, women, caregivers, leaders and CPCBN were consulted, gave views on project needs.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. The Child Protection Interventions will be implemented in line with the National, Regional and International Framework and related policies. This includes South Sudan Childrens'Act 2008, South Sudan Transitional constitution, Refugee act, UNHCRC, African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of Children, Paris Principles, Birth and Registrations, ILO among others. The project will also focus on provision of age appropriate information to the target populations on these policies and the explain adherence to the humanitarian principles (including Child Participation, children rights, Do No Harm and Best interests of the Child). Centrality of Protection principles will also be incorporated thrpought the programming cycle by coordinating with CPSC, different working groups, sectors and country teams to provide coordinated support to beneficiaries, avoid duplication, strengthen capacities of families, utilize protective factors and in the long run, minimize negative humanitarian impacts such as dependence.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Displaced population such as IDPS, returnees and affected communities including UASC are among the prioritized populations for this project. . In total, 400 beneficiaries (40 per location) comprising of 200 children aged 10 years to 17 years, and 200 men, women, vulnerable caregivers, leaders and CPCBN were consulted and provided additional inputs on the needs, proposed interventions and target groups for this project. Their perspectives and needs have been incorporated in development of this project to address identified needs and will be part of the beneficiaries to access and benefit from this project. Due to the ongoing crisis, some of the proposed intervention areas are still prone to ethnic-based conflict which might aggravate violence against women and children, cause displacement, disrupt agricultural and market-related activities. SCI will apply conflict sensitivity approach to programming and engagement of community leaders and local Authorities to provide integrated services in the hard to reach areas. Community acceptance on SCI programming is high and SCI has presence and access to all the locations proposed in this project and it’s seen as a neutral organization hence will continue to coordinate with community structures, leaders and local Authorities to provide lifesaving interventions including access to remote and inaccessible locations. Security focal person will continue to coordinate and collaborate with Government and security organs to regularly analyses information, provide advice to staffs, conduct risk assessment for project activities and ensure safe programming in the affected communities. The assumption is that security will remain relatively calm, caseworkers will continue accessing the beneficiaries per location and community based structures will continue supporting Child Protection activities. SCI will continue to work with the CPSC and other humanitarian agencies to secure children’s safe access to support affected communitities.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is strongly embedded on the socio ecological model hence holistically addressing issues affecting children at family, community, society and strengthening child’s own capacity to protect self and others from harm. In addition, the project focusses on strengthening community based structures while strengthening and equipping families with necessary tools to care and protect children as a long term and exit strategy. Th project also greatly focus on increase community sustainability knowledge transfer, participation, engagement, accountability and ownership. The project is also in line with the South Sudan National Social Protection Policy Framework hence will continue to coordinate and collaborate with the Government structures such as Ministry of Gender, Child and Social services, Ministry of Education among others. Government representatives will also be included in interagency capacity building initiatives and work with Ministry of Gender for eventual handing over of activities as a long term plan. As part of localization agenda, SCI will continue to provide technical support, capacity strengthening through trainings, mentorship and coaching of the UASC working group and CMTF members (who are mainly National CP organizations) to build national capacities as a long term strategy to sustain and retain capacities within the country.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

All the proposed interventions are aligned with the National objectives for the CPSC, PSS working group, CAAFAG guidelines, CMTF, UASC working group and the South Sudan National Social Protection Policy Framework. In refugee settings (Maban refugee camps), the interventions are well aligned with UNHCR Best Interests Procedures, Social Protection/IDPs and National Refugee Return Framework IA Case management SOPS framework for Protection of children goal 1-6 focused on safety of beneficiaries, participation, legal documentation, addressing specific needs and durable solutions for affected children and families. In addition, the project greatly contributes to all 3 CPSC objective 1 Enhance access to comprehensive case management services for vulnerable girls and boys with protection concerns including unaccompanied and separated children(SO1); Objective 2 Provide extremely vulnerable girls, boys and their caregivers with quality age and gender appropriate Psychosocial support through community based interventions (SO2) and Objective 3 Strengthen the capacity and resilience of children and communities to prevent and respond to violence through community based mechanisms, coordination and evidence based response (SO3) and HRP 2019 and 2020 plans. quality standards will also have applied in all interventions such as Revised CPMS standards, MHPSS guidelines in emergencies, Alternative care guidelines, PSS interagency manual, Comprehensive case management SOPs, Revised information Sharing and Data Protection Protocol, UASC SOP and CAAFAG SOPS/Guidelines among others.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project will target IDPs, Refugees, returnees, host community hence it is linked to short term and durable solutions such as community and family reintegration, placement and formalization of care arrangements for UASC , community reintegration , access to identification documents and lifesaving services among others. Comprehensive assessments, Best interest Assessments and Best Interests Determination will be conducted to inform durable solutions inline with child's Best Interests, safety and dignified support. The affected children and families will have access to comprehensive case management services, PSS support, lifesaving protection enhancing information, trainings and capacity building among others. The focus will be on information and knowledge transfer, strengthening resilience and positive coping mechanism, improved protective environment for children, community participation and engagement, addressing immediate and long term needs and sustained change all linked to the social ecological model of addressing children risks and vulnerabilities in all the spheres. The signed revitalized peace agreement and upcoming formation of the Transitional Government provides great opportunities to address Child Protection concerns, support family and community reintegration and implement durable solutions for affected populations. SCI will continue to coordinate with CPSC, CMTF and Protection cluster to explore durable solutions applicable in South Sudan.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

SCI project focus on strengthening the capacity of families and Community Based Mechanisms such as CBCPN, Adolescent peer to peer groups, children clubs, community leaders, National organizations as part of a sustainable and exit strategy for continuity of interventions beyond this project. Other SCI staff in Nutrition and Education who will continue to provide long-term support to beneficiaries beyond the project period will receive CP mainstreaming training. CBCPN, and children networks will continue to implement CP activities such as identification and linking children with service provides, awareness at family, community and school level. The community facilitators and staffs drawn from the targeted community will continue to manage, on voluntary basis, the community recreational and PSS to support children. CP Help desks established in key and strategic areas in the community, schools, hospitals, Nutritional centers and FSL distribution sites will remain functional to provide support to children. Furthermore, SCI will continue to fundraise more resources to bridge gaps and address any emerging needs. Advocacy with Government through Ministry of Gender will be carried out for increased allocation of resources to support CP in the locations and Ministry of Gender will be supported and mentored for eventual Handing over and taking up CP interventions as an exit strategy.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Save the Children implements complains and response mechanism which is integrated with established CP help desks in schools, OTP and Nutrition and Food distribution sites depending on the integrated CP project per location. This acts as one of the models for collecting information on issues affecting beneficiaries, documenting complaints or dissatisfaction of any of the activities, getting feedback and prioritization of beneficiaries needs to inform decision making and improvement of quality interventions. Similar model will be applied in this current project. The models also support early identification of vulnerable and marginalized beneficiaries, referrals and linkages to other services and enhance access to Child Protection information or services. SCI utilize complain desks, suggestion boxes , Posters, IEC materials, translation into local languages and involving the vulnerable groups to develop key messages. One to one approach to get additional feedback from beneficiaries will also be applied. The information will be entered in a database by MEAL officer per location to track actions and ensure issues requiring further attention by Senior Management are flagged without compromising safety and confidentiality of the beneficiary or staff. Through this project, SCI will continue to build the capacity of community structures on Gender Dynamics, Child safeguarding including reporting mechanism, AAP, conducting community assessments to inform needs among other cross cutting themes. Through community feedback mechanism and established CP Help desks, information on the projects will be disseminated allowing vulnerable groups to provide direct feedback to inform decision and action for quality programming. Some specific activities have also been proposed targeting Adolescents, men and women. Children will disability will be included in PSS and CM activities inline with set vulnerability criteria.
