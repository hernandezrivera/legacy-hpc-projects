Project Summary

This project aims to provide comprehensive Nutrition lifesaving Services focusing on the first 1000 days of life (from pregnancy to 24 months) in integration with other sectors (health, Protection, Wash) at community and health facility levels to IDPs and host population most vulnerable (children U5 and PLWs) Nutrition services will provide by the Health staff (community health workers, Nutrition Technicians, doctors, and Nurses).both preventive and curative nutrition services response will follow a:
multi-sectoral approach to prevent and treat malnutrition: Nutrition services in integration with:
1-protection sector: through screening and referring children’s malnutrition cases to child protection center to receive cases management services And Pregnant and lactating with malnutrition will be refereed to receive GBV case management services, Nutrition service will be delivered according UNICEF&WHO Recommendations feeding and care practices that are negatively impacted by neglect.
2- Wash Sector: WASH will be scaled up in the nutrition response by installing handwashing facilities in CMAM centers to promote hygiene practices and address diahorreal links to malnutrition
3- Health sector: through screening and treatment malnutrition cases in health facilities in coordination with health staff (doctor, nurse, Nutrition technician) and follow up malnutrition cases by community health workers teams.Infant and young child feeding interventions will be provided in or attached to communities, health facilities, Mother baby Areas and child friendly spaces in close collaboration and coordination with the health sector. Capacity building for health staff will be conducted to have suitable and needed kills to facilitate IYCF and maternal nutrition support –in particular a strong focus on one-to-one IYCF counselling skills which is one of the most effective interventions to improve feeding practices.Micronutrient deficiency prevention and control initiatives will be promoted through service platforms such as micronutrients supplementation within health facilities, during accelerated campaigns,Provision of management of acute malnutrition will be provided at the health facility and community levels and in integration with infant and young child feeding services and primary health care services.

4 - Needs

According to HNO Report 2020,Chronic malnutrition on the rise with almost half a million children stunted,is one out of eight children in Syria is chronically malnourished this ratio is worse in areas affected by displacements,returns,and over-burdened communities such as North East Syria,Idlib(1 out of 5 children),and Aleppo (1 out of 4 children ).Stunting in Syria is largely caused by poor infant feeding practices,access to quality diversified diet,and repeated illnesses.Throughout Syria, about 240,000 children U5 years,over 50,000 pregnant and lactating mothers are in need for urgent lifesaving curative nutrition services.At least 36,000 boys and 35,000 girls are acutely malnourished and are at increased risk of health complications,including death.Furthermore,it is the youngest children that are the most vulnerable and affected,with higher numbers of acute malnutrition amongst infants <6 months.one out of every three mothers and children are anemic.Anemia among mothers and children is caused by limited access to health care,reduced quality meals (diverse diet),child marriage,which could lead to increased maternal mortality and morbidity.Currently over one million children and over three million mothers in Syria are in a need of multisectoral support to treat and prevent anemia.Without this support the cycle of chronic malnutrition in Syria will continue,over 90% of mothers will develop complications, and 5% of them could possibly die.According to the smart survey in northern of Syria2019.The prevalence of GAM,defined as Weight-for-height Z scores(WHZ)<‐2 and/or oedema,in survey area,was 0.8% (0.4-1.7 95% C.I),and the prevalence of SAM,defined as WHZ <‐3 and/or oedema,was 0.7%(0.3-1.6 95% C.I)with no cases of oedema found. Prevalence of GAM appears higher in Girls (1.5%) than Boys(0.3%),but the difference was not statistically significant (p=0.156).The prevalence of stunting,defined (HAZ)<‐2 in children 6-59 months was17.8%(14.5-21.6 95% C.I.).

5 - Implementation of activities

Comprehensive preventive and curative Nutrition Services multi sectors integration will be delivered to outpatient and inpatients through health staff (doctors-Nurses- Community health workers- Nutrition technicians) service will deliver at 6 health PHCs and one inpatient facility through the next activities:- Provide facility and community-based one-on-one counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF-E) and maternal nutrition practices focusing on the first 1000 days of baby- Malnutrition screening for pregnant and lactating women and children under five years age and referring malnutrition cases (impatient &outpatient) to be treated in health facilities at the same time referring children and women with malnutrition to protection facility to be served according the protocols and follow up cases by community health workers who will deliver hygiene massages and wash will be scaled up in the nutrition response by installing handwashing facilities in CMAM centers to promote hygiene practices and address diahorreal links to malnutrition,- Distribution nutrition supplies (micronutrient and high energy biscuit and plumby doz) at community and facility level which contribute avoiding malnutrition and anemia,- group raise awareness sessions will be conducted at community and facility level with mothers and caregivers to enhance applying correct IYCF practices, breast feeding - all previous activities will be delivered by community health workers at community level during house hold visits and by health staff (Nutrition technicians, Nurse ,midwife, doctors)at health facility level ,nutrition technicians will coordinate with doctors and community health workers during treatment and follow up malnutrition cases.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk of GBV against of staff(CHWs&Nutrition technicians):Likelihood:Medium,Impact high Mitigation:staff will be selected form the targeted communities, each tow CHW will conduct house hold visits together as a team and conducting visits will be done in coordination with local authorities,staff will be trained to identiﬁcation of GBV cases.2-Risk of Exploitation or abuse by staff when accessing assistance and services,Likelihood:Low,Impact:High,Mitigation:Clear explanation to the community about available services and targeted beneficiaries,Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting,Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/assistance,through regular and targeted sessions, including separate sessions with different groups that may be more at risk,ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality,Design specific complaint mechanism.Provide training to staff for code of conduct and PSEA.3-Risk of discrimination in providing services.Likelihood:Low.Impact:High,Mitigation:Ensuring that the designed phase focused on the accessibility of services by vulnerable groups with effective community-based complaints mechanisms,and ensure adherence to humanitarian principles,the project adopted a“Do No Harm”while providing services.4-Lack of consideration for the safety and security to access services and targeting of civilian infrastructure.Likelihood:Low,Impact:High.Mitigation:Consider mobility patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing a static facility or a mobile service or combine both;Conduct safety audit to ensure safe access by beneficiaries with decreased mobility.Engage local authorities on safety and security in the community,Select staff for all activities with adequate knowledge of the community,but also respecting balance in gender,age,social profile.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Health facilities in safe areas are not vulnerable to targeting - Exchange rates are fixed and do not affect the purchase of materials according to the appropriate quantity and quality- Nutrition Supplies is available in time Risks- Targeting the health facilities of the project because of the war- Great changes in Exchange Rates - Nutrition supplies is not available Mitigating measures- Prepare an emergency plan so that we provide service to beneficiaries through mobile clinics - The budget includes a margin covering the expected change in the exchange rate- Secure inventory in the warehouse.
