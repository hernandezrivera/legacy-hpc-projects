Project Summary

AHF will maintain the provision of nutrition services through stabilization center in Sarmada children’s hospital which considered as is one of the specialized referral hospitals at north east of Idleb governorate for health and nutrition cases, 2 OTP centers in Sarmada and Salama, 12 RRTs covering the camps in commienties around Sarmada (in subdistrict Aldana) and 3 covering Salama IDPs camps, and one RRT covers Kaft Bonni area, the staff of the centers and the RRTs is qualified and well-trained in many topics in nutrition cluster.
The nutrition services include:
- Outpatients therapeutic Program Centre (OTP), 2 centers in Sarmada and Salama run by 1 pediatrician, 1 Nutritionist, 1 nurse and 1 data Entry cover OPD services on daily, providing assessment and management of outpatient pediatrics with malnutrition including SAM, MAM patient follow up, routine assessment and treatment for MAM and SAM without complication. So, receive referrals from RRTs to OTP Centre.
- Stabilization center in Sarmada children’s hospital: which run by 1 pediatrician, 1 Nutritionist, 3 nurses (1 nurse every 8 hours , 3 shift)and 1 data Entry , 3 cleaner cover inpatient services on daily 24/7 , providing daily assessment and management of inpatient pediatrics with SAM with complication.
- RRTs: 16 RRTs (12 in Dana subdistrict, 1 in Kafr Nabi and 3 in A'zaz subdistrict) each team includes: 1 Nutritionist, 2CHW, 1 data Entry and 1 nurse, cover outreach services:
Screening, referral to OTP center and follow up children 6-59, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished, Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers with moderately malnourished children
Distribute micronutrients for preventing of anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant, lactating women, IYCF-E recommended feeding practices, monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes, Support health/nutrition education and promotion and Nutrition Supplements distribution
Before the project implementation, M&E team will make a regular collecting the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. Monitoring and evaluation of the quality of the service provision through analyzing the collected data based on the Log-Frame, the project will be monitored and evaluated through three levels:
1) The operational, for which the project manager is responsible.
2) The technical, for which the technical team are responsible.
3) The objectives, for which the MEAL team is responsible.
Monitoring tools:
•Performance Indicator Tracking Tables (PITTs), issued in monthly basis.
•Satisfaction survey among beneficiaries and stakeholders will be analyzed and reported to stakeholders.
•Project Evaluation Report, by the end of the project-term.

4 - Needs

According to the HRP 2021 for Syria; Chronic malnutrition is evident in most sub-districts throughout Syria - notably in the Northwest of Syria (Aleppo, Idlib).Chronic malnutrition is also on the rise, with almost half a million children stunted (low height for age) in 2020 and face losing their future due to hindered learning and physical developments, as well as early death. While at least one out of eight children in Syria is chronically malnourished, this ratio is worse in areas affected by displacements, returns, and over-burdened communities such as Idlib (1 out of 5 children), and Aleppo (1 out of 4 children).Stunting in Syria is largely caused by poor infant feeding practices, access to quality diversified diet, and repeated illnesses. To prevent chronic malnutrition strengthening optimal infant feeding practices and maternal nutrition throughout the first 1,000 days of life is critical not only to the survival of children in Syria but for their future. Acute malnutrition remains relatively high among internally displaced children and mothers and the population affected by conflict. Throughout Syria, about 240,000 children under the age of five and over 50,000 pregnant and lactating mothers need urgent lifesaving curative nutrition services. Al least 36,000 boys and 35,000 girls are acutely malnourished and are at increased risk of health complications, including death. Furthermore, it is the youngest children that are the most vulnerable and affected, with higher numbers of acute malnutrition amongst infants under six months.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AHF will coordinate closely with the Nutrition cluster, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustment is needed. AHF has the access and qualified program team, the Equipment are available, the place is available, the fiels staff is available, the required supplies and medication available, the electricity is available by functioning electrical generators.Risk 1: Conflict related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors and shifting alliances (L)Response: Postponing the services till safety is back or relocation if the project's activities can't be provided in the same locations. Risk 2: Change of control in the services area (L)Response: A comprehensive security and safety plan will be prepared so that the staff and the patients will be secured and we will coordinate to relocate the services depend on the new needs. Risk 3: The number of patients exceeds the capacity of the centers (M)Response: In this case, we will coordinate with the cluster, local authorities and the partners on the ground to fill these gaps, also we will work on secure an additional fund to expand our services in the targeted locations. Risk 4: Finance: cash transfer to Syria and within Syria is one of the identified risks were a safe money transfer required in the absence of a banking system and considering the Turkish laws of using TL in payments. (L)Using PTT for all money transfer to Syria either for operational costs, or vendors and contractors.
