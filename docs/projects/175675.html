Project Summary

The wave of COVID-19 pandemic is a setback to the already fragile education system; therefore, the project will have a cross cutting component in the whole intervention to ensure all COVID-19 mitigation measures. The project intends to provide a holistic, comprehensive and intersectoral response to the problems of the Education System in EES and Lakes State, starting from the assumption that schools represent platforms through which also Protection, Nutrition, Hygiene, Health and Psychosocial needs of vulnerable children can be addressed. Moreover, AVSI response wants to support the entire education system, starting from nursery and primary school, up to secondary and university program, and including the alternative programs offered by the Accelerating Learning Program (ALP), such as the establishment of Adult Literacy centres. As the main sponsor of St. Mary College, the only institution in the country offering a 3-year diploma program devoted exclusively to the training of primary school teachers, AVSI has contributed to mitigate the downfall in the public and private investment for the training of new teachers and dealt with the challenges of providing in-service teacher training to primary schools. Finally, after 14 years of experience in running a comprehensive and personalized scholarship program for primary and secondary students in South Sudan, AVSI has refined an approach that has proved to reduced dropout, improve cognitive results, and promote female education. Therefore, learners and teachers from all the different steps of education system will be supported in an integrated way, by providing scholastic materials, training and sensitization sessions on subjects such as education, child protection, SGBV, health, psychological well-being including early-entrance-into-school awareness campaigns. In order to avoid the absenteeism of teachers in schools, the project intends to provide an integrated and comprehensive support to the teachers through the provision of food incentives, teacher mentoring in the schools, support to county education departments and payams, re-service trainings for female teachers in order to increase the enrolment of female teachers (only 20%). Furthermore, considered that many of the teachers have not completed S4 it will be important to complete their certificate, while they continue the teaching. Structural interventions will be done in schools to improve the quality and safety of the learning environments, i.e. Temporarily Learning Spaces, renovation of classrooms, construction of latrines and school kitchens, erection or rehabilitation of fences, construction of boarding schools or dormitories, drilling or Maintenance of boreholes to ensure that all the schools are having water point, for safe re-opening after Covid-19 pandemic. Schools will be used as an entry point to promote good hygiene and health practices through hygiene sessions, HIV/AIDS prevention and reproductive health sessions including Covid-19 prevention campaigns. The students and teachers will also be supported in improving their food security status, by receiving training and agricultural inputs for school gardens for their daily consumption. On the basis of a holistic concept of education, the project aims to involve also the other relevant actors in this process, first of all the parents and the communities at large. For these reason, parents, community members, PTAs & SMCs will be involved in Campaigns and meetings to promote the importance of education and discuss about sensitive topics such as education for girls, violence and SGBV, child protection, among other. The objectives of the project are: • To improve the quality of education and to ensure access to safe learning spaces for vulnerable children, adolescents and youth; • To provide access for children, adolescents and youth to integrated social protection services; • To promote resilience and capacity building of teachers, parents and community at large.

1 - Needs

Data from the latest HNO of SSD 2021, show that an estimated 3.4 million (51 per cent girls) school-aged children are expected to have lack of or inadequate access to education services in 2021. This is a slight increase from the 3.1 million girls and boys in need in 2020. The negative impact of COVID-19 and subsequent school closures resulted in an increased number of children in need of education support. As per HNO, children and youth, aged between 3 and 17, represent 44 per cent of South Sudan’s population. Education in the country has been severely impacted by successive years of conflict, economic instability, displacement and flooding. Only 6 out of every 10 school-aged children attend school. Less than 45 per cent of those attending are girls. The current trend in girls’ enrolment in schools in South Sudan is alarming. This increases the risk of girls ending up in an early or forced marriage. The education needs of children of particularly vulnerable groups like displaced people, refugees and returnees are acutely worsening in Eastern Equatoria where a high percentage of children do not have access to an education. Eastern Equatoria state is witnessing the most important influx of returns in the whole Country. As UNHCR planned for a reduction of South Sudanese refugee presence in Uganda of 100,000 in 2021, Covid19 may increase these figures. Flooding in South Sudan in 2020 has significantly impacted education infrastructure, as many flood-affected people took shelter in schools. This may affect the first phase reopening of schools as announced by the MoGEI.Based on AVSI’s experience in more than 60 schools supported in 2019-2020 across SSD, the average enrolment for P1 and P2 classes is 25% less compared to P3 and P5 classes. Likewise, dropout rates are greater for lower classes, especially for P1 pupils. Girls are another vulnerable group of students, both in terms of enrolment and dropout rates. In 33 AVSI’s supported schools the gender gap in terms of enrolment is small in Torit, Magwi and Ikwoto, from 53% in P1 to 59% in P8, remarkable in Kapoeta, and very severe in Cueibet - around 70%. Same in terms of dropout, especially among students of higher classes. These issues are strongly exacerbated by high turnover and lack of teachers underlined by the SSEC’s National Assessment (up to 40%). AVSI’s 2020 multisector survey in Magwi County shows that 43% of its population sent back their children from the school for lack of teachers, hence is one of the main drivers of absenteeism, and possibly dropout.Additionally, AVSI gathers information on the conditions of the schools. By doing so, AVSI found confirmation of the alarming WASH conditions, whereby the baseline conducted in primary schools in Cueibet supported by AVSI showed that the average number of pupils per latrine to be 150, well above the SHPERE standard. The lack of adequate teaching, learning materials and functioning school infrastructures, both in term of quantity and quality continues to be a major challenge. Finally, according to IPC analysis of November 2020, in Eastern Equatoria state an estimated 41.6% of the population faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 109,000 in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). In Lakes state, IPC analysis shows an estimated 55.6% of the population faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 208,000 in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Acute food insecurity has direct consequences in education, as it is a common coping strategy to withdraw children to school to reduce expenditure, have additional work-power and eventually get assets though marriages and dowry system. At the school level, the nutritional status of pupils is closely monitored by AVSI. By gathering anthropometric measures in June 2019 AVSI found that in 4 out of 10 schools of Cueibet more than 50% of children had severe thinness; non-supported schools are likely to be worse.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AVSI Foundation will place protection and gender issues at the centre of the intervention by engaging directly with the IDPs, returnees and host communities’ children and CWDs so that their needs, priorities, rights are addressed within the scope of the project. AVSI Foundation will always create conducive and interactive environments that encourage them to express their needs, rights and priorities. Apart from the active involvement of the rights holders in the project, AVSI Foundation will ensure that the duty bearers (government, PTAs/SMCS, institutions and non-state actors) play an effective role to promote the rights of the CWDs and girls. Much emphasis will be placed on these rights during the PTAs/SMCs trainings and the awareness-raising campaigns.The project is committed to gender equality (G2a marker). Activities will be developed with the active participation of women teachers and parents in all stages of the implementation process, so as to ensure that girls’ priority needs are taken into consideration and that WaSH infrastructures meet the right gender standards (IASC, 2015, Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action: Reducing risk, promoting resilience and aiding recovery). Key gender-mainstreamed activities:- Training will be conducted by both males and females facilitators;- Provision of dignity kits to allow regular attendance to school;- Training classes will be mixed and the participants will work together;- Training will cover menstrual hygiene and girls education awareness campaigns;- Hygiene clubs members will be both males and females;- Two boarding schools for girls will be supported in this project- GBV awareness campaigns- Latrines will be sex-differentiated- conduct safety audits to address major protection concerns, particularly regarding GBV

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concerning protection, AVSI Foundation will work collaboratively with other actors (UN agencies, NGOS, FBOs and CBOs) involved in child protection issues to safeguard the delivery of protection services in the schools and communities. It will work directly with the project school teachers and the PTAs to promote zero-tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse in the schools including all forms of discrimination against girls and CWDs. AVSI will be accountable to the beneficiaries by enabling them to participate in the project at the planning, implementation and evaluation phases. Finally, appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints will be set up.All AVSI South Sudan staff members have been trained on child protection, disability and gender mainstreaming, has own policies in place on PSEAH, Child Protection, Child Safeguarding, Code of Conducts that are explained and countersigned by each staff and key supplier and have capacity to share the importance of this policies with any stakeholder they interact with.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The capillary and continuous presence that AVSI has in the communities targeted by the intervention (AVSI has more than 80% of its staff, including senior positions outside Juba) , and the extent use of staff recruited locally among people recognized by the communities, ensures that such fruitful cooperation with project beneficiaries continues throughout the implementation of the project.All project’s beneficiaries (female and male IDPs and returnees children, CWDs and host communities’ children) will be considered as capable and active resources rather than as passive recipients who do not have a say in the project.To include the perspectives of the very pupils and teachers, moreover, AVSI conducts in the schools that supports surveys that provide a reading of people and students wellbeing, expectations, and opinion about the schools. For this AVSI has prepared specific tools adapted to the context and of proven efficacity. By running this survey, AVSI found that in the schools of Magwi, where more pronounced had been the displacement, nearly 40% of students reported to have lived in refugee camps over the course of the previous two years. In the first year of this proposed project, AVSI intends to conduct an equivalent Pupil-Teacher Survey in all the schools served by the project, to share and validate the results with the head teachers and to promote a reflection of its finding in the context of the peer-to-peer activities of knowledge sharing among teachers. Thanks to a very localized approach with AVSI staff regularly present and usually coming from the same community of project locations, accountability is pursued also directly through its staff that can act as bridge between top management and beneficiaries. Furthermore, considering the purposely field-oriented management team (officers and also project managers are field based), beneficiaries and affected population at large as constant possibility to interact and file complain to staff at different level. Furthermore, AVSI periodically shares finding of monitoring and evaluation activities with SMoE, PTAs and communities at large, ensuring that project decisions are justified, shared and finally owned by the affected population.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

AVSI has in place a tested system of yearly multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 400 questions (including question on GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a large number of interviews (more than 400 HHs) capable of providing detailed reference at the subcounty (payam) level, an analysis centred on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge is interiorized by AVSI staff and becomes the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within the community. In the last 12 months AVSI has produced 4 of such surveys which, along with other researches aimed at monitoring the results of AVSI’s projects, are accessible on the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response’s website. The questionnaire used for the Multi-Sector Survey can be accessed at this webpage https://ee.humanitarianresponse.info/x/#Yztl. One example of one of AVSI’s feedback mechanism happened in the Lake States where AVSI staff validated the finding of two researches on challenges faced by Persons with Disabilities (PwD) and on Gender Based Violence. The 3-day validation meeting was attended by key stakeholders including PwD. AVSI ensured that at least half of the 50 attendees were females in positions of authority at the institutional or community level
