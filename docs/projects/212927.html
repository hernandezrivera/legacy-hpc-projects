Project Summary

- Health:
As per the HNO 2024 report declares over 14.9 million people in Syria are estimated to be in need of life-saving primary and secondary health care assistance in 2024. Public health concerns loom large, the possibility of disease outbreaks, fueled by water contamination, poor sanitation, displacement and the resurgence of vaccine-preventable and endemic diseases is expected to be another main driver of needs, Particularly in Idleb Governorate, where its health sector severity classification is classified as 69% extreme. In addition, the earthquakes worsened the already fragile health care system in it as it did not only damaged health facilities, but also fragile medicine equipment and devices such as prosthetics, which are in particularly short supply.
This project addresses the immediate and crucial need to support healthcare institutions supporting affected population. Additionally, the project addresses the high number of amputations and disabilities resulting from both the earthquake and the ongoing conflict in NWS.
Improving access to life-saving primary health care services by operating 3 primary health care centers in NWS to continue providing health services to the local population, in addition to three mobile clinics and a center for installing prosthetic limbs and supplying the central warehouses with medical supplies.
ATAA will implement this project in coordination with SAMS which is operating 2 centers that ATAA will provide with supplies and consumables during the project.
1. Health needs assessment: This activity includes a detailed needs assessment targeting the centers will be supported before the provision of supplies and consumables in addition to health related assessment in coordination with health cluster in the mobile clinics locations to update the intervention accordingly.
2. Supply of medicines: the targeted PHCs will be provided with medical supplies and consumables according to the updated needs, in addition to the coordination with SAMS to provide the 2 centers SAMS operating with the same items (medicine, laboratory consumables, general consumables).
3. Storage and management: ATAA will enhance and improve the medical warehouse in Sarmada to allow storing the medical supplies based on WHO standards.

- Nutrition intervention
In Northwest Syria, where nutrition intervention is most urgently needed, malnutrition cases have surged, particularly after a devastating earthquake with profound effects on communities, especially pregnant/breastfeeding women and newborns. Statistics from HNO 2023 reveal alarming needs in Atareb and Dana:
Atareb Sub-district: The Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rate has doubled since the previous year. One in four children under 5 are stunted, facing irreversible damage. Dana Sub-district: SAM cases among children aged 6-59 months increased by 48% in 2023. Approximately 5% wasting prevalence was recorded, with some areas at 12%. Overall North-western Syria: 56% of pregnant and breastfeeding women are anemic. Millions of children are grappling with physical and cognitive impairments due to malnutrition. The project will focus on integrated and comprehensive nutrition intervention to reduce the effects of malnutrition and enhance positive coping mechanisms and support the food security and nutrition of the affected families starting from raising the awareness of targeted communities focusing on pregnant and breastfeeding women which have higher and direct impact on their children along with conducting the screening of malnutrition and the provision of Blanket Supplementary Feeding BSFP and the direct nutrition support of pregnant and breastfeeding women through strong referrals pathways.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Activities: 1. Blanket Supplementary Feeding (BSFP): Targeted boys and girls aged 6-23 months receive specialized nutritious foods to prevent acute and chronic malnutrition and micro-nutrient deficiencies. Target: all children aged 6-23 months. Total: 4140 (2318 M,1882 F) Modality: in-kind. Duration: from 6 months, until the child is 24 months of age 2. Nutrition Support for Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women: Nutrition Support for Pregnant and Breastfeeding women and girls (NSP): This approach contributes to the prevention of acute malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies among PLW, and children aged 6-23 months. The use of CVA under this approach is expected to improve access and consumption of locally available nutritious foods for PLW within a household that is receiving a General Food Assistance (identified according to FSC eligibility criteria) and Specialized Nutritious Food 11 (SNF) for children under two. Together with the SBC component, the individual nutrition top-up is provided in conjunction with the household food assistance.SBC will be implemented one time before, and one time after the provision of CVA to create awareness on locally available foods that arenutrient dense.Value: Each family will receive “65$” monthly for 4 months.Due to the unpredictable nature of conflicts, such as shelling or armed clashes, the S&S department closely monitors the project area, conducts daily risk assessments, and communicates potential scenarios and appropriate responses to the crisis cell. In addition, the department updates evacuation and contingency plans and provides field staff with training on expected scenarios and adherence to approved SOPs for a secure operational environment. Protection Risk Analysis:Risks include compromising dignity, lacking accountability, limited meaningful access, and inadequate feedback mechanisms. Assumptions might entail overlooking cultural sensitivities or underestimating gender-related vulnerabilities. Mitigation measures involve promoting dignity through respectful practices, ensuring accountability through transparent communication, enhancing meaningful access by addressing barriers, and establishing robust feedback channels. Additionally, recognizing and addressing gender-related risks is essential for equitable outcomes, necessitating tailored interventions and inclusive approaches. By integrating these elements, interventions can effectively navigate challenges, uphold ethical standards, and foster inclusive, responsive nutrition support in Northwest Syria.ATAA created a solid PSEA prevention and response system starts from the active and accessible reporting channels, and investigation planning and procedures with qualified and trained staff and network of the focal points with gender balance to enhance access to survivors and provide the response interventions based on survivor based approach to enhance PSEAH within the all planned interventions and with zero tolerance approach in place.
