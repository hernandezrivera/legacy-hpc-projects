Project Summary

The aim of this project will work toward responding and reducing Sexual and Gender-based violence in Rohingya refugee camps and host community. This will be accomplished through extensive awareness-raising activities involving men, women, girls, and boys from the community. In addition, as per response, this project will provide appropriate services to SGVB survivors such as case management, PSS, legal assistance and safe space. Referral linkages are made with relevant health services .01.01.

Objectives

Improved access to quality survivor centered services responding to individual needs, prevention and mitigating of GBV risks, and empowerment of women, girls and survivors of GBV

Needs

1)GBV affects women and girls disproportionately, in both Rohingya and host communities. Harmful traditional practices such as child marriage, polygamy are highly prevalent in both communities.2)There is insufficient access to GBV services, due to stigma but also due to a lack of information on services. 3)Men are power holders in key decision making at the household level in both communities, but more so in the Rohingya community4)There is a lack of formal female leadership in the Rohingya community. 5)Refugees reported an increase in child marriage following displacement and involving children as young as 13. The majority of respondents reported 15-18 as the average age of marriage for girls. Inadequate services for adolescents between 15 to 24 years of age remains a major concern. Only 3,349 adolescents have access to education or life skill training - out of the 117,700 in need. ( Situation Report Rohingya Refugee Crisis, 1st November 2018) 6)Have to Engage men and boys positively in addressing GBV, particularly domestic violence, sexual harassment targeting women and girls, and polygamy. 7) Despite the national legislation protecting women and girls from domestic violence and other forms of GBV, as well as the existence of One-stop Crisis Centers for hospital-based GBV services, survivors face severe challenges in accessing the justice and health system. Lack of knowledge of GBV among judges, police, and health workers presents a barrier for addressing impunity of perpetrators.

Activities or outputs

Following activities will be provided for both Rohingya refugees and host community 1) Safe Space for Women and Girls in which they will engage in recreational activities and case management support, PPS, legal assistance and referral assistance when needed 2 ) Case Management Services to SGBV survivors. 3) Male designated space/ center for them to receive life, leadership and occupational skills training. In addition, they will be sensitized in SGBV and be included in the mitigation efforts made to address SGBV.4) Individual/Group psychosocial support to adolescents boys and girls, men and women . 5) Awareness of GBV issues and services where survivors can receive assistance 6) Women's engagement and empowerment program initiatives 7) Skill development and Life Skills programming for adolescent/ youth girls and women 8) Community-based GBV risk mitigation services (strengthen community-based mechanism to address SGBV) 10) Distribute Dignity Kits/Commodities for women and girls 11) Capacity building of staff and volunteers in regards to SGBV, PSEA, DRR

Key project/performance indicators

Number of persons benefitted from awareness activities on GBV, social cohesion, peacebuilding, DRR, PSEA and GBV prevention and info on GBV response mechanism and available services Number of individuals benefitting from structured PSS and Case Management services that meet minimum standards Number of women and girls benefiting from structured skills development and livelihood programming with a private sector partnership (Aarong and others) Number of service providers and other humanitarian actors trained on GBV (GBV minimum standards, GBV mainstreaming, case management, GBVIMS, CMR, GBV IASC Guidelines, etc) Number of individuals successfully supported by enhanced Safe Spaces for women and girls and referral services
