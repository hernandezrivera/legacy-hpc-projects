Project Summary

In 2021, the Education Cluster conducted a Needs Assessment to provide an analysis of the functionality of schools in light of the impact of the 7 year conflict and COVID 19 pandemic. This Education Needs Assessment found out that majority of schools in South Sudan are 1-2 hours walking distance from the nearest County capital. This presents a problem in terms of children accessing schools and thus many children not attending regularly. 40.9% of schools were reported not accessible to children with disabilities (CwDs, 50.4% schools reported that their female learners did not receive any dignity kits and there was lack of functioning referral mechanisms in schools. Although enrollment has increased, attendance remains poor. Head teachers reported that only 57.4% of children attend school almost every day. The main reason reported for nonattendance was families not being able to pay fees. And the main reason for drop outs in female learners is early marriages, while for boys it’s finding employment opportunities. Another main reason for dropping out of schools included domestic duties and long distances to school. For teachers, failure to pay salaries was the number one reason for absentees.
The project shall focus on the three specific objectives;
1. Objective 1: Improve equitable access to safe education.
2. Objective 2: Improve the quality of inclusive education services.
3. Objective 3: Strengthen/enhance the education system.
Therefore, this proposal seeks to address the challenges facing the Education sector in Raja County, Western Bahr el Gazal. The project will focus on HRP Objective 1, 2 and 3.
The Project will target 2,625 children in Need (CiN) in Raja County (1,185 female and 1,440 male). Out of 2,625 children targeted, 450 (225 female and 225 male) will be children with disabilities (CwDs). It will also target 105 adults (teachers and PTAs), 45 female and 60 male) including. The total number of targeted beneficiaries will be 2730.
The proposed project will be relevant in the following ways:
By constructing of school infrastructure more classrooms will be available and schools will be able to enroll more children to learn in a protective and safe environment.
Community mobilization and Back to Learning campaigns will help change the perception of parents about the importance of education, particularly for girls and result in increased enrollment of children in schools.
Training teachers, PTA/SMCs and government officials will build the capacity of teachers and school officials with the knowledge and skills needed for effective teaching and better school management.
Provision of school materials will relieve parents of the financial burden to buy school supplies and encourage children to go to school and participate in the learning process.
The above shall be achieved through implementation of the following activities;
1. Enroll children in Pre-primary and Primary schools/learning centers
2. Construction of Temporally Learning Spaces accessible to CwDs
3. Construction of Gender Segregated Latrines with Hand Washing Facilities also accessible to CwDs
4. Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers
5. Provide emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials
6. Provide teaching and learning materials for ECD and primary school.
7. Train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children
8. Train and update teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition
9. Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change
10. Support conducting back to learning campaigns.

1 - Needs

This project seeks to address challenges to school functionality in Raja County.The needs this project aims to address include; physical barriers including long distance to school, lack of teaching and learning materials, inadequate learning spaces and untrained teachers, low capacity of the SMCs/PTA. Children are not attending school regularly because of long distances; teachers lack morale and are frequently absent due to low and delayed salaries. Most of the teachers are untrained, leading to poor delivery and low quality of education. In addition, teachers are not trained on how to teach or support Children with Disabilities (CwDs). Children with Disabilities face challenges during the learning process because schools cannot provide learners with adequate conditions to move and study with convenience, for example, they lack ramps, doors are too heavy to open and wash rooms are inaccessible. Schools also lack assistive or mobility devices to support the learning process for CwDs The Covid-19 pandemic also exacerbated educational inequalities leading to learning loss due to national lock down. This had negative impact particularly on girls who became further engaged in domestic responsibilities and faced greater risks to early marriages.

2 - Project activities

In order to overcome the challenges facing the communities and children in particular, the following activities will be implemented; 1. Construction of Temporally Learning Spaces: these shall be constructed in areas without schools or with schools under trees. Each TLS will have two classrooms. 2 TLSs with 4 classrooms shall be constructed at 4 school sites thus having a total of 8 TLSs construceted. During construction, the guidelines given by MoGEI on the standard size of a classroom shall be strictly considered. Each classroom shall have a ramp to facilitate accessibility by CwDs. 2. Construction of Gender Segregated Latrines: For every newly constructed TLS,1 gender segregated latrines shall be constructed. This is aimed at improving on Sanitation and hygiene of the new school. At each school, female learners shall have 3 stanza latrines, while the male learners shall have 1 stanza latrines. These Latrines shall also have ramps to facilitate accessibility by CwDs. 3. Enroll children in Pre-primary and Primary schools/learning centers. This shall involve mobilization of parents, learners, SMCs, PTAs through drives, door to door campaigns, radio announcements and holding of drives to attract and encourage parents to send their children to school.4. Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers; 5. Provide emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials, including sports and games equipment, sports jerseys, musical instruments (balls, javelin, team uniforms, and goal post), for both male and female learners, including children with disabilities. 6. Provide teaching and learning materials for ECD and primary school. These shall include classroom consumables such as; chalk, chalkboard mathematical sets, rulers, mathematical sets, pens, pencils, shading colors, and textbooks. 7. Train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children8. Train and update teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition9. Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change10. Support conducting back to learning campaigns.

3 - Project indicators

The success of the project shall be measure using the following indicators; 1. # of temporary learning spaces established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2. # of school children who got access to gender sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards. (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls) 3. # of boys, girls and adolescents (3-17) provided with access to quality education services 4. # of learners who received sanitation and hygiene materials 5. # of children and youth (3-17 years) who benefited from recreational material supplies. 6. # of teachers’ trained on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies. 7. # of teachers trained and updated on PSS, CP and referral mechanisms 8. # of community education committee members and local education officials trained in school management, Psycho-social Support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR) 9. # of children, teachers and PTA members provided with appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials and information on prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases. 10. # of school campaigns organized on back to school/learning.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project shall adopt beneficiary-centered mechanisms to ensure that no group of people is left out. Each category of beneficiary shall have a representative/focal person, who shall be responsible for collecting information and submitting it to Organization Focal persons. Use of hotlines, Suggestion boxes placed at accessible points, encouraging use of local language for individuals who are not literate. Women, girls and individuals with disabilities shall be represented and always given extra attention. EFO Focal persons will make quarterly visits to communities so as to receive verbal feedback from beneficiaries, particularly the illiterates.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will engage beneficiaries in information sharing when assessing needs, designing activities and reviewing and changing projects. We shall have regular consultation with beneficiaries to provide them updates on progress and challenges. The project management will carry out field visits, during which we will have quarterly meetings with beneficiaries' focal persons with focus groups according to gender and age. The beneficiaries shall be guided on selecting Gender Focal Persons, Disability Focal Persons and Youth Focal Person. These Focal Persons shall be trained on Feedback, Complaints Mechanism and Information Sharing. Use of Posters, banners, brochures. Use of Radio talk shows, Door to door Public Address System, shall be used in information sharing. A hotline (telephone number) will be provided to the beneficiaries and community Focal Persons for both providing feedback and complaints. There will be a Suggestion boxes which shall be anonymous to cater for confidentiality and shall be used by beneficiaries to report cases, challenges and share information.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Providing information on PSEA will involve training of beneficiaries Focal Persons with consideration of Gender and age, including persons with disabilities. EFO will conduct workshops on PSEA with SMCs, PTA members, school head teachers, teachers, community leaders, children, including persons with disabilities. Banners, posters, brochures and other reading materials shall be printed in both English language and local languages (easily understood by the community) and distributed to the beneficiaries according to Gender and age groups.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries gives firsthand information on challenges and needs of the community. This therefore shall be very useful in helping EFO adapt project assistance. EFO shall therefore listen, assess, analyze document and record all beneficiaries’ feedback. Strategies will be put in place, aimed at countering challenges or complaints from beneficiaries. Prioritization will be given people's areas of need and giving much attention to individual groups in relation to age, gender and disability. These different groups of beneficiaries shall also be involved in designing strategies, or even consulted on better methods/means of service delivery.
