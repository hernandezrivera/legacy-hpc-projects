1 - Needs

Libya continues to suffer from interlinked political, conflict and insecurity, and economic crises that are driving conflict, damaging its economy, weakening state institutions, and facilitating criminal gangs and non-state armed actors. The situation continues to cause great hardships to Libyans and non-Libyans, contributing to ongoing internal displacement of hundreds of thousands of civilians from their homes. Efforts undertaken by the international community were undermined by the military offensive of April 2019 by the self-proclaimed Libyan National Army (LNA) against the Government of National Accord (GNA) in an attempt to take control of the capital city of Tripoli. These clashes caused the displacement of over 120,000 persons and prevented tens of thousands of displaced persons to safely return their homes. Despite a reduction in hostilities following the June 2020 ceasefire, Libya continues to suffer from the impact of a protracted political crisis, economic decline and risk of hostilities. The humanitarian situation in Libya remains fragile as the absence of governance and rule of law contributes to widespread insecurity and criminality with an increase in abductions, targeted killings, robberies and trafficking. Within this environment of extended insecurity, the population is vulnerable to protection threats, especially IDPs and returnees increasingly resorting to negative coping strategies to meet basic needs. According to the HNO, the compounded political, security and economic factors have severely affected the abilities of families to withstand the impacts of the protracted crisis and ensure their basic welfare. With absent or broken social networks and institutions, many conflict-affected groups lack the capacity for self-protection and self-reliance. Each IDP group and specific vulnerable groups amongst these have their separate challenges and needs that will need to be addressed, with particular impacts on children, women and girls, older persons, and those with disabilities. Armed conflict and insecurity as well as individual human rights violations are the main causes of internal displacement in Libya with the deteriorating economic situation and lack of basic services aggravating factors. Protection monitoring and the MSNA identify protection concerns that include forced and exploitative labour, the presence of explosive hazards, discrimination against women and girls and displaced communities, as well as HLP issues, evictions targeting IDPs and returnees, and a need for legal advice and assistance and lack of civil documentation. The trauma of conflict, and hopelessness created by protracted displacement are psychological stressors. IDPs in Libya are at heightened risk of GBV due to breakdown of family and community structures, lack of access to services, economic hardship, and sectarian tensions. Many families, and particularly women and children with family members with perceived affiliation to former regime, continue to face discrimination and mistreatment, making them more vulnerable to negative coping strategies and exposing them to further GBV risks. UNHCR has identified that that the need for psychosocial support for conflict-affected groups is high. The Covid-19 pandemic has added a further complication by restricting the freedom of movement of IDPs because of the public health measures (such as curfews) to prevent the spread of the disease.

2 - Activities

UNHCR will maintain ongoing monitoring of IDP communities and protection context, and will timely respond to new displacement while contributing to a more favorable protection environment, in coordination with local authorities and in partnership with the sectors and other humanitarian and development actors. The office will maintain preparedness and emergency response capacity to contribute to inter-agency efforts in responding to new displacement; improve analysis of the IDP situation through protection and return monitoring, better target assistance and support evidence-based advocacy; enhance assistance and support to internally displaced population with specific needs or at heightened risk, through partners’ interventions or through referrals; and strengthen UNHCR leadership in sector coordination and participation in the inter-agency response emphasizing centrality of protection. UNHCR will expand protection monitoring and assessments to identify needs and inform future programming, implement specialized protection services and referral pathways to facilitate access, strengthening capacity-building initiatives for duty-bearers, national humanitarian actors and community members to identify/mitigate protection risks and reinforce community-based approaches.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.4. At-risk: Refugees and asylum-seekers in group living arrangements and shared spaces; Persons without sufficient resources to implement isolation and other preventive measures; detained persons; undocumented individuals with fear of seeking treatment or information. Mitigation: Safeguards and preventive measures to incorporated at all service locations, including distribution of masks, social distancing, disinfectant, education materials. Services shifted to telephone when possible. High-volume foot traffic reduced to focus on critical and urgent needs. Contribution to interagency COVID-19 efforts by UNHCR and partners. 4.8. Risk Groups: IDPs affiliated with particular factions in the past decade of conflict face heightened barriers to access durable solutions due to percieved affiliation. Persons of Tawergha background face obstacles to return due to failure to resolve persistent political problems concerning return in dignified, principled conditions. Restrictions on access to financial support for certain IDP groups reduce opportunities for sustainable solutions. Mitigation: UNHCR engages in advocacy at local and central levels on principle-based rights of displaced persons, and need for proactive resolution of identified barriers. Ensuring the UNHCR monitoring and services/assistance are impartially applied without regard to political or ethnic identities of displaced communities. 4.22. Persons with limited access to information and humanitarian actors less likely to obtain referral to appropriate services, including in particular women and children. Knowledge level and awareness of services is low. Mitigation: Increase outreach and familiarization sessions in project implementation, eg. on legal and HLP issues, to improve general awareness of rights and services available to the community. Ensure multiple available channels for information are available, including brochures, social media, CFM, hotlines, allowing different profiles more likelihood of access to information without requiring presence at service delivery locations. Increased participation in coordinated referral pathways.
