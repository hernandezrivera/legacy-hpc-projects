Project Summary

Re-establishing Primary Care in Hamesh Koreib: The primary healthcare system in HK is essentially non-existent. In an effort to meet basic health needs, SP will work to re-establish outpatient consultations for communicable diseases such as malaria, acute respiratory infections, and diarrhea through the provision of incentives to support staff provided by the MoH. Health workers to support HK exist in Kassala Town, however, because of the rural nature of HK and low wages, the health workers are not currently providing HK with the needed support. Samaritan’s Purse will provide incentives to health workers assigned by the MoH to work in HK . In addition to assistance with personnel, SP will also work to equip the local health facility buildings and infrastructure as necessary , ensuring that the structures are easily accessible to HK and essential medical equipment is inside. An unused government health facility is located next to HK, that is currently not in use. The SP office in Kassala will work with the MOH and health administrators to gain use of this facility in addition to SP’s previously constructed facility provided there is no additional cost incurred by SP. To promote the success of the above primary care interventions, SP is also committed to procuring basic medical supplies and pharmaceuticals for the healthcare facilities to a degree that will allow them to properly care for the local community.
Maternal, Newborn, and Child Health (MNCH): In an effort to focus interventions on the particularly vulnerable members of HK, in alignment with the needs expressed at the local level, SP will implement activities with a focus on MNCH. Samaritan’s Purse will support safe deliveries by supplying incentives for critical staffing with a certified midwife and medical officer as well as supplies at the existing HK birthing center. The primary care clinic team will work with the MoH EPI program in order to re-establish cold chain and static vaccination services in HK through the addition of a solar power vaccine refrigerator. The vaccines themselves are provided through UNICEF and the government. Basic supplies will be procured for the delivery center in the locality in coordination with state MoH. These efforts will bolster state efforts to improve MNCH services in the locality and will supplement the midwifery training provided through BHA funding. A government run ambulance is currently available at a fee. To help bolster its use and ensure successful transport to emergency care, SP will cover half of the payment necessary for its use.
TelKook Health Facility Infrastructure Support: To assist the health facility in TK with ongoing care and sufficient infrastructure, SP will reconstruct the roof on the mens ward. Currently, the facility is unable to use this ward due to the loss of the roof during recent rain and strong winds. Reconstruction of the roof will allow for the reopening of this ward and for full use of the established health center. In addition, SP will provide batteries and necessary repairs to the existing solar system, allowing for the return of electricity to the facility and supporting the functioning of supportive diagnostic and medical equipment that require electrical power to run.
Stabilization Center: Due to the high rates of SAM in HK, coupled with the inaccessibility of any kind of inpatient care for children with SAM complications, Samaritan’s Purse will incentivize staff provided by the MoH to run a Stabilization Center for children 0-59 months in HK. Referrals will come from local Community Nutrition Volunteers and Care Group Leader Mothers trained in MUAC and bilateral pitting edema screening through USAID-BHA funding. Children screened in the community or referred by their caregivers or from the existing OTP or TSFP who have medical complications will be treated according to the Sudan CMAM national protocols. The Stabilization Center facility will be staffed 24 hours a day.

1 - Needs

Approximately 13.4 million people in Sudan require humanitarian support in 2021 due to the ongoing effects of COVID-19, flooding, a fragile economy, high inflation, and a large refugee population . Disease outbreaks, malnutrition, and climatic shocks continue to affect the lives and livelihoods of many Sudanese. In October 2020, SP conducted an in-depth needs assessment within HK, TK, and other localities in Kassala State. Findings revealed that emergency/critical needs among target populations within rural Kassala are present in the Health, Nutrition, and Livelihoods sectors, among others . The assessment’s key findings show a severe lack of medical and health services, posing a great risk to rural communities. Delivery of and access to health services by the most vulnerable were negatively affected by the economic and political crisis in Sudan, as well as flooding during the rainy season. Needs continue to increase as health services have been stretched to their breaking point due to the lack of qualified personnel, a shortage of essential medicines and supplies, and inadequate infrastructure due to government austerity measures. Increasing cost and loss of raw materials for in country pharma production of medicines and medical supplies has restricted access to essential life-saving health care services. Top common morbidities reported are malaria, upper respiratory tract infections and diarrhea. No clear services are available for mothers and women of childbearing age. Health facilities required upgraded equipment as well. Mobile immunization campaigns within HK are reported as functioning, but a static vaccination program or Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI) is not currently operating or available. In HK, a catchment area containing approximately 300,000 people, the free of charge health services traditionally provided through a designated MOH facility are completely nonfunctioning. The health facility in HK lacks nurses and doctors. The closest referral hospital is in Kassala Town, a 3 hour drive away . The health facility in TK is currently functioning, but recently lost the roof covering the men’s ward, rendering the building useless and leaving it exposed to the elements. It is also in need of new batteries and repairs for the solar powered electrical system in order to run essential medical equipment.Identified in the SP needs assessment, the necessity for increased food and malnutrition support are also apparent because of increased prices of food coupled with suboptimal feeding practices and gaps in health services. Key informant interviews (KII) and focus group discussions (FGD) conducted in the assessment revealed that families are buying cheaper staple foods and decreasing intake of vegetables, fruit, and protein-rich foods because of rising costs. A majority of children who participated in the FGDs had only eaten biscuits and milk the previous day. Additionally, there is an influence from grandmothers discouraging exclusive breastfeeding and encouraging practice such as giving babies sugar water from 0-5 months and feeding meat to children only when they were older. Children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with complications are not receiving the life-saving nutrition or stabilization services they require.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Within the Health Sector, women, girls, men and boys all face unique challenges and obstacles in regards to their protection needs. In designing this activity, SP has been diligent in assessing the potential needs that may arise from a protection perspective and has done due diligence to mitigate the potential risks. For a summary of the protection needs present in Sudan and considered throughout the design of this activity, please refer to the final section of Annex D1. Needs Assessment and Analysis Report. Health care workers trained will be exposed to information that will increase knowledge and skills relevant to working with populations with unique needs (e.g. women, adolescents, children, persons with disabilities, and older people)Health Facilities that SP will assist will be accessible to women, children, the elderly, and those with disabilities to ensure that the most vulnerable community members of HK and TK are participating in the interventions and are receiving equal benefits of initiativesWhen designing the proposed activities as a whole and throughout each sector, SP met with and received feedback from members of the target communities in FGDs involving community leaders, and heard from women, girls, men and boys, as well as the elderlySP takes the abuse and exploitation of staff and activity participants very seriously. 7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Incorporated into the culture of SP is the zero tolerance policy towards SEA. A point person at the national level will work alongside the PMs and the team to ensure that safeguarding principles are embedded into all aspects of programming. All staff and representatives are provided with training on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), an understanding of the SP Code of Conduct, as well as the reporting mechanisms in place to report a concern of PSEA. In all SP field offices and intervention sites, awareness materials will be displayed to convey PSEA messages. All materials will be adapted to the local language and context. Project staff will inform and sensitize the local community/target population on awareness of PSEA and the requirement that SP staff adhere to a code of conduct, as well as ensure that the target population have knowledge of how to report an SEA concern or situation in a confidential manner.
