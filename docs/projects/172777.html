Project Summary

CFGB and ERDO is supporting PAOZ with a seven-month food assistance project in Masvingo province (Gutu and Zaka Districts), Zimbabwe. This project is providing a monthly food basket of full rations to 910 of the most vulnerable households (approximately 4,550 individuals) with an average household of 5 persons for Jan-April 2021. Each basket contains a full basket of 10kg maize kernels, 2kg beans and 1litre fortified cooking oil per person.
PAOZ intends to help communities in Zaka and Gutu who were/are severely affected by the COVID 19 pandemic recover through localy initiated developmental activities. These include food assistance (half basket) for 1,110 HHs (Oct-Dec 2021) as well as agriculture activities for a total of 600 HHs (out of the 1,110 HHs receiving food assistance). The agriculture activities include: Agricultural Activities (200 households), Kitchen Vegetable Gardens (200 households), Small animal husbandry (200 households), Scaling up Village Savings Loans (VSL) (300 households). Stated activities are sustainable due to the fact that before drought and the pandemic, the communities relied on similar livelihoods, which qualifies their skills. Trainings will be conducted to equip them beyond just subsistence but also small scale commercial. Food Assistance will also be implemented on the Humanitarian activities to cater for people with disabilitties and those who cannot take part in agricultural activities, as well as those part of the developmental activities.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The project team is in coordination with the District Development Coordinator (DDC) in Gutu and Zaka. According to the DDC Zaka, 76% of the population is food insecure and the Drought Relief Committee which includes departments from the DDC, police, Social Welfare, EMA and other stakeholders are working together to make sure everyone in entative from the DDC will be present at all distributions to ensure transparency and accountability of the process to the community and district/community leadership. Men and women involved in Community Development Committees and also in the existing Gender Advisory Committees have been voted on by the communities, as each committee member represents their peers. There are an equal number of men and women on each committee. These community committees are integral to decision making within all aspects of the project, these are local peple who account back to the affected people. However, the baseline surveys (needs assessment) done prior project commencement give the community an opportunity to state their priorities and preferances. The collected qualitative and quantitative data is then analyzed using excel and SPSS to determine quality then integrated in the project design. At every distribution points there will be suggestion boxes where beneficiaries can put through their complaints which will be handled by the Advisory committee they voted for, change requests are submitted and changes are updated to the project documents.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The beneficiaries are trained and encouraged to observe cleanliness all the times, during and after distributions, so that they do not litter the environment. Furthermore, they were advised to harvest dead woods and burn cooking oil bottles after use.
