Project Summary

The Syrian crisis has weakened intensely the capacity of the education system to address basic education needs. According to the Ministry of Education (MoE) over 120,000 teachers required capacity development, an estimated 180,000 educational personnel have left the education system and around 5,722 schools are either damaged or destroyed which has caused an acute need for additional safe learning spaces across the country affecting 2.45 million out of school. The continues spread of COVID-19 has widened the pre-existing education needs and created new gaps. School attendance was interrupted in Syria during 2019/2020 and 2020/2021 academic year, leading to children drop out and a setback in learning.
The project aims to enhance equitably and quality access to education to crisis affected IDPs, returnees and vulnerable children aged between 5-17 years. UNHCR education intervention includes the rehabilitation of damaged schools in a gender and disability sensitive modes and the provision of non-formal education in community centres. School rehabilitation will focus on areas hosting large number of returnees, as well as priority locations identified by Education Sector. The rehabilitation activities will target moderately damaged classrooms and WASH facilities with gender sensitive and disability friendly approach to safeguard the protection of boys and girls and ensure adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities. UNHCR pays specific attention to the future adolescents and youth populations that can contribute to resilience and social cohesion in their communities through facilitating their access to education. In line to AGD, AAP and rights-based approach, UNHCR will reach out to children and their parents during project monitoring activities and through focus group discussion seeking feedback including receipt of complaints regarding the project. Resources permit, UNHCR plans to rehabilitate 32 schools, and some 89,000 students will benefit from non-formal education activities in addition to the provision of capacity building training to 3,000 MoE personnel.

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria has hindered children from accessing and enjoying their full rights to education with an estimated 2.45 million children, or one in three, were already out of school. According to MoE over 120,000 teachers required capacity development, an estimated 180,000 educational personnel have left the education system and around 5,722 schools are either damaged or destroyed which has caused an acute need for additional safe learning spaces across the country. The COVID-19 outbreak has also had major implications on the education of children with the interruption of schools attendance in 2020/2021 academic year. UNHCR operates under the assumption that children with access to education are less exposed to harmful negative coping mechanisms such as child labour or other forms of exploitation, including recruitment by armed groups. The routine and milestones of going through the process of education offers children stability and a sense of normalcy. In order for Syrian society to be able to redefine itself post crisis, its population needs to go through a process of reintegration. UNHCR believes that the socialization effects provided by the interaction with other children to be an essential component of this process. Above all, access to education give children and their parents’ perspective for the future as it has direct impact in the capacity of families to generate income and plan for the future.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNHCR's interventions in education will be line with the “No Lost Generation ”initiative launched in 2013. With over 2.45 million children and youth out of school even before the pandemic, concentrated and joint efforts are essential to mitigate additional dropouts and ensure out-of-school children are back at school. UNHCR will participate in the Education Sector Working Group led by UNICEF and the Ministry of Education to coordinate education interventions and will respond to the identified needs with due consideration of security and resource limitations. The following education interventions planned for 2022-2023 build on the priorities and the identified needs of the education sector in addition to the good practices from previous years: UNHCR education interventions will include: non-formal education programmes such as remedial classes and catch up classes, education activities during summer, UNHCR education interventions will adhere to the Ministry of Education and World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines related to COVID-19. The implementation of all activities will ensure safety measures are in place to protect the students and teachers from COVID-19 including sterilization of buildings, distribution of personal and protective equipment in community centers and awareness-raising for students and teachers on the pandemic. Additionally, school rehabilitation and teacher training programmes conducted will benefit returnees, IDPs, and host communities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Inaccessibility to education due to lack of safety and insecurity including risks of explosives and mines. Mitigating measures: Ensure flexible and context informed mechanisms for school rehabilitation project to adjust to a changing context. Ensure that relevant authorities are aware of and are engaged with the projects and are committed to ensure basic safety/ access for project implementation and ensure that staff members, teachers and education personnel are trained in safety and security measures including in mine risk education. Risk 2: Insufficient safety and health standards at schools Mitigating measures: Close coordination with local education authorities, community leaders and school management resulting in clear agreements on every actors' responsibility to create and maintain a safe learning environment. Regular monitoring to verify adherence to these agreements.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to mandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following year.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. Risk 2: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow for more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning and implementation in response to market changes, such as allowing for increased price and reduced target. Risk 3: Threat of military offensives and disturbance in safety and security of some project areas or changes of security situation restricting implementation. Mitigating measures: Prioritize rehabilitation activities in safe and accessible locations. Ensure that projects on school rehabilitation in insecure locations, if any, have security plans for staff and the project beneficiaries have sufficient resources to consent to those plans.
