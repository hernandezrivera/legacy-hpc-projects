Project Summary

In 2024, 13.6 million people are still in need for WASH assistant all around the country according to HNO 2024, 5.5 million are displaced, including 2 million who live in last resort site.
Twelve years of crisis coupled with fragile environment (droughts) followed by the earthquake in February 2023 and its consequences, WASH needs remain critical and vital.
UNICEF through this project will support repair and rehabilitation of critical parts of water supply and sewerage systems, that witnessed destruction and deteriorated dramatically during the past 12 years, several criteria will be used for the project identification and selection, including cost per participant, level of project complexity, neutrality, accessibility, severity scale, number of returnees, number of IDPs, quality and quantity of water, and sanitation services currently available. At the same time UNICEF is supporting the usage of alternative power sources such as solar power for rehabilitated WASH systems to ensure longer term sustainable restoration for water services, power supply sector is at its worst since 2011. so, WASH interventions must consider power component to ensure systems functionality. UNICEF will also support the procurement of Sodium Hypochlorite countrywide as a preventive measure against AWD including cholera.
UNICEF will continue providing life-saving WASH interventions, as well as distributing supplies of non-food items related to WASH to the most vulnerable population in Syria. in lights of continuous internal displacement, also plans and preparation actions and contingency planning will also be maintained at a high level, together with stocks of necessary materials for responding rapidly to changing needs if access becomes more difficult or mass displacements occur in the northwest and/or northeast Syria.
Also, UNICEF will continue to ensure high level of coordination with major humanitarian actors and relevant stakeholders and advocacy efforts to respond to sector needs and reinforced sectoral and intersectoral coordination.
Humanitarian assistance is provided and coordinated between large variety of actors on the ground, operating from within Syria, Gaziantep, and NES with WoS, ensuring compliant strategies and responses are applied.
UNICEF as the sector lead agency, is working to ensure that sectoral coordination is practiced and applied at hubs levels and WoS.
PSEA strategy:
As part of the core commitment to children in humanitarian actions, UNICEF will continue strengthen the internal system, partners’ capacity, and networking with other actors to ensure the effectiveness of our PSEA programming. in 2024 PSEA strategic priorities are: safe and accessible reporting, accountability and investigation, quality and accessible survivor assistance and capacity building to humanitarian community members.
Protection Risk Analysis:
-Water distribution is not safe or accessible, especially for women, girls, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities (likelihood: low) mitigation in place: Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design, and implementation phases to ensure that the distance of water collection points remains at a minimum distance.
Ensure the accessibility of collective and low GBV risks WASH facilities to people with disabilities and promote gender-sensitive hygiene
messages/actions. Train partners and contractors on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Also, UNICEF includes the standard mitigation measures in all design for the water tank, filling points and sanitation blocks by engaging women with the process of selecting the location, pavement of the roads, increase lighting and visibility in locations where females will approach.
