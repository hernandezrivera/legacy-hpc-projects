Project Summary

The proposed response plan seeks to contribute to food security and livelihoods of communities in need (IPC3+) in Akobo, Uror, Ayod (Jonglei state), GPAA and Leer county through the implementation of CO2#: Increased food production through in-kind support of farming inputs, fishing kits, livestock restock treatment and vaccination, CO3#: Capacity Building on Good Agronomic Practices for both trainers and farmers, providing context-specific value chain inputs and training to increase food production, market interventions, fishery and farming value chains through an investment of post-harvest handling equipment, climate resistance agriculture, disaster risk reduction, amongst others.
This plan of 2024is to reach a total of 15,000 households (individuals; 24% women, 16% men, 36% girls and 24% boys) consisting of host communities, returnees, refugees and IDPs (Beneficiary type will be determined through a baseline). In 2024, Medicair plans to protect the livelihoods of vulnerable rural populations and improve food security through the provision of livelihood support during the main planting and dry season (crop and vegetable seeds, tools, swing kits), support food security information, analysis and coordination and strengthen absorptive capacity and build resilience at household and community level.
This HRP project will complement Medicair’s ongoing resilient programming to provide immediate quick impact activities while protecting the gains of resilience programming. Protection and Gender will be mainstreamed throughout the project's lifetime. The Do No Harm approach as enshrined in Medicair safe programming approach will be mainstreamed by ensuring participation and accountability to the affected population. This plan implemented by Medicair and the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security (MAFS) and aligned to FSL CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption i. Unconditional food distribution to returnees and refugees’ households, CO2: Enhance food production and CO3: Resilience building and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs aids.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces a dire humanitarian crisis, where acute food insecurity and malnutrition levels have risen to alarming rates, particularly in the states of Jonglei and Unity. The highly food-insecure populations of Akobo, Uror and Leer Counties are in locations with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), the macro-economic crisis, conflict and insecurity, and low agricultural production. Despite entering a favourable post-harvest period, projections from December 2022 to March 2023 paint a bleak picture (IPC projections 2022). According to the IPC Report November 2022, an overwhelming 7.8 million people, representing 64% of the nation’s population, are projected to suffer from Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Projections April to July 2023). With Akobo, Uror and Leer counties are currently in IPC 4. However, individuals will encounter Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) conditions if the current trends persist. Among all states, Unity State is expected to endure the harshest impact, with an estimated 77.2% of its population projected to struggle severely with food insecurity, closely followed by Jonglei at 74.2%. Approximately 1.4 million children under five faced acute malnutrition by June 2023, with a staggering number at risk of severe acute malnutrition. Jonglei State, in particular, reports a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) burden of 22%, with Unity State not far behind at 14% (ICP Report 2022) Populations across the Greater Akobo region of Jonglei state (Akobo and Uror) face extreme food insecurity and acute malnutrition (REACH, Akobo West and Waat Rapid Assessment 2023). The October 2022 Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) identified Akobo County as area-level phase 4 (emergency), with 5% of the population, or 11,348 people, projected to face phase 5 (catastrophic or famine). In Walgak, Shocks and displacement have negatively impacted livestock ownership and pastoralist livelihood activities. Reduced cattle ownership is a key driver of food insecurity in this area. 77% of displaced households and 60% of host community households reported livestock loss by theft or disease since December 2022. FGDs on population movement and FSL reported fearing ambushes, attacks, and abductions in remote bush areas, frequently noting violence against women and children as key protection concern.According to the IRNA Report 2022, Uror County has experienced years of intensified inter-communal conflict, cattle raiding, and general insecurity, which have formed the perfect storm for recurrent displacement, further made worse by a combination of heavy rains and massive floods. The ICP report 2022 places UROR at ICP 4 levels of food insecurity with these vulnerabilities and reduced access to essential goods and services in Uror County, diminishing the resilience of its most vulnerable people over the years. Women and girls are increasingly at risk of gender-based violence, including sexual violence, while searching for food or an income to support themselves and their families. Intra-domestic tensions are also increasing, heightening the risk of intimate partner violence. Adolescent girls are facing increased risks of child marriage. Their exposure to these risks increases when communities meet growing humanitarian needs driven by conflict, violence, climate shocks or food insecurity.As of November 2022, at least 25% of households in Leer County meet over 50% of their calorific assistance through humanitarian assistance, although the percentage of calorific assistance met through assistance is projected to decline to between 25% and 50% from December 2022 to July 2023. Strengthening skills and investment in post-harvest infrastructure (processing equipment, adequate and efficient storage systems) and supporting farmer organizations and cooperative movement would go a long in countering post-harvest losses.

2. Project activities

Activities are planned under CO2 and CO3. CO2: Enhance food production will be attained through. i) in-kind distribution of crop, vegetable, and fishing kits to 15,000 IPC3+ households (Akobo, Ayod and Pibor Ayod and Leer Twic East), a) Mobilization of communities, b) PIC formation and training, c) Identification and registration of beneficiaries d) Distribution of inputs, f) Post distribution monitoring, reporting.Medicair will enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood input and fishing kits. Fishing kits will be provided to locations with accessibility to fishing grounds to enable them to participate in fishing to meet their food needs and income supplement during crisis times. Rapid awareness on the proper use of fishing kits will be conducted to ensure that fishing is conducted in a way that does not deplete fishing capacity, reduce exposure to water pollution by responsible fishing and disposal and improve fish handling methods and hygiene to improve the nutrition of households; 2) provide crop and vegetable kits and tools. Vegetables, crop seeds and assorted tools will be provided as standard kits. 5 types of preferred vegetable seeds will be provided to the beneficiaries in a total of 8 Kg per household. A beneficiary preference of seeds will be conducted, and ecological and culturally adapted types of seeds will be prioritized. The distribution of seeds will be conducted both in Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism and static operations in locations with elevated IPC phase 4 and 5 and in which Medicair has established presence. Simple awareness creations on farming practices and methods as part of capacity building will be conducted on the spot during the distribution to ensure the recipient households know the production practices and methods; 3) Distribution of fishing kits and assorted vegetable seeds as standard kits. Medicair will distribute fishing and vegetable kits to the most vulnerable households to meet their livelihood assistance during the dry season. This will reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shock by 1) capacity building to beneficiaries receiving livelihood support such as GAPs and improved fishing and preservation. The project provision of seeds and tools such as fishing, vegetable and crop seeds will be complemented with capacity building to improve skills and knowledge towards improving production and productivity. CO3: Resilience building and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs aids. i) Strengthening livestock, fishery, and farming value chain through a) Infrastructural support on fish preservation infrastructures (fish drying racks, salting facilities, cold storages, and smoking facilities to 1000 fisher folks a) Farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, exchange visits and farmers’ shows b) Establishment of the demonstration plots c) Vocational training across the 5 targeted project locations Community mobilization and sensitization d) beneficiary identification and registration, training iii) Market development Training on SILC community groups. Climate action, including assessment of climate risk, Mapping of climate-related risks and dissemination of anticipated risks/disasters, construction or renovation of flood control dykes,
