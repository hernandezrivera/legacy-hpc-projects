Project Summary

The goal of the project is to provide quality education for Rohingya in the camps. In order to achieve the project’s goal, WVB intends to achieve the outcomes which are: Provision of pre-primary, primary, and secondary education; Provision on informal education for beneficiary aged 3-24; and implementation of measures to improve quality education and learning achievement. Under these outcomes the following outputs will be focused along with their respective activities: improve quality, safe and protective Early Child Development (ECD); improved quality, safe and protective primary education; and improved access to learning center for adolescents and youth. In order to improve access to quality, safe and protective education for children, early childhood education will be implemented with specific support of Early Childhood Development Centres. WV will conduct community, parent/caregiver targeted awareness sessions on the importance of early childhood education in the target locations. Children aged 3-5 year will be covered under Early Childhood Development (ECD) programme developed by WV. To ensure five milestone development areas of Social and emotional, Language/communication, Movement/physical development, Cognitive (learning, thinking, problem-solving) can be replicated for next learning stage of education.
In terms of Learning centre approach will be applied for grade 1 to 10 with age appropriate learners. In line with government resolution Myanmar curriculum will be followed for grade 1-10 learners. Innovative approaches including audio-visual media will be used to enhance the learning experience and reach more learners while ensuring quality to attain the required learning competencies at various levels. Also, scholarships will be provided to the learners assessing their excellence and competency to encourage learners and their parents to create competitive learning environment.
WV will use 220 learning centre/ multi-purpose centres in the targeted areas for smooth delivery of the programme implementation. All ECD centres and trainings will include the provision of and/or creation of inclusive print materials to support inclusion of children with disabilities as well as the formation of disability support committees consisting of caregivers of children with disabilities, facilitators and Disabled Persons Organisation (DPO) representatives. Centre management committee will work closely with programme management team and MEAL for developing learning progress monitoring tools, and finalizing checklist. They will submit monthly report and action plan based on monitoring progress report. It is anticipated that through these interventions, teachers, parents, caregivers and the broader community will prioritise early childhood learning, and improve access to knowledge and skills for stimulating learning and development for their children.
Students will be targeted, including dropouts, person with disability, non-goers etc. to provide vocational and life skills education to adolescents and youth. The programme will engage selected students in WV vocational existing training centres and enrol them in 3/ 6 month course as appropriate based on need assessment report. At the end of the course, plan of action will be prepared by the graduates with a yearly target and a monthly follow up. Furthermore, mobile vocational training centres will be scheduled for Person With Disability in satellite center of target area. After the course completion graduates will be linked with Homogeneous department, entities for income generation.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Lack of access of educational services has been a consistent issue in the Rohingya Camps and host communities of Cox's Bazar. The COVID-19 pandemic has further exacerbated this issue resulting in increased child marriages, dropouts from schools, and child labor. According to a report from the Protection Working Group in Cox's Bazar, In both Rohingya and host communities, the prevalence of dropout rose with age. Around 40% of host children (15 to 17 yrs.) and 70% of Rohingya of the same age group were not attending school or madrasa. Child labor was found to be more prevalentin the host children (13.5%) than the Rohingya children (9.5%). More than half of the adolescent boys (Host: 52.5% and Rohingya: 55.6%) at the age of 15-17 years were involved in child labor. Parents reported that 30.2% of host children worked in the host community for their families, whereas 374 (28%) of Rohingya children worked at least one hour per day for their families without being paid.The J-MSNA 2021 found that education enrolment was very low in particular among adolescents, and especially adolescent girls, because the longer Temporary Learning Centres (TLCs) closed in line with COVID-19 containment measures, and educational institutions were temporarily suspended. The limited access to education services was a major concern for the families and have impacted the new enrolment ratio . In the Camps, the education system covers the children between 4 and 14 years; there is no education for those over 14 years of age. The full-fledged implementation of Myanmar Curriculum Programme hasn’t started yet. The survey showed that, in the Camp community, 64% of HHs reported the intention to enrol their children in non-formal education. Among the ones who are enrolled in the learning centre, however, 8% do not attend regularly, primarily because of lack of awareness. Before that the learning centre closed in March 2020 due to the Covid-19 restrictions of the government, 45% of children were not enrolled [Figure 13]. Among the total respondents, 49% reported that children practice home-based learning through different modalities such as caregiver support and radio show. Overall, in the Rohingya community it’s common to think that girls don’t need to be educated and that only boys should study. Whenever a girl is a victim of violence, in most cases the family withdraws her from school forever and they move Camp to minimize the social stigma.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The camps and host communities that were chosen for this project does not have any other service providers implementing their activities. WVI takes part in monthly meetings with Education sector to coordinate with other service providers and discuss which mechanisms should be followed to avoid duplication. These mechanisms include regularly checking the FCN to see whether the target beneficiary is from the camps alloted to WVI and checking the attendance sheet in the LCs when a child is absent followed by home-visit to learn the reason of absence. If the child's absence was due to going to an LC of another service provider, a meeting is held with the respective parents of the child and representatives from the service providers to come to a decision of choosing a LC.The selected camps and host communities are not in isolated areas and are close to each other so the high overhead costs will not be an issue. The selected camps will also be provided with GBV prevention and response and child protection services thus allowing an integrated approach between sectors. Since the beginning of the influx, WVB has been implementing activities related to Education and will be continuing to do so.

3.2 - Gender

WVB has a strong gender equality and social inclusion (GESI) policy including an operational guideline to ensure that gender and social inclusion approach is adopted and monitored to improve the access of children and adolescents who are excluded to resources, to opportunities and benefits from the project interventions. This policy is to be applied in the proposed project. In addition to that WV will apply its experience in engaging Men, Boys and female faith leaders to reduce GBV and reduce child marriage. WVB’s gender equality policy maintains key principles that guide all of its interventions. Locally, and at the global level, WV benefits from expertise which is specifically dedicated towards mainstreaming protection and gender across all projects and sectors. At the refugee response, WVB is an active member of the child protection and GBV sub-sectors and is committed to protection mainstreaming across all of its programs.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

World Vision has zero tolerance of incidents of violence or abuse against children and other people in the communities we serve, either committed by our staff or others connected to our work. Safeguarding children and adults living in communities WVI serves is foundational to all their activities, programmes, and Lines of Ministry (relief, development, and advocacy). Central to everything we do is our commitment to do no harm to any child anywhere nor to adults living where World Vision has a programming presence. We uphold the best interests of children as a primary consideration in all actions and decisions. Safeguarding includes preventing, reporting, and responding to harm or abuse caused by WV employees and affiliates of children and of adults living where WV programming practice. WV is committed to continuous improvement of safeguarding efforts which emphasises prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and other forms of violence or harm.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Guided by the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), communities will be made aware of their rights and entitlements under the project, and all complaints and community feedback will be gathered on a continuous basis and responded to promptly to ensure accountability to the beneficiaries. Adequate mechanisms, which are accessible to older persons and people with disabilities, shall be put in place to allow beneficiaries to raise their complaints in an accessible, safe and dignified manner. Suggested adaptations/adjustments to Accountability practices for COVID-19 Emergency may include: providing Information (about implementing partners, expected behaviours of staff and their right to complain if we don’t fulfil them, planned activities including start and end dates, targeted beneficiaries and criteria); use local/national radio stations to disseminate key messages; if possible, distribute radios to key community members to be able to receive messages.

Environment protection skill, disaster preparedness, Rescue and First aid, Basic awareness on cyclone preparedness and warning system for students.
