Project Summary

The main objective of this intervention is to reduce the transmission of and exposure to water borne diseases amongst targeted at risk and vulnerable IDP populations. This shall be achieved through the provision of access to safe water, adequate and appropriate sanitation facilities and services. The project also aims to ensure that the targeted communities have the necessary information, knowledge and understanding to prevent water and sanitation related diseases. The project shall promote the equitable participation of both men and women in the planning and decision making processes, this will ultimately help to ensure that the entire affected population has safe and easy access to water supply, sanitation facilities and services, and that such services and facilities are appropriate. Special focus shall be given to the needs of women and children who bear greater risk of SGBV in the use communal sanitation facilities. The intervention shall support gender sensitive participatory processes for operation, maintenance and management of the sanitation facilities and services. The project also seeks to promote food hygiene by conducting nutrition (cooking) demonstrations, and promote improved health outcomes by screening and referring malnourished children to appropriate health care facilities.

Needs

Across Somalia there are multiple threats to the health and resilience of the population due to inadequate WASH facilities. 29 percent of the population get water from an unimproved water source and an additional 12 percent from surface water. In Mogadishu, many of the urban and peri urban poor, including IDPs, pay up to five times more for their water than those in rural areas. High water collection distances further limit access and water consumption for many people. In these overcrowded, typically urban/peri urban sites, public health risk is raised with only 27 per cent of residents having access to improved excreta disposal, i.e. a facility that avoids open defecation. Mass displacement throughout the country caused by unfavorable conditions, insecurity, and drought and flooding has significantly impacted the demographic make up of the country, leading to urban crowding and putting additional pressure on scarce resources and services in cities and towns. This is further increasing the risk of disease outbreaks due to limited access to safe water and poor sanitation and overburdened health services. In fact, the continued presence of these risk factors is responsible for the worst outbreak in recent history of cholera in Somalia in 2017. HIJRA assessments reveal IDP villages in Baidoa Daynille, Dharkineley - Banadir, and Wanla Weyn Lower Shabelle; are high risk poor hygiene environments with a general trend of cholera outbreak and insufficient coverage of WASH services. Populations living at these locations have been adversely affected by floods due to the heavy rains witnessed in 2018; this has led to the extensive degradation of water and sanitation infrastructure. Levels of access to water and sanitation services have been reduced considerably; the situation has witnessed the flooding of latrines and subsequent overflow of faecal matter , pools of stagnant water, increased open defecation, breakdown of water supply and distribution systems.

Activities

I. Supply of clean, safe and treated water through water fees payment to targeted population. 42, 916 men, 41,234 women, and 102,850 children making up 26,714 households shall have access to approximately 15 litres of safe drinking water per person per day for twelve months;II. Water distribution point construction and or rehabilitation; 9 boreholes shall be fitted with appropriate solar pumping system to reduce maintenance and operating cost long term.III. Distribution of 1,050 hygiene kits to the most vulnerable households;IV. 39,690 women and 17,010 men targeted by Community hygiene outreach activities, implemented by 90 (men and women) selected and trained community hygiene promoters. Participatory methods of hygiene education shall be employed to enable targeted beneficiaries develop the knowledge, attitudes and skills required for them to adopt healthy lifestyles. The promotion of positive behaviour towards general environmental cleanliness, hand washing with soap (at least before eating and after using the latrine). The correct use of latrines, cleaning of latrines, collection and proper disposal of solid waste shall also be promoted. Selected Community Hygiene Promoters shall help to monitor and encourage proper hygiene practice.V. Promoting food hygiene by conducting nutrition (cooking) demonstrations;VII. Rehabilitation and de-sludging of 600 latrines to serve the needs of 187,000 men, women and children. Latrines shall be fitted with lockable doors; lockable from the inside to protect people while they are using them. An appropriate hand washing point with soap, water and adequate drainage shall be made available.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The main objective of the proposed intervention is to reduce the transmission of and exposure to water borne diseases amongst targeted at risk and vulnerable IDP populations. This shall be achieved through the provision of access to safe water, adequate and appropriate sanitation facilities and services. The project also aims to ensure that the targeted communities have the necessary information, knowledge and understanding to prevent water and sanitation related diseases. A two-pronged strategy of: (i) providing immediate assistance, and (ii) facilitating knowledge and technology transfer to the targeted IDP and host communities shall be implemented. Immediate assistance shall be provided with the aim of saving lives, reducing and preventing the transmission of water borne diseases. Knowledge and technology transfer shall involve working to create a cost efficient and sustainable WASH services delivery system; and empowering the targeted IDP and host community groups towards self-management and reliance. This DRR approach is aimed at reducing the likelihood of risks and their consequences. Thus transiting from emergency to post emergency towards a durable solution. Empowered community members shall retain the knowledge and skills received, irrespective of whether they choose to return to their place of origin, integrate with host community or settle elsewhere. The Project shall be accountable to the targeted affected population by among other means; the provision of access to project information by means of an interactive mobile short message service [sms] and thereby improve Improved Beneficiary participation and programming outcomes. This shall also provide an effective platform for feedback and complaints, as well as ensure that All users are satisfied with the design and implementation of the project. HIJRA will ensure that there is an accountable management and maintenance system in place for the WASH services and facilities provided for by the project, with the overall aim of guaranteeing quality and value for money.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The majority of displaced populations are migrating towards mainly urban centers like Mogadishu, where the poor humanitarian situation in many of the informal settlements occupied by the displaced is being further aggravated by tension with host community and competition for access to WASH services. The basic needs of IDPs are not being fully met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing services, or from the sheer absence of services due to poor targeting of settlements. The project seeks to mitigate the above risks by means of scaling up of assistance to IDPs and providing similar assistance to host community groups in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic WASH services and facilities. IDP and host community leadership structures shall be involved in jointly assessing WASH needs with a view to reducing tension and conflict between communities. All targeted beneficiaries shall be identified and registered to prevent exclusion from services, especially for vulnerable groups.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project shall seek promote the equitable participation of both men and women in the planning and decision making processes, this will ultimately help to ensure that the entire affected population has safe and easy access to water supply, sanitation facilities and services, and that such services and facilities are appropriate. Special focus shall be given to the needs of women and children who bear greater risk of SGBV in the use communal sanitation facilities. The intervention shall support gender sensitive participatory processes for operation, maintenance and management of the sanitation facilities and services. The project also seeks to promote food hygiene by conducting nutrition (cooking) demonstrations, and promote improved health outcomes by screening and referring malnourished children to appropriate health care facilities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The current humanitarian context in the target location is contributing to among other risks, increased cases of GBV, especially for women and girls, often with inadequate access to reporting and referral mechanism or accountability of perpetrators. Measures shall be put in place to ensure WASH services and facilities promote and provide for the safety, security and dignity of all beneficiaries, but especially for women, children, the disabled and elderly.
