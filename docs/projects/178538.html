Project Summary

This is an 8-month program aimed at improving the access to & quality of educational opportunities for children, youth, families and education personnel in Idleb Gov. At the time of proposal submission, all targeted areas are accessible. The main component of the program will provide holistic support to 10 schools that are currently at-risk of closure due to a lack of financial & material support. The project also includes modular services for 10 additional school sites incl. educational supply distribution & protection interventions. In total, the program will reach 8000 children & youth (50% female), 230 school personnel (40% female) & 500 caretakers (60% female).
The project primarily targets out-of-school and educationally at-risk children and youth in Ariha and Jisr Ash-Shugur SDs. These areas are characterized by the 2021 HNO as having critical levels of need for education assistance, in part due to the continuation of conflict-related violence, the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic & ongoing shifts in the migration patterns of IDPs and returnees.The proposed action responds directly to the needs & priorities of teachers, care-givers & students as expressed during a participatory needs assessment completed by us during Dec2020.The action is also designed in line with the cluster’s priorities; as highlighted by 2021 HNO, the primary objectives are to (1) to improve academic teaching & learning (2) to promote student wellbeing and protection & (3) to strengthen educational access and resources.The project will begin with a 1-month start-up phase in May during which 190 educational personnel will receive refresher trainings on (1) child-centered pedagogy (2) PSS & child protection (3) emergency first aid & response & (4) health, hygiene and COVID-19 measures.Participant feedback will be sought on the relevance of trainings provided & suggestions for future topics.In parallel to this, light rehab works will be undertaken to ensure all 10 school sites meet the INEE minimum standards for infrastructure, incl. those around gender sensitivity & disability inclusion.Works will be prioritized thru a a participatory process involving students &educ personnel.
From Jun thru Aug, these 10 sites will run non-formal education programs for 2000 children (50% female).The aim of the 3-month summer session will be to prepare children for the academic school year thru both remedial and catch-up classes.In Sep, program sites will transition to provision of formal education which will reach 4000 children (50% female).In addition to quality academic instruction, students in both formal & non-formal programs will also benefit from structured Psychosocial Support (PSS) activities.While all educational activities are intended to be conducted in-person, the program also includes COVID-19 contingency plans for pandemic-related school closures. Schools will also be provided with basic classroom & teaching supplies & financial resources to cover essential operating costs such as teacher incentives. Where possible, student safety & protection will be furthered thru the creation of an external referral mechanism.Families will also be engaged thru Back-to-School Campaigns & PTAs at all sites.
Finally, we will also include modular interventions to address protection & access concerns at 10 other schools in the target areas.Here the project will provide 4000 additional children (50% female) with student kits as well as with mine-action training and awareness.
The proposed intervention reflects our deep contextual knowledge & technical experience in carrying out integrated, multi-sectoral programming in Idleb & across NWS since 2013.To date, we supported more than 7900 vulnerable IDP & host community children with quality, protective formal & non-formal learning opportunities.Thru these & other education initiatives, we built the relationships & rapport needed to carry out the proposed intervention effectively and in alignment with a Do No Harm approach.

4 - Needs

In addition to the 2021 HNO, this proposal reflects research undertaken by us at school sites targeted for holistic support. As of Dec 2020, 4277 children (52% female, 24% IDP) were attending these schools, of which 64% were primary school students ages 6-12 (NGO, Education Assessment, Dec 20). School personnel reported an additional 671 children (46% female) in the target areas were out-of-school. The research found top barriers to educational access and quality include a lack of interest in education & a lack of student learning materials. Further, longstanding factors relating to gender, displacement and disability status continue to hinder learning opportunities for children and youth in Northern Syria: females are likely to fall behind or remain out-of-school due to domestic responsibilities, while males are more affected by the need to work outside the home; displaced children are much more likely to be out-of-school overall (42% of OOSC are IDP); and disabled children face obstacles with unaccommodating learning environments and difficulties in traveling to and from school sites. Only two of the assessed schools provide structured recreational or PSS activities, while the others cite a lack of funding and resources as factors prohibiting services related to student wellbeing. Overall, school personnel see a lack of family interest in education and a lack of incentives as the top barriers to providing quality education. Teachers at 8 of the 9 assessed schools have gone without salaries since spring of 2020. Nearly all assessed schools are reported to require light rehabilitation work, with the need to winterize buildings a top concern. Nearly all schools noted high material barriers for children with disabilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

To begin, schools will undergo light rehab works. Concurrently, all teachers will be trained on pedagogy for student‐centred & mixed‐level classrooms. During the summer period, academic instruction will focus on basic numeracy & literacy skills for children who are out‐of‐school or at risk of dropping out. During the formal school year, schools offer a core set of academic classes following recognised curriculums. In both formal & non‐formal settings, academic teaching and learning is coupled with activities focused on student safety and wellbeing. School personnel receive training in child protection & PSS care and all students participate in weekly, age‐appropriate PSS sessions led by dedicated facilitators. Community outreach will include back‐to‐school campaigns & the creation of PTAs. Gender will be considered throughout the range of program activities including in rehab of WASH facilities, school personnel training & community outreach activities. Further, our dual focus on both primary & secondary students means that our programs support age & level‐appropriate quality instruction, especially for older adolescents and youth. All education activities are monitored, verified and/or assessed through a variety of tools and practices. Our education team in each area includes a dedicated MEAL officer, who oversees key monitoring and reporting activities during weekly site visits. This includes teacher and classroom observations, the ASER exam (academic), and the SDQ tool (PSS). As noted in the project summary, the intervention also includes student learning supply distribution and mine action trainings for 4000 children at an additional 10 schools in the target areas.Activities con'd in Section 7 due to space limits.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

CP considerations have been mainstreamed into the design, implementation & monitoring of all relevant activities including measures to protect children from violence, abuse & exploitation in and around school sites by peers, school personnel & actors in the conflict. In line with our child-safeguarding policy, the project will include training for school staff on CP measures like handling peer-to-peer harassment as well as skills for non-violent classroom and behavior management. As part of our child-safeguarding (CS) policy, school staff will and uphold our CS Code of Conduct, which details the appropriate, dignified treatment of children. Emergency response plans established as part of this project and the trainings and equipment provided to schools will prepare teachers, students and their families to stay safe in the face of natural disaster and conflict-related security threats. The project will provide equitable and impartial access to educational services, especially for marginalized and highly-vulnerable groups through activities and targeting practices that are based entirely on need and that are sensitive to gender, disability and displacement status. The proposed intervention considers the unique, gendered needs of boys and girls, as well those with physical and cognitive disabilities, as demonstrated in our plans for school rehabilitation. PSS classes and awareness sessions address children’s personal safety and strategies for avoiding harm through modules on topics like mine action. The child-friendly complaints mechanism detailed under AAP will also provide a ‘reactive’ channel for members of the target community to express and address project-related violations to their rights, wellbeing and protection.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Challenges to planned modalities for delivery due to COVID-19: Accommodation made to ensure that project activities in the first three months can be implemented despite lockdown and school closure measures in the target area. Schools will be well-prepared to continue with these practices should the start of the formal school year be postponed or disrupted. Attacks on and around schools: security situation closely monitored, with emergency response plans, threat notification systems, and sheltering or evacuation procedures for program sites established. School personnel trained in first aid and school safety, and schools outfitted with health and safety kits. If threats persist, schools will rely on alternative modalities for service delivery, including home schooling. Community tensions resulting from perceived inequalities related to selecting beneficiaries, hiring staff, and procuring project supplies or services: All project activities and procedures based on transparent, needs-focused criteria for engaging with local communities and markets. For example, teachers will be chosen and paid in line with education directorate policie. All procurements follow robust policies to prohibit partiality or nepotism. Changes regarding the security situation and displacement: Security situation closely monitored with emergency response and contingency plans in place, These include relocating program sites if necessary, as well alternative modalities of service delivery, such as home schooling. Border closures and delays in transporting project goods and supplies (MEDIUM): If supply lines are disrupted due to border closures, we rely on alternative channels for procurement in local markets, where possible.
