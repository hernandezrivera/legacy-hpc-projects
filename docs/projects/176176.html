Project Summary

American Refugee Committee's (ARC) nutrition response will focus on preventive and curative nutrition interventions in Kajo Keji and Aweil West counties targeting the most vulnerable members of the community mainly children under five years as well as pregnant and lactating women. ARC is present in Kajo Keji county implementing integrated nutrition, Protection and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) programs whereas in Aweil West county, ARC is implementing nutrition program.
The proposed interventions include community management of acute malnutrition through the Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP), Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) and community mobilization; promotion of appropriate Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices at the nutrition sites and in the community; and Vitamin A supplementation and deworming (VASD) targeting children under five years. The project aims to reduce morbidity and prevent mortality associated with acute malnutrition and subsequently improve maternal and child well-being. ARC will also work in coordination with other sectors such as WASH, Protection, Education, Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) and Health to strengthen integration and linkages of services with the goal of preventing acute malnutrition and to enhance recovery of beneficiaries undergoing nutrition rehabilitation.
The response will target the host community, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees in the above-mentioned locations primarily children under five years both boys and girls as well as pregnant and lactating women. The project aims to reach 2,412 children aged 6 to 59 months with severe acute malnutrition without medical complications, 6,900 children aged 6 to 59 months with moderate acute malnutrition, 8,884 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition, 84,071 children aged 6 to 59 months with Vitamin A supplements and 75,664 children aged 12 to 59 months with Albendazole tablets.
It is important to point out that ARC will target 50% of the SAM/MAM caseload as well as children under five years for Vitamin A supplementation and deworming in Aweil West county due to presence of another nutrition partner in the county whereas in Kajo-Keji county, ARC will target the entire proposed SAM/MAM caseload and children under five years for Vitamin A supplementation and deworming according to the 2021 HNO targets.

1 - Needs

The burden of acute malnutrition remains high in South Sudan. This could be attributed to the consequences of the perennial conflict resulting to acute food insecurity due to unsustainable livelihoods, limited humanitarian access, high inflation resulting to exorbitant food prices; climatic shocks; high morbidity with limited access to health care; poor hygiene practices; lack of adequate sanitation facilities; limited access to clean water, and sub-optimal maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices. Furthermore, COVID 19 outbreak has limited delivery of nutrition services due to restrictions that have been put in place to prevent transmission. The latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) situation analysis conducted in October and November 2020 revealed deteriorating food security and nutrition situation in South Sudan. The IPC analysis estimates that 1.4 million children aged 6-59 months and 483,000 pregnant and lactating women will be affected by acute malnutrition in 2021. The IPC analysis further estimates that approximately 84,232 children in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) and 101,624 children in Central Equatoria states will suffer acute malnutrition in 2021. These are within the locations where ARC is implementing nutrition programs i.e. Aweil West and Kajo Keji counties. Kajo Keji county is among the most affected counties in South Sudan by the continued political instability and fighting that has led to massive displacement of the population within the county and to the refugee camps in Uganda. However, from late 2019, the number of returnees increased substantially and is attributed to the improved security and the hope of stability brought about by the formation of the transitional government of national unity. However, the local authorities have indicated that an influx is expected in 2021 if peace continues to hold and access to various areas improve. Poor food security attributed to recurrent conflicts that has disrupted normal livelihood in the county coupled with other aggravating factors such as lack of adequate and safe water, poor sanitation facilities, lack of proper shelters, limited access to health care services common during displacements, exposes the population at the risk of acute malnutrition. Specific data on the prevalence of acute malnutrition in Kajo Keji is not available, however, the IPC analysis predicts that from January to March and April to July 2021, the population in county will be in acute food insecurity phase 3 (crisis) and phase 4 (emergency) respectively. NBeG state has had relatively better political stability as compared to other parts of the country, however, it has over the years reported high burden of acute malnutrition. This is consistent with the results of the SMART survey conducted in Aweil West in January 2020 which reported prevalence of GAM of 13.8% classified as serious based on WHO emergency threshold for acute malnutrition. It is important to also mention that Aweil West has perennially been affected by floods resulting in poor harvests. The IPC analysis predicts that from January to March and April to July 2021, the population in Aweil West will be in acute food insecurity phase 3 (crisis) and phase 4 (emergency) respectively. Maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices in South Sudan continue to be poor especially complementary feeding practices among children aged 6 to 23 months and has significantly contributed to the burden of acute malnutrition. This is consistent with the results of the FSNMS round 25 that reported minimum dietary diversity at 15% and minimum acceptable diet at 7%. Malnutrition can have long-term negative effects particularly during the first 1,000 days of a child’s life i.e. from pregnancy through to two years of age, therefore, prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition among children under five years, and pregnant and lactating women remains a significant need in the targeted locations.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ARC condemns and prohibits any behavior on the part of an ARC employee or volunteer which constitutes any form of sexual misconduct, including sexual harassment, sexual exploitation, and sexual violence towards any other staff member, beneficiary, or other individual participating in an ARC program or activity. All new employees and volunteers will receive an initial orientation and periodic refresher training on protection of sexual exploitation and abuse policy. Employees and volunteers will also sign a document verifying their understanding of the policy. Subsequently all employees, volunteers, and beneficiaries will be made aware of how to report complaints of sexual exploitation and abuse. The beneficiaries will be made aware of ARC’s code of conduct and compliant mechanism through meetings and discussions conducted through on-going activities for instance during sessions held before distributions, community mobilization sessions etc. throughout the project implementation period. ARC will investigate all allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse while keeping it confidential, and violations of the policy will lead to immediate termination of employment.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ARC will ensure that the most vulnerable members of the community are engaged at all phases of the project life cycle i.e. project design, planning, implementation and evaluation to obtain their contribution and ensure their unique needs are cared for. Furthermore, to avoid marginalization of vulnerable community members, more than one channel of communication will be available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Examples include one-on-one meetings at the nutrition sites and office, and discussions with a staff member during community events. In addition, to eliminate communication barrier, ARC will engage staff from the local community who are well versed with the local language.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ARC is committed to strengthening accountability to the affected population by listening to feedback and addressing grievances from those who participate, support or are affected by the program by giving both a voice, and the opportunity to influence key decisions. The aim is to build strong relationships with the community and to allow community feedback to shape and improve programming ensuring that interventions are relevant and appropriate. ARC has already established a relationship of two-way communication and transparency with target communities. Program plans will be communicated to the communities during inception and will continue throughout the implementation period through open forums and focus group discussions. In addition, ARC values complaints and feedback because it helps in improving programming. Focal points will be identified and oriented at all sites to lead on community complaints/accountability response mechanisms, including listening to and reporting feedback and complaints, ensuring timely response, and sensitizing communities on use and purpose of these mechanisms. Furthermore, ARC through the Project Management Committees in each nutrition site, will provide a means for beneficiaries to voice complaints and provide feedback on areas relevant to operations in a safe and dignified manner.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ARC will engage the local authorities, community leaders, representative of different groups e.g. people living with disabilities, influential members of the community such as religious leaders etc. and the targeted beneficiaries to outline project’s plan and purpose during project inception and throughout the implementation period through open forums and focus group discussions. In addition, ARC will conduct exit interviews to programme beneficiaries to establish their satisfaction, obtain feedback and areas for improvement on the quality of services provided at the nutrition sites. The feedback obtained from the current project progress will be used to shape and improve programming, ensuring that interventions are relevant and appropriate and meets their unique needs. For instance, identification of safe and accessible distribution points for the targeted beneficiaries for nutrition services, jointly planning distribution schedules so that times are convenient and safe for women and children.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

ARC will adhere to the IASC international standards and guidelines while delivering the proposed humanitarian services. ARC will uphold the “Do No Harm” principle and as much as possible minimize any negative impact the proposed interventions may have on the beneficiaries and ensure their safety, dignity and respect is prioritized during service delivery. ARC will also uphold the principles of impartiality, neutrality and participation in delivery of services by giving equal opportunity without any biasness to the members of the community regardless of their status, age, gender or affiliations etc. ARC will regularly conduct gender based violence safety audits in nutrition sites to identify and address protection/safety needs. Additionally, ARC will sensitize the nutrition workers and volunteers on gender and gender based violence, and safe referral ways for gender based violence survivors so that they are able to identify and refer survivors for support. ARC will also work with all program beneficiaries to plan the schedules so that times are convenient and safe for women and children. ARC will provide services in a manner that reduces the time spent at, travelling to and returning from nutrition service points. For isolated beneficiaries who take over a day to travel to the nutrition site, an appropriate ration will be considered after verification with community nutrition volunteers or establishment of an outreach/mobile site in the respective area. To mitigate gender-based violence, both men and women will be included in the process of selecting a safe and accessible distribution points where applicable for nutrition services.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

ARC will sensitize the nutrition workers and volunteers on gender and gender based violence, and safe referral ways for gender based violence survivors so that they are able to identify and refer survivors for support in a timely fashion. In addition, the nutrition workers and volunteers will work closely with their counterparts in the Protection sector to ensure that any project's beneficiary that need support either in the community or at the nutrition site is attended.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The project staff and volunteers will be trained on promotion of kitchen/home gardening. The nutrition workers and volunteers will thereafter sensitize the targeted beneficiaries and the community at large on kitchen/home gardening and encourage them to establish kitchen gardens at home. Kitchen/home gardening will build the resilience of the population through growing a variety of foods for consumption at household level and preservation of the surplus for future or selling for income. In addition, food produced will be sold to buy essentials that cannot be grown at the household level.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

ARC will work with other sectors such as WASH, Protection, Education, Food Security and Livelihoods and Health to address multiple needs of the children and women targeted in the nutrition project. This will enhance recovery of beneficiaries enrolled in the program for treatment as well as prevent new cases of acute malnutrition in the community. Furthermore, the project will be built in such a way that equal chance will be given to boys and girls below the age of five thus sex preference will not prevent access to nutrition services. In addition, any biased emerging gaps will be identified and addressed promptly.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The proposed interventions will focus on prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition among children under five years as indicated below: • Timely identification of children under five years with acute malnutrition in the community through the community nutrition volunteers and use of family led MUAC screening approach and subsequently referral of the identified acutely malnourished children to the nutrition sites/centre for treatment. This will enhance timely treatment before their nutritional status deteriorate. • Treatment of both severely and moderately malnourished children at the outpatient therapeutic programme and targeted supplementary feeding programmes respectively as per the Ministry of health CMAM guidelines.• Promotion of optimal infant and young child care and feeding practices including prevention of childhood illnesses to the primary caregivers through individual counselling and group education sessions, cooking demonstration sessions to educate on preparation of quality meals and dietary diversity, and establishment of kitchen gardens at the nutrition sites to demonstrate to the programme beneficiaries the production and utilization of nutrient-dense foods.• Strengthen coordination with other sectors such as WASH, Protection, GBV, Education, FSL and Health to enhance integration and linkages of services with the goal of improving treatment of beneficiaries under nutrition rehabilitation and preventing new cases of acute malnutrition. • Boost access and utilization of the nutrition services through the establishment of the outreach/mobile sites to minimize the distance travelled by targeted beneficiaries to receive nutrition services.
