Project Summary

This project proposal outlines a comprehensive plan to address and combat gender-based violence (GBV). The project aims to end violence against women and girls, promote gender equality, and empower survivors through a multi-faceted approach. By implementing community-based interventions, advocacy campaigns, and capacity-building initiatives, we aim to create a safe and inclusive society for all. This project will be implemented within a period of 12 months targeting 4000 direct beneficiaries (Men:800, Women:1500, Girls:1200,Boys:500) with a budget of USD 200,000 at approximately $ 50 per beneficiary.

1. Needs

From 15 April 2023, when fighting broke out in the Sudanese capital of Khartoum, violence has spread across the Sudan boarders into the neighboring Countries including South Sudan—leaving hundreds of civilians dead and over thousands returning to their native homes. Amidst the chaos and destruction, women, girls and people of all genders are facing another, often less visible threat: heightened sexual and gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse.Emergencies brings increased pressure that threatens the safety, health, security of well -being of the people, it brings increased number of partners for the humanitarian response, rapid recruitments with high staff turnover, high work pressures, and challenges to ensuring effective coordination and oversight, thereby increasing the risks of all forms of misconduct, including Gender based violence and SEA.Conflict drives sexual violence in multiple ways, rape and other sexual abuses are being directly perpetrated by armed forces, while heightened economic and societal instability provide the conditions for increased sexual exploitation, trafficking, and other forms of sexual abuse by security officers, business men and Aid workers.The breakdown of justice systems ,healthcare and other systems makes support even more harder for survivors to access safety. Already terrified, the threat of further abuse hangs heavy over those fleeing as they continue their dangerous journeys to reach the border crossing and transit centres. Majority of the returnees cross through Renk into South Sudan’s Upper Nile State, either by road or by boat to transit to Nasir County.Thousands are facing mounting, multiplying risks as they try to escape Sudan with their loved ones. Many lose their families and loved ones along the way as a result of lack of community communication on GBV and SEA reporting channels, Language barrier to read through the outreach materials on GBV and SEA, Lack of care services and care givers, age barriers, Lack of community-awareness campaigns on the risks of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) committed by aid workers and security officers, lack of knowledge in knowing their rights including early and forced marriages and other harmful cultural practices.Women sell their body, clothes and other belongings to buy food staffs due to economic crisis, others go into the bushes to sell fire wood putting them at further risk of predators as they venture far and often alone into the deserted bushes. Women selling tea in the market and working in restaurants and small pubs are in danger, with reports of traders and Aid workers exploiting them and coercing them into sex in exchange of Aid support items and essential goods. Most women and children sleep in open places without formal shelters, this increases their exposure to violence and abuse.The crisis is likely to lead to rising unintended pregnancies and sexually transmitted infections, on top of the physical and mental trauma hundreds of thousands are already suffering from.This project intends to be as inclusive as possible and to raise community awareness through community mobilizers on the risk of Gender Based Violence and sexual exploitation and abuse(SEA) committed to women and girls .The project will establish or strengthen proper reporting channels and feed-back mechanism and referral pathways as well as to ensure that victims know where and how to safely report Gender based Violence related cases and SEA when it does take place, the project intends to give appropriate reliable and updated messages through radio talk shows in the local language, audios , videos and printed messages that are tailored to local cultural context and to disseminate GBV outreach and communication materials. The project will use both the Community centered approach and victim centered approach to conduct PSS and case management processes. Finally the project intend to establish one safe space for women and girls.

2. Project activities

The key response priorities will includes;1.psychosocial support and trauma counselling to the survivors of Gender based violence2.Case management and case follow ups3.Establishment of a women and girls friendly space4.Awareness raising on the risks and dangers of GBV including early and forced marriages5. Capacity building Training of front line officers on keeping the environment safe and secure for women and girls6.Community dialogues with the leaders on positive cultural practices7.Distribution of dignitary kits8.Distribution of fuel efficient stoves9.Establishment of referral pathway10.Cash and voucher assistance11.Conducting an inception meeting with the local leadersGCAF will report the 5 Ws to the cluster coordination team and financial expenditures will be reported on the financial Tracking services system.
