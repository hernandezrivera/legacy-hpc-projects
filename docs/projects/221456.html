Project Summary

This project aims to prevent and end VAW/G by improving the living conditions and mental health of young widows in three conflict-affected regions (North West, South West, and the Far North) of Cameroon and also supporting internally displaced women and girls in three host communities: Yaoundé in the Centre region, Douala in the littoral, and Maroua in the far north region. The conditions of these young widows and internally displaced girls are reportedly deteriorating substantially due to numerous conflicts-related sexual violence (CRSV) that have come to exacerbate pre-existing VAW/G. The two conflicts that constitute the bases of this project and the long-term consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic have triggered massive widespread and systematic increases in violence against women and girls (VAW/G), particularly in Indigenous communities where sexual violence has not only been used as a tactic of war but also as a means to further propagate the marginalization and discrimination of young girls who are dropped from school and sent off in marriage to solve the precarious financial situations in families. The issue of child marriage rather compounds and exacerbates protracted humanitarian and economic crises than solves it. It creates further young widows who face significant obstacles to access essential services and the justice system or even stand the constant threats of retaliation from terrorist groups. The IDP girls, on the other hand, mostly live in urban slums and are confronted with severe inadequate resources to meet their housing, food, and health needs. The mortality rate linked to health challenges is reportedly on the increase due to ignorance of their Sexual and Reproductive Health Rights (SRHR). The fact that these IDP women live in deplorable slum conditions further contributes to the increasing incidence of sexual exploitation, rape, STIs, HIV/AIDS, and unwanted pregnancies. Limited access to essential economic and social services contributes to the challenges of extreme stress disorders and trauma among these young female IDPs.
To both target groups of this project, the constant attacks by armed NSAGs continue to intensify and exacerbate pre-existing gender inequalities and harmful social norms, thus amplifying the human rights violations such as harassment, sexual assault, forced displacements, forced marriages, and widowhood rites already faced by marginalized women and girls.
The project will empower the young widows survivors of the armed conflicts and acts of terrorism by providing them with access to comprehensive specialist services that include community mental health support and training on income-generating activities; establishing community safe spaces for IDP girls and young women at risk of sexual exploitation in their host communities to receive monthly empowerment sessions; creating young widow multi-dimensional cooperatives for thrift and loan schemes; and linking these cooperative schemes with multi-stakeholder service providers, government officials, and local council support structures. The project will also foster peer-to-peer learning and collective advocacy to end the violence against women in the context of the ongoing crisis in Cameroon. There will be awareness-raising actions on the need to support young widow survivors produced from cooperatives through community plays and local media programs. The target beneficiaries will be trained on hands-on skills for the development of livelihood support and entrepreneurship. The project will not only generate an increase in access to SRHR resources, information, and services for young widows and IDP girls; it will also bring solutions to the mental health challenges faced by the young widows and IDP girls. Creation and implementation of women’s cooperatives will lead to stronger, more inclusive movements for women’s entrepreneurships and incidentally impact the spread of SRHR knowledge and awareness.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

This project wants to prevent and end VAW/G, by improving the living conditions and mental health of young widows in three conflict-affected regions ( North West, South West and the Far North) of Cameroon and also support internally displaced women and girls in three host communities of Yaounde in the Centre region, Douala in the Littoral and Maroua in the Far North Region. This will be done through improving access for women and girls to essential, specialist, safe and adequate multisector services. The armed conflict in the two English Speaking regions of Cameroon and the Boko Haram insurgencies in the Far North of the country have had consequences on adolescent women and girl’s adverse SRH outcomes, such as unintended pregnancies, transactional sex, rapes, unsafe abortions, and maternal morbidity and death. These human-made humanitarian crisis, as with any armed conflict, has led to the loss of lives and property, internal displacements of the population from remote villages to major cities and out of the region, a disruption of the healthcare and educational systems, and a deterioration of the conditions of living and well-being of the populations affected especially for women and young girls. The project targets two main categories of crisis affected women and girls: 1) Young WidowsBoth the anglophone conflicts or the Boko Haram attacks, have resulted in many deaths, and significant destruction of critical assets, such as productive infrastructure, homes, schools and health facilities. With these constant attacks, there have been displacements of about a million people It has had a significant negative effect on human life in the three crisis regions of the project. About 6,000 fatalities have been registered for the Anglophone crisis and another ……………. relating to the Boko Haram attacks. The majority of these registered fatalities are young men. The widows that this project targets are the wives of such men victimized by the crisis. Apart from the general multiple challenges they face as widows, including limited healthcare access, property rights, social support, and harmful traditional practices, impacting their well-being across various dimensions, such widows suffer other multi-dimensional challenges and additionally face specific forms of discrimination and hardship that limit their opportunities to access justice, education for their children or even make decisions about their reproductive and family life. There is also experienced some form of specific violence relating to their sexual mental health and emotional stability. 2) Internally Displaced Young GirlsAccording to the October 2023 report from OCHA Cameroon, at least 3,400 new cases of internally displaced persons were recorded in September and October 2023 from the NW and SW regions. This number can be added to already existing IDPs living in urban slums in Douala, Yaounde and Maroua. With the impact of the Covid 19 pandemic and the frequent menace from cholera out breaks, most of the cases of deaths reportedly linked to urban slum dwellers, the living conditions of these Internally Displaced Girls are reportedly deteriorating substantially thus, exacerbating the pre-existing VAW/G they already face. The underfunding of humanitarian aid continues to deprive thousands of these women in deplorable living conditions who lack the barest means of livelihood support to carter for food, health and shelter. To most of these girls, the disruption of healthcare and hygiene services, unsafe shelter and constant displacement, socio-economic regression with lack of skills and start-up capitals, doubly jeopardize their humanitarian crisis and expose them to the trauma of sexual exploitation, recruited to work in hotels as sex workers, and other forms of human rights violations. To be independent and regain their human values, they would need to be empowered economically and supported psychologically to increase their resilience to mental health deficiencies.
