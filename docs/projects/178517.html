Project Summary

UNHCR will provide WASH services in locations receiving returnees, through the repair and rehabilitation of water supply networks and support to sewage and solid waste management systems. Sector Objective 1: Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria. The project will assist 332,800 individual /66,560 households.
Due consideration will be given to ensure that WASH facilities are equally accessible to both returnees and affected host communities. Through provided support to target populations the project will reduce the number of persons exposed to the risks of displacement mainstream and reduce risks of GBV and PSEA. The network of over 2,800 outreach volunteers will ensure greater outreach to people in remote and hard-to-reach areas, for community engagement and consultations on project implementation to prevent SGBV and PSEA. Various participatory methods including FGDs and community/household visits will be used to consult with Syrian IDPs, returnees and host community. UNHCR works on raising awareness among UNHCR and Partner staff on PSEA, through basic PSEA trainings organized by UNHCR periodically ( 82 partner staff trained in 2019).
Partners will be strongly encouraged to provide input to the 4W regularly and in timely manner.

4 - Needs

The destruction or damage to water supply systems, combined with the displacement and drain of qualified technical WASH staff, have decreased communities’ access to safe water. According to the 2021 Needs and Response Summary, 36% of assessed households rely on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs. Despite some improvements over the past year, at least 70% of sewage is untreated and at least half of the sewage systems are either not functional or only partially functional, leading to significant community-level health risks. About 12 per cent of garbage inappropriately disposed, the WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant repair and operational support (incl. capacity building for operation and maintenance of infrastructure and on-grid electricity supply).

5 - Implementation of activities

To support areas that have shown spontaneous return and empowerment of the host community, UNHCR will work with other partners in water and sanitation sector by implementing a simple sanitation programme in returnee areas. This programme will include different types of activities such as replacing broken pipes connecting residential buildings, schools or clinics to the main sewer system network, repairing damaged inspection rooms or increasing the number of inspection rooms to ensure better capacity. Operation, maintenance and solid waste management systems (municipal and medical) will be supported in addition to providing the necessary material to reduce overall public health risks and environmental pollution caused by the deterioration of solid waste management system. Coordination will be maintained with other actors to perform integrated projects to support shelter objectives in coordination with local authorities and WASH Sector. In doing so, due consideration will be given to ensuring that WASH facilities are equally accessible to both returnees and host communities. Comprehensively, UNHCR will support a total of 256,800 persons of concern through the distribution of 480 communal solid waste bins & repair of 24,000 linear meters of sewage networks. An additional 76,000 persons will benefit from interventions to improve access to water supply. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk 2: Theft of WASH rehabilitation and construction materials, such as generators, pipes, pumps, etc. during the implementation of WASH projects. Mitigating measures: UNHCR and it is partners will engage with the local authorities and the community to ensure local ownership of the project and promote cost recovery systems for longer term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. Coordination with MOLAE to ensure water and sanitation systems are functional after completion of the projects. Risk 3: Hazards relating to mishandling of WASH materials, such as chlorine and gasoline, resulting in explosion and human casualty Mitigating measures: Safety training to partner staff. Strict management to ensure full compliance with relevant SOPs.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to mandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following year.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. Risk 2: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow for more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning and implementation in response to market changes, such as allowing for increased price and reduced target.
