Project Summary

The project will ensure equitable, comprehensive and essential primary health care services. The project will provide direct clinical service inclusive of basic primary healthcare, reproductive health, and referral services. Clinicians at supported health facilities will be trained on child protection basic concepts, child safeguarding, child rights, confidentiality and on how to be child/adolescent-friendly providers. The scope of services will include preventative and curative services aligned with the essential package of primary health care services for Cox’s Bazar as agreed with the ISCG health sector working group.

Needs

The key health needs for the Rohingya and host communities are very similar; prevention and treatment of communicable diseases, antenatal and post-natal care services, emergency obstetric care services, reproductive and child health, and Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) clinical case management. The Rohingya refugee children, their care-givers and many others have witnessed or have suffered resulting in potential physical or mental health implications which require specialised care. While health facility utilization is within the normal range of 2-4 consultations per person per year (Sphere standards), critical gaps remain in access to 24/7 services with each functional 24/7 primary health care center serving on average 54,000 refugees (standard 1:20,000). Primary health care services in the camps are unable to service everyone in need. Hygiene and living conditions are poor which increases the health risks of the population. Immunisation coverage is very low (<3%) among Rohingya prior to the resulting in a high risk of vaccine preventable disease outbreaks .Sexual and reproductive health needs of refugee women require urgent support: approximately 60,000 women are estimated to be pregnant and require basic or comprehensive obstetric care. There are currently multiple communicable disease outbreaks within the Rohingya and the host population, confirming the understanding that the current setting is a “perfect storm” for disease outbreaks.

Activities or outputs

1. Recruit and train clinical and non-clinical staff for running the 9 health posts and PHCCs2. Upgrade and rehabilitate the health facilities to semi durable structures3. Procure pharmaceuticals, medical supplies, equipment and non-medical supplies4. Support referral mechanisms for patients to access secondary care facilities5. Ensure community engagement aligning to the endorsed Community Health Working Group’s guidelines: recruitment, training and supportive supervision of community health volunteers and traditional birth attendants as safe motherhood promoters6. Integrate WASH, Nutrition and MHPSS activities into the health facilities and PHCC, including the maintenance of the Diarrhoel Treatment Centre in Camp 21 7. Improve accountability to the beneficiaries through utilization of the complaints, feedback and response mechanisms, and the Child Safeguarding Policy
