Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect the livelihoods, enhance the resilience, productive capacity and enhance the food security of vulnerable farmers and herders who are living in the Gaza Strip. The objective is drawn from the following Country Strategic Objectives and FSS activity:
• Country Strategic Objective #3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
• FSS Objective #1: Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced.
Food security in Palestine continues to be affected by protracted nature of the crisis, repeated shocks and continued restriction of freedom of movement, mainly for the most vulnerable families under the national social safety net (SSN), in term and type of frequency assistance. The project focuses on the most vulnerable, including small-scale farming households who live in the most vulnerable communities and have been affected by the ongoing deterioration of the situation, the recent hostilities during August 2022 and/or COVID-19 crisis in Gaza Strip.
The project aims to support affected communities in Gaza by rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and providing time critical productive inputs and tailored technical support, for small scale women and men farmers, breeders and water wells and improving their production through using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience, support to non-agricultural livelihoods. The activities are designed based on standards adopted by technical working groups of the FSS, and in line with the accountability to affected populations (AAP) with respecting the rights, dignity and protection of all segments of affected communities.
The project will provide support to the livelihoods of 2,520 individuals (individuals, based on the average size 5.6) across the Gaza Strip, representing approximately 1,243 women and girls and 1,277 men and boys through In-Kind assistance modality with the project overall budget of 1,404,600 USD. All the activities are closely coordinated with FSS, MoA, stakeholders and local and international NGOs in the targeted locations. Moreover, and as part of cross cutting activities, women’s role and participation at a community level will be promoted through active engagement with local project committees and local council committees.
To strengthen its accountability toward beneficiaries and partners, CARE applies and has an easily accessible, community-based Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM), which also attends to more sensitive, SEA-related complaints. CARE has a global policy on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection explicitly outlining unacceptable behavior, and investigation processes, and follow up, as well as the Safeguarding Code of Conduct. All project’s team will receive an orientation on the PSEA related issues.

1 - Needs

Food security in Palestine continues to be affected by prolonged conflict, economic stagnation, restrictions on trade and access to resources, and high unemployment and poverty rates. Lack of means to afford nutritious and healthy food and significant consumption gap due to the uneven economic recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic and widespread supply chain disruption caused by the Ukraine/Russia crisis are among the main drivers of food insecurity. The inability to overcome the consumption gap pushes the affected population to use negative coping strategies, sometimes already exhausted, thereby increasing the number of vulnerable persons. About 1.8 million individuals (33.6% of the population) suffer from moderate to severe food Insecurity. In 2022, approximately 1 out of 3 people experienced some form of food insecurity, 84% (1.54 million people) of which are expected to need humanitarian assistance. 77% of those in need of humanitarian assistance are in the Gaza Strip (1.19 million people). People with a female-headed household are found to be more in need of assistance than people headed by males (29% vs 26%), HNO 2023. In Gaza Strip, the recurring hostilities have exacerbated already the dire living conditions, undermined by years of blockade, the ongoing wage crisis of PA and de-facto civil servants which have had a severe disruptive impact on the entire food system, including producers, processors, marketers, transporters and consumers. Small producers have been hit by the reduced demand, the unprecedented challenges in securing basic inputs, and the impediments in accessing credit and markets. Nearly 64.4% of Gaza population are food insecure (WFP 2022) and the unemployment rate reached 44.1% (PCBS 2022). With already 53% of people reported living below the poverty line, poverty rates will increase further if this situation persists (HNO 2023). Farmer communities in Gaza face challenges in their protection, livelihoods, and resilience due to ongoing crisis, blockades, and climate change shocks. In Sept.2022, our local partner MA’AN have conducted a quick needs assessment that included several meetings and discussions with targeted communities’ members whose livelihoods had been affected during the last military aggression on Gaza Strip including PWD, male and female herders/farmers from different age groups were engaged like adolescents, youth and elderly where their inputs guided the design of the intervention. The assessment resulted that the damages in agricultural productive assets and the occurred economic loss for the targeted communities affected tangibly their livelihoods; besides, no interventions have been conducted to support them to restore their livelihoods according to the farmers. Part of the damaged farmers & breeders resumed partially their farming or breeding activities, while others reported that their farming and breeding activities are totally disrupted because of the occurred damages. According to the MoA in Gaza Strip, the last hostilities in August 2022 hit tens of agricultural lands, greenhouses, livestock, and poultry farms, causing damages and losses estimated at $1m. The significant shortages of electricity have left 2 million residents of Gaza with just a few hours of power a day, turning many aspects of ever women, men, boys’ and girls’ life miserable and raising additional deterioration to the already impoverished Gazan. This has had a direct impact on the delivery of services, including water supply, sanitation, health, education and agriculture. The overall guiding principle for CARE Palestine (WBG) theory of change is adopting an impact driven approach, acknowledging that our impact groups (women, girls, boys and youth) require emergency and early recovery supports, however, CARE will utilize interventions that address both immediate needs, as well as the underlying drivers of those needs. CARE focuses on restoring the capacity of vulnerable farming communities and livestock holders and enhancing the ability of women and men farmers to recover from the protracted crises and cope with the environmental shocks. Based on CARE various reflections and learning, the gender transformative programming, female Gazans negative experiences of the crisis are compounded by social norms which inhibit their access to food, inputs, finance and livelihoods’ services, as well as lower control over income and household decision making. CARE had coordinated with the MoA and FSS to identify the priority needs to support the agriculture sector in Gaza Strip and to provide for the restoration of the livelihoods of the women and men farmers and breeders. The identified priorities included rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for small-scale farmers including the open field and the greenhouses, poultry farms rehabilitation including the provision of renewable power supply, rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs for livestock farms, rehabilitation of the irrigation water wells and water carrier lines as a complementary activity for wells and land rehabilitation activities, and the rehabilitation of renewable power supply systems of the irrigation water wells. According to the conducted consultations with MoA/FSS team, the targeted areas were identified including all Gaza strip governorates. The intervention builds on CARE/MAAN cumulative experiences and comprehensive assessment. The lack of possibilities to rebuild these agricultural infrastructures leave women and men households unable to provide neither for their own consumption, nor for income generation.

2 - Activities

The intervention will aim to restore and enhance the resilience, food security, productive capacity of vulnerable households in Gaza to protract their livelihoods considering the local impact of climate change, COVID-19 pandemic and the last escalation in Gaza. The project was designed in coordination with many actors including MoA, FSS, and other stakeholders (as per FSS coordination mechanisms 2023) in order to avoid duplication and maximize the number of beneficiaries who are in need. The project aims to support affected communities in Gaza Strip by rehabilitation and provision of productive assets & inputs, for small scale women & men farmers, breeders & water wells including promotion of technical capacities and improving their production using Build Back Better approach for increased resilience. The activities are designed based on needs of FSS HRP 2023 priorities for Gaza, and in line with the accountability to affected populations (AAP) with respecting the rights, dignity and protection of all segments of affected communities. The activities will be implemented in partnership with MAAN who will be responsible of the technical implementation at the field level. CARE will be responsible of the overall quality management, technical support in certain areas like marketing, gender and protection, GBV, PSEA, ME, reporting, as well as for the feedback and complaint mechanism, and will act as the main contact for donors and stakeholders. CARE will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of communities and beneficiaries with line ministries and with organizations work in the same area. The project team will finalize, validate the selection criteria per activity to ensure transparency. The criteria will look into socioeconomic status/vulnerability, livelihood conditions, affected people from the last escalation and COVID-19, access, small-scale farmers, women headed households, PWD, availability of water, availability of agricultural services, previous benefit from similar projects. All the project target, measurement and methodology will be disaggregated by Age, gender and diversity. The activities will be as follows Activities O #1: Lands Rehabilitation 1: Conduct a baseline assessment/selection of end beneficiaries. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, disability and vulnerabilities. 3: Cleaning, ploughing & installing the irrigation system. 4: Distributing farming manual tools, accessories, local fertilizers, seeds/seedling ($1400/beneficiary). Activities O #2: Greenhouses Rehabilitation1: Conduct a baseline assessment/selection of end beneficiaries. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, disability and vulnerabilities. 3: Cleaning the greenhouses & installing plastic sheeting covers, pipes and insect mesh. 4: Installations of the irrigation system & distributing fertilizers ($1200/beneficiary). Activities O #3: Poultry Farm Rehabilitation1: Conduct a baseline assessment/selection of end beneficiaries. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, disability, and vulnerabilities. 3: Rehabilitating the poultry farms ($2000/beneficiary). 4: Installing solar system (1 kw) for the targeted poultry farms. 4: Provide extension services in coordination with MoA.Activities O #4: Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs for Livestock farms 1: Conduct a baseline assessment/selection of livestock/shed farms. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, disability and vulnerabilities. 3: Rehabilitating livestock for female & male farmers and provision of productive assets & inputs for the farms ($2000/beneficiary). Activities will include improving the structural design, building partitions, installing feeders, drinkers & drainage systems and introducing manure management. 4: Provide extension services in coordination with MoA. Veterinary services will be provided to the farms (4 visits/farm). Activities O #5: Rehabilitation of Water Wells and Carrier Lines.1: Conduct a baseline assessment/selection of end localities & beneficiaries in close coordination with the MoA as main source of water wells identification. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying the localities &in close coordination with MoA. 3: Rehabilitating water wells & installing carrier lines as complementary activity to water wells & land rehabilitation in order to improve access to water for many farmers. Activities O #6: Installation/Rehabilitation of renewable power supply for Irrigation water wells. 1: Conduct a baseline assessment/selection of end localities & beneficiaries. 2: Conduct needs assessments to identifying the areas of damages. 3: Installing/rehabilitating the solar system for the water wells (20-25 kw) as per their outlet capacity needs.The main selection criteria for all the above activities will consider the socio-economic conditions and the damages occurred due to last escalation in Gaza. All the activities, selection process and extension services will be conducted in close coordination with MoA/FSS. CARE Palestine’s confidential feedback & accountability mechanism (FAM) designed incorporating the Core Humanitarian Standards on Quality and Accountability, and CARE’s own Feedback and Accountability Working Group’s 2020 Guidance, will be shared with the communities and the teams, enabling targeted population to reach out to CARE. The FAM ensures that the interests and/or issues of the beneficiaries are continuously addressed safely in a timely manner and are in line with CARE's 'Do No Harm' approach and Humanitarian Accountability Framework, which protect CARE's beneficiaries from any unintended harm caused by the project implementation, & it enables community members to provide feedback that will be used to improve the services. CARE/MAAN staff also distribute the FAM contact cards, so any feedback/complaints can be brought forth directly to CARE.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

CARE is committed to assuring accountability to stakeholders in all aspects and phases of the project. At the inception stage, community members helped inform the design of project activities, the composition of the teams, type of the support, urgent issues/needs, etc. CARE took into consideration the different needs of the different groups residing in the targeted communities (sex and age). Monitoring has been carried out through the on-sight monitoring of distributions and activities, and it always includes segregation of data by sex and age (SADD). Partners follow the same monitoring guidelines and reporting. CARE’s Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM) builds on our previous experience with humanitarian accountability, and it is in-formed by standards and guidance to ensure quality and accountability to our partners and stakeholders, and to support existing organizational and technical standards, with which to bring about sustainable change to communities in need. It was designed and piloted in other projects within CARE allowing targeted population to reach out to CARE for any feedback/complaint about the project activities, criteria, inputs, or behavior by any of the project’s staff (CARE and Partners). CARE will continue to raise community awareness regarding FAM, regularly reporting on feedback/complaints as part of CARE’s continuous learning agenda. CARE has started the distribution of the FAM cards in targeted communities and started to receive feedback/complaints via identified emails and phone numbers. Feedback/complaints are documented in a log sheet by the Focal Point / Accountability Officer responsible for monitoring the progress of each input until the feedback loop is closed. The data will be reported and used for management decisions and analyzing trends. CARE also is planning to conduct a community-based survey that will further assess accessibility of the mechanism, as well as preferred channels of communication.
