Project Summary

In 2019, the increase in threats and attacks by armed groups in Cameroon's Far North Region continued to lead to major population movements, increased difficulties in access to basic social services for children and lack of opportunities for adolescents and youth, and further weakened community ties.
According to the latest DTM report at the time of writing this proposal (Round 19, August 2019), the displaced population is estimated at 428,289 individuals in the Far North region (270,870 IDPs, 46,845 out-of-camp refugees from Nigeria and 110,574 returnees). It is estimated that children account for 62% of the displaced population.
With the majority of populations in need being children internally displaced, returnees, but also children from communities hosting IDPs and returnees, the child protection (CP) response needs to be maintained in order to continue to assist new children returning to Far North Region taking into account that separation from family members continue to take place and affect children, protect all those vulnerable from violent extremism and gender-based violence but also assist in their reinsertion into their communities while mistrust, stigmatization and suspicion of collaboration and affiliation with Boko Haram are still present.
The key result of the project which is in line with the objectives of the protection sector, is that 181,365 conflict-affected children among whom 92,496 girls, are protected from violence, abuse and exploitation and live in a protective environment in the targeted localities of in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava, and Mayo Tsanaga Divisions of the Far-North Region of Cameroon.
The project builds on previously established dynamics and community structures and will provide psychosocial support (PSS) to 181,365 children, 92,496 girls and 88,869 boys.
During the child friendly spaces activities, partners will continue to identify unaccompanied and separated children (based on previous trends and on partners capacities, the project plans to assist around 263 UASC including 60% of new cases), ensuring and providing those children with PSS, alternative care arrangements with the capacity building of foster families, reunification with their families of origin and reinsertion in their communities. All will benefit from close individual follow-up.
To address both social norms but also to ensure smooth community reinsertion, the project will work at strengthening community mobilization and will organize community activities around prevention of gender-based violence including activities to abandon child marriage, intergenerational dialogue, social cohesion. Based on the projections, a total of 362,700 persons (women, men, girls, boys) are planned to be reached through community mobilization activities. Partnership with religious and traditional leaders, and chiefery including female stakeholders will be an important channel to display messages and address complex issues. In complement, the work on youth empowerment and youth participation will enable to merge with the other members of the communities and promote prevention and protection of children from violence and exploitation.
Following up on strategic planning with Far North regional decentralized and deconcentrated authorities, the issue of conflict-affected children without a birth certificates came out strongly.
The project aims at supporting 9,000 conflict-affected in obtaining a birth certificate.
165 children suspected of association with armed groups are expected to be identified, will receive PSS, and will be supported for family and community reintegration.
UNICEF will continue to closely work with civil society implementing partners, providing them with capacity building, supporting the setting up SOPs for case management and referral. UNICEF will also strengthen the coordination mechanism led by the Regional Delegation of Social Affairs ensuring a merging with justice and law enforcement authorities.

Besoins / Needs

NEEDS In 2019, the increase in threats and attacks by non state armed groups (NSAG) in Far North continued to lead to major population movements, increased difficulties in access to basic social services for children and lack of opportunities for adolescents/youth, and further weakened community ties. Children represent about 62% of IDPs in the region. Due to insecurity, many children had to flee, sometimes witnesses or victims of violence; involving high levels of stress and, thus, potentially, significant negative consequences for their cognitive and emotional development. Family separations due to sudden displacements continue to occur, although to a lesser extent than at the beginning of the crisis. Abduction and recruitment of children by NSAG continues to be a major issue, particularly for girls aged 9 to 13 and boys aged 10 to 17. In addition, children who were formerly associated with NSAG face real difficulties in reintegrating into their communities. Adolescent girls (10-17) report a wide range of acts of violence. They face sexual harassment and violence, as well as kidnapping and forced marriage by NSAG. Most of the girls who have managed to escape from NSAG are either pregnant or accompanied by children born from sexual violence perpetrated by NSAG. They face stigmatization and are sometimes forced by their own families and communities to marry their abusers. Finally, children without birth certificates face significant difficulties in accessing education. Although this problem affects both girls and boys, girls are more vulnerable to it because of the burdens or social norms that limit girls' rights to access formal education. This situation also raises the issue of their protection in the event of abuse, violence or exploitation, where the issue of age determination is crucial for better protection and adequate PSS. It also exposes them to child marriages, which are a major problem in the region, aggravated by the crisis since 2013.
