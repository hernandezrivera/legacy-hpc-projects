Project Summary

This project is proposed to be implemented and adapted using continuous assessment and learning to understand the wider environment and build on existing initiatives, programmes and services to address the psychosocial, economic and social needs of the target beneficiaries in selected locations in Yobe State. This will also entail a holistic need-based response at both individual, community and structural levels, working with a range of stakeholders to ensure conflict-sensitive, equitable, efficient and sustainable reintegration of affected persons in their host communities. Furthermore, the programme aims to empower returnees to attain self-sufficiency, social stability and psychosocial well-being to pursue their individual and collective aspirations and build resilience and resistance against the drivers of future shocks. Through tailored activities and empowerment initiatives, we would support returnees, their families and communities to take ownership of their reintegration process through active participation at all levels of implementation.
To equitably respond to the priority needs of the beneficiaries, YOPCODI would constructively and consistently engage and build trusting relationships with returnees to help them navigate through their challenges. This shall involve identifying and responding to their individual and collective vulnerabilities to reduce their exposure to social and economic risks and enhance their opportunities to accessing productive and sustainable livelihoods. Essentially, we would adopt a participatory approach to encourage local ownership of the reintegration process in the best interest of both the returnees and their host communities. To achieve this, the programme would engage relevant stakeholders at various levels to support, tolerate, accommodate and integrate returnees into existing community structures and systems, thereby combating discrimination, stigmatization and improving social cohesion to achieve sustainable peace and collective prosperity. Based on beneficiaries’ interests, availability of inputs, Market viability and feasibility, targeted Women and Men returnees would be empowered with vocational skills to engage in income generation activities (IGAs), Rain-fed and irrigation farming at individual and cooperative levels as well as communal asset creation, to help them recover lost livelihood opportunities and build resilience against future migration. YOPCODI would also work closely with other community, LGA and state level partners and stakeholders to improve access to social services (Education, Healthcare and WASH) for both returnees and their communities of return, including the provision of structured psychosocial support services.
Our approach in a nutshell, would be participatory, beneficiary and community-centred as well as inclusive. The key innovation is the adoption of Human Rights Based Approach in all layers of its implementation.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in North-East Nigeria has become protracted, with the populace bearing the brunt of the conflict that has resulted in widespread displacement, destroyed infrastructure, and collapsed basic social services. Threats of attacks by non-state armed groups and restrictions in movements continue to have negative impacts on trade, livelihoods and markets, leaving a substantial proportion of the population relying on humanitarian assistance. As of June 2022, 2,012,704 people are still displaced in the three most affected states - Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States – with 80 percent of the displaced population being women and children.The impact on freedom of movement, livelihoods, markets, and humanitarian access have resulted in high levels of food insecurity and malnutrition across the region: Northeast Nigeria, Niger, Chad and Northern Cameroon. The IPC Acute Malnutrition Analysis September 2021 – August 2022 conducted in November 2021 shows a deteriorating nutrition situation with the number of people in need of treatment increasing by from 1,148,906 in 2021 (lean season) to 1,376,363 in 2022 (postharvest data) which translate to about 18.6% increase despite the difference in season. The recent Cadre Harmonise analysis conducted in March 2022 and the FEWSNET Nigeria Food Security Outlook March 2022 – January 2023 suggested deterioration of the nutrition and food security situation with more people facing crisis or worse. The CH analysis shows that 3,536,991 people were in crisis or worse for the period of March-May 2022, which was posited to increase to 4,647,602 people in crisis for the period of June-August 2022. These numbers are similar to the numbers seeing in 2016/17 at the peak of the crisis before the massive scale-up of 2017/18. The situation is worse for the 1,110,166 people living in inaccessible areas with 689,126 expected to be in crisis for the period of October-December 2021 which will increase to 867,470 for the period of June to August 2022. This is mainly attributed to sustained conflict associated with the insurgents coupled with the COVID-19 pandemic and associated restrictions.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure gender sensitivity, promote equity and embed sustainability measures through the project, YOPCODI would critically take into cognizance the rights, needs, aspirations and priorities of both men, women and children from the design to the implementation of all proposed activities. Age appropriateness in the allocation of activities and delivery of responses would be a key consideration throughout the interventions. We would also institute formidable social safeguard mechanisms to all forms prevent gender-based violence (GBV) amongst beneficiaries of the programme. Gender awareness and education would be embedded into the design and delivery of individual and communal asset creation activities. Women especially, would be empowered with marketable skills and expand their access to resources to enable them have agency to prevent themselves from abuse through both traditional norms and practices as well poverty induced violation of their rights and dignity.
