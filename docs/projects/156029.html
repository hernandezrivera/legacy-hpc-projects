Project Summary

- The Project is proposed to increase the education chances for children in Syria whom are suffering from war and its effects, Space of Hope NGO will do the needful to basically rehabilitate 7 schools in western rural Aleppo to accommodate 3000 students (Boys & Girls) for the age range between 6-14 years old in order of Increase safe and equitable access to formal and non formal education for crisis affected children and youth.
- Awareness campaigns for parents and children as well will be provided continuously, along with a specialized team which will be responsible for the regular educational activities during the school year.
- Also the project will promote quality teaching, lifesaving and life sustaining messages through training of teachers and provide incentives for them.
- in addition to the trainings that the teachers will receive, they will have a constant salaries to contribute in the continuity of the education process .
- In addition to enhance the children's Psychological condition, PSS specialists/ social workers will be dedicated to these schools in order to monitor, report & manage cases.
- Stationary will be provided for 3000 students (Boys & Girls) in order of enhancing the education quality and encourage out of school children to continue their education.

Needs

In the 1st of September 2018, Space of Hope (SoH) M&E team conducted a needs assessment for education sector in western and northern countryside of Aleppo governorate in eleven Sub-Districts, called Atareb, ABzmo, Kfr ammah, Kfr taal, Tqad, Big urem, Khan al Asal, Maree, Dabiq, albel camp and Mrg ehtmilat camp . The assessment was launched in order to determine the level of special needs of students and out of school children affected by the Syrian crisis, the overall education situations, the percentages of the children drop out of schools, Findings indicate that these areas contains approximately 50-57% IDPs from southern Syria and from the camps located in the northern of Idleb governance , which are in need in several services including Education, 25% of the children are out of schools for a duration of 6 months - two years , and 50% of the students are at risk of dropping out of schools due to the lack of services provided for the schools in these areas.7 of those areas in the westren countryside of Aleppo contains 32 schools, only 10 schools are supported and the others are partly functioning due to the lack of support, which puts the students at a high risk of dropping out .KIs suggested some activities to be conducted if there is a possibility of supporting pre-existing schools , identified as below:• providing salaries for educational staff.• providing trainings for the teachers • distributing stationary and textbooks • rehabilitating some parts of the schools and wash facilities According to the KIs and analysis of the assessment results, the needs for supporting existing schools is highly recommended in order of guaranteeing the continuity of educational process. Many of the same needs were addressed in the HNO report of 2019 and the other reports from other cluster members shows that the need for supported schools is wild.

Implementation of activities

SOH will prepare a training plan, an activity plan and a monitoring plan and will share these with the staff at the start of the project.SOH will have overall responsibility for the project, including technical advice, monitoring of outputs and reporting.1- the education activities : The main activities of the project are:• Provide children and youth with formal education from 1-12 grades • Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns • Rehabilitate and construct gender-sensitive WASH facilities• Provide students with learning materials (stationary, pens, notebooks), school bags and school in a Carton kit in formal and non-formal settings• Provide professional development to teaching staff (child-centred and protective pedagogy, active learning, self-learning, life-skills, Curriculum B, ECCE, New Curriculum, psychosocial support and referral mechanisms, Education in Emergencies (EiE) and national/INEE minimum standards (MS))• Provide teachers and education personnel with incentives• Provide children with school-based psychosocial support programs • Provide children with recreational materials • Parent Teacher Associations (PTA) supported or established2- the protection sector: SOH has a separate protection program and one of the strongest partner of UNICEF in this field, the PSS activities will be provided by the specialized staff of our protection program where they will be visiting schools weekly bases and they will have a case manager that will monitor the behaviors of students and provide the needed support /referrals. the PSS cases will be transferred to our centers in Atareb district (we have 4 CFS centers fully equipped with materials and specialized staff ), other referrals will be as the pathways states in the protection cluster (where we had a part of developing these pathways )3- the monitoring plan : SoH team will focus on monitoring the activities on daily basis, field reports will be provided to ensure smooth and quality of the project’s implementation. We will commit to the OCHA’s format and timetable in our project’s reports. Monitoring tools will ensure the participation of students (males and females) and community members (males and females) and local councils in the monitoring and evaluation process. Monitoring tools will include: interviews, questionnaires, opinion boxes and discussion groups focused with kids, community and other stakeholders as needed. Obtained results will be presented and discussed with the concerned authorities in this area, which includes beneficiaries and partners in a transparent manner. We will be holding in the middle of the project an Internal evaluation by the official monitoring and evaluation of SoH. At the end of the project we will hold an assessment to measure achieved results to compare the status quo ante, before the project’s intervention. Opinion Boxes will be placed in each school for students, to encourage them to express themselves. Here are some examples of how to monitor some of the indicators: # Students (male and female) who are enrolled in school (check lists of schools and monitoring children and their continuity in schools). # Buildings that have been processed (by monitoring the site and reports from local councils).SoH has developed several tools and approaches to ensure close monitoring of its programs including: • Weekly Activity Reports, Monthly Plan• Feedback and complaints mechanisms• Routine site monitoring visits and observation monitoring• Training Assessments• Mobile data collection

Protection Risk Analysis

- Increase risks of GBV if latrines are not separated or there are no locks on the doors,SOH organization will allocate some of the fund to rehabilitate wash facilities and establish ones specialized for girls.- Increase risks of GBV if classrooms not separated for each (boys and girls )SOH organization will establish separated classrooms for girls in order of encouraging parents to send their girls to schools, and will conduct awareness campaigns to promote the issue .

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: attack of school building or surrounding area and general violence Mitigation: move schools underground if this is determined to be a safe mitigation measure, provide safety/security equipment schools and carryout related training with period drills (training supported by the Civil Defense and health professionals); suspend school when its deemed too dangerous for children to attend. Risk: inability to move supplies/materials cross the Turkey/Syria border or on transit roads to project areas.Mitigation: when possible purchase locally and pre stock items when border/roads are accessible. Risk: looting or destruction of warehouse Mitigation: store items in numerous smaller warehouses to reduce impact of any warehouse getting affected; ensure good relations with local authorities and their support of education Risk: fighting will return to the areas of intervention, preventing the project teams from working and the beneficiaries from receiving the services.Mitigation: SOH security focal point will follow the security situation on the ground and will advise staff ofany increased risk and of any areas they should immediately leave or avoid. All project teams will carry communication equipment with themat all times and remain in contact with their security focal points or advisers. Project activities will be suspended if the security focal points consider the risks to be too great. If the fighting spreads to the project areas, and if the logistical and security situation allows, the teamsmay move to new locations and continue project activities.SOH has experience of working under such conditions and will remain fluid and adaptive, allowing for project methodologies and modalities to change, as the situation on the ground demands, also SOH has a duty of care policy that determine suitable mitigation in case of emergencies .
