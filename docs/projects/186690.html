Project Summary

YIPDI is proposing an 8-month intervention in Dikwa, Bama, Maffa, and Jere LGAs,Borno State to respond to child protection needs. The goal of the project is to reduce conflict-related risks and vulnerabilities affecting
children, adolescents and caregivers by strengthening access to a range of life-saving, child-sensitive and youth-friendly protection services reducing and responding to children’ exposure to violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect. These services include 1) providing children with quality and tailored case management services,: including support to on-going caseloads and respond to newly identified cases including children of parents affected by Covid; 2) reinforcing the provision of psychosocial support to children, adolescents and caregivers in need (including piloting of YIPDI’s remote PSS curriculum for children and adolescent (12-18 years) to improve stress coping mechanism; 3) strengthening community capacities, through training/mentoring of Community-based CP focal points and volunteers, to protect children against abuse and exploitation as well as healthrelated risks, including COVID-19, using an integrated approach with WASH and health teams.
YIPDI is recognized in the NE as a major CP actor with strong expertise and field work experience in
Maiduguri, Dikwa, Mafa and Bama and Damboa Through its work, YIPDI has developed excellent working
relationships with local authorities, and is well accepted by community members. YIPDI will use this
leverage to tailor this intervention and maximise its impact and beneficiary reach.

1 - Needs

In 2020, an estimated 1.8 million people in NE Nigeria are in need of child protection support, 1.1 million of whom are targeted in the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). The risks children face are high, and include SGBV, child marriage, child labor, recruitment, and exploitation. 1.7 million children and adolescents are in need of psychosocial support services, and a least 8,700 children affected by protection risks need alternative care provision. Borno is the most affected state, representing 77% of the subsectors’ target population. Case management services for affected children, including UASC, remain a significant gap and priority for the CPSS – in the first quarter of 2020, progress towards the targeted placement of children facing protection risks was only at 18%. These vulnerabilities have been further heightened by the outbreak of COVID-19 and associated response measures, including the closure of schools that affect mental and psychosocial wellbeing as well as increased exposure to abuse and exploitation – the integration of education and child protection support in this time is therefore crucial.

2 - Activities

Provide child protection case management (CM) services to children meeting YIPDI vulnerability criteria, implemented through a community-based approach, either remotely or face to face. Provide refresher training to Case Work Officers and volunteers on case management, safeguarding, preventing sexual exploitation and abuse, identifying children with psychological distress, new vulnerabilities/risks caused by COVID, in line with CPMS, Inter-Agency CM guidelines, and YIPDI protocols (on COVID 19 risks and mitigation measures). Mental health and psychosocial support services are provided to children, adolescents & caregivers through adapted service delivery methods and need relative to COVID 19 Conduct case management beneficiary satisfaction survey Rehabilitate existing CFS in Dikwa, Bama, Jere, and Maffa to support adult PSS activities in small groups with COVID 19 protective measures in place. Train YIPDI staff and Volunteers to support the implementation of the YIPDI remote PSS curriculum (self-guided home-based PSS during pandemics) & COVID prevention risks & control. provide structured PSS / Structured Emotional Support Group Sessions for male and female caregivers Support/ Facilitate adolescent peer learning through small group sessions to increase knowledge of community members on providing support to children with psychosocial distress. Community-based child protection and referral mechanisms are strengthened to support prevention and response to child abuse & exploitation Support CBPF (with IEC materials: posters and flyers) to deliver community awareness sessions (in small groups) on child protection - risks and mitigation to people in the community. (The sessions will be guided strictly by the COVID 19 risks and mitigation measures imposed by the state authorities) Support CBPF to take initiatives to mitigate violence against children in the community using risk/protection mapping and action to address the identified protection concerns/risks. Provide materials and technical support to CBFPs to develop and action work plans for the identification, reporting, and referral of cases (with support from the YIPDI MEAL team) Conduct monthly mentoring and coaching sessions for the 45 focal points and 3 YIPDI Community Development Officers on cases identification, documentation, and referrals Conduct refresher training for 45 CBFPs and 3 Community Development Officers on safe identification, reporting, and referrals of vulnerable and at-risk children (including children of caregivers affected by COVID 19) Train 45 Community Based Child Protection Focal Points already in place (CBFP) and 49 volunteers (PSS volunteers, casework volunteers, outreach volunteers) on COVID 19 prevention control, risk mitigation and community engagement; and existing community services available during COVID 19 The casework volunteers assist the casework officer in identifying the children and refer them for case management. This strategy is justified as Tdh must anticipate changes in the context that could prevent the staff (among which the casework officer) to access the field.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The proposed action is directly targeting defined groups to contribute to increased gender and age equality and decreasing risks and vulnerabilities associated with gender and age in the target population, as such the proposed action has been evaluated through Gender with Age Marker (GAM) Assessment and scored with GAM code 4. The gender and age have been reflected in both design phase focusing on key GEMs, specifically: A) Gender Analysis (the needs, roles and dynamics of women, girls, boys, and men in different age groups are understood), D) Tailored Activities (Women, girls, boys, and men in different age groups get the right resources), G) Influence on Project (women, girls, boys, and men in appropriate age groups influence decisions throughout the project), as well as J) Benefits (women, girls, boys, and men in different age groups get different benefits); and monitoring phase focusing on supporting GEMs (B) Collect and Analyze SADD, E) Protection from GBV,PSEA, CPMS H) Feedback & Complaints, K) Satisfaction, C) Good Targeting, F) Coordination with others , I) Transparency, and L) Project Problems) both in terms of YIPDI’s MEAL and cooperation with other partner
