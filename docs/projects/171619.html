Project Summary

This project targets drought-affected communities (women, children and men) in assessed areas of the togdheer and sanaag regions of North-West Somalia. Drought affected communities typically suffer from two types of deﬁciencies (water and fodder). The project aims to increase availability of these two resources and strengthen communities ’drought preparedness and resilience. The project will empower communities through Cash for Work (CfW), creating jobs and assets. All HARADO CfW projects employ 30% - 36% women, target youth for many of the jobs and give special attention to disadvantaged families and those who cannot participate in physical work due to disabilities or old age, as well as women with young children, with unconditional cash or alternative ways to participate in asset rehabilitation and creation will protect these communities against future shocks. It will also increase access to water sources and fodder when adversely affected by drought. The project will also assist target communities to harvest fodder and store it, with bailing systems for eﬃcient storage and use during dry seasons or drought. Beneﬁciaries can also sell fodder, if developed as a value chain product. This helps communities to diversify their incomes and bolsters the local economy. In agro-pastoral areas, the project will train 50 farmers in modern farming techniques, on the basis of the Farmer Field Schools approach, and train local extension agents selected from the communities. The project will also link these farmers to markets and promote mobile banking - Zaad services - for savings. This is crucial for long-term resilience. The project will also help communities to save local seeds for future use. The humanitarian crisis in Somalia is among the old’s most complex and protracted emergencies. Growing conﬂict across the country and simultaneous environmental hazards, including recurrent droughts, severely disrupt the livelihoods of Somalia ’s 4.7 million people. Assessments by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) and Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWSNET) (Post Gu, 2020) also indicate that, without humanitarian aid, up to 4.7 million people across Somalia (including Somaliland) will face severe hunger through December 2020. FEWSNET and FSNAU ﬁndings further anticipate the livelihood zones of Eastern Somaliland to be at high risk and to slide from phase 2 (stressed) to phase 3 (crises) or phase 4 (emergency), within the coming months. Widespread malnutrition persists. across Somalia and one million children are expected to be acutely malnourished over the next year, including 180,000 children who are likely to become severely malnourished if the humanitarian community fails to scale up its support. Finally, the analysis projects more than 4.7 million people to be internally displaced across the country. Against this background and in line with the Food Security Cluster guidelines, the project aims at providing lifesaving and resilience building assistance to the most vulnerable and affected families. The overall objective of the suggested project is that the food and livelihood security situation of vulnerable IDP and host community families in Eastern Somaliland is stabilized and communities are more resilient towards adverse weather conditions and conﬂict. In order to achieve the objective, the project offers: a) Conditional Cash Transfers for improved access to food and b) agriculture and livelihood support activities (trainings, quality seeds support) c) Support by rehabilitation of existing water sources d) fodder training support to increase fodder availability for livestock in target locations. All the initiatives will boost individual and collective resilience. The project will target 12,750 beneﬁciaries (men, women, boys and girls) and will have duration of 12 months (January 2021 –December 2021) to allow families to recover from adverse weather conditions.

Project Activities

Component 1: Emergency Conditional Cash for at least 100 households. The proposed project will support food insecure pastoral and agro-pastoral households with conditional cash transfers. This will be delivered on a monthly basis for at least Six Months. Conditional cash transfers through Cash for Work (CfW) will be delivered in the light of recurrent droughts, where it has become apparent that the collective resilience of drought-affected communities should be fostered. In close consultation with affected communities and village leaders, communal assets such as rangelands will be selected for rehabilitation through cash for work in preparation for the rains. In addition, the CfW component is aimed at providing short-term employment opportunities to people who are able to work. The cash values to be transferred on a monthly basis are provided by the Cash Working Group (CWG) for Somalia based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB). The MEB takes into consideration a minimum culturally adjusted mix of basic food items and non-food items (NFI) a Somali family requires per month. The latest cash transfer values for families in Togdheer are USD 75 and USD 100 for Sanaag. Component 2: Agricultural Support In order to discourage displacement and keep households productive on their land, agro-pastoral households will be supported with farming inputs at opportune times based on the farming season. Such inputs will include quality seeds to plant and trainings in agricultural best practices. At least 50 pastoral households will be supported with training and seeds support. Component 3: fodder production support that includes training in fodder and distribution of fodder production tools will be given to at least 50 households in the target villages. In total, the project will directly support approximately 2,125 vulnerable households, 12,750 (Men, Women, Boys and Girls) within pastoral and agro-pastoral villages classiﬁed to be in IPC 3 and 4 in Eastern Somaliland. Component 4: rehabilitation/construction of water resources a) Support desilting of 3 big dams with networks of mini system. b) Rehabilitate/construct 5 shallow wells. This includes approximately 1,800 households who will beneﬁt from the rehabilitated communal assets. Post Distribution Monitoring: At the midterm phase of the project, especially after the distribution of animals and the ﬁrst round of Cash transfers, a post- distribution monitoring survey will be conducted to evaluate the satisfaction levels of the beneﬁciaries.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash based interventions have been used in Somalia by the humanitarian organizations since 003. using cash based intervention is more important in consideration to the standards set by the cash working group for Somalia. Apart from this, Justification for using cash or vouchers is to empower needy beneficiaries to have their basic household needs, purchasing power and repay loans they incurred during hardships. this will also empower them to restore their communal products assets affected by either droughts or locust breeding. in addition to that, using cash or vouchers will enable beneficiaries to have job opportunities in the project period.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project is designed to be part of new business process model for cash based transfers to the local markets to sustain local market functionality in compliance with market functionality index (MFI). The project is mainly designed to describe the key dimensions of Assortment of essential goods, Availability, Price, Resilience of supply chains, Market competition, Infrastructure, Services, Food quality, and Access and protection.

Mitigation Measures

To overcome the identiﬁed risks, HARADO will undertake the following standard measures: 1. Ensure that beneﬁciary registration targets the most invisible groups in the community, considering the power relations, and the speciﬁc needs of any group according to age, gender, and disability in order to avoid targeting and registration exacerbating vulnerability. 2. Adopt and implement protocols to ensure that vulnerable populations, such as women, children, members of minority clans as well as persons with disabilities access humanitarian assistance. 3. Ensure that the situation of all displaced populations, including those who are not in camps or broader settlements, as well as those dispersed in smaller groups and living with host families is reﬂected in humanitarian assessments and targeting. 4. Avoid turning cash transfers into a pull factor if there are other alternatives. 5. Avoid registering only the male head-of- household/head of family and never exclude the registration of other adult family members or unaccompanied minors. 6. Consider the frequency and rates of transfers and other distributions to avoid any eventual protection risks –cash rates will be based on the rates as set by the Cash Working Group of Somalia. 7. Disaggregate beneﬁciaries by gender and age. (Personal data and lists of recipients will be ﬁrmly restricted to concerned actors and safe from unintended use, due to the sensitivity of the information.) 8. Provide information on all project activities through different methods such as community meetings and group discussions to ensure communication reaches all beneﬁciaries. 9. Make cash transfers through mobile money (ZAAD), which eases access and promotes the dignity of beneﬁciaries. 10. Promote its Toll Free Number as part of a complaints mechanism to address individual concerns and ensure accountability. Complaints/ feedback will be analyzed regularly and action taken. 11. Ensure that all staff involved in the project have received training in prevention and response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and other protection issues.
