Project Summary

This project will be implemented in the 3 Counties of Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North and Lafon to provide emergency integrated essential health care services to improve demand, access and utilization to the most vulnerable population in Eastern Equatoria State Counties targeting 10,200 (4470 males & 5,730 females) in need for humanitarian Emergency health care services through provision of lifesaving emergency assistance in line with national and international humanitarian standards, and also the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, disease burden seasonality, and trauma-related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, response readiness, response, and mitigation tailored to activities with special attention given to providing vulnerable groups including women, children and persons with disabilities in context of IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities.
Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment and Needs (OPEN) has over 9 years of experience in implementing Clinical and preventive Health packages in both emergency and development in the Eight Counties of Eastern and 2 Counties of Western Equatoria State ( Ibba and Maridi) to support community based surveillance, COVID-19 vaccination, EVD outbreak responses, EPI routine vaccination, Risk Communication and Community Engagement, support point of Entry at Nimule and Pogee including Regional Cross border coordination under “One Health Approach” with funding from USAID. OPEN also supported other health interventions with support from Christian connection for International health (CCIH), World Relief International, Save The Children, and World Vision International to improve access to essential health services that contribute to the overall reduction of the high maternal mortality rates and under-five mortality rates and mitigate the transmission of vaccine-preventable diseases and other infectious/zoonotic diseases.
With this current appeal, OPEN will support 3 direct primary health care (1PHCU in Kapoeta East, 1 PHCU in Kapoeta North and 1 PHCU in Lafon) including 3 Mobile Clinics at least one per county as comprehensive Health service delivery and Human resource for Health including infection prevention and control (IPC), WASH measures and other appropriate prevention management activities complementing the ongoing Community based surveillance in the 8 Counties.
The response plan will provide quality and equitable health services to the targeted vulnerable population, including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, Mental Health and Psychosocial support (MHPSS) and Gender Based Violence (GBV). This response will go along with Universal Health Coverage principles and prioritize prevention, preparedness, response, and mitigation for vulnerable groups through collaboration, coordination and complementarities of Boma Health Initiative (BHI), integrated Community case Management (iCCM) given OPEN had established 2,540 functional Home Health Promoters 2,540 to support HSTP Partners.
The response will support 3 primary health care (1PHCU in Kapoeta East, 1 PHCU in Kapoeta North and 1 PHCU in Lafon) including 3 Mobile Clinics at least one per health facilities and community-based systems to serve people in need to strengthen disease surveillance and reporting system in a realization of the three health cluster objectives: -
1. Objective 1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations.
2. Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases.
3. Objective 3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.

1. Needs

According to IPC recent report, April – July 2025, in the lean-season projection period, 43 percent of the population in 3 counties of Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North and Lafon in Eastern Equatoria State will likely face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and the other 34 percent of the population of the 5 Counties will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity as a result of deterioration in the food security situation is driven by the complete depletion of household food stocks, high market dependence amid high staple food prices and limited income-earning opportunities, a reduced availability of wild foods, and conflict and insecurity related to cattle raiding and herder-farmer disputes, which will disrupt trade flows and market functionality across the region. Reference IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition Analysis September 2024 - July 2025 Published On 18 November, 2024. Additionally, the counties are experiencing poor agricultural practices and insecurity caused by cattle keepers, and reliance on pastoralist livelihoods, scarce/very low access to water for household and inadequate numbers of functional health facilities across the Counties due to limited support to Primary care units under Health system transformation project (HSTP) also impact on people’s ability to access medical facilities, care and appropriate medicines in hard to reach areas. Also, lack of enough healthcare workers was commonly reported as low pay and security concerns affecting health workers and consistently malaria remained the main reported cause of death and much higher proportions (between 80%-86%) reported malaria as the main cause of death in all the Counties. These combination factors with low access to health facilities are likely to result in poor health outcomes. The risk of waterborne disease and possible epidemic outbreaks remained relatively high given the imported outbreak of Cholera from Sudanese Refugees in Renk, Malakal, Juba and Aweil West Counties in South Sudan. Furthermore, the Needs contextual analysis from, IRNA and EPI Reports indicated that the incidence rate for common diseases remains immense. Hence, due to the high disease burden resulting from common diseases, epidemic outbreaks, pandemics and neglected diseases, the population targeted for humanitarian healthcare services is based on multiple drivers including, displacements, malnutrition, high disease burden from vaccine-preventable and communicable diseases including Seasonal outbreaks of common diseases such as cholera and measles with continued endemicity of malaria is posing a threat to the fragile health system that requires action to promote integrated programming at Health facility and Community outreach through existing community health structures such as BHI, CBS and ICCM coordination for efficiencies and convergence response, in terms of both common geographical locations and complementary activities with other implementing partners. South Sudan: Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan 2024 (Issued November 2023) | OCHA (unocha.org)South Sudan | IPC - Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (ipcinfo.org).

2. Project activities

The intervention will provide quality health services to people affected by health events, including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, MHPSS (Mental Health and Psychosocial support) and GBV (Gender Based Violence). OPEN’s response will also improve sexual reproductive health services of EmONC (emergency newborn and obstetric care), family planning, PMTCT (prevention of mother to child transmission), Clinical Management of Rape and supplies for vulnerable women and girls. The intervention will use referral mechanism to enhance efficiency, integration, and quality of care. The response will follow Universal Health Coverage principles and prioritize prevention, preparedness, response, and mitigation for vulnerable groups. The response will also continue EVD (Ebola Virus Disease) prevention, preparedness, and readiness activities and other viral hemorrhagic fevers. The intervention will also strengthen technical, operational, and field coordination with WASH, FSL, Nutrition, Child Protection, GBV partners through County and State Ministry of Health support while also support disease surveillance and reporting system.
