Project Summary

Limited access to energy and others ressources for agricultural purpose has been identified as a very pressing issue in Borno State (FAO, WFP and UNHCR, 2019). An assessment conducted by WFP, UNHCR and FAO in 4 LGAs of Borno state in 2018, including Gwoza where this project will be implemented, revealed that while 95% of households depend on firewood and charcoal for their daily cooking energy needs, around 76% of them are not able to cover these needs. This insufficient access to energy has exposed vulnerable people to a number of challenges and risks; which directly hampers food security and nutrition (e.g. insufficient fuel to cook food), increases deforestation (e.g. unsustainable felling of trees for household fuel) as well as protection risks, specifically for women and girls (e.g. harassment, assault, physical and sexual violence when collecting wood fuel) and health risks (smoke inhalation provoking respiratory illnesses). The same assessment showed that 85% of surveyed HHs indicated that they face protection risks when collecting firewood, 89% face nutrition risks due to insufficient access to energy and 92% reporting the decrease of wood fuel in their immediate environment in the past year.
Other humanitarian organisations have attempted to address the aforementioned issues using improved stoves that operates with either less amount of wood and or charcoal or on solar energy (Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES)). These technologies are already tested or scaled up in Nigeria and other contexts. Though these technologies have proved fruitful. However, even though using wood as fuel for FES has the advantage of using less firewood than traditional stoves, and is appropriate in the Nigeria contexts where wood is available and can be, in one way or another, easily accessible by the target population, in the case of Northern Nigeria, specifically the Bay States, environmental degradation has resulted in significant lack of firewood around IDPs’ immediate environment, resulting in women and girls having to go far afield from military trenches (relative safety) to look for this resource.
NCA Nigeria is proposing to put in place community-based faecal sludge management system (collection, transportation, treatment, and revalorization of sludges) that can increase the utilization of byproducts. The feasibility study did this year into account environmental, economic, social, technical, financial and legal dimensions of this solution came up with finding which will be used, in collaboration with HO, to design a ready to be implemented community-based faecal sludge management and revalorization project. The study was implemented in Monguno LGA and ongoing in Biu LGA, in collaboration with BOSEPA to strengthen ownership of the initiative by local stakeholders and their collaboration with private sector.
The project will start with a webinar involving key WASH actors operating in Borno, and even in others states, including the Water and Sanitation technical working group, focusing on the scope of the activities, in order to gather their contributions/inputs apart from their participation. A user centered approach was used by conducting various consultations with different groups in the targeted communities: women, men, elderly, disables, IDPs, host communities. This contributed to include their various needs while designing the project. A qualified NCA WaSH staff will be specifically assigned to work with all stakeholders to ensure good community mobilization and participation throughout the implementation.
The transformation products of faecal waste will be used for agriculture, energy production for lighting and also for domestic gas. Training will be provided by the consultant in operation and maintenance of the plants once they are in place.

1 - Needs

In all the IDP camps, humanitarian actors are providing various services including construction/rehabilitation of communal and households’ latrines along with O&M activities such as de-sludging. Borno state host the highest number of IDPs with many of them living in camps and camplike settings. Of the 88 camps in the state, only 49% receive regular de-sludging services provided by humanitarian actors, with UNICEF removing the highest amount of sludge - 20,952 m3 per month. In locations such as Monguno, finding space to dump the sludge is a cause for concern and poses a huge problem for humanitarians as they try to preserve sanitation within the camps and camplike settings. 2 - Activities

The faecal sludge management processing project aims to find a solution to the sanitation problem encountered in the IDP camps.Indeed, there is a double problem of space: the host populations no longer want to give land to build new sludge disposal sites, there is no more space in the sanitary corridors to build new infrastructures. The cost of the emptying operation is also high and unsafe. Added to this is the security problem which makes it impossible to look for remote sites. The combination of all these problems makes it difficult to fill the latrines in some camps.Faced with all this, NCA thought of setting up an innovative project by setting up a structure for the management of faecal waste and also its recovery for economically and socially profitable purposes,This innovation project, therefore, will provide a solution that bridges al the concerns. The project team will work with private sector to develop a model of sluge management technology with the following characteristics:• operational– can be work during dry and rainy season• Sustainability - Ability to be use for many years • Protection – Can provide needed protection for vulnerable women and girls • Efficacy – Efficient and has the capability to product gas, fertilizer for multipurpose.• Sustainable and Cost effective maintenance - Can be easily maintained by users. Materials for repairs are readily and cheaply available. This project will start with a workshop/webinar, involving key humanitarian actors in NE Nigeria to solicit their interest in the initiative, followed by a detailed needs assessment to be conducted by a consultant with appropriate background in the domain, recruited by NCA. This will help gain deep understanding of the challenge and foster beneficiaries’ involvement and buy-in. The definition of key criteria will inform the open market dialogue to be initiated with private companies and innovators, through virtual workshops and meetings. This will also bring out ideas of solutions that can be adapted specifically for the NE Nigerian context. A vital aspect of this criteria will be in the area of sustainability of the solution and the availability of local raw materials for local scale up and maintenance, which will be done by women and adolescent girls, whilst preserving the environment. The resulted innovative solution will then be defined through an open, innovative, and competitive procurement process focusing on the functions as per the criteria developed from the need’s assessment rather than the feature. The selected prototype will then be acquired through partnership with the private company or innovator that submitted the most competitive product in terms of solving the aforementioned challenge, fulfils the defined criteria and is financially viable. Regular monitoring and final evaluation by NCA team will be carried out during and at the end of the production stage. Lessons learnt will be documented and capitalised on after completion of prototype production and training of targeted populations by NCA staff going forward. A final webinar/workshop will be conducted with other humanitarian actors to showcase the product to encourage uptake and dissemination of lessons learnt.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Sensitivity to cross-cutting issues is fundamental in NCA programmes and gender and equity issues is a strategic commitment.NCA's intention is to look at protection as a cross-cutting issue with the aim of "Do No Harm". When women and children seek help to meet their needs, they are susceptible to certain protection risks and therefore NCA will seek to mitigate those risks. Therefore, to ensure that proposed activities do not create a protection risk for certain groups such as women, youth and children, NCA will take special precautions. For example, by ensuring that sensitization sessions are conducted in a way that does not increase the risks, and by adapting the time, place and staff to these vulnerable groups. And we will seek to limit their travel to health centres and/or their reception on site in order to limit unnecessary exposures. Sensitivity to cross-cutting issues is also reflected in the integration of women into community management structures and the adoption of practices such as the separation of male and female latrines in public places. The policy is firmly rooted in the promotion of women to key local governance positions enabling them to influence and guide decisions to address the concerns of the most disadvantaged. The situational analysis within communities identifies priority needs, while prioritising vulnerability criteria to ensure interventions that benefit the disadvantaged (i.e. dimensioning of facilities appropriate to the needs of children and people living with reduced mobility). Priority will be given to establishing a programme accountability framework at the level of each health area. This will bring together all local leaders, rights holders and other stakeholders to ensure regular information sharing, strengthen self-assessment involving the role/responsibility of each actor and the establishment of appropriate governance systems. The participatory approach will build the capacity of local actors and structures (masons, artisanal repairers, management committee), under a governance system for water services and water safety under the leadership of the accountability framework through the community improvement plan. To promote the local market, local contractors will be strongly involved in the local labour force and as local suppliers of the materials they are able to produce, including cement blocks.
