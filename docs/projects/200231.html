Project Summary

Based on the multidimensional poverty index, it is alarming to note that 1.45 million school-aged children in Lebanon are in dire need of support to access education, with 662,000 of them being Lebanese (52% girls), more than 715,000 being Syrians (51% girls), around 13,300 being migrants (73% girls), and about 56,000 being Palestine refugees (52% girls). This calls for urgent intervention to ensure that these vulnerable children and youth are not left behind.
In response to this dire situation, WVL is committed to targeting the most vulnerable children, youth, Lebanese, Palestinians, and migrants, along with their caregivers under the Emergency Response Programme (ERP). The intervention will be focused on ensuring equitable and inclusive access to, participation in, and completion of safe and protective education for all learners in Lebanon. Specifically, WVL will be working towards two major outcomes, the first of which will ensure access to education for the most vulnerable children and their caregivers. To achieve this, WVL will kick-start a holistic intervention starting with a Back to School Campaign where children will be referred to learning, supported with cash for education to eliminate the economic barrier to education, and provided with retention classes to ensure they acquire the necessary foundational skills that will allow them to stay in education. Moreover, children registered in retention programs will receive snacks, hygiene materials, and health prevention measures and supplies. Caregivers will also be targeted to engage in the education of their children, while learning centers will be supported to continue their operational activities. WVL will also ensure access to learning materials based on our experience in remote learning provision for different age groups. The second outcome aims to improve teaching and learning by focusing on learning outcomes for children and youth through enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education services that are safe, protective, adapted, and inclusive for multi-crisis situations. This will be achieved by training teachers on formal and non-formal education, providing psychosocial support to children, and monitoring the number of children progressing to the next school year.Despite the challenges and difficulties that families and their children are facing, WVL is committed to ensuring that education is not de-prioritized in the face of the curtailing crises in the country. We will leverage our presence in the areas and our long-standing partnerships in the communities, utilizing previously implemented outreach strategies that have proven effective in reaching the most vulnerable children and youth among the targeted population, such as through local partners, door-to-door, media including social media, and phone calls.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

The Back to School Campaign will target 2,000 children (1,000 girls & 1,000 boys) through community outreach and engagement to refer them to public formal education or Multiple Flexible Pathways. WVL will also provide cash for education to 796 children (398 girls & 398 boys) to address economic barriers. In addition, 897 children (454 girls & 543 boys) will receive regulated retention support through Remedial Education and Homework Support, including life skills sessions. WVL will provide nutritious snacks to 700 children (350 girls & 350 boys) in its learning centers, and engage 7,390 caregivers in awareness sessions to improve their involvement in their children's education. To support education institutions, WVL will provide health-related prevention measures and supplies to 45 formal and non-formal education institutions, and cover the operating costs of 54 schools, including fuel. WVL will also provide low-cost solutions for inclusive remote learning and support 5,598 children (2,799 girls & 2,799 boys) to receive relevant customized learning support.As part of the second outcome, WVL will train 76 teachers and education personnel (67 women & 9 men) on quality standards, PSS, and child protection and wellbeing. WVL will also monitor the progress of 498 children (249 girls & 249 boys) who will be moving on to the next school year, and continue supporting 20,737 children and youth (10,369 girls & 10,369 boys).

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

WVL will use a participatory approach to ensure equal access for all and to provide safe, inclusive, and gender-sensitive learning environments and training materials. The project will consider identified barriers that create obstacles for school-aged boys and girls, as well as male and female caregivers and community members when planning their participation and benefits from the provided sessions. Child protection, gender equality, and social inclusion questions will be incorporated into the routine project monitoring and evaluation, disaggregated and analyzed by sex and age to understand the benefits of the services and remaining challenges for both genders. WVL also supports the inclusion of children with disabilities in its programs, with a focus on lessening the barriers to disability by identifying CWDs using the WGQ, raising awareness at the community level to create more understanding and acceptance, and tailoring activities whenever necessary

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

World Vision Lebanon will guarantee the presence of a functional and accessibleAccountability system to enhance beneficiary and community participation as well asownership of project design and implementation. Targeted beneficiaries will have accessto information packages including information about World Vision, the project and thecommunity response mechanism via SMS, flyers, helplines and/or information sessionsheld at field level. The Accountability team will ensure that a help desk is available duringmajor activities. Feedback and consultation activities will be conducted during the projectimplementation to obtain input on the beneficiaries’ preferences and needs to informprogrammatic decisions. Complaints will be confidentially handled and closed within thetimeframe after proper actions and measures are taken to solve it. Finally, monthly reportswill be drafted to provide updates on finalized activities, pending and solved complaints.
