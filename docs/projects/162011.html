Project Summary

"Keeping in line with the Nutrition Sector strategy and in line with WFP’s approach to nutrition service provision, the proposed project will aim to provide the right assistance to the right people in the twelve month time frame.
WV though funding support from WFP and UNICEF, will implement an integrated nutrition program which will focus on provision of lifesaving care, and promotion of optimal health and nutrition practices, prevention of malnutrition. The WV integrated nutrition WV will utilize treatment approaches such as Targeted Supplementary feeding, Community Management of At risk Mothers and Infants (CMAMI) and Outpatient therapeutic program to provide lifesaving care. These will be complemented by preventive services which include approaches such as Targeted and blanket supplementary feeding, Blanket Supplementary feeding, targeted counseling, Social Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) and awareness raising, GMP, micronutrient supplementation amongst and community mobilization amongst others, Community Therapeutic Care (CTC)These integrated nutrition services will primarily to deliver its target assistance to children between the ages of 0-59 months, adolescent girls and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW).
Main activities which will be co - funded through this funding
CMAMI - Community Management of at Risk Mothers and Infants
OTP and TSFP - Management of Acute malnutrition
Blanket Supplementary feeding (BSFP)
Growth Monitoring and Promotion
Peer Support groups (Mother to mother support groups and Adolescent girls groups)
IFA supplementation.
Deworming targeting adolescent girls
Awareness raising targeting - PLWS, Grandmothers, Community leaders, Household heads etc
Commemoration of World Breastfeeding Week
Traditional/faith healers awareness raising on Nutrition (including optimal child care practices)
The funds will be used as partner contribution/match funding for the WFP/UNICEF funded integrated nutrition program. The partner contribution will cover staffing, project activities, office costs, volunteer’s stipends, supplies, logistics, travel and communications related costs
* This funding will not result in any additional beneficiaries but will contribute that the WFP/UNICEF funded project beneficiaries’ service provision.

Needs

"An estimated 910,619 Rohingya refugees reside in the Cox’s Bazar district of Bangladesh, with more than 741,577 individuals having arrived since 25 August 2017, using extreme violence and widespread abuse of fundamental human rights in the Rakhine state, Myanmar, forced them to flee their homes. Of the total, 54.2% constitute children between the ages 0 - 17 years, while 31.9% of the population are adolescent girls and women, with a total female population constituting 51.9%. The Government of Bangladesh leads an international humanitarian response effort reaching 1.2 million Rohingya refugees and host community members in the Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilas . Of the total population, 18.18% constitute children under the age of 5 years. Recently published emergency Nutrition and Health assessment round-3 shows the Global Acute Malnutrition(GAM) rates have reduced from 19, 3% (Round 1) to 12% (Round 2) to 11% round 3 . Despite these positive strides, the presence of aggravating factors makes it necessary to continue nutrition interventions including selective feeding interventions; Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP), Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program (BSFP), and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF). Over half of children aged 6-59 months have anemia. According to the WFP Refugee influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA), 10% of Rohingya have unacceptable dietary diversity, compared to only 1% in the host community . While the nutrition cluster/sector and its partners have made significant strides in reducing the levels of GAM in the Rohingya refugee camps. There is need for continued emergency lifesaving care, this urgent need is worsened by the presence of aggravating factors. The age group 0-5 months has been inadequately targeted as there are limited nutrition interventions directly at risk infants targeting infants. The current coverage of Community management of at risk mothers and infant is less than 50% of the camps, despite evidence suggesting high proportion of at risk mother and infant pairs within in the Rohingya refugee community. There is thus an urgent need to roll out CMAMI (Community Management of at risk Mothers and Infants) to other camps as well. In addition to the emergency live saving care, optimal health and nutrition seeking behaviors need to be promoted through structure behavior change communication programing, so as to achieve sustained behavior change.
