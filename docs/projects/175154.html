Project Summary

The project will operate in the south of Chad country in Moissala, the department of Barh Sarah, one of the departments of the Mandoul Region in southern Chad. This region is hosting a large number of returnees from bordering Central African Republic, CAR, IDP's and hosting populations. Currently Chad is hosting more than 69 000 people running from conflict, many of whom are children. The situation for the refugees as well as host communities is becoming more and more permanent as the Central African Republic is still in crisis. The project will target more than 6910 children (, returness, IDP's and children from host communities - from the villages of Dilingala, Dembo, Gon and Silambi, close to the country border. To mitigate the negative affects on the future lives of these children, the proposed intervention aims to contribute to improved access to formal and non-formal, safe and inclusive quality education, to improve learning but also to improve child protection and child wellness. This will be achieved by improving learning enviroments, providing learning material, build teachers' capacity to work with the target group, sensitize communities on the value of education for both boys and girls, support quality-ensuring eductation structures, amongs other things. The project will also focus on giving opportunity for out-of-school youth, to gain life skills to increase their chances to be economically self-sustained. By providing youth with access to vocational training and economical support, this will allow them to be less exposed to exploitation. To implement this project, SOS CHAD will work in collaboration with local partners and the state technical services to ensure the sustainability of the intervention.
The goal of the project is aimed to Improve access to formal and non-formal, safe and inclusive quality education, for children; girls and boys returnees and IDP’s as well as children in host villages of Dembo, Gon, Silambi and Dilingala in Moïssala, affected by Central Africa republic crisis.
The project aims to achieve two major intermediate goals:
6910 aged 6-12 years children including 3975 girls (returnees, IDP, and Host Communities) have access to inclusive and quality basic education in a protective learning environment in the refugees sites of Gon, Dembo, Dilingala and Silambi. 50 youth refugees, returnees, displaced and host community dropout and out-of-school aged from 15 to 17 aged in refugees sites of Gon, Silambi, Dilingala and Dembo have access to safe, quality vocational training.
The Key activities fro fulfilling these are: - Training of 61 teachers psychosocial support and other thematic: methodologies, lesson planning, culture of peace and dialogue among religious - Support development and implementation of four (4) Risk reduction plan and codes of conduct - Setting up and training 4 GBV clubs of 16 members in twelve (12) schools - Provide school medicine chest in 12 schools and training of managers - Providing of dignity kits for 80 - Provide school supplies, manuals for 6910 school children aged 6-12 including 3975 girls and pedagogical kits for 61 teachers - Support education inspection of Moissala in the pedagogical supervision - Provide pedagogical kit to 61 community teachers - Setting up and training of 8 school Management committees of 48 members in 4 schools ( 4 School Children Parents associations, 12 School Children Mother’s associations) - Vocational trainings for 50 vulnerable girls, boys and male, female youth - Providing incentives for 50 trainees.

Implementing Partners

SOS CHAD will be the major player in the direct implementation of the project activities on the ground. However, it will strongly collaborate with the local technical services in charge of education, with community-based organizations and with the existing community-based child protection mechanisms.
During the implementation of the project, SOS CHAD will be technically supported by the SOS Emergency Response (ER) team, which sits at the International Office Region for West Central and Northern Africa (WCNA) in Dakar, Senegal, funding partners and others partners in the fields.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, in the south of the country, despite political efforts by both countries to facilitate their voluntary return, 69 000 returnees from Chad is currently facing reintegration communities difficulties. The data on literacy in Chad show that nearly 78% of the population aged 15 and over is illiterate. This proportion reaches 86% among women. Nearly 19% of girls are enrolled at the average level compared to 41 % of boys. Of this number, the average school completion rate for girls is 9 % while that for boys is 25%. According to SOS CHAD’s multi-sectoral needs assessment carried out at the end of July 2019, key informants and group discussions reveal that, in Moissala girls education remain a concern in returnees, IDP and host communities: they often drop out of school very early because they are exposed to early pregnancies and forced marriages, often as a result of social norms unfavourable to girls and a precarious economic situation. There is no alternative education for children out of school. The textbook ratio is 17 pupils/manuals. At the primary level, out of 838 community teachers only 99 are qualified. According to Chad interim education plan 2018-2020, three (3) students out of four (4) have been taught by teachers with a completed degree or certificate no higher than a high-school level at the national level. 84.3% of students at the end of primary school have a low level in literacy and 80.9% in mathematics; Educational structures are not functional or even not exist. It should be noted that there is also a lack of logistical resources for the pedagogical supervision of teachers. As of this, the project is aiming to address the poor quality of education through strengthening of teacher capacity, improving security, improving child participation, awareness raising on GBV among children and in the community, improving fundraising capacity for schools and providing educational material, among other things. The project is to Improve access to formal and non-formal, safe and inclusive quality education, for children; girls and boys returnees and IDP’s on the sites as well as children in host villages of Dembo, Gon, Silambi and Dilingala in Moïssala, affected by Central Africa republic crisis. The project aims to achieve two major intermediate goals: 6910 school children aged 6-12 including 3975 girls (returnees, IDP, and Host Communities) have access to inclusive and quality basic education in a protective learning environment in the villages of Gon, Dembo, Dilingala and Silambi - 50 youth returnees, displaced and host community dropout and out-of-school aged from 14 to 18 aged in villages of Gon, Silambi, Dilingala and Dembo have access to safe, quality vocational training. The Key activities fro fulfilling these are: - Training of 61 teachers psychosocial support and other thematic: methodologies, lesson planning, culture of peace and dialogue among religious - Support development and implementation of four (4) Risk reduction plan and codes of conduct - Setting up and training 4 GBV clubs of 16 members in twelve (12) schools - Provide school medicine chest in 12 schools and training of managers - Providing of dignity kits for 80 - Provide school supplies, manuals for 6910 school children aged 6-12 including 3975 girls and pedagogical kits for 61 teachers - Support education inspection of Moissala in the pedagogical supervision - Provide pedagogical kit to 61 community teachers - Setting up and training of 8 school Management committees of 48 members in 4 schools ( 4 School Children Parents associations, 12 School Children Mother’s associations) - Vocational trainings for 50 vulnerable girls, boys and male, female youth - Providing incentives for 50 trainees.
