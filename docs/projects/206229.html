Project Summary

South Sudan continues to undergo complex and multidimensional protection risks in 2023. Prior to the outbreak of violence in Sudan on 15 April 2023, the country grappled with displacement caused by inter-communal violence catalyzed by competition over scarce resources, politically instigated violence, cattle raiding, revenge killings and the impact of climate change epitomized by drought and floods.
This year, the macroeconomic conditions continued to deteriorate with the increase of global interest rates sending the currency into free fall with little hope for recovery and causing inflation. The increased cost of living simultaneously coincided with reduced donor funding across the entire humanitarian sector, as the needs of displaced populations continued to increase.
The prevailing context persisted and was worsened by an influx of the returnees and refugees in April from Sudan which stand at 84,400 HH/ 367,800 individuals. 82% being South Sudanese returnees; a larger population being concentrated in Upper Nile and CES. Additionally, Jonglei has received returnees from Gambella in Ethiopia
Heightened protection risks ranged from: Gender Based Violence including Intimate Partner Violence, sexual violence, psychological trauma, to: lack of access to documentation to support housing land and property reclamation, illegal occupation, theft, destruction of personal property. Child protection challenges such as child marriage and family separation are also present.
Within this context, population movements have been influenced by this intricate web of pull and push factors and increased vulnerabilities among displaced populations as they seek safety, humanitarian assistance and attempt to rebuild their lives. The 2024, UNHCR HRP will focus on life saving humanitarian interventions linked to: i() General protection - monitoring and analysis to identify needs and provide tailored protection response; (ii) GBV - provision of case management, cash based assistance, psychosocial support and awareness to support prevention; (iii) Child protection - identification of children at risk, cash based assistance, physical support and training for individuals working with children through the community based protection networks and referral pathways; and (v) HLP - through legal aid and counseling, cash assistance, strengthening and establishing alternative dispute mechanisms.
Lifesaving humanitarian assistance will be provided to women, men, boys and girls based on need. Profiling will be conducted to identify the most vulnerable. Identification will be guided by the age, gender and diversity policy which ensures inclusion for all groups including persons with disabilities, youth, women, elderly and LGBTQ+. Activities will consider accountability to affect people and seek to ensure communication and transparency, participation and inclusion, feedback and response as well as adaptations and learning. Needs will be addressed through: direct implementation, implementation with partners, mobile teams to ensure access to hard-to-reach areas where there is need, cash and in-kind support. Response will further consider interventions intended for durable solutions such as legal aid, access to documentation, reintegration and cash-based assistance.

1. Needs

UNHCR and partners conducted a Participatory Assessments (PA) comprising of 180 Focus Groups Discussion held in September 2023 across Upper Nile, CES, Jonglei and Bentiu. The FGDs were grouped based on AGD to ensure inclusion and evidence-based decision making based on different age groups, genders and diversity components to guide tailored interventions that addressed critical needs. The assessment looked at: GBV, NFI, shelter, CP, Durable Solutions, WASH, among others. Results of the PA verify the protection environment is not only complex but also multidimensional, with, multiple forms of protection interventions are required to support one individual; for instance, displaced people had experienced various forms of GBV, were generally in bad health, and poor nutrition and endured poor physical conditions as they struggled to meet basic needs. Respondents prioritized the following thematic areas: support to GBV survivors with dignity kits, cash assistance, income generating activities; child protection through family reunification, psychosocial support and case management; HLP through legal aid and counseling etc. Assessed needs were further complemented by information form the: (i) Protection Monitoring System which relies on information gathered from key informants to monitor and analyze the protection environment; (ii) The protection analytical framework for the second quarter of 2023 which scored South Sudan servility as high for the following protection risks: GBV, Attacks on Civilians and other Unlawful Killings, and Attacks on Civilian Objects, Psychological / Emotional Abuse or Inflicted Distress. The analysis noted that lack of livelihoods led to repeat displacements and negative coping mechanisms, inferences can be drawn to the potential risk of PSEA cases becoming rampant even within the protection environment. Confirming the need to integrate solutions and resilience building to lifesaving assistance from the start, as well as the need to collect desegregated data on affected persons based to guide interventions and establishment of feedback mechanisms to create avenues of reporting abuse To ensure targeted and tailored assistance based on vulnerabilities, UNCHR has an existing methodology for identifying and assisting persons with specific needs including disabled persons and the elderly. As such persons with PSN codes are deliberately included in assessments, consultations and decision making. UNHCR identified Persons with Specific Needs through fields mission assessments with implementing partners, using mobile teams to distinguish persons with specific needs from the general population. Once identified, PSNs are classified into codes to establish the nature of assistance they may require: Some PSN codes include: Single Parent (SP) without support, Woman at risk (WR), SGBV (SV), Child at risk (CR), Unaccompanied or separated child (SC), Elderly at risk (ER), Person with Disabilities (DS), Considering the above, project will seek to respond to needs through: 1. Equitable life saving assistance tailored to specific needs based on vulnerabilities and guided by age, gender and diversity. 2. Protection monitoring to address protection gaps to inform coordination, advocacy and response. 3. Promoting the transition between humanitarian assistance and long-term development assistance to build resilience, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities. Interventions will be guided by accountability to affected persons and the UNHCR AGD policy, while efforts will be taken to protected person of concern through the UNCHR PSEA policy’s and related IEC material. The approach articulates inroads for inclusion of all persons, evidence-based programming and communication with communities. Interventions will seek to incorporate solutions dimensions from the start to ease the transition from humanitarian assistance to long term development and resilience.

2. Project activities

Activities will center on life saving interventions, general protection, HLP, child protection and GVB. Throughout the delivery of assistance efforts will be made to ensure communication is maintained with persons of concern and approved activities on the ground are based participatory assessments and inclusion of the needs and views of affected people. Feedback mechanisms which will be diverse ranging from hotlines, FGD, suggestion boxes, and protection desks will not only collect information but share feedback with persons of concern on the action taken to ensure the communication circuit is complete; they will also facilities reporting on PSEA. In doing so the intervention will remain accountable to affected people. Critical specifics on interventions are as follows: General protection: Identification of protection and specific needs among populations of concern, including among IDP, returnees and host communities will be conducted through mobile and static presence or protection desks and further supported by the protection monitoring system. This will inform analysis, identify trends to support advocacy, response and programming. Equitable life saving assistance tailored to specific needs based on vulnerabilities will be guided by AGD. Tailored protection interventions and services will address heightened vulnerabilities and protection gaps. Gaps will be addressed through direct implementation, implementation with partners, referrals and community-based structures. Gender Based Violence: Provision of critical support to GBV survivors will not only address the needs of women and girls but also men and boys, as well as other population groups such as the elderly, disabled and member of the LGBTQ+ community. Structural factors, such as gender inequality, also compound protection risks, notably for women and girls. Patriarchal structures and traditions expose women and girls to GBV, violence and abuse. While traditional perception discourage reporting of GBV by men and boys: Support provided will endeavor to facilitate access to: Case management, psychosocial support for survivors to regain their dignity; awareness raising to support prevention. Other forms of support will include: cash assistance and GBV risks mitigation training for non-GBV humanitarian workers. Child protection: Efforts will be made to develop child friendly advocacy and psychosocial support to affected children. Returnee children have undergone significant trauma from the Sudan conflict; it is estimated that 60% of the population who have returned to Rotriak in Unity are children, many of them have undergone direct or indirect exposure to violence and conflict. Specific response activities will include: case management including through cash-based interventions, identification of children at risk, referrals for identification, family tracing and reunification, alongside awareness sessions and training community leaders and actors on child protection in emergencies. Housing Land and Property: The project will facilitate solutions to address challenges to securing housing, land and property as part of the transition from humanitarian assistance to long term development and resilience. Provision will be made for IDPs, returnees, and host communities to be issued with identification documents to enable them to replace lost ownership documents. Further, outreach, legal aid/representation, counseling will be provided to build knowledge on HLP issues among victims of land grabbing or illegal occupation in locations such as Uppernile in Fashoda, Malakal, Nassir and Baliet. Community land committees and alternative dispute mechanisms will be established or strengthened to capacitate communities to address HLP disputes at the Payam Level. Advocacy with Government will be initiated to request for allocation of land for IDPs in protracted displacement such as those in Malakal POC, (Upper Nile), Rotriak (Unity), IDP Camps (CES) as a solution to HLP challenges.
