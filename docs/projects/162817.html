Project Summary

The proposed action intends to address urgent needs and provide emergency support to conflict-affected population in Blue Nile State, with specific focus on addressing gaps in humanitarian services in the target areas including gaps in FSL, WASH and Protection. The beneficiaries of the program are Displaced Persons and destitute host communities who are still suffering from the protracted crises which exposed them to socioeconomic hardships and disrupted their livelihoods since the inception of the conflict in 2011. A Rapid Needs Assessment was conducted by VSF-G staff in January 2018 in target areas has revealed critical gaps in humanitarian needs to be immediately addressed. VSF-G and partners in Blue Nile State have conducted an inter-agency assessment including HAC, UN Agencies, INGOs and NNGOs in newly accessible areas particularly in Gissan Locality, the findings show grave needs in food security and livelihood opportunities. This project has been purposely designed to address the critical needs of the conflict affected DPs and host communities in Blue Nile State. The proposed project would achieve its objectives through implementation of interrelated activities in Food security and livelihood sector to timely address urgent humanitarian needs of the affected population and building their self-reliance and reduce their dependency on humanitarian assistance. Shortage of safe water supply in IDPs settlement in Roseries and Damazine Localities forces those affected people to collect water for domestic use from unsafe sources (River), the communities are at high risk of water borne-diseases, especially with the Acute Watery Diarrhea outbreaks in 2016-2017. The impact of poor water on nutritional status of children, breastfeeding mothers and water-borne diseases is considered critical and urgent. Main activities include food security and livelihood activities, the project will provide assorted vegetable seeds and extension training to 1,000 women-headed households, support for fishery production to 200 households intends to improve the living conditions of vulnerable fishing families by providing improved means to ensure better nutritional status and income generating activities through the provision of fishery training and equipment, restocking with goats and poultry to 340 HHs aims to rebuild livestock assets and increase production capacity of targeted households. Vaccination and treatment programs for animals belonging to 6,000 households aim to protect livestock assets of the conflict-affected populations. Under protection sector; one women multipurpose center will be established, and used for dissemination of GBV and protection messages, raising awareness on hygiene, and building the capacities of community members especially women and girls in food processing and livelihood alternatives. Through the women center 300 women from vulnerable communities will be trained on response to Gender-Based Violence, and awareness campaigns will be carried out in 5 communities. To promote the advocacy for women protection in the targeted areas, VSF-G will undertake training of government counterparts on Protection, Gender Based Violence, Referral Pathways establishment and Psychosocial Support to victims

1 - Needs

This project is designed in line with State Strategic Plans for FSL, WASH and Protection, Clusters Specific Objectives and Humanitarian Response Plan 2018 Objectives. The activities proposed under this action will build on previous VSF-G similar experiences in responding to humanitarian needs of conflict-affected populations in Blue Nile, South Kordofan and Central Darfur States though implementation of emergency lifesaving activities to address WASH, FSL and Protection needs. The project will also complement other interventions being implemented by UN agencies, INGOs and NGOs in the targeted localities such as FAO, WFP, ADRA, World Vision, Practical Action, Islamic Relief Worldwide, Mubadiroon, Almasar, CORD, AORD, Nida’ and Labina. VSF-G will ensure sufficient coordination and cross checking is undertaken to avoid possible overlaps and duplication of efforts. This project builds upon similar proven experience and expertise of VSF-G and its partners in FSL, WASH and Protection based emergency responses for IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities. The proposed activities will complement nutrition and health interventions implemented by partners in the targeted localities. VSF-G intends to draw experience to reach affected communities in the project area through working together with government line ministries to enhance the effectiveness and sustainability of this project. These will be achieved through engaging them from the beginning in start-up workshop, to create sufficient awareness and plan jointly for scheduling of the activities in a detailed implementation plan. Maintaining regular communication with line ministries, participating via monthly meetings, as well as involving them in project monitoring and evaluation activities during the course of the project implementation. This project will be implemented through collaboration with a local partner, which will be selected upon local partners capacity assessment, the implementing partner will add value to the project through their knowledge of the communities and participation in community mobilization and distribution activities, VSF-G will build the capacity of the local partner and provide technical backstopping.This action is a multi-sectoral endeavor, aiming to respond to the emergency needs of the conflict-affected population with focus on gaps unaddressed by other interventions. A holistic and comprehensive approach will be employed to upgrade the living conditions of the affected population through implementation of spectrum of interrelating activities across WASH, Agriculture and Food Security and Protection sectors. The program implementation incorporates a broad community mobilization and participation approaches, building trust among community members to attain self-reliance, and to ensure ownership and sustainability. VSF-G strategic plan emphasizes continued implementation of FSL interventions in Blue Nile, South Kordofan, Central Darfur, West Darfur and West Kordofan States. It embodies strong commitment to focus on high vulnerable states in the country. VSF-G strategic objectives have been developed through comprehensive discussion with key stakeholders at all levels including participation of the headquarter office and regional office.

2 - Activities / Outputs

The main activities include: rehabilitation of one surface water structure (hafir) and maintenance/rehabilitation of four water pumps, to increase the accessibility of vulnerable households to safe water for domestic use, and to build the technical and management capacities of community-based water committees to be able to carry out simple maintenance and to contribute positively to water resource management. Furthermore under the hygiene promotion subsector, the project will promote good hygiene practice among the community through awareness creation events. In agriculture and food security; the main activities will be: provision of assorted vegetable seeds, and extension training to 1,000 women-headed households; support for fishery production to 200 households intends to improve the living conditions of vulnerable fishing families by providing improved means to ensure better nutritional status and income generating activities through the provision of fishery training and equipment; restocking with goats and poultry to 340 households aims to rebuild livestock assets and increase production capacity of targeted households. Vaccination and treatment programs for animals belonging to 6,000 households aim to protect livestock assets of the conflict-affected populations. Under protection sector; one women multipurpose center will be established, and used for dissemination of GBV and protection messages, raising awareness on hygiene, and building the capacities of community members especially women and girls in food processing and livelihood alternatives. Through the women center 300 women from vulnerable communities will be trained on response to Gender-Based Violence, and awareness campaigns will be carried out in 5 communities. To promote the advocacy for women protection in Blue Nile State and in the targeted areas, VSF-G will undertake training of government counterparts on Protection, Gender Based Violence, Referral Pathways establishment and Psychosocial Support to victims/survivors.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The proposed grant will enhance improved resource management and equal access to available resources. Furthermore, VSF G has considered seriously the ‘environmental marker across all the project designed activities. ’The project will protect and restore the natural resource base in the livelihoods systems of the poor and vulnerable households through redressing environmental degradation, increasing efficiency and promoting conservation of soil, water and biomass resources. The waste will be incinerated safely and each training will include element of environment protection

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF G will apply different standards and best practices in emergency response including SPHERE standards, LEGS, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Natural Resource Management (NRM) and "Do No Harm'' approaches in the implementation of this project activities. It will be taken into consideration that the transfer of resources (project inputs and services including agricultural commodities and cash) into a resource-scarce environment can trigger conflict in the communities. Careful assessment of the local context, examining how humanitarian assistance would interact with the current situation in the target areas, and the creation of options for project revision to improve its quality will be carried out. this assessment will include analysis of: (i) dividers and sources of tensions between groups: systems and institutions; attitudes and actions; values and interests; experiences; symbols and occasions; etc.; (ii) connectors across sub-groups and the local capacities for peace; (iii) the project in terms of why; where; what; when; with whom; by whom and how and its impact on dividers/tensions and connectors/local capacities for peace. The partners will work together to ensure that the project is implemented in a way that can strengthen local capacities for peace, build on connectors that bring communities together, and reduce the divisions and sources of tensions that can lead to destructive conflict. To ensure that no negative impact should result from this project the following will be undertaken: just distribution of resources, creating or strengthening networks of relationships across divisions, strengthening good governance, the use of participatory processes for decision making, supporting traditional or indigenous mechanisms for conflict resolution and reconciliation, inclusion of diversity of ethnic or religious groups, gender, and youth in project activities and leadership structures. Selection of beneficiaries will be performed with due consideration of “Do No Harm” principles, ensuring fair representation of different vulnerable groups regardless of their ethnicity, religion or gender. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be socialized with communities and ample opportunity will be provided to raise concerns or complaints. Selection criteria for all project activities will focus on female-headed HHs, child-headed HHs, orphans, elders and persons with disabilities. The population of the targeted localities; Abugubeiha, Abukarshola, Talodi/Leri and Rashad includes a high proportion of vulnerable individuals; targeted HHs might have lost a family members during the conflict, mainly men, which explains the considerable number of women-headed HHs among the targeted communities; those women have to act as the breadwinners for their children and often their elders on their own. Enabling them safe and dignified access to basic services is part of the protection mainstreaming in this program.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

VSF G adopts Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations in setting standards, building capacity, communication, participation, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. IASC commitments with regard to AAP are incorporated in this plan including governance and integration of feedback and accountability mechanisms into program design, maintaining transparency and dialogue between the partners and the affected population, participation and involvement of the target population in design, monitoring and evaluation of this project, establishment of strong communication systems to ensure feedback is received, processed and responded to timely and effectively. All the while mainstreaming protection and considering the safety and confidentiality of the target population. VSFG approach focuses on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elders, persons with disabilities among other vulnerable and or marginalized groups. Community participation will be promoted throughout all the stages of the program; communities’ feedback was the most significant element for the needs assessments conducted before proposing this action- the proposed intervention is based on communities’ needs. Community consultation meetings will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure that the project is well understood by target communities and raise the communities’ awareness about their participatory roles and responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the program partners toward them. Participation will be promoted through the involvement of beneficiaries in the formation of community committees and planning the implementation schedule. The community committees will be created to organize and mobilize the beneficiaries, participate in the development of beneficiary selection criteria, distribution of inputs and monitoring and evaluation of the intervention. Representatives from a cross-section (concerning age, gender, and diversity) will be ensured to participate as community committee members and in the monitoring and evaluation of the project. VSF G have intensive experience working with the target communities and established relationships and inclusive community structures which will facilitate the establishment of effective feedback mechanisms. The most appropriate mechanisms will be established in consultation with community members, taking into consideration literacy rates, accessibility to communication devices and the presence of suitable sites for establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of response
