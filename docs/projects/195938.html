Project Summary

This project aims to provide integrated activities under the Early Recovery sector. It aims to support income-generating activities through VT and small business grants. And improving access to markets and other service facilities by rehabilitation the roads.
The project will support 300 direct BNFs by the vocational training activities, and supporting 20,600 BNFs by the rehabilitation works in the communities of Mhambel, Jis Shughur, Sharan, and Jandairis sub-districts.
The vocational training support activities:
1- Supporting 200 BNFs (50 % females, 50 % males) of start-up business through VT on most 12 professions needed by society, which were demonstrated by need assessment findings.
200 BNFs will attend theoretical sessions for 20 hours during one week.
Eligible trainees will attend practical sessions by presenting to the contracted service providers. Every trainee must attend daily (3 hours per day) for one month to practice the profession.
The service provider will get on 100 $ for training 3 to 5 trainees in one month through two or 3 shifts.
Eligible BNFs will attend business development training for three days. They will learn to write the proposal.
IHR will evaluate the 200 project proposals and select the best 100 ones to receive small grants with a value of 1000 $ for two batches.
2- Supporting 100 BNFs of existing businesses through capacity building and soft skills training for developing their business.
IHR will evaluate the 100 project proposals and select the best 50 ones to receive small grants with a value of 600 $ for two batches.
IHR will create the community representatives board (CRB) consisting of members of several segments of the community, including women and PWDs. They will engage in the project stages of planning, BNFs selection, and proposals evaluation.
The BNFs will be selected from the most vulnerable persons, giving high priority to female-headed households and PWDs to allow them active participation and benefit from the activities. where the percentage of selected PWDs is 10% out of the total selected beneficiaries.
The vocational training will consider the traditional condition and special terms of the participation of women and PWDs. Where the training is designed to facilitate reaching training centers easily. Specializing a suitable place and time for PDWs, and specializing vehicle for transporting them to the training center. giving them enough time and space for active participation in the training agenda.
- For roads rehabilitation:
The project will provide critical improvements to roads and infrastructure through:
- Rehabilitating affected roads that connect IDP sites to host communities and facilitate people's movement to the markets and other services; facilitations.
This project plans to rehabilitate 4 kms of roads in four sites of Mhambel, Jis Shughur, Sharan, and Jandairis subdistricts.
The project will train the key stakeholders of those targeted communities on asset management to ensure that the infrastructure will be maintained beyond the project.
Overall the project will directly benefit 20,600 people (3,300 HHs). Those populations are going to use those roads to reach agricultural lands, schools, markets, and movements to the other communities.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, the NW region is facing huge humanitarian needs. The unstable situation in NWS area caused by the ongoing war in Syria, and the effects of COVID-19 and cholera epidemic has increasingly adverse effects on the economic and social situation. Both IDPs and HCs struggle to find a job to secure their income and get their basic needs. Which reflects negatively on the other life services such as education, health and nutrition.IHR assessed the needs by KII and FGDs meeting with community members including females, PWDs, youth and adults. It aimed to engage all community segments with the assessment to ensure that their needs were expressed. Where IHR collected their feedback to design activities in this intervention.NA results showed lack of lasting security in Syria, lack of shelter and access to services, and earning opportunities are the main barriers against the rotation of the lifecycle. This led to a catastrophic increase in unemployment and poverty. The infrastructure in NW Syria has been effected negatively by the crisis. Perhaps the most prominent of these affected sectors are the roads, which are considered the main nerve for people's moving toward markets and other service facilities. The heavy winter rains triggered flooding, which damaged roads and drainage networks and negatively impacted the physical access as well as the safety and dignity of residents. This further limits access to markets, livelihood opportunities, health, and others

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Base on IHR PSEA policy and before starting project interventions, IHR will conduct a SEA risk assessment to identify all potential SEA risks such as :1- Risk of SEA act by non-PSEA trained staff while providing the support to BNFs, 2-BNFs donot report SEA cases for IHR staff, which results in the IHR being unable to investigate these cases and provide the needed assistance to them.Mitigation: After an in-depthassessment for these risks, IHR through collaboration with PSEA focal point will bedesigned the mitigation plan for all these risks as follows Mitigation measures:1- IHR will ensure that all its staff had signed a code of conduct to prevent acts of SEA by its staff and all recruited staff should attend PSEA training before any contact with BNFs.2- Beneficiary awareness-raising (Beneficiaries must be aware of: what SEA is, what their rights are in regard to SEA, what they can do if SEA occurs.3- Establish safe andaccessible community reporting channels to report SEA complaints tool will be also available (e.g. complaints box/telephone hotline to deal with these cases)

The possibility of adolescents facing Abuse, Violence, and Harassment during the project activities Probability: Low Impact: High Mitigation measures: - All staff members, volunteers, and suppliers have signed the code of conduct, child safeguarding policy, and PSEA Policy. - The PSEA adviser and Protection adviser follow up on any case.- The parents don't allow their adolescents girls and youth females to attend the programs.Probability: Medium.Impact: High. 9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1- All protective tools and materials needed to avoid COVID-19 infections are available during the project cycle.2- Timely disbursement of funds.3- Stable security situation.Risks and mitigation: 1- Corvid -19 and cholera outbreak in the target location.Mitigation: Ensuring that all protective measures had been carefully considered and applied to avoid infections at all stages of the project such as social distancing at least two meters, providing masks and gloves for all persons involved (field staff, vendors, drivers, and ensuring the availability of sterilization materials at distribution points). Holding awareness sessions by targeting people to raise their awareness about these diseasesز2- Delay in funds Mitigation: Senior level of coordination with the SCHF team and responding to requirements to push them to release the requested payments at the optimal dates. 3- Changing the security situation Mitigation: Monitoring and analysis security situation, temporary suspension of field works. When it is impossible to implement the project due to the bad security condition, IHR will develop a plan B for project implementation to change the project location to safer areas after advanced collaboration and coordination with the SCHF team and ERL cluster.4- Cash not spent on intended needs. Mitigation: The CVA will be restricted to proposal components delivered by the beneficiary to start his business only.
