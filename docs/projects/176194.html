Project Summary

The integrated CP. GBV and MRE project is in line with the Protection Cluster Objectives, the project will ensure provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services; and prevent and mitigate Protection risks through enhanced preparedness. The project targets to reach an average of 60,000 (CP), 10,000 (GBV) and 8,600 (MRA) boys and girls and their 1200 caregivers. The project will also engage 90,000 individuals (30,000 boys and 25,000 girls, each, and 20,000 men and 15,000women) in awareness raising meetings focused on CP, MRA and GBV, in partnership with the CP community structures.
The CP component aspires to enhance access to comprehensive case management services for vulnerable girls and boys with protection concerns such as UASC; provide vulnerable girls and boys, their caregivers and families, with quality age and gender-appropriate PSS, following community-based approaches and best practices, and strengthen capacity of girls and boys, their caregivers, families, and communities, to prevent and respond to violence through systems and community-strengthening mechanisms; FTR of UASC and placement in alternative care within framework of comprehensive case management, referrals to specialized services); focused PSS for CAAFAG capacity building of CP staff, frontline workers, teachers, government/local authorities in CPiE; strengthening CBCPCS and case management (identification & documentation of children with protection concerns, case management capacity building) – reflecting the Child Protection Sub Cluster’s overall strategy and objectives.
For GBV, the project will strengthen life-saving assistance meeting the unique and unmet needs of child survivors, specifically adolescent girls, and engaging male/female youth and adolescents in GBV prevention. The project will include the following key interventions in all locations, based on specific assessed need, disaggregated by sex and age: GBV case management; provision of PSS; safe referrals (medial, legal, livelihood, other); provision of legal advice to survivors; GBV information management; engagement of and advocacy to police and justice actors (formal and traditional) to strengthen access to justice; monitoring of GBV risks for prevention/mitigation through safety audit & safety perception mapping; community engagement interventions to prevent and respond to GBV such as establishment of Women & Girl Safe Spaces & comprehensive programming per the WGSS South Sudan Guidelines; establishment/strengthening of community based protection/GBV structures and systems including women’s groups; community based advocacy and awareness raising, especially engaging male/female youth as change agents in adoption of positive masculinities, respect for women and girls’ equal rights, ending of social acceptance of violence (especially sexual/physical violence) and positive attitudes/behaviours that promote the end of GBV.
The project will also implement MRE activities, according to IMAS, NTSG, World Vision approved MRE SOPs and in alignment with the MASC – MRE Working Group and South Sudan National Mine Action Strategy 2018-2021: Training and accreditation of MRE Teams through approved third-party trainer/training programme; MRE Assessment; Community engagement / mobilization of women, girls, men and boys; Provision of integrated MRE and Community Liaison, linked with Child Protection and Education to particularly reach girls and boys in and out of school, and facilitate integration of MRE in schools; Use of approved/standard MRE materials (i.e. banners, leaflets, warning signs, etc) and age-appropriate/gender-sensitive means of communication; Use of all IMSMA tools and forms for complete and timely information management/reporting per SOPs; Implementation of Quality Monitoring System including Quality Assurance (internal, external) and Quality Controls; Advocacy to greater prioritize MRE as a life-saving humanitarian intervention, especially for children.

1 - Needs

An estimated 2.7 million people, including 2.3 million children, are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection in 2021. Children are disproportionately affected by crisis and exposed to multiple protection risks like recruitment by armed groups, psychosocial distress, family separation, violence and exploitation in 61 most affected counties. Since the outbreak of violence in December 2013, 26,18 (12,214girls and 13,970 boys) cases of unaccompanied and separated children (UASCs), missing and other vulnerable children have been documented in South Sudan and only 6,348 reunified, with up to 4,000 children yet to be reunified. High level of stress, loss of friends and family members are also taking a toll on mental health and emotional development of children, further exacerbated by the COVID-19 outbreak. Studies suggest that at least one in three respondents met the criteria for a mental disorder. A recent FSNMS assessment found that 30 per cent of children had behavioral change, showing signs of distress due to repeated exposure to conflict and shocks.In 2021, some 2 million people are estimated to be affected by GBV. Armed conflict, sub-national organized violence, COVID-19 restrictions and flooding have exacerbated the already extreme levels of GBV. About 97 per cent of the reported GBV incident survivors, were female. A fifth of survivors were less than 18 years old. Intimate partners accounted for more than half of the alleged perpetrators. Displaced women and girls continue to bear the brunt of GBV. A third of people displaced live in communities where women and girls avoid certain areas due to fear for their safety, while basic GBV risk mitigation measures around sanitation facilities remain extremely rare outside large IDP camps. Similarly, 49 per cent of affected women and girls who are in need of services have no access to GBV services and only 12 per cent of the community have awareness on available GBV services. Based on the 2020 GBV IMS data analysis, 29 per cent of GBV survivors in need of safe house did not obtain the service because of lack of safe houses. Similarly, 49 per cent of affected women and girls who are in need of services have no access to GBV services and only 12 per cent of the community have awareness on available GBV services. Based on the 2020 GBV IMS data analysis, 29 per cent of GBV survivors in need of safe house did not obtain the service because of lack. The continuous conflict dynamics and natural disasters perpetuated women’s vulnerability and exposure to GBV. As such, many women and girls stay in the PoC sites, transitioned to the IDP camps because of concerns about sexual violence and other forms of GBV. Formal and customary laws on marriage, land and property rights, among other support discrimination against women and afford them few legal rights. In South Sudan, 19.33 million m² of land is suspected of being contaminated with landmine and explosive remnants of war (ERW). The highest level of contamination is predominantly located in the Equatorias, along the main routes and areas for returnees from Uganda. Explosive hazards inhibit civilians from collecting water or firewood, cultivating land, attending school, and receiving health care services, among other critical daily needs. They further hinder humanitarian organizations from accessing vulnerable communities or providing lifesaving assistance in conflict-affected areas. While substantial progress has been made in the monitoring and clearing of explosive contamination, landmines and ERW continue to pose a threat to civilians and humanitarians in South Sudan. In 2021, more than 654,800 people are estimated to be at risk of injury or death from of landmines and ERW.

2 - Project activities

For Child Protection the project prioritizes Case Management for vulnerable and at risk children, Support to UASC by facilitating Family Tracing and Renunciation TR for Unaccompanied and Separated Children; Provision of MHPSS to children and their caregivers, capacity building of actors on CPiE for WVI staff and community volunteers etc; supporting community based child Protection including Committee, children and adolescent clubs/groups, life skill sessions, peer to peer support, awareness raising , establishing /strengthen CP referral; establishment /strengthening CP referral pathways and awareness raising for 60,000 people targeted under CP sub cluster.With the high prevalence of GBV cases as a result of forced/early marriages, the dowry system, intimate partner violence, and risks from cultural tasks assigned to women/girls, WV will implement a comprehensive GBV response from provision of PSS services, engaging women and girls in IGA activities at the Women and girls safe spaces, as well as improving awareness on GBV forms, causes and consequences; strengthen Child Protection and GBV reporting mechanisms, referral pathways, engage local authorities and community leaders to act as leaders or advocates for improved CP reporting and monitoring, as well as to take local leadership in CP and GBV prevention. Protection Objective 2 – the project will prevent, mitigate and respond to Mine Risk issues through enhanced preparedness and resilience. Key Activities - EORE Assessment - Training and accreditation of EORE Teams (dedicated team leader and educators) - Implementation of age and gender sensitive EORE continuously provided to women, girls, boys and men - Support integration of EORE into schools - Printing and/or Use and/or Distribution of EORE Materials (Banners, Leaflets, Warning Signs, etc) - Use of tools/forms for IMSMA Reporting - Quality Monitoring System - Advocacy for EORE In alignment with the MASC & South Sudan National Mine Action Strategy, the project will contribute to Strategic Goal 3 – Mine Risk Education. WV will focus exclusively on EORE, integrating age & gender sensitive approaches to EORE with Child Protection, Education and other humanitarian programming. All EORE activities will be according to WV’s approved/desk accredited EORE SOPs (July 2018). The project will include dedicated EORE Teams to ensure prioritization of EORE objectives and compliance with International Mine Action Standards EORE Programming and Implementation (IMAS), Quality Assurance & Controls, Reporting per IMSMA. EORE Teams will complete annual EORE Training provided by approved UNMAS/NMAA third party trainers, with training plans reviewed and approved prior; this will be followed by annual accreditation by UNMAS/NMAA for all team members. All EORE content and methods will follow approved IMAS/NTSG/EORE SOPs. The project will procure, print and/or receive in-kind approved EORE banners, leaflets and warning signs (international, local) to facilitate EORE. All EORE will be reported per EORE SOPs and IMSMA, including the completion and timely submission of EORE Weekly Report, Monthly Progress Reports. Reporting will also include for Hazards, Victim & Accident Reports per SOPs. In alignment with the NMAS 2018-2021, World Vision will also contribute to Strategic Goal 1.The project takes note of the differences in needs among the different groups, for instance, physical protection and privacy are important for GBV, CP and MA prevention meaningful access is critical for disabled persons. Information regarding specific needs for the different groups of people will be obtained through needs analysis conducted using focus group discussions and key informant interviews. The information obtained will be critical for decision making, in case WV find needs beyond the scope of the project’s work, referrals are made to other clusters /sectors for appropriate actions.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Information about sexual exploitation and abuse will be shared in community meetings, community trainings and awareness sessions. They will be made to understand the vice and how to report.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will conduct needs analysis of identified prior to implementation of project activities to identify needs of vulnerable groups. The needs analysis will be held to identify needs for each target group. The project will acquire feedback after implementing project activities to determine the extend to which beneficiaries are satisfied with the service provided. This will be done through focus group discussions and post distribution monitoring (PDM) of supplies. The project will be deliberate to conduct FGDs for these special groups of persons with disabilities, illiterate and the elderly, to know how they are being impacted by the project, and where we can adjust to fit in their needs to benefit maximally for the project interventions.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Project information will reach community through holding meetings with local leaders, volunteers and local committee members. Community sensitization meetings will be carried as at the project launch and throughout in order for beneficiaries to understand their entitlements and roles to play. Complaint Feedback mechanism will be set up in schools and the community by mounting helpdesks, suggestion boxes and we as mobile phones numbers. Project monitoring visits carried by World Vision team will enhance relationships with communities and the levels of interactions and community meetings will also offer opportunity for sharing information with beneficiaries and as well as responding to the complaints and feedback raised by the community. Site Help Desks information Boards at service points/schools/health facilities House to house - Verbal Community mobilization – Verbal Mass information Communication – Radio (where possible) Distribution of IEC Materials, Help Desks, QMS, Participatory MRE sessions that seek feedback at conclusion of community sessions, per MRE SOPs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback will be gathered from vulnerable members of the community by deliberately interacting with them and including them in sample population when conducting surveys, monitoring visits and post distribution monitoring. They will also be encouraged to voice their views through the complaint feedback mechanism that would be set in the community. Feedback will be discussed at the office level as well as with the community and appropriate action agreed upon will be implemented to improve the project’s service delivery. This will entail either feedback good practice to be continued or adopted in other areas or a complaint on something that require to stop or be improved.
