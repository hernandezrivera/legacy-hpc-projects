Project Summary

The project aims at providing local communities including host communities and returnees with WASH activities to strengthen their resilience. The activities of the proposed project will provide basic services, especially clean drinking water and personal hygiene kits inside schools, through several activities that include providing services with high efficiency and respecting the dignity of the beneficiaries without discrimination. The project focuses on installing solar energy systems for working boreholes in those areas to secure clean drinking water and rehabilitation of the WASH facilities in schools to ensure clean and sustainable environment for students within their schools.
The project targets Rural Damascus area, specifically, Al-Tall, Menin, Jabaadin, Beit Sahm, Nashabiyeh, and Qasemiyeh.
The activities are as identified per location as follows:
Menin: WIS including the rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in the Menin Secondary School for Girls. The estimated number of BNFs is 400 (400 G).
Al-Tall: WIS including the rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in Dhahiat Albaath Secondary School. The number of BNFs is 600 (600 B).
Jabaadin: WIS including the rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in Martyr Ali Al Taweel School second cycle and Martyr Muhammad Wafa Al-Taweel School first cycle. The number of BNFs is 508(254 B, 254 G).
Beit Sahm: Providing and installing solar power for one borehole. The number of BNFs is 6000 individuals /1200HHs (1200 W,1200 M,2000G,1600 B).
Nashabiyeh: Providing and installing solar power for one borehole. The number of BNFs is 7000 individuals /1400HHs (1400 W,1400 M,2700 G,1500B).
Qasemiyeh: Providing and installing solar power for one borehole. The number of BNFs is 3500 individuals /700 HHs (700 W, 700 M, 1100 G, 1000 B).
EPDC has an independent MEAL department that monitors the implementation of all activities during and after the implementation of the activity, through periodic visits as identified in the action plan for each activity. The MEAL team accompanies the programs team during the handover and reception of the rehabilitation work sites. The MEAL team department is responsible for introducing the complaints response mechanism to the project stakeholders especially, the beneficiaries. They will ensure the channels of complaints (complaints box, phone number, email, WhatsApp) are visible and clear by distributing business cards, setting up roll-ups, and receiving the complaints through the on-site monitor. The MEAL department is also responsible for eliciting the results after implementing the activities to produce learning.
All the activities included in this project require direct coordination with government institutions responsible for this type of activity. EPDC has coordinated in advance with the MOE to reach the most affected schools regarding the activity of rehabilitating sanitation facilities. EPDC has also coordinated with the Directorate of Water Resources in Damascus and Rural Damascus to obtain information on the actual need to operate boreholes in the proposed locations. EPDC has conducted field visits to verify the actual needs for the implementation of these activities. The completion and implementation of these activities require obtaining special approvals from the two authorities mentioned within a specific period of time. EPDC has never been denied similar approvals given the existing experience of EPDC in implementing WASH projects in Rural Damascus and the ongoing MOU with MOE.

3 - Needs

Ten years into the Syrian conflict, the situation remains complex and is continuously challenging the ability to meet the needs of the affected population. According to HNO 2022, an estimated 14.6 million people currently remain in need of inter-sectoral humanitarian assistance- an increase of 1.2 million from 2021, with about 44.4% of those in severe need. The rapid rise in needs is driven by a deepening economic crisis, climatic shocks, and recurring hostilities. Additionally, much of the country’s public infrastructure and services have been damaged or destroyed. Further, electricity shortage and fuel shortages further undermine the functionality of essential services, livelihoods, and delivery of assistance. There are major gaps in the provision of and access to essential basic services. Most notably at least 47 % of the population relies on unsafe water sources; at least 70 % of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewage systems are not functional and only half of the primary health care centers are functional. According to HNAP Nationwide Governorate Profiles (2022) published by WFP, about 88% of the population are lacking access to safe water. Because of the ongoing financial deterioration, Covid-19, Cholera and shortage in services and resources such as fuel and electricity, the people's ability to secure clean and safe water for drinking, EPDC plan to target the Rural Damascus with WIS and providing and installing solar power systems to boreholes.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

EPDC will ensure that protection principles are mainstreamed in all stages of the project (from design to implementation and monitoring/evaluation), and gender-specific needs are taken into account and addressed throughout the activities. EPDC will apply protection and gender mainstreaming as follows: prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm to the targeted communities. The proposed activities will follow the principle of ‘Do no harm’, encouraging inclusive participation of women and men, and ensuring relevance across population groups in all activities by taking into consideration differing needs.Throughout the implementation of all activities, EPDC will ensure that strong mitigation measures are put in place to prevent risks of GBV and SEA by rehabilitating separate gender latrines and installing locks on the doors and also conduct the WIS activities during the summer break to ensure the safety of the children.EPDC MEAL team Unit will also conduct monitoring on the conducted rehabilitation works to ensure it is conducted in a safe and timely manner. The team will also ensure at the end of the works delivery that beneficiaries are satisfied with provided works and the behavior of EPDC staff and contractors, enabling EPDC to take corrective action in a timely manner. EPDC will bear into consideration PWD by ensuring PWD latrines are rehabilitated in each of the targeted schools. At the same time, EPDC will make sure that the CRM channels are clear to BNFs.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1.Security situation is stable and no sudden displacement movement occurs2.Market is stable and materials are available3.The weather conditions are good so they do not disrupt the rehabilitation works4.The precautionary measures against C-19 and AWD/Cholera are in place and the project does not risk their spreadRisks and Mitigation:1.The security situation escalates and movement is restricted accordingly. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: Coordinate with the local community, official authorities, and relevant sector. Plan mitigation measures to ensure safety of beneficiaries, EPDC staff, and contractors. If the situation keeps escalating, negotiate scale-up in other project locations that are safer.2. Market is unstable and materials are no longer available or their prices increase to a point disrupting the works. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Communicate any challenges in the availability and prices of materials and negotiate alternatives similar in quality.3. The weather conditions are bad causing delay in implementation of the activities. Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation: Adhere to workplan and keep updated with weather forecast. The WIS will take place during summer break so that the schools become ready for the students.4.The project increase risk of C-19 and AWD/Cholera spread. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: EPDC team will ensure the use of PPEs on site including gloves, facemasks, and hand sanitizers.
