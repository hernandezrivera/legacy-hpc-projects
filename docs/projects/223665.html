Project Summary

This project seeks to strengthen community-based child protection systems and promote the reintegration of vulnerable children in conflict-affected communities in Borno and Adamawa. By creating safe spaces, providing psychosocial support, and equipping community members with the skills to identify and respond to child protection risks, the initiative aims to foster resilience and restore normalcy in the lives of affected children including those living with disability.

1 - Needs

Conflict and insecurity in Northeast Nigeria have severely disrupted the lives of children, leaving many vulnerable to violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, and psycho-social distress. Children in internally displaced persons (IDP) camps, secondary displaced children, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), and children with disabilities under the age of 17 face heightened protection risks. These include recruitment by non-state armed groups, early marriage, sexual violence, inadequate parental care, and exclusion from essential services. These risks are compounded by limited access to food, education, health services, significantly affecting children’s physical, mental, and emotional well-being.In 2024, the ongoing conflict and socio-economic instability are projected to exacerbate these challenges, increasing the demand for child protection interventions. This project aims to address these needs by strengthening community-based protection mechanisms, providing psychosocial support, and facilitating the reintegration of vulnerable children, including UASC, children formerly associated with armed groups (CAAG), and children with disabilities. Additionally, the project emphasizes community ownership and sustainability through the active participation of local stakeholders.

2 - Activities

Act. 1. Provide group and individual counseling services to 125 children and adolescents Act. 2. Establishing 4 child-friendly spaces (2 in each state) in project locations to provide a supportive environment for children.Act. 3. Provide psychosocial support and tailored case management for 8315 boys and girls, including those affected by grave violations of child rights and children with disability.Act.4. Training 170 community-based child protection committee (CBCPC) members and social welfare workers to address key child protection issues, including identifying and supporting UASC and children with disabilities.Act. 5. Facilitating socio-economic reintegration for 100 children, using skill training and innovative artistic therapies such as advocacy podcasts, storytelling, and expressive arts to support emotional recovery and resilience.Act. 6. Conduct community dialogues sessions and town hall meetings to raise awareness on child protection issues, positive parenting and existing referral pathways targeting 3000 beneficiaries

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project integrates protection principles to ensure that all activities are safe, accessible, and inclusive. Sirri Care Foundation will:• Work in consultation with IDPs, host communities including persons with disability to promote the full participation of men, women, boys, and girls.• Mainstream GBV prevention by raising awareness, disseminating information, and establishing trusted reporting systems for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).• Respond to PSEA cases through referrals to specialized services while ensuring confidentiality and dignity for survivors.• Ensure that children with disabilities and UASC have equal access to information about services and referral pathways.• Regularly train project staff and community volunteers on protection principles, including age, gender, and diversity considerations, to enhance the delivery of inclusive interventions.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure the voices of the most vulnerable community members are heard, the project will thus;• Establish gender-sensitive complaint mechanisms, including booths, feedback desks, and strategically placed boxes.• Provide accessible channels for women, girls, boys, female-headed households, UASC, and children with disabilities to raise concerns about program quality and staff behavior.• Organize regular community feedback sessions to review complaints and refine project strategies, ensuring they align with the needs of the most marginalized groups.• Collaborate with community leaders, social welfare workers, and CBCPC members to ensure transparent and responsive complaint resolution processes.• Include an anonymous feedback system to encourage open reporting without fear of reprisal.
