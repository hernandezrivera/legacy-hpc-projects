Project Summary

This project aims to provide multi-sectoral support in Khartoum and Gedaref targeting South Sudanese refugees, Ethiopian refugees, other refugees and host communities based on the needs and capacity, with a focus on protection, GBV, FSL, S/NFI, and energy and environment activities. DRC currently supports refugee populations from Ethiopia, South Sudan and other counties in Khartoum and Gedaref. In 2022, DRC will seek to strengthen the refugee response.

1 - Needs

Sudan hosts more than 1.1 million refugees who live in different parts of the country. Sudan hosts the largest population of South Sudanese refugees in the region, with more than 762,704 individuals reported across the country in 2021 out of whom 51 per cent are women. Most were farmers and small livestock holders who have lost access to their lands and livestock assets - prime sources of income, thus affecting their food security and nutrition status. In 2021, Sudan also hosts over 100,000 Eritrean refugees and an influx of 200,000 new Ethiopian, Eritrean and third-nationality refugees are expected to arrive within the next six months as they flee Tigray, the norther region of Ethiopia where armed conflict has erupted early November 2020. For planning purposes, needs are based on HNO, RCF's multi-sectoral severity ranking and any other needs assessment resources available at this stage. Upon implementation, DRC dedicates the inception steps of its projects on conducting targeted, area-bases needs analyses that inform the activities and best approaches to adopt in each context. Through DRC’s work with refugees in Khartoum and Gedaref, the organization has seen a growing need for protection services, and emergency support, including shelter, NFI and food support.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Based on the needs and its technicality, DRC will prioritize meeting the protection needs of refugee populations, including GBV. DRC will work to raise awareness on protection topic, establish and/or reinforce community based structures, strengthen the capacity of service providers and provide Cash under MPCA as well as materiel support for SGBV survivors. DRC will also seek to improve the food security of refugees through Cash for work activities. DRC will also provide shelter and NFIs to refugees, as these are currently critical needs. DRC will ensure that protection is considered in all service provision and distributions.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Shelter and NFI distribution can have an environmental impact as many materials contain single-use plastics. DRC will continue to look into sourcing more local and environmentally friendly NFI and shelter materials whenever possible. In addition, DRC will seek to source locally available food assistance to avoid the environmental impact of importation and transportation over long distances.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

DRC develops activities and selection criteria based on a context specific risk analysis and continued engagement with local communities. All activities and delivery methods are developed in consideration of the risks and vulnerabilities of local communities and target individuals/groups. DRC approaches are built around community-based protection which are particularly effective at mitigating risk factors and strengthening protective factors in the community. Throughout implementation, DRC conducts ongoing protection monitoring in order to identify new risks and vulnerabilities and assess the impact of our programming on the protection environment. In 2021, DRC has strengthened protection mainstreaming through trainings of all its programme staff.
