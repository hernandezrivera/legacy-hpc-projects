Project Summary

SI is intervening in Maiduguri, Ngala, Dikwa, Askira Uba and Monguno . SI have carried shelter response in various locations across Borno State and will continue as the need arises. The Emergency Shelter for the IDPs, is aimed at supporting displaced persons affected by crisis in Borno state.
The sole aim of the intervention involves distribution and construction of shelters to support population with desperate need of shelter. Despite all efforts by all humanitarian shelter actors, and with the crises in its tenth year, over 97,000 households (sector, shelter gaps July 2019) are in desperate needs of shelters in Borno state, this shows there is still the need to step up with shelter intervention across the state. SI plans to distribute Emergency shelter kits, construct and distribute emergency shelter (BAMA), and transitional shelter.
The targets remain ever continues movement of new arrivals at various LGA’s and existing IDPs residing in camps. The intervention is not limited to these groups of populations only as we have witnessed excess flooding which have displaced large numbers of populations without shelters. Fire outbreaks in camps has it shares in displacing persons from there shelters and rendering them homeless.

1 - Needs

The complex emergency and conflict in North East Nigeria started in 2009 and has resulted in the displacement of over 2 million people . Overall, 7.1 million people are in dire need of assistance across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states . The situation of IDPs is critical, as they are living in severely congested temporary camps, prone to regular epidemics, rain, flooding and fire incidents . Due to increased hostilities and insecurity, the number of registered displacements continue to increase (by 2% over last 4 months’ period), and is expected to continue, as the Nigerian Government Forces recently adopted the strategy of establishing “super camps” to strengthen their defences in areas with population concentration, thereby removing troops from remote areas. This strategy will result in further displacements of IDPs, moving to secure locations in August and September 2019 alone, over 26 thousand persons moved triggered by factors such as conflict, insecurity, fear of attacks, military operations and poor living conditions, were registered. Recent series of assessments conducted in North East Borno (Shelter GAPs in NE Nigeria July) by shelter/CCCM & NFI partners reported 97,817 households in need of shelter in 14 LGAs in Borno. The emergency shelter distributed by partners have a short lifespan. Many shelters which were distributed years ago are in dire need of replacement and repairs. The assessment in July showed that over 13,272 shelters need immediate replacements in Borno. Over 6,500 households either live out in open or use makeshift shelter made of blankets or wrappers. Flooding and camp fires pose a serious threat to shelters in congested camps. Rainy season flooding during months of July and August 2019 affected over 10,757 HH across 68 camps in Dikwa, Gwoza, Jerem Kagam Magumeri , Maiduguri, Konduga, Monguno and Ngala. Other LGAs were also affected. Fire outbreaks in April- June and then November 2019 resulted in destruction of thousands of shelters in Gajigana, Monguno, Dikwa and Maiduguri. In an assessment carried out by SI in its areas of intervention, 20% of female respondents reported living in emergency shelters compared to male respondents (6%) and 56% of respondents reported damage to their shelters, caused by storms (61%), flooding (12%), conflict (11%), and the environment (11%). The CCCM partners identified congestion in the camps and land acquisition for construction of humanitarian infrastructures as main bottle neck for humanitarian shelter response. As a result, provision of adequate humanitarian response to these needs is impacted.

2 - Activities

Emergency shelter Kits: With continues movement of persons from one location to another, the number of new arrivals continue to increase across different locations. SI has been intervening through this activity and will continue to provide emergency shelters kits in coordination with Shelter/CCCM & NFI sector, a strategy which has proven to be successful in all areas of SI intervention. For the proposed project year 2020, SI through its proposed 1000 Emergency Shelter Kits will continue to respond to the expected movements of population in its 4 areas of intervention as important influxes have been witnessed all across Borno State. While the design of the shelter will remain unchanged from previous OFDA cycle, SI will continue to monitor how safe recipients feel in the shelters, through periodic Focus Group Discussions. As emergency shelters cannot be locked, beneficiaries often report feeling unsafe in the shelters. In the coming phase of the project, SI will conduct further consultations with beneficiaries to look at how to support them to find a way to reduce their vulnerability to theft and enhance protection. The approach is carried out via distribution of these kits to the new arrivals who in turn will be guided on how to carry out the constructions themselves. Special vulnerable groups will be assisted with the construction of their shelters.Emergency shelter (BAMA):According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2019 for Nigeria, emergency shelter needs have increased in a context of continued large-scale displacements. The HNO states that some 44% of IDPs in camps in need of emergency shelters are living in self-made/makeshift shelters in Borno State. In most sites, shelters need to be upgraded as a result of the short lifespan of shelters and population movements, especially with the recently passed rainy season. The unavailability of land to meet the shelter needs of new arrivals is also a major constraint, and existing camps need to be decongestedIn light of all these challenges, SI is planning to carry out Emergency shelter construction and distribution of 1000 Emergency shelters (BAMA) to the neediest persons residing in camps across SI intervening locations. The project is built on the principle that all people must have access to minimal life conditions and the that humanitarian needs have to be met as quickly as possible, without distinction of sex, ethnicity, nationality, religion or social status.Transitional shelter: in most LGAs across Borno state, over 80% of the IDP population live in makeshift shelters or emergency shelters constructed by Humanitarian aid agencies over past years without possibility of return in the short/mid-term to replace or upgrade these shelters. SI proposes to ensure a community-based construction of transitional shelters, offering more privacy and a longer lifespan. In 2020, SI will therefore intervene with 250 T-shelters and ensure the training of carpenters and will hire unskilled workers from the community. SI will provide technical support to the targeted most vulnerable households in the construction of the shelters with a design respecting SPHERE standards and dedicated support teams. Shelter repair kits will thus be distributed in order for communities to be able to maintain longer their habitation;Provision of Shelter Repair Kits: SI plans to distribute 1500 shelter repair kits in Maiduguri, Dikwa, Monguno and Ngala for shelters in high need for rehabilitation.Prepositioning and Distribution of 4000 NFI Kits: SI plans to distribute 4000 NFI kits in Maiduguri, Dikwa, Monguno and Ngala. There is a huge need for NFI kits. SI proposes to preposition 2000 NFI kits for pipeline to be used in emergencies while 2,000 kits will be distributed to cater the needs of IDPs.
