Project Summary

This project aims to ensure appropriate and effective WASH preparedness, a strong capacity for emergency response towards a coordinated and integrated approach in all WASH‑related interventions, in concert with all WASH actors, as well as to strengthen national WASH governance system in accountability, advocacy, evidence‑based planning and inter/intra‑sectorial coordination. The Cluster coordination support interventions for enhancing the WASH gender and protection-response programme including disability mainstreaming in WASH actions.
The project also aims at strengthening cross sectoral information management practices such as WASH and Health data analysis, but also to better monitor the quality of the WASH response through dedicated systems (response monitoring, quality assurance, feedback and complaints mechanism) for operational gap analysis.

Needs

UNICEF, as Cluster Lead Agency, has been ensuring the coordination of the WASH Cluster in oPt since 2009. Whilst UNICEF has been supporting the transition of cluster coordination functions to the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) since 2013, it has also provided continuous support to WASH Cluster partners for a more transparent and improved coordinated WASH response in the West Bank and Gaza. In that respect, UNICEF has been supporting capacity‑building of cluster coordination functions to PWA and the concept has been well-developed and moving forward over recent years. Given ongoing structural reforms within the water and wastewater sector, formal structures and resources have yet to be established to empower PWA with the leadership to facilitate all cluster activities and developing and maintaining a strategic vision and operational response plan that will enable cluster partners to respond more effectively to the needs of the affected population. In 2018, UNICEF sustained its support to sectoral coordination through the provision of a dedicated Sector Coordinator and Information Manager working within the PWA office to strengthen essential coordination functions and the capacitation of existing staff. This WASH coordination unit has continued to advance the Cluster work plan to improve information management and analysis of the WASH situation and the response of partners, support the integration of development and humanitarian frameworks, support contingency planning and preparedness exercises amongst sector members and facilitate technical exchange as well as learning and capacitation between members. Pending the completion of reforms within the PWA, this project seeks to ensure continued coordination capacity at the national level, to support the transition process and to further strengthen humanitarian coordination, and ensure preparedness, mitigation and response capacity for potential spikes of conflict and seasonal natural disasters. A number of specific needs have been identified, including but not limited for the support to: ‑ Managing and advancing the cluster work plan: including the distribution of coordination/sectoral work to other actors, based on discussions and agreements with all relevant stakeholders. Ensure prioritization following the protection framework, evidence‑based actions, and gap‑filling and to enhance accountable, predictable and effective emergency WASH response to the affected population.‑ Partner capacity-building and programme quality: to address capacity needs identified through the cluster contingency planning process, a series of trainings/exercises will be developed and implemented with partners; in addition to continued efforts to support mainstreaming of the Core Humanitarian Standards (including gender and protection mainstreaming).‑ Technical working groups: to address arising issues or to maximize learning, for example, gender and protection‑mainstreaming, monitoring of the quality of the WASH response, KAP surveys, desludging practices, among others. ‑ Inter/intra‑sectoral coordination: to strengthen formal links between the WASH sector and other relevant sectors, such as Education and Health, in the exchange of WASH information, advocacy and fund mobilization, and identifying lessons‑learned and best‑practices for integrated programming. ‑ Engaging in sectoral reform discussions: to identify potential areas of integration between development and humanitarian frameworks, and to support implementation once established.

Activities

The activities planned to be undertaken by the humanitarian WASH coordination unit will include, but are not limited to the following: −Support the coordination & information management of Gaza’s sub-national WASH Cluster taking in consideration unstable conditions that are subject to interruption from effects of ongoing conflict, power failures and seasonal climate risks, and harsh weather in winter and summer.−Support emergency planning and response in relation to water quality, winterization/summarization, planning basic service delivery associated with electricity and emergency fuel for essential water and sanitation services in addition to materials entry into Gaza.−Continue the implementation of the transition strategy with PWA and UNICEF as co-chairs of the clusters in the West Bank. −Support the capacity-building of governmental & non-governmental stakeholders to assume responsibility as outlined in the transition plan of the West Bank. Continued support to the PWA/service providers on key information and data management for adequate analysis, monitoring and reporting in both the West Bank and Gaza.−Facilitate and support harmonised assessments and monitoring, data collection and analysis of key vulnerabilities, including water scarcity, water quality, flood risk, WASH in institutions (health & schools) among others in West bank & Gaza. Ensure response monitoring data is regularly collected and analysed to provide regular & clear information on gaps to all WASH stakeholders.−Support service delivery for acute vulnerabilities of populations in Gaza and West Bank according to humanitarian caseload through coordination of WASH partners, including government authorities and development actors.−Support WASH actors in exchanging best practices and strengthening capacity in terms of community-based DRR / resilience, mainstreaming of protection and gender issues and technical competencies in West Bank and Gaza.−Inform strategic decision making of the HC and HCT for the humanitarian response, including a review of the humanitarian architecture while strengthening sub national WASH Cluster coordination in Gaza.−Participate in planning & strategy development of Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) in oPt and strengthening further Cluster strategic plans and informing government master plans.−Advocate for greater dialogue on inter/intra sectoral and crosscutting issues, in particular WASH/Education, WASH/Health, WASH/Food Security, and deeper gender analysis in the WASH sector, including dialogue on establishing links between emergency and development WASH actions through interventions that build resilience of affected communities in oPt. A specific focus on WASH/public health data analysis will be given through the identification key data gaps to inform further cross-sectoral data collection and analyses.−Advocacy utilising the added value of WASH partners combined with evidence-based gap analysis for strengthened advocacy messaging & support to existing structures. Working with Advocacy groups (ex-EWASH) for resource mobilization, materials entry to Gaza, electricity & emergency fuel and explore alternative energy sources in WASH actions among others in oPt. −Continuous & dedicated support to the Gender Focal Point who will inform the project vetting process of the Cluster response plan from a gender & protection perspective. The Gender focal point will also work in collaboration with UN Women & OCHA to support cluster level interventions & capacity of partners on mainstreaming gender, aligned with HNO/HRP 2019 priorities; facilitate participation of women’s organizations in the Cluster structure as well as the dissemination of good practices & knowledge.-The Cluster will support partners in humanitarian WASH projects through the HPC cycle to ensure quality of project outcomes to most vulnerable communities (including the disabled & elderly).
