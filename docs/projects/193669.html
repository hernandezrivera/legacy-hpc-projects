Project Summary

While people were still recovering from the shock of May 2021 hostilities, another round of escalation hit Gaza in August 2022. Besides their human and psychosocial costs, the August 2022 military escalation affected around 1,500 housing units (HNO 2023). Repeated cycle of conflicts and the lack of a political horizon have further compounded the unique socio-economic conditions of blockade which prevail in Gaza. Given the volatility of the security situation, UNRWA in Gaza, which acts as the coordination center for humanitarian response and interventions during emergencies, requires to maintain a high and efficient level of emergency preparedness.
In response, UNRWA aims to provide emergency shelter assistance for affected families through (1) Transitional Shelter Cash Assistance for up to 12 months, and (2) shelter repair for refugee families affected by the August 2022 escalation. The Agency will also continue to enhance its emergency preparedness through: (1) the provision and stockpiling of Non-Food Items (NFIs) in case of emergencies, (2) the provision of assistive devices to people with disabilities affected by emergencies, (3) the maintenance of 54 Designated Emergency Shelters (DESs) and 25 Mobile Emergency Shelters (MESs), (4) equipping DESs and MESs with emergency health response needs, (5) the rehabilitation of Area offices spaces for emergencies, and (6) the training of UNRWA staff for emergency preparedness.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2023 estimated that 591,000 Palestinians in Gaza are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023 to access an adequate shelter including basic households’ supplies, which is an essential component of providing people with a minimum level of privacy, protection and dignity. Gaza is one of the most densely populated areas in the world, with 2.11 million Palestinians living in an area that is 365 km2 with a population density of 5,800 per square kilometer. Gaza suffers from a chronic housing shortage of 120,000 units, with an annual average of 14,000 housing units needed to meet natural population growth. While the occupation remains a major driver of shelter needs in Gaza, the housing crisis is impacted by a combination of different factors; escalations, the blockade, the high costs of construction, and limited economic opportunities because of over 16 years of Israeli restrictions on the import of building materials and equipment, the deteriorating socio-economic situation, and recurring waves of hostilities (HNO 2023). The 2022 Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment found that 59.8 per cent of the assessed households in the Gaza Strip reported existing shelter damage, defects, or issues at their shelters, such as cracks in some walls at 42.7 per cent, openings or cracks in the roof at 30.3 per cent, broken or cracked windows in 17.3 per cent, lack of or bad condition of kitchen 12.7 per cent, and lack of or bad condition of bathroom 9.2 per cent. The MSNA also shows that 83.4 per cent of households took on debt in the 3 months prior to data collection reporting shelter repair/reconstruction as the primary reason. According to the Shelter Cluster, the escalation in August 2022 left around 1,500 housing units damaged or destroyed including 97 units severely damaged, and 26 units destroyed, leaving around 600 individuals still internally displaced. Following the August 2022 hostilities, UNRWA completed the initial assessment of 88 uninhabitable Palestine refugee shelters (21 totally destroyed and 67 severely damaged) in coordination with the Shelter Cluster. UNRWA also initially identified 583 families whose shelters require major repair (220 cases) and minor repair (363 cases) pending further assessments and verification. Additionally, the MSNA WASH analysis revealed that 12.0 per cent of households in Gaza reported being affected by flooding in the 3 years prior to the MSNA data collection, of these households, 41.2 per cent reported their shelter being impacted by flooding, and 87.1 per cent reported floods disrupting their daily activities. The August 2022 escalation was another reminder after the May 2021 hostilities that the situation of Gaza continues to be fragile, and continued focus on the emergency preparedness is necessary. UNRWA in Gaza acts as the coordination center for humanitarian response and interventions, requiring continued maintenance of a high and efficient level of emergency preparedness.

2 - Activities

The Agency plans to support shelter repair works for around 600 Palestine refugee shelters affected by the August 2022 hostilities, varying degrees of damage and needing minor, major or severe damage repair works. The shelters that already received support – damage between US$ 500 and US$ 2,000 – will be excluded. The number of cases for each damage category would be finalized during the assessment and verification process. Eligible refugee families will receive cash grants and technical assistance for shelter rehabilitation work. This assistance measure will be implemented through the self-help approach or a local company that may be contracted depending on the extent of the damage as assessed by UNRWA engineers and verified in coordination with the Shelter Cluster.The Agency will also provide TSCA for up to 12 months to the families whose primary residence became uninhabitable while the reconstruction effort is made in 2023. Priority will be extended to vulnerable refugees, including female and adolescent-headed households, families that include persons with disabilities, and families with elderly relatives.The fragile political situation, unpredictable import restrictions, and global shortages of raw materials, mean there is a significant risk of possible shortages in building materials and equipment in 2023, affecting the cost of proposed interventions. UNRWA continues to monitor the availability and cost of materials in the local market in coordination with the Shelter Cluster. Before refugees are invited to sign the undertaking, UNRWA will verify that the agreed cost of the repairs is still valid. Across all shelter interventions, UNRWA will continue to coordinate with the Shelter Cluster to ensure consistency in assistance. In order to respond to any emergencies, UNRWA will maintain a high and efficient level of emergency readiness in terms of system, process, procedures, and human resources. In preparedness and response to small-scale sudden-onset disasters, such as fires, winter storms, and floods, which are expected to increase in the low-lying Gaza Strip with climate change, UNRWA will secure the capacity to provide NFIs to an estimated 13,000 affected refugee households. These include blankets, mattresses, kitchen kits, cleaning kits, and nylon and tarpaulin sheets, that will be stockpiled in Agency warehouses as a preparedness measure. UNRWA will also enhance its inclusive approach by securing a stock of assistive devices available to support the needs of up to 3,000 persons with disabilities during emergencies.UNRWA will continue to ensure that Agency’s 54 schools equipped to function as Designated Emergency Shelters (DESs) and recently established 25 Mobile Emergency Shelters (MESs) are maintained, secured, and kept ready for emergencies. DESs provide shelter and protection for internally displaced persons (IDPs) with adequate WASH facilities, safety, and security during emergencies, while MESs provides the same needs outside of DESs accessible areas. UNRWA also aims to secure enough health equipment stocks to cover the need for medical points at 54 DESs and 25 MESs that would be activated during the emergency. Furthermore, UNRWA plans to enhance its Area Offices in terms of readiness, safety, and security level as these offices serve the community outside of DESs in times of high, moderate, and low risk during the emergency. UNRWA plans to keep continuous capacity building and enhancement of 2,000 staff members on the emergency roster by carrying out emergency simulations and training. As a member of PSEA network, UNRWA commits to all PSEA mandatory activities across its programmes, and the interventions included in this proposal are not exception. UNRWA adopts a safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on consultations with the beneficiaries and accessible to different groups. The PSEA focal points are trained to receive complaints in multitude of ways, including a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints, and a centralized confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints are in place. To ensure that the beneficiaries are aware of PSEA mechanism, PSEA awareness raising material will be disseminated to all project recipients. UNRWA regularly engage with HCT oPt PSEA network, and project staff will engage with UNRWA’s representative to this body via training and through project monitoring.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback is sent to the management for information, evaluation and corrective action as required. The beneficiaries’ feedback is incorporated in the report to help develop lessons learned and contributes to continuously improve UNRWA’s interventions.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

UNRWA ensures that refugees have direct and regular access to UNRWA staff through its extensive network of area offices, camp services offices, schools, health centres and other installations. A Communication with Communities (CwC) approach is implemented in Gaza that enhances accountability and transparency with stakeholders and assists in meeting the needs of crisis-affected people. Furthermore, telephone hotlines and help lines are established in support of the operation to maximize efficiency and to minimize movement and in-person transactions. The dedicated staff responsible for handling beneficiaries’ concerns and inquiries provide needed information and assistance. UNRWA also runs its own portal where beneficiaries are encouraged to raise any general query with regards to any issue including the quality of services delivered. Staff, majority of whom are drawn from the refugee communities they serve, are involved in the design and monitoring of UNRWA humanitarian response.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNRWA has a multitude of options to receive feedback and ensure accountability to the affected population, including in-person reception in all UNRWA installations, a targeted online and telephone complaints portal (nominal and anonymous), a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints; a centralized (Amman) confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints. Direct (in person) and indirect (press, social media, etc) outreach is further undertaken to disseminate the information on the above mechanisms.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations with a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestine refugees' rights. An integrated humanitarian response, with the inclusion of shelter assistance, is directly related to a growing focus on resilience to withstand and recover from crisis. The TSCA and shelter support reduces current risks by providing relief to strained families and enhancing refugees’ sense of dignity and self-reliance. UNRWA’s dedicated Protection and Neutrality Office guarantees the provision of its services in an impartial and non-discriminatory way. UNRWA regularly assess the level of compliance with Protection standards of the services provided to refugees through a Protection Audit. Across all shelter interventions, UNRWA is in close coordinate with the Shelter Cluster and the Ministry of Public Works and Housing to ensure consistency of assistance.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is mandated to protect the rights of Palestine refugees and provide basic needs in emergencies and non-emergency situations. Assistance is provided based on needs without discrimination. UNRWA remains committed to identifying the specific needs of females and addressing these needs during the implementation of all programmes/services. In Gaza, females represent around 49.5% of total population. UNRWA prioritises protection mainstreaming by incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in all its programmes/services. In addition, UNRWA arranges for people’s access to assistance and pays special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. The location of UNRWA facilities also takes into account safety and access concerns, in particular for women and girls, as well as catchment area and distance from beneficiaries. Existing DESs take into consideration of the considered-to-be less-exposed location throughout the Gaza Strip and the placement of WASH facilities inside the shelter to take into account gender considerations. The mobile DESs will provide further support to people who have difficulties reaching to existing DESs. UNRWA pays special attention to female-headed households, divorced or widowed women who may need assistance for obtaining housing and property rights. UNRWA also implements a requirement for dual signature of men and women for housing options.
