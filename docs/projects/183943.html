Project Summary

With the dynamic of the current political situation and deterioration of security situation in Darfur State, EORE is aiming to address short term emergency needs as well as to contribute to long term sustainable response in line with the thematic areas of the national plan for protection of civilians, . NADA used to deliver EORE projects in support of UNAMID since 2014. In light of UNAMID exit strategy and its’ planned closure in 2020 and UNMAS’s Sudan decision to take over the responsibility to deal with explosive hazards; NADA finds it very efficient and effective to work under United Nations Country Team (UNCT) and hence UNMAS in addressing at risk communities needs with regard to EORE in Darfur. It is paramount to launch EORE activities in support of UNMAS program after the complete closure of UNAMID and hence ODO to ensure better planning and smooth handover. Political and security stability will encourage voluntary return and as thus big numbers of population will be at higher risk in moving back to their home villages and towns without comprehensive knowledge about the present of EO. The strategy of NADA in implementing the project activities is to provide EORE services that meet short-and long-term needs with special focus in the remote areas, rural villages and among nomads. NADA plans the EORE program following the International Mine Action Standards (IMAS) and SNMAS. In the short term, emergency EORE will be provided to at-risk population such as IDPs, returnees, nomads and local communities in the form of direct presentation, Public Information Campaigns (PIDs), and radio program broadcasting. Affected communities including EO victims and PWDs will be given awareness raising on the rights and issues of victims and PWDs. The long-term strategy will focus on integrating EORE into the existing community services and structures through training of social workers, community leaders, members of the community based protection networks (CBPN and community volunteers including children on CB EORE and local population. This EORE project will consider raising landmines/ERW victims’ awareness in terms of pretension of incidence and awareness of rights and victim-related services providers. The project is designed to response to the objectives of Sudan Mine Action Multi-year plan 2020-2023 which aim to have Sudan free of landmine/ERW by 2023. The project also considers the coming of UN Integrated Transitional Assistance Mission in Sudan (UNITAMS), for that the project will be delivered through immediate EORE and community based responses. In all its EORE projects; NADA considered two aspects a) age and gender – appropriate methods of communication will be used. Messages for drama, songs, and public campaigns were developed for different age group with full consideration of culture. NMAC and UNMAS will be consulted prior to printing /production and/or distribution of messages. b) New innovation NADA will pilot new innovation such as social media. The project will be delivered using different methodologies such as direct presentation, social media such as Facebook and YouTube, radio and stable or mobile public campaigns as well as CB EORE targeting social workers and community members. This will contribute to better access of communities to information through the presence of trained community members. Also the project will consider delivery of EORE to school children during the school year, while focusing on the other children out of school during the school holidays, IDP camps, settlements, gathering sites and villages of return and displacement. During limitation of EORE teams ‘movement, the project team will focus on CB EORE, radio and social media channels to ensure wider coverage in case of road locks due to insecurity or COVID outbreak.

1 - Needs

Sudan undertaken radical political change which resulted in signing Juba peace agreement, other developments, adoption of the national plan for protection of civilians, establishment of national and state level committees for the implementation of the national plan, recent military coup etc. Long lasting internal conflicts in Darfur have resulted in people displacement and a legacy of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) including Explosive Ordnance (EO). Contamination of EO led to severe restrictions on emergency and developmental aid. also led to deaths and injury of community members. Majority of livelihood activities in Darfur, are either farmers or nomads. The fear of present of EO and unexploded ordnance (UXOs) impacted agricultural communities and farmers who are afraid to use their land; nomads and cattle headers cannot use pastures to graze their cattle in some areas. In Darfur, according to the Information Management System of Mine Action (IMSMA) which is managed by NMAC, EO contamination resulted in the casualty of 790 individuals in the year 2021. Accidents occurred in villages outside the internal displaced persons (IDPs) camps and left victims who are men, women as well as children. As of September 2021 IMSMA report; 15, 179, 177 square kilometers were released in the five Darfur States; this together with implementation of peace agreement encouraged the movement of the IDPs and Sudanese refugees back to their home villages . This community movement will increase the possibility of more accidents and new victims; if communities will not be reached by EORE at appropriate time. In Darfur context; EORE should be implemented at short term and long term strategies in light of the new UN political integrated transition Assistance Mission in Sudan and the political and security dynamic ,government and humanitarian actors are planning to support the returnees in their home villages since the voluntary return and protection of civilian program are already launched in the five states. Populations in risk and need an urgent EORE program prior to their trip back home as their areas are known for wars and battles and might be contaminated of EO. Moreover, residents of villages, camps and areas required EORE to strengthen their coping mechanism in dealing with the suspected hazard areas or contaminated areas by ERW till clearance operations are achieved. Contamination of ERW including EO and UXOs affected boys, girls, men and women, it prevents normal access to basic needs such as schools, health facilities, security / police centers etc. In some location access to education i.e accessible roads to schools and hospital is very risky which contributed to lack of education and health services to vulnerable communities in particularly children and women. In Darfur women and children have additional social roles such as brining water and fire wood from far distances, this explore them to higher risks of being injured or killed due to the present of EO and/ or UXOs. Women and girls some time reach to 5- 9 Km away from their residence searching for fire wood. Newly arrived returnees especially those who were Sudanese refugees in other countries such as Chad and Central Africa republic are lacking any information of EO /UXOs present risks, impact and prevention ways. Children in particularly from nomads’ groups moved for long distance with their catties and they are facing daily risk of getting injured or maimed due to the contamination of the EO or /and UXOs due to security situation in particularly in remote areas such as Umbro and Tina in North Darfur, Golo and Reckoro in central Darfur, limited EORE projects were executed in those areas. This impacted the safety of population and limited their movement and access to livelihood intervention as a result their poverty and vulnerability increased and wellbeing and dignity were negatively affected.
