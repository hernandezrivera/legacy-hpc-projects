Project Summary

Over 2022, the intervention plan will focus on immediate emergency life-saving WASH interventions and support the resilience of existing WASH systems (water supply and sewer networks, wastewater treatment, solid waste management) and WASH in institutions, such as schools and healthcare facilities, ensuring coordinated and cohesive response and provide comprehensive WASH services for affected people in northwest Syria living in IDP sites and host communities.
The intervention will focus on providing emergency WASH services in the short term and provide a sustainable service in the long-term to the affected people in Aleppo and Idelb governorates. Shafak through this intervention will provide emergency WASH services for 620,000 individuals living in camps and communities. Shafak will provide full package WASH emergency services to provide rapid responses and strengthen the community resilience to adapt to living conditions in the affected communities
The intervention plan will ensure safe access, availability to basic WASH services, and quality assurance based on WASH cluster guidelines and COVID-19 mitigation measures for the planned activities. The intervention will comprise the following components:
a) Rehabilitation/upgrade of 20 water pumping stations including equipping the stations with electrical and mechanical equipment and installing renewable energy systems to reduce the running costs benefiting 100,000 Individuals. In addition, support Water Association Units (WAUs) through providing monthly running costs for the stations (fuel, chlorine, engine oil, and incentives for workers) and promote a cost recovery system. As well as train the local communities and apply water safety plans.
b) Provision of WASH emergency services for 400,000 individuals living in camps including water trucking and water quality monitoring, water containers, construction/ repair of emergency latrines, providing cleaning materials for latrines and latrine cleaning, desludging of septic tanks, and solid waste management.
c) Rehabilitation of sewage networks to connect the remote sites, camps, public facilities (schools and health centers) in addition to replacing the damaged sewer lines benefiting 75,000 Individuals.
d) Rehabilitation of water networks to connect the remote sites, camps, public facilities (schools and health centers) in addition to replacing the damaged water lines benefiting 30,000 Individuals.
e) Supporting the local operators and municipalities to continue the garbage collection and disposal of the waste from 20 communities (around 220,000 individuals), schools, health centers, and surrounding IDPs sites. This support will cover providing fuel for garbage vehicles, and maintaining/repair of the broken ones in addition to supporting workers’ incentives. Also, rehabilitation of 10 dumpsites.
f) Conducting hygiene promotion sessions about the improvement of hygiene practices and behaviors for 60,000 individuals.
g) 50,000 hygiene kits (approx. 300,000 individuals) will be distributed to the IDPs and host communities who don’t have the capacity to secure hygiene materials.
h) provision of WASH services to 5 education centers and 5 health facilities.
Shafak will ensure support the innovative solutions as possible in its proposed interventions for dealing with water drought issues through supporting solar-powered systems, wastewater treatment. In addition to applying environmental improvement projects through developing solid waste management treatment and elevating the bad impacts of neglecting SWM support.
As for 2023, according to Shafak's strategy and fund availability, it is expected to implement the same activities with the same size and budget.

4 - Needs

The situation in NWS remains grim, with 3.4 million persons in need of humanitarian aid. Out of the total 4.4 million persons residing in NWS, over 70% are considered people in acute need of life-saving assistance. Compared to 2020, the number of people in need has increased by almost 20%. Women and children are disproportionally affected and overrepresented in the statistics. Long-term damage to civilian infrastructure has led to a direct negative impact on communities’ access to services. With the economic crisis and devaluation of the SYP, over 90 percent of the Syrian population are living under the poverty line. Additionally, the water levels in the water sources remain critically low, limiting the access to water for people living in northern Aleppo and Idleb. Low rainfall and damages to water stations and networks have further aggravated the reliable access to water for households and communal use. Given these conditions, many households remain vulnerable to lack of access to safe water supplies. With approximately 50 percent of the population relying on water trucking to address their basic needs. Moreover, the sanitation facilities are still neglected and need comprehensive support to fit the population density in the communities. While the need for sanitation services needs supports.These factors pose significant public health risks that have harmful cascade effects on communities in terms of inequality to access to the WASH services.

5 - Implementation of activities

The WASH response will reach 620,000 people, 60% of them are IDPs. Shafak will provide comprehensive WASH services through implementing the activities directed by their teams in the field. ●Provision of WASH life-saving services in camps: Shafak will provide potable water through water trucking in alignment with the water quality monitoring process and install water storage. With regards to sanitation, Shafak will construct latrines and rehabilitate the damaged ones, desludging of septic tanks, support the solid waste management and install garbage bins. ●Rehabilitation of sewage/water networks, Shafak will connect the camps, remote neighborhoods with sewage and water networks in addition to replacin the damaged parts.●Support the water pumping stations by providing/repairing electrical and mechanical equipment, equipping the boreholes, upgrading the power sources in the station by installing solar-powered systems. ●Establishment of the cost recovery system, through gradually supporting for the water pumping station through secure fuel, oil, chlorine and cover the workers' incentives.●Solid waste management; Shafak will support 16 municipalities to maintain garbage collection and removal from host communities and surrounding camps, in addition, to rehabilitate landfills to ensure dispose of the garbage with no harm to the environment; ●Hygiene promotion; Shafak will distribute 60,000 HKs for the most vulnerable people and implement hygiene promotion awareness sessions.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The WASH program will ensure a “Do No Harm” principle, and has carried out a Protection Risk Analysis as part of its sector strategy development. Potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the WASH sector strategy and related activities, along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources required for monitoring those risks have been identified. The risks might include inequality in accessing services, work safety, gender-based violence (GBV) and exposure to explosive hazard . Regarding emergency services delivery, the sector will ensure that SPHERE/sector agreed standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context during each part of intervention implementation, especially during emergency sanitation infrastructure implementation phase when the risk of GBV is higher. WASH partners will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls,children, people with disabilities, female-headed households) to greater protection risks,and particularly to GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of each project, with a need to put the necessary mechanisms in place to facilitate feed back from users.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Gender remains on the top of Shafak priorities and is highlighted as one of the key objectives outlined in Shafak’s strategy. Shafak adheres to all other international minimum standards, including the SPHERE, IASC, Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Child Protection minimum standards as well as other technical guidelines of the protection cluster and its sub-clusters. Shafak employs a gender policy and procedures to operationalize the application of the Strategic objective for Gender. Key to this is Shafak’s Gender Minimum Standards, which include requirements on 1) Collection of sex and age disaggregated data 2) Inclusion of gender indicators in the monitoring and evaluation plan 3) Inclusion of gender indicators in the employment plan. Consequently, all implemented and planned activities include gender disaggregation of all information collected. This relates to the design of activities addressing women, girls, boys, men, elders, and people with disabilities as well as those activities that entail the participation of community members.
