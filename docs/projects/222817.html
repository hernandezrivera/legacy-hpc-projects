Project Summary

The International Organization for Migration (IOM) is committed to enhancing food security and disaster risk reduction for both Rohingya refugees and host communities in Bangladesh. In 2025, IOM will implement a series of activities aimed at improving livelihoods, fostering social cohesion, and strengthening disaster preparedness. These activities will be implemented in close collaboration with other UN and development agencies to avoid duplications and improve quality.
IOM will provide income generation support through crops and agriculture to 900 Rohingya individuals. This initiative aims to equip participants with the necessary skills and resources to cultivate crops, thereby enhancing their food security and creating sustainable income opportunities. Similarly, IOM will extend income generation support through crops and agriculture to 300 host community beneficiaries, with an added focus on market linkage. This approach ensures that the beneficiaries can effectively sell their produce, thereby improving their economic stability and contributing to the local economy.
In addition to agricultural support, IOM will provide dry fish production support to 350 host community individuals. This support will include training and in-kind resources, enabling participants to engage in sustainable dry fish production practices. By enhancing their skills and providing the necessary tools, IOM aims to improve the livelihoods of these individuals and promote food security within the community.
The selection criteria for all activities will be based on eligibility criteria such as female-headed households, income below the poverty line, and widows. IOM will also focus on disability (at least 3%) throughout the selection process to ensure inclusive support for livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities. Women empowerment, leadership, and decision-making will be promoted by incorporating income opportunities, entrepreneurship opportunities, business development training, and financial literacy for women in the host community. Additionally, self-reliance opportunities for women in the camps will be ensured to promote sustainable development and integration into society. At least 50% of women participation will be ensured, besides other gender groups which will also be prioritized based on availability.
To foster social cohesion, IOM will engage 750 (500 host and 250 Rohingya) beneficiaries from both the Rohingya and host communities in various activities. These activities will include discussions, cultural events, and market system integration. By bringing together individuals from different backgrounds, IOM aims to promote mutual understanding, reduce tensions, and build a sense of community.
To enhance the disaster resilience of host communities and support food security initiatives, IOM will conduct several disaster risk reduction and risk management activities in collaboration with other UN agency and stakeholders in Ukhiya and Teknaf. IOM will work to enhance the resilience of approximately 65,000 host community members through various initiatives. These initiatives will include the renovation of cyclone shelters, watershed management, community awareness programs, and the capacity building of disaster management committees. Construction interventions will be implemented through the cash-for-work (CfW) approach, providing the local communities with income opportunities. Additionally, IOM will strengthen the Cyclone Preparedness Program by enhancing the skills and knowledge of its members, ensuring that the community is better prepared to respond to natural disasters.
Through these comprehensive activities, IOM aims to improve food security, promote social cohesion, and enhance disaster preparedness for both the Rohingya and host communities. The project underscores IOM's commitment to inclusive development and the well-being of all affected populations.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The socioeconomic conditions in Bangladesh have worsened due to political shifts, inflation, and price hikes, affecting the livelihoods, food security, income, and employment opportunities of host communities. In July 2024, Bangladesh’s inflation hit a five-year high at 11.66%, significantly impacting both host communities and Rohingya refugees. Shifts in global funding priorities have also strained resources, impacting food security and cash-for-work programs for refugees. In March 2023, ration cuts in camps further reduced food security due to funding shortfalls. The ISNA 2024 report shows that 45% of Rohingya refugees rely solely on humanitarian assistance, while the remaining 55% supplement this aid with other income sources, underscoring the need for targeted livelihoods and skills development to address both host and refugee vulnerabilities. The report also highlights that 97% of Rohingya households experience moderate to high vulnerability, emphasizing the urgency for food security and disaster preparedness. Host community vulnerability is at its highest since 2017, with 69% of households struggling to meet essential needs. Homestead gardening and Fresh Food Corner programs have only reached 16% of households, with just 10% benefiting from them over the last three years. Additionally, 79.21% of persons with disabilities face barriers in accessing humanitarian assistance, emphasizing the need for inclusive support. The FAO Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis (RIMA) 2024 report underscores the extreme vulnerabilities within both communities, exacerbated by natural disasters, economic challenges, and funding constraints. In 2023, 86% of Rohingya and 57% of Bangladeshi households reported at least one significant shock. Key resilience factors include income, crop diversity, technology adoption, and capacity-building. The average monthly income for Rohingya households was BDT 3,288, markedly lower than the BDT 24,331 for Bangladeshi households. The 2024 Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA) Report reveals that 97% of Rohingya and 69% of Bangladeshi households faced moderate to high vulnerability in 2023. Economic vulnerability among Rohingya households increased by 3% compared to 2022, with 24% unable to afford the minimum food basket. Additionally, 83% of Rohingya households rely heavily on humanitarian aid to meet food needs, while 40% of Bangladeshi households fall below the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB). Frequent heavy rain, floods, cyclones, and landslides in this area threaten food security and underscore the critical need for Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) measures in Rohingya and host communities. The region faces persistent risks of floods and landslides, particularly during cyclone seasons, and environmental damage within refugee camps, including deforestation, waste mismanagement, and water contamination, exacerbates these issues. Significant DRR gaps persist in host communities, which lack functional evacuation shelters and disaster-resilient infrastructure. Rohingya camps and host communities also share canals, where waste accumulation has led to waterlogging and increased mosquito breeding, posing additional health and safety risks.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IOM will ensure the mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of activities. ISNA 2024 found 33% of households reported needing protection services or support. The guiding principle for these activities is ""Do no harm,"" especially for vulnerable people. The privacy of beneficiaries and the confidentiality of shared content will be maintained during the design and implementation of the project. Beneficiaries will also receive awareness-raising sessions to protect them. In case any incident giving rise to protection concerns occurs, IOM will follow referral pathways.IOM will deliver services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities, ensuring cultural appropriateness and avoiding any unintended negative consequences. Assistance and services will be provided based on needs, without discrimination based on age, sex, gender, nationality, race, or ethnic allegiance. Services and assistance will be offered on an adequate scale, within safe and easy reach of beneficiaries, at locations known to the beneficiaries , and accessible to all groups, including medical cases, Persons with Disability, and discriminated groups To ensure that activities are participatory, the project will conduct needs assessments. Surveys will be conducted within the refugee and host communities to identify their specific needs and preferences; the project will also involve the community members in the monitoring of project activities and results to foster transparency and accountability. Through these participatory methods, the project ensures that community voices are actively shaping FSS activities, making programs more relevant and impactful.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Age and disability are cross-cutting components mainstreamed throughout all of IOM’s work with its beneficiaries . As a technical partner in the Age and Disability Technical Working Group, IOM promotes age and disability inclusion. IOM's beneficiaries are primarily individuals aged 18 years and above, excluding child beneficiaries. The organization plans to include youth and adults as direct beneficiaries of self-reliance and livelihoods activities. However, these activities will indirectly benefit all age groups within the targeted households. Interventions will be designed to safeguard the children of female-headed households. Training centers will be equipped with breastfeeding corners and child-friendly spaces, allowing mothers to attend training courses while their children are safely accommodated.All planned activities for the year will consider individuals from various races, ethnicities, persons with special needs or disabilities, and the elderly population. The aim is to benefit all individuals in need. IOM will place special emphasis on minority groups within the community to help integrate them into society. Activities will be tailored to their specific needs and skills based on the outcomes of needs assessments. At least 1% of the beneficiary list will include persons with disabilities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability is a vital and integral component of this proposed project. During the needs assessment, affected populations including men, women, gender-diverse groups, and persons with disabilities in all targeted locations will be consulted for their needs and priorities. They will also have the opportunity to contribute to the design and planning of trade selection, opportunities, and market analysis, with support from IOM, and access to information sharing and data collection sessions. The affected people will lead in making decisions on their roles, local resource mobilization, and livelihoods/self-reliance skills, considering safety, security, and gender dynamics. Specific project activities will be planned based on their needs, priorities, and context. No one will be left out of skill development opportunities. IOM's Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is already established at the project sites to receive regular feedback from beneficiaries regarding the activities. The privacy of beneficiaries and the confidentiality of content will be ensured, and responses to their feedback will be delivered regularly with diligence.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM maintains a Zero Tolerance Policy toward sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), binding all staff, implementing partners, contractors, vendors, and service providers to high standards of behavior at all times. SEA against affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and can result in disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution.A dedicated PSEA Officer supports ongoing efforts to combat SEA, ensuring adherence to PSEA principles. IOM continues to roll out PSEA training and Training of Trainers sessions for PSEA Champions, aiming to equip all IOM staff, implementing partner staff, and volunteers with essential knowledge of PSEA guidelines and mandatory reporting systems.During the project’s initial implementation phase, SEA-related risks are screened, and mitigation measures are identified to enhance safe programming for affected populations. IEC materials are developed to raise awareness among communities about their rights to free assistance, reporting mechanisms, and available referral services. The PSEA Officer, in collaboration with the program team, conducts regular consultations and focus group discussions with beneficiaries to improve awareness and address concerns about SEA.IOM has an online reporting platform, WEAREALLIN.IOM.INT, for reporting SEA incidents. Recognizing potential access barriers, IOM has also established alternative complaint and feedback mechanisms to receive SEA-related concerns from beneficiaries and community members. IOM’s specialized investigation team ensures follow-up on SEA complaints, with access to protection and support services for survivors provided in coordination with its MPHSS-Protection team, the PSEA network, and existing GBV and Child Protection referral pathways.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Through awareness raising activities the host community will be advised to follow the climate smart agricultural practices such as raised bed farming, cultivation of salt tolerant varieties, shifting planting time in possible cases, mixed cropping, etc. to avoid the damage to crops from frequent floods and high tides resulting from the effects of climate change. In addition, IOM will encourage the community to construct water reservoirs/watershed for harvesting rainwater to facilitate agricultural activities in dry season, and to construct temporary small drainage in their agricultural fields to ensure effective drainage of water in the rainy season so that the damage of standing water to crops can be avoided/minimized. IOM will also support the community to implement small structural disaster mitigation works such as construction of drainage system/watershed management, plantation to support the natural environment, etc. so that agricultural fields and the community settlement areas can be better protected from the impacts of climate. IOM will renovate cyclone shelters and prepare the community and local government institutions to protect the lives and livelihood assets of vulnerable people from cyclones, floods and landslides, events which are increasing in intensity and frequency due to climate change.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The host community will be trained on protecting their livelihood/ agriculture assets from floods and cyclones. In addition, IOM will motivate the community to start family level savings, storing of fodders for poultry and firewood, and protection of shelters as preparation for disaster seasons. IOM maintains a Rohingya Response Business Continuity Plan (BCP), and contingency stocks to support overall EPR efforts for refugees and host community via relevant Sectors and ISCG.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Engaging with youth is a priority for IOM. The Organization is committed to developing activities targeting youth and young people on the move, addressing their needs and vulnerabilities and promoting meaningful youth engagement. IOM applies IASC Guidelines on working with and for Young People in Humanitarian and Protracted Crises. Under the project, IOM will engage beneficiaries over 18 years old who also represent the youth group. These beneficiaries will receive technical skills training with in-kind support to generate income that will enable them to contribute to their family's food security.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project heavily focuses on building the self-reliance and resilience of the Rohingya refugees. To achieve the objective, the beneficiaries will receive in-kind support. The activities will help them to meet their food needs and earn income from surplus food production.Moreover the project targets livelihood skills which the beneficiaires can use to build their livelihoods and generate income following their return to Myanmar.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The proposal aligns with the District Development and Growth Priorities (DDGP) by addressing key areas such as enhancing food security, promoting social cohesion, and strengthening disaster preparedness. IOM's project includes income generation support through agriculture and dry fish production for host communities, which directly contributes to the DDGP's goals of improving livelihoods and economic stability.Additionally, the project emphasizes inclusive support for vulnerable groups, including female-headed households and persons with disabilities, aligning with the DDGP's focus on social protection and gender equality. The disaster risk reduction activities, such as renovating cyclone shelters and watershed management, support the DDGP's priorities of enhancing disaster resilience and sustainable development. Overall, the project proposal complements the DDGP's objectives by fostering sustainable development and resilience in Cox's Bazar.
