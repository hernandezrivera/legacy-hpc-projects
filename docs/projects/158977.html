Project Summary

The current Great March of Return and the long-lasting blockade imposed on Palestinians
in the Gaza Strip are hitting the already bleeding wound of Gaza. This horrible situation
adversely impacted all aspects of life including education. This proposed project aims to
reduce vulnerability among Gaza’s Students by improvement of primary education
through strengthening access to education services and to upgrade the capacity of the
target area school counsellors in the field of PSS. The project will intervene at the most
vulnerable, underprivileged, borderline and poor areas (northern area of North
governorate Beit Lahia BL and eastern area of Gaza governorate EG). The benefited children will be the most vulnerable, primary school children. Moreover, the project will help students’ families by providing school uniform, school bags and stationaries. Providing PSS sessions for the children and refer them to specialized program. On the other hand, the project will upgrade the school counsellors in the field of psychosocial support. The project will target 600 children in 4 schools in BL and EG out of 2948 students (age from 6-12 years old males and females) and 24 counsellors (age from 25 to 45 years males and females). The children are selected according to the data from the principals of the selected schools regarding their economic and academic conditions will receive school kits, psychosocial sessions and remedial classes in Arabic, English and Math. Needs assessment will be implemented during the project as focus groups and interviews with the direct and indirect beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

The Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) declares that “all girls and boys in the world are entitled to the right to survival; the right to development (including the right to be educated); the right to protection from all forms of abuse, neglect, and exploitation; and the right to participation in matters that affect their lives and that prepare children to take on increasing roles of responsibility as they mature” (Johnstone, 2011). Inclusive Education and Child-Friendly Schools. Miske Witt & Associates Inc.In the Gaza Strip, the deteriorating humanitarian, political and economic situation has affected 480,013 basic, secondary and kindergarten (KG) students and teachers. Deteriorating living conditions, unpaid teacher salaries and lack of school budgets call into question the ability of the education system to deliver adequate education to half a million students. Some 70% of UNRWA schools and 63% of those run by the Ministry of Education (MoE) already operate on a double-shift system (some UNRWA schools even on a triple-shift system), reducing instructional hours on core subjects and foundation learning. Poverty is deepening where as much as 80% of the population depends on some form of humanitarian assistance while unemployment exceeded 50% (71% for women), including over 400,000 children living in poverty. Families often cannot afford basic education supplies for their children, risking drop out, to which girls are more vulnerable. Continuing shortages in electricity also restrict students’ study time and ability to concentrate and learn and negatively impacts their right and ability to pursue education at school and at home. Children who drop out are at risk of child labour or of participating in life-threatening activities. The deterioration of the education services has had negative repercussions on the psychosocial wellbeing of children and teachers. Consequently, learning outcomes sank and children experience difficulties in reading and writing. Effectively, some 50% of students (aged 5-17 years) do not achieve their full educational potential. Moreover, according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019 there is an increasing trend of psychosocial distress across Gaza population, with estimated 52,098 – including 26,049 children – with mental health and psychosocial problems linked with the Great March of Return(GMR)demonstrations,in addition to 210,000 adults and over 295,000 children already acutely vulnerable by end of 2017.The intervention in the aforementioned areas will be according to the Education Objective 1-Increase equitable safe access to inclusive,quality educational services for vulnerable children; Relates to Strategic Objective2regarding the activity:Provision of and train teacher in utilising emergency,inclusive education supplies (including stationary, student kits, teaching supplies, uniforms, solar systems or fuel)for vulnerable and unserved students and teachers.Due to lack of access to updated and recent information on state of education in Gaza,YEC has currently conducted primary needs assessment.The method of need assessment includes key informants and schools’ directors.The interviewed decision makers were Deputy Minister of Ministry of Education(MoE)(Dr. Ziad Thabet),Director of studies and information at MoE(Ashraf Saleh),and Head of Documentation Department(Amal Hamada).The assessment was revealed that:The number of primary schools at Eastern Gaza is 14 include 9340 students; while the number of primary schools at Beit Lahia in North governorate is 5include 3370students.The number of school counsellors at Eastern Gaza is 27(10males and 17 females); while the number of school counsellors in Beit Lahia is 2 (9male and 12female)(MoE, 2019)Studies and Information Department.Schools counsellors have received training in:body and mind, and school mental health.However, they need training in:counselling by play, family counselling,dealing with educational problems related to psychological

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

As the project is targeting children from 6 to 12 years old (Females and Males) and counsellors (Males and Females age from 25-45) in ARAs and rest of Gaza; it will ensure that girls and teachers are not put at risk of increased harm in the implementation of all activities such as distributing the kits. In each activity; the beneficiaries will be explained the need of the activity, how it will affect the project and acknowledge them of the process of implementation. Consultation will be made with the schools' principles before and during the implementation. During the implementation of the activities in the schools; the educators will emphasis on the topic of the children's right of education and how they can obtain it. Monitoring during the all stages of project will be practiced.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The selected schools are in the most of the marginalized areas and ARAs in the Gaza Strip; as the target beneficiaries are considered vulnerable. The project's criteria is to select the most vulnerable children (600 out of 2948 children) age from 6-12 years old without discrimination according to data from the principles of schools. The children who have learning difficulties according to psychosocial problems will be referred to specialized program to have more assistance.
