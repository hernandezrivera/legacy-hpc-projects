Project Summary

The proposed project aims to strengthen the ability of vulnerable Palestinian school communities (students, parents, and staff) to cope with protection threats and shocks. The main drivers of education-related vulnerabilities include attacks on schools, movement restrictions, poor and/or inadequate classrooms and WASH facilities, and poverty. These factors undermine the quality of education, generate protection concerns, negatively affect the mental wellbeing of school communities, and contribute to early school dropout.
The proposed intervention aligns with HRP 2023 and directly responds to the Education Cluster’s Strategic Objectives (1,2, and 3). It also addresses the education-related protection and wellbeing needs of students, teachers, and staff of vulnerable schools in the West Bank. MA’AN Development Center is planning to target 20 schools in vulnerable communities located mainly in Areas C (87%), H2 (8%), and A&B (5%) in the governorates of Hebron (Khirbet al Majaz, Masafer Yatta, Um al Kher, Jinba, Khallet Athaba, Susiya, Umm Qussa, Ad Deirat, Hebron old city), Bethlehem (Al Khadr, Jubbet adh Dhib, Khirbet ad Deir), and Jerusalem (Khan an Ahmar – Abu al Helw, Jaba’, Anata). Thereby, directly benefiting 3341 individuals: 2198 boys, 853 girls, 140 men, 136 women – including 61 PWDs.
The project will contribute to ensuring that the staff & students (in the 20 targeted schools) affected by the education-related violations & the pandemic benefit from protective environment [O1] through:
-Provision of school-based psychosocial support & child resiliency sessions to school staff and students: sessions will include specialized trainings on child’s growth and child’s rights (for both students & teachers), child resiliency (with teachers) facilitating psychosocial support program for children, & specialized professional follow up. The project will also facilitate a referral system.
- Strengthen advocacy on education-related violations in the WB and ensure that schools staff & students benefit from protective environment – through implementing fun activities for children that include rights topics, then school staff will assist children in implementing advocacy initiatives
The project will contribute to Increasing equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children [O2] through:
-Provision of essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to target schools to ensure that their environments are inclusive and safe for students and teachers.
-Support school-aged CWDs to access education services – through adaptations to school infrastructure, building capacity of school staff to accommodate for CWDs needs, detect student learning difficulties and provide appropriate referrals to specialists
The project will also contribute to supporting children & school staff in vulnerable schools to prepare & respond to emergencies [O3] through:
-providing structured preparedness packages and post-emergency responses: project will facilitate first aid trainings, self-aid trainings, stress management and DRR sessions for target students &staff
The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as MA’AN adopts implementation approaches & risk mitigation measures that are guided by protection principles. MA’AN considers safety, dignity, privacy, meaningful access, participation &accountability in all stages of the project. The proposed intervention will ensure that the needs of children and adolescent students, teachers, and school support staff (including PWDs) are accounted for. The proposed intervention aims to complement responses proposed by other aid actors amidst increased education-related violations and protection threats of students & staff in Area C and H2. The intervention will be further coordinated with all relevant stakeholders to ensure an effective approach including, MoE, Legal and protection aid actors, and community members.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2023 estimates 608,556 Palestinians (49% females) across the oPt, the vast majority (96%) of whom are school-age children, including around 25,385 Children with Disabilities (CWDs), will need humanitarian assistance to access education in 2023. Over 35% of them live in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Children in Area C, Hebron H2 and East Jerusalem are the most vulnerable. They have been facing challenges and barriers to education: There is a general lack of school-based psychosocial support as they have been facing regular attacks and harassment to, from and during school and other protection threats. This is causing them to develop psychosocial distress. However, psychosocial support is not vastly available at the school for children if needed (as reported by around 84,000 HHs with school-aged children in Gaza, Area C, Hebron H2 and EJ). Another challenge would be the coercive environment created by Israel when enforcing community members to displacement from their homes. Also, a coercive environment is created when Israeli forces attack schools (including students and staff) and destruct education infrastructure. Also, Protection risks on the way to school is a major reason for children dropping out of schools. According to MSNA, 19.8% of boys and 13.6% of girls have dropped out from schools in the last 12 months. 55% of the boys and 36% of the girls in WB who dropped out in the last 12 months was due to conflict related protection risks on the way to and from schools. Schools in Area C and EJ are also at continuing risk of stop work or demolition orders, due to lack of building permits which are near impossible to obtain. In total, 56 schools serving around 6,500 students in the most vulnerable areas of the West Bank are under threat of full or partial demolition in East Jerusalem and Area C, due to Israeli-issued demolition orders. In addition, these orders prevent rehabilitation and expansion of schools. Furthermore, most schools in Area C and H2 area of Hebron don’t meet the minimum standards for safe learning environments. Schools are either in rented residential buildings which lacks facilities, safe play areas and WASH facilities. Other schools in area C are built from caravans or tire structures and don’t meet the safety of the Ministry of Education and the INEE MS. Also, Children with disabilities are facing numerous challenges to access quality education. MSNA states that 13.6% of school-aged children with disabilities dropped out of school in the previous school year, and the major challenges CWD face in education are bullying (42.4% of the HHs with children with disabilities), adaptability of infrastructure (29% of the HHs with children with disabilities).During September - October 2022, MA’AN conducted a rapid assessment to further inform the HNO and MSNA and in order to plan an efficient, impactful humanitarian response. The assessment included desk research, consultations with MoE directorates, local councils and various field visits to vulnerable communities in the West Bank. MoE also provided MA'AN with a list of needs for vulnerable schools in the WB (total: 227). Qualitative findings supported the MSNA as most communities visited have schools that are lacking adequate, safe, and accessible infrastructure where some of them have shortage of classrooms and lack libraries, play areas, and labs. Also, most communities visited where constantly facing attacks by Israeli forces and settlers in the communities in general, while some have had these incidents happen in the schools – thereby directly affecting students and teachers. These schools do not provide psychosocial support for students and have no DRR plan in place for such emergencies. MA’AN therefore identified a need for a comprehensive approach that targets both students and staff to improve their wellbeing and enhance their coping mechanisms and emergency preparedness while enhancing their access to adequate, safe, and accessible physical learning environment (infrastructure). MA’AN identified 20 vulnerable schools (out of the 227 provided by MoE) based on cluster priorities and based on MA’AN’s field assessment. These schools were selected to be targeted for infrastructure rehabilitation and inputs provision, while their students & staff will be engaged in various psychosocial resiliency and DRR activities. Schools identified are in the governorates of Hebron (Khirbet al Majaz (1), Masafer Yatta (2), Um al Kher (1), Khallet Athaba (1), Susiya (1), Umm Qussa (1), Ad Deirat (1), Hebron old city (3)), Bethlehem (Al Khadr (2), Jubbet adh Dhib (1), Khirbet ad Deir (1)), and Jerusalem (Khan an Ahmar – Abu al Helw (1), Jaba’ (1), Anata (2)). Targeted schools in Hebron Old City are in Area H2: students and staff are subjected to checkpoints daily. They have also been exposed to frequent raids from Israeli forces (including random searches, firing tear gas canisters, and detaining students and/or staff). The two targeted schools in Al Khadr are in Areas A&B: these schools have been selected since they are close to a settlement and “bypass road 60” which is dedicated for Israeli use only. Their location has exposed them to frequent settler attacks interrupting the educational process. The rest, and majority, of the targeted schools are in Area C and these have been facing similar conditions: being close to settlements, military points and the wall, exposed to settler attacks and Israeli raids, and have unsafe infrastructure (& no adaptation to PWDs needs). Also 8 of these schools are under the threat of demolition according to MoE. Also, MA’AN identified that out of the 20 identified schools, 10 of them have counselors while the rest don’t have any. All these issues reflect an urgent need for interventions in these schools that would alleviate the stress that both students and school staff face, improve their coping mechanism, and improve their readiness for dealing with such threats and emergencies.

2 - Activities

MA’AN will continue to coordinate with education relevant stakeholders at all phases such as MoE, directorates, & education cluster to map out implementation plan & explore special regulatory considerations for implementation. Community Based Project Support Committees (CBPSCs) will be formed in each location, a known MA’AN approach when intervening in communities for increased community ownership, protection mainstreaming & accountability. CBPSCs help inform the needs of their communities & the different marginalized groups. They will serve an integral role in increasing accountability & reporting protection concerns throughout the response & will be comprised of representatives from (local council, school staff, counselors, parents, & other community members including women & PWDs). The project capitalizes on MA’AN’s extensive experience in providing psychosocial and implementing child resilience methodology guided by IASC guidelines on MHPSS. If needed, MA’AN will seek coordinated support from partner organizations for legal follow-up & specific protection concerns, taking into account protection vulnerabilities through responsive, remedial & preventive support. Any arising protection cases (ie GBV, child protection concerns) whether through the Complaints Response Mechanism or through field monitoring will be safely referred to relevant protection actors through the Protection cluster & existing referral pathways. Risk assessments will be conducted with MoE, school management, staff, and community members in order to plan rehabilitation works & mitigate any risks associated in Areas C & H2. MHPSS assessment will also be conducted to further customize the response. The proposed response includes the following activities:A0.1Needs assessments: at the beginning of the intervention, together with stakeholders & local Authorities, will be carried out to verify any change of needs and/or priorities, & assess the security situation. 0.2 Orientation meetings: explaining the project to caregivers will facilitate young girls’ participation in some areas 0.3 Formation & training of Community-Based Project Support Committees (CBPSCs): after the orientation & selection meetings, MA’AN will conduct several trainings for the CBPSCs and school counselors, if available, in order for them to lead as a protection task force in localities. Training will focus on safeguarding, Gender, Protection; training will include topics related to PSEA & child protection. They will be informed on the CRM system, how to access it, & how to file complaints &/or send in feedback.A.1 conduct specialized training on child’s growth & rights for teachers: this activity aims to develop teachers & counselors’ knowledge about children’s needs & to understand the different stages of growth to facilitate the provision of services to them according to their needs & age. The training also analyzes the challenges faced by children within their community, enables teachers & support staff to understand causes of distress & well-being, analyses the signs & symptoms of distress in children, & discusses the appropriate needed support accordingly. A2 conduct specialized training on child’s growth & rights for children & adolescent students: to enhance child’s knowledge, strengthens self-confidence & self-awareness. A3 conduct first aid training for teachers & counselors. A4 conduct self-aid training sessions for both school staff & students & DRR sessions: to enhance capacity, preparedness &awareness about self-protection & protection of peers (related to physical & psychological problems/abuses that children encounter). A5 Conduct stress management workshops for teachers & counselors: providing them with mechanisms to manage their own stressors by providing self-care tips & skills to deal with daily challenges, identifying self-support & external sources of support, in addition, to managing the tension & job burnout that teachers may reach. These workshops contribute to alleviating the different stressors of male & female teachers & support staff, as the impact of stressors can be different depending on gender & coping mechanisms. A6 conduct stress management workshops for students: similar to the one conducted with teachers but more tailored towards children’s needs, workshops also focus on enhancing children’s ability to express stressors by offering psycho-emotional release. A7 Conduct training on child resiliency for teachers & counselors to facilitate psychosocial support programs: this activity targets the teachers & children, with teachers being trained in facilitation skills that enable them to provide methodologies of psychological support to children. Engaging teachers & counselors in this training encourages them to contribute to a safer environment for their schools. A8 Supervision & specialized professional follow up on the psychosocial support provided by trained counselors &teachers, equipping them with further tools & skills. A9 Referral System: helps to meet the needs of children most affected by crisis through referring them to specialized counseling & connecting them to support networks. A10 Conduct workshops on Detection of learning difficulties: enabling teachers & counselors to deal with differences in children’s learning patterns, detect learning difficulties & disabilities & identify different approaches (including referral mechanisms if needed). A11 implement fun interactive Children activities providing them with knowledge about their rights, protection mechanisms, how to access resources, & guide them to prepare for initiatives. A12 implementation of children-led initiatives/ advocacy campaigns aiming to raise awareness, promote, & advocate for their right to a safe education environment A13. infrastructure rehabilitation: based on MoE’s list of needs, MA’AN will invest in the rehabilitation of 20 schools’ infrastructure within INEE standards -Includes adaption for PWDs when needed.

Monitoring will be done in accordance with MA’AN MEAL system & by all those partaking in the project including beneficiaries. Field staff will cooperate with the MEAL Officer in ensuring that the indicators are being met & that the best strategies are being used. MA’AN Project Manager will be responsible for overall monitoring, evaluation & reporting. The indicators & sources of verification will be the base for collecting data & observation on the ground by the project coordinator. Monitoring will be shared with relevant stakeholders including project committee to include MoE, & Education Cluster. All information will be shared with donor, especially any lessons learned. The tools that will be used are baseline survey (at the start) end line survey (at project’s close) as well as interviews, field visits, general observation & reports. Special attention will be given for conducting interviews & data collection with young girls, female teachers, counselors, & school staff ensuring their comfort to speak freely. MA’AN will ensure presence of female staff during these meetings to ensure active participation of girls & women for transparent communication of gender & age-based needs. MA’AN also has local coordinators in the areas of intervention who are familiar with the communities having access to information & relations that would ensure smooth implementation. The intervention will be supported by a CRM accessible to beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries through several channels. The CRM will ensure that all complaints are recorded, responded to & handled according to their sensitivity. Beneficiaries will be sensitized on its function, process & usage. CRMs are customized under each project for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a confidential and secure way duly reported to MAAN’s management. All feedback received will feed to inform the design of project activities & relevance to vulnerable children’s needs. The number of targeted boys is higher because some of the most vulnerable schools targeted are boys only schools.

Any feedback, complaints, recommendations received through the CRM or any other channels from target localities will inform the corrective measures and redesign interventions.Beneficiaries will be regularly informed, at the start of the project, of what they can expect from the project and staff, as well as being provided with clearinformation on details about how they can give feedback and file complaints if they experience or witness misconduct. Satisfaction assessments will be implemented (including child-friendly ones) using developed surveys andthrough quarterly focus group discussions and interviews with women, men, girls, and boys. Any GBV or child protection cases reported to MA’AN will be dealt with in utmost sensitivity. MA’AN will consult the Protection Consortium & depending on the case, might refer the victim to a specialized organization.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

MA’AN’s Complaints Response Mechanism is easily accessible, takes into account the specificities of the local context & culture, as well as project resources & beneficiaries’ vulnerabilities. At the onset of the project, MA’AN staff will coordinate with all relevant stakeholders in the community in order to call for an orientation meeting and ensure that representatives from schools, caregivers/parents, local councils, and other community members are present. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as MA’AN is guided by protection principles, considering safety, dignity, privacy, meaningful access, participation &accountability in all stages of the project. Addressing the needs of vulnerable school students and staff while involving community members & stakeholders as leaders throughout the project’s implementation, the proposed intervention seeks to minimize implementation risk & enforce protection mainstreaming and the “do no harm” policy. MA’AN will ensure that the proposed intervention does not in any way further harm or negatively affect the children (girls and boys students) and women and men (teachers, counselors, & local community members). CBPSCs will be involved throughout every phase of project implementation. MA’AN has previous experience in working in firing zones, the security situation is monitored closely on an ongoing basis by MA’AN to ensure timely advice & relevant security precautions can be implemented. Furthermore, MA’ANs local coordinators & continuous presence in the communities along with the CBPSC members will report regularly on any risks to safety & security during implementation to mitigate time & effectiveness impact. When needed, MA’AN has depended on security & safety approaches to infrastructure work in Area C. To ensure safety & security beneficiaries during implementation & in line with the “Do No Harm” principles, MA’AN ensures that infrastructure work in all its forms in Area C do not affect the arial view of shelter dimensions in the community to reduce chances of future demolition orders. When needed, provision & rehabilitation work is done during night hours as a response to security & financial threats in the target Areas. The communities targeted by the project are very risky locations (surrounded by settlements or under ICA threat of demolition), therefore MA’AN will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during weekends) in coordinated times with MoE and the CBPSCs to assess the security situation of the community. MA’AN mobilizes/employs local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur. When possible, in alignment with its procurement policy & guidelines, MA’AN procures from local sources. MA’AN has been working for many years in Area C & H2, & has a good relationship with the communities, so it will be made aware of any problem with ICA or IDF in the communities in real time. MA’AN will also remain in contact with various partners working on protection & legal cases & reporting incidents. For each phase of the proposed intervention, MA’AN will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks including PSEA associated with the action. The risks identification will be done through assessment and community consultations. Furthermore, MA’AN employs female MEAL staff as focal points for the CFRM which also handles PSEA. CFRM info along with PSEA networks will be present on all project outreach materials. The program activities include gender and safeguarding including PSEA trainings for community members for clear PSEA messaging and awareness. On institutional level MA’AN staff are required to be trained on PSEA policy, Staff code of Conduct and Child protection policy. Furthermore, MA’AN staff follow strict health, hygiene, & safety measures (that were adopted during the spread of COVID-19) in order to mitigate the risk of harm being done to targeted beneficiaries and community members.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposed intervention targets the most education-vulnerable children and adolescents in 20 schools in Area C, H2, and Jerusalem. Beneficiaries will be identified using the following selection criteria: Students (boys and girls) in schools in the most vulnerable communities will be targeted by the project. The selection criteria for the schools include: schools with poor performance based on the MoE data, schools that are more prone to attacks by Israeli forces and harassment by settlers, schools next to check points, schools where children are hard to reach by teachers, schools located in the most vulnerable poor communities, need urgent rehabilitation of facilities, and adaptations for PWDs.Both school staff and children from these schools will benefit from the comprehensive interventions focused on wellbeing, child resiliency, and emergency preparedness.Based on its previous experience, MA’AN recognizes that participation of young girls in some communities is more sensitive due to social-cultural concerns, hence, MA’AN will adapt interventions to ensure access and acceptance by girls and their parents/ caregivers. The methodologies used by MA’AN will ensure suitability for both males and females. When necessary, some methods will be readjusted to respond better to the different needs of male and female students, teachers, and appropriate to the local and cultural context.Moreover, the CBPC will ensure representation of these marginalized groups and relevant stakeholders and that their needs will be accounted for in.It is important to note that MA’AN adheres to the codes of conduct and standards that have been developed by the international humanitarian community (including IASC). MA’AN has set standards of behavior in humanitarian settings seeking to maintain the high standards of independence, effectiveness, and impact. MA’AN recognizes its obligation to provide humanitarian assistance wherever it is needed. We strive to alleviate human suffering amongst those least able to withstand the stress caused by disaster. We base the provision of relief aid upon a thorough assessment of the needs of the disaster victims and the local capacities already in place to meet those needs. We endeavor to respect the culture, structures and customs of the communities we are working in. We do not tie the promise, delivery or distribution of assistance to the embracing or acceptance of a particular political or religious creed. Additionally, in order to protect our independence, we seek to avoid dependence upon a single funding source. We place a high priority on the proper co-ordination of our emergency responses, and we make sure to engage and build local capacities while doing so. Intended beneficiaries are always involved in the design, management and implementation of the assistance we provide. We also endeavor to minimize the negative impact of humanitarian assistance, seeking to avoid long-term beneficiary dependence upon external aid. Most importantly, we hold ourselves accountable to both those we seek to assist and those from whom we accept resources.
