Project Summary

Lebanon is facing multiple, unprecedented crises. The COVID-19 global pandemic, in addition to a crippling year-long economic downturn and the acute impacts of the August 2020 explosions in Beirut, has compounded challenges in an already weak public health sector. The cumulative impact has meant that the most vulnerable populations – women and children face even greater hardships and UNICEF’s effort to increase access to quality health services is more critical than ever. Through this project, UNICEF will seek to minimize the risk of further spread of COVID-19 and communicable diseases among vulnerable Lebanese and migrant populations through strengthening the COVID and communicable diseases intervention at the 5 Border Center Points and increasing the capacities and knowledge of staff working at the all Point of Entry. This will also enable UNICEF to reach mothers and children from the most vulnerable communities among the Lebanese and migrant workers and aim to enhance their health seeking behaviors and knowledge on MNCAH including immunization.
Thereby, the project will target the 3 layers of enabling environment, demand and supply to support and enhance maternal and child access to qualitative services through:
- Capacity building of health care providers including community midwives on updated ANC/PNC guidelines
- Enhancing community health behaviors and preventive practices among vulnerable Lebanese and migrants through capacity building and dispatch of 100 frontline workers who will be providing integrated maternal and child health messaging.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

UNICEF aims to provide needed support to the public health system through enhancing MNCAH health seeking behaviours, knowledge and referrals with focus on the following key interventions:• Train 350 HCWs to minimize the risk of maternal/ neonatal mortality in coordination with UNFPA and 300 on GBV• 250 community health workers will be recruited to conduct community outreach and mobilization activities to strengthen community trust in the PHCs and to better represent the variety of services offered. • 80 community-based midwives will be recruited in collaboration with the Lebanese order of Midwives, UNFPA and MOPH to provide support to the available workforce in health facilities • 100,000 vulnerable Lebanese and migrants will be reached with integrated MNCAH package.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

UNICEF ensures through its implementation the highest standards to mitigate protection risks towards the community, direct right holders and active stakeholder and includes the below guiding clauses in all partnership agreements.• PSEA: Implementing partners are required to ensure that staff involved are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for PSEA, and the internal reporting mechanisms for SEA cases, through mandatory trainings and signature of the code of conduct and SG bulletin.• Gender, equity and social inclusion: UNICEF acknowledges that its program will not disrupt structural inequalities and cultural norms that lead to the marginalization of women and girls as well as people with disability and migrant workers in Lebanon. The proposed initiatives will promote equitable access to health services and will specifically address gender-specific, unaddressed needs in maternal and child health and inclusion for people with disability.HCW PSS will be noted.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

UNICEF has made Accountability to Affected People (AAP) a priority in its Strategic Plan, UNICEF is adopting an institutional strategy and action plan for scaling up AAP. UNICEF’s responsibility to ensure programmes generate meaningful and relevant results and outcomes for girls, boys, women and men, in accordance with their specific needs, priorities and preferences through actively involving them in the program planning, implementation and evaluation. Moreover, UNICEF has existing processes to ensure that targeted beneficiaries are informed about their rights, expected standards of conduct by implementing partners, available services, and how to access them. Beneficiaries will have access to project specific information through sessions held by implementing partners and monitored through UNICEF field offices. In addition, both implementing partners and UNICEF are required to consult with the community and municipalities and ensure their active participation in the project.
