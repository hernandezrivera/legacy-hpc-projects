Project Summary

The improved and sustained food security project will be implemented in Gedo and Galmudug regions and will target and benefit 14400 individuals in need (These include 10,080 displaced persons and 2010 persons with disability). The project will facilitate 950 households (5700 people) with conditional and unconditional cash transfers to facilitate immediate access to food for vulnerable communities living in areas with severe acute food insecurity. These beneficiaries include 3990 Displaced persons, 1800 persons belonging to the host communities and 855 persons living with a disability. This figure includes 3192 children. 350 households (2100 people) with pregnant and lactating women, PWD, the ill and the elderly will receive cash vouchers while 300 households including IDP and pastoralist households who are food insecure, will be targeted with unconditional cash transfers. Priority will be given to women or households with PWD and the elderly. 300 households (1800 persons) will be targeted during Cash for Work Interventions. Priority will be given to women who are physically capable of participating in agricultural and livestock assets rehabilitation. All cash rates will be based on the rates as set by the Cash working group of the targeted districts. Conditional and unconditional cash transfers will increase the purchasing power for targeted households to access food commodities available in the local markets for at least 3 months as they recover their assets and livelihood. The cash is a dignified approach to supporting these households to make choices of types and quantities of food to purchase. Post Distribution Surveys will be conducted to monitor the use of transferred cash and redeemed vouchers and the benefit this support has brought the families.
The project will also support livelihoods of 1450 households (8700 people) from Dollow, Abudwak and Dhusamareb as well as displaced persons located to sustain enhance their household access to food through the protection of their livelihoods. This will include training and provision of seasonally appropriate agricultural inputs to 400 farmers. Short term training on pest control and use of crop cover to reduce flooding and loss of crop swill ensure farmers reduce the loss of yield through pest and plods. The project also targets 100 IDP women and youth with income-generating activities to improve household food security 200 IDP women will be supported to take up backyard poultry rearing for household consumption and commercial benefits to the households. 200 IDP women will be facilitated with knowledge and skills to establish kitchen gardens which will improve the household dietary intake.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The UCT modality will provide the beneficiaries with the power of choice on the preferred foods to purchase and at what quantities at a time. UCT is also safe and does not present any risks to the beneficiaries, unlike in-kind support. With UCT it is easy to consider more vulnerable persons such as women, PLWD, elderly thus this is chosen to ensure more inclusivity in the targeting. UCT will also help in strengthening local markets as the beneficiaries will purchase the preferred foods from local suppliers. This will not only avoid disruption of the food markets that may be occasioned by in-kind food supplies from outside but will also inject cash in the local markets.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

NAPAD currently have staff working and residing in the targeted project districts who continuously provide information on the local markets during the design of the project. The staff monitored the trends in the availability of different essential foods in the local markets and the prices. Additionally, they gathered information on the quality of different foodstuffs in the market. Observations in the target district markets are that the markets have experienced minimal fluctuations despite the movement restrictions brought by COVID 19 outbreak. Additionally, NAPAD continuously read the WFP Joint Market and Supply Chain Update. The generally stable markets informed the choice of UCT in the project.

Mitigation Measures

To mitigate against risks highlighted above, NAPAD will ensure that all conditional and unconditional cash transfers which include (CFW) will be conducted to beneficiaries electronically through local mobile service providers. This is after information verification during and after biometric registration of beneficiaries. These measures will ensure the safety of beneficiaries. All beneficiary data will be safely stored by NAPAD for accountability. Distributions will be conducted from targeted villages to ensure that beneficiaries do not have to travel to other location to get their vouchers. All vouchers are redeemable at local stores ensuring that beneficiaries do not have to travel far and in insecure areas to access required commodities. All cash rates will be based on the rates as set by the Cash working group of Somalia. NAPAD will be keen to issue food vouchers to the most vulnerable and in particular female-headed households, child-headed households, pregnant and lactating women with under 5-year-olds and older persons and unaccompanied children in their name. Both IDP and host community members will be targeted to receive the vouchers and CFW to avoid conflicts, tension or harassment over the food and resources. It has been a practice of NAPAD to consult the community, clan and local authority leaders in the identification of beneficiaries of food vouchers and the individuals involved in Cash for Work. This has ensured that there is a balanced division of resources, community ownership for better management of assets and provision of security for beneficiaries as well as assets. Distributions will be conducted regularly to ensure that beneficiaries will not run out of food commodities until the next distribution. NAPAD has ensured that its food security field officers are gender-balanced and include different local clans including the Bantus. This was effected to ensure that women and girls, minority tribes like the Bantus and people with disability issues are attended to adequately especially during beneficiary registration and distributions. NAPAD’s balanced staffing profile has and will continue to enhance NAPAD’s reporting and feedback mechanism. Communities have access to NAPAD staff numbers and NAPADs email address for any information sharing. NAPAD will also conduct pre and post-distribution surveys to capture any complaints and recommendations from the community. NAPAD staff and contractors have signed a child protection policy and will also be expected to be formally committed to adhering to humanitarian code of conduct during all interactions with vulnerable populations. NAPAD will continue to conduct refresher child protection and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training to its staff and affiliates so they continue to have capacities to identify and react effectively to protection issues. Continued engagement of school-based clubs in child advocacy will ensure that children and especially girls will have skills and knowledge on their safety during voucher distributions. Additionally, project inception community mobilization meetings will be key in sharing protection information to leaders and beneficiaries on modalities of maintaining individual security during distributions activities. This will also be an opportunity to share with especially women and girl that do not have to pay or provide services/favors to anyone in exchange for receiving food assistance and that they should ensure their safety by walking in groups to their local distribution points.

Please provide further explanation

NAPAD is registered to operate in Galmudug, Banadir and Jubaland. The Local administration, relevant District Ministries of Agriculture, Livestock and Ministry of Women and Human Rights as well as the Ministry of Youth, community leadership and local IDP and Host community are key in identification of project beneficiaries, sites and resources. This will enhance legality of the interventions, enhance ownership as well as mitigate against potential conflicts among the clans, IDPs, and host communities. On one end, an engaged local leadership will offer sufficient protective environment to beneficiaries and the investments of the project. On the other hand, the local leadership will ensure the sustainability of the project upon handing over of the project. After each project is commissioned, an MOU stipulating ownership, management and maintenance roles of each project will be signed between all local elders, local administration and community groups. The proposed project is linked to the food security and livelihoods objectives of Somali National Development Plan and Food security cluster targets with an emphasis on implementing the objectives of Food security cluster response and developing the resilience of the food security and livelihoods sector to benefit more displaced and non-displaced households.
