Project Summary

This project aims to urgently respond to the current emerging essential needs among severely impacted households (HHs) to enhance their access to food and other basic needs and restore their livelihood capacities. This project will build upon Mercy Corps’ current Cash Programme, which responds to continually increasing needs amongst targeted vulnerable groups, including those whose situation has been severely exacerbated by COVID-19.

Through unconditional cash assistance, this project will identify and select the most vulnerable HHs in three groups of acutely vulnerable HHs from four governorates: 1) vulnerable farmers and breeders from Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah, including Access Restricted Areas (ARAs); 2) vulnerable fishers from Gaza, and Rafah; and 3) vulnerable HHs from the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) unserved waiting lists of Social Safety Net (SSN) with focus on Female-Headed Households (FHHs), and daily-wage laborers who lost their source of income and livelihood from Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah.
Through this project, Mercy Corps and its local NGO partners – Gaza Culture and Development Group and Al Foukhary Association for Country Development – will target 720 vulnerable HHs (3,960 individuals), broken down as follows:
1) 228 eligible vulnerable Farmer and Breeder HHs (from Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah) identified through combined HH vulnerability assessment and livelihood technical assessment. Each selected Farmer and Breeder HH will receive an average amount of $1,200 in three installments based on their priority needs;
2) 120 eligible vulnerable Fisher HHs (from Gaza) identified through combined HH vulnerability assessment and livelihood technical assessment. Each selected Fisher HH will receive an average amount of $ 1,002 in three installments based on their priority needs;
3) 372 highly vulnerable SSN and Daily-Wage laborer HHs (from Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah) will receive $1,016 over four months ($254 monthly installments).
This urgent cash support will allow vulnerable HHs to have flexible, reliable, and timely financial support to address their pressing and critical needs, restore their livelihoods, build resilience and improve agriculture and fishery production capacity. Mercy Corps will distribute cash installments through Electronic Debit Cards (e-cards) from PalPay and Bank of Palestine, which has extensive geographic coverage and is highly accessible through ATMs across the Gaza Strip. This will ensure that beneficiaries can access cash assistance outside regular bank work hours and even if banks are limiting operations due to the COVID-19 crisis and restricted movement measures.
By utilizing cash as a modality for direct assistance, beneficiaries have greater power in determining how to meet their own urgent needs, empowering them and maintaining their dignity while reducing their vulnerability and contributing to the restoration of their food security. Additionally, in the Gaza Strip, local markets are functional, and vendors continue to have adequate stock available; therefore, cash assistance is an appropriate humanitarian response modality to meet critical basic needs.
Through well-established evidence-based vulnerability and technical targeting, Mercy Corps and partners will use the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF), and Technical Criteria to identify, assess and verify selected vulnerable HHs to receive cash assistance, monitor activities, and ensure full coordination and engagement with the MoSD, Ministry of Agriculture, the community and all other relevant stakeholders.
Additionally, Mercy Corps will promote community ownership, accountability, and participation throughout the project through baseline assessments, orientation, Post Distribution Monitoring, and its Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanism (CARM), while also mobilizing critical stakeholders to more deeply engage with identified vulnerable HHs.

1 - Needs

With continuing restrictions on movement and access in Gaza for 13 years, and the severe Palestinian Authority liquidity crisis in 2019, economic momentum faces significant challenges and has further deteriorated the humanitarian and socio-economic situation. In the wake of a collapsing economy in Gaza, Palestinian living standards have continued to decline, with aid flows no longer providing the impetus for growth. The COVID-19 crisis since March 2020, and the outbreak in Gaza since August 2020 is having a damaging impact on an already constrained economy struggling to emerge, making the immediate prospects for the Palestinian people difficult (World Bank). As poverty deepens, vital services are underfunded, inaccessible, or non-existent. The low quality of essential services does not meet the individual's most basic needs and is inadequate to respond to the current crisis's heightened vulnerabilities. All socio-economic indicators and humanitarian conditions continue to decline as nearly 72% of Gaza people are food insecure (FSS-HNO). Unemployment in Q2 of 2020 was 49.1% (PCBS). The pandemic has also pushed many households into poverty, with a forecasted increase of at least 10% in the poverty rate (World Bank). Over the initial months of the pandemic, many businesses, especially small enterprises, could not withstand the burden of months of closure, and many had to shut down permanently. Since the beginning of the COVID-19 crises in March 2020, nearly 8,700 daily-wage laborers in the domestic tourism sector, 80% of registered taxi drivers in Gaza (~2,090), and 10,000 construction sector workers lost their jobs (General Federation of Trade Unions, Gaza Construction Industry Association). An assessment of Farmers and Breeders under the current Cash Programme (Mercy Corps, Aug 2020) has revealed a range of gaps at the household (HH) and livelihood levels: 92% of the farmers have accrued debts with a median of $2,000. Of those, HHs took debts to cover food needs (79%), NFI needs (28%), healthcare needs (28%), and educational needs (20%). 54% of HHs bought food on credit, while 40% reduced expenditure on non-food items. 92% of the assessed farmers and breeders fall below the national Deep Poverty Line. Farmers and Breeders' baseline assessments have shown a range of priority needs: 64% of the farmers' first priority is open land rehabilitation, 20% greenhouse rehabilitation, 14% new/repair water catchment. 20% of farmers' second priority is an energy source. Likewise, 60% of the breeders need farm repairs, 76% need essential tools and equipment, 17% need an energy source, and 74% need a heating source. Mercy Corps’ assessment of Fishers (Sep 2020) shows that most fishers used at least one of the common coping strategies due to lack of financial capacity: 81% of HHs bought food on credit or borrowed money to cover their needs. To mitigate food insecurity, 56% reduced the number of daily meals, 53% curbed adults' consumption to provide sufficient food for children, and 50% consumed less food during meals at least three times a week. Fisher HHs reported they were unable or partially able to meet basic needs, including food (90%), healthcare (96%), and HH essential items (97%). Additionally, 93% of the assessed fisher HHs fall below the national Deep Poverty Line. The technical assessment showed that fishers' priority is to repair or buy new fishing nets and tools (53%), repair the engine or the generator (31%), rehabilitate their boat (44%), or repair their boat lighting system (28%). Assessment for SSN Male-Headed and Female-Headed Households (Mercy Corps and NRC, Sep 2020) found that the sample's (2,656 HHs) unemployment rate was 71%. HHs resort to a range of coping strategies to cope amid high unemployment, including buying food and goods on credit or using borrowed money (74%) and reducing non-food expenditure (40%). Over 90% of the HHs found to be below the national Deep Poverty Line, and 99% of them were unable or partially able to meet food needs. Based on the needs assessments, Mercy Corps used the Gender Analysis Framework tool to help identify the gender-specific information needed to design and deliver smart, targeted project activities that meet the needs of both men and women. The tools help to analyze gender around five domains 1) Laws, regulations, and institutional practices; 2) Cultural norms and beliefs; 3) Gender roles, responsibilities, and time used; 4) Access to and control over assets and resources, and 5) Patterns of power and decision-making. Please see the Gender and Age Marker (GAM) reference number G215332183.

2 - Activities

Mercy Corps and its local partners (Gaza Group and Al Foukhary) will target the most vulnerable and food-insecure HHs in two major groups: Group 1) farmers, breeders, and fishers; and Group 2) HHs and FHHs on the Social Safety Net (SSN) waiting list and daily-wage laborers who have been impacted by COVID-19 and lost their source of income. The project will be implemented in Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah. For Group 1 (farmers, breeders, and fishers), the selection will be through an open application process and based on two assessments: a HH vulnerability assessment using the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF), combined with a technical vulnerability assessment tool developed based on established selection criteria. PMTF is a methodology that helps to identify and target the most in need of humanitarian assistance programs. It uses multivariate regression to correlate specific proxies, including HHs’ characteristics, income, assets, coping strategy index, food consumption, expenditures, debts, etc. Technical selection criteria for farmers, breeders, and fishers will include: i) dependency on agricultural livelihood as their sole source of income; ii) livelihood assets or lands proximity to ARAs; iii) small farmers, breeders, or fishers with small possession of livelihood properties; iv) currently affected by crises of COVID-19 and purchasing power losses; v) have incurred losses to their livelihood because of the economic decline; vi) have lost their agriculture/fishery capacity or are close to losing their agricultural/fishery production due to increases in operating costs; and vii) have not received cash assistance or grants over the past three months. For Group 2 (SSN HHs and FHHs, and Daily-Wage Laborers), the selection will be done by obtaining lists from the MoSD and based on vulnerability assessment of HHs and FHHs using the PMTF exclusively. Mercy Corps will consider the automated scored results of the PMTF counted upon the home to home baseline survey as a sole inclusion and exclusion factor to identify the households who will benefit from the program. The most eligible SSN and daily-wage laborer HHs below the Deep Poverty Line and have the lowest PMTF scores (most vulnerable) will be prioritized to benefit from cash assistance. Mercy Corps and Local Partners will provide each selected beneficiary household with an Electronic Debit Cash Card (e-card) that they will use to access the cash from ATMs or purchase directly from shops of their selection. Before cash distributions, Mercy Corps and Local Partners will provide printed orientation material packages to all selected beneficiaries, share information, educate the beneficiaries on the project, and clarify the best possible ways to benefit from the assistance. The orientation materials will provide all project information and communication with Mercy Corps and will include information about the project and activities; clear instructions on using Electronic Debit Cards for the first time; bank and ATM locations in Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah governorate; project Monitoring and Evaluation; and Mercy Corps’ CARM system means of contact. Additionally, in the locations where program activities will be conducted, posters will be displayed describing the CARM and providing a Mercy Corps call center number and email address for beneficiaries (or any other interested party) to contact program staff with concerns, complaints or questions. Local partner NGOs are also required to adhere to Mercy Corps’ CARM policy when implementing program activities. The cash transfer amounts and top-ups will be distributed for each group of beneficiaries in multiple installments: Group 1) Farmers, Breeders, and Fishers (Unconditional – Restricted Cash): Each of the selected 228 farmers and breeders (97 Gaza, 41 Middle Area, 55 Khan Younis, and 35 Rafah) and 120 fishers (120 Gaza) will receive an average amount 1,200 USD (farmers) and 1,002 USD (fishers) in three installments, in line with technical assessment priority livelihood restoration and materials inputs that need to be decided by the farmers, breeders, and fishers themselves. Group 2) SSN FHHs and HHs, and Daily-Wage Laborer HHs (Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance): Each of the selected 192 SSN FHHs and HHs (82 Gaza, 34 Middle Area, 47 Khan Younis, and 29 Rafah) and 180 daily-wage laborers HHs (77 Gaza, 32 Middle Area, 44 Khan Younis, and 27 Rafah) will receive 254 USD of multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) monthly, for a period of four consecutive months. The MPCA value was informed by Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket developed by Mercy Corps in collaboration with the Cash Working Group. Finally, Mercy Corps’ comprehensive monitoring combined with a well-established CARM system allows program staff and partners teams to have real-time access to information on the ground. This also gives program teams and managers the ability to quickly adapt programming as necessary and maintain our strict standards and highest quality standards.

3 - Indicators

Throughout the project's life, Mercy Corps will ensure multi-layered monitoring, evaluation, and learning (MEL) at all levels. Mercy Corps' MEL unit will be involved from the early stages of project implementation. It will ensure that progress on achieving the outputs and outcomes is tracked by conducting regular site visits, spot checks, Data Quality Assurance (DQA), Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), and frequent beneficiaries' feedback surveys. Learning and PDM processes will be standardized across all groups and designed to capture the project's impact on the households, including goods and services purchased, amount spent and used, how decisions were made and intra-household dynamics, accountability, and their satisfaction and feedback. Local partners' enumerators will conduct PDM visits under the supervision of Mercy Corps, and data will be collected using PDM tools developed on Mercy Corps project central CommCare database. CARM system will be leveraged to allow community members and beneficiaries to voice their feedback, concerns, and complaints. Mercy Corps' project team will oversee the overall project activities implementation under Mercy Corps' management direction. In contrast, the partner NGO teams – Gaza Group and Al Foukhary – will be responsible for implementing farmer, breeder, and fisher related activities. Mercy Corps' project team will work closely with partners' project teams to provide all required technical and administrative support to them to ensure all project activities are jointly planned and implemented consistently and as per quality standards. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Mercy Corps Palestine has a robust Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanism (CARM) system backed by a global system. The system includes a Mercy Corps hosted and a dedicated call center with a free hotline through which a trained female operator is capable of addressing simple project related inquiries from beneficiaries and other community members. All CARM complaints or feedback are registered on an online tracking database in six categories. The six categories are i) Request for information, ii) Requests for assistance, iii) Minor dissatisfaction with program activities, iv) Major dissatisfaction with project activities or the behavior of Mercy Corps Team Members or Partners, v) Fraud or corruption by Mercy Corps Team Members or Partners vi) Sexual exploitation and abuse, child abuse, sexual misconduct, human trafficking by Mercy Corps Team Members or Partners. The first four categories (i, ii, iii, and iv) are sent to respective Mercy Corps members as per CARM policy, who prepare a response and communicate feedback back to callers promptly. The program team immediately adopts corrective actions, and report back to community members who submitted information requests or complaints. Data from the CARM tracking database are collected and analyzed regularly, and an internal report is produced in line with Mercy Corps Global CARM Minimum Standards policy. Other categorized sensitive complaints (v, and vi), which include fraud, corruption, child abuse, or sexual misconduct, are processed through a well-established dedicated safeguarding committee trained by Mercy Corps under direct supervision from both the Country Director and Mercy Corps’ Headquarters Integrity Team. Mercy Corps will give beneficiaries all possible means to convey their feedback about their concerns and specific needs, not only during assessments but also throughout the project life through direct contact, surveys, PDMs, and CARM.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

To appropriately address identified needs, Mercy Corps proposes a high-impact project that expands on the success of the current Mercy Corps Cash Programme to urgently meet the basic needs of highly vulnerable HHs in these community groups to help them cover their basic needs, reduce their use of negative coping strategies, avoid incurring further debt and restore their production capacity, resilience, and food security. In designing this project, Mercy Corps built on the success and learning of the current cash programming, in addition to its standard procedure of gender and protection mainstreaming. Thorough risks, protection, and gender analyses were conducted to inform the design of activities, management of the project, mitigation plans, and safety and security measures for both participant beneficiaries and project staff. Mercy Corps provides refresher trainings regularly to all Mercy Corps team members and local partners on Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanisms (CARM), Safeguarding policies, including the protection of program participants and code of conduct. Likewise, all program participant beneficiaries and community members will be oriented on approaching Mercy Corps and providing feedback or complaints using CARM as described in (Activities, and 8.6.c under Feedback and Complaints). Each project beneficiary receives an orientation package that includes information about the project, CARM, and safety guidelines on cash handling. Mercy Corps collect and share data about beneficiaries using the Data Minimization Approach, Beneficiary Privacy, and Data Security Protocols: i.e., Mercy Corps gathers only the data that is adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary to determine the eligibility of humanitarian assistance and required to measure the impact, and to cross-check to avoid duplicate assistance. Beneficiary Data are collected, synced, and stored on Mercy Corps’ highly secured CommCare database, which is only accessible to authorized MEL staff. Mercy Corps will distribute cash assistance in this project through Electronic Debit Cards (e-cards) from PalPay and Bank of Palestine, which has extensive geographic coverage and is highly accessible by beneficiaries through ATMs and Branches in Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah. This will ensure that cash assistance will be accessible by beneficiaries even outside regular banks' work hours, and even if banks are limiting their operations due to the COVID-19 crisis and restricted movements measures, comply with COVID-19 social distancing measures, and avoid health hazards resulting from crowds inside banks to collect cash. If local authorities impose movement restriction due to COVID-19, Mercy Corps and local partner NGOs will coordinate with authorities to distribute cards to participants door-to-door and consider all health safety measures to both teams and beneficiaries.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through well-established evidence-based vulnerability and technical targeting, Mercy Corps and local partner NGOs will use the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF), and Technical Criteria to identify, assess and verify selected vulnerable HHs to receive cash assistance, monitor activities, and ensure full coordination and engagement with the line ministries (Ministry of Social Development [MoSD], and Ministry of Agriculture), the community and all relevant stakeholders. The identification of eligible beneficiary farmers, breeders, and fishers will be carried out through an open and transparent application process using established vulnerability criteria and mobile data collection tools. Social Safety Net (SSN), and daily-wage laborers vulnerable households will be identified from the MoSD unserved waiting lists of who lost their source of income and livelihood from Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah. The potential beneficiary's data will be synced on the Mercy Corps database (CommCare). Home-to-home vulnerability assessments will be conducted to all beneficiaries to confirm their eligibility according to established selection criteria for each beneficiary group. The PMTF will be used as the standardized selection criteria for targeting all of the project's beneficiary HHs. It is a methodology that helps to identify and target the most in need of humanitarian assistance. It uses multivariate regression to correlate specific proxies, including HHs' characteristics, income, assets, coping strategy index, food consumption, expenditures, debts, and others. For farmer, breeder, or fishers beneficiaries, the selection will be based on two assessments: The vulnerability assessment of farmer and fisher HHs using the PMTF, combined with a technical vulnerability assessment tool developed based on established selection criteria. Final lists of selected HHs will be cross-checked with the FSS member’s database, INGOs providing similar assistance, and local authorities to avoid duplication. People with special needs (elderly, pregnant and lactating women, people with chronic health conditions, disabled) and their caregivers will be consulted during the life of the program to understand their safety and dignity concerns. Mercy Corps and local partners' teams will identify the people with special needs during the household assessment surveys. Mercy Corps will provide printed orientation material packages to all selected beneficiaries, share information, educate the beneficiaries on the project, and clarify the best possible ways to benefit from the assistance (please see Activities, and 8.6.c). Beneficiaries with special needs or have difficulties due to illiteracy, or cultural reasons (e.g. needing a male relative to accompany a female household member) will be assisted to identify and select trustee "Helpers," such as first-degree family members, to assist them in receiving their cash/cashing out from ATMs. During the implementation and monitoring of project activities, the Mercy Corps program team and local partners teams can go to the home of people with special needs to collect feedback and ensure they received the assistance.
