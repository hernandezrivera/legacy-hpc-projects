Project Summary

In its seventh year, the armed conflict in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in eastern Ukraine continues to have a devastating effect on people, with 3,4 million in need of humanitarian assistance and protection. The Covid-19 pandemic has been further aggravating humanitarian needs, particularly for people living in rural areas close to the contact line and most notably elderly, who lost access to humanitarian assistance and social payments due to travel-restrictions, are unable to cover their basic needs amid rising prices, and resort to negative coping strategies. With overlapping vulnerabilities and multiple needs, the protracted crisis in Ukraine requires a multisector approach that links the alleviation of immediate basic needs with measures that increase resilience and promote recovery at community level
The project is aimed to contribute to sustaining and safeguarding of the lives of vulnerable populations and internally displaced Ukrainians at conflict-affected communities in Donetsk and Luhansk regions through sustainable recovery and resilience measures. The contribution is supposed to make through two strategic objectives:
• SO1: Vulnerable conflict-affected households (HHs) along the contact line in non-government controlled areas (NGCA), especially FHHs & HHs with elderly people, are able to secure their basic needs and sustain their lives. (56% female, 44% male)
• SO2: Vulnerable conflict-affected HHs along the contact line in government-controlled areas (GCA), especially FHHs & HHs with elderly people, have improved their health and resilience amid Covid-19 and recovered their livelihoods towards increased sustainability. (70% female, 30% male).
Target group: Direct target group: 12,820 individual unique beneficiaries (7,858 women, 4,962 men) living close to the contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk in government-controlled (GCA) and non-government controlled areas (NGCA). Indirect target group: 1,382 indirect individuals living close to the contact line in GCA.
Project Geography: Valuiske, Makarove, Vilhove and Zolote-4 in Luhansk oblast, and Novobakhmutivka and Novoselivka in Donetsk oblast, Ukraine.
Expected results:
1.1. At least 1,440 vulnerable households of IDPs and host population in NGCA have improved living conditions through winterization support and hygiene assistance. (56% female, 44% male)
2.1. At least 300 highly vulnerable people with special needs in GCA have improved their hygiene conditions through the provision of hygiene kits, 150 of whom have additionally improved their physical health through the provision of homecare services. (70% female, 30% male).
2.2. At least 240 vulnerable people (including 150 highly vulnerable people with special needs who receive homecare services) benefit from improved psychosocial well-being (75% female, 25% male).
2.3. At least 480 people in 6 target settlements benefit from improved community cooperation to support their resilience towards the effects of the ongoing conflict and the Covid-19 pandemic. (70% female, & 30% male)
2.4. At least 9.000 people in 6 target settlements in GCA, especially women and elderly, have increased awareness about Covid-19 related risks and prevention measures, 1.200 of whom have decreased vulnerability against the pandemic by obtaining PPE kits. (63% female, 37% male)
2.5. At least 60 residents (80% women and 20% men) of IDPs and host population have improved capacity to start and re-start businesses, 30 of whom engage in income generating activities based on increased knowledge, skills and business grants.
2.6. At least 45 staff of implementing partner organisations operating in GCA or NGCA have increased capacity to implement interventions that mainstream gender and protection and follow a community development approach.

1 - Needs

In its seventh year, the armed conflict in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in eastern Ukraine continues to have a devastating effect on the 5,2 million people living in the conflict-affected areas. Considered a protracted humanitarian crisis, it leaves 3,4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance and protection, and 1,4 million internally displaced. Even though the security situation has improved since the latest ceasefire in July 2020, the humanitarian situation remains critical. In addition, there is growing concern that the COVID-19 pandemic will further aggravate humanitarian needs, due to the large proportion of vulnerable people, poor healthcare and lack of adequate infrastructure for prevention. Even under conservative estimations, absolute poverty is expected to rise from 27% to 44%.The situation is particularly grave for the elderly, people living in rural areas along the contact line, IDPs, people with specific needs as well as girls and women, who experience a 50% increased risk of gender-based violence and financial difficulties. With overlapping vulnerabilities and multiple needs, the protracted crisis in Ukraine requires a multisector approach that links the alleviation of immediate basic needs with measures that increase resilience and promote recovery. In particular, the problem analysis of conflict-affected communities has highlighted: 1. Increasing vulnerability and multiple needs in isolated GCA settlements, particularly of elderly people with disabilities: The elderly make up 32% of the conflict-affected population overall, and 41% of the population within 0-5km distance to the contact line. Women are particularly affected; with a higher life expectancy by 10 years, they make up almost two thirds of the retirement age population in Ukraine. C-UA monitoring data has shown that the needs remain particularly acute for elderly HHs with special needs, which require a variety of home-based care services and assistance with hygiene, medical supplies and services, communication, personal care and personal protective equipment to maintain their health, psychosocial well-being and allow them to live in dignity. C-UA estimates that up to 10-13% of the conflict-affected population in the target area need homecare. 2. Lack of livelihoods opportunities for women in isolated GCA settlements: COVID-19 has significantly impacted the livelihoods of people in conflict-affected settlements, with 82% of HHs reporting increased prices of food and hygiene items, 38% of HHs reporting a decrease in monthly income (and a further 22% being impacted by loss of household income. Women have shown to be particularly affected by the socio-economic impact of COVID-19. Local conflict-affected people require equipment for growing agricultural products as well as innovative solutions to selling their products. Without access to income-generating opportunities to improve their living conditions, they will continue to depend on humanitarian and government assistance.3. Unmet basic needs of conflict and COVID-19 affected people in NGCA: The situation is particularly dire for elderly people and people with disabilities, who virtually all rely on social benefits and pensions as their only source of income. With the full closure of the checking points during the lockdown, which usually register up to 1.2 million crossings per month, and the restricted reopening since, thousands of people remain deprived of essential services, pensions and social payments. Since the outbreak of COVID-19, 40% of HHs in NGCA report an increase in prices for hygiene items, and 70% an increase in food prices. This has led to a dramatic increase in negative coping strategies. The Shelter Cluster estimates that in the upcoming winter season, winterization support remains a life-saving need for 160,000 people for whom heating materials are often unavailable or costly. There is an increased need for hygiene assistance in highly vulnerable NGCA HHs.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

The overall objective of the proposed project is to contribute to sustaining and safeguarding the lives of vulnerable populations and internally displaced Ukrainians in conflict-affected communities in Donetsk and Luhansk regions through sustainable recovery and resilience measures. This is planned to be achieved through the following Specific Objectives with disaggregation on Cluster:Shelter (SO1, NGCA): 640 HHs (approximately 1651 people, 56% female, 44% male) in NGCA will improve living conditions through the procurement and distribution of heating materials. According to Shelter Cluster recommendations, each household will receive 3 tons of heating materials, which will cover the demand for the whole season. Distribution of heating materials at NGCA to be realized through the local organization Caritas.WASH (SO1, SO2, NGCA/GCA): 1) Distribution of 900 hygiene kits for 300 elderly with special needs, including 150 bedridden and immobile people (3 kits per person) in GCA. The distribution will be done by mobile teams in cooperation with CFW workers in 3 rounds. 2) Distribution of 1600 hygiene kits for 800 HHs in NGCA (approx. 2064 people, 56% female, 44% male) for vulnerable people will improve their hygiene conditions through procurement and distribution of hygiene kits. The composition of hygiene kits for both the target groups will be agreed with the Cluster. PROTECTION (SO2, GCA): 1) Development and printing of information material about Covid-19 related risks, including gender-based violence, protection measures, and mitigation strategies: The content of the information leaflets is based on patterns designed by the WHO (Covid-19 risk mitigation measures) and the National Police Service (GBV prevention measures). 2) 1.200 locals will decrease the vulnerability against the pandemic by obtaining PPE kits (63% female, 37% male). 3) A study on GBV issues in the project locations will also be conducted.HEALTH (SO2, GCA): 1) Provision of 1,440 psychosocial support sessions for 240 people (at least 6 sessions per person on average, depending on the need of beneficiaries): Depending on their needs, beneficiaries will receive up to 6 individual PSS sessions (in person or via phone) in accordance with WHO and IASC guidelines and recommendations. Psychologists will receive ongoing supervision to discuss methodologies as well as any support-related problems. 2) Provision of non-specialized homecare services to 150 elderly with special needs, including bedridden and immobile people, through 15 trained Cash for Work workers, twice per week (70% female, 30% male). LIVELIHOOD (SO2, GCA): 1) Provision of 24 business skills training sessions for at least 60 active residents (48 women and 12 men) from 6 settlements (business planning, marketing and sales, market analysis, financial literacy, legal and taxation aspects) - 4 cycles of 6 training sessions overall, 15 participants per cycle on average. 2) Selection and distribution of business grants for 30 active residents (24 women and 6 men) from 6 settlements (EUR 1.500 each in average): Once participants completed the training, they submit a grant application form to a grant committee. 3) Grantees have the opportunity to receive further mentoring and business coaching (4,5h over 3 months) from an external expert. The expert will be selected according to the area of specializations and needs of the beneficiary
