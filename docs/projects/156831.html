Project Summary

Syria Relief (SR) aim to continue providing integrated lifesaving and life-sustaining assistance to populations most impacted by the conflict in North Syria. In Deir Ez-Zor (DEZ) and Ar raqqa governorates, populations subject to shifting frontlines need assistance to address their basic needs in the context of humanitarian coverage gaps. SR one of the few actors able to access security-tensed, volatile areas along the DEZ East Line, closest to the frontline, based on their operational capacity and knowledge of the area and context. Supporting three health facilities and three Primary Health Centers in Ar-Raqqa and DEZ governorates with lifesaving and life sustaining healthcare.

Needs

SR MEAL team conducted assessments for qualitative data collection in the selected areas of DEZ and Ar-Raqqa governorates. The assessment aimed at identifying local health facilities and mapping the services provided, clearly specifying if these services are provided for free or are paid services as well as assessing the situation, needs, in particular medical needs, of the local communities in the considered areas through interview with the local population. SR also conducted a general assessment in both locations to provide a comprehensive description of the humanitarian situation within the assessed areas to achieve an appropriate response in health sector.SR led a medical assessment of the facilities in NES, encompassing DEZ and Ar-Raqqa facilities. In DEZ, a rural area already impacted by medical underservice before the crisis, the sole services available are private health facilities hosted in small private houses and that do not have the capacity to provide adequate services to the entire population in the area. IDP flows has formed small cities which largely overburden the health structures of a previously rural, far less populated area. In terms of surgery or trauma care, most services are provided in unaffordable private clinics, located further north in Al Hassakeh, or in GoS-held territories bringing the risk of having to cross-border and approach active frontlines. The same issue is found in Ar-Raqqa governorate where closest facilities are in Tal Abyad (1.5hour drive), in Aleppo or GoS-held areas. Clear protection concerns were raised by this assessment as no health facility in Ar-Raqqa governorate is equipped for people with disabilities, and elderlies declare not being able to cover the large distances separating them from aforementioned facilities.November 2018: Health and Basic Needs Assessment (SR), Al-Busayrah, Khasham, Thiban, Hajin, Sur, Raqqa sub-districts (Ar-Raqqa and DEZ governorates). November 2018: Rapid needs assessment (SI), Thiban Sub-district (Abu Hamam city - DEZ)

Implementation of activities

The main activities are the following;- Rehabilitation and refurbishment of three health facilities and three PHCs in Ar-Raqqa City and DeirEzzor - Provision of Primary Health Care services- Provision of a mobile services unit- Provision of 4 ambulances services in Raqqa and Deir Azzor - Provision of free of charge lifesaving emergency and surgical assistance in three health facilities- Provision of sexual and reproductive health care in Raqqa and DZ, including family planning counselling- Protection training and outreach, MPSS and case management for children and women- Contingency and emergency preparedness- Medical training for Health staff including (IYCF-E Training (Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency) + TOT, Basic Emergency Obstetrics and Newborn Care (BEmONC), Integrated Management of Newborn and Childhood Illness (IMNCI), Primary Healthcare in Emergencies and Emergencies Response.- Providing Trauma / Primary Healthcare in Emergencies and Emergencies Response training and Infection prevention and control training.- SR will train staff to do vaccination campaign - SR will coordinate with local health departments and other INGOs to select aria of vaccination - SR will out reach team to provide awareness sessions on vaccination- SR will provide vaccination through its facilities and mobile team

Protection Risk Analysis

-Health facilities and health staff facing violence:Applying de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties involved to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL).Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG) and humanitarian leadershipReporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase.-Gender Base Violence affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package.Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. -Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEAA PSEA policy describes standard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awarenessraising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In NES: If the security situation degrades severely, SR will monitor the situation on the ground and adjust existing contingency plans per different scenarios. In case of fighting resuming or shift of frontlines, SR will strive to maintain their presence, eventually reducing international staff in NES to maintain essential staff. Should the situation become too risky, services may have to be temporarily suspended in accord with donor. As soon as the situation stabilizes, essential staff will return into NES. In case of GoS takeover of part or whole of NES, both SR have launched a registration process in Damascus in anticipation. Should this risk materialize without the registration process being completed and approved by GoS, SR are mapping local potential actors able to take over part of the activities or provide umbrella support to allow completion of the programme. This would however significantly impact the implementation of the proposed intervention and SR would communicate timely with the donor. In case of GoT takeover of NES border areas, SR team will be ready to move South from Amuda to Tal Tamer, either temporally or permanently, to maintain its capacity to deliver Humanitarian services to the affected population. SR has also secured an official registration in Turkey that should enable to maintain access inside the newly areas taken over by GoT. In case of deteriorating relationship with local councils, camps managers or key actors impeding the project implementation, SR staff will reiterate their mandate. SR are transparent through all actions and communicate with all stakeholders to ensure humanitarian principles. Unforeseen delays in the procurement process may prevent timely supply of medicine and/or critical equipment to the intervention. However, SR follow a purchase plan taking into consideration potential delays. SR is also very familiar with the supply and custom process and market of the area. If qualified staff is not available in the selected areas, SR will consider assuming resident doctors who can prove their experience and receive sign off from the Health Directorate. The recruitment process will start promptly to maximize the time available to find the correct staff. Delays or loss of funds transferred to the chosen locations might cause delays. However, SR has been transferring funds inside Syria using fully registered and due diligenced Money Transfer Agents (Hawala) in Iraq. All MTAs are selected according to strict logistics and finance quotations process and anti-terrorist checks per donor regulations.
