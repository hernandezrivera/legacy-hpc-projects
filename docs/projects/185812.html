Project Summary

"The Emergency Food Security and Vulnerable Livelihood (EFSVL) Rohingya Response Programme aims to respond to the compounded risks of Covid-19 and pre-existing vulnerabilities of women and girls, men and boys and at-risk individuals among them in Rohingya and host communities. This project proposes to continue providing food security and livelihood support to vulnerable women, men, boys and girls of Rohingya & Host Community (HC) people for the lifesaving interventions to improve access to adequate diversified food access and livelihood opportunity. Intervention activities include COVID-19 livelihood support services like Unconditional Cash Grants (UCG); Cloth masks & cloth Sanitary Napkin products, complimentary food support to host community farmer’s market initiatives; enhancing DRR resilience linkages to food security; self-reliance and sustainable livelihood opportunity etc. In addition, the EFSVL project will work on Homestead Garden & Pit Composting, rural infrastructure through Cash for Work (CfW) approach, entrepreneurship and small trade business through Income Generating Activities (IGAs) and Vocational Training (VT) for the host communities through a market-based livelihood opportunity. As a result of the COVID-19 Pandemic context, the specific needs of food security & livelihood support, and Government policy restrictions on direct cash injection in the Rohingya camps, Oxfam prefers to do self-reliance activities (e.g. skill-based development training and input supports) for the Rohingya participants at the camps and COVID-19 livelihood activities (e.g. cloth masks and sanitary napkins production etc.) for the most vulnerable. Oxfam will do necessary coordination and cohesion between two communities (Rohingya and Host) by integrating EFSVL work with Oxfam’s Gender and Protection departments. The proposed interventions (e.g. IGAs such as Handicraft’s exhibition from both communities; Market based voucher initiatives; etc.) will contribute to strengthening the relationship and social cohesion within and between Rohingya and host communities. Assisting participants to live with good health, dignity and better safety.
Oxfam’s has a global commitment to build the capacity of local organizations through a partnership approach. Oxfam’s EFSVL sector will continue to work with local partners as part of its programme strategy. Oxfam will review the EFSVL strategy to cope with the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and Rohingya crises in 2022. "

1 - Identified Needs

"Access to fresh and nutritious food continues to be an obstacle for the refugee population residing in Cox’s Bazar camps and host community areas due to the ongoing Rohingya crises and in addition to the COVID-19 pandemic. In April 2021, The World Food Programme’s (WFP) Rohingya Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 4) found that “Overall vulnerability levels have reached an all-time high in the refugee camps since the influx, with latest findings showing that 96 percent of all refugees are moderate to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance”. Food insecurity for both Rohingya and surrounding host communities has increased since 2019, primarily due to the COVID-19 pandemic. A previous assessment conducted by WFP found that by the end of 2020, 86 percent of Rohingya refugees were highly vulnerable to poverty and hunger, up from 70 percent in 2019. While within host communities, members considered moderately or highly vulnerable in terms of food insecurity increased to 51 percent in 2020 compared to 41 percent in 2019. This increase is reflected where poverty rates were found to have increased in 2020 and onwards (REVA-4, summary report-April 2021) due to the CV-19 impacts. Due to the food insecurity, at the household level, vulnerable groups adopted mainly livelihood coping strategies to access food support. Compared to 2019 the livelihood coping strategies increased in credit-dependency of Rohingya households (62 percent). Respectively host communities dependency changed from 41 to 53 percent. Overall, two third of Rohingya and one-third of host community households are involved in a crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies to cover food and/or basic needs compromising their resilience and future productivity. Both groups have increased the selling of their assets to access food and other basic needs. One out of three income sources in the camps come from negative coping methods such as selling assistance, borrowing money, or relying on help from relatives and friends. For Rohingya households with an income source, monthly earnings are on average 75 percent lower than host community households and are equivalent to 37 percent of the Minimum Expenditure Basket, barely covering for basic needs (REVA 04 April 2021). (Source: WFP REVA April 2021): Overall household vulnerability was significantly correlated with household income and the nature of the main income activity. Eighty-six percent of Rohingya households who were highly vulnerable had the lowest average household income levels (BDT 2,736), while the same for highly vulnerable host community households (11 percent) is BDT 6,822.As Covid-19 measures were in place in Cox’s Bazar, food access reduced and became more insufficient for refugees, and this trend was more for single mothers, households headed by persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly. Disproportionate realities of women and girls were exacerbated during the Covid-19 pandemic which impacted their mobility thereby reducing access to basic needs, further reducing their limited access to income, and increasing the burden of care work due to staying at home protocols. Women who are mostly in the informal economy and rely on daily wage and minimum have very few safety nets, especially in host communities. Across host and Rohingya communities, the experience of GBV also increased including IPV and early marriage. (Rapid Gender Analysis on Covid-19, October 2020."

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Multiple Purpose with Value Voucher: Oxfam EFSVL team will explore the multi-purpose value voucher programming for the Rohingya Refugees to fulfil their needs in addition to FFV (ongoing system) and WFP Food Baskets. Target: 5,000 multiple value vouchers for 5,000 HHs. Obj_01.2. Homestead Gardening & Pit Composting support to increase diversified nutritious fresh vegetables at the household level for consumption and to gain income opportunities. Target: 1000 HHs (HC) & 500 HHs (Camp). Obj_02.3. Income Generating Activities (IGAs): Entrepreneurship business development interventions for the HC, enhancing their skills and competencies to increase income-generating opportunities through different small or trade businesses. Under these IGA interventions Oxfam provides skilled developed training and necessary input supports (e.g. cash grant; input supports) to the target beneficiaries. Target: 3500 HHs. Obj_02.4. Vocational Training (VT) entrepreneurship skills for self-employment in the HC area. The team will also look at linkage into the market system through a rapid market assessment to ensure they can sell their skills or if they can start on their own. (e.g. tailoring; repairs; etc.). Target: 500 HHs. Obj_03.5. Dried Fish Livelihoods opportunity will introduce dried fish activities for the HC where they will receive training and necessary inputs support for the sector (e.g. cash grant). Target: 200 HHs. Obj_03.6. Provide awareness-raising sessions on food security and/or nutrition, and COVID-19 pandemic, including awareness sessions on sustainable livelihoods and food security for better understanding and utilization of food resources. C19 awareness sessions enhancing COVID 19 prevention practices. Target: 10000 individuals. Obj_03.7. Provide training on farming techniques and/or alternative livelihood practices to reduce the adverse impact on natural resources in the Rohingya camps and HC areas. Target: 5000 HHs. Obj_03.8. Cash for Work (CfW) and Women’s care work schemes. Target: 1000 project participants with 80 CfW schemes. Obj_03.9. Village Saving and Loan Association (VSLA) approach in sustainable livelihood interventions to establish groups of women and men who save together and can take loans from those savings. Target: 500 project participants. Obj_03.10. Literacy and Numeracy Functional Classes for the beneficiaries to enhance their business skills and competencies. Targeting illiterate or uneducated project participants to improve literacy and numeracy, and increase their knowledge on basic business management, record keeping, daily transaction; profit and losses, etc. Target: 1100 project participants. Obj_03.11. Special Attention on Self-reliance activities for Rohingya Refugees. Oxfam will explore training needs to enhance skill and ability to increase income opportunity at household level. Special focus will be paid to the most vulnerable women & adolescent groups Target: 2000 project participants. Obj_02.12. Participants produce cloth masks to protect against CV19 and sanitary napkin pads for increasing hygiene practices. Target: 1000 project participants for making cloth masks (250,000) and sanitary napkins (200,000). Obj_02.13. Waste management control to have a better impact on the natural environment. In this scenario, Oxfam will introduce waste management system/control to decompose waste materials to be used as manure for agricultural cultivation. Target: 200 project participants. Obj_02.14. Strengthen Sustainable Natural Resource Management Target: 200 project participants. Obj_02.15. Contingency plan for the emergency operation (e.g. NFT; Complementary food packages, Unconditional cash supports etc.) will be incorporated in all food security and livelihood projects. Target: 4000 project participants (Contingency) & 3000 UCG. Obj_03.
