Project Summary

Decades of conflict in South Sudan have exacerbated and heightened the child protection risks for boys and girls including sexual violence and exploitation significantly affecting boys and girls thus having a grave impact on the children’s safety and well-being worsening family separation, abuse and recruitment of children into the armed forces and armed groups. shocks that manifested inform of severe flooding and drought in different parts of the Country. Climate change and climate shocks have caused displacement across Jonglei and Upper Nile. This situation has been heightened by Sudan Crisis due to the influx of returnees and refugees as a result to the conflict that broke out in Sudan on 15th April 2023. Most individual's majority children lack access to basic services and are under extreme distress causing numerators child protection concerns. These conditions have greatly impacted People’s livelihood; while raising protection concerns especially for women and girls, hence limiting access to basic public services such as education, Health, Shelter, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene and food. children are often weakened or broken down in times of crisis and exposing them to several grave violations including family separation, sexual abuse and recruitment into armed forces and armed groups.
According to the UNOCH South Sudan Flood Snapshots 15th November 2024, an estimated number exceeding 1.4 million people remain affected by flooding across South Sudan. More than 379,000 people are flood-displaced across 22 counties and Abyei. In Upper Nile State, recent assessments identified over 32,000 flood-affected people in Renk County 6,000 people in Maban County and 400,000 in canal/piji.
Since the start of the Sudan emergency, some 840,000 new arrivals have sought safety in South Sudan. Most, (77%) are South Sudanese nationals returning home, whilst the rest include 180,000 Sudanese refugees as well as smaller numbers of other foreign nationals. In addition to the Sudan response, according to UNHCR over two million people were forcibly displaced by civil war, sub-national conflict, and four years of consecutive flooding and climate-related shocks. People’s food security situation has deteriorated due to multiple shocks, including flooding, ongoing conflict, displacement and a high cost of living. As per the October 2023 IPC, approximately 7.1 of South Sudan's total population of 12.4 million are projected to face crisis-level or more severe acute food insecurity. As of October 2024, the Sudan crisis has resulted in significant humanitarian challenges for those crossing into South Sudan. Updated figures indicate that over 243,940 individuals (56,661 households) have entered South Sudan since the conflict began in April 2023. Approximately 97% of these entries occur via the Joda/Renk border crossing, underscoring its critical role in managing the influx. Of these arrivals, 44% are in transit or temporarily settled in Renk, while 32% have settled in Upper Nile, with others scattered across various areas.
The proposed project aims to build resilience among the vulnerable children and families affected by conflict, and natural disasters that have been made worse by the Sudan Crisis in Jonglei and Upper Nile state through the integrated through multi-sector Child protection and Community-based Mental Health and psychosocial support (MPHSS) without compromising the Gender and disability mainstreaming services to the IDPs, Returnees and Host communities in Malakal, Panyikang, Fashoda ,Canal Pigi and Bor South Counties. The intervention will be through coordination with UN Agencies, International and National Organizations and the government specifically Ministry of Gender Child and Social Welfare.
This project aims to provide child protection and MHPSS services for 9600 children and adults.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face a complex and multifaceted protection crisis, with conflict, natural disasters, and ongoing violence leading to mass displacement and heightened vulnerability among children and families. Over 2.2 million people are currently displaced, with conflict and climate-related disasters such as drought and flooding severely impacting the resilience of herding and farming communities. The humanitarian needs are acute, particularly in the prioritized areas of Upper Nile State (Melut, Fashoda, Malakal, Panyikang) and Jonglei (Bor South, Canal Pigi). These regions are hosting a significant influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees from Sudan who are experiencing life-threatening conditions. The situation for children in Upper Nile and Jonglei states of South Sudan is critical, with ongoing protection challenges exacerbated by conflict, displacement, and environmental factors. In Upper Nile and Jonglei, there are significant numbers of unaccompanied and separated children. Protection needs in Upper Nile and Jonglei have been heightened due to the ongoing conflict and inter-communal violence and the impact of ongoing conflict, such as clashes between communities in Jonglei, contributes significantly to these issues, making it harder for humanitarian efforts to reach vulnerable populations (UNICEF, 2024; UNOCHA, 2024).Despite these efforts, gaps remain in the child protection response especially the shortage of resources for effectively managing the needs of unaccompanied and separated children, particularly in remote areas where access to humanitarian aid is limited due to insecurity and logistical challenges. Furthermore, the broader protection response faces hurdles in providing adequate education, shelter, and health services to children affected by displacement and violence.According to the Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS+) data by Save the Children, South Sudan is currently facing a critical situation with a high number of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). As of now, there are 3,197 unaccompanied children (962 boys and 2,235 girls) and 6,097 separated children (2,066 boys and 4,301 girls) across the country. In the Upper Nile region, the number of UASC stands at 584 children (296 boys and 288 girls), while Jonglei state has 447 children (259 boys and 188 girls).The presence of such a high number of unaccompanied and separated children, particularly in conflict-prone areas like Upper Nile and Jonglei, places these children at significant risk of exploitation, abuse, neglect, and trafficking. Many of these children are without the protection of family, making them highly vulnerable to various forms of harm, especially during times of crisis. The lack of family care and community protection systems in these regions increases the risk of further harm to these children. They may face barriers to accessing essential services such as education, healthcare, and adequate nutrition. The establishment of child protection programs, family reunification efforts, and safe spaces is necessary to address these gaps and provide immediate relief.Upper Nile and Jonglei have experienced considerable instability, which exacerbates the vulnerability of children. Interventions must be geared toward immediate protection to prevent further displacement and insecurity for these children, ensuring they have access to safety, care, and rehabilitation.
