Project Summary

This project is based on QC strategic plan for the year 2023, covering the most urgent needs as per to cluster and HNO needs in NWS. Through this project, QC will continue the support for education needs in NWS as follow:
1- Rehab of 300 classrooms in NWS. Rehab works will cover all maintenance works in addition to medium and light construction works, WASH facilities rehab, painting and beatification works, and making sure schools are CwDs friendly in each detail. The activity is planned to target 21,000 students (%52 girls, %2 CwDs), teachers and educational staff and will prioritize formal schools in crowded communities in coordination with education cluster and EDs. Where needed, classrooms furniture will be provided to enhance teaching and learning environment.
2- Expanding the capacity of schools in NWS, through the construction of 45 classrooms in already existed schools in the selected districts. This will create more education opportunities for children (girls and boys including CwDs) to join education in their community. The activity is to increase the education seats in schools to allow around 3,150 students (%50 girls, %2 CwDs) to join education in their communities.
3- Construction of 24 classrooms school in Azaz district. It's a primary school to absorb all children who are OOS due to the lack of educational spaces. The activity will provide educational space for 1,680 students (%50 girls, %2 CwDs) in Azaz, as one of the most crowded districts in NWS.
4- Training 1,500 teachers (%60 females, %2 CwDs) in NWS focusing on their gaps and weaknesses. It's 60 hours training based on a need assessment to capture training topics in coordination with EDs.
5- Printing of 430,000 textbooks sets to be distributed on all children in Idleb and western rural of Aleppo. It's SIG curriculum will be printed and delivered for free to 430,000 school-age children (%50 girls, %3 CwDs).
6- Support 60 secondary schools in Idleb and western rural of Aleppo. The support is to cover teachers stipends, running costs, fuel, stationery for teachers and students, and maintenance costs. The activity is planned to target 22,500 (%52 girls, %2 CwDs) students in 60 secondary schools in Aleppo and Idleb in NWS.
The 2023 project is planned to target 479,830 students and educational staff in NWS (%51 girls, %60 females - estimated %2 CVA, %2 CwDs). All activities will be implemented based on the needs in areas witnesses severity 3 to 5 foe education needs in NWS (Azaz, Idleb, Albab, Atareb, Afrin, Harim, Jisr Al-shughur, etc.). Detailed need assessments will be conducted in the area of implementation to prioritize and justify the intervention. MoUs/Protocols will be signed with education stakeholders to ensure proper coordination on ground. Education cluster will be involved in need assessments and location prioritization to make sure targeting most vulnerable locations and populations, and to prevent and duplication. A comprehensive MEAL process is always implemented to monitor and evaluate educational project, learn from previous experiences, and to increase the transparency and accountability for all the implemented activities, making sure that community in each area is involved in planning, and have access to express their opinion through proper channels (i.e. PTAs in schools, FGDs in rehab projects, etc.). Surveys, need assessments, and quality checklist will be implemented to ensure quality-based intervention. Project will provide a full package support to children in NWS, starting from construction and rehab to expand learning spaces, thus, increasing access for all children to a positive education environment. Also, to train teachers and to print and distribute textbooks will positively impact the educational quality allover NWS. Supporting secondary schools will also ensure the education continuity in the most vulnerable level of education in NWS. QC will implement the project directly and through partners.

3 - Needs

As per to HNO 2023, 1.2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance are increased from 2021. Syria is still one of the biggest humanitarian crisis in the last decades. HNO also indicates that 6.6M PiN for education, 47% female (3.1 M), 53% male (3.5M), 97% CHILDREN (3-17) 6.4M, 3% ADULTS (18-59) 0.2M, 7% with disability 0.5M. The most urgent education needs that QC is going to respond to are classified below reaching both girls and boys (strategic) as below: 1- Support education continuity in formal secondary schools: as per to EDs in NWS, more than half of secondary schools are not supported with incentives, stationery, and running costs.2- Increase educational spaces in NWS through rehab, construction and expanding the existed formal schools: according to NHO, 1 school out of 3 is either destroyed, damaged, or used for other purposes. And the highest ratio for children in a classroom was in Idleb (1:178).3- Provision of school textbooks to formal schools: still the textbooks are classified as a continuous need every academic year. The absence of school textbooks could ruin the education process whole if not supported. 4- Promote teachers capacity through focused training: EDs is still urging NGOs to support to enhance education quality in NWS through a well-designed, need-based training, that enhance teachers performance and knowledge in education and protection topics.5- Non-formal education activities are still a need as many children in NWS are still OOS.

4 - Implementation of activities

1- Rehab of schools will focus on the partially damaged schools in NWS. Rehab works will cover electricity and WASH systems, doors, windows, classrooms, corridors, school yards, side walls, painting works and school furniture. For classrooms expanding activity, they will be constructed in the school yards, and classroom furniture will be provided. QC will handover the rehabilitated/expanded schools to EDs after completion. 2- A 24 classrooms primary school will be constructed from scratch in Azaz district. QC will sign cooperation protocol with ED and local council in Azaz. Later, engineering designs will be conducted based on the land donated by local council. Then, school furniture will provided to fully equip the school and hand it over to ED later. 3- 60 Hours teachers training will be conducted in coordination with EDs and Education cluster. Need assessment will be shared with selected teachers to identify their training needs. Training materials will be shared with education cluster for the feedback, and follow-up visits will be conducted to all trainees in their schools after training to evaluate their performance. 4- Textbooks will be printed and distributed to 430,000 children. QC is printing Arabic, English, Mathematics, Biology, Physic, Chemistry, Algebra, Geometry textbooks to keep neutrality. 5- 60 Secondary schools will be selected in Idleb and Aleppo. Full support will be provided to schools including teachers stipends, running costs, stationery, etc.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat: Children are aggressive in school and creating problems with their peers and teachers. Mitigation: - Provision of structured PSS to all children in schools, using aimed activities to increase the teamwork and enhance communication among children in school. - Activating referral mechanism to specialized protection experts for the necessary cases. Protection Risk/Threat: Violence, physical punishment or bullying in schools.Mitigating: -Sessions are offered by QC to the children in a child-friendly language to know what the expected behaviors are from the staff, and how they can report if any misbehavior occurs. -All C staff must attend the basic protection and safeguarding training and sign the code of conduct, in addition to effective communication with children, safe identification and referral, non-violent classroom management, safe photographing Protection. Risk/Threat: The security situation escalated, and shelling and clashes took place in Mitigation Measures: - During project period, access and liaison officer will maintain up-to-date info about security context in coordination with stakeholders and OCHA and will take immediate response if any risk hit. moreover, he will issue a monthly security report and share it with all the staff. - Train teachers and other staff on contingency plan and the different security scenarios. - Train children on evacuation plan, giving special care to CwDs.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk/Threat: Harassments incidents in schools are not reported because children don't know about it and/or don't know how to report on that.Mitigation Measures: - Conduct orientation sessions to teachers (females and males) on the PSEA code of conduct. - Conduct Awareness sessions on PSEA to all children in formal schools in the targeted camps inline with BTL campaigns.- QC outreach team to explain to children the existing CRM and how to report on incidents. Risk /Threat: Children are over-exposed to the risk of sexual harm by adults such asteachers, school personnel, organization staff, other adults or older students.Mitigation measures: - QC will ensure a survivor centered approach that imposessafety and confidentiality of the affected person. - QC have an updated servicemapping to refer such cases to experienced organizations. - QC has a PSEA Code ofconduct, that all children and Edu personnel are aware of and commit to apply.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: - Access for people and goods through Turkey to project locations is assured. Risk 1: - Security council resolution 2504 about cross-border assistance is not renewed (Likelihood: High - Impact: Medium )Mitigation Measures:- To work closely with education cluster and other stakeholders to follow the alternative scenarios. - To agree with the suppliers to provide all the goods to inside of Syria through border gates as a commercial materials. Assumption2: - Procurement of required materials is done on time to support the on-going education activities. Risk 2: Prices of materials are increased due to the big inflation in Turkey, as most of goods are being imported (Likelihood: Med - Impact: Med). Mitigations: - Budget activities in $, which will mitigate, to some extent, the jumping in prices. - Announce tenders on web, and encouraging suppliers to apply for the tender. Then, choosing the offer with best price and good quality. - Inform donor about the inflation and ask for technical advice. Assumption 3: EDs are using the same printed SIG curriculum.Risk 3: EDs are changing the curriculum to a modified one.Mitigation: - Support consultation meetings to agree on the revised version of curriculum.- Start all the meetings in early summer to be able to design curriculum and print it.- Printing over 400,000 copy to ensure all children having new textbooks. - Print only the scientific textbooks after EDs amendment.
