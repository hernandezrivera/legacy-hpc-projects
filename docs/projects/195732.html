Project Summary

In 2022, 14.6 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance. This constitutes the highest number of people in need since the onset of hostilities in 2011 and is expected to even further increase in 2023. In a context of high inflation and rapidly increasing food, fuel and basic goods prices, households' income gaps and thus debt loads are increasing. As a response, the project aims to enhance the ability of 6,537 vulnerable households (HHs) in Al-Hasakeh (Al Masaken), Aleppo (Jabal Badro), Homs (City), Hama (City), Damascus (Dwelaa) and Rural Damascus (Jdeidt Artouz, Sahnaya, and Daraya) affected by the crisis in government-held Syria to protect and sustain their lives. Following the guidance and the needs identified in the HNO 2022 and NFI sector HNO 2023, all selected localities have a severity scale of 3 or above. Winterization Assistance (WA) covering additional costs during the winter months (for 4 months) will be provided as per the SNFI/CWG guidance. This is especially relevant in the context of soaring prices for basic winter commodities and continuous deterioration of the currency, while at the same time salaries have remained stagnant. With winterization needs covered, HHs will be able to prevent the recourse to irreversible negative coping mechanisms and even reduce the ones already in place. Meanwhile, Cash for Winter transfer values (TVs) (146,250 SYP per month for a period of 4 month, totalling 585,000 SYP) are based on the recommendations of the CWG/NFI sector concerning the winter response and represent a monetization of the recommended winter NFIs in addition to 50 litres of subsidized fuel. The amounts are considered preliminary and are based on updated price assessments and harmonization efforts of the NFI WG and the CWG (last updated in November 2022).
The purpose of the project is that vulnerable, crisis-affected communities have improved ability to cover their winterization needs, hence reducing the adoption of harmful coping strategies. The assumption is therefore as follows: (IF) winterization assistance is provided to vulnerable communities (host, IDP, returnee) in Syria, (THEN) beneficiaries can meet their winterization needs with dignity and freedom of choice, and thus avoid having to resort to negative coping strategies.
The field teams continuously register HHs through outreach activities and home visits in the communities. Beneficiaries will also be registered through internal referrals from national partners and external referrals from NGOs and UN agencies. The implementing partner team will assess the vulnerability of registered beneficiaries through a basic needs scoring system which was developed by Caritas Switzerland (CACH) and Caritas Syria (CS) in December 2017 and refined in 2019 and 2022 to reflect the changing context.
All beneficiaries selected to receive winterization will also benefit from Basic Needs (BN) assistance from other funded programming. This approach will ensure that the full package of assistance (BN +Winter) is provided to the identified most vulnerable population.

3 - Needs

The number of people in need of NFI in Syria is estimated to increase from 4.9 m in 2022 to 5.7 m people in 2023. To contribute toward increased needs and mitigate effects of extreme weather conditions, the provision of winterization assistance will allow HHs to meet their increased costs for clothing, blankets, and fuel during the winter months. Needs are identified in CACH' needs assessment and are based on SNFI HRP 2022-23. By basing selection on vulnerabilities established through a basic need scoring system, the project prioritizes the most vulnerable to exposure while ensuring sufficient capacity of the local markets. The economic crisis and ongoing inflation have in the past year continued to further exacerbate the situation, aggravating peoples’ dire living situation and leading to increased food insecurity, protection needs, and amplified negative coping strategies. To address vulnerable communities (host, IDP, returnee) in Syria and increase investment in local communities, this project will deliver life-sustaining assistance through winterization assistance as the core modality. The intervention is planned in line with the recommendations of the CWG/SNFI and approved by government authorities. To ensure community engagement and that individual needs are understood, this project engages with HHs during project design and activity implementation. PDM findings and a complaint and feedback mechanism in place will enhance assistance programming based on actual needs.

4 - Implementation of activities

Provision of Winterization through MPCT to 6,537 HHs following the TV (585,000 SYP *Cash for winter NFI-minimum+50 liters subsidized*) as discussed with and recommended by the SNFI/CWG. Selection will be based on vulnerabilities established through a basic need scoring system, targeting extremely vulnerable HHs (score 10+).1: Select beneficiaries in need of winterization assistance. HHs will be identified through different channels and assessed based on the scoring system. 2: Eligible beneficiaries will be informed about their nomination and the requirements to participate in the intervention through awareness sessions. The process including the KYC & FCRMs will be explained, and consent requested from all eligible beneficiaries to move forward. 3: Teams will conduct regular market monitoring through surveys to ensure markets remain functional and are not affected negatively. Results will be shared with SNFI/CWG. 4: Transfer of funds to eligible beneficiaries and follow up on the no show report to ensure all beneficiaries are receiving the assistance. Finance and program reconciliation to take place. 5: PDMs to be conducted monthly, outcome monitoring, and FGDs to be conducted. Monitoring will be split (80%CS, 20%CACH independent field monitors) to validate the data gathered. 6: Reporting to take place as per the agreed reporting cycles. All winterization beneficiaries will also receive basic needs MPCT assistance from other CACH ongoing programming to ensure best coverage.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to perceived unfairness in distribution of assistance.M1: Community and religious leaders as well as communities will be transparently and well informed of the objective nature of the criteria and of the process to distribution. Selection criteria are transparent, and groups are fully informed.R2: Abuse of authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion).M2: CACH and CS ensure that their staffs are aware of the Code of Conduct, internal measures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation are in place.R3: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety).M3: All the FSPs offices must be near the targeted beneficiaries so that they may withdraw the transfers and ensure a safe journey to or from the FSP offices. HHs are free to choose who receives the assistance, ensuring safe access to FSP and reducing the risk of HH tensions or protection risks.R4: Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain population.M4: Scope of outreach activities in coordination with stakeholders in each location will be expanded; hard-to-reach/marginalized categories for assistance will be prioritized in scoring system.R5: Risk of corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection.M5: Strengthen over-sight mechanism and outreach to affected population. CACH and CS ensure that their staffs are aware of the CoC and develop a range of measures to minimize risk of corruption.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA is a key priority for CACH and finds expression in regulations and mechanisms to prevent and address all forms of abuse and sexual misconduct committed by representatives or partners involved in the delivery of our work. The policies are underpinned by CACH’s CoC which is clearly stating that any form of SEA is not tolerated (zero-tolerance strategy). All staff and partner organizations need to sign up to CACHs CoC, wherein it is clearly stated that reports of misconduct are investigated with all due diligence. To reduce the likelihood of abuse, a complaint mechanism will be in place to ensure that beneficiaries and staff have a safe space to indicate irregularities.R1: Assessment / outreach teams miss identification of hard-to-reach (HTR) groups during outreach and/or assessment phase (most vulnerable do not receive assistance).M1: Scope of outreach activities in coordination with stakeholders in each location will be expanded; HTR / marginalized categories for assistance will be prioritized in scoring system.R2: Assistance increases household tensions.M2: HHs with instances of CP or GBV will be referred to case managers, or to specialized protection agencies, PDM will monitor intrahousehold relationships.R3: Abuse of authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion).M3: CACH and CS ensure that their staffs are aware of the CoC, internal measures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation are in place.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: The economic situation in the country makes FSPs unable to disburse the cash requirements due to shortages in liquidity. M1: Increase number of FSPs; Maintain close monitoring of liquidity of FSPs; Maintain a flexible methodology that can switch between cash and in-kind when neededR2: Landlords increase rent, taking advantage of beneficiaries receiving cash M2: Advocate for fair regulations with authorities; Liaise with landlords to fix rent; establish contract or rent agreement; encourage advance rent paymentR3: Social tensions heightened when non-recipients see cash delivered to recipientsM3: Communicate the targeting criteria and program details such as TV clearly to the communities; Build on existing community leadership and structures; risk and conflict-sensitive program design; do not target by population category of IDPs/returnees/host communities, instead consider other categorical targeting e.g. elderly, children, women, etc.R4: Further devaluation of SYP / higher fuel prices increases prices / market shutdown M4: Closer monitoring of needs and shifts in patterns of expenditure, price, and market; adjust TV; maintain a flexible methodology that can switch between cash and in-kind; consider a small transfer top-up in addition to the regular TVR5: GoS restricts CVA as a modality and available transfer mechanisms are limited in some project areas M5: In case of no access for a prolonged time, in-kind house to house assistance will be considered
