Project Summary

The project aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality through improving access to life-saving preventive and curative health services in Jezira, White Nile, Red Sea, Gedaref, Khartoum, South Kordofan, and Blue Nile states in Sudan. The main beneficiaries are persons affected by the recent crisis caused by armed conflicts between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) including internally displaced communities (IDPs), and vulnerable host communities targeting children under five years of age, adolescent girls, women, boys and men. The project will hotspot areas affected by the current crisis as well as IDP-receiving states and will support the existing Ministry of Health (MoH) capacity to deliver lifesaving primary health care services, including management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, support reproductive; maternal, and child health services including strengthening of expanded program for immunization; mental health and psychosocial support. The IRC will build the capacity of MoH staff, and provide essential drugs and medical supplies, including supplies for infection prevention and control, as well as infrastructure improvement and surge teams in the form of mobile medical teams (MMTs) to quickly respond to health emergencies. This is well aligned with the IRC’s Sudan Response Strategy.
Protection services will also be integrated into the IRC’s health programming to achieve greater outcomes for women and girls, including prevention and response to Gender Based Violence (GBV). Through a community-based approach, the project will ensure equity by supporting community health volunteers to provide community health packages to hard-to-reach and underserved communities. The IRC WASH teams will closely support the HF teams to improve facility WASH infrastructures and practices including medical and non-medical waste management, hand washing stations, and hygiene promotion.
The IRC has a strong capacity to deliver health services in Sudan with current support including strengthening the MoH capacity to deliver integrated health services in Gedaref, Khartoum, Jazira, and Blue Nile states, and currently expanding to White Nile and Red Sea states. Since 2019, the IRC has supported the delivery of integrated health, nutrition, protection, livelihoods, and WASH services providing a one-stop center to deliver life-saving interventions to beneficiaries in a safe way. The IRC health technical excellence unit at the headquarter in NY with regional representation in Nairobi provides technical oversight and guidance for the adaptation of quality health standards, training, and technical supervision including frequent field presence to support country teams. Even in the face of the current crisis, the IRC has maintained in-country capacity and regional support to continue to deliver essential health services.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing its programs, the IRC makes every effort to address protection mainstreaming in the technical design. From design to implementation and monitoring, the core protection principles of ‘Do No Harm’, meaningful access, safety and dignity, and participation will be mainstreamed. The IRC health team works closely with protection colleagues to identify protection risks, vulnerabilities and referral pathways for services. The action will ensure availability of clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS) through the training of health facility staff and preposition of supplies for clinical management of rape (CMR). Providers will be trained on ensuring privacy and confidentiality including in the health information system (HIS). The IRC will also train key providers at the health facilities and in the communities on psychological first aid to offer mental health support and enhance rights of individuals with mental illness.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The health team will conduct regular client interviews to collect feedback on their experience during the visit to the health service point. This will cover waiting time, quality of services, medications received, their perception on how well they were treated, what can be improved at the facility, among others. The IRC also generates additional feedback through the protection work in the community with the feedback shared with the health team for action. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its MEAL Team and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC protection team will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). The IRC will provide multiple channels of feedback to beneficiaries including focus group discussions (FGDs), KIIs, feedback boxes, and a hotline.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Overall, the IRC has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC has the following policies in place: Child Safeguarding Policy; Beneficiary Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy; Harassment Free Workplace Policy. All allegations and investigation information received is maintained in a confidential centralized case management system. Cases are evaluated and prioritized when received taking into account, among other things, whether the alleged conduct is ongoing or historical. The IRC will ensure that all new staff hired for IRC Sudan are trained on and understand the seriousness with which the IRC takes such issues. The IRC will also participate in the UN-led PSEA network, ensuring communities and staff understand PSEA and how to access reporting channels. The IRC also has a neutral department called the global Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, who is responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
