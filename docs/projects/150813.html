Project Summary

Medair will implement a Community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) in the targeted locations. The nutrition program will be integrated through linking the health and WASH interventions at both the health facility and the community level. Prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition including severe acute malnutrition (SAM), moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), micronutrient deficiency prevention and control will be implemented in the 10 supported primary health care facilities and their catchment villages through the community health program as well as outreach services, where it is still a necessity in 2019.
Medair will work closely with stabilization centers for severe malnutrition with complications and develop MOU for referrals. Medair has established a referral system in most communities which works very well. This includes transportation of the at-risk patients to the referral centers for treatment.
Medair’s health facility and community staff will disseminate behavior change communication at both the facility and community level. Medair’s 999 Community Volunteers will continue carrying out bi-weekly household visits in the supported villages. Behavior Change Communication will be given with an emphasis on creating awareness of the direct link between poor feeding practices (starting from exclusive breastfeeding to weaning practices) and malnutrition. Medair will ensure that prevention and management of micro-nutrient deficiencies is integrated into the CMAM interventions. This will ensure Vitamin A supplements are administered to children in line with the national protocol.
Staff at both the facility and community levels will be trained on management of acute malnutrition in order to build their capacity to be able to address acute malnutrition in a sustainable way after the program ends.
Over the years Medair has developed accountability mechanisms that reach across all stakeholders, including but not limited to actual beneficiaries. Complaints, compliments and other propositions are routinely received, reviewed and carefully addressed.
Medair has developed an M&E system that will improve and facilitate the process of data collection and analysis from health facilities, from community workers and from the Medair supervisors. Data will be disaggregated by age and sex to allow for more careful analysis of how the program engages these groups. This will help Medair to monitor its progress against project objectives, activities and indicators by utilizing streamlined data collection tools in line with methods already in place at the health facilities and communities. Community input will also be included through community meetings. At the end of the project cycle, Medair will carry out an external evaluation with a full review of achievement of indicators, implementation of activities, beneficiary feedback, lessons learned and recommendations for future projects. The purpose of the review will be to determine the outcomes of the project, and the effectiveness and the efficiency in achieving those objectives.

Needs

Somalia is coming out of a period of extended drought (4 failed rainy seasons, 1 good rainy season, with flooding along rivers, and early predictions for the Deyr 2018 rainy season quite good). and is still seeing a high number of people depending on humanitarian aid. Large scale humanitarian assistance as well as climatic improvements in 2018 improved the food security of Somalis (HRP 2019 draft). However, 1.5M Somalis still face acute food security crisis. According to the 2018 Post Gu technical release by FSNAU/FEWSNET, most of the 13 IDP settlements have been classified as crisis (IPC phase 3) between now and the end of 2018. The level of acute malnutrition is still high in several population groups due to underlying/structural causes. GAM prevalence is Critical (15-29.9%) in 12 out of 33 populations surveyed. Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence is Critical (≥4-5.6) only among Mogadishu Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) settlement, where 40% of this project is concentrated and in Guban livelihood. Crude Death Rate (CDR) is Critical (1 to <2/10 000/day) in 3 out of 33 population groups surveyed, including Mogadishu IDPs. Integrated humanitarian assistance needs to be sustained in order to maintain the recent improvements as well as prevent further deterioration of the nutrition situation. Needs are high in IDP settlements targeted by this project. There are still many malnourished children also in the rural areas targeted, where continued treatment needs to be offered accompanied by Behaviour Change Communication (BCC) to make the link from the emergency situation to building resilience in the communities. Integrated support interventions should be sustained to maintain recent improvements as well as prevent further deterioration in the nutrition situation.

Activities

CMAM will be implemented in 10 fixed nutrition sites and up to 13 mobile outreach sites, where it may still be necessary in 2019 for both OTP and TSFP programs. SC cases will be referred, unless numbers of cases in the supported facilities warrant establishment of SCs. Medair will train 30 health staff in the management of acute malnutrition. 999 CHWs will also receive refresher training on referrals, active case finding and defaulter tracing. Family approach will be piloted with some CHWs, who will train neighbour mothers how to measure MUAC. This will increase screening and will empower mothers to monitor the nutrition status of their children. Medair will work closely with WFP and UNICEF for RUTF, RUSF, and related supplies. The value of these supplies has been included in the budget. Medair will seek to purchase some buffer stocks funded by other donors to avoid stock-outs. CHWs will do home visits upon discharge from TSFP, 2 weeks post discharge, and in subsequent home visits. Caregivers will be given information on signs and symptoms that should prompt return to the clinic or nutrition site. Active and passive case finding continue as noted above. Medair supervisors will accompany them. Where necessary, Medair will support the rehabilitation of nutrition sites in the targeted health facilities.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Medair’s humanitarian interventions are aligned with the Federal Government of Somalia’s National Development Plan 2017 to improve capacity to deliver access to integrated health and nutrition services for the people of Somalia. The program strategy is aligned with the broader organisational aims to respond to the greatest humanitarian needs of communities in areas where little assistance has been provided, particularly locations that are difficult—but reasonably secure—to reach. Medair carries out integrated multi-sectoral interventions that are both lifesaving (emergency) and medium-term (recovery and early rehabilitation). Medair’s approach links relief and rehabilitation to development. Specifically, this integrated project promotes effective health promotion, and health-seeking behaviors to improve the community’s resilience to current and future public health emergencies. It also focuses on capacity building and equipping health facilities to ensure a stronger local response to outbreaks and nutritional emergencies in the future. In addition to health and nutrition services, Medair will incorporate sanitation and access to clean water activities into the humanitarian response in Banadir, Jubaland, Hirshabelle and South West States of Somalia, emphasing health and hygiene promotion. Medair will implement its programs in accordance with the DoH strategic plans, specifically the guidelines for the Essential Package of Health Services (EPHS). In areas identified for their needs and gaps, Medair supports primary health care facilities and their catchment areas alongside local partner NGOs. Medair’s strategy puts heavy emphasis on community health care, working with CHWs in Care Groups, or in rural areas, in integrated community case management (iCCM) models. Medair partners with trusted and reliable local NGOs who share a similar vision, building their capacity to ensure continuity and sustainability of current program results. Medair has developed an exit and expansion strategy linked to its middle- and long-term vision, linked to capacity building It sets indicators and minimum capacity criteria that local partners, health facilities and locations must meet in order for Medair to exit/ transition and move to other vulnerable locations. This strategy also defines partner agency selection with matrixes that are based on organizational values for accountability. For this project, a strong emphasis will be placed on developing local capacity as an exit strategy from the start. Medair aims to increase the capacity of the local authority in terms of regulation, control and setting up accountability mechanisms. Medair has endeavored to ensure sustainability of its program gains in Somalia by including the partners in all programmatic planning. One of the supported facilities (Shibis) successfully transitioned in June 2016 to the development phase after being incorporated into EPHS and handed over to another organisation.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Medair recognizes women and children are among the most vulnerable groups during conflicts. Through the implementation of this project, Medair will ensure services are accessible to both the host and the displaced populations. No person will be denied services based on gender or any other characteristic, including ethnicity. This needs to be a very intentional action for some groups of IDPs. Many of the IDPs in the areas where Medair is working in Mogadishu have come from the areas where Medair is working in Lower and Middle Shabelle. This helps in awareness of their needs. In order to assess discrimination or corruption, Medair will give beneficiaries the opportunity to evaluate services received, primarily through exit interviews at health facilities; the results will be compiled into regular Beneficiary Accountability reports.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The specific focus of the integrated activities will be on the health of women of reproductive age and children U5. Medair will promote dignity and privacy will be promoted, with facilities that are accessible to persons with disabilities, and by targeting/including men as partners in nutrition counseling for families. In consultation with local NGO partners, Somali staff and beneficiaries, Medair continues to engage men as major decision makers in Somali culture. In rural areas where women are often responsible for household chores and parenting, family finances are largely controlled by the male gender indicating decision-making power. Here, women are also tending the family’s livestock. This activity keeps them away and makes self-care and care for children very difficult. Consequently, women and children are at a greater risk for disease and malnutrition. Medair’s approach is influenced by cultural observations, such as barriers for change in feeding practices that are rooted in customs and traditions. Medair supports staffing in HF that balances male and female clinicians. Medair also focuses on training up the network of Care Group Volunteers, the majority of whom are women, selected from within and by the communities where they live and work. These women are trusted by their community members and can broach sensitive topics of conversation, such as feeding practices and other health topics as cultural insiders. These women are also able to reach influential mothers-in-law, who often can impact the decisions of fathers about health care seeking behavior and the use of family finances for health and nutrition. Care Group Volunteers also help identify households of persons with disabilities and special needs through household visits. Volunteers are trained to include these in health, nutrition, and hygiene promotion and to assess for any specific health needs. Transportation to health facilities can be arranged as necessary.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Services will be provided as close as possible to the beneficiaries, which reduces the risks to beneficiaries who may have to travel to access services. The Care Group Model and iCCM further optimize protection by bringing services to the household level in a low-key pattern that can continue to function during periods of tension. Medair will support a taxi referral network to ensure that beneficiaries with complicated cases safely reach the appropriate level of care, if possible because of insecurity. Medair recognises women and children are among the most vulnerable groups during conflicts. Through the implementation of this project, Medair will ensure services are accessible to both the host and the displaced populations. The Community networks will provide assistance to women in the community through dissemination of behavior change communication. Medair has incorporated a mental health component to help the targeted populations deal with trauma following a crisis.
