Project Summary

The proposed interventions are for cholera preparedness and response in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States. SI is the current lead for the Cholera Task Force under the WASH sector. The Task Force preparedness activities will be included as part of preparations for the 2020 cholera season, as well as SI specific preparedness and response activities. SI specific preperedness activities are as follows :
- Chlorination of water points and water quality monitoring at source and point of use for free residual chlorine & turbidity. This activity will typically be implemented between August and December 2020.
- Communication and mass awareness raising campaigns, including production and dissemination of IEC materials. Household and community level hygiene promotion with clear cholera messages
- Desludging of latrines prior to the rainy season in SI areas of operation
- Prepositioning of contingency WASH supplies as a preparedness measure (IEC materials, chlorine, consumable hygiene kits and cholera kits) to be able to immediately respond in situations when cholera cases are being detected.
- Environmental clean-up campaigns
-
In terms of response, SI is proposing to conduct the following activities, through its emergency team, in a 72h windows following a case or alert:
- Household disinfection of suspected cholera cases and their radius of 30m
- Distribution of consumable Cholera kits for households with suspected cases and immediate surrounding households
-
The Cholera Task Force led by SI will carry out the following activities for the three states in Northern Nigeria as part of its preparedness plan:
- Mapping of all partners, donors and areas of intervention per LGA, ward and community
- Mapping of partners’ funding capacity
- Compilation of available contingency stock for the common pipeline
- Development and Update reporting and monitoring tools
- Training of partners on WASH SOPs for cholera intervention, including water chlorination, household disinfection and hygiene promotion
- Preparation and dissemination of dashboard

1 - Needs

The Security situation in the North East region has caused massive internal and secondary displacements, destruction of personal property, congestion in camps and host communities resulting in competition over limited resources. Overally, 7.1 million people are in dire need of assistance across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states (HRP 2019-2021). The Displacements Tracking Matrix (DTM) round 27 conducted from 25 March to 29 May 2019, related to WASH services in camp and camp-like settings and host communities (HC) in Borno state indicated that in camp and camp like settings: the average amount of water available per person per day (/p/d) was 10 to 15 liters (L) in 58% of sampled sites, more than 15L/p for 31% and 5 to 10 L/p for 12% of the sites. On another hand, 95% of the toilets are ‘not hygienic’, gender segregated toilets were available in 34% of sites and handwashing stations were found in 16% of sites. In host communities, 50% of the sites had 10 to 15L/p/d of water; 15L/p/d for 34% and 5 to 10 L/p/d of water was available in 14% of the sites. 97% of the sites had ‘not hygienic’ toilets, 6% had gender segregated toilets and 8% had handwashing facilities but nearly none of them had soap. These conditions predisposes the communities to water borne diseases and have made cholera endemic in BAY states.Flooding, strong winds and sandstorms have resulted in substantial damage of WASH facilities, and congested populations in displacement sites have led to an environment highly vulnerable to waterborne diseases. Cholera outbreaks have struck each year; in 2018 it affected most of Borno State and 5.712 suspected cases and 73 deaths were reported. In 2019, a number of cholera cases were reported from Adamawa state, and a high number of AWD cases in Borno State were reported, although a cholera outbreak was not declared. As at 8 November, a total of 818 cases, with 4 deaths, were reported from the cholera outbreak in Yola North, Yola South and Girei. At the time of writing this proposal, the outbreak is yet to be contained. The report released on flooding by OCHA Nigeria recently indicates that a total of over 21,000 households have been affected by the torrential rains. These rains have destroyed the WASH facilities and which further exacerbates a high likelihood of cholera epidemics. This strongly justifies the need for strong cholera preparedness and response activites.Solidarites International leads the Cholera Task Force which coordinates cholera preparedness and response in BAY states. The need for continued support to partners is required in terms of targeting hot spots, partner mapping, compilation of available contigency stock, training on SOPs and reporting of partner activities. This is in line with the WASH sector priorities for 2020 of partner rationalization, common pipeline approach development, cholera preparedness and LGA strengthening. This proposal will include activities that strengthen partner and sector preparedness for cholera response in order to mitigate the impact of cholera in the BAY states in 2020

2 - Activities

Cholera activities will be conducted in BAY States through support of review of the current cholera outbreak and development of contingency plans for 2020, implementation of preparedness and response activities and continued leading of the Cholera Task Force. SI’s areas of operation and those will be considered for outbreak response through its dedicated emergency team. Preparedness activities will include: - Chlorination of 100 water points and water quality monitoring at source and point of use of free residual chlorine & turbidity. The activity will typically be implemented from August to December 2020. - Communication and mass awareness raising campaigns, including production and dissemination of IEC materials with clear cholera messages as well as Household and community level hygiene promotion sessions - Desludging of 1500 latrines prior to the rainy season in SI areas of operation. Male and female latrines will be targeted to improve access to sanitation for both sexes.- Prepositioning of contingency WASH supplies as preparedness measure (IEC materials, chlorine, consumable hygiene kits and cholera kits) to be able to immediately respond in situations when cholera cases are being detected;- Environmental clean up campaigns every first day of the month from August to December in the targeted areas.SI response capacity will include: - Distribution of 5000 Cholera kits (if needed) for households with suspected cases and immediate surrounding households- Household disinfection of 1500 suspected cholera cases and their radius of 30mThe Cholera Task Force led by SI will carry out the following activities for the three states in Northern Nigeria:- Risk analysis, mapping and development of contingency plans- Mapping of all partners, donors and areas of intervention per LGA, ward and community - Mapping of partners’ funding capacity- Compilation of available contingency stock - Update reporting and monitoring tools through improving the kobo tool and dashboard which SI assisted the WASH Sector to develop in 2019. TUFT University will also help SI to develop a monitoring system for partner activities at outcome level- Training of partners on WASH SOPs for cholera interventions, including water chlorination, household disinfection and hygiene promotion. 3 trainings will be conducted in the 3 states prior to the onset of the season

Solidarites strives to include all stakeholders as much as possible in its intervention. Joint planning and review meetings will be held between the project staff and relevant stakeholders involved in the project from the camp and community level to capture lessons learnt for better programming.Solidarites will also involve communities via staff hiring (Daily Workers, CHPs), so that they can benefit from the project interventions, not only as beneficiaries but also actively supporting implementation of activity. Solidarites will mainstream Core Humanitarian Standards to ensure quality and effectiveness of assistance and demonstrate greater accountability to affected populations. Throughout the project, SI will strive to respect the five Accountability to Affected Population commitments, as defined by the IASC. The project beneficiaries are systematically included in the decision making process at each and every step of project cycle. SI is committed to ensuring beneficiaries’ involvement to ensure the project fits their expectations and needs and to avoid negative side effects. The methodology followed is inspired by URD guidebook for participation. SI will ensure the respect of the Do No Harm principle through analyzing the possible unintended effect of each step of the project implementation to better mitigate them (i.e., beneficiary selection criteria etc.) and ensure that distributions do not put beneficiaries at risk during or after the distribution of inputs, through the presence of MEAL team observers during all distributions and PDMs. In addition, SI will ensure that its existing Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), managed by the MEAL department, will be accessible to all beneficiaries under this project, and that affected communities have been properly sensitized on how to access and use the CRM.
