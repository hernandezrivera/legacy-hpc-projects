Project Summary

ACF’s WASH project for 2019 is in line with the WASH cluster strategy 2019 for the HRP, aiming at improving and sustain the quality of the WASH services to the targeted population groups. The main objective of the project is to improve the well-being of out of camp displaced, returnee and host community populations by ensuring access to well managed WASH services, well maintained WASH infrastructure at health and education facilities, and improved community level wastewater and solid waste management. ACF will also work to support and build the capacities of local NGO’s. Within the 2019 HRP there is a drive to keep an emergency response capacity, but to focus more on sustaining and/or improving WASH services of all kinds when possible after immediate needs have been served. During 2019 ACF aims to keep a focus on out-of-camp restoring basic WASH services for highly vulnerable population in areas of return, displacement, high risk and in other shocks areas s (cluster objective 2 and 3), and developing the linkage of recovery activities to support a more durable, sustainable and cost effective public service (cluster objectives 4). This will be achieved by capitalizing on ACF’s ongoing presence in Ninewa and Dohuk, and focusing on the added value of technically sound and well documented interventions, and by developing linkages with other core sectors such as MHPSS and livelihoods. During 2019, ACF will strengthen its current WASH intervention at schools and health facilities level, mainstreaming gender for the provided services. ACF WASH intervention will target Ninewa, Dohuk, Anbar and Salah Al-Adin. Currently ACF has presence in the governorates of Ninewa and Dohuk and in 2019 new areas of intervention have been considered including Anbar and Salah Al-Adin, where needs are high.
The intervention in 2019 is expected to target 123,000 individual of population in needs among Returnees, Host Community and IDPs in the mentioned governorates. According to IOM DTM as of 31 October 2018, the total number of returnees was at 4,113,660 across 1,568 locations of return. 1,866,594 IDPs remain in displacement. Ninewa (1,591,734 returnees) remains the highest governorate witnessing returns in October, followed by Anbar (1,283,292) and Salah al-Din (575,736). About 50% of IDPs report lack of basic services, including water and waste removal, as a reason they are not returning to their homes, and 20% or more of communities in the targeted areas report significant water access issues.

Needs

In 2018, the Iraq crisis enters in its fifth year. Over 31,000 houses have been destroyed due to ISIS conflict, in addition to hundreds of public and commercial facilities and infrastructure projects. The World Bank estimates that reconstruction could take at least ten years and will cost upwards of $80 USD. Almost half of the population requiring assistance reside in Ninewa. Returns, displacement, and secondary displacement across Iraq remains complicated with some families unable to return home – due to security factors, lack of basic services (water access, sanitation infrastructures, livelihood opportunities, destruction of houses, etc.). Other families, with perceived affiliations with IS will remain in the camps with no possibility to return to their areas of origin. According to IOM DTM as of 31 October 2018, the total number of returnees was at 4,113,660 across 1,568 locations of return. 1,866,594 IDPs remain in displacement. Ninewa (1,591,734 returnees) remains the highest governorate witnessing returns in October, followed by Anbar (1,283,292) and Salah al-Din (575,736). About 50% of IDPs report lack of basic services, including water and waste removal, as a reason they are not returning to their homes.Throughout 2018, ACF has conducted a number of assessments in Ninewa and Dohuk to identify the WaSH needs, held discussions with the Directorates of Water, Sanitation, and other relevant authorities. ACF has also reviewed the shared assessments and reports of other organizations, including REACH Initiative World Bank and WaSH Cluster, for Ninewa, Salah Al-Adin, Dohuk and Anbar. The findings include: 1. The health facilities have staff available, and are being utilised regularly by the catchment community despite being severely damaged and even having no beds available.2. The health facilities have severe hygiene issues present in the form of damaged latrines, including few are gender segregated or with access for the less abled and the elderly, many cases of diarrhea coming in for treatment, and very limited reported access to hygiene items. 3. Schools are reopening but their WaSH infrastructure is badly damaged, and some do not have gender-segregated toilets, nor do they have places where females can clean themselves during menstruation.4. The water networks, extraction and distribution systems, and treatment plants are severely damaged and despite ongoing repairs will still need repair and rehabilitation work in 2019. . In Ninewa and Salah Al-Adin 20% of assessed communities reported significant water supply issues, while it was 40% in Anbar. 5. A Ninewa water quality study found 90% of samples had insufficient residual chlorine, and 30% of the remaining 10% had bacteriological contamination.6. The drainage networks are severely damaged, causing flooding and providing breeding areas for disease vectors and polluting the environment. Work has started on the drainage networks in 2018 but more work is needed in 2019.7. Solid Waste collection system is severely disrupted and is only slowly restarting. The municipalities and communities need support to restart the collection systems that existed before the conflict.8. Household showers and toilets need repair and women have indicated safety concerns about using family latrines. Also ACF found that there are access issues for elderly and the disabled using latrines.9. REACH Intention surveys have indicated that the lack of WaSH infrastructure in the areas of return is one of the primary issues preventing people from returning home.Under these conditions there can be outbreaks of infection due to the improper hygiene conditions, the majority of people are already stressed and thus have lower immunity to infection. ACF is seeking to improve the hygiene conditions (including water, solid waste, toilets) in areas of high risk of infection such as PHCs and schools as well as rehabilitating the basic WaSH infrastructure in Ninewa, Salah Al-din, and Anbar.Emergency responseDuring 2018, there were not any rapid onset needs as the larger scale military interventions decreased, but in 2019 there will continue to be the threats due to circumstances such as increased insecurity in some areas due to ISIS infiltration, forced returns, sectarian violence, or disease outbreak.. Areas where activities are planned are in Ninewa, Salah Al-din, and Anbar.Based on these needs, ACF's 2019 WASH activities in Iraq will support the IDPs, returnees and host communities in areas outside of camps.. When appropriate ACF will provide a full WaSH package, depending on the assessed needs, location specificities and access; whenever possible the WASH activities will be part of an integrated intervention provided by ACF including MHCP, Nutrition, and FSL. The core aim is to address acute needs, alleviate suffering and promote dignity and sustainability.

Protection Risk Analysis

Specific protection risks identified by ACF include: 1) Security and political environment in the targeted areas deteriorates. The security threats includes possible ISIS attacks, and possible violence due to tensions between tribal and ethnic groups. While the political issues include tensions between Kurdish Region of Iraq and Federal Iraq, the militia groups running the roadblocks and the local communities and humanitarian agencies. There is also uncertainty due to the newly install Government in Federal Iraq, as it is new and thus unproven and there are tensions between the Ministries and the Governors. 2) Reduced access to targeted population At times due to security or political issues, ACF has found itself unable to access its project sites. When there are access issues ACF coordinates with UN OCHA.There are multiple levels of lack of access:• Foreign staff cannot access the project sites, but national workers can.• Foreign and non-local national staff cannot access the project site but local staff can.• No staff has access to the project site. 3) Unexploded ordinance and mines in the targeted areas 4) Inter group tensions can occur that could have the potential for violence (IDP/refugees/host community), along with tension between ACF & communities/beneficiaries due to targeting issues. 5) Major outbreak of epidemic disease or a natural or manmade disaster occurs. This can affect the wash intervention and communities in many ways. If the disease is a waterborne disease, then the response to it must include WaSH interventions, if the outbreak is non-waterborne there is still often a WaSH component to the response. Manmade or natural disasters can damage WaSH infrastructure, make implementation sites inaccessible or displace populations. The displaced populations often need WaSH support in their new locations. All of these will require modification of the planned WaSH intervention.

Mitigation Measures

1) ACF has a Safety and Security Advisor based in Iraq to maintain abreast of all new safety and security issues as they represent themselves as well as maintaining awareness for consisting safety / security concerns. ACF’s Safety and Security Advisor is a member of the coordination team and is consulted when considering new locations / areas for projects. ACF constantly monitors the security and political environment by maintaining contacts with UN agencies and other partners (INSO, local authorities). ACF frequently revises/updates its operational security plan. If the conditions warrant, ACF may temporarily stop implementation in areas, or change areas or methodology of implementation. 2) ACF ensures access through regular and effective communication and cooperation with authorities at Governorate and community level and with other relevant actors. ACF reports access issues to UNOCHA. If needed, ACF will try to implement remote implementation through local staff, etc, in order to continue to serve the beneficiaries. 3) ACF works with UNMAS and the mine/UXO clearance agencies to train ACF and contractor staff and to ensure that work areas are cleared of mines and UXOs. When a UXO or mines is found. ACF will report it and request it be cleared. If it is in an area where a work is being implemented work will be stopped until the danger is removed. 4) ACF will integrate the recommendations developed on “conflict sensitivity approach into Project Cycle Management”. ACF conflict sensitivity lenses used in the country include meeting with community leaders and local officials to discuss and coordinate projects. ACF will have well-designed eligibility criteria and targeting based on context, community inputs, evidence and objectives of transfers; may need to modify targeting to ensure inclusion of different groups, host community, women, etc. ACF clearly explains its rationale on operational strategy to the community, local leaders and government authorities. ACF establishes and explains a Complaint and Feedback mechanism for the beneficiaries. Post distribution monitoring to include questions on social relations and “Do No Harm”. ACF ensures transparency on criteria and clear implementation guidelines. 5) Major outbreak of epidemic disease occurs or a natural or manmade disaster occursACF will respond to major population displacement, disease outbreak or natural or manmade disaster in its area of operation by modifying its work to meet the new conditions and new needs.

How is your project accountable to affected population

For this project, ACF will have feedbacks and complaints mechanism system for each sector and activities, part of the MEAL plan. In addition to establishing a formal feedback and complaints mechanisms, the collection of feedback and complaint will also be incorporated into regular monitoring activities during implementation. For instance, routine on-site monitoring conducted by project and MEAL team will be an opportunity to ask beneficiaries questions to solicit feedback.Direct interview, formal surveys (such as post distribution monitoring, satisfaction survey) and informal ones (fields visit) will also allow the ACF team to get feedbacks from the population we aim to help and evaluate not only their satisfaction regarding to ACF services but also relevancy and quality of our actions. Feedback obtained through these mechanisms will be included in analysis and, when possible, will be used to inform programmatic decision-making and adjust the intervention. Analyzed feedback will be reported to relevant stakeholders and managers and lessons learned will feed into decision making, readjustment of project plans when possible, and as well as, future project planning.During the implementation, ACF will also directly involve its beneficiaries in the decisions related to them: - Households and individuals will give their informed consent to benefit from the project’s activities, including ACF’s direct services and referrals to other organizations. - ACF’s beneficiaries will always have the right to refuse the offered services. - ACF’s beneficiaries will have opportunities to provide feedback on the services, in particular through MEAL survey ACF aims at building the capacity of the community. Therefore, ACF will also involve community through the identification of areas and certain types of activities. Moreover, ACF will work in a close collaboration with the different authorities concerned which will be also a mean to ensure the sustainability of the project. In this regard, liaison with relevant governmental departments for field assessments (joint where possible) and written authorizations will be ensured.When conducting formal and informal interviews with beneficiaries, ethical consideration such as confidentiality is always an important consideration. Therefore, ACF teams will constantly reassuring respondents who are consulted or who voluntarily gave feedback, that the information they are giving will remain confidential and will be kept within ACF. The consent of the beneficiaries will be asked prior to each interview; none beneficiaries will be forced to answer the questions. In case of any critical or sensitive protection issues that may be raised, ACF team will report the sensitive case to the identified senior management team.
