Project Summary

ADA -HRNP 2024 programming seek to address the needs of the highly vulnerable populations affected by the prolonged crises in Unity, Upper Nile and Jongei States. ADA will provide timely life-saving and resilience-strengthening assistance to returnees, protracted and newly displaced IDPs, and residents through multi-sectoral Cash base and emergency shelter and non-food items interventions to holistically restore community dignity. 49% of this response will be channeled to protracted and newly displaced IDPs, 26% returnees and 25% most affected residents. The main focus and the target of this programing shall be women and children, elderly 60+, female headed household, single parents, child headed household and separated children, pregnant or lactating mothers, persons without community links and those who are living with severe sickness. ADA HRNP 2024 programing is anticipating to directly reach a total number of 14,500 HHs, approximately 87,000 individuals in Upper Nile, Jonglei and Unity States. ADA will target 3,000 HHs, 18,000 individuals in Renk for a period of 6 months, 1,500 HHs 9,000 individuals in Malakal and 1,000 HHs 6,000 individuals in Meluth, of Upper Nile State. ADA will also target 2,500 HHs 15,000 individuals in New and Old Fangak, 1,000 HHs 6,000 individuals in Pigi canal, 1,500 HHs 9,000 individuals in Nyirol, 900 HHs 5,400 in Ayod of Jonglei State, 1,500 HHs 9,000 individuals in Rubkona, 900 HHs 5,400 individuals in Mayendit, 700 HHs 4,200 individuals in Mayom of Unity State. The above mentioned counties are expected to be IPC 4 and IPC 5. ADA is already a static partner in the mentioned counties, it will be collaborating with other mobile partners in the same counties. Partners will include IOM on CCM responses, NRC protection, PAH for WASH, and MedAir for multi-sector interventions. In all cases, ADA will endeavor to provide interventions that would contribute to the cluster strategies and programming for the most vulnerable communities. Where a single-sector approach will be used, ADA will ensure integrating it with protection mainstreaming, Do No Harm principle will be the guiding principle in ADA activities, land housing, and property rights. ADA will ensure an inclusive engagement with all key stakeholders in the target areas and in all the programing. ADA will keenly adopt the cluster objective, of ensuring provision of timely and adequate access to safe and dignified emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced people or populations with new vulnerabilities. Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and the host communities who are unable to meet their SNFI needs. Support the most vulnerable returnees, residents and IDPs access adequate durable housing options that will enable them self-recover and build resilience of affected population to recover from shocks. ADA in all her integrated responses will, aim at protection mainstreaming including the establishment of community social networks, conducting protection training to project committees to implement, and conducting mine risk awareness sessions through the committees to ensure the safety of the communities. ADA is having an active Cash base funding from CAFORD that aims to cover the needs of 3,000 individuals in Renk, and this funding will continue up February 2024. That amount to 15% of this HNRP and will contribute to and boost the resilience and improving the living conditions of the 87,000 affected populations through improved housing solutions. ADA, through a mobile and static approaches, will coordinate responses with the Cluster coordinator and SFPs and monitor both the single and multi-sector services through its static and roving teams to deep field locations in all its project areas.
The project will be implemented over a period of 6 and 12 months in all the 10 counties of Upper Nile, Jonglei and Unity States.

1. Needs

South Sudan is highly prone to both natural and manmade disasters, continuous flooding in some part of the country and drought in other areas had negatively affect agriculture, disrupt livelihoods and blocked humanitarian access. Conflict, subnational and revenge killings among the neighboring communities, plus the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan and Ethiopia are disrupting livelihoods and forcing the displacement of people. Low agriculture production resulting in high humanitarian needs in South Sudan. According to South Sudan needs overview, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that 9.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023, at least 76% of South Sudan's population. More than 7 million people are expected to experience severe food insecurity and other displacements in 2024. At least 148,000 returnees from abroad (mostly from Sudan and Ethiopia) remained unable to reach their homes. Partners in Rotriak (Rubkona), Akobo, Fangak, Malakal, Meluth and Renk have been receiving more returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia. Most of the returnees stacked in Renk or Roriak and other entry points, looking for means to reach their final destination. According to IRNA assessment conducted by partners in different locations shows that more returnees have arrived in the country, and their needs were not much attended to by the partners due to lack of funding. Women and children are mostly affected compared to other groups. The returnee’s children lacking schools as they returned. Many children are malnourished since the parents couldn’t provide enough food for their children. The data gathered from several sources i.e. REACH surveys, OCHA IPC acute malnutrition snapshots March 2023-July 2024 and IRNA, conducted by partners on ground confirms that there are significant needs within the internal displaced communities and returnees in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity States. In 2024 HNRP, ADA is seeking to provide humanitarian support to 10 counties: Renk, Malakal and Meluth of Upper Nile state, Fangak, Nyirol, Pigi Canal and Ayod of Jonglei State. Rubkona, Mayom and Mayendit of Unity State. The three states might slide into IPC 4 and 5 level according to South Sudan IPC acute food insecurity snapshot, all the above mentioned counties are expected to face IPC 4 level in the map. The IDPs, returnees and the residents have vast needs that include shelter/NFI, WASH, Education, Protection, GBV, FSL, Health, and Nutrition. ADA in the HNRP 2024 program will provide ES/NFI and Multi-Purpose cash assistance for the people in need to prepare for the continuous flooding and can carter for their home needs strengthen their resilient in the 5 years to come. This cash will prevent both the IDPs and the residents from opting to negative copying mechanism. The ES/NFI will reduce over sharing of limited resources (an average of 5 families living in the same shelter), some are selling alcohol and others harmful things. ADA has the experience in the recent responses in Renk, Malakal, New and Old Fangak, Pigi Canal and Panyikang Ayod and Nyirol. ADA through RRF funding responded to some of the ES/NFI needs in New Fangak that covered needs of 1,000 HHs, however, it has not covered the need of all the people in needs. ADA will continue to provide life-saving cash assistance, in the areas where market is functional, especially in Malakal, Renk, pigi Canal, Nyirol, Fangak, Mayendit and Mayom to cover the various needs of the IDPs, both protracted and newly displaced IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable residents affected by disasters and conflicts.

2. Project activities

ES/NFI: 1) ADA will mobilize and sensitize the affected community through appropriate channels, early warning, awareness sessions on disaster/flood risk reduction will be conducted. Family will be train on how to create strong dike, and built semi concrete homes. Local authorities, women, church leaders, chiefs and persons PSN will be engaged & consulted on the needs, and be used as focal and entry points for the project activities. 2) Needs analysis of 87,500 beneficiaries will be conducted to determine the vulnerability of the populations and select project beneficiaries. ADA will use household questionnaire, FGD, KII and observation. 3) Registration of most vulnerable 87,000 beneficiaries will be done. ADA will consult with local authorities, and other stake holders. The project will target 49% of IDPs; 25% of returnees and 26% vulnerable. 60% will be female and 40% male. Household-to-household verification will be used to select the most vulnerable community. Tokens will be issued to facilitate easy identification during distribution. 4) Distribution of SNFI will be conducted, safe distribution points will be identified at all project areas. ADA will consult its protection team and partners to conduct safety audit, to ensure safety of the affected persons. Community mobilizers will inform the beneficiaries about the distribution dates, venue and time. distribution will take place at an appropriate point not more than 30 minutes’ walk to mitigate protection incidences during return home. 5) ADA will conduct PDM three weeks after the distribution to evaluate the project impact and feedbacks. Enumerators, will be identified, trained and deployed to collect data. A random sampling method will be used to pick respondents to ensure each beneficiary has an equal chance of being selected to provide their opinions. MPCA: 6) Induction meetings will be conducted to explain the project objectives/purpose, activities, target, locations, selection criteria & roles of key stakeholders in the project locations. 7) Key messaging and Baseline survey will be conducted as mentioned above, baseline survey will be conducted to understand the needs and the survival basket of the beneficiaries. ADA team will collect data for this specific activity. 8) Market assessment will be conducted in all the target locations; enumerators will be hire to collect data for a period of 7 days in each target location. 9) Registration of the beneficiaries shall be conducted in each location, the most vulnerable people shall be registered and shall receive cash tokens for easy recognition. 10) Cash distribution shall be conducted after the registration. 8 enumerators shall be hire to call out the names of the beneficiaries, collect data for rapid assessment, and controlled the desks for sump prints, complain and feedback desks, and shall work with the team to make sure the distribution process is successful. 20 casual’s laborer's shall be selected to help in both crowd controlling, water delivery to the beneficiaries and the staff during the registration and the cash distribution. Activities 11) PDM shall be conducted in all the project locations, ADA team shall employ the same procedures as mentioned above. ADA will target 18,000 individuals’ returnees and residents in Renk for a period of 6 months through provision of NFIs and cash vouchers to address multiple home needs. ADA will conduct construction of communal shelters, rehabilitation of Shelters, provision of cash and hiring of boats to facilitate the transportation of the returnees to reach their final destination. Shelter upgrades will be conducted for the IDPs, residents and the returnees in their secondary displacement in Renk.
