Project Summary

This project is targeting Returnees ,conflict affected host community and IDPS living in Yei county in Central Equitoria , Yirol East and Yirol West of Lakes State and Duk county of Jonglei State who are at high risk of gender based violence, death, injury and life threatening diseases like HIV/AIDS, STIs and reproductive health related illnesses aggravated due to violence. The primary purpose of the project is to address compounding effects of converging factors i.e severe food insecurity /loss of livelihoods, economic decline, protection threats and lack of services which contributes and aggravates the prevalence of GBV . CIDO will adopt an integrated approach and coordinate with WASH, Health, MHPSS, FSL, Education and Nutrition partners to prevent protection risks and to provide timely GBV response services to men, women, boys and girls affected by conflict. As such CIDO will use its leadership role to prioritize joint safety audits, capacity building sessions and GBV mainstreaming activities with multi sector partners CIDO will also ensure that the project staff adhere to the AAP principles as well as PSEA principles while delivering the activities at the project sites. The target population is 12000 individuals broken down as 500 boys and 3000 girls, 8000 women 500 men. The project has a special focus on adolescent girls and boys, female headed households and people living with disability The project will be implemented for a period of 12 months with an estimated budget of USD 360,000. CIDO will implement this project directly in the proposed locations in collaboration with the target population, the administration in counties and partners on ground to promote durable solutions, sustainability and achieve maximum project results. The project activities are designed in line with the four GBV Sub cluster HRP 2020 cluster objectives and the activities focus on mitigating GBV risks, preventing and responding to GBV cases through; WGFS programming, Case management / Individual PSS, community Outreach and dialogues, EMAP and safe referrals. It is expected that by the end of the project period, lives of vulnerable women and girls will be saved, they will have increased access to multi sector services , life skills and positive coping mechanisms entrenched in them. It is anticipated that through EMAP boys and men will be better informed on GBV prevention mechanisms in emergencies and will make meaningful sustainable contribution to the prevention of GBV and overall population needs of safer dignified lives.

1 - Needs

A convergence of factors including severe food insecurity, economic decline, armed conflict, returnee influx and lack of services has contributed to increased GBV cases with IPV recording highest in 2018. RRC figures of september 2019 estimates that a total of 8,000 returnees have arrived in Yei since March 2019 with final destinations in payams of Yei like Tore, Mugwo, Otogo and urban Yei. IOM /DTM report of October 2019 indicates that there 28,924 returnees currently living in Duk County. According to REACH Assessment May 2019, FGD participants reported a small-scale migration from Yirol West to Yirol Town, Aluak-Luak and Mingkaman between January 2019 and April 2019.The population have limited access to GBV prevention and response services . Many of the returnee men boys , girls and women suffer emotional distress of coping with life amidst existing social factors like loss of land and property and often have to depend on host community for casual jobs for survival. There is need to engage both male and female community leaders to put in place GBV risk mitigation strategies.South Sudan’s localized famine and continued scope of severe food insecurity demonstrate the compounding impact of multiple needs, including food security, nutrition, and health and WASH which affect men, boys, women and girls differently. Women and girls are traditionally required to provide food for their families yet men dominate and make decisions in regards to their farm income. Food insecurity increases the risks of SEA and transactional sex among men and women. According to IPC Analysis report released in September 2019 ,Yirol East of former Lakes State has an estimated 10,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). This is critical considering the fact that Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) classification is when a household group has an extreme lack of food and/or other basic needs even with the full employment of coping strategies. As a result there is usually an increase in stress levels and Intimate partner violence affecting bread winners who are mainly women and girls. Same scenario is in Duk county where CIDO is delivering an Emergency Shelter and NFI activities. In addition 1200 HH have been affected by the current floods in Duk county ( IRNA assessment , October 2019) with evident loss of livelihoods by men and women. There is need for a coordinated and integrated approach with Nutrition ,FSL partners and Mental health partners to provide specialized PSS services and access to livelihood options for women and girls so as to strengthen resilience and positive coping mechanisms. There is need to coordinate with WASH partners to ensure that women and girls are not exposed to GBV risks and have access to safe and clean water. CIDO is currently delivering a Health project in Yirol West and will coordinate with other health partners to build the capacity of health workers in CMR services Apart from the problem of patriarchy which contributes to social injustices affecting women and girls , Cattle raiding closely linked to early and forced marriage remains the main protection concern for both men and women . Reach Assessment report of March 2019 indicates that Yirol East County saw an increase from 8% in January to 37% in March in the proportion of settlements reporting cattle raiding as the main protection concern for women who are often raped during cattle raiding and inter-communal clashes. There is need to liaise with partners to ensure girls, women boys and men access GBV prevention messaging, CMR and psychosocial services within the GBV minimum package per IASC guidelines. There is need to enhance coordination with local authority and Multi sectoral partners to strengthen GBV risk mitigation strategies , prevention and response activities for both adult and child survivors .

2 - Activities

1. Conduct safety audits and protection risk analysis with multi sector partners ( WASH, Health, Nutrition, FSL Education ) 2. Conduct training of community/frontline workers on comprehensive case management 3. Conduct training of community/frontline workers on PSS/ PFA and community Based PSS4. Update referral pathways and sensitize the community on its use.5. Conduct comprehensive case management and safe referrals targeting women, men, boys and girls in collaboration and coordination with child protection partners.6. Mobilize community based protection committees and target women, men, girls and boys with individual, group, PSS services and referrals for specialized PSS 7. Strengthen/Equip one WGFS in Yei and establish 3 new WGFS targeting Yirol East, Yirol West and Duk counties 8. Conduct referrals of women, men, girls and boys supported to legal aid and legal counselling services in close coordination with general protection partners including law society in Yei.9. Conduct awareness campaigns targeting women, men, girls and boys on GBV prevention and risk mitigation strategies including PSEA in coordination with FSL and WASH partners10. Conduct awareness of boys , male youth and adult men on EMAP through FGDs.11. Distribute dignity kits and hygiene kits targeting women of reproductive age and adolescent girls in collaboration with menstrual health and hygiene partners and WASH partners.12. Conduct training of community mobilisers and sectoral staff on GBV risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning ( WASH, FSl, Health, Education and Nutrition)13. Conduct training of women and girls on how to make Fuel efficient stoves and use of alternative fuel in order to mitigate GBV risks associated with firewood collection.14. Conduct assessment on available livelihood options and needs of women and girls and provide training on various IGA activities at WGFS. CIDO will coordinate the activity with FSL partners and conduct appropriate referrals to livelihood opportunities like village savings and loans and seed/farm input distributions.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with national objectives - such as the national Strategy of the ministry of Gender , child and social welfare, Strategic national action plan for ending child Marriage and National action plan (NAP ) on women peace and security

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the project environment is sufficiently safe and stable. CIDO has carried out protection assessments and risk analysis including safety audits and has presence in all the locations targeted. CIDO is implementing a health project in Yirol, S/NFI project in Duk and GBV project in Yei. Being a national partner CIDO is keen to promote local but durable solutions and the benchmark lies in protection issues. CIDO will promote the use of existing community structures to mitigate GBV risks and at the same time prioritize coordinated and integrated approach for delivering humanitarian services.CIDO's project staff have been trained on PSEA and have signed on codes of conduct stipulated by CIDO’s PSEA policy and are committed to abide by the six PSEA principles. The trained staff will address issues around prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) at project sites . Communities will be made aware of the complaint and feedback mechanisms in place and channels for reporting SEA.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes the project includes sustainability/handover in the plan. CIDO is a community based entity and works with local community structures to enhance community ownership of the project. Local community structures like CBPNs, Teen clubs and women support groups form a key part of the stakeholders in order to provide a mechanism of continuity at the close of the project.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CIDO has designed the project to be participatory so that not only leaders actively engage in the intervention but also men, boys, girls and women at community level so as to ensure that the targte population needs are met and durable solutions given priority. The target population will be involved in the project needs assessments, implementation, and monitoring .CIDO has standard feedback mechanism procedures that are entailed in our response across South Sudan. CIDO staff will periodically interact with beneficiaries to obtain views on project implementation, and suggestions for better service delivery. Feedback pathways will be clearly stipulated to beneficiaries in local dialects that can easily be understood by the communities. The project will design feedback forms, setup suggestion boxes, organise focus group discussions, setup call/sms lines , help desks,dedicated to feedback. Community perception on project deliverables will be sought through local community leaders and representatives of different groups including women, boys, girls, people with special needs and the elderly. Lessons learnt will be compiled and feedback into the project to improve service delivery.
