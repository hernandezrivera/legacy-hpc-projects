Project Summary

The overall aim is to reduce mortality, morbidity and undernutrition by providing life-saving nutrition, health and WaSH services in health facilities and communities.
1. Affected populations have improved access to quality primary healthcare services in the targeted areas for reduction of morbidity and mortality
2. Affected populations have access to quality nutrition treatment and preventive services through strengthening the capacity of health facilities
The program will be focusing on; health system for provision of primary health care services that includes basic management and prevention of communicable for all age groups from children to adults, antenatal and postnatal care services, support to uncomplicated vaginal delivery services and minor perinatal complications, support to referral services. It will also focus on support to Routine Childhood Immunization in collaboration with SMOH and WHO, support to provision of clinical management of minor physical injury, and prepositioning for Cholera and Measles outbreak in the targeted LGAs and finally provision of nutrition services. Providing medical equipment, supplies and pharmaceuticals and nutritional supplies to the health facilities through the award and in coordination with SMOH and LGA authorities. Reinforcement of technical capacity through sector/sub-sector specific technical trainings for quality service delivery. Building institutional and community capacity for promotion and provision of appropriate IYCF-E integrated with priority health and hygiene practices.
Target:
1. Children: 0-59 months of age for malnutrition treatment and other children for provision of primary health services.
2. Women: key target of the project, both in its direct activities (e.g. Care Groups) and preventive and curative health services, as well as with the women empowerment agenda applied across the project.
3. Men: men in the targeted communities will be provided with primary health care services for the management of non-communicable diseases (NCD) and communicable diseases.

1 - Needs

Nigeria’s Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states in the Northeast have been facing one of the most severe humanitarian crises in the world for over a decade, characterized by insecurity, large-scale population displacement, chronic food shortages and the collapse of basic social services (Humanitarian Response Plan-HRP, March 2021). The limited capacity to effectively respond to sudden onset emergencies due to escalating insecurity has resulted in disruptions of basic services. The crisis has exacerbated pre-existing multiple interrelated factors: high prevalence of water borne and preventable childhood diseases (diarrhea, acute respiratory infections and malaria), lack/limited access to quality nutrition and health services, poor health infrastructure, poor hygiene, and sanitation; poor maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) and care practices; sub-optimal hygiene conditions and seasonal food insecurity and reduced agricultural production, high levels of unemployment and poverty. The latter is characterized by food poverty, whereas communities lack knowledge of nutritionally balanced diets and food processing methods to preserve the nutritional content of food. North-East Nigeria remains highly endemic for diarrheal diseases due to seasonal patterns, lack of access to potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and vulnerabilities as a result of continuous displacement and lower access to essential health care. New influxes of IDPs, refugees and returnees into overcrowded and under-serviced camps and settlements also exacerbate the risk of disease outbreaks (HRP 2019-2021). Prevalence of malnutrition, communicable and water-borne diseases are high.According to Nutrition Sector udpates (October 2021) the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance findings (NFSS) Round 10 (October 2021) shows that the nutritional situation in Borno and Yobe states has deteriorated compared to the previous assessment October 2020, and is the highest since the start of nutrition surveillance in North East Nigeria (2016). The main factors associated with acute malnutrition in Borno includes lack of access to humanitarian services, high prevalence of morbidity, food insecurity and factors associated with conflict. The main causes of the high malnutrition rates in Yobe are attributed to chronic underdevelopment. The nutrition sector admitted 148, 169 children with severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) between January and October 2021, an average of more than 14,000 children with increased risk of death without treatment. Among these, ACF contribution to the SAM admissions was 26,811 (18%) of the sector admissions.

2 - Activities

The interventions proposed by Action Against Hunger will provide lifesaving health and nutrition activities including the provision of treatment and prevention of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) services in health facilities (outpatient therapeutic programs/OTPs and stabilization centers/SC) and communities, building the capacity of health workers to provide quality physical and mental health services, health facility rehabilitations and provision of medical supplies and equipment. AAH interventions will contribute to the achievement of the objectives of the Health and Nutrition Sectors for 2022. The response will also directly contribute to specific objectives of the Health and Nutrition Sectors’ response plans as outlined in the HRP, and the Nigeria Nutrition in Emergencies Sector Strategy and Response Plan (2020-2022). AAH will continue to support the State Ministry of Health and SPHCDA to expand the provision of primary health care beyond urban areas to newly accessible areas for adequate health assistance where there are gaps not covered by any other partners cover all population age and demographic groups, while providing standard packages for groups such as children under five, pregnant and lactating women (PLW).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Across all components of the programme, data collection and reporting will be done in a gender and socially inclusive manner. Thus, data will be disaggregated by age, gender, disability and other vulnerability markers such as head or member of single-parent household and unaccompanied/orphaned child. Due to the social dynamics characterised by the lower participation of women and girls in most communities, emphasis shall be given to the constitution of project teams with up to 70% female staff to ensure service access to the target beneficiaries. Action Against Hunger teams will conduct a baseline gender analysis/assessment in the first quarter of program implementation, an interim progress assessment at the end of the first year and an end of project assessment at project close out to measure the gender and inclusion gaps of the project and how they are met in the course of implementation

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Participatory monitoring will ensure to get feedback from beneficiaries. This will be done through a feedback and complaint response mechanism established for all projects as a way of soliciting, receiving, processing and responding to the feedback and complaints. These are embedded in the monitoring system and beneficiaries and stakeholders are sensitized on this feedback mechanism and toll-free number so that they understand how to express concerns relating to the project. Feedback on ACF activities will also be solicited during post distribution monitoring and meetings with communities. Discussions with beneficiaries/ community leaders will be done regularly to address emerging issues and to ensure community obligations and participation in project implementation. This approach has helped to establish a relationship of trust between staff and communities in various projects and to improve the impact of the response.
