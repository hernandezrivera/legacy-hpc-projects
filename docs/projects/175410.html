Project Summary

The project will provide urgent livelihoods assistance to 417,500 (83,500 households) most vulnerable IDPs and host communities (HC) affected by insecurity in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa through electronic vouchers and/or in-kind and cash distribution, supporting agriculture production.
The intervention aims at supporting the winter season (March-August 2021) and the main planting season (from November 2021) contributing to beneficiaries’ self-reliance and to reduced dependency from host communities and food aid.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will provide urgent livelihoods assistance to 420,000 (84,000 HH) most vulnerable Internally Displaced People (IDPs) and host communities affected by insecurity in the provinces of Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa. The distribution of agricultural inputs will be done preferably through an electronic voucher system. During the lean season the project will also deliver cash transfers to 4 000 most vulnerable IDPs women head of families in Pemba who do not have access to arable land and therefore haven’t received agricultural support.The agricultural kit that will be made available through the e-vouchers will serve for planting at least 0.5 Ha for the main planting season.The kit for the winter season will be adapted to local conditions and could be also used for establishing home gardens contributing also to improve nutrition of children.Beneficiaries of the project will be rural displaced populations from the northern districts of Cabo Delgado who have fled their homes looking for safer places. The inclusion of host communities as beneficiaries will contribute to the improvement of the fragile food security status (IPC phase 3 and above) as well as help to mitigate social tensions between host communities and IDPs. Among IDPs, priority will be given to women headed households, large families with children under 5 years old, elderly and other disadvantaged groups. Targeting and distribution activities will be carried out in coordination and close collaboration with WFP and IOM ensuring better identification of beneficiaries and efficient use of resources.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will undertake the activities in districts where it will be possible to ensure access and the beneficiaries will be able to plant and grow their crops in security until the harvest. FAO will target the southern districts of Cabo Delgado, and selected districts in Nampula and Niassa where IDPs are located.Priority will be given to the districts with higher number of IDPs with access to arable land in Cabo Delgado (Ancuabe, Chiure, Mecufi, Montepuez, Metuge and Balama), in Nampula (Erati, Nacala, Monapo, Rapale, Nampula, Meconta and Memba) and in Niassa (Lichinga, Lago, Sanga, Maua, Marrupa, Cuamba , Mecula).The voucher distribution will target all these districts for the winter season in February/March 2021 and the main planting season starting in November 2021.Identification/registration of beneficiaries, vouchers distribution and technical assistance will be ensured with the collaboration with Implementing Partners (National and International NGOs, Provincial and District Services of Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development).Through this intervention, FAO will provide IDP farmers with access to agricultural inputs such as seeds of OPV (Open Pollinated Varieties) of maize, sorghum, cowpea, beans, groundnuts and assorted vegetables as well as farming tools such as hoes, machetes, sickles and watering cans.Beneficiaries will have the choice to select among an assortment of pre-identified inputs through electronic vouchers (e-vouchers) valued at approximately USD20 for the winter season and USD45 for the main planting season. The e-voucher will offer beneficiaries a free choice among the list of items pre-selected and approved by FAO and Government Technical Services. The cash distribution will be done in the district of Pemba during the lean season to benefit 4 000 most vulnerable women headed Households who have not received agricultural assistance because the lack of access to land or manpower to cultivate.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

As part of the UN's strategy to address SEA, and sexual harassment, measures are in place to prevent, detect, report and take action against personnel, who commit these inexcusable and abhorrent acts. This includes but are not limited to: i) advocate at the senior leadership of UNCT on strict adherence and zero tolerance to SEA; ii) ensure completion of mandatory training on SEA by all staff; iii) establish mechanisms for address SEA matters; iv) create an enabling working environment for staff members; FAO has a provincial focal point for SEA in all provinces and staff will be provided with practical guidance on how to respond to disclosures of sexual harassment. The above measures will be extended to service providers. If appropriate, specific training and advocacy awareness raising sessions to service providers will be conducted.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The project will contribute to gender equality and women’s empowerment by ensuring that inequalities are not perpetuated during project implementation. Community consultation will always include women and other disadvantages groups’ representation. The project will prioritize as beneficiaries (at least 50%), women’s headed households, large families with children under 5 years old, elderly and other disadvantaged groups. It will also, take into consideration women’s workload and their caregiving responsibilities when designing capacity building trainings and access to input distributions and other activities. Sensitization sessions for Gender Equality and Women’s Empowerment (GEWE) and GBV Preventions will be conducted during the life cycle of the project.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project doesn’t specifically target people with disability although families with disabled people will be considered as priority during the beneficiaries’ selection.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The increased number of IDPs arriving in the southern districts of Cabo Delgado is creating an additional pressure on the already fragile natural resources of the province. IDPs and local communities rely on firewood collection to provide fuel for cooking.FAO will conduct an assessment on the impact of the increased population in the areas of resettlement of IDPs and will recommend alternative and innovative solutions to minimize the pressure on natural resources such as production and use of fuel-efficient cookstoves, alternative fuel and support to sustainable management of wood fuel resources (tree nurseries, community woodlots, agroforestry, etc.).
