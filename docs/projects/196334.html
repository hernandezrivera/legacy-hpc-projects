Project Summary

"Save the Children will deliver an inclusive, gender responsive & comprehensive healthcare services through 7 HP and 1 PHCC over the JRP period in 8 camps of Ukhiya and Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar to ensure equitable access to essential healthcare services for diverse Rohingya refugees and host communities including special-need populations (LGBTQI). Project will ensure gender responsive health facilities through sex segregated areas/rooms and presence of sufficient female staff. Those Health facilities will deliver primary health care service with special focus on 24/7 emergency care, sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health (SRMNCAH); mental health and psychosocial support; physical rehabilitation including assistive device provision and noncommunicable diseases in line with the minimum package of essential Health services (MPEHS). Along with regular health consultation, PHCC will offer IPD service, laboratory; PMTCT; BEmONC service with 5 bedded maternity unit & SGBV services (CMR, IPV and Domestic Violence). All this health facility will offer integrated services with Nutrition (IYCF-E & C-MAMI), Rehabilitation, WASH, Early childhood development (ECD) and Child Protection by ensuring coordination and referral in between, ensuring a child friendly corner considering “Do no harm policy”. To ensure IPC measure, safe disposal practice and to avoid chance of infection, a dedicated IPC focal will work closely with facility level IPC committee and provide necessary guidance aligned with IPC WG and MoH. The recently closed SARI-ITC structure will remain standby to support as a multifunctional outbreak center where other communicable diseases (Dengue, AWD, Measles, Diphtheriae, etc.) will be managed along with the COVID-19 if there is any surge. Clinical services will be complemented with health promotion and community engagement activities through a robust community health worker (CHWs) to encourage timely access to healthcare and address existing challenges in the community level and community-based surveillance will be continued to identify and COVID-19 patients in the communities. Engagement of women, community, and religious leaders to raise awareness of health and identify gender-responsive operations at health facility and arrange the necessary intervention to address those. All staff and volunteers will be trained on gender, GBV, Protection, PSEAH, CP, child safeguarding in health responses. To enhance clinical excellence, team will be trained on identified needs. There will be 2 Mobile Medical team (MMT) aligned with Emergency Preparedness Response Working Group (EPRWG) will be providing services in any emergency health response following any natural or manmade disaster. All the health facilities will be accessible and environment friendly for all age group and for PWD by ensuring ramp, disable friendly latrine, triage area, signage, lighting, and skilled workforce to prioritize and manage geriatric population and PWD.
SCI Bangladesh has a robust MEAL system and according to the Program’s Logical Framework, develop a MEAL plan including Monitoring Plan that specifies indicators, data disaggregated by sex, age and disability etc. MEAL team will apply IPTT, Quality Benchmark (QB) and SCI Global Medical Team recommended Clinical Standard scorecard in all the health facility to monitor the quality of the key activities as part of monitoring. Multiple channels are using for receiving complaints and feedback from beneficiaries such as the toll-free hotline, help desks, Phone Based PDM and Online feedback response mechanism (FRM). SCI gender focal person will ensure Implementation of gender action plan and monitoring of activities in health indicators. S/he will maintain coordination with GiHA and all the health partners to share update on gender responsive health developments, including needs, gaps, challenges which helps to Identify good gender-sensitive practices regarding the health response."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Since 2017, there is wider need of health services which fulfill by the different health partners with in Rohingya camps. The overall health and hygienic situation in the camps is poor and existing with enormous room of improvement. According to MSNA, 2021, though there are only 9% household (HH) expressed Health as priority need, it’s shown multiple segregated need of older population and person with disabilities. According to Health Bulletin, August 2022, approximately 73% of births now occur in health care facilities, a number that has increased over the last years. Only 17% of pregnant women are receiving 4 ANC visits indicates most of the pregnant women are not accessing adequate ANC & PNC services increasing the risk of maternal & neonatal mortality and morbidity. As per 2021 SRHWG annual bulletin: 5.4 % Rohingya refugee girls aged less than 18 years are still more likely to give birth compared to 2.8% within host communities. Facility maternal deaths reported increased from 16 in 2020 to 23 in 2021 (MPMSR report). Only 52% Rohingya women are using any contraception method where only 5.39% are using Long-Acting Reversible Contraceptive method (LARC) which also critical gap in service accessing. According to REACH BGD Factsheet (May 2021), In Camp, the prevalence of person with disabilities (aged 2 and above) was 12%. About 17% & 35% of HH reportedly had at least one older person and at least one People with Disabilities respectively. Overall, more than half the person with disabilities had reportedly not received any assistive devices despite needing them. Findings in End Line Evaluation of SCI (September 2021), need of Blood donation bank for critical patients, adequate specialized service like eye care, dental care, advanced skin disease and orthopedic treatment etc. also identified. Additionally, the need to scale up structured and effective MHPSS services remains urgent. The SRH needs for high-risk and special-need populations (LGBTQI), including survivors of GBV & adolescents, are yet to be addressed. Special populations groups including persons with diverse gender and sexual orientation face a variety of different risk factors to sexual violence and additional barriers to accessing essential health services. Married and pregnant adolescent girls still need to be targeted through facility and community support groups to reduce their high risks of maternal mortality and morbidity and still there is room to strengthen SRH care. Reasonable male engagement in health services yet not achieve, thus affect health-seeking behavior due to unequal power exercise in decision making on SRH for their spouses. GBV is under-reported, and clinical staff have limited capacity to manage its medical and psychosocial impact. There are multiple communicable & other disease outbreaks such as Dengue, Cholera, AWD, chickenpox, and measles confirming the understanding that the current setting is a “perfect storm” for disease outbreaks. Also, burden of non-communicable diseases also must be addressed with necessary preparation in facility and community level. Increased burden of communicable and non-communicable diseases prevalence claims the strengthening of lab services in camp level. Since Rohingya influx, several extreme weather conditions, fire incidents were experienced which questioned the readiness to deal with those emergencies. EPR plan in Health facility and center level is the very time demanding and obvious requirement to minimize the massive loss of human life and other resources. There is gap of integrated health services leading beneficiary sufferings in terms of movement from facility, service availability and accessibility. All the facility can integrate the nutrition, rehabilitation, and Early Childhood Development (ECD) services can also plan to support the education and child protection thematic area under the health services.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Pregnant & lactating mother and diverse populations identified as vulnerable groups with the identification of potential threat for them. Community strength and coping strategy also assess to ensure participation of all age and gender groups. Analyze the identified need, risk, and strengths, all the interventions will plan with necessary resource mobilization and capacity enhancement initiatives. All the project objectives and indicators will set prioritizing the vulnerable group including PWD & LGBTQI. Accessibility of all the interventions with no harm and accountability of service providers will be secured by the technical supervision and sufficient implications of monitoring process. Empowerment and participations of programme participants will be ensured by the applying multiple channels for complaint Feedback Response and Reporting Mechanism

3.2 - Gender

Health programme spontaneously adhere all the population irrespective of age, sex and disability due to its service modality and data to be segregated in SADD. Interventions plan based on need of the gender group with the attainment of gender responsive human and other resources. Adequate female staffs and volunteers will be there to ensure gender responsive health services. Gender segregated Infra-structure and WASH facilities (separate toilet for male & female, breast feeding corner, separate waiting & triage arear for male & female, CFS to ensure tension free consultation for mother with child) also engineered considering the vulnerability. Community activities also planned to ensure the participation and empowerment of women. Meaningful access to diversified group like LGBTQI also adopted as regular charge. Significant male engagement also prioritizes in both community and facility level to ensure women empowerment, enhance decision making power of women in terms of health seekin

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

SCI is bound to safeguard the basic rights and dignity of the Rohingya and host children residing in the camps in its territory by its self-developing policies. It ensures the measures to tailor their specific vulnerabilities and developmental needs, to enable them to enjoy their rights in the existing circumstances of critical health, safety, and security. For the enjoyment of health-related rights of Rohingya children SC has providing comprehensive emergency assistance and health care to safeguard them through healthcare services, healthcare procedures and related conditions which has work together effectively. In addition, SC has well established Global Safeguarding policy and reporting system and all health service intervention has adopted by child safeguarding policy and all staffs & volunteers are well trained on child safeguarding. Health programme also focuses on geriatric population considering the incontinence, nutritional status, and rehabilitation requirements.

3.4 - Diversity

Through a holistic and multi-level approach, various efforts to support the health response have taken place throughout the SC humanitarian crisis response, including Rohingya response where SC has ensured provision of emergency medicines and supplies, health education for all vulnerable population. All health intervention has continued despite the social constraints. In Rohingya community there are enormous social & religious myths and misbelief regarding the different health issue specially on reproductive health and women rights & empowerment. Also, persons with disabilities are deprived with inadequate medicine (for neurological issue), assistive devices supply, ramp, handrails, disable friendly toilets etc. This project will focus to reduce the religious and social taboos and misbeliefs and will ensure the sufficient supplies of medicine, assistive device by ensuring necessary referral.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

SCI conducted multi sector needs assessment in a participatory approach by engaging the community people of different age and sex groups which includes health & nutrition sector, outcome assessment & lesson learnt and Children consultations in both Rohingya and host community from September 2021-2022. Building staff & volunteers competencies for accountable programming also make sure during this project period. Multiple channels (proactive and reactive mechanisms) to collect feedback like Community meetings, exit interviews, FGD with all age group, hotlines, helpdesk, children’s consultation, child friendly mechanism and post distribution monitoring mechanisms are practised that are accessible to all including the elderly and people with disabilities, as a gender & disability inclusive platform for community members to raise their complaints in a safe and dignified manner. Received Feedback and Complaints are closely monitored and analyzed, and referred to relevant sectors weekly.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

To ensure minimum impact on environment, SCI Health program intricately works with WASH team for proper waste management which has been produced in health facilities. There has a proper waste management system within in PHCC premises. In all health facility, there are dedicated personnel to ensure proper waste management procedure. In all health infrastructure, treated bamboo were used as a construction material. Treated bamboo ensures longevity of the structure, which creates less adverse effect on the environment by using less bamboo. SCI have introduced vertical tree plantation in health facility premises with the use of waste plastic bottle. Tree plantation activity in all health post and PHCC premises is continuing to reduce the risk of infrastructural damage during any disaster. Capacity building of staffs and volunteers on response during disaster/emergency is also performing as regular process.
