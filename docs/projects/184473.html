Project Summary

Decades of conflict and natural disasters including repeated droughts and seasonal flooding followed by limited government and institutional support have had a profound negative effect on the food security situation and social service sector in Somalia. Nearly seven in ten Somalis live on less than USD 1.90 per day, the sixth-highest poverty rate in sub-Saharan Africa. Poverty is both widespread and deep, particularly for households in rural areas and internally displaced person (IDP) settlements. The number of food-insecure people experiencing long-term stress is trending upwards, indicating that chronic food insecurity persists, requiring sustainable solutions. Recurrent climate-related shocks and political instability are key drivers of food insecurity and pose continuing risks to crop production and food access. FSNAU and FEWS NET projected a total of 3.5 million out of the 15.8m people face acute food insecurity (IPC 3 and above) – crisis or worse until December 2021. The riverine areas of Hiran, Middle Shabelle, Lower Shabelle, Gedo, Middle Juba and Lower Juba were some of the worst-affected areas. Many of the IDPs rely on relief aid and have no skills or capital to start alternative livelihoods. In Mogadishu (Deynille, Dharkeynley and Kaxda), Jubaland (Kismayo, Dolow, Bardere and Belet-Hawa), Southwest (Biadoa, Afgoye and Wanlaweyn), Galmadug (Guriceel, Abudwak and Dhusamareb) currently lead in the number of IDPs hosted.
Based on the above; CoDHNet makes this proposal to Improve lifesaving food Security and food related crisis to 12,000 affected populations in Wanlaweyn district of Somalia. Targeting will be carried out according to clear and transparent targeting criteria involving all stakeholders. Systematic respect of the “do no harm” principle will also be adhered to. Protection principles will be mainstreamed to ensure respect for the rights of all individuals including people with disabilities, regardless of their age, gender, social, ethnic, or other background, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (e.g., human rights law, international humanitarian law and refugee law). The proposed project will target 2,000 households including IDP households, benefiting around 12,000 beneficiaries (1800 men, 3000 women, 3600 girls, and 3,600 boys including households living with people with disabilities).
The overall objective of the project is to improve access to food, and increase food related livelihoods to the affected communities and IDPs through:
1) Provision of unconditional cash transfers to 1,000 IDP households in Wanlaweyn District benefiting around 6,000 (900 men, 1500 women, 1800 girls and 1800 boys) giving priority to women, people with disabilities and child-headed households.
2) Provision of appropriate agricultural inputs (e.g., quality seed, farm tools, land preparation support); to 500 agropastoral farmers in Wanlaweyn district benefitting 3,000 people (450 men, 750 women, 900 girls and 900 boys including households with people with disabilities).
3) Provide fodder seeds to support for 500 agropastoral farmers in Wanlaweyn district, benefitting around 3,000 people (450 men, 750 women, 900 girls and 900 boys including households with people with disabilities). .
4) Conduct 3 days training to 100 lead farmers (50 men and 50 women) on modern crop technologies to help increase the production of the farmers.
CoDHNet will maintain regular communication, and coordination mechanisms with all the project stakeholders including the community, local authority, regional food security cluster, cluster partners, and will submit monthly reports through 4W Matrix to FSC and OCHA will receive monthly financial and narrative reports for accountability purposes.

Project Objective: The overall objective of the project is to improve lifesaving food security and livelihoods support for crisis affected Populations and IDPs in Wanla Weyn district. Project outcome: 1,000 food-in-secure households and IDPs in IPC 4 benefiting 6,000 people (900 men, 1500 women, 1800 girls and 1800 boys including people with disabilities) in Wanla Weyn district improved their food security and food related livelihoods. Project output 1: Improved access to food to 1,000 IDPs benefiting around 6,000 people (900 men, 1500 women, 1800 girls and 1800 boys including disabilities) facing severe acute food insecurity in Wanla Weyn district through unconditional cash transfer/voucher. Activity 1.1.Provide unconditional cash transfer/voucher to improve immediate food security to 1,000 IDPs in Wanla Weyn Disttrict, benefiting around 6,000 people (900 men, 1500 women, 1800 girls and 1800 boys including people with disabilities) in Wanla Weyn District, facing severe acute food insecurity. Each household will receive 70% of cash/voucher-based intervention for a period of two months, which is US$69/month. The cash-based intervention in Wanla Weyn is US$69 in accordance with CWG (January 2021). Project output 2:1000 agropastoral farmers in Wanla Weyn district composing 6,000 people (900 men, 1500 women, 1800 boys, and 1800 girls including households with disabilities) have improved their food related livelihoods and increased their farming production through the provision of seasonal agricultural inputs including fodder seeds and seasonal seeds along with the GAP training coincide with the agricultural off-season harvest period. Activity 2.1: Provide assorted agricultural inputs to 500 agropastoral households’ benefiting around 3,000 people (450 men, 750 women, 900 boys, and 900 girls including households with people with disabilities) selected with the consultations of the community leaders, women groups, people with disabilities, and minorities from the communities in Wanla Weyn district. Each agropastoral farmer will receive: Cereal seeds (10 kgs maize), Legume seeds (5 kgs cowpea), Assorted vegetable seeds (240 grams), land preparation for 3 hours tractor hiring and basic farming tools (Hoes, fork, and Shovel). Activity 2.2: Provide fodder seeds (e.g., Sudan grass) to 500 agropastoral households benefiting around 3,000 affected people (450 men, 750 women, 900 girls and 900 boys including HHs with disabilities and IDPs) selected from the communities in Wanla Weyn district. Each agropastoral household will receive 5kg of quality-controlled Sudan seeds and is expected to harvest 6-8 tons for a period of two and half months. The Sudanese Grass production will be an alternative and sustainable source of pasture/fodder for livestock during the drought period. Activity 2.3: To keep the sustainability of the farming production, conduct three days training to 100 lead-farmers on Good Agricultural Practices (GAP). the trained lead-farmers will train their follow farmers on the knowledge they gained from the training. Ensuring the centrality of protection and accountability to the affected people, CoDHNet will ensure the protection of the beneficiaries during access and utilization of services by reducing further risks or causing a conflict of interest. The distribution of the agricultural input and the registration of the beneficiaries will be done through assigned safe stores and locations respectively, which are near the settlements of the beneficiaries and will be distributed in the daytime to avoid the risk against women against GBV.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash help the beneficiaries meet a variety of needs, including access to food, water, healthcare, shelter, that allow them to build and support livelihoods. Cash transfers were more cost-efficient than direct food transfers or vouchers. They had lower costs per beneficiary and lower administrative costs. They also produced greater multiplier effects, stimulating local economies around beneficiaries' homes via indirect market benefits. Vouchers were more cost-efficient than direct food transfers, although less efficient than cash transfers. Cash-based approaches and vouchers also create beneficial multiplier effects in local markets that in-kind food distribution does not.: When goods are available and accessible locally, cash or voucher-based interventions have the potential to be more rapid than bringing supply from out of the country. When beneﬁciaries use local markets, agencies also eliminate logistical and import costs. However, the additional staff, security, and logistical resources sometimes required to distribute cash or manage a voucher programme must also be factored in.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Cash-based approaches and vouchers create beneficial multiplier effects in local markets that in-kind food distribution does not. This project utilises a market- based approach programming by employing modalities such as vouchers or cash that have proven effective in supporting local markets. It also aims to promote household purchasing power and access to basic needs that are provided in local markets. By minimising external procurement of assets, it provides opportunities for local market actors to interact and strengthen and stabilise the local demand and supply the required commodities. In designing this project, CoDHNet will assess the capacity of local markets to respond to the increased demand. The geographic spread, timing, and caseloads of the proposed cash interventions consider pressures on local markets and its functionality.
