Project Summary

The Project will rehabilitate 500 damaged houses for returnees and hosts in two key regions in Syria (rural Damascus and rural Aleppo) to positively cope with life conditions after 8 years of war and social disasters. The renovation works will be executed by operating main repairs in the damaged houses in order to make them safe homes for vulnerable families that will find safety, protection and privacy in their own house. The Project aims to boost the safe return and the resilience process of the population of concern.

Needs

Close to eight years into the crisis, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain deep and far-reaching. The conﬂict has war torn countless lives, over half of the country's population have ﬂed their homes, and 13 million people remain in need of humanitarian assistance, with 5.2 million of them facing acute humanitarian needs.Millions of people have fallen into poverty, and many were forced to resort to harmful coping while facing increasing protection risks. After the stop of hostilities and an ease in access restrictions, the region of rural Aleppo and rural Damascus targeted by the present Project are witnessing a process of returning after the prolonged hostilities and instability caused extensive displacement. The Population of Concern found herself in the midst of a devastated area, with enormous infrastructural damages and a disruption of service provisions. As the crisis endures, people's resourcefulness alone remains inadequate to counter deprivation that - for millions- is only mitigated by humanitarian assistance. We have focused, in harmony with the HRP, into the SHELTER/ needs. All targeted regions within this project are newly liberated areas and currently receive a huge numbers of returnees as well as contain many partially damaged houses that we can with reasonable funding and effort help them to rebuild along with their lives. Need assessments resources: - Key persons in targeted regions. - Information from out medical teams working on the ground.

Implementation of activities

MSJM has its own engineering team to supervise the works. Nevertheless due to the distances between the locations, MSJM will launch a bidding process to hire the best contractor who will act under the supervision of our teams. 1- Coordination with the relative authorities to get approvals. 2- Hiring the required staff, both administrative and tecnnical. 3- Launching and processing a tender for the contractors4- Launching and processing the required Procurement for materials and tools. 5- Form a complete file with civilian and technical data of potential candidates who will beneﬁt of this project in the respective locations 6- Segregate the beneficiaries following a standard severity scale (Beneficiaries must be owners – It is possible to renovate the habitat within the financial frame of the Project – Priority to vulnerable families, women household, families with persons with disability 7- Executing the rehabilitation works as following (safety (door and windows, a toilet, a kitchen, one living room) 8- M&E visits and reporting9- Mid-term narrative report 8- Final narrative report.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risks: Risk1: Beneficiaries’ identification that absolutely depends on civil documents and papers of property may deprives people who had lost their documents through events to beneﬁt from this project. Mitigation measure: ﬁnding an alternative documents in case of their losing in agreement with local relative authorities and MOSAL and MOLA Risk 2: most vulnerable groups(femal-headed households-disabled - elderly) may be victims of exploitation by staff through registration stage, especially women and girls who will be subjected to sexual harassment and abuse. Mitigation measure: - engage committees from local community in registration process as well as employment of trained on (code of conduct) and with good reputation staff. - Monitoring this process by M&E section. - advertisement of a complaint mechanism (complaint Box or hot line.....) to receive complaints from beneﬁciaries. Risk 3: Situation of tension may be generated between persons in the local community due to accepting cases and refusing another.Mitigation measures: clear selection criteria and transparent process of selecting efficiencies

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Delay of getting required approvals from local authorities.Mitigation measure: Prior close coordination with authorities and ministries concerned to secure speeding up the routine procedures.Risk 1: Change of the exchange rate.Mitigation measure: In the MoU with the vendors that have succeeded in the Procurement process we will introduce a clause about the possible variance of exchange rate in order to stabilize it.
