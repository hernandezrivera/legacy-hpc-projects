Project Summary

This project targeting 113,080 people, 30,000 women 21,000 men, 38,950, boys ,and 23130 girls, and is an integrated GP, GBV, CP and HLP aims at prevention and mitigating various protection risks through provision of protection services for the conflict-affected population, returnees, and host communities through emergency mobile response to key entries point of Rotriak, Renk and Malakal, as well as protection that will informed MHA response through static presence in Unity and Jongelei States respectively, and activities will includes; protection monitoring, Housing, land and property(HLP), CP/mine risk education Programming using community based approaches and accountability to the affected population, GBV services including creation of safe referral pathway, PSS in CFs WGFs in all project locations as elaborated in the activities section.
Mobile Humanitarian Agency (MHA) will use the existing community-based structures as working with CBPNs at the community level throughout our programming proven to be the right approach to better understand various protection needs in the communities, and for the sustainability of the protection activities.

MHA will carry out routine protection monitoring to identify the potentials protection concern and will ensure involves the community in the all stages of the project design and implementation phases to have the true need and the priority from the beneficiaries themselves.
MHA will work with the affected communities of returnees and IDPs in various location and assess their both in village and urban setting to assess their HLP related for immediate attention and response. In urban setting, MHA provide legal awareness session to the affected communities, provide legal assistance and legal aid to ensure IDPs and returnees have access to services including civil registration and are able to recover lost documents and property.

1. Needs

South Sudan remains one of the most fragile countries in the world. Although a revitalized peace agreement was signed in 2018, the implementation of key aspects remains incomplete. South Sudan is grappling with multiple interrelated crises. Conflict, subnational and localized violence, climatic shocks, and the ripple effects of the global food crisis continue to push millions of South Sudanese into dire hunger, disrupt livelihoods, and exacerbate entrenched vulnerabilities, threatening peace and stability. Limited access to basic services reinforces marginalization and entrenched inequity, especially for the most vulnerable groups in hard-to-reach areas. Five years of consecutive unprecedented floods also brought South Sudan to the forefront of the climate crisis, displacing over 300,000 people. As a result, humanitarian needs remain alarmingly highfar exceeding available resources. In 2023, over 9.4 million people are in need of urgent assistance and protection, a staggering 76 percent of South Sudan's population. The ongoing Sudan crisis and related cross-border population movements continue to aggravate an already dire situation. Since the outbreak of fighting in mid-April, the influx of people fleeing Sudan has increased exponentially. As of July 14, to DTM, an estimated 171,181 people – 91 percent of whom are South Sudanese nationals have fled South Sudan as returnees and refugees, requiring immediate humanitarian and protection assistance. The actual numbers are likely to be higher as crisis-affected people entered South Sudan without registration. There are 12 key entry points along the Sudan-South Sudan border, with the majority coming in through Renk Upper Nile, Panakuach Unity, Abyei, and Kiir Adem and Majokyinthiou (Northern Bahr el Ghazal). The most common transient and final destinations are known to be Abyei, Akobo, Aweil, Fangak, Juba, Malakal, Unity State, and Greater Tonj. The majority of returnees are vulnerable with little or no cash or assets upon arrival, and limited incomes to meet basic needs, absorb shocks and be self-reliant in final destinations. They share resources with host communities, putting pressure on already limited resources that result in increased people in need overreliance on negative coping strategies, and a potential for tensions between returnees and host populations. The Sudan crisis has detrimentally impacted South Sudan’s economic outlook, leading to an increase in food prices and fuel costs, particularly in areas along the borders that rely heavily on imports from Sudan. Goods are now primarily being supplied to these northern areas from Juba, contributing to an increase in prices. Since the beginning of the Sudan crisis, the prices of food in the border areas with Sudan, compounded by the effects of subnational and localized insecurity and rains, have increased by 20 to 84 percent. The current economic situation has a major risk of exacerbating the protection risks and vulnerabilities of the population, especially amongst the most vulnerable groups such as returnees, pregnant and nursing mothers, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities.Reference to HRP 2024 the anticipated severity level, 5.33 million people are in critical needs of protection services across the country. looking into specific needs in the proposed project locations,of Renk, Rotriak/Rubkona, Malakal, Akobo, Nyirol, Canal/Pigi, Mayiandit counties.

2. Project activities

MHA through integrated approach will develop consistent key messaging that will be informed by the findings from the protection monitoring analysis. MHA will carry out routine protection monitoring with the purpose of gathering critical protection information that will be process and used in the design of the project and response to the protection concerned. MHA will work with communities of affected population and protection partners, and will develop key protection awareness raising messages and information on availability of services including facilitated referral to access servicesIndividual Protection Assistance, IPA is the response granted to a person with specific needs (PSN) after being assessed and need identified. MHA will provide in-kind and cash support to individuals identified to be highly vulnerable and exposed to protection risks. MHA will Work with protection partners on the ground though static or mobile activities to enhance the referral of PSNs, especially in emergency situations to access live-saving emergency assistance.MHA will work with protection partners and communities of affected people IDPs, returnees’ refuges and the host communities, establish or enhance existing community-based structures and implement activities that constitute alternatives to displacement enable persons of concern to live in dignity and prepare the ground for the durable solution.MHA will work with protection partners, development and peace actors as well as authorities and existing community-based structures and implement activities designed to enhance community resiliencies, PSEA, peace-building activities and trainings through AGD approach that aims to transform the youth to be change agents in their communities.MHA in coordination with different actors will share the information and provide assistance to people with HLP needs in the areas of return.MHA will undertake HLP awareness raising sessions with the person of concerns within the view of dissemination of HLP rights and other critical information. MHA will provide legal assistance to the affected clients and accompany them to claim their rights if they are unable to do so by themselves to claim their rights and appear for them in those institutions that have come in contact with thempsychosocial support (PSS) to mitigate the negative impacts among children, MHA will construct child friendly spaces (CFSs) in the areas of operations. Through Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS) One main strategy is to ensure children and adolescents access to recreational and educational activities which contribute to improving their psychosocial well-being and to relieved them from many years psychological pain, and the CFS shall include a space for indoor and outdoor activities, and games. MHA will Work with protection partners and communities to identify children with disabilities and at risk to provide individual specialised case management services and MHPSS that meet their unique needs, individual direct cash-based assistance to cover the protection need.MHA will Work with protection partners, authorities and existing community-based structures to develop child protection messages, that will be disseminated through awareness raising activities.MHA will work with protection partners, as well as authorities and existing community-based structures to organise and implement CPiE trainings for community leaders including non-protection partners.Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) MHA will identify, register, trace and reunify unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and missing children and document the caseload in the CPIMS. MHA will conduct GBV awareness through house-to-house visit, focused group interventions, campaigns, and mass media including free recorded messages on GBV prevention among the conflict affected population.MHA will strengthen Psychosocial Support service provision through capacity building and field monitoring visit.
