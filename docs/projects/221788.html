Project Summary

The project aims to response to gaps created by challenging situations in South Sudan resulted from wars, communal conflicts, deteriorating economy, COVID-19, and natural disasters such as flooding that displaced thousands of people and disrupted their livelihood in Yirol East, Twice East, Ayod, Yirol West and Awerial Counties. According to IPC analysis, between September and November 2024, estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) and within this total, 41,000 are in catastrophe, 1,707,000 million people in emergency level, 4,555,000 people in crisis, 4,508,000 people are stressed while only 2,631,000 of the analysed population are in need of food security.
In the first projection period from December 2024 – March 2025, 6.1 million people which is approximately 45% of the total analysed will be in (IPC phase 3 or above) with 31,000 people in catastrophe, 1,713,000in emergency, 4330,000 people in crisis, 4,573,000 people stressed and only 2,793,000 will be in need of food security.
The IPC second projection projected that 7.7 approximately 57% of the total analysed population will be in (IPC Pahes 3 or above) and these results are an indication of the worsening situation of food security and nutrition in the country. According to the analysed population, 63,000 which is more than a half of the first projection will be in catastrophe, 2,534,000 more than a half of the first projection will be in emergency level, 5,091,000 people will be in crisis, 3,830,000 people will be stressed while only 1,926,000 people will be in need of food security. The above analysis only indicate the worsening food security situation and now coupled by main drivers such as Economic crisis, conflict and insecurity, climatic shocks, flooding which is 10% higher in 2024 compared to 2023 and the floods not only displaced people but also disrupted delivery of humanitarian support, and contributed to low access to health services in the face of increased disease burden driven poor sanitation practices thus increasing the risk of acute malnutrition and lastly, low agricultural production as a result of unpredictable and extreme weather patterns, poor access to quality seeds and use of traditional farming methods negatively impacted agricultural production, resulting in increased humanitarian food assistance needs.
The November 2024 – March 2025, Famine and Early Warning Systems (FEWS NET) still maintained the risk of famine (IPC phase 5) in low lying and flood prone counties near the Sudd wetland and major river basins citing that this will burden the locals in these flood prone areas especially in Upper Nile Jonglei and parts of Unity state. The same FEWSNET cited localized conflicts as drivers that will worsen the food security situations especially in Lakes where road ambushes happened on October 6th in Rumbek East and September 28 in Rumbek North counties negatively impacting the lives of those affected.
Help – Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe Just like other Humanitarian Organizations that are trying their best to response to specific needs of the vulnerable people, has so far secured 33 months (1st July 2023 – 31st March 2026) project titled “Integrated WASH, Food Security and Nutrition services to conflict and disaster affected persons in Lakes and Jonglei States, South Sudan” with WASH, nutrition and FSL as the thematic focus areas. Under the Food Security and Livelihood thematic area, Help Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe has planned to response to these worsening situations in the year 2024 by putting more emphasis on provision of seeds and farm tools to 1500 vulnerable households, training of 1500 HHs to improve their traditional farming knowledge with modern production that are more productive, provision of fishing gears to 1000 households to diversify their livelihood sources and improve on their protein dietary intakes.

1. Needs

In the face of the turbulent world, South Sudan, a world youngest nation birthed through referendum in 2011 did not enjoy much of the freedoms they struggled and longed for when the country plunged in the midst of unending crisis resulted from climate shocks (four years precarious flooding and dry spells), COVID-19 pandemic, and the deteriorating economy with South Sudan pounds depreciating against united states dollars, conflict and insecurity including the spillover effects from Sudan conflict that forcefully displaced people and also compromising agricultural production in many parts of South Sudan. According to IPC analysis, according to acute food insecurity first projection situation overview (December 2024 - Mar 2025), from this period, it is projected that 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analysed) will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 4.33 million people (32 percent of the population analyzed) are likely to face IPC Phase 3 (Crisis), 1.71 million people (13 percent of the population analysed) will likely face IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees (0.2 percent of the population analysed) will likely experience IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The main drivers of an unending worsening of humanitarian situation in South Suda include the Economic crisis, conflict and insecurity that has and is still increasing the rate of displacement in Lakes, Jonglei, upper Nile, and Warrap states, climatic shock characterized by frequent floods and dry spells, and the low agricultural production worsened by unpredictable and extreme weather patterns, poor access to quality seeds and use of traditional farming method impacting agricultural production.At the moment, the county houses over 117,840 individuals as IDPs both within and outside Yirol East and West Counties. These individuals share most of the livelihood opportunities with the host communities. If the number keeps growing, the humanitarian situation in Yirol East and West counties will deteriorate and many people will be affected. The IRNA assessment conducted in the month of 21st – 27th October 2024 reported in Yirol East and West Counties reported a total of 117,840 individuals were displaced by flood in Yirol East County. Among the above number, 26395 Individuals were citizens of Yirol East County while 91,445 individuals were IDPs from Panyijaar and Twic East County displaced by the flooding. In Yirol West County, 2210 households were displaced by flooding impacting the lives of 11,005 individuals in Mapuordit, Gher and Aluakluak payams with cases of 27 (12 males and 15 females) who died when their boat capsized while rowing to the highland areas for safety. Another 18 (8 males and 10 females). The impacted households adopted some coping mechanisms such as borrowing from relatives and well-wishers, limitation of portion of meals eaten a day, restrict consumption by adults for small children and elderly.The recommendations from the two (2) assessments indicated that there is a high need to respond to the situation by providing emergency food distribution and distribution of agricultural inputs (seeds and tools)Help is gearing towards achieving a more stable community that is food secure and malnutrition free by increasing emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs distribution, distribution of fishing kits, increase food access through unconditional food, and training the farmers on how to produce vegetable in flood affected areas by using aerial gardening. The activities under this project include provision of seeds and farm tools to 1500 vulnerable households, training of 1500 HHs to improve their traditional farming knowledge with modern production that are more productive, provision of fishing gears to 1000 households to diversify their livelihood sources and improve on their protein dietary intakes.

2. Project activities

The activities under this project include lean season activities and those that will be done throughout the year 2025. Lean season activities from April 2025 - September 2025 include,• Distribution of unconditional food to 1000 most vulnerable households (female headed households, Child headed households and vulnerable males’ headed households, person with disabilities and IDPs) • Distribution of inputs (seeds and farm tools), to 1500 HHs both males, females and children doing agriculture • Distribution of fishing kits 1000 HHs to diversify the livelihood sources and improve their dietary intake• Asset creation through Cash Asset (CFA) to 1500 HHs restricted to agricultural production as a response to upsurging food insecurity Activities after that will continue through• Agronomic and postharvest handling trainings to 1500 HHs that will receive seeds and farm tools to improve food production level• Formation and strengthening of Natural Resource Management Committee (NRMC) through establishment of tree nurseries, distribution of tree seedlings, and raising awareness on the maintenance of natural resources (water bodies, forests, land). • Awareness on Nutrition and GBV
