Project Summary

In Zimbabwe, women, men and children are faced with a multi-risk environment, made up slow and rapid onset emergencies. The majority of the people are at risk of multiple hydro-meteorological hazards such as disease epidemics (typhoid, COVID-19 and cholera), flooding and drought. Seasonal forecast is pointing at normal to above normal rainfall which is associated with potential of flooding. Drought and flooding negatively impact on the provision of safe water thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases among the vulnerable communities.
According to RWIMS October 2020, 48.7% of water points nationally are fully functional, 44% of the population have access to any type of toilet, 66% have pot racks and 59% have refuse pits.
The project would like to increase proportion of women, children, men and people with disability having access to water sources of sufficient quality and availability by an average 21.6% and reduce the proportion of women, women and girls of reproductive age, children, men, older persons and people with disability facing environmental sanitation problems by an average 18.6%. The project would like to reduce open defecation through construction of self-sponsored latrines, promote household hygiene through construction of hygiene enabling facilities and promote hand hygiene through hygiene promotion targeting women, children, boys, girls, men and people with disability.

1 - Activities

In order to increasing access to water sources of sufficient quality and availability the project will repair or rehabilitate broken down or partially function boreholes. The project will also establish solar powered PWS at two high yielding boreholes per district and for multi-purpose use, targeting institutions and community, balancing demand and yield in Hurungwe, Makonde and Kariba. Refresher training for WPCs and water quality monitoring for all water points. Sanitation and hygiene promotion through road shows, S and CHCs while PSEA, GBV and AAP training will target DWSSC, WWSSC and community leaders in Hurungwe, Makonde Kadoma urban, Kariba and Mudzi. In consultation with communities, communication and feedback mechanisms to address issues related to humanitarian response will be established. Hygiene and dignity kits will be distributed to vulnerable households affected by humanitarian crisis in target districts. 1. Baseline survey on GBV, SEA, disability and feedback mechanism 2. Estabilsh PWS targeting institutions, business places and communities (2 Hurungwe, 1 Makonde, 2 Kariba)3. Repair water points (96 Hurungwe, 47 Makonde, 62 Kariba)4. Hygiene promotion at public places and markets through road shows and community gatherings8. Q&A sessions during road shows9. Distribute IEC materials targeting, public places and institutions10. Training of health club facilitators for 205 health clubs11. Establishment and strengthening of S&CHS12. Distribution of NFIs 13. Post distribution monitoring

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

It is recognized that regular input and feedback from communities is vital to better measure performance and results and improves the relationship between humanitarian aid workers and the community. Feedback can be structured and proactively sought, for example through surveys or community meetings, or unstructured and passivelysought, by monitoring rumors and social media.The project will establish common feedback mechanisms (CFM) in consultation with target groups. A baseline survey will be conducted to identify at risk groups and determine WASH facilities that will be targeted for repair or rehabilitation, gather views of target groups on type of structured feedback mechanism to be set up or approve a proposed mechanism and modalities for responding to issues raised. The CFM will be used for sharing information with affected communities and to address complaints and feedback. It will also serve as a tool that generates information for planning, performance evaluation and decision-making. It is hoped that the project will use suggestion boxes, toll free line and community meetings as structured CFM.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Capacity tests will be done for all high yielding water points targeted for development into PWS. This will be done to determine sustainable levels of water use to avoid over drawing and as a safety measure extraction of water will metered and consumption will also be monitored with meters. The project will also raise awareness of the importance of water conservation. Water quality monitoring will be done and results shared with beneficiaries. Storm water drains will be constructed where necessary to protect the environment and WPCs will be encouraged to use live fencing or walls in order to contribute to reduction in environmental degradation when protecting water points from domestic animals as this reduces frequent cutting down of trees. To protect existing water sources from faecal contamination communities will be encouraged to locate latrines downstream of wells, at least 30m from groundwater sources and at least 1.5m above the water table. In camps fitting pit latrines with concrete slabs will eliminate the need for secondary wooden slabs or supporting beams and facilitates easy cleaning.
