Project Summary

With the continued influx of returnees Sudan, Uganda, Congo and Kenya and refugees from Sudan the humanitarian situation of South Sudan is worsened further to emergency or catastrophic levels. In the counties of Akobo, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol, and Fangak within Jonglei State people have suffered the brunt of floods destroying livelihoods of communities, killing animals, damaging houses and public infrastructure at devastating levels. This has been further worsened by the incidents of insecurity and violence that have together led to displacements that worsened the situation especially for children, women, and girls by increasing their vulnerabilities. The influx of returnees from Khartoum who have been repatriated to their counties have added more burden to the host community due to limited resources.
As South Sudan continues to grapple with high levels of violence, insecurity, flood, diseases, and economic crisis, it is clear that the humanitarian situations will not be reversed to a stable state if not supported across the country. The Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition situations in the counties of Akobo, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol and Fangak are IPC 4 (Emergency state) that requires robust interventions to avoid any further catastrophic state for the vulnerable groups.
Aid Link Organization (ALO) has established presence in the counties targeted for this intervention and therefore proposes to provide lifesaving Food Security & Livelihoods (FSL) services to 84,160 individuals/households that are vulnerable and train them on improved agricultural practices to support their livelihoods and sustain their lives.
To be able to successfully implement the activities in this project, ALO seeks for $ 500,000.

1. Needs

People of South Sudan have continued to suffer from deteriorating economic crisis, climatic shocks especially floods, conflict and insecurity. From the beginning of 2024, there have been influx of returnees from Uganda, Kenya, Sudan, and Congo this influx is further increased by the people fleeing conflict in Sudan as refugees hence exacerbating the dire situations and putting additional pressure on the already fragile situation in the country. According to the IPC report, over 1.7 million people are in IPC4 (Emergency); during the lean season it is estimated that >7.6 million people will be in IPC 3 or above. The nutrition situation in the country is dire as already 2 million children 6-59 months, and >1 million PBWs suffering from acute malnutrition due to nutrition insecurity in Jonglei, NBG, UNS & Warrap as epicenters. This already worsens the poor indicators for health, WASH, FSL and safety nets across the country. The country already grapples with elevated morbidities among children under 5 years especially with common childhood illnesses such as malaria, URTI, diarrhea and other immunizable diseases. The key drivers of acute food insecurity are economic crisis due to currency depreciation, high food and commodity prices, poverty; conflict and insecurity; climatic shocks especially floods that have displaced people, destroyed farms, killed animals; low agricultural production due to floods, extreme dry spells, and pests/diseases. In the counties of Akobo, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol, and Fangak within Jonglei State have suffered the brunt of floods destroying livelihoods of communities, killing animals, damaging houses and public infrastructure at devastating levels. This has been further worsened by the incidents of insecurity and violence that have together led to displacements that worsened the situation especially for children, women, and girls by increasing their vulnerabilities. The influx of returnees from Khartoum who have been repatriated to their counties have added more burden to the host community due to limited resources. It is a fact that the government is not able to cater for the needs of the people as all sectors are not adequately financed and supported with many civil servants unpaid, or low irregularly paid that has led to high attrition rate among government employees. The agriculture sector has totally been abandoned hence food for the population are imported from neighboring countries. There is, therefore, need to support vulnerable groups such as children, women, and girls through provision of lifesaving FSL interventions to leverage their efforts in surviving the various shocks that they face.

2. Project activities

Aid Link Organization (ALO) is a lead implementing partner in all the selected counties providing lifesaving FSL services with good community network and understanding of the social cohesion. ALO ensures support to the community are delivered with extreme care that protects vulnerable community from any form of vice such as SEA. In reference to the needs identified ALO proposes to implement activities that aligns to the FSL cluster objectives and strategic objectives that serves the vulnerable groups in the selected IPC 4 counties. The activities shall save lives and improve the livelihoods of the vulnerable groups. The activities are: CLFSC/CO2Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. • Provide 84160 people with agricultural inputs/vegetable & fishing kits• Vaccinate/treat 2,000 livestock in the targeted locations to support the livelihoods of the vulnerable groups. • Establish 35 kitchen gardens Nutrition facilities/Schools/Household to support self-production of food especially for the caregivers of children in the nutrition program. CLFSC/CO3Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes• Conduct capacity building/training of 800 farmer groups on improved livelihood productivity, market development, Anticipatory Actions.
