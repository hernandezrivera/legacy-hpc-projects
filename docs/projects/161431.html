Project Summary

To protect livelihoods of vulnerable rural and urban populations and improve food security through the provision of livelihood support during the main planting and dry season (crop and vegetable seeds, tools, fishing kits, livestock vaccines and drugs), and support food security information, analysis and coordination.

1 - Needs

The proportion of population in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity during the 2019 lean season (54% of the population) is lower than the number of people facing acute food insecurity during the same period in 2018 (59% of the population). This is mainly driven by reduced insecurity which has enabled households to access markets, livelihood options and humanitarian assistance. However, high levels of acute food insecurity still persist. In the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projection period of September to December 2019, the food security situation is expected to improve with the harvest, though four counties are still expected to remain in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). However, during the January to April 2020 projection period, the food security situation is likely to start deteriorating with 14 counties expected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4).Key DriversFood availability: after the signing of the revitalised peace agreement in September 2018, there is slight improvement in the security situation in the country, which has encouraged voluntary returning of some farmers to their villages and accessing far fields. This has contributed to the cultivation and planting of more fields, which may contribute to increasing production.Access to food: The ongoing economic crisis, lack of agricultural produce to sell at the peak of the lean season, depleted household asset base and limited employment opportunities continue to constrain availability of income for most households. Erosion of household purchasing power, subsequent reduced demand for commodities, limited market functionality, and degradation of road networks in the rainy season are a set of factors that do not provide much incentive for traders – thus resulting in markets characterized by low supplies and high food prices. During the IPC projection periods, availability of harvests provides access to income from agricultural sales and also leads to a reduction of food prices as supplies in the markets increase.Food utilization: chronic nature of waterborne diseases, low use of latrines, poor personal hygiene and living environments, and limited access to soap for hand washing, along with poor access to health services lead to high incidences of diseases that not only affect the health of the population, but also negatively affects availability of labour and leads to reduced income at household level.Stability: insecurity, unfavourable weather conditions such as floods and dry spells; instability and diminished activity in the markets as a result of degraded infrastructure because of the rains are likely to cause food shortages and lead to an increase in food prices; limited employment and income generating activities in the current context of economic collapse will also limit households’ consistent ability to access adequate food.

2 - Activities

In the planting seasons:• Distribute 450,000 crop kits (maize, sorghum, cowpea and tool)• Conduct seed fairs reaching 85,000 households (510,000 people)• Distribute 485,000 vegetable kits (5-7 types of vegetable seed and tool)• Distribution 200,000 fishing kits (hooks, monofilament, 2 twine)• Training of farmers In the dry season:• Distribute 200,000 vegetable kits (5-7 types of vegetable seed and tool)• Distribution 200,000 fishing kits (hooks, monofilament, 2 twine)• Training of farmers Throughout the year where needed• Distribute 100,000 rapid response kits consisting of amaranth, collard, okra, tool and/or fishing kit dependent on needs• Vaccinate 7,000,000 livestock• Treat 1,000,000 livestock• For food security information, collection, analysis, and coordination• Support crop assessment, seed security assessment• Develop crop watch bulletins• Support food security and livelihoods cluster coordination

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Priority is given to the most vulnerable, including displacement-affected communities. Where IDPs are not willing or able to return home and have been given (temporary) land by the government, FAO will assist them with seeds and tools, as well as training.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The voucher-based interventions will feed into community systems by involving local seed producers, farmer groups and nurseries. In addition government/Ministry of Agriculture are involved in the early stages of sensitisation of projects.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with national objectives, including social protection, national refugee return framework.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Following the signing of the revitalised peace agreement in September 2018, there is slight improvement in the security situation in the country, which has encouraged voluntary returning of some farmers to their villages and accessing far fields. This has contributed to the cultivation and planting of more fields, which may contribute to increasing production. In light of any risks or unintended consequences of the environment in regard to protection, human and security element on targeting households at high risk, FAO and our partners work with various UN agencies through coordination and partnership, including inter-sector cluster groups. Accordingly, harmful effects are likely to occur when assistance is not well targeted to the most food insecure households, and when the local market is relatively poorly integrated with broader national and regional markets. FAO especially work with WFP on synergies as a safety net to mitigate those unintended consequences. The implementation of the ELRP is in line with the FAO Human Rights Based Approach on the ‘’Right to Food’’ and the seven principles on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP). FAO implementing partners continue to receive training on protection from sexual exploitation and sexual abuse (PSEA) and AAP to ensure protection and accountability to beneficiaries across programme activities, in line with good AAP practices.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

FAO's relationship with implementing partners has the aim of establishing more durable collaborations on the ground and building the capacity of service providers. This is to enable empowerment of community based actors and community members. At the same time, FAO is providing training directly to beneficiaries on best agronomic practices, pest management, and support community networks of extension farmers and community animal health workers to improve livelihoods as well as local knowledge

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

FAO’s project designs and implementation make use of community committees that gather representatives, including individuals with disabilities, who are not literate, women and youth representatives, among others, to participate and engage in the project. FAO provides training to implementing partners on how to mobilise and sensitise communities to allow for truly participative approaches. FAO is increasingly creating collaborations across thematic areas to ensure they can refer to cases of GBV or other to the appropriate services. FAO operates and implements its projects in areas that are relatively secure, i.e. where there is no active fighting and mines have been cleared by UNMAS, to ensure beneficiaries and staff are not put in danger. There is a potential risk of dispute over land/water, however FAO works very closely with local communities to ensure host communities, IDPs and returnees that are assisted have been provided land for planting and have access to adequate water sources. FAO also ensures to target vulnerable beneficiaries from across the categories (host, refugee, returnee, IDP) to mitigate community tensions surrounding resources. FAO also targets beneficiaries and communities country wide, not just priority areas, in part to avoid creating pull factors that lead to further displacement. FAO’s projects are implemented to avoid putting women in any danger. Distribution points are located close to their homes and during daylight hours to minimize the risk of exploitation. In addition, Emergency livelihood kits have been designed to be lightweight, enabling women to transport them easily. FAO’s vegetable kits enable women and their families to have increased access to nutritious food sources, while cultivating vegetables close to home.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

FAO is formally signed-up and committed to the IASC Commitments on Accountability to Affected People (AAP). These aim to produce quality humanitarian and development programs by maximizing the dignified and active involvement of communities in all aspects of programs that affect their lives mostly on how power Imbalance can be reduced. FAOSS has a functional feedback mechanism including suggestion boxes, feedback desks, toll free hotline number, AAP focal points that are trained and employed by FAO in each county. The various options for feedback and complaints are expected to facilitate access to the system to ensure the most vulnerable feel safe to speak up. FAO’s project designs and implementation make use of community committees that gather individuals with disabilities, who are not literate, women and youth representatives, among others, to participate and engage in the project. FAO is working with UN PSEA Task force on community feedback mechanism (CBCM). The NGO forum (national and International) is part of the PSEA task force on PSEA. They benefit from the CBCM set by the taskforce. All Implementing partners (IP) are regulated under a Letter of agreement with FAO that include PSEA and training on PSEA and corruption specifically. FAO also support at cluster level PSEA and anticorruption training/awareness. FAO officers and M&E staff monitor such aspects closely.
