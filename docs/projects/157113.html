Project Summary

According to HRP 2019, the project will focus on Outcome 2; to improved access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services for 33,700 vulnerable protected IDPs disagregrated by gender and age in 3 localities of Kadugli, Abujebaha, Alabassya in South Kordofan and Nyala in South Darfur State for duration of 12 month.
We would contribute to improve, expand and sustain access to safe drinking water for 16250 persons, 3900 households for Safe sanitation facilities and 33700 persons for improved hygiene knowledge and practice disaggregated by gender and age with 97% of target population are protracted IDPs. ASSIST will adapt the following approaches;
• Operation and maintenance of 15 hand pumps and upgrade 5 hand pumps into mini water yards, provision of spare parts, 6 toolkits, formulation and trained 8 water user committees, introduce tariff to support in O&M and promote sustainability of water supply.
• Support installation Solar system to power water yards; conduct training of solar operators.
• Integrated Water resources Management, by organizing education, outreach sessions in schools, and community based lectures, Reforestation and watershed rehabilitation, water quality monitoring, schools mobilization and involvement and organize voluntary cleanup campaigns.
• CLTS approach used to improve sanitation practices; communities will facilitate to conduct their own appraisal and analysis of open defecation and design a plan of action to reduce open defecation and scale up HHs latrines, construction of 300 latrines.
• CATS approach; used to address communities participation in digging 200 latrines pits and fencing, ASSIST will provide subsided slab and cement concert while support the most vulnerable IDPs through; construction of 150 shared HHs gender sensitive latrines for a persons with disability and elderly.
• Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation transformation; will used to improve hygiene behavior practices among IDPs and vulnerable host community through 4 PHAST training and training of 100 hygiene promoters female and male, organizing 80 hygiene awareness and 150 solid waste management awareness session and clean up campaigns and celebration Global hand washing day.
• Cash Based Intervention; will introduced for women and girls of reproductive age to distribute hygiene kits through Cash voucher.
Accountability to affected population, will be part of approaches to ensure active participation of communities to improve information sharing, communication and feedback. ASSIST will ensure sharing information about project in a timely, accessible and inclusive way that to enable beneficiaries to play an effective role in decisions making, moreover AAP will ensure beneficiaries participation in all project cycle, also will introduce complaints and response mechanisms, to ensure feedback, including prevention of sexual abuse and exploitation.
ASSIST will collaborate and work closely with HAC and WES and other stakeholder to undertake the supervision WASH activities, and to ensuring an equitable provision of services for IDPs and vulnerable host communities.
ASSIST will promote role of women through raise awareness for local authorities and community leaders to empower and encourage women, girls and youth to participate in water user committees to be main part of decision-making process. The prolonged protracted situation of the IDPs have resulted to excessive tree cutting for shelter construction and wood worsen by the expansion of desert resulted to depletion of forestry resources making it difficult to access materials for latrine construction and nearest access point is 10-15 km, and reduced water table levels to an average of above 50 meters. Therefore, ASSIST propose to Use of Solar system to power water yards alternative to use diesel fuel, and use soil stabilize block for latrine wall construction instead of wood and grasses.

Needs

According to the inter-agency assessment conducted in South Kordofan State between June 2017 and May 2018 for IDPs, returnees and host community in Kadugli, Abujebiha, Alabassya locality respectively, the findings indicate that, more than 14 hand pumps are non-functional, 7 water yards are broken down and hand dug well is the major source for drinking water, limited water storage facilities, women and girls remained at constant cycle of water collection which have negatively affected girls attending schools, women health and exposure to insecurity risk. 85% of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community practice open defecation, lack of basic hygiene knowledge, promoters and access to soap. 99% of the target persons have no knowledge of risk associated lack of hand washing with soap, women and girls of reproductive age have basically no hygiene materials. In remote areas assessed 1 jerry can of water (20 litters) cost 40 SDG, a bag of cement is SDG 450 and Iron rod SDG 900 and piece of Soap SDG 35 which makes access safe drinking, safe sanitation facility construction unaffordable for the most vulnerable target population and vulnerable women and children headed households are among the waste affected. The Limited purchasing power of vulnerable households further expected to affect their ability to access Safe water, sanitation and hygiene services which could negatively affect the nutrition, health, food, protection of women and girls among the vulnerable household.According the WASH baseline survey for Nyala Locality conducted Oct, 394 households were interviewed.The household access to safe water source within surveyed communities was found to be reasonably fair at 52.3% and (22.1%) public/stand pipe sources. Unsafe water sources was stated by 48.9% for (open well) and 14.1% from wadi. The average water consumption is 15.4 liters per person during rainy season and 22.2 L/P/d during dry season. Since more than 52.3% of the households are accessible to safe water sources, more interventions aimed at improving existing water sources or constructing new water sources are needed, building capacity of the WASH Committees. The proportion of HHs accessible to temporary latrines was 47%, improvement in the sanitation aimed at increasing the proportions of households accessible to latrines are recommended. To enhance sustainability, latrines with concrete slab should be constructed and the households should be encouraged to embrace the practice of transferring the slab to new latrine when the present are full. The critical times for hand washing practice are relatively low at 47 %, 44% for variable (before both preparing food and eating) respectively. Hand washing practice after defecation and after handling a child who has defecated was said to be at (21%, 10% respectively, arguably the most important time to wash ones hands to prevent spread of diarrhea. Safe disposal of child’s stool was confirmed by 45.2% of the respondent caregivers.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Environment impact that may results from project implementation includes; All pit latrines will pollute the surrounding soil. This is only significant, however, if the pollution enters a potable and usable groundwater source in sufficient concentrations will make the water dangerous to drink without being (expensively) treated. Pollution from latrines takes two forms: bacterial and chemical. Bacterial pollution, although a direct health hazard, is usually quickly rendered. Household’s latrines construction causes excessive tree cutting for shelter and wood for latrine slab fitting and worsen by the expansion of desert will result into depletion of forestry resources which will make it difficult to access materials for latrine construction with the nearest point access point 10-15 km. Mitigation measures suggested;For Sanitation; ASSIST together with IDPs and host community will select soil type that composes of gravels, coarse and medium sand that is moist, soil and will not stick together with high infiltration rate 50 liters/m2/day. Secondly, Pit latrines, which will be properly designed, located, and constructed because it is relatively cheap, easy to operate and maintain, can be improved regularly, produce minimal health and environmental hazards, and can be managed by local communities. The pit latrine can be use dispose off execrate and sullage, which will avoid sullage disposal in an open to drain which will reduce the diverse effects on flat and poor drained land that will prevent our target beneficiaries being exposed to significant health and environmental hazards such as flies, mosquitoes, vermin infections and of fensive odours.Secondly, Water upgrading, ASSIST adapt use of Solar system to power water yards alternative to use diesel fuel, training of water user community in integrated water resource management eg. Watershed management and reforestation, introduce concrete cement poles instead of tree woods/poles, and use soil stabilize block for latrine wall construction instead of wood and grasses.
