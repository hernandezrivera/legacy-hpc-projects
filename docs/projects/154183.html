Project Summary

There are approximately 170,922 displaced people, comprised of 102,335 registered people and an estimated 68,587 people who are not registered estimated people, who have been living in unsafe conditions in the Lake Chad region since 2015. In addition to this, there are also some 11,264 individuals newly arrived in the region (2,252 households); comprised of 7,984 displaced people and 3,280 returnees and 680 impacted host populations who are in need of support. The needs of displaced and host households continue to be only partially addressed. Priority needs continue to be access to nutrition (the baseline studies and PDMs have shown that 80% of the money received by households via Oxfam cash transfers during 2017 is being spent on food) and drinking water (there is access to drinking water across only 52% of the region).
In addition to this, there is an urgent and pressing need for protection among displaced, returnees and host people, ahead of receiving support and care, in order to prevent violence and save lives: lack of/limited knowledge of support services for protection cases, also linked to a gap in coverage for such services. Moreover, 90% of the women asked in the gender analysis report said they were lacking basic feminine hygiene products, especially underwear and reusable cloths for use during menstruation. Sexual violence constitutes 22% of the protection incidents documented between January and June 2017 (a total of 1,428 protection incidents); hence the need to strengthen community protection/prevention mechanisms and implement mitigation measures
