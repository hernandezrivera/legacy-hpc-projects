Project Summary

The hereby action aims at providing life-saving and life-protecting assistance to vulnerable Syrian in Eastern Ghouta, rural Hama and Dara’a, responding to acute needs in the Protection sector alining itself with the fifth objective of the protection sector. As, the eight-year long conflict in Syria continues to have a devastating impact on every child across the country as 5.7 million of children across Syria are still in need of Child Protection (2020 HNO). All targeted governorates have been identified as relevant for intervention, with regards to the needs identified and to TGH operational capacities. Moreover, those governorates might face new emergency needs, such as population displacement and returns. The targeted areas have long been underserved by humanitarian actors, due to insecurity and access constraints.
The proposed intervention was designed according to the Child Protection (CP) needs identified during TGH Protection current projects, team field visits and assessments. Locations were pre-selected on the basis of objective criteria i.e. specific relation with the conflict, level of needs, rank on UN severity scale, presence of other agencies, links with the community, etc. TGH engaged in the assessment of needs in the shortlisted locations, focusing on different sectors of intervention for which it has already developed relationships with line ministries and other stakeholders - namely WASH, Shelter, Education, Protection and Livelihood. The needs assessment included the use of HH and key informant interview. The questionnaire allowed appraising the targeted communities’ vulnerability with regards to various topics and indicators, whether they were directly linked with the action or not. For the three areas, the lack of education services brings in protection issues such as child labor and early marriages, especially for girls, therefore raising gender concern.
The community-based approach in each locality will consist in selecting a committee from local youth and adults (Community Based Child Protection Committees: CBCPC) that will be in charge of collecting data, implementing and raising awareness about the protection issues. The selected committee will be identifying in coordination with TGH specialized Case Workers (CW), the children in need of CP services. For every targeted child, a complete case diagnosis and management (from case file closure or referral to other specialized services according to the child needs) will be done. The project includes components aiming at strengthening the resilience of the targeted communities through their inclusion in the activities and capacity building and increased awareness of all actors. Psychosocial support (PSS) session will be ensured through an approach that ensures the sustainability of the service, by giving facilitators from the communities the skills necessary to conduct these activities.Noteworthy is that, although not fully developed under this sector specific project description, the targeted locations have also been selected to be part of a more comprehensive response. They are thus also targeted by WASH, Education and Livelihood activities under other projects. In an integrated approach, training of teachers on child protection and PSS activities that will take place in school will be ensured trough Education sector activities. The synergy ensured by TGH between its several sectors of intervention aims at increasing the impact of the Protection activities described below. These projects also represent important entry points within the communities, where conducting sensitive projects on CP need a deep relationship with communities.
Gender is mainstreamed in the design of the action, which will ensure an equal access to the provided services to women, men, girls and boys.

4 - Needs

The needs have been assessed in the course of the implementation of TGH projects in Hama, Rural Damascus and Dara’a. This geographical coverage allowed gathering information from local stakeholders as well as through field observations from which the list of potential interventions has been drawn. Communities directly expressed their needs during the following assessment and as such, remain involved in the design of TGH project. The crisis environment has strained the capacities of families and communities to care and protect children. Psychosocial distress is reported in 42% of surveyed households (HRP 2020) demonstrating the cumulative toll on child and adult wellbeing with immediate, and if not addressed, life term consequences. This has been confirmed in the frame of the CP services TGH is currently delivering in Hama and Rural Damascus. For e.g. 73% of the interviewees declaring that children suffer from anxiety and fear. Psychosocial destress, physical violence on children from their parents or caretakers, early marriage and child labor are the main risks on CP reported to TGH case workers. Based on focus group conducted and interviews of specific groups of vulnerable people, TGH identified children as particularly exposed to the effect of conflict and displacement. The main needs identified are related to children at high risk of being OoS, to suffer from abuse, to be forced to live in the street, with an important proportion of child labor. However, women and girls are also at risk and significantly vulnerable especially when single headed household. In addition, as mentioned, conflict affected people in rural areas are suffering from a limited access to assistance and basic services. This situation is worsened by the inadequate presence of protection actors. Community structures are in need of support to be able to perform their protection functions. Indeed, in 41% of the interviews the person declared that CP concerns are rarely taken care of.

5 - Implementation of activities

Act 1. Community-based child protection for girls and boys is available in targeted locations 1.1: Community-based Child Protection for girls and boys To favor the identification of children facing CP issues, TGH decided of a community-based approach. TGH will set up CBCPC. After being trained, this committee will participate to CP and PSS activities with TGH staffs.1.2: Awareness campaigns on CP conducted by community and supported by TGHTGH will support the CBCPC members in the organization of awareness campaigns through various modalities such as door to door conversation. Act 2: Specialized child protection services for girls and boys are available in targeted locations 2.1: Case management and referral of identified cases to specialized services or other relevant assistance providers Case management process starts with a diagnosis established between the child and the CP personnel who run a case review and determine a case plan. TGH will refer the identified cases to the relevant service whenever available or address issues itself if applicable. The case will be followed by TGH CP personnel and the child’s situation is assessed regularly to assess his progression.2.2: PSS directed to children in needNon-structured PSS will be the first step enabling TGH to support children traumatized by the conflict. In a second phase, the activities will be age and gender sensitive and scheduled with clear aims such as children cognitive well-being. Act 3: Strengthen human resource capacity to respond to child protection concerns3.1: Strengthen HR capacity to respond to CP concerns. TGH will provide trainings to CBCPC members to increase their capacities. The training will introduce participants to Child rights, explain them how to identify signs of abuse, etc. M&E: TGH uses the CPIMS form commonly used in other countries for case management follow-up and monitoring. In addition, results of small-scale surveys and focus groups will be organized.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A) Content of awareness is not fully adapted to the social and cultural environment/ existing traditional practices conflict with CP principles. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: The community-based committee will create and support the process to undertake explanation to the community and foment acceptance of the intervention and ensure its sustainability. B) Risk of creating/perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families (likelihood: Medium; Impact: High) and tension within the family, including risks to physical violence (likelihood: Medium; Impact: High). Mitigation: Ensure consultation (before and after the activities) with potential beneficiaries and targeted individuals for proper design. Conduct family level analysis to provide a family based comprehensive response. C) Inappropriate response to individual cases which can lead to further harm to the child. Likelihood: Low; Impact: Medium Mitigation: TGH case workers will receive capacity building on case management and will be the key resource persons to act as facilitators along with the CP committees. Strict confidentiality rules on the cases managed will be ensured (selection of proper locations to conduct activities; protection of cases files etc.). D) Exploitation or abuse by staff members, service providers. Likelihood medium; Impact: high.Mitigation: TGH will request at least 2 years of experience and recommendation letter from previous employers to make sure of the quality of the services and preserve child safe environment. TGH will organize information and awareness session for staffs, volunteers and contractors before any activity at school to embed culture of child protection and safety to all actors. A child safety code of conduct will be signed by all actors involved in the implementation of the project. TGH field-based team will also organize a close monitoring of the work done by contractors.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Security level does not deteriorate to an extent that prevents prevent staff to be deployed. No major change of the current balance leads to major upheavals. No major security incident targeting staff occurs. TGH authorization to operate in Syria and field acess remains granted. Syrian laws ruling humanitarian organizations remain favourable. Risks: (A) Suspension of TGH authorization to operate (B) access to area is suspended due to military operations or attacks (C) Access to beneficiaries is limited by local authorities (D) New population displacement in relation to conflict (E) Security incident targeting staff (F) Attempts from backlisted entities to bid for services, goods or work contracts (G) Sexual harassment from individuals in relation with action (H) Attempts to fraud and corruption by staff (I) Diversion of the provided assistance bringing in inequality towards the assistance (J) MoE confirms the restriction imposed to PSS activities in schools. (H) Inflation doesn’t allow the organization to implement all activities with the allocated budget.Mitigation: A: Maintaining of good relationships with national counterparts. B: Targeting of various regions to allow adjustments. C - Local authorities’ openness to individual selection of beneficiaries proven through previous TGH programs D - Enforcement of TGH security procedures and training if staff on these relates to risk E - Systematic check of potential suppliers and contractors through FinScanF- Endorsement by all personnel of TGH anti-abuse policy & strict enforcement of this policy G- Endorsement by all personnel of TGH anti-fraud policy & whistle-blowing mechanism H- Adoption of systematic cross-checking mechanismsI- Early contacts with relevant stakeholders prior to the implementation of the action Involvement and empowerment of community members J- Continuous advocacy with the MOE (H) Monitoring of prices and co-funding with other project.
