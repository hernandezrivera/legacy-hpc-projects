Project Summary

The HNO of 2023 estimates that 3.4 million, comprising of 99% children below 17 years of age need education services in 2023, the number in need for education in 2024, is likely to be more than the figure for 2023. The education sector has been severely affected by a number of factors that have negatively impacted leaning. Insecurity led to school closures and limited access to learning facilities. Flooding has affected the school infrastructure destroying learning materials including the temporal learning spaces (TLS)’ previously established. Over 190 schools were destroyed by the flooding water. Integrated Rapid Need Assessment (IRNA) reports from both Fangak and Koch Counties conducted in August and December 2021 respectively indicate massive flooding and destruction of school infrastructure across the counties. Poor renumeration, delayed salaries and incentives has led to high teacher absenteeism and turn over. Inadequate learning spaces, inappropriate facilities for children with disabilities, untrained teachers and lack of learning materials also hamper learning. With crops destroyed by the flooding and in other instances limited access to farms as a result of violence reduces crop production with negative impacts on food security. There is a direct linkage between food security and access to education. Without food at home, formal education becomes secondary need as community adopts negative coping strategies including hunting wild food which limits school time or relocation which limits access to education.
Based on these needs, World Relief seeks to intervene in seven counties, with the aim of mitigating these challenges and building resilience within the Education sector. This project will target and support 5,212 learners in Early Childhood Development (ECD), and Primary and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) Centers, 48 teachers and 20 school management committee members (SMCs) in Koch and Leer counties. The intervention will ensure children out of school, internally displaced persons (IDP) and host community children, adolescents/youth aged 3-17 years have equitable access to safe and protective learning opportunities. This will be done through establishment and rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces, latrines and handwashing facilities in the project sites. The project will aim to establish/rehabilitate temporary learning spaces (TLS). The project will also conduct back-to-learning campaigns, train and provide incentives to teachers, and integrate nutrition in education through school feeding programme to encourage learning.
Coordination with County Education departments and local authorities will be a key implementation strategy. WRSS will also involve stakeholders through the Parents Teaches Association (PTA) and directly with community to receive feedback and improve programming. This intervention will work with protection partners to develop community complaints and feedback mechanism as well as a referral pathway. This will improve access to education by the most vulnerable segment of leaners including Children Living with Disabilities (CLwD), girls, adolescents and survivors of gender-based violence (GBV).

1. Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) of 2023 estimated that 3.4 million, comprising of 99% children will need education services in 2023, the number for 2024 will be much higher. Education Needs Assessment (ENA) conducted in 2021 notes that the education sector has been severely affected by a number of catastrophic factors that have negatively impacted learning. Insecurity and communal conflicts and violence, caused destruction of school infrastructure creating limited access to learning facilities. Flooding has persisted in many parts of South Sudan for three consecutive years creating negative impact on Education, affected the school infrastructure including TLS, which had been previously established. The HNO of 2022 noted that over 190 schools have been destroyed by the flooding water has not been done. Integrated Rapid Need Assessment (IRNA) report from Koch County conducted in December 2021 indicated massive flooding and destruction of school infrastructure across the counties. The ENA (p. 36-37) found that there has been a decline in back to learning enrolment due to a sharp increase in protection risks, particularly for girls, as well as economic pressure on families. Economic pressure in the wake of the Covid-19 pandemic triggered food insecurity across South Sudan, which contribute to irregular attendance and dropout rates, and impact learning as children arrive at school hungry. Another factor noted in ENA report is the issue of poor and or delayed salaries and incentives which led to high teacher absenteeism and turn over. Inadequate learning spaces, inappropriate facilities for children with disabilities, untrained teachers and lack of learning materials also hamper learning. Similarly, flooding and violence negatively affected the food security situation. With crops destroyed by the flooding and in other instances limited access to farms as a result of violence reduces crop production with negative impacts on food security. All the target counties for this project are classified as IPC 3+ with Fangak County classified as IPC 4 with pockets of IPC 5. There is a direct linkage between food security and access to education. When children are not able to have sufficient food at home, education becomes a secondary need. School enrolment and attendance drops drastically as children are also involved in searching for food and other negative coping strategies. The HNO of 2023 noted lack of proper education infrastructure, including gender-segregated WASH facilities, as one of the key barriers to access safe education services, affecting mostly girls. An estimated 17,030 classrooms are operating under trees or in open air, exposing children to protection risks and harsh weather conditions. Almost 63 per cent of primary schools are without any latrines and 52 per cent have either limited or no access to safe drinking water. 108 According to the Education Needs Assessment (ENA), more than 60 per cent of these latrines are inaccessible to students with disabilities. WRSS has been involved in Education supporting learners in ECD, Primary and ALPs. Until 2020, WRSS was running 21 schools in Rubkona County with an enrolment of 16,658. In Koch County, Unity State, there were 18 schools with a total enrolment of 2,582. In Fangak County, Jonglei State, WRSS had 21 schools with an enrolment of 5,758. In Guit County Unity State, there were 11 schools with enrolment of 2,352. From 2021 to 2023 WRSS supported the following: 897 learners in primary, 400 in secondary education; 784 in adult education and 250 boys and girls in Second Chance education in Koch County. WRSS has maintained contact with County authorities including County Director of Education, RRC and County Commissioners. County authorities have continually raised above needs and requested WRSS to intervene in Education sector. Without this intervention, the future of children in these counties remain bleak.

2. Project activities

Activities will be implemented in line with the three Education Sector objectives. Sector Objective 1: Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 865,920 emergencies affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys). Activities will include; (i). Provide temporary learning spaces (TLS), semi-permanent structures that are safe, inclusive and gender sensitive. (ii). Provide gender-segregated and accessible WASH facilities (latrines, hand-washing facilities, girls' change rooms) for girls and boys. (iii). Carry out community mobilization targeting out of school children and youth to improve access to education. (iv) Enrollment of boys and girls in ECD. (v) Enrollment of boys and girls in Primary schools, Secondary schools. (vi) Enrolment of boys and girls and youth in alternative education services. (vii) Distribute sanitation/hygiene kits for boys and girls in schools to prevent communicable diseases. (viii) Provide dignity kits. (ix) Conduct community engagement and awareness raising sessions for inclusion of girls in education. (x) Provide assistive devices and equipment for children with disabilities. (xi) Provide school meals for learners. (xii) Implement school gardening programs. Under the Sectoral Objective 2: Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle. The activities will include: (i) Provide emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials. To achieve this, the project will integrate child friendly spaces (CFS) into schools. These CFS’ will have school clubs that include girl guides and scouts for boys. They will have drawing and art competitions. (ii) Provide teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary, and secondary school education. (iii) Train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning processes for emergency-affected children. (iv) To retain teachers in school and enhance access to learning, the project will provide an incentive and support package to all teachers starting from ECD centers, Primary schools and teachers under the ALP centers. (v) Train teachers and learners on PSS, Child Protection, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition Sector. (v) Support school inspection and supervision in crisis affected areas support. (vi) Support second chance education. Objective 3. Strengthen/enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national/local education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities. Activities will include: (i) Train School management Committees (SMCs)/PTA and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change. Support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate IEC materials. (ii) Support back to school learning campaigns to mitigate effects of food insecurity on education and encourage learners to stay in school especially during the lean period, this intervention will include a school feeding program. Through this intervention, learners will be provided with fortified meals during lunch. This feeding will be managed by the PTA on a day-to-day basis. To build resilience, each school will have a kitchen garden to train both PTA and school children on vegetable growing. Vegetables harvested will supplement school meals. to mitigate Protection concerns, WRSS will establish a community complain and feedback mechanism by placing a suggestion box in all schools, initiate community focus group discussions. Similarly, WRSS will conduct sensitization to raise community awareness of systems in place for representation of their different interests and needs.
