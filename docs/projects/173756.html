Project Summary

The crisis situation in the North West and South West (NW/SW) Regions continues to deteriorate with overwhelming consequences on the livelihoods of affected families and host communities.
In total 245,911 people received food security assistance in August 2020 in the NWSW regions. In the Northwest and Southwest regions, where agricultural production was lower than average for four consecutive years due to ongoing socio-political conflicts, this year's harvests are running out earlier than usual. Due to lower than average harvests in July 2020, poor households in the regions’ most affected by the conflict are already nearly out of stored food. This places them in a Crisis (IPC Phase 3) food situation earlier than usual. Agricultural revenue remains lower than average, despite sales of grains and cash crops such as coffee and cacao. IDPs and host communities are identified to be food insecure and this situation will probably get worse as current food assistance provided is inadequate compared to the fast-increasing needs of the affected populations. The current people in need (PIN) identified under the physical and mental well-being humanitarian consequence is significantly higher than the 330,000 from last year (2019) which is mainly due to the deteriorating security situation with many weeks of imposed lockdowns, lack of access to farmlands, continuous population displacements to almost inaccessible locations in search of protection and/or security. The security situation in the NW/SW Regions remains fragile and precarious and the IDPs in hard to reach areas remain the prioritized group of people to receive humanitarian assistance. Increase in displacement of individuals means increasing vulnerabilities amongst them and their host families. Markets are either non-functional or overstretched in some areas leaving in-kind assistance as the option for humanitarian assistance. The inability of IDPs and hosts to adequate and balance food to their families has led to many children dropping out of school in order to consider other coping strategies like child labour and child farming.
Acute Malnutrition was identified during EPDA’s intervention in Bakassi which is a hard to reach area in the Ndian Division in particular but generally acute malnultrition is identified through the EFSA assessment of the food cluster.
EPDA will first of all identify and list all the malnourished children less than 5 years for acute malnutrition and treatment in three health district (HD) areas (Limbe, Muyuka and Tiko) in the South West Region. Also many IDP households and families in these divisions are increasingly being led by women as most men have been killed and many others recruited into the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs). This has made the situation more difficult for the women to provide basic need of food and shelter to their families.
The project aims at promoting social cohesion and enhancing sustainable livelihood among vulnerable IDPs and affected population in Mezam, Bui, Meme, Ndian and Fako divisions in the NW and SW regions of Cameroon as well as find ways to capture and use methods in management of acute malnutrition, to improve coverage to care for affected children.
EPDA will engage in capacity building and support with start-up materials in food farms, livestock and income generating activities to support affected IDPs, families, and host families while ensuring respect for covid-19 preventive measures.

1 - Besoins / Needs

EPDA through its WASH interventions in 2019/2020 took note of approximately 500 to 2000 children, boys, girls and women in the Ndian and Fako divisions alone who are in need of emergency nutrition and food assistance. From the Rapid emergency food security assessments (EFSA), a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 5.1% and 7.1% in NW and SW regions respectively was revealed. Analysis from Mid Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening data showed 1.2% proxy prevalence in the SW. There is a higher possibility that there are locations (hard to reach) with high nutrition and food security vulnerabilities that have not been identified. Reduced food production, disruptions of household livelihoods and increase in prices of staple food plus the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic have contributed to deteriorating food insecurity in the Fako, Ndian, Meme and mezam and Bui divisions considering that majority of affected population (rural IDP families) rely on markets and farmer supplies for adequate nutritional food intake. The displaced population and hunger-stricken households may continue to be exposed to poor food security outcomes earlier than expected. Also poor humanitarian food security and nutrition coverage are low or near absent in hard to reach areas of above targeted divisions, hence need for intervention with food farmers’ capacity building to engage in livestock and market gardening as well as income generating activities. Also implementing nutrition support program highlights the need for nutrition services delivery. The coverage of Vitamin A supplementation is going to be absolutely necessary and categorical for affected communities with little nutrition coverage. This project targets over 2,500 beneficiaries (40% men and 60% women in livelihood and agricultural support for the food security cluster program and 7,400 beneficiaries basically malnourished boys, girls and effected households (men/women) for the Nutrition Cluster program.
