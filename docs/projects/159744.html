Project Summary

Up to 2.1 million people across Somalia face severe hunger through December 2019, bringing the total number of Somalis expected to be food insecure by the end of the year to 6.3 million.
Delayed and sporadic rains during the 2019 Gu’s cropping season (April-June) coupled with low river levels resulted in the poorest cereal harvest since the 2011 famine threaten an already fragile food security situation in the country.
Climatic shocks coupled with conflict, widespread poverty and vulnerability are among the key drivers that have trapped millions of Somalis in severe hunger (IPC Phase 3, "Crisis" and IPC Phase 4, "emergency") and malnutrition.
According to the 2019 Post-Gu’ results issued on 2 September by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) and Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWSNET), cereal production was up to 70 per cent below average in the southern areas for the Gu’ 2019 cropping season. The resulting shortfall is linked to abnormally high prices of sorghum observed throughout the season.
Qatar Red Crescent Society will improve access to food to the most vulnerable urban poor and IDPs in Baidoa, Gardo, Balcad, and Afgoi. QRC will undertake activities such as Selection of 4250 drought-affected households of the vulnerable community of urban poor and IDPs chosen through registering families/HHs with the support of the local authority giving priority to Female-Headed Households (1859 for unconditional cash transfer and 1594 for conditional cash transfer and 797 Food Vouchers). A total of 25500 people will benefit from the program at the district level. Social Mobilization and community sensitization will be undertaken once; Selection of a local trustful traders/vendors through bid for Baidoa, Gardo, Balcad, and Afgoi districts and MoUs signed and providers paid; Distribution of identification of vouchers having the details of the products to be provided, The total beneficiary targeted will be 4150 Households which will translate to 25500 individuals from the vulnerable communities in Gardo, Baidoa, Balcad, and Afgoi (6885 men, 17340 women, Boys 612 and Girls 668), Strengthen the monitoring and evaluation activities in order to optimize the project implementation. The overall goal of this emergency project is to provide life-saving assistance to 4250 most affected households in urban poor and IDPs integrated into host communities in food crisis in Gardo, Afgoi, and Baidoa of Somalia through provision of conditional and unconditional cash transfers(cash and food) in order to improve food accessibility to 25500 beneficiaries (boys, girls, men and women) in Livelihood crisis in Gardo, Afgoi, Balcad, and Baidoa to prevent death due to starvation.
Food security interventions will have conditional, and unconditional and vouchers as modalities of interventions with each modality guided by market dynamics- availability of goods in the food baskets within the local markets, ability to source and prices and availability of service providers for respective modality. Unconditional transfers will target vulnerable beneficiaries- pregnant and lactating women, under five children, eldery and sickly. Conditional transfers will target labor rich but vulnerable households as well as pregnant and lactating women linked to nutrition-sensitive food security interventions for instance nutrition classes. The conditional transfer will also target the rehabilitation of community assets.
In this project, QRCS is planning to use three different modalities of unconditional cash, conditional cash, and food vouchers because it plans to target different profile beneficiaries in the same project location. For example, the unconditional cash will be targeted to female-headed households in IDPs while​ the conditional cash will be targeted to the urban poor among host communities. The food voucher will be targeted to the rural poor.

Exclusion and marginalization: - Ensure that both men and women have equal and fair access to work opportunities (including cash/food-for-work schemes), micro-credit, cash transfers, agricultural inputs, and skills training and that they receive the same benefits for their input and work.-Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to FSL needs. Different criteria may affect power dynamics.-Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant andlactating women, and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific foodand nutritional requirements and preferences for food distribution points and livelihood services.-Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response-Reinforce the capacity of the community - Encourage collective livelihood projects over those thatbenefit individuals. Collective practices contribute to community protection and resilience, particularly in rural community economies which are closely linked with social networks, community structures, and cultural practices.-Coordinate with organizations working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases. 4d. Protection mainstreaming: -Set up accessible, confidential and well-understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Please provide further explanation

Increase in the purchasing power through cash for work arrangement will enable food insecure households to access to food. This will alleviate effects of short term hunger besides addressing acute malnutrition to gain access to fundamental needs such as food due raised purchasing power. In addition, cash injection will help targeted households rebuild their asset bases following loss of entire livelihood support assets through past chronic droughts. The cash injection will not only allow the households to access immediate food but will enable them improve household purchasing power, which will boost the recovery process and therefore reverse the current unabated slide into the destitution by vulnerable populations. Similarly, as a result of cash injection through wages, there will be increased purchasing power among targeted beneficiaries. This will in turn ease strain on local social support networks, decrease deterioration of health and nutrition conditions. Beneficiaries will repay debts and in the process re-activate credit mechanisms. As a result of improved access to food in the market, there will be increased food consumption. Rehabilitation of dams will increase access to clean water for domestic and livestock use for beneficiaries .Targeted Districts has suffered water shortages for many years especially during dry seasons. In view of this rehabilitation of dams will not only increase water availability in the midterm but it will also improve the household economy by making this money to be available for other basics of life for the targeted while building the capacity of the people to face shocks. Increased income will be used to access livestock production inputs such as drugs which will save animals from common diseases. In the past, beneficiaries sell their animals during the times of distress and so when the rains come, an opportunity for livelihood recovery is missed. Hence in the medium-term, recovery of animals which will have been spared will accelerate general livelihood recovery and enhance food security and self-reliance. In addition to the direct beneficiaries, the cash injection as provided in the project budget will have a multiplier effect to benefit traders, transporters, shopkeepers and service providers in the community, thus revitalizing the local economy. The project will also benefit the larger community, as direct beneficiaries share their income with their relatives, families and communities at large. Given the current harsh socio-economic condition characterized by poverty and unemployment among other indicators, provision for food vouchers in the budget will enable poor households to access food. This will contribute to the reduction of malnutrition in the households.
