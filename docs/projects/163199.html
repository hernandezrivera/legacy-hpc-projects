Project Summary

To respond to the urgent needs being faced by women, men boys and girls particularly children and young adolescent in the North West Region of Cameroon, Plan International plans to strengthen and provide protection services through the proposed project. Plan will contribute to increased access to a protective environment by providing Child Friendly Spaces with Psycho-social Support, Case Management & referral services, to at least 15000 people in affected communities, hence contribute to strengthening the Community Based Child Protection mechanisms, prevention of and response to SGBV.

Besoins / Needs

Background• The Anglophone Crisis in Cameroon has been ongoing since Oct 2016, when violent clashes erupted between the military and armed groups fighting for separation of the predominantly English-speaking North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions from the rest of Francophone Cameroon. This has driven over 530,000 women, girls, men and boys into internal displacement, including an estimated 35% women and 40% children (UNHCR/CP AOR, Apr 2019). • Ever since, the humanitarian situation continues to deteriorate with serious protection incidents, excessive human rights abuses, and violence against civilians. Displacement is increasing with the continuous destruction of villages and infrastructure by warring parties, fueling a climate of insecurity with the brunt of the conflict affecting the general population. Despite the massive scale of the crisis and immense needs, international attention has been scarce, leading the situation to be described as “the world’s most neglected displacement crisis” (NRC, Jun 2019). • The escalation of violence has prevented schools from operating normally as parents fear for the safety of their children. Disruption of education is putting children at increased risk of exploitation, including child labour or early marriage. The groups particularly at risk are adolescent boys and girls, who are confronted with additional risk of being recruited into the NSAGs. The loss of critical years of education creates gaps around financial knowledge, life and business skills. • The escalation has also led to major destruction of villages, properties and national identity documents including birth certificates and titled deeds. One of the disheartening results is family separation, resulting in an increasing number of separated and unaccompanied children being identified.Child protection needs• Children have been particularly affected with more than 80% of schools in the region now closed (Plan International Cameroon/PIC,SitRep: Education Cluster Strategy, Aug 2019). As a result, more than 700,000 children have been out of school for nearly three years (Education Cluster, 2019), increasing their vulnerability and putting them at risk.• All children are at risk of kidnappings, which are steadily increasing in communities affected by the conflict. The majority of CP mechanisms at the community level have been broken. Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) in targeted areas are urgently in need of assistance, including alternative care and family tracing and reunification support (FTR). Family separation directly affects their access to basic services and they face exposure to violence and abuse. Children associated with armed forces and groups (CAAFAG) and those at risk of recruitment are of key protection concern due to their exposure to unrelenting violence, abuse and exploitation, stigmatization and harassment. The protection risks facing boys and girls in this crisis are extremely grave and life-threatening and require urgent attention.• In March2019, Needs assessment conducted by Plan International and CARE International in the NW and SW regions have identified significant Child Protection and GBV risks, and many abuses committed against children including attacks on children and on schools, physical and psychological violence, sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), and risk of recruitment into armed groups, child labour, exploitation and neglect. Young girls are at high risk of SGBV, including rape, unwanted pregnancies and forced prostitution. • Through the proposed project, Plan will contribute to increased access to a protective environment by providing Child Friendly Spaces with Psycho-social Support, Case Management & referral services, contribute to strengthening the Community Based Child Protection mechanisms, prevention of and response to SGBV.
