Project Summary

The aim of this project is to strengthen the child protection framework and provide a comprehensive package integrating child protection and psychosocial support in all sectors to ensure that children are protected and have access to basic services during the acute crisis.

Besoins / Needs

In Cameroon, 4.3 million people need humanitarian assistance, one in five, with women and children being the most vulnerable. . The Far North region is especially affected by the conflict in the Lake Chad Basin as well as the refugee movements and internal displacement caused. The displaced population is estimated at 423,835 individuals (262,831 IDPs, 50,981 out of camp refugees and 110,023 returnees) . The education system and therefore thousands of children are affected by the crisis in the region. The Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) of 2018 reports that 93 schools have closed in Cameroon due to the conflict in the north of the country. A total of 45,000 children are affected and do either no longer attend school at all or attend a school outside of their home community. This increases the risk of forced labour and recruitment by armed groups, as well as the risk of early child marriages. There are huge shortages in terms of school infrastructure (classrooms and sanitary facilities) and teaching and learning materials for school pupils and teachers, as well as a lack of teachers. The average ratio of school pupils per teacher in these districts is around 115 to 1; the classrooms are overcrowded, in some cases there are between 120 and 150 children in one classroom and 70% of the teachers have no educational skills whatsoever when it comes to offering psychosocial support to children who have in many cases been traumatised by the conflict. The Ministry of Basic Education, together with partners, undertakes efforts to counteract the crisis in the education sector. The protection of (refugee) children from child labour and exploitation is one of the key indicators in all measurement plans. Doing this, however, equal access to formal and informal education for (fled and displaced) boys and girls is not adequately promoted. This is most obvious in rural areas where the situation referring to equal access of younger girls and adolescent girls to school education and vocational training is observed as especially inequal/negative. The 16th round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM November 2018) identified, 245,725 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) (63%), 40,396 Unregistered Refugees (10%), and 105,906 Returnees (27%) in the Far North Region of Cameroon. It was found that almost half of them were displaced within the last 12 months and 62% of the displaced population are currently living in host communities. While host communities are willing to assist the displaced, they often lack the means to do. The department of Logone and Chari continues to host the highest number of displaced population, currently hosting 125,711 of displaced, with the department of Mayo Sava coming second with 59,506.
