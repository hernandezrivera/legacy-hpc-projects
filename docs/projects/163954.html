Project Summary

The project aims to maintain and improve equitable access to formal and non-formal education for the most vulnerable groups in Maaret Tamsrin and Afrin sub-districts and the surroundings camps. The project is targeting 3300 for formal and non-formal education activities, children aged between (5-17) years old including 1750 boys and 1550 girls with focus on providing accessibility to children and people with disability. The project also provides job opportunities for more than 90 educational actors from both genders.
IHR aiming to meet the objective of this project through six main interviews:
1-IHR will conduct BTL campaign in 4 communities at the community level and in the targeted camp. Activities will target children, community leaders and caregivers.
2-Maintain and improve access to equitable formal education for 3300 children in Maaret Tamsrin and Afrin sub-districts. Focusing on safe drinking water accessibility in schools and education centers.
Academic calendar will include providing psycho-social support sessions provided by specialists and the teachers will have service maps and train on referral mechanisms to the nearest protection and GBV centres. Taking into consideration gender sensitive WASH facilities for both students and teachers.
3- Enhancing access to equitable non-formal education for 980 out-of-school children in both communities and Camps (550 boys, 430 girls) in 7 education centres in Maaret Tamsrin and Afrin. Through this component remedial, literacy and numeracy classes are provided to school aged children to help them reengage in formal education and teachers are supported with incentive.
4-IHR will hire 2 Centres for (CwD). The centers will work on reintegration of CwD in the FE. The centres will be provided with furniture and safety equipment, including white boards, desks, equipment, etc. The main target will be children in camps.
PSS (I DEAL), recreational activities (SRA) and non-formal education. The centres will help (CwD) to be reintegrated with formal education.
5-IHR will conduct hygiene raising awareness campaigns to meet ASO1 in coordination with WASH cluster.
6- The schools and education centres will be selected in coordination with DoE and local councils in targeted areas.
IHR will repair the schools in the mentioned locations. Repairing is limited to light repair in classrooms, paint, doors, windows common spaces and WASH facilities. The WASH component will be further discussed with WASH cluster.
Educational personnel will be selected through transparent process in participation with DoE. Children and teachers with disability are encouraged to engage in education system by providing transportation. Educational actors are provided with capacity building courses to ensure providing protective and effective learning through multiple training including: INEE standards-classroom management and planning-PSS–alternatives of corporal punishment–Public safety principles/Education patterns, active learning- dealing and integration the Disabled, inclusive, gender sensitive, protective pedagogy and psycho-social support. It’s worth to mention that IHR will take into account the disabled children while designing PSS and recreational activities.
It’s worth to mention that All IHR interventions will participate in raising the community awareness about the dangers of ERWs, furthermore IHR expect that 90% of cluster objectives in reducing the impact of ERWs in project location and in the same time, IHR will target children in schools with PFA, PSS sessions.
All above activities will take in consideration integration of persons with disabled, GBV survivors.
Plan B:
The target areas are one of the safest areas in Syria, but in case of inaccessibility, IHR depending on contingency plan will move to another workplaces (Sarmada- Dana-Afrin).

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2019, 2.1 million children in Syria are out of school, 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out. More than one in three schools are damaged or destroyed, while others are used for purposes not related to education. During the past years, the education sector in the north of Syria has witnessed many changes at all levels. Despite the great efforts exerted by the directorates of education in the north of Syria, the education sector is still under construction with a lot of variables to take into consideration due to the fluctuating context. IHR team conducted the assessment study in two districts: Afrin and Maaret Tamsrin. IHR team coordinated with ED and the LC to conduct this assessment. Students 6–11 years old formed the largest age group of students in the assessed communities accounting for 58% of the total enrolled students. Female students aged 6–11 constituted 30% of the total number of enrolled students. Students aged 12-15 years constituted 28% of the total number of enrolled students and students aged 16-18 years constituted 14 % of both genders. The percentage of male students in the targeted communities represents 51 % of total students, while female students represent 49%. Respondents noted the importance of providing support for schools' rehabilitation and securing transportation for students. As for teachers, it was found that 30% have no source of income and 20% are not specialists. Thus, the students' dropout may reach 20% of the total children. The respondents suggested solutions to reduce this phenomenon, which was summarized as the following Providing the teachers' training, the establishment of no formal educational centres, schools rehabilitation, raising the parents' awareness to send their children back to schools. About 27% of students have special needs, including visual impairment (28%), mobility issues (24%), cognitive impairment (17%), hearing problems (14%), communication difficulty (11%) and self-care difficulties (6%).

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risks: Fighting between military groups in the region, military operation and shelling escalation are kind of possible security risks.Mitigation:The security situation in Maaret Tamsrin is considered to be stable compared to other areas of conflict in Syria. No direct airstrikes or shelling were recorded during the previous months. IHR maintains detailed security protocol for its field staff. The security team inside Syria are linked with other NGO teams and community stakeholders for information sharing. The decision of suspension will be taken in daily bases with up-to-date information. Schools, Education centers for CWD and education centers are all in contact with through radio contact which is maintained among the teams and security team to ensure information flow. Also, schools and education centers are provided with safety equipment used in emergency situations like firefighting equipment and first aid kits. Additional that all staff will get safety and security training to deal with emergency situations (Evacuation -first aid)It’s worth to mention that IHR developed attached contingency plan for all the target are and in case high risk sitation IHR immediately will provide cars to evacuate the team, CWD and secure new work place to continue the activities in (Dana-Sarmada-Afrin)It’s worth to mention that IHR has ongoing project in mentioned area and we have protocols with Turkish authorities to implement Education and protection project in olive branch areas Operational Risks:Risk: miscommunication due to remote management difficulties between Turkey and Syria staff which could affect implementation. probability: MediumImpact: MediumMitigation:Ensure monthly visits with monthly meeting for staff who have ability to cross to Syria whom are registered with appropriate authorities in Turkey. Face-to-face meetings can avoid many problems and close the gap between Syria and Turkey staff.Providing cars during risks will be available by IHR.
