1 - Needs

Identified needs and activities proposed by ACTED have been informed by monthly CCCM site profiling assessments conducted by ACTED as leading CCCM agency in all 11 ACTED-managed camps in Dikwa throughout 2020 and 2021; ACTED’s CCCM progress monitoring assessments carried out in in 2020 and 2021 and a joint drainage assessment conducted in June 2020 in partnership with a specialized civil engineering consultancy. Common themes highlighted by assessments findings, as well as field observations and monthly beneficiary complaints and response reports consistently included high risks of flood occurrences, as well as high impact risks as a result of flooding, increasing protection risks and overall vulnerability of IDP populations and impacting the accessibility of communal infrastructures and services. In Dikwa, ACTED’s CCCM assessments and the joint drainage assessment highlight that camp settings are particularly prone to and affected by flooding. High water retention, combined with intense and long-lasting rainfall during the rainy season, yearly from May to September, result in regular flooding of camp sites in Dikwa. In such instances, both shelters and communal infrastructures are flooded and become inaccessible. The joint drainage assessment highlighted that 60% of camp areas in Dikwa become inaccessible in the aftermath of a flood event. This impacts the mobility of IDP residents, especially for vulnerable populations, such as the elderly or persons with disabilities. It also affects the ability of humanitarian organizations to respond effectively to needs of affected households. Moreover, floodwater affects shelters, as households are regularly flooded and become inaccessible during the rainy season. Only 8% of shelters is not vulnerable to flooding, with 71% even being extremely vulnerable to flooding. As such, every year, IDPs affected by flooding are forced to leave their shelters and seek refuge beyond the trench parameters until they receive the needed support for repair and maintenance of their household. In addition to shelters, basic communal facilities such as latrines and water points are also reported to be affected by flooding: 86% of latrines and 89% of water points are reported to become ineffective following floods. This presents a serious public health risk. All assessments highlight the need to replace temporary drainages in IDP settings with more durable drainage solutions to help prevent flood occurrences. Flood occurrences pose a major challenge to the sanitation, hygiene, and living standards of IDPs in camp settings in Dikwa. This was also reflected in the 2020 MSNA, with 46% of IDPs in Borno State having both WASH and Shelter LSGs. The need for sustainable flood mitigation was once again reaffirmed in the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan, which listed improving camp conditions and services as especially relevant to Strategic Objective 1 ‘Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and protection interventions to the most vulnerable’. As such, ACTED developed drainage plans for each targeted camp site based on the joint drainage assessment. However, due to the acute lack of funding at the time these plans have not yet been realized.

2 - Activities

As the lead CCCM Agency of 11 IDP camps in Dikwa, ACTED proposes a CCCM intervention to construct concrete drainage solutions in Dikwa. The Action will directly improve living conditions amongst vulnerable IDPs by decreasing the risk of flooding in vulnerable camp zones. In line with the CCCM and S/NFI Sector objectives, the project will enable a coordinated response to critical risks in existing IDP camp sites to enable coordinated, principled humanitarian action to enhance timely, unhindered and equitable access to multi-sector assistance and protection interventions in Borno State. ACTED’s life-saving CCCM services, including critical site improvements, will provide strong coordination amongst operational actors operating in Dikwa IDP sites and strengthen rationalization of aid at camp-level to ensure pragmatic, principled, and protection-oriented programming. The Action will thereby directly respond to critical flood risks amongst IDPs currently residing in IDP camps in Dikwa town, while neatly complementing ACTED’s ongoing CCCM programming in Dikwa. The intervention will provide a strong value add through ACTED’s continued CCCM presence in Dikwa, enabling close ties to IDP populations, positive working relationships with local authorities, as well as a comprehensive understanding of the operational context. ACTED will be able to capitalize on the strong acceptance as a stable CCCM actor amongst communities and inter-agency partners, while tested and established internal referral pathways will provide a valuable opportunity to build upon past engagement and lessons learnt to further fine tune the CCCM response under the proposed grant for adaptive, evidence-based and accountable humanitarian response programming.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Key protection principles have informed the design of the project and will be actively present as a core component of implementation. ACTED will ensure that activities conducted prioritize the wellbeing, safety and dignity of beneficiaries at all times. Activities will avoid incurring any harm, will ensure meaningful access to CCCM assistance, and will promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries. All ACTED staff will uphold protection principles at all times, as signatories to ACTED’s Code of Conduct, including ACTED’s Policy on PSEA. Moreover, as a leading CCCM agency in all proposed target sites, ACTED will actively engage with GBV and protection partners to refer protection cases identified through the CRM. In order to ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across the project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: 1) Prioritize safety, dignity, and do no harm: ACTED will continuously monitor the security context in areas of intervention. ACTED’s CCCM team will ensure that community meetings and sensitization campaigns are located in safe and accessible locations and at a reasonable distance from beneficiary homes. Gender-segregated queues with mandatory COVID-19 social distancing measures and available disinfectant materials will be mandated to all camp-level communal and distribution sites. Under the CCCM component, ACTED will integrate gender and protection mainstreaming, ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups in decision-making processes. Strong referral mechanisms will be enacted, enabling direct referral of any vulnerable IDPs to relevant service providers, including victims of assault or GBV. 2) Ensure meaningful and equal access: the CCCM Team will strengthen participatory camp governance structures, facilitating direct inclusion of beneficiary groups into decision-making processes, facilitating greater equitable and meaningful access to assistance programs. ACTED will take additional steps to ensure that PWDs can be reached, as well as other vulnerable groups, and ensure that information on services and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacy. 3) Promote accountability, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries: Beneficiaries will be members of camp committees, ensuring strong participation in camp governance, channeling ideas and influencing the wider humanitarian coordination and response planning, and play a vital role in the implementation of site maintenance activities, providing foundation for community-led camp management and interventions. Daily laborers, hired to support the construction of the drainage network, will be recruited from the IDP community to provide employment opportunities, with hiring process reliant on a strict equal opportunities basis and considerate of protection and gender aspects. Accountability will also stem strongly from the CRM, ensuring timely and appropriate response to complaints. Moreover, PDM exercises will assess beneficiary satisfaction and inform further programming.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED’s established Complaints and Response Feedback Mechanism (CRM), mainstreamed throughout all ACTED-led interventions in Northeast Nigeria, facilitates safe, accessible and transparent feedback channels for affected populations. ACTED’s CRM enables beneficiaries to voice concerns, complaints and suggestions, while informing ACTED’s partner coordination efforts at camp-level and thereby increasing overall accountability of service providers and the wider humanitarian response. CRM mediums include complaints boxes, complaints desks, a telephone hotline and email. ACTED’s independent AME Unit will maintain responsibility for collecting and responding to all reports, addressing complaints through protection-centered case-management support in a timely and transparent manner. ACTED’s CRM provides an effective two-way communication channel, ensuring direct links to target populations, and inclusion of beneficiary and broader community voices in program implementation. Further, information gained through the CRM is looped back into the design of program approaches, as ACTED continuously adjusts intervention activities according to feedback received. Moreover, in ACTED-managed sites, Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) are operational. As opposed to the CRM, the CFM is for site-wide feedback related to all partner activities, rather than ACTED implementation only. This allows ACTED to gather feedback on all activities in the sites and ensure beneficiaries have an avenue to express their feedback on all site-related issues. Finally, ACTED is committed to humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and ensures the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by monitoring impact of project activities to ensure activities do not inadvertently cause tensions among targeted populations. All ACTED staff will be strictly compliant with the code of conduct, and any breach of the code of conduct will be responded to with serious measure.
