Project Summary

UNICEF will provide support to ensure equitable quality and gender sensitive maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health and immunization services as well as provision of sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services including counselling and referral for GBV and support to children, pregnant and lactating women (inclusive of children with HIV - PMTCT), with inclusion of people with disabilities. The project will focus and ensure adherence with Minimum Package of Essential Health services for primary health care in PHCs in Rohingya camps, and comprehensive newborn care support. UNICEF work with other partners both in health sector and other sectors to ensure integrated health services are provided. The project will target 485,553 direct beneficiaries (423,896, refugees and 61,657 people in host communities). The project will be implemented in 6 health facilities in the camps (6 PHCs ), and 1 CEMONC facility, in camp and strengthen newborn health care in the camp through newborn technical sub-committee coordination, government health system and health facilities in adjacent host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf (2 UHCs) and SCANU at Sadar hospital. Following the rationalization exercise in 2022 in the health sector, and further agreements with the SAG, UNICEF will continue to work in 6 PHCs, in camps 5, 8W, 10, 16, 17 and 18 to provide 24/7 maternal and newborn care. A special focus will continue to support well functional Newborn Stabilization Unit (NSU) with Kangaroo Mother care, across all Upazila health complexes, Special Newborn Care unit (SCANU) at district hospital. UNICEF will continue to play a leading role in supporting vaccine logistic and supply and ensure vaccine availability in all service provision sites in all the camps and host communities in Cox's bazar district. UNICEF will continue to provide technical support, supply and transportation of vaccines and logistic and enhance cold chain capacity and advocacy and social mobilization for strengthening immunization services across all the camps. UNICEF will ensure the HPV vaccination of adolescent girls 10 -14 years, and Td Vaccination of pregnant women. UNICEF will support the vaccination of all new arrivals children under-5 years on all antigens to ensure no zero dose children in camps. UNICEF will continue to strengthen Health Management Information System (HMIS/DHIS2/OpenSRP/OpenMRS) through deployment of technical staff to the office of Civil Surgeon and build capacity of service providers on HMIS and support the government in rolling out e-tracker to improve data collection and monitoring on immunization and maternal health services and reduce zero dose. UNICEF has developed emergency response plan to ensure better preparedness and respond to emergency and disasters including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control measures in all the supported health facilities and support scale up of vaccinations related to outbreak and responses not limited to COVID 19 vaccination program, Cholera, Hepatitis C and HPV in all camps and host communities. UNICEF will ensure risk communication and community engagement, including through CHWs, for the promotion of preventive behaviour for disease prevention and mobilize communities to increase uptake of routine immunization, capacity building of front-line workers on communication skills for the strengthening of continuum of care for health services (MNCAH) and managing disinformation related to disease outbreaks through engagement with communities and key social influencers. UNICEF intends to conduct a study on gender in immunization. UNICEF will continue to work closely with other sectors, including CP/GBV, to provide inclusive, integrated, and age-responsive GBV/SRH services including MHPSS, clinical management of rape, voluntary family planning, STI testing and treatment, and case management referral

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The situation for Rohingya refugees continues to be dire, after the marking of the 7th year since the conflict in Rakhine state that resulted in the displacement of now recorded 1 million Rohingya refugees in 33 camps in Cox’s Bazar and in Bhasan Char. Despite collaborative efforts to ensure equitable access to quality health services by the health sector partners, UNICEF and Government of Bangladesh in line with the Minimum Essential Package of Essential Health Services, various health concerns remains. An ongoing confirmed Cholera outbreak continues widespread in the camps with 249 culture confirmed cases in 27 camps. The 2024 Public Health Needs Assessment highlighted the top priorities by the Rohingya for consultation were skin diseases, acute respiratory tract infections, AWD/Cholera, GIT problems, eye diseases, dengue, and Non-communicable diseases (Hypertension, Diabetes Mellitus, Mental Health conditions). 16% were persons with disability. 93% of the population receive services from NGO health facilities with less than 1% reporting more than one hour to access a health facility but 22% have reported access difficulties in last three months. The prevalence of Hepatitis C is found to be 19.6% with limited treatment capacity due to high-cost implication. Vaccination campaigns of OPV have been done, yet with much more required to reduce zero-dose children. In maternal health, close to 51% of deliveries still take place in the community, with 37 maternal deaths reported and 370 newborn deaths as of September 2024 highlighting a quality-of-care concern. Only 10% of married women as per SRH survey 2024 have knowledge on five danger signs of pregnancy. Only 69% of PNC was provided for deliveries at facility level. 10% of women 15-49 years have experience physical violence and 6% sexual violence in past 12 months. A GBV Sub-Sector report from Q2 2024 revealed that sexual violence accounted for 10.4% of the reported GBV incidents (including 5.8% rape cases and 4.2% child sexual abuse cases), and these incidents are increasingly being reported. Intimate partner violence constituted 73.8% of all reported GBV incidents. During the period of January to September, CP sub-sector CPIMS+ report recorded 4.53% cases of child marriage.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNICEF adheres to the protection principles and ensures access to health services to all and avoid discrimination, ensure safety and dignity of the beneficiaries, meaningful access, being accountable, and ensuring participation of all groups. The human right of all the beneficiaries is protected and system are put in place to ensure that none of the population is abused, neglected, or exploited while seeking health services through training, orientation of service providers and monitoring. UNICEF work together with partners, within the sector and across sectors to prevent and respond to GBV, recognizing the systemic exposure to GBV, while helping to ensure that support is available for all survivors of sexual violence (including violence against children). UNICEF has zero tolerance for SEA and SH. UNICEF maintains environment that prevent and protect from sexual exploitation and abuse, and provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance, and support to survivors.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project will ensure that there is no discrimination with regards to age for all age groups, specifically children, and the elderly with fast-tracked mechanisms for access to health services through triage, and follow up support. Persons with disabilities will be targeted in the design, monitoring and implementation phases. Access to health facilities will ensure disability friendly ramps, and prioritization for access to services. The results of the project and achievement will be disaggregated based on age and disability. UNICEF allocates and delivers assistance based on needs and without discrimination. All staff on the project will be trained and oriented to respect and appreciate other people irrespective of their difference in terms of nationality, sexual orientation, ethnicity, race, sex, language, disability, religious belief, gender identity, political or other opinions. A workplace culture embraces diversity and there is zero tolerance for discrimination. The health facilities and services are accessible to all, and some actions have been taken to improve access and service to the people with disabilities. For example, all health facilities supported by UNICEF have ramp in the infrastructure with the availability of wheelchair. Actions have been taken to improve the latrine to be accessible (despite the difficult terrain). Community health workers have been orientated to identify and approach the PWD for further support when needed.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will ensure community members are provided adequate information on the type of health services available at the various health facilities. Appropriate feedback mechanism/system already established in all health facilities for community members to provide their feedback about the services and all concerns and complaints will be adequately and timely addressed. In UNICEF supported facilities, addressing feedback from communities is crucial, thus we established complaint and suggestion box, exit interview, and linkage with Information Feedback Centres (IFCs) in the camps. In addition, feedback from communities also taken from communities meeting led by communities/religious leaders e.g., Majhi, Imam etc. All feedbacks collected were processed (registered and reviewed) during quality improvement meeting every month. All complaints are then discussed, and the facilities will prioritize the issues to be addressed and take action.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNICEF child-centred approach to reduce disaster and climate risk and building resilience in development and humanitarian setting, both for and with children and young people. DRR and climate change mitigation measures are embedded in the programme and UNICEF ensured that partners trained and incorporated these in programme planning and implementation. UNICEF allocated funds with partners for contingency to which they can use when significant event occurred e.g. cyclone, flood, landslide, fire. This is applied since the beginning of the response to the crisis to strengthen and maintain the resilience. The project will support improvements of Health facilities to be climate resilient. Work with WHO and other sectors such as WASH to predict and respond to climate related health threats -heat waves and disease outbreak. All facilities have waste management in place and the workers received regular training as part of infection prevention and control. Where possible, solar power shall be installed along with other energy saving initiatives as part of climate-sensitive actions.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

UNICEF and it's partners have developed Emergency Preparedness plans to cater for natural (Monsoon, flooding, cyclone, typhoon) and man-made (fires) emergencies. This includes the phases of early preparedness, alert, response, and resilience phases. Standard protocols are in place to strengthen the health facility preparedness, availability of mobile teams as required, and the mobilizing of contingency resources for immediate response. There is stockpiling of medicines for emergencies, and as well required vaccination campaigns will be in place in response to disease emergencies. UNICEF works to strengthen the government's preparedness efforts and timely responses to these emergencies, with early warning and alert systems in place. Our responses to emergencies will follow a conflict-sensitivity approach, with contingency planning for new arrivals of Rohingya refugees and catering for new displacements.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

UNICEF focus on youth will be through non-discriminatory service provision to all age groups inclusive of adolescents and youth age 15-24. Adolescents will be provided safe spaces within the PHC to have life skills and health education sessions that are gender segregated for both males and females. This youth clubs will be provided with appropriate health services to meet their gender-specific age requirements such as IFA for adolescent girls
