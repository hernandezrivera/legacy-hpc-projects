Project Summary

The public health indicators are worsening due to persistent pockets of insecurity and violence, intra and inter-communal conflicts, massive and spontaneous returns, sparse basic services, disease outbreaks, climate shocks (flooding), soaring food prices, a devalued currency and chronic food shortages have left several families struggling with insecure access to food and livelihoods.
In 2020, CHADO through combined emergency clinical packages of Mobile Clinics, PHCU’s and PHCC’s in Manyo and Fashoda Counties of UNS, Akobo County, Jonglei State and Guit County of Unity State, intend to reach vulnerable population in hard-to-reach with 17,500 OPD consultations, facilitate 500 normal deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants, provide 52 quality and timely weekly EWARS/IDSR reports from the 4 focus Counties, facilitate the vaccination of 6,600 children with 3 doses of pentavalent vaccine and provide maternal healthcare services to 14,126 women of child bearing age of IDPs, Returnees and Host community including FP, ANC, PNC, IPT 1 and 2, de-worming and TT Immunization, including child care services (routine immunization, Vit A and growth monitoring) to 7,346 girls and 8,346 boys, including accelerated mass measles campaigns and polio campaign.
CHADO will provide treatment and psychosocial support to 75 mentally ill persons including referrals for specialized psychiatric treatment and provide MHPSS to 2,700 persons with disability including referrals for specialized services provided by other partners
The staff will manage 3,000 uncomplicated diarrhea cases with ORS and Zinc, manage 9,200 uncomplicated malaria cases with ACT, and manage 2,700 uncomplicated respiratory infection (pneumonia) treated with Amoxicillin.
The emergency health packages will reach 22,500 people with health education and promotion to prevent disease outbreaks integrated with community-based disease surveillance, reach 27,500 with awareness raising community engagements on GBV prevention, drug abuse, peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence and facilitate 12 coordination meetings with WASH/Protection/GBV/CP/Nutrition/FSL and Education partners to build synergies to realize holistic intervention in the four (4) focus Counties.

1 - Needs

According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC)’s projections for January - April 2020, flagged that 1.21 million people will be in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), 4.29 million people in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis). Between January to April 2020 projected period, 14 counties are expected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) including Guit, Akobo and Fashoda Counties, while Manyo County is presenting a likelihood of IPC Phase 3 (Critical). Key informant interviews, focus groups discussions (FGDs) and desk reviews were conducted as part of need assessment to inform this proposal. In the needs assessment, a number of health risks and emergency health needs for this proposal were identified based on facts. First, there will be an increased malnutrition, heightened risk illness and death for neonatal and children due to pneumonia, malaria, diarrhoea, and measles related cases. Second, pregnant women are at greater risk of complications from obstetric emergencies. Third, there will be risk of increased incidents of sexual violence and an increase in the number of people requiring help for addictions, substance abuse, mental health illnesses and psychosocial support. In addition, from the IPC January-April 2020 projections, about 1.2 million children aged 6-59 months in South Sudan are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition. Most facilities are mainly run by unskilled staff and deliveries are consequently mainly assisted by unskilled staff compounded by lack of physical access to health facilities especially during the rainy season. In Fashoda County the 2 PHCC’s and 6 PHCU’s are supported by the County Health Department (CHD) and Cordaid, with funding from World Bank/UNICEF. Population in Need (PIN) are 7,435 people (3,792 females and 3,643 males). CHADO will establish 1 Mobile Clinic and 1 PHCU to reach the under-served population in Fashoda County. In Manyo County the PIN are 38,255 people (19,510 females and 18,745 males), among them are 2,740 returnees. CHADO will establish 1 Mobile Clinic, 1 PHCC and 1 PHCU. The PHCC and PHCU are not among those been supported by Cordiad/World bank/UNICEF. In Akobo East (Walgak zone), there is 1 PHCC+ and 4 PHCU’s but no single mobile clinic for hard-to-reach communities. Therefore, CHADO is proposing 1 Mobile Clinic, 1 PHCU and 1 PHCC to strengthen referrals and treatment diseases. The PIN targets in Akobo is 60,290 people. CHADO is targeting 33% of the PIN in Akobo County 20,097 people (10,250 females and 9,847 males) among the population are 4,499 returnees. In Guit County, CHADO has operational presence as health partner (Lot 20 HPF-3 consortium member) and Education partner. Guit County has 14 registered facilities but only 4 PHCU’s and 1 PHCC is funded by the HPF-3. With the high rate of returnees from the Bentiu PoC, Juba and across the Sudan border. In Guit County, 1 PHCC and 1 PHCU’s will be supported with 1 mobile clinic. The PIN in Guit County are 43,771 people (22,333 females and 21,448 males). Among the estimated PIN in Guit, 17,160 are returnees. CHADO will work with WASH, Protection, Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) and Education partners to address their health needs. CHADO will promote good health and hygiene in schools with Education and WASH partners. With Protection cluster and gender-based violence cluster (CP and GBV), health education messaging. CHADO will work with nutrition partners in providing Nutrition Education to pregnant and lactating women (PLW) during antenatal (ANC) and expanded program on immunization (EPI) days and in (OTPs). CHADO will coordinate with FSL partners to ensure children caregivers and PLWs are provided with vegetable seeds and encourage home vegetable gardening to diversify diet. The resultant outcome is that the target communities both women, men, girls and boys, including the aged and persons with disabilities, IDPs, returnees and IDPs, will have a reduced caseload of diseases and enhanced resilience against shocks.

2 - Activities

The programmatic activities are designed to align with the Health Cluster’s three strategic health outcome objectives, namely, to : 1) reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases; 2) improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care; and 3) to increase access to services for survivors, disabled and mental health disorders. In this proposal, activities pertaining to immunization against vaccine preventable diseases, disease surveillance and outbreak response, EWAR/IDSR reporting and case management for selected diseases (i.e., integrated case management of malnutrition, malaria, diarrhea and pneumonia are aligned to the achievement of strategic outcome 1. Activities pertaining to support for static integrated facilities (i.e., hospitals, PHCCs and PHCUs), WASH in health facilities, OPD consultations, sexual and reproductive health services, access to and delivery by skilled birth attendants, are linked and aligned to strategic outcome 2. Activities pertaining to MPHSS, provision of services for gender-based violence and disability services are aligned to strategic outcome 3.Activity 1: Conduct 6,667 OPD consultations in static facilities/mobile clinics Activity 2: Facilitate 200 normal deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants Activity 3: Provide 52 timely and weekly EWARS/IDSR reports from the 4 focus Counties Activity 4: Facilitate 3,600 children with 3 doses of pentavalent vaccineActivity 5: Provide maternal healthcare services to 3,126 women of child bearing age of IDPs, returnees and host community including FP, ANC, PNC, IPT 1 and 2, de-worming and TT immunization.Activity 6: Provide child healthcare services (routine immunization, Vit A supplementation and child growth monitoring) to 1,300 girls and 1,300 boys, including accelerated mass measles and polio campaigns.Activity 7: Provide psychiatric treatment and psychosocial support to 75 mentally ill persons Activity 8: Provide community-based level 2 and level 3 (non-specialized) MHPSS to 2,700 persons living with disabilities (PLWD's), including referrals for specialized services from other partners. 8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Access to assistance is one of the main pillars in the design and implementation of the project. Quality measures will be used to quantify and track barriers hindering or limiting beneficiaries access to quality humanitarian assistance. Measurement of access will provide information on beneficiaries registered timely and having appropriate access to assistance. This may include: access to several components of the humanitarian assistance not limited to structural barriers impacting access of women, men, boys and girls. Using simple beneficiary scorecards and interviews collected randomly especially during monthly support supervision using QSC, a desk review will periodically be made to assess satisfaction of access of beneficiaries on services provided. This is identify disparities if any and the ability of CHADO to address the identified needs. The indicators are designed to capture the safety of beneficiaries in the monitoring of activities. All the activities will be utilizing the "Do no harm" approach for the local communities. Our beneficiaries will learn about the Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) strategies as part of the life-skills component so they are equipped to overcome spikes and shocks. A community safety audit will be conducted to ensure safety of women and girls while accessing health facilities and services

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CHADO will conduct patient satisfaction with services survey and / or participatory monitoring and citizen engagement with vulnerable members of the community, namely, people living with disabilities, illiterate persons, women and girls, boys and men, to obtain their feedback and make improvements. CHAOD will also use community-based complaints mechanisms such as use of local leaders like local chiefs, county authorities, religious leaders, the beneficiary’s makes up the complaint mechanism/ feedback mechanism as stipulated in CHADO transparency policy. CHADO feedback and complaints mechanisms defines the purpose and limitations of the mechanisms (e.g. steps involved in processing and responding to complaints, how to handle PSEA reports/complaints), addresses confidentiality and non-retaliation issues, is updated regularly and assigns clear roles and responsibilities. The complaints and response mechanisms, is well-constructed and operational, are means for to ensure that there is a safe and confidential way for beneficiaries to raise concerns and receive some kind of response, from everyday problems which ranges from quality of health services to sensitive complaints regarding corruption, misuse of assets, staff misconduct or sexual exploitation and abuse. CHADO complaints and feedback systems is effectively tailored to each context with particular attention to the communications environment. Reliable responses from CHADO, completes the feedback loop.
