Project Summary

GBV is the biggest protection concern in South Sudan. Exacerbated by a variety of factors including armed conflict, inter-communal fighting, climate change and forced displacement 2.7 million people will be in people need of GBV interventions in 2024. Of these, 546,000 people will be targeted by GBV prevention, mitigation, and response interventions. The severity of protection needs for 2024 is very critical in 25 localities with severity classification levels 4 across 10 states and 3 administrative areas in South Sudan. Among the targeted people will be IDPs, returnees and host communities. UNFPA will ensure inclusion of gender, disability, and age in the identification of beneficiaries. The people with disability will be part of the planning, implementation, and monitoring. UNFPA will establish a special mechanism for the involvement of people with disabilities in all levels of the project.
UNFPA and its implementing partner plan to reach 114,051 (women 69571, men 5703, boys 11,405, girls 25,091 and PWD 2,281) vulnerable people including returnees, people with disabilities and poor host communities with lifesaving and specialized GBV services across the country through GBV prevention interventions engaging men and boys through Accountable Practice (EMAP); Start, Awareness, Support, Action (SASA), and Community Cares Program. The planed interventions will also include economic empowerment, livelihood support, and awareness sessions and sensitization on PSEA. GBV risk mitigation activities will include integrating GBV actions into other sectorial response, conducting multisectoral safety audits, distributing fuel-efficient stoves, distribution of dignity kits and delivering cash assistance to vulnerable women and girls.
Under the GBV response the priority interventions will include GBV case management service, psychosocial support, safety and security services, legal assistance, provision of services in safe houses, and establishing / strengthening Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) and GBV referral pathways. The livelihood and economic support in the Women and Girls Friendly Space will also act as an entry point for women and girls to receive more psychosocial support and other empowerment activities. On the other hand, some of these activities will be introduced as income-generating activities to allow women and girls to earn some income to meet their basic needs and referrals for livelihood support with other program supporting women and girls.
UNFPA will organize regular coordination meetings among key stakeholders, including government agencies, international organizations, non-governmental organizations, and local communities. These meetings can serve as a platform for sharing information, aligning strategies, and fostering collaboration to ensure a more coherent and comprehensive response to health crises. Through the proposed activities UNFPA will ensure continuity of critical services in targeted areas to ensure that GBV programs are maintained considering the current economic crisis and its impact on those who were already in need of humanitarian assistance. The project will also support strengthening capacity building on GBV through training of the GBV partners and non-GBV humanitarian actors ((Linked to the HRP Strategic Objective 2)
UNFPA will procure and distribute Dignity Kits (DK) to women and girls of reproductive age to enhance their Dignity and hygiene as well as use DK programming as an entry point to provide women and girls with information on GBV prevention, and GBV service availability including information and services on Sexual Reproductive Health.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face overlapping crises including climate change, conflict, and displacement all of which continue to negatively impact the resilience and vulnerability of people across the country. Close to 95 percent of the population depends on climate sensitive livelihoods like rain-fed agriculture, pastoralism and livestock keeping, as a result floods and droughts significantly disrupt livelihoods, leading to food insecurity and weakening community resilience while increasing the vulnerability of women and girls. Climate change has impacted the countries already fragile and lack the GBV services and led to more frequent and severe weather events, such as floods and droughts, which disrupted GBV and increased tension within households due to the challenging economic situation. The 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that 2.7 million people, mainly women and girls, are at risk of GBV. The priority areas are found in 25 counties that fall under severity 4 classification, and there is an urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and scaling up of response services in these prioritized locations. The GBV AOR aims to reach 546,000 people including the response of returnees from Sudan which are 838,667 individuals. According to the 2023 GBV Information Management System, 44% of the GBV incidents occurred in the context of Intimate Partner Violence. Conflict related Sexual Violence has also thrived in a protracted conflict setting. GBV poses a significant concern in the community, as evidenced by the national prevalence study on violence against women and men conducted in 2022. The study revealed that 58% of women and girls experienced at least one form of GBV over the last 12 months. The sudden influx of people led to shortage of the basic services and challenge to meet the increased demand. Referring to the safety audit finding conducted by UNFPA and partners in Upper Nile state, many of the returnees’ sites are extremely crowded and lack of privacy increased risk of harm to women and girls in these settings due to congestion. Inadequate sanitation facilities in PoC sites, IDP camps, reception sites and transit centers led to an unpredictable risk of an outbreak of diseases that have compromised the health and well-being of IDPs, returnees and refugees, limited access to essential services, spread of diseases, limited women, and girls’ access to livelihood and IGAs. GBV underreporting is a concern due to lack of community awareness and lack of access to GBV services thus majorities of the cases remain silent. In addition to insecurity and fear revenge from perpetrators and their families.Based on the monitoring mission carried out by UNFPA team to Bor, Pibor and Kapoeta from September 11 to September 22, 2023, women and girls identified many types of the GBV incidents and concern occurred in the areas included including rape and physical violence. They also explained that Child and Force marriages are common practice because of the economic status. This practice is widely spread, and it takes time for mindset. Limited access to essential services and food was raised as top protection concern increased women vulnerability and GBV risks. Lack of GBV services and PSS services due to lack of the funding and insecurity was raised as a significant challenges and exposed the women and girls in secondary harm including being impregnated by the military who were meant to take care of them temporarily due to transport challenges. Furthermore, Safety concerns related to the abduction and Rape of women and girls in the northern part of the Jungle state is a concern raised by the community due to insecurity and tensions.

2. Project activities

• Provide life- saving specialized quality GBV services, including health care/CMR, case management and psychosocial support, and reinforce referral pathways. Support civil society including organizations including women led organizations, and government actors to provide GBV specialized survivor-centred services including clinical management of rape and other health services; psychosocial support (PFA, individual & group PSS, and refer to specialized MH services); appropriate, quality case management services including coordinated care and support to navigate available services as well as referral services to connect GBV survivors to appropriate, quality, multi-sectorial services in a timely, safe and confidential manner. To overcome the accessibility challenges as result of bad road condition due to insecurity and rainy season GBV assistance will be provided in different ways; remotely (case management and Psychological Support Services), mobile and static service delivery points. Build the capacity of GBV and SRH partners on effective, acceptable, and timely clinical management of rape (CMR) for the provision of timely CMR services to rape survivors, etc.• Establish and support Women and Girls Friendly Spaces to provide for women and girls to access information and several activities aimed at socially empowering women and girls. While some activities such as tea talks and crafts’ making build on the social empowerment, life skills training and training on fuel efficient stoves aim at mitigating exposure to GBV risks. WGFS will support women and girls to access quality services through establishing/ supporting existing GBV referral pathway mechanisms, information and activities that promote healing, well-being, and empowerment.• Promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response include advocating and supporting integration of GBV and survivor centred support across different clusters, strengthen capacity of non-GBV service providers, conducting safety audits and provision of the GBV cash assistance and economic support. Build the capacity of GBV and SRH partners on effective, acceptable, and timely clinical management of rape (CMR) for the provision of timely CMR services to rape survivors.• Conduct community consultations which will also create a sense of ownership and agency of the population over issues that affect their lives. Capacity building on GBV related issues such as awareness sessions on protection risks, elements of psycho-social support, and knowledge about the existing referral pathways will contribute to strengthen capacity of the communities including youth and men to prevent and respond to GBV in a safe and timely manner, to seek sustainable solutions and to address issues of stigma and shame. Social and gender norms change communication will be considered in the GBV program interventions and advocacy. • Procurement and distribution of the emergency items; Dignity Kit will be distributed to vulnerable women and girls to enhance dignity and hygiene of women and girls as well as use it as an entry point to enable women and girls access information on GBV prevention and risk mitigation including information on GBV service delivery points.• Build the capacity of government line ministries and national research institutions to conduct gender analysis on the disproportionate impact of climate change on women and girls in South Sudan.• Strengthen GBV Coordination mechanism at national and sub-national level and strengthen data collection and information management - GBV Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and locality-level referral pathways operationalized - Coordinated GBV intervention strengthened at state level by strategic action plans and capacity building of implementing partners (remotely as necessary) - Conduct GBV assessments and strengthen mechanisms for safe and ethical data collection.
