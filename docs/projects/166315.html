Project Summary

This LoC-Din project titled “Camp Management Support to tackle COVID-19 in informal settings, Borno State” is a cross sectoral project that will complement efforts of the government, UN agencies and other partners in containing the spread of the Corona Virus disease (COVID-19) among IDPs in informal settings in MMC and Jere LGAs who are already suffering from the impact of prolonged armed conflict in the area. LoC-Din will set up a project implementation team made up of Project Coordinator, Finance Manager, M&E Officer, project team leads, site facilitators and community volunteers who will work as a team to effectively provide cross sectoral services in 10 informal IDP camps in target locations. It will coordinate partners and monitor service provision to ensure essential services get to the most vulnerable in a way that discourages duplication.
The project will mainstream gender and protection throughout its life cycle. The humanitarian principle will be applied through out to ensure assistance is rendered based on vulnerability and needs, but with the involvement and participation of all relevant stakeholders and community members to give room for sustainability. The project will take care to address all cross-cutting issues of GBV, child protection, marginalisation, physical disabilities, mental health and psycho-social support, sanitation and hygiene, among others. The project will establish physical presence in selected locations to engage frequently with persons of concern, holding informal dialogues, and noting issues, challenges and concerns that may not be revealed by more formal mechanisms.
In this project, Jere and MMC are targeted in Borno State because they are the two LGAs identified as “Very high” risk locations in terms of COVID-19 importation and spread in the State according to the final draft of the HRP COVID19 2020 Addendum. Their “very high” risk ratings are associated with their proximity to the International Airport, their strategic locations serving as hubs of international humanitarian workers from affected countries, hosts to crowded IDPs and their weak health systems.
LoC-Din through this project, is targeting 5000 IDPs who are women, men, boys, girls and children including the elderly, minority groups and people with physical disabilities. 70% of target populations will be females who are mostly breadwinners, caregivers, GBV survivors, psychologically truamatised, old and weak and are vulnerable.
Additionally, this project will target 500 most vulnerable females (who are breadwinners of their families, caregivers and foster parents of unaccompanied children, survivors of GBV and domestic violence, and those whose sources of livelihoods have been affected as a result of lockdowns and restrictions of movements) for small conditional cash based intervention. This intervention will be assessed monthly at designated vendor points by beneficiaries through voucher for the period of 7 months.
The proposed activities by LoC-Din will be monitored directly by LoC-Din project office in Maiduguri with technical supports from the CCCM/Shelter and NFI sector. LoC-Din's Maiduguri office will provide direct financial management and oversight of the stated activities. Weekly internal reporting will be provided by the site facilitators and volunteers and will be technically reviewed by the CCCM Project Manager based in Maiduguri. Over the course of implementation, field visits will be conducted in order to monitor the project activities against the target stated within the proposal. Internal reporting, monitoring and evaluation will take note of all constraints or impediments to activities in order to undertake a regular evaluation of project goals and implementing strategies. Project updates will be uploaded on the UN GMS and FTS online platforms from time to time. A final narrative and financial report will be produced at the end of the project in compliance with donor standards and requirements.

1 - Needs

The Nigerian North Eastern region has witnessed over 10 years of armed conflicts caused by Boko Haram insurgentswith devastating effect on the people of the region. Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states are mostly affected. Over 8.2 million people have been displaced with over 7.1 million people in need of humanitarian assistance according to the 2019 UN Humanitarian Needs Assessment. Borno State remains the epicenter of this crisis with 80% of affected populations residing in the state. In Borno alone, about 1.3 million people have been displaced out of which 265,782 are in Maiduguri, the State capital. With renewed waves of displacement ongoing, the number of new arrivals and informal camps are on the rise with increased overcrowdings. Women and adolescent girls have increasing Gender Based Violence issues to contend with by the day. Malnutrition, very poor sanitation and hygiene practices and near absence or poor health and WASH facilities have all combined to aggravate the plights of IDPs in informal settings, particularly children and women. The corona virus disease which appeared in December 2019 in China has spread rapidly around the world and has been found to be highly contagious. At-risk countries like Nigeria without proper medical facilities are mostly devastated as cases have been on the increase in March and April, with cases and casualties already reported in Borno State. IDPs in informal camps, particularly in Jere and MMC, more than before, experience hunger and malnutrition as Border closures are squeezing humanitarian relief supply channels. Lockdowns and restrictions on movement and international staff are limiting the delivery of assistance. Governance, social systems and health infrastructures and services are very poor. Displaced populations in informal sectors have the weakest CCCM, health and WASH infrastructures and services, and are most vulnerable due to the crowded, unhealthy and uncoordinated physical environment they live in. While the government is taking measures to slow the continued spread of COVID-19 in the country, LoC-Din will work to complement government efforts in informal IDP camps and host communities against the spread of the disease in Jere and MMC, Borno State through dedicated camp management supports. Lack of proper coordination and service monitoring during crisis leads to duplications and waste of scarce resources. LoC-Din will coordinate with partners and monitor service provision through the establishment and management of camps committees and COVID-19 complaint and feedback mechanisms (COVID-19 CFM) in all project locations. Feedbacks gathered from the established and functional committees and COVID19 CFM will inform decisions on advocacies for COVID19 related multi-sectoral interventions and general improvement in living conditions of the IDPs project locations. Confinement resulting from lockdowns constitutes a significant challenge to women’s economic needs, especially those that rely on the informal sector and humanitarian assistance for their livelihoods. This project will target 500 most vulnerable females (who are breadwinners of their families, caregivers and foster parents of unaccompanied children, survivors of GBV and domestic violence, and those whose sources of livelihoods have been affected as a result of lockdowns and restrictions of movements) for small conditional cash based intervention. This cash based intervention will be assessed monthly at designated vendor points by beneficiaries through voucher for the period of 7 months.

2 - Activities

The project will deploy site facilitators and community Volunteers to 10 informal camps: a total of 24 facilitators and community volunteers will be deployed to provide cross sectoral services including full facilitation and site levels coordination of assistance and protection against COVID 19. The project will enhance proper site level service mappings to report gaps and prevent duplication. They will carry out service mappings in camps and report gaps for immediate response. They will support awareness raisings and risks communication efforts in project through community engagement in project locations. The project will provide basic protective equipment for facilitators and community volunteers including other frontline workers. The project will undertake community engagement to scale up risks communication and awareness on COVID-19 prevention. LoC-Din will work closely with education partners to ensure the right messages reach target population at the right time. We will also work closely with Translators Without Borders (TWB) to ensure all messages developed in English are correctly transcribed in major local languages (Hausa and Kanuri) The project will coordinate with government at the state and LGAs levels including relevant partners to disseminate critical key health and WASH messages to camp communities. LoC-Din will work closely with education and WASH partners to ensure site facilitators and community volunteers are trained to undertake these tasks in project locations. Already developed IEC materials in form of posters, banners, handbills and fliers including audio messages will be reproduced and disseminated widely across all project locations. To ensure basic sanitation and COVID-19 recommended hygiene protocols are observed and practice in all project locations, hand washing points and sanitizers will be provided and installed at strategic entry points in all camps to ensure people wash and sanitize their hands regularly to protect against the spread of the disease. Regular inspection of health and WASH facilities will also be carried out in all locations. The project will identify 500 most vulnerable females and support them with small Cash Based intervention. This will be done through the voucher system where beneficiaries will be given voucher to access food stuff monthly at a designated vendor points for the period of 7 months covering the project duration. This intervention will alleviate their sufferings and reduce the hostilities that come with engaging with vulnerable IDPs without direct food interventions. The project will set up and maintain COVID-19 Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM). This will be done through consultation with affected population to identify the most suitable CFM to be used. However, CFM help desks, CFM boxes, hotlines and audio recording systems have been used by CCCM professionals in the state and have all proved to be successful. The project will leverage on this success stories to adopt same if acceptable by affected populations.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will make use of the accountability to affected population (AAP) framework throughout the project. The project will take care to set up and maintain COVID-19 Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) in all locations we will be working. This will be done through consultation with affected populations to identify the most effective, efficient and accessible CFM to be used. However, CFM help desks, CFM boxes, hotlines and audio recording systems have been used by CCCM professionals in the project areas and have all proved to be successful. The project will leverage on this success stories to adopt same if acceptable by affected populations. Additionally, all our online platforms (facebook, twiter, instagram, WhasApp, emails and text messages) will be available to take complaints and feedback for beneficiaries, community members, partners and donors alike. Our site facilitators and volunteers will also be in the field 8am to 5pm officially to directly recieve complaints and feedback from those who may be unable to use other available platforms. CFM forms will be designed and taken to field daily for this purpose to ensure that no one is left out.
