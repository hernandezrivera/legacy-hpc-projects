Project Name

Start Date

01/01/2022

End Date

31/12/2022

Project Summary

This project aims to reach 84,746 beneficiaries comprising of 38,408 Host Communities, 35,998 IDPs and 10,344 Returnees (about 2% of the Health Cluster target) in 5 Counties of Juba, Lainya, Magwi, Ikotos and Panyikang. The target beneficiaries will include male children under 5, female children under 5, male children age 5-17 years, female children aged 5-17 years, male adults aged 18-60, female adults aged 18-60, male adult aged over 60 and female adult aged over 60 from all the different grouping of Host Communities, IDPs and Returnees. The selection of the 5 Counties of (Juba, Lainya, Magwi, Ikotos, and Panyikang) was based on profound presence of HealthLink South Sudan in the locations, inter-sectoral severity level, health severity, and current conflict evaluation of each county. HealthLink South Sudan intents to achieve about 3% of the cluster target across the selected 14 counties in Central & Eastern Equatoria, and Upper Nile States. This project will complement the current development projects in most of the selected counties with focus on delivery of integrated lifesaving essential health care services. HealthLink South Sudan intends to implement activities to achieve 3 main Health Cluster Strategic objectives. Specific activities will include but not limited to; SO1: Vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4 and 5 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to meet their basic needs. 1. 1 - Needs

5.5m people are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. There is increasing numbers of people in need for health care services from 2017 to 2022 (Humanitarian Needs Overview, HNO, UNOCHA 2022). Majority of the vulnerable populations are in severe, extreme and catastrophic status with 30%, 33%, and 20% respectively. The 5 Counties selected for health care services are in state of level 4 inter-sectoral severity except for Magwi and Ikotos counties, and between level 2 to 4 severity of health care needs (Humanitarian Needs Overview, HNO, UNOCHA 2022). However, with the fragile situation in the Country, there is currently ongoing conflicts between the cattle herders from Bor South and the Communities in Magwi with over 17,000 internally displaced populations (IDPs) in Magwi Town. This fresh displacement is still on going and the number is expected to increase. Already the routine health care services is overstretched hence increasing the severity level of health needs in Magwi County (IRNA Assessment Report, 8th March 2022). Critical associated factors compounding access to health care in the 5 selected counties include distances of more than 5km away from the nearest health (SSHHS 2008, NHSSP 2017-2022), inter-communal and civil conflicts, persistent and prolonged flooding and underdeveloped health care system. Worsening the current health situation is the common sub national conflicts especially in Magwi and Panyikang Counties, a third consecutive years of major flooding mostly in Panyikang, some parts of Juba and Ikotos Counties and the impact of Covid-19 across the selected Counties. In Mangala Payam (Juba County), there is an ongoing persistent conflict between the Mundari and Bari communities affecting health care delivery. Overall, the above situation is associated with poor health indicators, for example high mortality rate of under 5 children at 90.7/1,000 live births and maternal mortality rate of 789/100,000 live births (South Sudan Mortality Statistics, 2016) among others. Displaced populations suffer more constraints in accessing lifesaving and essential health care, with children under 5 most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases as well as women of reproductive age facing high health risks of unskilled care during pregnancy and child births. Immunization coverage over 2 years (2017 & 2018) for all vaccine preventable diseases have persistently remained below 50%. This has created a big gap of 42% of children under 1 year at risk of measles (WHO EPI Report Sept 2018). Recurrent outbreaks, and or upsurge of epidemic prone and other infectious diseases including acute watery diarrhoea/cholera, measles, malaria, meningitis and hepatitis E compounded by flooding and food insecurity presents increased susceptibility to morbidity and mortality especially to pregnant mothers, children under 5 years of age, people with disability and the elderly (60 years plus). Target population include 84,746 individuals consisting of 38,404 host communities, 35,998 IDPS and 10,344 Returnees.Over stretching the health care system is the current ongoing Covid-19 pandemic with recorded 17,044 positive cases and 138 deaths (CFR 0.8%). A sustained community transmission is still ongoing with 7-day average positivity rate of 1.1% (Covid-19 Weekly updates, MoH Emergency Operations Center, 10th March 2022). With the current the current implementation of the revitalized peace process the anticipated returnees would even pose greater risk to Covid-19 infection, outbreak of other epidemic prone diseases (Measles, Hepatitis E, AWD/Cholera), and Malaria upsurge in the Country. This necessitates for early and adequate preparedness and response to potential outbreaks while strengthening the current response to Covid-19 across all the response pillars. Cross cutting issues like people with disabilities, mental disorders, HIV/AIDS, TB and non-communicable diseases including lifesaving trauma care are being cut-off from life-saving services.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

HLSS provides an environment where beneficiaries have the confidence to provide feed-backs on health care projects being delivered to them. This can be in form of short messages, phone call-backs, voice messages, and focus group discussions and community meetings. The information collected will be analyzed, and provided responses accordingly at different levels, and will also guide for inclusion of missing components or change of course whenever it is appropriate and in the interest of both beneficiaries and the donors.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HLSS clearly recognizes the value of community and stakeholder engagement as an essential part of project planning, implementation, monitoring and collaborative decision making in regards to the project. Under this service delivery area, HLSS intends to employ three (3) approaches in engaging communities; (i) Conduct Monthly Boma and Payam health meetings, (ii) conducting advocacy/public campaigns for utilization of community level health care services; (iii) conducting defaulter tracing, follow up and referrals. (i) Conduct Monthly Boma and Payam Health Meeting HLSS proposes to extend support to BHTs/Payam health administration for conducting monthly review meetings and activity planning to ensure effective implementation of the project. This forum will also provide opportunities for giving feedback to communities as a way of transparency and accountability to beneficiaries. (ii) Advocacy/Public Campaigns for Utilization of Community Level Health Care Services HLSS will work with the SMOH, CHD, Payam health officers, BHCs, and BHTs to conduct various meetings/campaigns at County, Payam, Boma and community levels. The meetings will target stakeholders and beneficiaries to understand the scope of this project including BHI components and strategies and to make sure the communities understand that BHI is part and parcel of the health care delivery system adopted to increase universal access to services and utilization. HLSS will also use mass media platform such as local FM radios and posters to advocate for the availability of the services offered under BHI at community levels. This is expected to increase understanding and utilization of the services by all the vulnerable populations in the project areas. (iii) Conducting Defaulter Tracing, Follow up and Referrals HLSS will support the BHWs to conduct defaulter tracing for EPI, TB and HIV services, children on treatment for MAM/SAM and follow up of ANC/lactating mothers, conduct early and prompt referrals of patients in critical condition and children under five years with danger signs using bicycles, motorcycles, or using stretchers from the Community to health facilities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Providing information about SEA to the beneficiaries is very critical to enhance community reporting of any form of SEA committed with the beneficiaries. As a bottom line, HLSS has PSEA policies and practices aim to prevent sexual exploitation and sexual abuse by its employees and it provides that any allegations of SEA are responded to in a timely and appropriate manner. All new staff in emergency context are oriented on this policy before deployment to deliver health care services to the beneficiaries. It is the principle of operations that HLSS ensures beneficiaries are oriented on their responsibilities of reporting SEA through a confidential protection desk that is always made available and linked to CMR services and legal support. This is always done during various community engagement process in the course of the project implementation. HLSS will also ensure beneficiaries education on SEA and reporting channel throughout the project period. The community-based feedback mechanisms to prevent and respond to SEA will be established. New staff at all service delivery points (static facility, mobile clinics and community based health care workers) will be trained in how to handle such cases with the level of confidentiality they deserve.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project has been designed to benefit the host community, IDPs, returnees and other affected populations. The project intends to ensure that the services provided doesn't pose threats to the beneficiaries but rather address protection concerns across the population group targeted through carrying out joint activities and integrating some of the services within the population targeted. To prevent conflicts, this project intends to engage the local authorities in the proposed project locations for their full engagement during the project implementation to minimize some protection concerns that would arise such as Land disputes over project sites. The implementation of this projects will also ensure equal access to men, women, boys and girls to services. Special consideration will be put in place to ensure adequate space for provision of CMR services to survivors of SGBV, Psychosocial Support with high level of privacy and confidentiality. Different approaches such as Conducting Monthly Boma and Payam health meetings, conducting advocacy/public campaigns for utilization of community level health care services, and conducting defaulter tracing, follow up and referrals will be adopted to enhance community participation that would contribute to risk reduction.
