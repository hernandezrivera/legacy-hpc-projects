Project Summary

The project seeks to build up and expand the ongoing the curative, preventive and promotive primary and secondary health care services (including SRH, MHPSS services and COVID-19 response) for refugees and surrounding host communities. Outreach services will be provided through Community Health Workers and Community Psychosocial volunteers. Facility based primary healthcare (including MHPSS and SRH services) are implemented through 21 camp based PHCs and HPs which cater for the needs for women, men, boys and girls; services provided are aligned to the essential health service package endorsed by the health sector and MoH. Efforts contributing to contain the COVID-19 outbreak include the operation of 2 SARI ITCs and one ICU, quarantine facilities, community based surveillance and risk communication. Medical referral support to access secondary, and tertiary health care will be provided for 50% of the camps. Outbreak/emergency preparedness and response will be strengthened, procurement of medicine and medical supplies including contingency stocks will be undertaken. Health sector coordination will be supported through CHWG, MHPSS WG, field coordination and other sector coordination forums.

1 - Identified Needs

The COVID-19 pandemic affected the health status of the population in the camps and led to disruption in health programmes in 2020. More than 360 refugees were infected with COVID-19 so far. Efforts for awareness raising, early case identification, contact tracing and treatment will need to be continued throughout 2021. Fears of being infected in health facilities led to a decline in consultations by 52% from quarter 1 to quarter 2 in 2020 in UNHCR supported facilities. This affected likewise the already low uptake of immunization and disrupted the treatment of non-communicable diseases and mental health. Nevertheless, crude and under-5 mortality rates remained within the UNHCR standard with 0.17 and 0.47 deaths/ 1,000/ month respectively with no significant difference in sex disaggregated mortality rates (Sept. 2020). Health facilities built during the acute phase of the emergency need to be replaced with transitional facilities and integrated with nutrition; lifesaving secondary and tertiary health referrals are supported only by UNHCR, IOM, and MSF which will need to continue. The mental health and psychosocial support needs are immense: many refugees, male and female of all ages, present with psychosocial distress and mental health symptoms, the need for support being aggravated by the COVID-19 situation. Refugee engagement in health programmes will need to be expanded to enhance community ownership further. The risk of communicable disease outbreaks remains high due to overcrowded living conditions, poor health and hygiene practices, inadequate water and sanitation facilities, and low immunization uptake. Access to maternal and reproductive healthcare services expanded in 2020, but services are not reaching all refugees in need. Skilled delivery uptake increased from 18% in the beginning of 2018 to 56.1% in August 2020 but falls short of the targeted 90%. The uptake of preventive services including antenatal (ANC) and postnatal care was interrupted due to COVID-19. Fears of being infected in the health facilities led to reduction of ANC visits by 38% from quarter 1 to quarter 2 in UNHCR supported facilities. The reports of 12 maternal deaths in the first 8 months of 2020 highlight the need to intensify support to Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services. A key concern remains the access to comprehensive emergency obstetric and neonatal care (CEmONC). The specific health needs of female sex workers, MSM and transgender people are addressed through a limited number of specialized facilities and require further expansion.

2 - Activities or outputs

Provision of comprehensive primary health care services: Promotion of access to promotive, preventive, curative, rehabilitative and palliative care, tailored as necessary to the specific needs of people of all age groups, including those with disabilities, adolescents and people who sell or exchange sex. Provision of curative, preventive and promotive health care in line with the ESP through 10 health centres, 11 health posts and 9 MHPSS centres with integrated specialized services such as dental care and physiotherapy. Provision of essential medicines including NCD and psychotropic medication. Staff capacity building, expansion of health committees to enhance community engagement and dialogue. Strengthen data collection to monitor quality of care using Balanced Score Card and quarterly monitoring visits; strengthen access and utilization through irHIS. Support access to sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and HIV services: Provision of SRH service to women, men and adolescents through IPs. Provision of Clinical Management of rape for survivors, linkages to other supportive protection, legal and social services. Provision of counselling, SRH and protection services to key populations through Drop-In Centres.Enhance existing referral system and access to secondary/tertiary health care services for host community and refugees: Maintain 10 ambulances for emergency medical referrals. Support patients with translation, obtaining services and conduct payment of hospital referral charges through dedicated referral worker, incl. support for life-saving emergency referrals to non-UNHCR partners in UNHCR’s area of responsibility. Construct outpatient department in Sadar hospital and support provision of HR.Integrate mental health in primary health and protection services: Maintain psychologists and psychosocial counsellors at health facilities and community-based points of access and care including in community centres and client homes. Maintain continuous supervision and monitoring of MH trained and newly recruited Volunteers of health and protection partners to assist in identification and referral of refugees with complex needs. Maintain supervision and monitoring of 100 refugee lay counsellors to provide basic counselling.Build community capacity for healthy living through a community-based health promotion and surveillance programme: Continuous training of 400 female/male community health workers (CHWs). Awareness raising through home visits, community gatherings and other methods patient referral to health facilities.Enhance prevention and treatment of COVID-19: Provide treatment for refugees and host community through 2 SARI ITCs as well as Intensive Care Unit and severe patient ward in district Sadar hospital. Maintain one quarantine facilities for contacts and new arrivals.Community engagement and awareness raising, community based surveillance, counselling on COVID-19 and patient referral.Support management of major public health risks including outbreak and disaster preparedness and response: Support emergency response during outbreak through enhancing community awareness, early case identification, case management and referral through intensive public health messaging by CHWs and mobile medical teams. PFA and first aid by CHWs, provision of mobile medical teams, camp wide referral through ambulance system to functioning facilities. Maintain stocks for emergency response. Support outbreak prevention through community based surveillance.Support health sector coordination and information management efforts: Continue active contribution and engagement in health sector coordination, strategic advisory group and working groups. In support of the health sector and in close cooperation with relevant partners, co- chair Community Health and MHPSS working group. In support of the health sector, strengthen field level health coordination through dedicated camp health focal points.
