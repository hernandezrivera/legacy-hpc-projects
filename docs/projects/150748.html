Project Summary

Since March 2018, the Gaza Strip has been witnessing a progressive deterioration of the political, social and security environments. This situation has been contributing towards volatility in the security context, as well as for a rise of the instability levels in Gaza. Furthermore, since 30 March 2018, it was observed that there was a significant increase in the number of demonstrations under what is called the "Great March of Return". Since March 2018, demonstrations are taking place at several border areas, East of Gaza Strip on daily/weekly basis. The participation of children in the demonstrations exposes children to different kinds of risk, including the risk of being injured or even killed. According to the OCHA (As of Oct. 2018), 38 children (36 boys and 2 girls) were killed and 4250 were injured by Israeli occupation fire since the beginning of the Great March of Return, along Gaza's borders. Recently (Oct 2018), OCHA has identified providing mental health and psychosocial support to children affected by violence in the context of the Great March of Return as well as those whose parents or close family members are directly affected as a key humanitarian response. Along with providing child protection services for children who have protection concerns, Tdh has been providing child protection services to injured children during the Great March of Return. So far, about 100 of injured children (99% boys) have benefited from child protection case management and more than 120 children (99% boys) benefited from structured PSS. This project is a continuation of Tdh intervention targeting injured children and their families across the Gaza Strip and children who have protection concerns. The project aims to target 8900 beneficiaries, including 900 children. The proposed activities will include responsive, remedial, and environment-building child protection activities to 250 recently injured children and 400 children (200 boys and 200 girls) who have recently lost parents or close family members, and 250 children who have protection concerns. The main child protection activities will include, but not limited to, structured psychosocial support, psychological first aid, protection case management, individual and group counseling sessions, and referral to specialized health and mental health services as needed. Additionally, under this Action, awareness sessions on how to deal with traumatized children- victims of the current situation will be conducted targeting 8000 members, including families who have injured cases and schools' staff, and staff of CBOs dealing with injured cases. In coordination with the line ministries, the implementing partners and FSS, this project will also provide cash assistance support for 200 families of injured children that face additional concerns to support them in the care of children. The targeted beneficiaries, including children and adults from different age groups, including seniors were involved in the design of the project, including the proposed activities. Additionally, both men and women from different targeted localities were involved in the project through identifying the needs and activities. During the implementation, beneficiaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities. The activities of the project are tailored according specific needs of children, taking into accounts their gender and their age groups.

Needs

Palestinian children older than 11 years old living in Gaza have now witnessed three conflicts and have only known life in Gaza under closure, resulting in an increase of the number of children displaying high levels of stress. Children and families’ resilience capacity continues to be eroded, as the needs of the population have exhausted the usual family coping mechanisms. According to the PCBS (2018), families struggle to get by with one of the highest unemployment rates in the world at 49.1%.According to a recent report published by the PCBS (March 2018), 53% of Gaza's population live below the poverty line. Despite the provision of specialized mental health and psychosocial support to thousands of children over the past years, children remain distressed (UNICEF, 2016). The Child Protection Rapid Assessment (2018) has shown that physical and sexual violence against children are common across Gaza as stated by more than two-third of study participants. Moreover, the Child Protection Rapid Assessment (2018) found significant patters including 96% of respondents identifying psychosocial distress among children as one the main child protection concerns in Gaza. According all Key Informants across Gaza, distress manifestations include children being more aggressive (85%), bed-wetting (76%), unusual crying and screaming (73%), violence against younger children (72%), and unwillingness to go to school (69%). Since March 2018, the Gaza Strip has been witnessing a progressive deterioration of the political, social and security environments. This situation has been contributing towards volatility in the security context, as well as for a rise of the instability levels in Gaza. Furthermore. In the Gaza Strip, according to PCBS, 94.5% of children age 1-14 years were subjected to at least one form of psychological are physical punishment by household's members (PCBS, 2015). The PCBS surveys (2016) indicate that psychological violence is the most common form of violence against students by teachers followed by physical violence. The findings of Tdh participatory school reviews (Tdh, 2016) indicate that more than one half of child respondents (57.2%) stated that physical violence happened to them” frequently”. Since 30 March 2018, it was observed that there was a significant increase in the number of demonstrations under what is called the "Great March of Return". Since March 2018, demonstrations are taking place at several border areas, East of Gaza Strip on daily/weekly basis. The participation of children in the demonstrations exposes children to different kinds of risk, including the risk of being injured or even killed. According to the OCHA (2018), 38 children (36 boys and 2 girls) were killed and 4250 were injured by Israeli occupation fire since the beginning of the Great March of Return, along Gaza's borders. This has further compound to already fragile situation of many impoverished families unable to deal with the additional needs for the care of their injured children for which economic resources are needed. All these factors illustrate the importance of providing children and youth with integrated child protection services, including structured PSS and child protection case management. Recently (Oct 2018), OCHA has identified providing mental health and psychosocial support to children affected by violence in the context of the Great March of Return as well as those whose parents or close family members are directly affected as a key humanitarian response. Along with providing child protection services for children who have protection concerns, Tdh has been providing child protection services to injured children during the Great March of Return. So far, 100 of injured children (99% boys) have benefited from child protection case management and more than 120 children benefited from structured PSS (99% boys). The design of this project, including the activities was discussed with the community members from project localities.

Activities

The complexity of the current situation in the Gaza Strip requires multifaceted approaches including expansion of the provision of comprehensive child protection services to vulnerable children. Under this Action, the two-output are: (1). Child protection services including case management and PSS are available to vulnerable children, including victims/ survivals of GBV, physical violence, dropout of schools, and working children, in the Gaza Strip, (2). Child protection services are available for children and families of children who have been recently injured during the demonstrations of the Great March of Return, including case management, Psychological First Aid (PFA), and structured psychological support (PSS) services. The proposed activities will include provision of responsive, remedial, and environment-building child protection interventions to 250 recently injured children and 400 children (200 boys and 200 girls) who have recently lost parents or close family members, and 250 children who have protection concerns. The responsive activities will include (1). Provision of PFA and child protection case management to identified cases of children recently injured and children who have protection concerns and (2). Provision of PFA and case management to identified children who have been recently lost family members as a result of the Great March of Return Demonstrations. The proposed responsive activities aim to prevent further harm, and restoring the well-being of affected-children. With aim of increasing children’s social skills and increasing resilience, remedial activities will include (1). Provision of child protection case management, including individual and group counseling sessions, joint child-parent interaction activities, and life skills to children have been recently injured and children who have protection concerns such as victims/survivals of GBV and children victims of physical and sexual violence, children victims of child labour, and dropout of schools. (2) Provision of structured PSS to children who have been recently injured and children who have child protection concerns. According to child's age, different PSS manuals will be used, for instance, children below the age of 12, Psychosocial Rehabilitation of Children Affected by Armed Conflicts and/or Violence Guide will be used and Healing and Education through Art Guide will be used for children aged from 13 to 18 years old. (3). Provision of financial support to 200 families of children who were injured recently, 100$ per family. The financial support will be used to cover the expenses of injured children, as health care, food, and transportation. Financial support will be given, conditional to supplying a medical certificate as prove of injury as a result of Great March of Return, in coordination with ministries, partners, and FSS. Environment- building activities include conducting awareness sessions on how to deal with traumatized children- victims of the ongoing situation. The sessions will target families who have injured cases, schools' staff, and staff of CBOs dealing with such cases. In total, 8000 members will be targeted (4000 men and 4000 women). Both boys and girls will receive protection services according to their assessed needs. As the number of injured cases among boys is higher than among girls, the project will provide protection services mainly for boys injured during the Great March of Return, however, all identified or referred cases of injured girls will be targeted. As girls are more subject to Gender-Based Violence (GBV), the number of girls benefiting from protection services for victims and survivals of GBV will be higher than boys. However, all identified cases of boys survivals of GBV will benefit from the protection activities. Services will be provided to all children in need, regardless of their age groups; services will be also provided to their families regardless of the gender and age of the head of the household.

The plan will comprise the indicators, assessments and means of verification tools defined in the logical framework. For case management activities, Tdh will use the Child Protection Web-based Case Management System which will allow generating timely reliable and accurate data on project beneficiaries and activities. Tdh will also oversee the monitoring tools and regular field visits will be organized to monitor the well implementation of the activities, the adequate follow up of procedures and standards and collect beneficiaries’ feedback. As of Tdh efforts of coordination and information sharing, Tdh will comply with different reporting requirements: monthly reporting will be provided at sector level or as per request. Finally, Tdh will coordinate closely its activities with organizations that provide child protection services such as Tamer Institute for Community Education, Maan Development Center, and Gaza Community Mental health Programme to avoid duplication of services. Tdh will also report its intervention to Protection cluster, Child Protection Working Group, and FSS. Tdh will respect, protect and fulfill beneficiaries’ rights, including protecting beneficiaries’ personal information such as names and Id numbers. Tdh will also provide equal access to services to all identified children, including children with disabilities. The Best Interest of the child will be considered as the uppermost priority and the do no harm principle will be applied. All interventions will seek to avoid exacerbating the situation for children by ensuring properly planned and implemented interventions that take into account children’s needs and access to humanitarian services. In addition to that, participation and non-discrimination will also be ensured.
