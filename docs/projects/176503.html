Project Summary

In line with the 2021 Health Cluster objectives, the overall goal of this intervention is to reduce the morbidity and mortality of children U5 (boys and girls), PLW, older people and other vulnerable groups and to decrease the burden of epidemic prone diseases in Kapoeta South, East and North counties in Eastern Equatoria, and in Tonj East and South counties, and in Twic (Mother Theresa Hospital) in Warrap State through the enhancing of the health services. The proposed initiative builds on the long-lasting experience and presence of CCM in the targeted areas, complementing the current health and nutrition programming to ensure safe and equitable access to critical integrated basic services to enable 318,528 prioritized target people (including IDPs and returnees) to meet their basic needs in the targeted areas. Through a network of 68 health facilities, the proposed project will contribute to the Health Cluster objectives by improving access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems, reducing excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities, and increasing access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly.
Furthermore, the proposed activities will address the specific needs and concerns of different gender and age groups through age, gender and diversity mainstreaming.
Through a holistic approach of health system strengthening, the project specific purposes include:
1. To increase access to lifesavings, essential and emergency health care services in Greater Kapoeta (Kapoeta North, South and East Counties) and Greater Tonj (Tonj East and South) addressing the main causes of mortality amongst vulnerable populations, particularly women and children with limited or no access to health services, IDPs and other vulnerable groups, including case management of severe acute malnutrition of U5.
2. To increase CHD capacities to prevent diseases outbreak, targeting hard to reach and vulnerable communities. Specifically, the current health and nutrition programming of CCM in Tonj East and Kapoeta counties will be complemented and strengthened to address cholera prevention and control measures aiming for a strategic shift from response to prevention across all relevant sectors in order to (i) Enhance the preventive and response interventions at both HFs and communities’ level and (ii) support the strengthening of the capacity of the County Health System to prevent, detect and respond to such unexpected outbreaks. It is therefore crucial that CCM and SMoH/CHDs continue to maintain the ability to prevent, detect and respond to health emergencies shifting on prevention.
3. To incorporate environmental health into the current health and nutrition programming, promoting Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) practices into health care programs delivered to protect health workers, patients and the community from health care-associated infections. The project intends to combine the reinforcement of the health services while promoting IPC practices into health care programs delivered in Tonj South, Tonj East, Twic, Kapoeta Est, North and South counties.
The proposed programming has been designed in view of the effective and close collaboration established with the local authorities, at both County and State level, to ensure it properly responds to the critical needs of the targeted populations. To ensure increased and quality access and utilization of health care services at both facility and community levels, ownership and responsibility by SMOHs and CHDs are essential. Finally, an effective reporting system is integrated with sectors monitoring systems. Boosting the health data management at CHD and State level will promote the culture of using the evidence for planning, managing and sustaining programmatic intervention

1 - Needs

In 2021, an estimate of 318,528 South Sudanese children, women and men in the targeted areas need humanitarian healthcare services. The prevailing healthcare challenges are exacerbated by the lack of infection prevention and control measures, water quality control, waste management and high malnutrition. CCM intends strengthening all levels of health care in order to enhance delivery of integrated health services across the targeted areas. This will be achieved through a continuous capacity building process of the SMOH and CHDs, which will enable the provision of uninterrupted quality essential and emergency health services to local communities. The population in the targeted areas have very low access to health care facilities and sub-standard maternal care coupled with malaria endemicity and seasonal outbreaks of communicable diseases including measles and cholera. CCM implements health system strengthening through the Health Pooled Fund (HPF) in 68 health facilities, but a large proportion of the counties remain uncovered. In Greater Kapoeta 13 health facilities are non-functional because of lack of funding to support staff and provide essential drugs (6 PHCUs in Kapoeta East, 3 in Kapoeta North and4 in Kapoeta South). Priority is being given to Kapoeta East County where in Jie Payam in which the community relies only the Boma Health Workers (BHWs) because of lack of health facility and Kauto Payam Also, populations deep in the Peikim. Specifically, the health system in the areas faces several challenges, mainly attributed to: i) Low health service coverage worsened by the poor road network and the inaccessibility of some health facilities particularly during the rainy seasons; ii) Deplorable health facility infrastructures and lack of space for health care services at primary and secondary level; iii) Lack of administration and technical capacities at county health department level; for data management and reporting, and drug supply chain and management; iv) Lack of qualified trained health workers in the area combined with high turn-over of skilled staff due to lack of accommodation and inappropriate retention strategy. For IPC concerns, healthcare facilities without proper management become ideal settings for the transmission of infections between individuals which eventually trickles back to the community. According to WHO, 1 in 10 patients low/middle-income countries, gets an infection while receiving care. The compliance of health facility workers with key elements of IPC remains low due to lack of educational efforts and awareness building. Other areas of concern in the targeted counties include poor awareness and knowledge about HCAI and IPC, lack of capacity of SMOH/CHD with the surveillance of HCAI and compliance with IPC practices, outbreaks and bacterial resistance, and limited resources for environmental requirements at the facility level (related to water and sanitation, waste management). Specifically, unsafe waste management represents a major challenge in the health facilities. Experiences show that, often, health care waste management does not follow proper standards and procedures. Main need is therefore to assist the SMoH/CHDs to reduce HCAI by supporting with the assessment, planning, and implementation of infection prevention and control policies and guidelines at all HFs levels. Furthermore, cholera presents a major strain in health services across the country and major gaps and concerns still remain. Both counties are typically at-risk due to inadequate access to clean water and extensive lack of basic sanitary infrastructure and, consequently, critical suboptimal hygiene practices (low practice of handwashing, high proportion of open defecation, improper water conservation and treatment at household level poor food conservation). The situation is worsened because of the compounded effect of flooding, displacement by intercommunal violence and poor water, hygiene and sanitation.

2 - Project activities

The proposed initiative will support the Health Cluster objectives 1 and 2 . The following activities will be done: 1.1 Increasing CHD capacities to prevent diseases outbreak, targeting hard to reach and vulnerable communities. Specifically, the current health and nutrition programming of CCM in Tonj East and Kapoeta counties will be complemented and strengthened to address cholera prevention and control measures aiming for a strategic shift from response to prevention across all relevant sectors in order to (i) Enhance the preventive and response interventions at both HFs and communities’ level and (ii) support the strengthening of the capacity of the County Health System to prevent, detect and respond to such unexpected outbreaks. It is therefore crucial that CCM and SMoH/CHDs continue to maintain the ability to prevent, detect and respond to health emergencies shifting on prevention. Conduct an exploratory baseline assessment of the current situation at each HF level aimed at identifying local determinants of the HFs associated infections burden;1.2 Collection of patients and community feedbacks on service delivery and health staff attitude; 1.3 Development and preposition of IPC guidelines, policies, protocols and standard operating procedures in all HFs;1.4 Training of 130 health facility workers on IPC programme management; 1.5 Training of 23 health workers on IPC in outbreak response; 1.6 Training of 65 support staff in the health facility on appropriate and routine IPC practices;1.7 Appointment of technical team (IPC team) made of trained professionals at health facility level; 1.8 Procurement of appropriate IPC materials and equipment (e.g alcohol-based hand rubs, soap and water, personal protective equipment (PPE), detergent, mops, buckets, etc.); 1.9 Provision (construction and/or rehabilitation) of adequate number and appropriate position of hand hygiene facilities; 1.10 Provision (construction and/or rehabilitation) of health care waste management facilities (e.g incinerators, waste collection containers for non-infectious general waste, infectious waste and sharps waste, etc); 1.11. Monitoring/audit of adherence with IPC standards; 1.12 Conduct health education sessions and sensitization events on health, hygiene and sanitation; 1.13 Design and production of IEC material (e.g leaflets and posters) and messages (including use of different channels, e.g community radio) for community engagement activities that are appropriate for the target populations in terms of language, educational level and cultural contexts; 1.14 Health and hygiene education campaigns through community mobilization activities aiming at the adoption of appropriate hygiene practices (such as hand-washing with soap, safe preparation and storage of food and safe disposal of the faeces of children, etc); 1.15 Training of health facilities workers and CHD staff on emergency response and prevention of disease outbreak in TE and KAP;1.16 Definition of a joint plan of action at county level to describe the interventions needed in case of an outbreak and the distribution of roles and responsibilities among stakeholders;1.17 Strengthen surveillance and information sharing by building the capacity for data collection and analysis;1.18 Training of health facility workers on cholera prevention and control in TE and KAP; 1.19 Procurement of tools to ensure optimal coordination at county level (phones, dashboard, maps, etc); 1.1.1 Procurement and preposition of hand washing stations for 12 health facilities; 1.1.2 Training of BHCs and community volunteers on hygiene promotion, and timely detection and referral of suspected cases in TE and KAP; 1.1.3 Training key stakeholders on water quality monitoring; 1.2 Scaling up outreaches on routine and supplementary immunization; 1.3 Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response; 2.1 Integration MHPSS and SGBV
