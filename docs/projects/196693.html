Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to the development of a coordinated and common approach to land release procedures, as well as the sustainability of the HMA activities within NES. To this end, iMMAP will lead the development of land release SOPs, in coordination with the NESMAO and HMA actors, and in the absence of existing NMAS. This will support HMA actors conduct their activities safely, effectively, and efficiently, as well as support coordinated reporting and the NESMAO’s role in coordinating and monitoring the NES mine action program. The findings of the region-wide NTS survey will be used to develop a three-to-five-year land release (technical survey and clearance) strategy and workplan, which will focus on prioritizing and releasing all identified suspected and confirmed contaminated areas. In parallel, iMMAP will focus on strengthening the capacity of the NESMAO, as the authority for mine action in NES, allowing for a gradual handover of the HMA coordination activities. iMMAP understands that the engagement of the local administration in the HMA coordination efforts is essential to ensure the smooth continuity of HMA activities, as well as the long-term sustainability of the HMA program.
iMMAP’s proposed response aims to achieve the following outcomes by the end of the project:
Outcome 1: The land release process in Northeast Syria is enhanced, ensuring standardized, safe, and cost-efficient operations.
Outcome 2: The NESMAO capacity in mine action and information management is strengthened, enabling them to effectively monitor the progress and achievements of CWD activities in NES.

3 - Needs

Explosive Ordnance (IEDs, landmines, ERWs) in Northeast Syria contamination continues to pose a great danger to population and hinder economic and social development. Based in the 2022 MSNA, one in three communities is reported to be contaminated by Explosive Ordnance. iMMAP reported that 22 million m² are confirmed or suspected to be contaminated in addition to 800 hazards (source IMSMA data base managed by iMMAP since 2018). In the absence of a strong National Mine Action Authority (NESMAO set up in 2021), and lack of National Mine Action Standards, along with an absence of a clearance prioritization system, HMA actors carry out their operations according to their individual identified needs and SOPs. As such, the implementation of a harmonized operational approach and reporting system amongst HMA actors is compromised. Through iMMAP’s HMA coordination efforts, HMA actors have expressed the need for establishing SOPs in NES (acting as National Mine Action standards) to enable coordinated, safe, and effective management and implementation of operations. The need to train the NESMAO on their roles, responsibilities, Information management and monitoring is also essential to ensure a sustainable HMA program.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project is not foreseeing final beneficiaries amongst the population. The beneficiaries of the action will be members of the NESMAO and HMA organizations in NES. They shall not be exposed through the implementation of these activities to protection related risks.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

iMMAP does not directly implement humanitarian activities through this action, minimizing the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse towards final beneficiaries. Nevertheless, a risk related to iMMAP members and external stakeholders (NESMAO, HMA partners) is existing. This risk is mitigated with an internal Global PSEA policy dating from December 2020. Each new member is trained and sign the policy while joining the organization. A complaint mechanism policy is in addition in place in Northeast Syria to allow raising any alert related to PSEA and taking adequate measures after being investigated, either towards iMMAP personal or external stakeholders.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The main risk identified lays in the recrudescence of security incidents in NES, causing disrupting of activities and evacuation of international staff members: All international members received a HEAT training beforehand. Security SOPs and evacuation plan are reviewed regularly and enforced by a dedicated NES security team. The second main risk raised for this project is linked to the potential lack of engagement and alignment from the NESMAO. The latter has created unnecessary obstacles in the past, cause the disruption of HMA activities for several weeks. To minimize this risk, iMMAP has involved the NESMAO in the project design and will set up a monthly coordination meeting between the NESMAO and the HMA partners to update on activities progress and discuss challenges faced.
