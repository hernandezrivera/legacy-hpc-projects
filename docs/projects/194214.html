Project Summary

The project will ensure provision of timely, well-coordinated and integrated humanitarian intervention to 31,420 IDPs to improve their well-being, their resilience and participation in decision-making processes. The delivery of CCCM activities in informal sites will be conducted through the deployment of mobile/roving teams. Narrative Hub is quite conversant with CCCM cluster strategy having been trained by the cluster in 2021. A multi-sector approach between CCCM, GBV, ES/SNFI and protection will be prioritized at the sites where needs will be commonly identified by the CCCM in coordination with ES/NFI and protection and all activities will be integrated during the implementation period to achieve the cluster set objectives in locations where needs are very highly identified. Narrative Hub has both static and mobile presence in the location providing protection services and therefore, the intervention will include services like support to GBV survivors, psychosocial support and raising awareness sessions for men, women, boys and girls, as well as mitigation measures. This will be done in the form of training. It will respond to emergency needs by empowering community and strengthening the capacities of local actors to have access to both essentials camp management service. Through CCCM Cluster support, the project will contribute significantly to service delivery in hard-to-reach areas of Tonj East, Gogrial East and Gogrial West of Warrap State, coordinating multi-sectorial assistance through organizing site level coordination meetings with community members from both displaced and host communities; conducting site maintenance and service mapping activities to support minimum living standard; and protection of people with special needs in coordination with actors; and strengthening community structure by training community members on CCCM and humanitarian response. Community engagement will be a priority to the project for purposes of accountability to the affected population (AAP). COVID-19 protocols and SOPs will be observed during the implementation of the activities to control the spread of the virus.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 of South Sudan notes that an estimated 835,000 people were affected by severe flooding between May and December 2021. Warrap State is one of the most affected in terms of crop and livestock production. According to IRNA Report conducted in Gogrial West on 12th October 2021 found that 13,860 (2,310HHs) people were affected and living in displaced sites, which is high land area near the market, some in the churches, while others integrated themselves with host communities. Seven (7) Schools where directly affected by the flooding submerged in water not accessible to school children. The UN’s Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO) assessment findings indicated that 65,107 hectares of land planted with cereals had been damaged, with an estimated loss of 37,624 tonnes of grain in the flood-affected areas. More people are likely to experience severe food insecurity in 2022. An estimated 8.3 million people, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). The counties of most concern is in Warrap State (specifically Greater Tonj). Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. In 2022, the precarious situation is expected to be further exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country.An estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourishment. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent was reported in people in twenty-six counties in six states (Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Eastern Equatoria). The leading cause of morbidity is malaria, followed by acute respiratory infections and acute watery diarrhoea. In 2022, morbidity rates are expected to increase proportionally for each of these causes as they did in 2021, compared to 2020. People are at constant risk of an outbreak of infection.A continuation and increase in sub-national violence reported in many states across the country, including Warrap (particularly Greater Tonj), will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2022, even though the local markets are still functional. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a threat; women and girls are exposed to risks when carrying out their routine activities, which is further exacerbated due to flooding that limits livelihood opportunities (e.g. collection of firewood and charcoal production). Feeling unsafe, some women and girls avoid areas such as water points, latrines, distribution areas, markets and firewood gathering sites. Many violations go under-reported. Women, children, older persons and persons with disabilities (PWDs) are particularly vulnerable, exposing them to risks of exploitation.REACH Situation Monitoring report for Warrap State between January and March 2021 notes that apart from flooding, the displacement in Warrap state was also likely driven by sub-national and localized conflict, particularly from Greater Tonj, to Gogrial East, Tonj South and Tonj North in Warrap which the displaced population are living in camp-like settings. This has resulted in increased insecurity as the vulnerable population search for a source of livelihood for themselves and their animals. Based on the observations, focus group discussions (FGDs) and key informant interviews (KIIs) conducted, the situation requires immediate humanitarian response to provide lifesaving assistance through food, shelter, NFI kits (plastic sheets, blankets, buckets or Jerricans and mosquito nets), WASH kits distribution and health support.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Narrative Hub Cash Based Interventions is intended to contribute to the recovery of local markets and financial infrastructure following disruptions caused by flood, conflict and COVID-19 pandemic. Throughout the country, people have been facing price inflation and a shortage of hard cash, which has reduced their purchasing power and further put vulnerable households at risk. Narrative Hub has considered options to scale up cash-based interventions as an effective means of assisting vulnerable households. In 2019 - 2020, Narrative Hub used CBI approach in its main responses particularly on protection, and identified critical information in terms of market functionality and prices of basic goods and thus making the CBI an integral part of intervention. A key milestone is the integration of CBI at field levels where teams provide regular and accurate information on the prices and availability of basic food and non-food items, working closely with CWG. The field officers collect and analyze data on the prices of basic items and assess the market in their respective locations.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

After careful market assessment, analysis and based on recommendations of market functionality in Warrap, Narrative Hub will apply Cash-Based Interventions (or CBIs) including cash assistance (unrestricted or restricted) where beneficiaries (households) will be provided with vouchers or cash to directly purchase and access NFIs and Shelter materials to specific suppliers, specific items and a set of determined value. Narrative Hub will also apply cash for work initiative when it comes to direct implementation of some interventions such as construction of shelters, or clearing the camps. Based on the assessed context Narrative Hub will use most appropriate modalities electronic cash transfer; hard cash payments; electronic vouchers; and paper vouchers. Cash and voucher system has become the most preferred approach of supporting communities affected by emergencies. It provides affected population with greater choice, flexibility and transparency through their ability to choose what basic items their household needs and when to shop. CBI empowers beneficiaries, particularly women, reduce negative coping mechanisms and have broader multiplier effects for both IDPs/returnee and host community populations than traditional in-kind assistance. As Narrative Hub, vouchers will allow for more flexible provision of NFIs and reduce pressure on logistics chains. Site monitoring and post distribution monitoring will also allow Narrative Hub to understand beneficiaries’ shopping preferences and needs and identify vulnerable households for a follow up support. CBI will have wider benefits for host communities especially the small local business include those owned by youth and women groups through Village Savings and Loans Association. Narrative Hub will collaborate with local business to implement cash and voucher in the affected areas with market functionalities. Replacing a range of in-kind items with cash in any form also reduces the resale of distributed items by beneficiaries which immediately decreases the value and impact of the provided assistance, tends to distort local markets and negatively impacts neighboring host communities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

While Narrative Hub sets up a feedback and complains mechanism at the project location among the beneficiaries, at Juba level, a committee composed of Juba staff and led by the M&E will be in-charge of receiving those feedbacks directly from the field locations and conducting the feedback analysis. Each feedback will be collated and grouped accordingly into areas that will ensure they are better addressed. Those that are related to programming will be addressed from the perspective of the beneficiaries advise and lenses and they are at the centre of the project. Whether it is changing the meeting or distribution location or time, that will be changed, and beneficiaries will be informed of the changes through downward feedback from Juba office. The M&E staff will travel to the field locations to communicate directly with beneficiaries and also notify the field staff of the changes that has been made.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Narrative Hub has presence in the locations and hence there is already community engagement and trust with beneficiaries as well as the local authorities, who already had given some of the needs to help design this project. Narrative Hub will spend some time with beneficiaries at the inception of the project creating an awareness of the project and its goal including how to report complains and feedback and how they will be addressed. Narrative Hub will then set up an implementation committee composed of the same beneficiaries. Beneficiaries and other agencies on ground will be involved in monitoring to continuously review and and gaps will be identified and discussed jointly and addressed in order to meet the main goal of the project. Key community members including individuals living with disabilities, women and girls will be involved during joint monitoring of the project and from the different constituency groups. A complains and feedback committee will be selected by beneficiaries to engage beneficiaries, listen to their stories and give upward feedback. For those who can write, a complains box will be put around distribution locations for upward complains and feedback. Where necessary, focused group discussions will be held at project site with beneficiaries and community leaders to provide feedback. Sampling for FGDs will be representative for all affected groups of people. Where distribution is involved, a PDM will be conducted to harvest beneficiary feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Narrative Hub will ensure that training is conducted to all the staff members both at Juba office and field staff on PSEA before the beginning of the project implementation. During signing of contracts, all staff will sign a PSEA code of conduct. The training will then be extended to beneficiaries during project inception. This training will to beneficiaries will be done by Juba based staff members and not by field teams to avoid biases, victimization or any conflict of interest. The beneficiaries will be trained on communication channels available to them to report PSEA and contact persons.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A complains and feedback committee will be selected by beneficiaries to engage every beneficiary (PWDs, individuals who are not literate, women and girls), listen to their stories and give upward feedback. Where necessary, focused group discussions of each constituency (PWDs, individuals who are not literate, women and girls), will be held at project site with them to provide feedback. Where distribution is involved, a PDM (which will involve individual interviews, FGDs and observations) will be conducted to harvest beneficiary feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. NH is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. NH is a very active national NGO on child protection in Jonglei State and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. NH with other agencies will develop a referral pathway in Uror, and will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools NH will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. NH staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. NH is also a very active member of Protection Cluster.
