Project Summary

Below average rains leading to acute water shortage, influx of IDPs thus overcrowding and floods in
specific target areas still present need for WASH services. Increased prevalence of AWD/cholera outbreak and other water borne diseases have been reported in target districts especially among IDP. In all target areas the situation is compounded by limited access to sanitation facilities due to increased number of displaced women, men, boys and girls that have settled in IDP camps, retrogressive cultural beliefs, poor programming and dwindling funds thus straining existing facilities and services. Common cases of open defecation,careless solid waste disposal and poor hygiene practices are common manifestation that
need to be addressed. A number of women, men boys and girls still cover very long distance in search of safe water and queue at water points . This " does harm" by exposing them to hazards such as sexual violation and exploitation especially among women and girls.Among IDP ,due to multiple displacement, their livelihood assets were lost including sanitation facilities and water points.The situation is exacerbates the synergistic relationship between WASH status and acute malnutrition rates among boys and girls aged 6 59 months and pregnant lactating women. The situation is likely to worsen if efforts are not concerted to address it. Among the rural hard to reach areas of Mudug, access to clean water and sanitation facilities is so limited. Few households have toilets, clean drinking water and sufficient water for hand washing and other hygiene practices, at the back drop of few health services/facilities. Being a hard to reach area
with capacity challenges, there is increased prevalence of water washed and water borne infections. Integrating community initiatives to behavior change with interventions from other sectors like health, protection and Nutrition therefore becomes inevitable.
The proposed project seeks to address these key issues through various interventions with a strong multisectorial lense, community integration, and with an aim of mainstreaming protection and gender, while uphodfing accountability to affected population. SAF UK has been working with Kismayo IDP and host communities across lower Juba, and Kahda with IDPs in Banadir for many years, having successfully completed many health programs. Among health interventions is the management of AWD cholera and malnutrition in the regions. Part of the success is attributed to strong integration with WASH in provision of hygiene and sanitation services and enabling factors. The proposed project seeks to leverage on lessons learned and scale up this all important primary prevention activity. Efforts of this multisectoral collaboration has seen drastic contribution to significant decrease in the prevalence of AWD/cholera and other water related diseases to less than acceptable thresholds in these areas.
In Hobyo SAF UK has recently scaled up health services to cover almost all villages in North Hobyo. Water and Sanitation situation in these areas covered is however very wanating. The proposed project will therefore seek to cover these villages with water, sanitation and hygiene promotion services. These will include distribution of much needed hygiene kits and water tracking during drought like conditions. The program will once again leverage on the opportunity readily provided by health programs that have fore run good community relationship,existing community structures (that will support interventions and ensure sustainability of projects) and strong and good working relationship with government entities and ministries right up to grassroot levels.
The project will also seek to establish management boards and integrate these with the already existing community health boards.
Capacity building of staff, community and government officials will remain top with strong supportive supervision to establish acquired knowledge and skill and sustain gains.

Mitigation Measures

For IDP in Mogadisho the main risk is displacement. for this the organisation hasmaintained a database for all bene􀀀ciaries with telephone contacts and that ofrelatives. For those in rural Mudug the risk is spontenious clan clashes. While it isdi􀀀cult to deal with this, we have ensured that services are provided for the differentclans in their areas equitably. Interclan clashes therefore have minimal effect ourprograms. in all location the organisation will seek to provide safety nets byimplementing BSFP . Victims of GBV will also be provided with an organisational GBVfocal point who will link survivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensureIDp have access to basic services in an equitable and digni􀀀ed manner with equalityalso observed in job allocation in projects serving IDP population. Among IDP the riskof exclusion based on tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certaincommunity who found it hard to access services in the recent past based on their tribeand skin color. The organisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actuallyreaches out to all community members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is notbased on tribe but professional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid ofcultures who have learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved tothe target communities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs andwomen so that equity is achieved.All gender will be awarded opportunity to bene􀀀t in equitably. Women and youth whoare mostly left out in recruitment will be considered in all projects and positions will beawarded equitably. Needs assessment will include all community groups so that an allconclusive, agreeable and fair assessment is reached. both men and women leaderswith an additional youth representative will be part of community health board as perorganisational policy. This will ensure decisions made are all inclusive. SAF UKpolicies have sought to put men at the helm of needs affecting their wives, daughtersand female relatives. The organisation's APP framework seeks to ensure menparticipate through a􀀀rmative action for men ensuring that men are not excluded frominterventions targeting women bene􀀀ciaries. Children who are often left out will notonly be seen as bene􀀀ciaries but their views on service delivery ad need willoccasionally be sought by a professional child specialist so that the whole communityparticipates in program design and implementation. Community diagnosis,community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will seek to include all communitygroups to ensure all are reached.Both in Mogadisho and Kismayo security risk is minimized by ensuring all staff gothrough the internal security vetting every quarter and present a clearance letter fromministry of internal security. They must also get reference from a local leader like anelder or imam who is accountable. These must also be known by local elders fromtheir tribe who pledge accountability. This ensures that our staff are not related to anynon state actors planning violence. For those in rural Mudug, the risk is spontaneousclan clashes. While it is di􀀀cult to deal with this, we have ensured that services areprovided for the different clans in their geographical areas equitably. Inter clan clashestherefore have minimal effect to our programs. However after conversations withelders, it was agreed that all clans are served by the same staff regardless of their clanbackground and without discrimination and threats to ones life and property. This isslowly but surely contributing to cohesion among the various clans and a realizationof each others strengths.
