Project Summary

The project was designed to improve the lives of vulnerable women and girls (including women and girls with disabilities) by enhancing access to integrated gender-based violence services inside and outside the WGSSs managed by SEWAR in Al-bab in NWS.
The main activities that will be implemented in WGSS:
-specialized GBV services, preventive activities, vocational training, Individual Protection Assistance (IPA), Awareness sessions on GBV, Life skills programs for women and girls and providing small grants for 40 women to start a small project.
For the other two WGSSs in Al-bab SEWAR will implement
-Response activities: (Focused PSS for GBV survivors, GBV Case management, including a responsive case fund, and Referral to other services.
-Preventive activities, such as GBV awareness-raising (GBV-SC toolkit), information sessions about early marriage, and Covid related awareness sessions.
-Empowerment activities: life skills (My safety my wellbeing for adolescent girls), PSS group session, recreational activities
dignity kits distribution for women and girls, vocational training for W&G, such as hairdressing, sewing, wool, Literacy and ..etc.
-Emergency Response Package including PFA, referrals, registration
-Capacity-building activities include training and refresher sessions on PSEA to all SEWAR staff in the location.
-Training for non-specialized GBV service providers, on GBV basic concepts focusing on health actors, cash and voucher distributors workers, and early recovery actors.
-providing small grants for 10 women to start
a small project with the aim of generating income and economic independence for the women and girls in WGSSs and camps as per specific criteria
rovision of awareness-raising for how to deal with PWD and their rights; for their families disabilities and including them in community
-Provision of safe referrals for female GBV survivors and women and adolescent girls at risk to SEWAR WGSS in Al-bab through the mobile team.
The WGSS are equipped to receive women and girls with disabilities and all SEWAR staff received many training on how to deal with the PWDs.
After assessing the type of disability and the need and desire of women and girls with disabilities, they are directed to activities appropriate to their situation.
and regarding the elderly women, we will target them during the implementation of the activities in proportion to their specific needs, like their age, and health status.

3 - Needs

According to VOICES FROM SYRIA 2022, the most common form of violence mentioned by women and girls in NWS was IPV and family violence, digital/online violence, restrictions of rights and movements, denial of education - linked to early marriage and child labor, deprivation of inheritance especially widows, sexual exploitation and harassment - especially at work and for rent and early marriage continues to be mentioned. women and girls with disability are most commonly subjected to physical, psychological, and social violence: including IPV, psychological violence by the family, public bullying in school, social stigma, and neglect. in addition, widowed women are at increased risk for Forced re-marriage and forced abandonment of children in some cases. elderly women are subjected to neglect by family members, perceived as an economic burden, and they are exposed to some physical and emotional violence by adult children, in addition to unmet medical needs, distance to services and general vulnerability are mentioned as key deterrents from seeking protection services or reaching distribution points According to HNO 2023, These forms of violence are systematically normalized and justified through patriarchal gender norms and institutions that maintain gender inequality. Women and girls’ capacities to cope through positive coping mechanisms are constantly reducing. Silence is the most common coping mechanism to deal with GBV.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: 1-Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also leads to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services, Impact: High, Mitigating: 1. Service mapping includes only those that can provide survivor centred services 2. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral. Protection risk:2- Persons with disabilities, the elderly, and widows have difficulty accessing to WGSSs.Impact: High. mitigation:Provide transportation, and mobile teams will be allocated to reach those who cannot access the WGSSs: Protection risk: 3-Lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to WGSS and access services Impact: High Mitigating measures: Thorough gender analysis prior to the start of programming. Equip all WGSS with child-care services. Provide transportation support for persons with disability. Include persons with disability in all conducted assessments at the level of WGSS and/or community to understand their needs and gaps in their access to services. Protection risk: 4-Some beneficiaries were exposed to PSEA by SEWAR staff mitigation: raise awareness among the beneficiaries about complaints mechanisms regularly.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on SEWAR needs assessment conducted within the targeted location, SEWAR will priorities the most vulnerable persons to receive services during this project. However, they may face risks of exploitation by service providers or by another actor in the community and SEWAR will work on mitigation measures through the following: 1. Ensure participants know services were to disclose if violence occurs 2. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and how to report 3. Establish good relations with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles, and stick to them. 4. ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible for all. 5. train all staff and contractors on PSEA CoD. Also, the project is designed to enable equal and impartial access through community consultation and participation across age, gender and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups, not to exclude any groups and provide the identified services with dignity and impartiality. Starting with the project's first needs assessments and during the whole project period, SEWAR will focus on using the "Do No Harm" approach to integrate conflict sensitivity wherever the feedback (complaint) mechanisms will be in place and all beneficiaries must be aware of them. It will be confirmed that the service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against beneficiaries. Apply the principle of “different needs equal opportunities.”


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: 1) the large spread of COVID-19 in targeted locations. Risks: 1-Movement restriction to basic services due to the high increase in the number of COVID-19 cases.mitigation: Providing remote response services. 2-Safety and health of the training participants linked to COIVD-19.mitigation: All PPE and social distancing measures will be taken into consideration during the training. Assumption: 2) a High number of GBV cases in camps. Risks: 1-Preventing women, girls, and PWDs from accessing services within the widow's camps.mitigation: Ensuring the provision of services for family members and camp management during the implementation of the project activities and in particular, awareness activities and advocacy campaigns to mitigate risks and reduce the risks of preventing the access of beneficiaries to services, Provide transportation support for them. Assumption: 3) all WGSSs managed by SEWAR are located in safe areas. Risks : 1-Changing the security situation that interferes with the implementation of the project. Mitigating Measure: We will establish a response plan that can be activated according to the situation. Designing the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation so as to achieve the wanted results.
