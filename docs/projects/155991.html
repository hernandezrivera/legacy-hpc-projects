Project Summary

As part of this 12 months action, SCSD will support the capacities of 4,000 households at least, living in NW Syria (Idleb and Aleppo governorates) by providing life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support, and contributes towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.
As part of this action SCSD will support the capacities of 2,800 Vulnerable IDPs, Host Communities, and Returnees households living in Atareb and Jebel Saman sub districts (Aleppo Governorate) and Idleb, Bennsh, Badama, Janudiyeh, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Kafr Nobol, Khan Shaykun, Maarrat An Numan, Ariha, Armanaz, Dana and Harim sub districts (Idleb Governorate), to heat their shelter during the winter season 2019, prioritizing female or child headed households and elderly people and considering people with disabilities. Using in kind distribution modality, SCSD, will organize three distribution periods during the project implementation as part of which identified beneficiaries will be able to access roughly 360 liters of Diesel fuel in total (120 liter of fuel per month upon to the cluster’s recommendation).
This program includes house rehabilitation activities for 1,200 most vulnerable households (1200 private shelter units in total) in the targeted sub districts which will target both IDPs and host communities, this activity includes rehabilitating partially damaged units through ceiling repair, walls buildings and holes fixing, doors and windows, infrastructure, electricity network and solar panels, plastering, plumping and providing the water tank. This component will be guided by the shelter rehabilitation guidance published by SNFI cluster in 2018 and ensuring that HLP documentations are well carried out in parallel with field coordination with the relevant local councils.
The program will seek feedback from both male and female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access and equal opportunities.

Needs

According to SNFI HNO 2019, there are 4.7 million people in need of shelter support in Syria due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters. And 4.4 million people require NFI assistance, a 6 per cent decline from the 2018 HNO, and the second year in a row that the total number of people in need of NFI assistance has reduced. The number of people in need of NFI assistance in Idleb has increased by over 600,000, indicating that the affordability of items is affecting stable areas as well as those more directly impacted by the hostilities. The number of people in need of shelter assistance has increased in Idleb and Aleppo in North Syria. Overall, from the 2018 HNO, the severity of need has increased in only 21 per cent of sub-districts. Overall 300,000 more people now living in severity 4 and 5 areas over the last year with an overall increase in the number of people in need of 570,000. There has also been a significant worsening in overall shelter arrangements in Syria, with 36 per cent of sub-districts reporting an increase in the proportion of communities living in damaged or temporary shelters. As in previous years, the primary providers of shelter assistance and support are host households with an estimated 520,000 households hosting others. In Syria, households traditionally listed the male spouse (i.e. not female) in HLP documentation. As a result of the crisis, Syria has an increased number of female heads of household and separated households. Given the above-mentioned protection risks related to HLP and the specific need for HLP documentation as a prerequisite to receive certain shelter assistance and rights, women face an additional burden in accessing their rights and this support. According to the HNO 2019 the largest numbers of PIN are in Aleppo (1,130,815) and the Idleb (847,579) governorates. Moreover, according to Syria Shelter and NFI Assessment: Idleb Governorate and Surrounding Areas conducted by REACH (August 2018):• 36% of households reported shelter adequacy issues. Of those, top 5 issues: Lack of heating, Lack of insulation from the cold, Lack of bathing facilities, Lack of lighting around the shelter and Lack of space inside shelter.• Of the 47% of households reporting shelter damage, 37% were unable to conduct repairs in the three months preceding data collection. Of those, top 3 reasons: Shelter and repair materials were too expensive, Repairs require professionals, but services were unaffordable and Lack of authorization to do repairs.• 54% of households reportedly received no information about shelter support in the last year.• 73% of households reported possessing legal documentation to prove their occupancy status. Of these, top 3 most common types of documentation: Green or other Tabou (Real estate registry), Rent or lease agreement and Sales contract.• 54% of households reportedly received no information about NFI support in the past year.• 84% of households reportedly adopted coping strategies in response to a lack of access to NFIs, most commonly: Spending savings, borrowing money and Selling household assets. The program is considering targeting areas of severity scale number 4-5 in both Aleppo and Idleb and aims at addressing humanitarian needs. Communities reported different NFI needs among different groups based on gender and age. For instance, boys and girls were reported as being in greater need of clothing and shoes; men as in need of heating fuel, light sources, and water containers; women and girls as in need of sanitary pads; women as needing kitchen-related items and; the elderly as requiring blankets, energy and light sources. These findings from HNO 2019 demonstrate the wide range of items that households continue to struggle to access.

Implementation of activities

The program considers addressing the following activities/outputs: 1. 1,200 vulnerable households (7200 women, men, girls and boys) will obtain enhanced shelter arrangements that provide security and personal safety, protection from the climate and enhanced resistance to illness and disease through damaged houses rehabilitation, coordination with the local councils, and contracting with the contractors, considering HLP issues and develop a plan for implementation. Finally those rehabilitated units will be delivered to the targeted assessed beneficiaries. In addition to Ongoing monitoring to ensure satisfactory progress and accountability to beneficiaries through SCSD MEAL team (consisting of both males and females) along with media documentation.As part of its strategy, SCSD aims to include host-community and IDPs population in order to ensure the acceptance of the project and avoid creating tensions between the populations. In the framework of this project, SCSD and will identify HHs who do not have the financial means to undertake rehabilitation/repair of their homes and purchase fuel for heating and/or cooking purposes during the winter. SCSD will identify the beneficiaries through an application process (following an advertisement of the project in the community) using a scoring methodology to determine the most vulnerable criteria. In order to identify and reach target beneficiaries, SCSD works extensively in conjunction with LCs and community groups with access to demographic data. The application process will include a short questionnaire (between 8 and 11 questions) related to the following topics:- Number of households members- Number of children below the age of 5 years in the household- Number of household members able to work- Number of household members with a regular salary for the last 3 months- Sources of income- Monthly average income- Debts Application from vulnerable groups including women, elderly and child head of HHs will be prioritized.SCSD considers that the above-mentioned groups are the most vulnerable as they do not have the same access to livelihood opportunities as males (reasons related to security concerns and skills). Therefore, their capacity to access Shelter assistance and heating elements is lower, forcing females to resort to negative coping mechanisms, so this project will target at least 20% female/child headed household and 30% HHs with disabled/elderly members from the total number of the targeted households.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk1: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls.Likelihood: High. Impact: HighMitigation measures:1. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism2. Train staff on code of conduct3. Code of conduct in all staff contracts 4. Address any SEA complaints Protection risk 2: The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. In multi-household dwellings, lack of doors and partitions for sleeping and changing clothes can increase exposure to sexual harassment and assault. Tensions linked to overcrowding may lead to an escalation of intimate partner violence and other forms of domestic violence. Where situational and risk analyses are not systematically conducted, these risks might not be identified and rectified. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided. We will include women, girlsand other at-risk groups to determine such things as if systems/criteria are in place to determine how shelters are being allocated; if they are built for safety and privacy; and/or if shelter materials and shelter-related NFIs are being distributed in areas that are safe. b) Consultations with women, girls, boy and men should be organized. c) community safety audits. Protection risk 3: Due to the security situation (concerns about GBV), the female members may not be able to participate in trainings.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium Mitigation measures: SCSD will be keen to coordinate with local councils to select the safest place to conduct trainings as well, means of transport will be available back and forth (especially for females) for safe access - as much as possible - to the training Hall.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Assumption 1: SCSD has full access to the targeted communities and the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the program.Risk: Areas of operation is hard to reach and escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in targeted area affecting the SCSD’s ability to operate and access it.Mitigation strategy:- SCSD will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities.- Hiring of field staff and volunteers from the local community of the targeted areas.- Purchase of materials from the local market with guarantee the Do No Harm principles.- Contracting with local contractors/suppliers.- Communicate, coordinate and consultation intensively with Local councils, stakeholders, other NGOs operating in the areas and community leaders.- Re-direction of programming into other areas.• Assumption 2: Armed opposition groups and government-controlled areas continue promoting an enabling environment for aid delivery and information gathering in Syria, and aid delivery continues to be accepted and possible in Syria despite of the presence of various armed groups.Risk: Implementation of the durable activities is prevented/ stopped by regular clashes, violence, or an upsurge in criminality including theft or kidnapping.Mitigation strategy: SCSD will monitor the security developments and reported criminal instances/violence along its main delivery routes and in its areas of operation. If an increase in criminality/violence is noted, alternative routes will be used and additional warehouses will be rented to mitigate against the possibility of an armed group seizing control of all of SCSD stock inside Syria, or consider alternative areas/activities under the planned intervention.• Assumption 3: Markets are functioning properly in the areas identified.Risk: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access and/or supplies.Mitigation strategy:(a) SCSD will conduct a Rapid Assessment for Markets (RAM) in the initial stage of the program.(b) The situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. In case the situation would change, SCSD would consider relocating the area of intervention where the required conditions are met.• Assumption 4Risk 4: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated Likelihood: High Mitigation: 1. Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, 2. Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP, 3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed, 4. Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form and verify the documents available.
