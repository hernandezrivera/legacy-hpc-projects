Project Summary

NRC’s shelter program in Libya seeks to improve the living conditions and physical protection and well-being of conflict affected populations and internally displaced persons through the maintenance and rehabilitation of houses for vulnerable families residing in or intending to return to damaged or substandard houses in urban settings, the rehabilitation works will focus in improving protection from the elements, physical security and safety as well as improving access to functioning sanitation facilities and water. The intervention will aim at supporting a total of 160 households divided between Benghazi and Tripoli.
The program will also promote improved access to basic services through the investment in rehabilitating and upgrading community infrastructure such as health facilities, power utilities, roads, or communal buildings providing essential services to the public. This component of the program will be mainly focused in Tripoli in areas that have been heavily affected and damaged by the conflict during the past year. The Program will aim at providing infrastructure solutions in three neighborhoods benefiting an estimated 1,500 individuals.
In Tripoli the program will also promote safe and dignified returns of IDP’s that remain unable to return due to the physical conditions of their shelters or due to the lack of adequate community infrastructure.

1 - Needs

As of August 2020, IOM has reported the numbers of internally displaced and returnee populations in Libya to be 392,241 and 493,716 respectively. Shelter remains to be one of the top priority needs for affected populations, with 10% of returnees and 30% of IDP population reporting accommodation to be their top priority need across the country with shelter being identified as one of the top priority needs across the country. The people in need (PiN) calculations of the shelter sector shows 3,692 IDP’s and 9,752IDP’s residing in substandard or damaged dwellings in Benghazi and Tripoli respectively, the two areas targeted by this intervention. The lack of supported access to adequate and affordable shelter quite often results in households residing in inadequate and substandard shelters which can have direct negative impact on physical protection, privacy, health and well-being of occupants. In the eastern regions of the country, many vulnerable returnee and IDP families (both new and protracted) are forced to reside in substandard shelters due to limited financial ability to seek better accommodation arrangements or undertake any maintenance and rehabilitation works, COVID-19 restrictions and the closure of borders has exacerbated the needs further reducing the resilience and ability of vulnerable families to access adequate shelters. Limited access to adequate and sustainable shelter solutions might also result in increased risk of evection for vulnerable families, which might lead to further displacement, loss of assets, loss of social networks and reduced resilience. In Tripoli, the sustained use of air strikes and artillery shelling during the past year of the conflict resulted in massive damage to the infrastructure and to residential properties, particularly in southern Tripoli districts such as Ain Zara and Abu Salim that will be targeted by the program. The return of IDP’s in Tripoli will be considerably challenged by the physical condition of their shelters, the amount of damaged that was sustained and their ability to rehabilitate their shelters or bring it up to acceptable standards. Many families are already returning to damaged or inadequate houses as they struggle to continue paying for rented accommodation. The ability of vulnerable households to return will also be challenged by the lack of adequate infrastructure such as roads, power utilities and health facilities.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention will support vulnerable households residing in, or intending to return to damaged or sub-standard dwellings through the provision of and integrated package of shelter and WASH upgrades. The works will mainly focus at improving the protection from the elements, safety, security, privacy and access to functioning sanitation facilities and kitchens. The works will include installation of new windows, doors, sanitary fixtures, electrical connections as well as repairing damaged walls and ceilings. NRC will work with local authorities such as municipal councils and social affairs offices as well as with community leaders and representatives to identify vulnerable families in need of shelter assistance. potential beneficiaries will be assessed and vulnerability will be evaluated using the vulnerability score card produced by the SNFI sector, once families are selected a more detailed technical assessment will be carried out to examine the scope of damages and identify works needed to bring the shelter up to minimum standards. During the technical assessment the household residing/returning to the shelter will be consulted in order to identify the priority of the works and to ensure that works reflect the needs and priorities of the household. For community infrastructure projects NRC will work with local authorities and community representative to identify neighbourhoods most in need and to priorities works and design the scope of the intervention. For such interventions NRC’s team will conduct detailed area assessments to examine needs and feasibility of implementing such works. For all rehabilitation, upgrades and infrastructure woks NRC technical team will be responsible for the design and production of technical documents and specifications such as BoQ’s, Scope of works and contracts. All works will be implemented through licensed and qualified construction companies and will be monitored and supervised by NRC’s technical team.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.8- the proposed intervention invests in durable housing and community infrastructure that will increase the resilience of targeted households and communities, the involvement of beneficiaries in the design of the scope of intervention ensures that the approach is beneficiary centred and that households have choice and will access the provided solutions with dignity 4.16 – when targeting IDP’s residing in rental accommodation NRC will work closely with its information counselling and legal assistance (ICLA) program to ensure tenure agreements are signed and to intervene through mediation in case any disputes arise. The shelter programme through its post occupancy monitoring exercises and outreach team will continue to monitor any households identified as at risk of evection following the rehabilitation works, in case disputes arise or risk of eviction is assessed to be increasing the issues will be referred internally to NRC's ICLA team. 4.17 – for household level intervention the land titles and proof of ownership will be verified by the shelter team during the technical assessment phase. For community infrastructure projects, written approvals will be obtained from local authorities to reduce the risk of any HLP disputes. The shelter intervention will also be supported by NRC’s ICLA program to build capacity of shelter staff and provide technical guidelines on HLP topics. 4.20 – NRC, through its security and access team, will coordinate closely with demining actors and the local authorities to ensure that program activities will be conducted in areas that have been surveyed and cleared of mines, IED’s and explosive hazards.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

In addition to the common feedback mechanism, NRC has an internal CFM to address non-sensitive/sensitive complaints and feedback regarding our program implementation. complaints and feedback received through the common feedback mechanism will also be managed through NRC's internal CFM unit which will refer the complaints/suggestions or requests for assistance to the shelter team or senior programme management based on the nature and sensitivity of the complaint or feedback. The shelter team and other communication channels (hotline) also exist to address technical issues related to rehab and upgrade works. Finally, a post construction survey will be administered to beneficiaries to assess the rehab process and works and understand the impact of the intervention on improving living conditions.
