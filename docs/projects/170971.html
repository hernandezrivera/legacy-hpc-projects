Project Summary

While 2020 post GU results show favorable outcomes yet for nutrition, it is estimated that in 2021 around 2.3 million people (1.14 million for treatment services and 1.16 million for preventive services) in Somalia will require both lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition support and this number includes children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women and girls as well. Out of the total in need, around 511,706 are IDPs as they are prone to vulnerability due to being displaced either as a result of conflict and/or food shortages. The Somalia 2020 Micronutrient Survey launched in August 2020 shows that although there are some improvements yet there is an increased susceptibility of mothers and children to malnutrition. It indicates that around 40.2% of non-pregnant women and 47% of pregnant women are anemic and similarly around 43% of children below five years are anemic, and both these indicators reveal a serious public health challenge. The same survey shows that the situation of Infant & Young Child Feeding practices below acceptable standards.
The program seeks to improve meaningful access and utilization of lifesaving preventative and curative stand-alone and integrated nutrition services for women and children living in the most affected areas in Sool (Taleex), Middle Shabelle (Balcad), and Bay (Badioa/) districts using a combination of static and mobile sites.
It contributes to Cluster Strategic Objective 1 and 2 through both preventative and curative nutrition services targeting 16,729 beneficiaries: (6,831 boys, 7,401 girls), and 2, 497 pregnant and lactating women through access to providing equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (06-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished cases, and strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on improving nutritional status through the provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate IYCF practices in emergency and micronutrient interventions. Where plausible integrating to WASH and health programs.
The program takes into account the nutritional needs of people with disabilities and accordingly in the planning of response will use the 15% global disability estimates.
The project activities; screening of the target population; 6-59 months of SAM treatment (Balad 345, Taleex 116, Baidoa 344). 6-59 months old for MAM treatment ( Balad 1759, Taleex 484, and Baidoa 1046). MAM Pregnant and Lactating Woman (PLW) treatment (Balad 406, Taleex 118 and Baidoa 1141.)
Implement lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups will be through the provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate IYCF practices in an emergency, and micronutrient interventions. PLW accessing preventative nutrition services will be targeted in Baidoa. Boys and girls (06-23 months and 6-59 months) receiving preventive services through supplementary nutrition services, and multiple micronutrient and Vitamin A supplements.
Staff capacity will be enhanced; training of 42 (16 male and 26 female) Nutrition staff on IMAM; 50 (23 male and 27 female) Community Nutritional Workers in basic IYCF/-E and all staff in PSEA orientation.
Data collected and used will be disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to effectively inform programming at all phases- design, planning, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. The programming will undertake a continuous assessment of power dynamics to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access and discrimination. Nutrition staff and committees will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) to ensure inclusivity in the consultation process of the program phases.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.- Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire, or violence.-Ensure the location and access routes to nutrition facilities and services are safe. QRCS will Identify central locations and bring services closer to vulnerable populations in IDPs and host communities. For example, QRCS will use mobile outreach teams to provide services to populations living further away from static facilities to reduce their risk of exposure to protection issues. -Ensure the location, distance and access routes to the nutrition facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups.-Ensure services are offered within time to ensure beneficiaries travel back safely. 2. Risk of women, girls, and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance.-Design and clearly mark separate facilities for males and females and account for persons with disabilities and children.-In coordination with protection actors, train nutrition staff to be able to identify and appropriately respond to victims of grave human rights violations, including rape and physical abuse, and set up referral networks and protocols to protect the privacy of victims.-Ensure all staff, implementing partners, and volunteers in direct contact with patients, health staff, and beneficiaries have received training in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).-In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of community nutrition workers to monitor, report, and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms.-In cooperation with protection actors, establish joint reporting and accountability mechanisms to report incidents concerning hospitals/nutrition sites.-Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures, and processes that affect them.-Set up accessible, confidential, and well-understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions.-Promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at the nutrition centers, phone lines. 4. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources.-Avoid stakeholders tensions, by providing the local/host community with access to IDP camp nutrition/health facilities.-Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable; identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community, including the host community, and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups.-Adopt clear beneficiary selection criteria communicated to all stakeholders and target communities.-Identify the power dynamics in the area.-Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to their nutrition needs to mitigate external interference.-Ensuring all the stakeholders are well represented to mitigate to buffer any possible interference.

Please provide further explanation

Protection Environment:QRC has ongoing projects in the project areas under consideration and has already put in place protection measures. Though they still require to be strengthened, we will ensure measures already in place allow beneficiaries to safely access their services, without causing harm, and for promoting meaningful access, accountability, and participation of beneficiaries in the provision of nutrition assistance. By mainstreaming protection into nutrition programming, QRC will maximize the positive impacts of nutrition programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations' access and enjoy their rights. Further, all nutrition staff will be oriented on PSEA. Affected Communities:The project will have inbuilt mechanisms to prevent, reduce, and mitigate community tensions, by providing all communities with access to nutrition facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of nutrition services and facilities. Consult both displaced and host communities about nutrition needs and assess whether nutrition interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. Community Engagement:The project will work with the target districts' health ministries and existing nutrition and health working groups at the sub-national level. Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. Communicate information through various means including community networks to ensure communication reaches the broader community and consider literacy levels (eg. health community workers, door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms).
