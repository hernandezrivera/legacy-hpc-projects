Project Summary

This project aims to empower women and girls as well as engaging men and boys through accountable practice (EMAP) on behavioral change to become allies in their communities to address and prevent gender-based violence (GBV). The project will focus on behavioral change, advocacy, and economic empowerment to support survivors and prevent GBV in the future. Survivors of sexual gender based violence (SGBV) and other forms of GBV will undergo multi-sectorial case management. Through awareness about GBV and its impacts on individuals, families, and communities, provide training and resources to support survivors of GBV and promote their economic independence, Advocate for policy Implementation and community support to prevent and address GBV effectively, empower women and girls to speak out against GBV and access the resources and support they need. The target for this project is 1250 direct beneficiaries and 5000 indirect beneficiaries. The project location will be in International Secondary School (ISS) and Arabic camp as well host community in Ngala LGA of Borno State Northeast Nigeria.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian context in northeast Nigeria remains complex. Continuous insecurity, in conjunction with government mandated camp closures, has created further displacement challenges. Many people are unable to return sustainably to their places of origin or have difficulties becoming self-reliant and require assistance to meet their most critical needs and enjoy their fundamental rights. Food insecurity has also become a significant challenge, and has cut down the basic needs of many persons who are already affected by the continuous insecurity. This combination of circumstances has created an environment ripe for the prevalence of Gender Base Violence (GBV). Particularly, women and girls became more vulnerable whom may be forced to adopt negative coping strategies to survive. The Protection analysis under the HNO 2024 highlighted that there is a significant need for Gender Based Violence (GBV) prevention and response services (39 per cent girls, 16 per cent boys, 35 per cent women, and 6 per cent people with disabilities). Of them 43 per cent are from host communities, 32 per cent are internally displaced persons, and the rest are returnees. It is important to note that GBV, sexual violence, child sexual abuse and intimate partner violence (IPV), continues to be prevalent across the BAY states.

2 - Activities

Conduct community workshops Men and boys from the community and IDP camps will be mobilized for the workshop. This will aid to promote gender equality between male and female within the project location.Discussion Group sessions (DGS) on GBV The discussion group session will comprise men and boys and issues around GBV (types of GBV in the community, root causes, consequences and preventive measures on GBV). This activity will be conducted on weekly basis (EMAP). Community awareness on GBVThis activity will be conducted three times a week and community members will be sensitized on the GBV and its negative effect. Community volunteers will reach out to members of the community through house-to-house sensitization. A total number of individuals will be targeted for this activity.Economic empowerment Survivors of GBV within the community/camp will be provided with IGA and they will be trained on financial management. Dignity Kits distributionDignity kits will be given to GBV survivors and vulnerable persons within project locations (Camps and host community).MHPSSSurvivors will be provided with mental health and psycho-social support, through counseling services and this activity will be conducted by our staff.ReferralsCollaborate with other organizations on the referral pathway to increase support for GBV prevention and survivor support.GBV case management and provision of specialized services: Direct support services: provide direct support such as the provision of information on available services and service providers, safe and confidential GBV case management, psychological first aid, and material assistance based on specific needs following eligibility criteria set.  Provision of PSS: The survivors after being assessed by the caseworkers and given their informed consent, will be provided with PSS services including individual and group counseling, wellness, and well-being activities, play and recreational activities, learning skills acquisition/vocational training, and basic literacy. The PSS activities will be carried out at Women and Girls Empowerment Centre (WGEC) providing a space for safe interaction and communication. This intervention aims at enhancing the beneficiaries’ quality of life, resilience, and recovery.  Coordination: coordinate with other service providers, should the case require interagency support. Caseworkers will continually work at updating the available referral pathways and service providers in the proposed locations of implementation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure that feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, the project will implement a targeted and inclusive feedback mechanism using the following strategies: community engagement and participation, suggestion boxes in implementing locations, organization toll free line, printing of IEC materials to inform program participants on how to get feedback. The aim to this feedback system will prioritize the voices of the most vulnerable community members, ensuring their perspectives are heard, valued, and integrated into project activities.
