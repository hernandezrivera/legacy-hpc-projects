Project Summary

More than six years after the conflict started, the needs of affected populations in
affected areas of eastern Ukraine exceed the capacity of the Government of
Ukraine (GoU) and require the continuation of vital assistance from the international humanitarian community.
Data collection and analyses conducted as part of NRC’s responses over the 2017-2020 period
show that the number of food-insecure people, although somewhat decreased, is still very
high. On average, the share of severely food insecure households ranges around 1% of the population in conflict-affected areas, but in localities next to the Line of Contact (LoC) it is
estimated to be at least 50% greater. As confirmed by several analyses done by relevant
stakeholders, the most vulnerable groups are single-member and elderly households,
families with several children or with disabled members, and those whose breadwinners
are chronically unemployed or without regular employment. Also, residents of rural
areas are significantly more vulnerable and surprisingly more often food insecure
compared to those living in urban or semi-urban areas. Due to limited access to land, difficulty accessing markets, unfavourable prices and depleted savings, agricultural households can hardly maintain their own food production. Given the high unemployment rate, especially among IDPs, in March 2019 only 46% of IDPs were
employed.
To be able to meet their basic needs, conflict-affected populations regularly resort to
negative coping strategies; according to IOM, 55% of IDPs were applying at least one
coping strategy in April–June 2020 in GCA.
To address these various needs, NRC will continue its comprehensive support to the most vulnerable conflict-affected population in GCA to enable their:
(1) access to food,
(2) restoration of productive assets, and
(3) promote employment and income generation opportunities.
The proposed intervention will cover Cluster Objective 1 (Ensure immediate access to food for the most vulnerable people affected by conflict or СOVID 19) and Cluster Objective 2 (Improve food security through the provision of emergency and time-critical agriculture assistance).
The target number of beneficiaries is 17 860, including 8 100 for unconditional food security and multi-purpose cash assistance.
NRC has been implementing activities addressing access to food and early recovery in
east Ukraine GCA since 2016. Programme activities are aligned with relevant GoU strategies and attempt to create synergies/linkages with the activities of recovery/development actors (HDN).
NRC will target the most vulnerable IDPs and
conflict-affected host communities along the LoC. Beneficiaries will be selected from different age and social groups prioritising elderly, female-headed or households with two or more children, persons with disabilities/medical cases and persons without a regular income.

1 - Needs

Although in recent years there were positive developments in Ukraine, such as a gradualincrease in salary and pensions’ levels, due to the protracted character of the conflict in the eastern part of the country, the population has been unable to regain their economic security. Food access remains a key concern in the affected areas; especially along the LoC and during the winter season. Because a high share of the population have incomes below the poverty level, food inflation, and very high actual food basket costs, access to food, particularly for IDPs and vulnerable families along the LoC, remains a challenge. As their savings and resources have been depleted many of them have to continue resorting to negative coping mechanisms, including reduction of food intake, disposal of productive assets, particularly livestock, borrowing money/buying food on credit, and reduction in other essential expenditures. According to IOM’s NMS Report, 55% of IDP households in April–June 2020 in GCAreported using at least one coping strategy. The most commonly used strategies werespending savings (40%), reducing essential health expenditures (23%), and borrowing money (20%). According to Cash Working Group (CWG) data, the income gap for the vulnerable population residing close to the line of contact has grown by 11% since October 2018 and the total annual increase for MEB reaching at least 12% by the end of 2020. Findings from the 2019 Ukraine Humanitarian Needs Overview show that more than 180,000 people in the GCA areas of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts remain food insecure – mainly households headed by women with children, elderly persons living alone and chronically ill or disabled people. Due to insufficient data and access difficulties in NGCA, the exact number of food-insecure people is unknown but is estimated at some 285,000 people. The Ukraine 2020 HNO highlights that the rural population close to the LoC are still food insecure due to limited financial resources to buy enoughFood; a consequence of very low levels of income per household member. Therefore, the needs forthe agricultural type of assistance in rural areas along the LoC remain high. According to HNO2019 (latest data available), around 21,000 rural households residing within 20 km from the LoC in GCA and 39,000 households in NGCA rely on self-production for their food and are in critical need ofagricultural inputs to support their income-generating and food production activities. Inline with these findings, and according to Economic Security Assessment published by REACHin March 2019, rural households were significantly more likely to have utilised at least one coping strategy (62%) than were urban households (40%). Likewise, households within 0-5 km of the LoC were more likely (58%) than were households between 5-20 km of LoC (38%). Rural areas within 0-5km of LoC were particularly likely to have used livelihoodscoping strategies, with 63% of households reporting having used at least one in the 30 days prior to assessment. COVID-19 and corresponding quarantine measures appear to have had an immediate impact on an already food insecure area (0–5 km) along the contact line. As reported by IOM (Impact of COVID-19 Disease and Related Restrictions on Small Business and Vulnerable Populations in Eastern Conflict Area, April 2020) and confirmed by NRC’s research (Food Security and Livelihoods Assessment, May 2020), 76% of households stated that they had been impacted by COVID-19, and 82% households were impacted by increased prices of food and hygiene items. About 38% of households showed decreased monthly income compared to baseline data. Within these households, the average decrease in monthly income was approximately 34%.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

The response will be focused on achieving the four main goals/outcomes via a set of activities, as follows: 1. The most vulnerable and food insecure conflict-affected households have access to food – Distribution of cash grants for basic food needs: • Season - winter months, when needs are greater; beneficiary households identified and registered per FSLC vulnerability criteria, prioritising those residing along the LoC (Cash Assistance). • Amount of cash grants established in line with FSLC and CWG’s recommendations. • Cash transfers to be done via national postal system Ukrposhta as direct delivery to beneficiaries, to minimise the risks,• Selected households receive cash grants labelled for meeting basic food needs, • Post distribution monitoring (PDM) carried out 2 - 3 weeks after distributions. 2. Improved food security and self-sustenance of the conflict-affected population via restoration of agricultural productive capacity along the LoC – via technical and financial assistance: • Early spring - provision of labelled cash grants for procurement of agricultural inputs to selected vulnerable households for agricultural activities (Agriculture Inputs). • Year-round: Provision of cash grants to support backyard poultry and kitchen gardening activities (Backyard Farming Support).• Year-round: Provision of livelihood cash grants for income-oriented food producers (households) (Sustainable Livelihood Support).• Year-round: Community-based livelihood initiatives, covering market restoration, value chain development and infrastructure rehabilitation (Community-based Livelihood Projects).• Year-round: Provision of agricultural training and field advisory assistance to support the application of adequate technologies and secure good yields, quality and incomes (Training and Advisory). Coordination: NRC will continue working closely and coordinating with the FSLC, CWG and relevant stakeholders throughout the project’s planning andimplementation. Gender Mainstreaming: NRC will ensure that there is the active participation of women, girls, men and boys in all project activities. The programme will take into consideration unique needs of individuals/social groups to promote women’s role as producers and providers of income, and equitable access to food and livelihoodopportunities to the elderly, adults, boys, girls, men and women. Female-headed households will be prioritised, and the majority of the project beneficiaries will be women. A participatory approach will be used in analysing needs/preferences and in identifying themost vulnerable. Beneficiary data will be disaggregated as follows: elderly (60+;male/female); adults (18-59 male/female); children (5-17 male/female) and children (<5male/female).
