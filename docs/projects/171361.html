Project Summary

The intervention will support the most vulnerable farmers in Rafah, Middle Area and Khan
Yunis governorates whose livelihoods are impacted by reduced capacity to acquire basic
inputs and whose productive assets are affected by natural and man-made hazards. As
such, the productive capacities and food security of small-scale farmers will be improved
through a mix of cash assistance and in-kind modalities, thus contributing to increased
resilience for themselves and their wider communities. The project is informed by ACTED
assessments (updated in September 2020) and assessments by The Union of Agricultural
Work Committees’ (UAWC) undertaken in August/September 2020, extensive
consultations with key stakeholders, and recommendations from the Ministry of
Agriculture (MoA), Food Security Sector (FSS) and Cash Working Group (CWG) in Gaza.
Specifically, ACTED and UAWC will implement a multi-pronged approach to protect livelihoods and strengthen food security through the following means:
• Providing emergency multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to help HHs suffering from limited access to food and income opportunities meet their basic food needs and alleviate the impact of the COVID-19 crisis;
• Facilitating the full and/or partial rehabilitation of land and agricultural assets using a self-repair approach through the provision of cash-for-assets directly to selected farmers and cash for-work to external workers;
• Rehabilitating agricultural water ponds and wells for improved water storage to improve agricultural productivity and income through conditional cash assistance;
• Facilitating the rehabilitation of greenhouses to increase the growing season through the
provision of cash-for-assets directly to selected farmers and cash-for-work to external
workers;
• Strengthening existing Community-based Protection Committees (CBPCs) through the
conduct of refresher trainings to mainstream protection principles within their
communities

The combined population of the five target localities, namely Shokat as Sufi (17800),
Al Bayuk (9800), Orayba (4000), Wadi as Salqa (9500) and Khuzaa (12500) at
53600 will indirectly benefit from the intervention through the injection of cash into the
local economy, the increase of fresh produce within local markets, and the enhanced
protection delivered by the CBPCs. More specifically, the intervention will directly benefit
1020 HH beneficiaries/5628 individuals through the following: 150 farmers and their
respective HHs (825 individuals) will benefit from rehabilitation of their land through the
provision of cash-for-assets directly to selected farmers and cash-for-work to external
workers to work on the land of the selected farmers, as well as increased food security
through MPCA to meet their immediate basic food needs; 210 farmers and their respective
HHs (1155 individuals) will benefit from the rehabilitation of 30 water ponds and 4 wells
for improved water storage; 50 vulnerable farmers and their respective HHs (275
individuals) will benefit from the rehabilitation of their greenhouses; 230 skilled and 380
unskilled workers and their respective HHs (3355 individuals) will benefit from the
provision of short-term employment through cash-for-work and finally 3 CBPCs (1 per
governorate) comprised of 18 members will benefit from refresher training to better
ensure mainstreaming of protection principles. It is expected that the 5628 beneficiaries will be comprised of 146 PwD.
The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, the disabled and the elderly. Gender principles will be mainstreamed throughout the entire action and CBPCs will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as GBV with a particular focus given to women and children. Finally, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be core to the proposed Action.

1 - Needs

1.6 million Gazans are considered food insecure, representing 72% of the food insecure within oPt (HNO 2021). The unemployment rate in the Gaza Strip during the second quarter of 2020 reached 49.1%, up from 45.5% in the previous quarter, thus reaching a historical high. This rising unemployment and poverty, largely due to the economic slowdown caused by COVID-19 restrictions, has severely disrupted the food system at all levels, particularly among small producers. More specifically, as a consequence of multiple episodes of war, associated loss of land and damage to irrigation systems and agricultural assets combined with existing restrictions imposed on cultivable land and imports/exports, lack of financial capacity and reluctance towards investment, Gaza’s agricultural sector has shrunk to below 5% of GDP. Vulnerable farmers have been particularly affected and have lost their main means of livelihood. Some 3,000 farmers owning or working land located up to 1,000 meters from Israel’s perimeter fence face significant insecurity due to Israel's "open fire" policy, discouraging their access to, and development of, their assets. This has significantly affected the ability of farmers to produce their own food and to supply local markets with fresh produce. The assessment (originally undertaken in March 2020 and updated / verified in September 2020)was conducted through a qualitative and participatory approach, consisting of Focus Groups Discussions (FGDs) with 91 farmers in the targeted communities and 13 Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) with officials and other NGOs. Due to the onset of COVID-19, the updated needs assessment in September 2020 was comprised solely of KIIs undertaken by phone. To ensure that the distinct needs, priorities and concerns of females were incorporated into project design, the needs assessment ensured strong representation of females within the FGDs. Further, during implementation, their needs will be ensured through active consultations during follow up needs assessment in which the BoQs and workplans are developed, to ensure that their self-identified most pressing needs are fed into project design, with at least one male and one female per HH consulted during this process to ensure the integration of gender-specific needs and concerns. Further, boys, girls, PwDs, the elderly and other marginalized groups will also be consulted during this process to ensure that their needs are similarly reflected into any assistance provided. CPBC members will be present during these follow-up assessments and the CRM will be made available to all beneficiaries who might feel that their needs were not reflected through this process. Finally, ACTED’s AMEU will measure indicators disaggregated by gender such as # of farmers (males/ females) beneficiaries receiving inputs and services to hold ACTED accountable to ensuring assistance is tailored by gender. The assessment showed the following constraints: - Water scarcity due to the reduction of water level in the underground wells, lack of collecting water ponds, and high levels of water salinity do not support the production of most crops and limit the farmers’ choice of crops, whilst also reducing the life of the irrigation network and causing weak harvests. - The lack of good irrigation water and the monopoly of a few large-scale farmers of the water wells made the cost of transporting the water to the small-scale farmers high (2.5 ILS/cubic meter). - The lack of suitable water wells and ponds in the eastern areas of Rafah governorate and further destruction of land and property due to the spraying of herbicides to the farmland. - The high price, low quality and availability (due to the Israeli ‘dual use’ list) of pesticides, fertilizers, seeds and agricultural equipment strongly affect the productive capacity of farms throughout the agricultural season. As a result of this un-supportive environment, farmers lost their sources of income and are food insecure or vulnerable to food insecurity.- The COVID-19 crisis is disproportionately affecting vulnerable groups and deepening pre-existing vulnerabilities, especially for women, the disabled and the elderly. In August and September 2020, UAWC undertook a needs assessment through consultations with the MoA and UAWC Agricultural Committees. The following findings were produced:- Greenhouses needing rehabilitation: 3000 in Rafah, 2500 in Khan Yunis and 700 Middle Area.- Donums of land needing rehabilitation: 7500 in Rafah, 2000 in Khan Yunis and 1500 in Middle Area. - Water ponds needing rehabilitation: 200 in Rafah, 800 in Khan Yunis and 120 in Middle Area.

2 - Activities

A1 - Beneficiary Selection: 290 beneficiaries will be selected through 3 calls for applications (150 for A2/3, 90 for A4 and 50 for A5) advertised through multiple platforms including information sessions to introduce eligibility criteria and the overall application process. Technical and socio-economic assessments will be conducted for those confirmed as eligible by the MoA. A panel will score the assessed farmers to select the most vulnerable. ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to ensure fair selection. The final list will be shared with the MoA and key actors to avoid duplication. UAWC agronomists will produce tailored BOQs and clear workplans. A2 - Provision of emergency MPCA: ACTED will provide 1 instalment in local currency via bank transfer of MPCA to the 150 farmers under A3 to help meet their immediate basic needs, allowing them to fully utilise assistance for its intended purpose. In line with the Gaza CWG, the agreed transfer value for MPCA is 60% of the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) at 1,196 ILS (350 USD). A3 - Urgent rehabilitation of 300 donums of land and agricultural assets: ACTED will provide 150 farmers with cash assistance to facilitate the rehabilitation of 300 donums of land (2 donums per farmer on average). A self-repair approach will be used through the provision of cash-for-assets (average 3,364 ILS (1,000 USD)) directly to selected farmers to purchase agricultural inputs needed for rehabilitation works such as small irrigation network components, fertilizers, seeds etc, and cash-for-work to external workers to assist the workplan. Cash-for-assets will be disbursed in 3 instalments (40%, 40%, 20%) via bank transfer/cheque. The workplan will be monitored through visits to each HH by ACTED to review progress and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel subsequent instalments. Each farmer will benefit from the labour of 3 workers external to the HH who will receive cash-for-work. As such, 450 workers (150 skilled and 300 unskilled) will benefit from cash-for-work (average 1,050 ILS (312 USD)) paid to them in 1 instalment upon successful completion of the work. A4 -Rehabilitation of 30 agricultural water ponds and 4 wells (inclusive of installation of solar panels for irrigation which will be strongly coordinated with MoA and FAO): 90 vulnerable farmers (30 groups of 3 on average) will benefit from improved water ponds. A self-repair approach will be used through the provision of cash-for assets (average 5618 ILS (1670 USD)) directly to each group and cash-for work to external workers to assist. As such, 60 workers (30 skilled and 30 unskilled) will benefit from cash-for-work (average 726 ILS (216 USD)) paid to them in 1 instalment upon successful completion of the work. Each group will be expected to sign an agreement for their joint undertaking, appointing 1 member to receive the cash instalments and committing to adhere to their tailored BoQs and workplans. The workplan will be monitored through visits by UAWC and will provide corrective actions. 120 farmers will benefit from the rehabilitation of 4 communal wells nominated by MoA. A competitive tender will be launched to select the contractor(s) for this work. A5: Facilitating the rehabilitation of 50 greenhouses: To extend the growing season, a self-repair approach will be used through the provision of cash-for assets (average 3365 ILS (1000 USD)) directly to 50 selected farmers and cash-for work to external workers to assist workplans. As such, 100 workers (50 skilled and 50 unskilled) will benefit from cash-for-work (average 726 ILS (216 USD)) paid in 1 instalment upon successful completion of the work.A6: Strengthening 3 CBPCs through the conduct of refresher training. 3 existing CBPCs will be strengthened through refresher training, including on PSEA. UAWC will also administer training on food security and best agricultural practices. CBPCs will be tasked with conducting awareness raising sessions to disseminate information to ensure any assets rehabilitated or installed are efficiently maintained to maximize impact. 30% of the CBPC’s membership will remain composed of females.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. Specifically, ACTED’s Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) is independent from the Program Department and will be in charge of reinforcing the projects’ accountability throughout the project cycle management by regularly assessing project implementation. AMEU is an essential part of ACTED’s project management structure, providing a mechanism to crosscheck and give recommendations on the work of the project team and partners. AMEU leads assessment of baseline situations of beneficiaries through data collection, monitors ongoing implementation through regular field visits and consultations with beneficiaries, and evaluates the outcome of the activities. This methodology ensures the AMEU uses an outcome monitoring approach to analyse the effect of the action on target groups. ACTED will measure the following indicators: # of farmers (males/ females) beneficiaries receiving inputs and services (# of individuals not HHs); # of dunums made productive or improved production capacity; # of water wells rehabilitated or provided; m3 of water storage rehabilitated/provided (tanks, ponds); # of male and female individuals benefiting from CfW; # of male and female individuals benefiting from other conditional cash support; # of male and female individuals benefiting from unconditional cash support; % of female members are participating actively in the community based agriculture committees; # of Community-based Protection Committees (CBPCs) reinforced throughout a refreshing training, % of CBPCs' members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the refreshing training; % of HH reporting that the humanitarian assistance delivered is appropriate and satisfying. ACTED’s project management team will use the Project Management Framework (PMF) tool to plan and monitor implementation to assess progress and highlight blocking points. The action will promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency, in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM will enable beneficiaries and other stakeholders to provide feedback and suggestions directly to ACTED staff through a phone line, post, email, and complaint boxes in relevant locations, as well as directly to partner’s staff members. The CRM should ensure that beneficiaries can easily and without fear of repercussion communicate cases of abuse of power by financial institutions, community leaders and/or other key actors involved in the cash-transfer program and/or aspects of program and/or transfer modality that inadvertently have negative implications. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Financial monitoring will be undertaken by all project partners and then consolidated by ACTED, with external audits of the action taking place on an annual basis. ACTED staff will request from UAWC monthly reporting on activities on top of contractual obligations to ensure all indicators are on track and register the beneficiaries’ satisfaction and any difficulties encountered. This monthly reporting will minimise the risk of delays in the activities’ implementation, that any change in the implementation of the project is notified and approved by oPt HF and finally that all indicators are on track and if needed corrective measures are taken to reach the target. Finally, it will enable ACTED to provide comprehensive and clear interim and final reports to oPt HF.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED and UAWC will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, whatsapp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co-applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that co-applicants learn from the feedback provided through this process.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. In particular, ACTED and UAWC will undertake regular visits to the communities and beneficiary HHs to ensure that protection concerns are incorporated throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for women, children and other vulnerable groups. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis. Finally, all activities will be conducted in strict adherence of COVID-19 mitigation measures including social distancing and personal protective equipment (PPE) to minimise the risk of COVID-19 transmission among beneficiaries, staff and stakeholder alike

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have specific needs and assistance will therefore be adapted accordingly. To this end, ACTED’s Protection Officer will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and family and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project. ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process and all applicants will be notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM. Similarly, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted during the assessments to ensure BoQs and workplans are responsive to their self-identified most pressing needs. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per HH will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are taken into account, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, the elderly and other marginalised persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities. In addition, CBPC members and ACTED staff will be present to ensure the integration of HH concerns into BoQ and workplan design. In the event beneficiaries are dissatisfied with their respective BoQ and/or workplan an appeal mechanism will be offered through the CBPCs and ACTED’s CRM. If the CBPCs or ACTED/UAWC staff consider the request justified, a new BoQ and/or workplan will be produced by a different agronomist. To mitigate this, all staff involved in the assessment process will undergo extensive refresher training in protection standards prior to the assessments to ensure equal and impartial access to assistance. Finally, the project will deliberately target the most vulnerable groups for inclusion into the program. For instance, women involved in farming activities in the targeted areas will be approached and highly encouraged to participate in the CBPCs to ensure that membership remains at 40% female at a minimum. After all, women represent a significant but underserved segment of the agricultural workforce in the target areas. Similar efforts will be made to ensure that vulnerabilities do not act as a barrier to accessing aid. For instance, CBPCs will conduct HH visits to assist in the completion and collection of applications for the vulnerable (including elderly, people with disabilities) that might be unable to reach the registration site.
