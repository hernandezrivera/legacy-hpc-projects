Project Summary

This project seeks to address key child protection needs and gaps linked to the crisis in NWSW Cameroon, through targeted PSS and case management to meet the needs of 29,780 vulnerable children and caregivers, and through working with communities and local partners to strengthen the capacity of communities to be able to prevent, identify and respond to child protection risks in their communities.
In the NW and SW, specific focus will be on the most at-risk children, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed groups (CAAG), and children at-risk of gender-based violence (GBV). They will be supported through targeted case management services to meet the most acute needs, including family tracing and reunification (FTR) and the provision of alternative care. In addition, community-based child-friendly spaces will provide safe spaces for children to play and learn key age-appropriate and gender-sensitive life skills, improving their resilience and wellbeing. Community-based child protection capacity will be further strengthened through the establishment of inclusive community-based child protection committees (CBCPCs), sensitisation of caregivers and key community members, and strengthening of community referral pathways through training of community volunteers, teachers and social workers.

In the West and Littoral, the focus will be on providing support to underserved IDP children, particularly UASC and those vulnerable to risks such as SGBV and child labour. Integrating child protection alongside Street Child’s education activities, this will include the establishment of child-friendly spaces, training of teachers to be able to provide PFA and PSS, strengthening of community referral pathways, and providing case management support (including family tracing and reunification and/or provision of alternative care).

1 - Besoins / Needs

As a result of the ongoing conflict and displacement, the child protection risks in the NW and SW regions are severe: an estimated 1.1 million children in the are in need of child protection support, including more than 16,000 unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) (MSNA, 2020). The conflict and ongoing human rights violations continue put children at risk of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation, including recruitment into armed groups, SGBV, early marriage and child labour. This is compounded by the lack of education opportunities in the NW/SW which has increased children’s exposure to risks, not least due to the high number of unaccompanied children sent away from their families to try to access education elsewhere. Girls and boys of different ages face different risks; girls more likely to be exposed to SGBV and early pregnancy, for example, and boys (particularly of adolescent age) most vulnerable to recruitment into armed groups or detention. The psychosocial needs of children in the conflict-affected communities are high, with the breakdown of community support and protection offered by school environments worsening the psychological impacts and distress faced. Street Child will provide psychosocial support and opportunities for children to play through child-friendly spaces, with community members trained as PSS facilitators to lead age-appropriate activities and provide key life skills lessons, as well as able to identify and refer children in need of more specialised support. PSS activities will be designed in a gender- , age- and disability-sensitive way to be able to respond to the different risks and capacities faced by different groups of children, and to promote inclusion and reduce stigmatisation. Case management of acutely at-risk children, including UASC, CAAG and survivors of SGBV, has been identified as a gap in the existing response in the region. There is a need to improve case management in line with global standards, including FTR services, inter-sector referrals and follow-up, and provision of alternative care. Street Child will build the case management capacity of partners, including in the provision of alternative care or family tracing and reunification for UASC and reintegration support for CAAG, to ensure that the needs of these children are addressed in line with best practices. In addition, referral pathways will be strengthened to promote quicker and more effective inter-sectoral referrals to specialised support services where needed, with timely follow-up. In the context of NWSW, where stable access to hard-to-reach communities is a challenge (including as a result of COVID-19 in 2020), Street Child aims to build the capacity of communities to prevent, identify, refer and respond to child protection concerns. This includes through awareness raising on key child protection topics (such as SGBV and child labour), the activation and training of community-based child protection committees, and the strengthening of community referral pathways. All community-based activities will seek to be inclusive of different gender, age and disability groups. Responding to the identified need of limited capacity of local partners, Street Child will partner with and build the technical and institutional capacity of local partners in line with global standards and best practices, aiming to holistically improve the quality of child protection service provision in the region. This response will target the NW, SW, West and Littoral regions. In the NW/SW, a key focus will be on case management and building of case management capacity in relation to the ongoing conflict. In the West and Littoral, child protection activities (including PSS support and case management for UASC) will be integrated with education-focused activities targeting IDP children from NW/SW, and specific IDP households identified as particularly at-risk of child labour and exploitation will receive prevention support.
