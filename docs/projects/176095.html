Project Summary

The South Sudan humanitarian needs overview of 2021 notes that an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices within the year. Some of the main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. As of the 2021 WASH severity mapping, 1.9 million people spread across different counties and states in the country are currently in WASH severity level 5 while 4 million are in severity level 4. Centre for emergency and Development Support (CEDS) is proposing to carry out WASH interventions in Aweil East, Aweil South and Aweil North Counties in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Toni East and Tonj South counties in Warrap State, Cueibet and Rumbek counties in Lakes states, Jur River and Wau counties in Western Bahr El Ghazal in response to the humanitarian needs to contribute to mitigation of WASH related morbidities and mortalities. CEDS intends to reach a total of 45,720 beneficiaries (19,217 males, 10,401 females and 16,002 children) with different WASH interventions for a period of 12 months as from March 2020. We propose to carry activities including but not limited to minor and major rehabilitations of boreholes and water points in and around nutrition facilities, form or re-establish and strengthen the capacity of water user committees, construct and or rehabilitate sanitation facilities and carry out hygiene and sanitation promotion in the targeted locations. The WASH and Nutrition integration is intended to rehabilitate and construct boreholes and intuitional VIP latrines at or near OTP/TSFP sites, conduct training of Nutrition team members on minimum WASH aspects and distribute lifesaving WASH NFIs/ household famine prevention packages to the mothers/Caregivers of children with MAM/SAM cases. CEDS will intentionally ensure that Covid-19 preventive measures are integrated within this programming and Covid-19 preventive guidelines and regulations are observed throughout these project implementations. It is expected that after this intervention, the targeted communities will have access to safe and accessible portable water, as well as dignified and context appropriate sanitation facilities. It is also expected that the targeted communities will have knowledge and will be practicing good hygiene and sanitation practices.

1 - Needs

Fragmented humanitarian presence and access, a lack of maintenance of essential infrastructure, new waves of conflict and displacement, and climatic hazards have severely compromised the functionality and adequacy of WASH and agricultural infrastructure and practices in South Sudan and created an urgent need for emergency assistance. The overall environmental health, hygiene and sanitation situation is particularly poor in target communities. Average water consumption level is 5.8 l/p/d—far lower than the Sphere and WASH Sector-recommended 7.5-15 l/p/d; 68% of the population has no access to latrines and hand-washing facilities. Communities experience increased incidence of waterborne and water-related diseases (e.g., schistosomiasis, malaria, diarrhea), widespread open defecation, and decreased health and increased environmental contamination associated with poor sanitation and hygiene. In 2021, the HNO notes that an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers lack access to sufficient and quality WASH services. As access to water sources is predominately communal in South Sudan, the limited access to WASH services impacts all population groups. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities. High WASH severity can be attributed to a reliance on surface water, a lack of sanitation facilities, and areas witnessing high insecurity. Though a number of counties are counties in the country are affected, a number of counties in Lakes and Warrap State including Rumbek East, Cueibet, Tonj East and Tonj North are currently classified in WASH severity level 4 and 5 thus calling for urgent WASH intervention

2 - Project activities

CEDS will carry out the following activities to address the needs: 1. Conduct consultative inception meetings with Subnational Clusters, concerned state key line ministries, and community leaders/stakeholders to discuss different roles and responsibilities to be played from the onset, during and after the implementation of the project. 2. Conduct at least 60 focus group discussions (FGDs) with various beneficiary groups women, girls, individuals living with disabilities by age and gender disaggregation to have a consensus of the suitable sites for both water sources and sanitation facilities that will be rehabilitated through this project to mitigate and avert all forms of gender-based violence and harms against women, girls and individuals living with disabilities. 3. Conduct a fresh or refresher training of 30 hand pump mechanics, at least 5 pump mechanics per county for the 6 IPC phase 4/5 counties in 3 states to carry out the rehabilitation of the non-functional handpumps in accordance with standard specifications and guidelines of the respective state for quality assurance. 4. Establishment/training of water management 60 committees of seven-member each (420 members, representing 70% women, 29% men and 1% of individuals living with disabilities) for every community borehole which will be rehabilitated to strengthen the capacity and create sense of ownership of the beneficiary communities to assume full responsibility of operation and maintenance (O&M) of their water points instead of reliance on external support for everything. 5. Carry out minor and major rehabilitation of 60 non-functional handpumps, which includes replacement of worn-out parts, reconstruction of pump platforms, channels and animal troughs sited in or around the OTPs/Nutrition Sites in the 6 target IPC 4/5 counties. 6. Carry out water quality analysis with Biological/Bacteriological, Chemical and Physical parameters for 60 rehabilitated water sources (boreholes fitted with handpumps) to ensure provision of safe drinking water for the vulnerable communities indeed. 7. Construction of 30 new gender-segregated pit latrine blocks of 2 stances in OTPs/Nutrition Sites, 5 per county in 9 counties of four states. 6. Rehabilitation of 30 existing pit latrines in OTPs/Nutrition Sites. 8. Installation of 120 handwashing facilities for the newly constructed and rehabilitated latrines. 9. Training of 30 Community Hygiene Promoters/Volunteers, 5 per count and 10 per state to carry out dissemination of integrated hygiene promotion and Covid-19 messaging to raise awareness. 10. Conduct integrated hygiene promotion and Covid-19 awareness-raising, emphasizing on key hygiene messages and risk communication and community engagement at the frontline OTPs/Nutrition sites, mothers/Caregivers of children with SAM/MAM in mother-to-mother support groups and households surrounding the facilities, targeting a total of 120,000 individuals, including the 3,000 households that will be reached through household-to-household visits. 11. Training of Nutrition Team on minimum WASH aspects in OTP/TSFP sites in 6 priority counties. 12. Distribution of assorted lifesaving WASH NFIs to 3,000 most vulnerable mothers/caregivers of children admitted and discharged with SAM cases. 13. Training/demonstration on menstrual hygiene management (MHM) and distribution of 3,000 MHM Kits for adolescent school girls and young women of menstrual age. 14. Establishment of feedback mechanisms with nominated focal point persons among trained water management committee members and hygiene promoters at the county and community level with clear roles and responsibilities and conduct basic training on complaints, feedback, management of GBV/SEA cases, referrals, and trauma healing support. 15. Conduct at least 2 joint monitoring field visits to project sites with relevant state line ministries and Subnational Clusters (WASH & Nutrition).

3 - Project indicators

The success of the project will be measured through the following indicators: 1. # of consultative inception meetings conducted. 2. # of focus group discussions (FGDs) conducted with various beneficiary groups; 3. # of hand pump mechanics trained per country/state; 4. # of water management committees establishment/trained and functional; 5. # of existing hand pumps repaired/rehabilitated; 6. # of rehabilitated boreholes with water quality analyzed for Biological/Bacteriological, Chemical and Physical parameters; 7. # of new gender-segregated pit latrine blocks of 2 stance constructed in OTPs/Nutrition Sites; 8. # of existing institutional pit latrine blocks rehabilitated in OTPs/Nutrition Sites; 9. # of handwashing facilities procured and installed for the new constructed and rehabilitated latrines; 10. # of Hygiene Promoters/Volunteers trained; 10. # of individuals reached through integrated hygiene promotion and Covid-19 awareness messaging at the frontline OTPs/Nutrition sites; 11. # of mothers/Caregivers of children with SAM/MAM cases treated and discharged received WASH NFIs; 12. # of households around facilities reached with key hygiene promotion messages through household-to-household visits; 13. # of Nutrition Team members trained on minimum WASH aspects in OTP/TSFP sites; 14. # of adolescent girls and young women of menstrual age trained and received MHM Kits; 14. # of feedback mechanisms centres with focal point persons established and functional; 15. # of joint monitoring field visits to project sites conducted.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CEDS will during the information sharing sessions inform the beneficiaries about its policy of zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse and tell the beneficiaries how to report suspected cases of sexual exploitation or abuse. CEDS will also produce IEC materials on PSEA and distribute to the targeted beneficiaries. The project committees that will be formed will also be trained on PSEA and how to report the same. The committee will also have an individual tasked as the focal point for PSEA issues of the project implementation. CEDS has in place a PSEA policy in which all the new staff are inducted. CEDS will ensure that all the staff for this proposed project are inducted in this policy and the consent to adhere to the same. CEDS will also ensure that all the other stakeholders working together with the organisation on this project are made aware of and in cases where they will have a direct touch with the beneficiaries, they consent to adhere to these policies. On a routine basis, CEDS will carry out refresher trainings and awareness sessions for the staff and ensure that they are aware of developments and other new initiative in terms of PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CEDS will make intentional efforts to ensure that different categories of the targeted beneficiaries are given a chance to air their views on how they want the project implemented. CEDS will carry out community meetings with the different vulnerable groups such as the disabled, the elderly and women on their own to get their views about the project that will then be incorporated into the project implementation processes. CEDS will also have representatives of the different vulnerable groups in both the project implementation committees and so that their interests are represented in the project implementation processes.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CEDS appreciates that it key to share information with the targeted beneficiaries for effective implementation of the project. CEDS will arrange community meetings where information about the project will shared. Additionally, CEDS will use community leaders to pass project information. Prior to distributions of NFIS and borehole rehabilitations, CEDS will hold community meetings where beneficiaries will be told on their roles and how to give feedbacks in any case they are not satisfied with what is being done. CEDS will equally set up different feedback channels including but not limited to hotlines, feedback volunteers, suggestion boxes in order to provide the targeted beneficiaries with the opportunity to give feedback about the project. CEDS will also use the project committees to whom the beneficiaries can give their feedback about the project. CEDS AAP officer will also hold feedback collection meetings with different groups and the feedback collected will be incorporated into the project.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback that will be collected from the project through the various channels including the feedback hotlines, help desks, feedback volunteers and routine feedback meetings that will be set up will be channeled to AAP officer who will then sort and classify them according to their nature. The feedback will then be cascaded to the various levels of project management team. Those feedback that will need immediate response will be acted on as fast as possible and feedback shared with the respective beneficiaries who raised them. Sensitive feedback will be handled with the sensitivity that it requires. All in all, the feedback that will be received during the project implementation will be used to better the project implementation process for instance distribution processes, beneficiary selection criteria among others will be modelled based on the feedback received from the beneficiaries. Feedback collected could also be used in the design of the new project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CEDS, will always comply with the core humanitarian standards (CHS) to assess and document the level of risks and conduct a regular risks analysis, their likelihood throughout the project implementation to ensure that necessary protection measures are put in place. Constant consultation with all relevant key stakeholders, including beneficiary community influential leaders, Women, Youth and religious groups will be conducted throughout the project implementation to avert any foreseen risks: CEDS will carry out specific activities including trainings to the water user committees and other community leaders to mitigate the risk of sexual exploitation and violence against women, girls and persons living with disabilities. CEDS has a set of policies in place, which includes the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Staff Code of Conduct, Conflict of Interest among others. All CEDS employees have been trained on PSEA and other organization’s policies; both new and old CEDS’ employees undergo a mandatory routine training on PSEA after every three months and are required to sign a set of such policies along with their contracts every time they sign or their contracts are renewed to strengthen the policies on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse. CEDS at the start of this project will conduct a refresher training for all the staff and volunteers on this policy to enhance tier awareness on the same. This policy will as well be shared with other organizational collaborators. CEDS’ policies include mechanisms where cases of sexual abuse including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and GBV will be reported at community level through the established mechanisms. CEDS has in place whistle-blowing mechanisms for receiving and reporting sexual exploitation issues, where it deliberately ensures that all SEA and any other forms of sexual violence, exploitation, and GBV are handled after careful investigation with substantial evidence. It’s expected that these policies, the mandatory scheduled training on PSEA as well as the channels for receiving and reporting the cases are considered to mitigate the risk of exposure to sexual violence and abuse throughout project implementation.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This project will address critical gaps in the WASH Services and contribute in the decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3, 4 and 5 through the provision of improved WASH services in or near OTP/TSFP sites: distribution of life-saving WASH NFIs to mothers/Caregivers of children with MAM/SAM cases, orientation/training of Nutrition Team members on minimum aspects of WASH, rehabilitation/construction of boreholes and institutional VIP latrines, awareness-raising through the dissemination of integrated hygiene promotion, sanitation and Covid-19 messages with the provision of handwashing station to improve hygiene and develop the culture of handwashing with clean water and soap during critical times thus prevents spread of diseases among children under age 5 who are the most vulnerable groups in the IPC 3, 4 and 5.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will involve all target beneficiary groups, women, girls, children, the elderly, individuals with disabilities in many ways. From the initial design phase of the project, consultative meetings will be conducted with all groups to listen to their views so that their viewpoints are documented and reflected in the project design. Adolescent school girls and young women will be trained on MHM and receive MHM kits, in the design of latrines, e.g. provision design for persons living with disabilities or special needs. Persons with disabilities face not only social and physical discrimination but also infrastructure lack design thus, CEDS will engage and involve persons with disabilities in decision-making during the design of WASH infrastructures to accommodate their special needs as well as consideration to be given to the identified most vulnerable groups during the WASH NFIs distribution.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

This project will greatly contribute in the decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4 in an integrated approach with Nutrition. CEDS will work closely with Nutrition and Health Clusters to identify areas of concerns and design a well-coordinated response that will include but not limited to provision of improved WASH services, orientation/training of Nutrition Team members on minimum aspects of WASH, rehabilitation/construction of boreholes and institutional VIP latrines at or near OTP/TSFP sites and hygiene and sanitation promotion with provision hand washing facilities in the OTP/TSFP sites to improve hygiene and develop the culture of handwashing with clean water and soap during critical times thus prevents spread of diseases among children under age 5.
