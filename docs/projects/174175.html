Project Summary


DRC intervention is focused on activities that will minimize and mitigate the impact of Explosive Ordnance in conflict affected communities through the implementation of humanitarian mine action activities especially Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE), NTS Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD).

The EORE activities target the returnees, IDPs, and host communities by raising their awareness of the threat from Explosive Ordnance (EO) with Covid-19 message incorporated and to promote safe behaviours and good practices among the large number of civilians at risk particularly the children in the shortest possible time. Further, Non-Technical Survey (NTS) will be conducted to identify, and report not only confirmed or suspected hazardous areas to enable freedom of movement, but also to gather and prepare community’s needs assessments, enable early recovery activities and expansion of humanitarian operations. In addition, the project will support in building the capacity of the Police EOD personnel in Northeast Nigeria to enable them dispose explosive ordnance (EO) to prevent harm to civilians and to remove obstructions to securing livelihoods needed for recovery from conflict. The proposed project will have three integrated components with a transversal advisory axis focused on EO mitigation. The three components are Explosive Ordnance Risk Education, Non-Technical Survey, and Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD). DRC will closely work with the relevant stakeholders to (a) promote safe behavior and good practices to reduce accident with EO through delivery of tailored awareness messages; (b) identify and report confirmed or suspected hazardous areas and prepare; and (c) build the capacity of the Nigerian Police EOD Unit to dispose dangerous items following strict measures. As such, the proposed intervention will provide multiplier effect as it ensures returnees, IDPs and host communities are provided with the knowledge that helps to act safely in the event of encountering explosive hazards in their environment. Also, to determine their needs and the prioritization for future intervention and clearance operations. Further, build the capacity of Nigerian Police EOD Unit in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States to dispose Explosive Ordnance (EO). Hence, the intervention will create a secure environment for vulnerable people, development, releasing contaminated land for productive use by removing blockages to humanitarian and development assistance.
Humanitarian mine action is a precursor for peacebuilding, stabilization and sustainable development. As such, the proposed intervention is a key contributing element of the nexus between peace, health and security and development.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in BAY States in the northeast states have been experiencing a significant humanitarian crisis since 2009, which has resulted from the on-going armed conflict between Armed Opposition Groups (AOG), Nigerian Security Forces and the Multi-National Joint Task Force (MNJTF). A decade long conflict has left north-east Nigeria littered with dangerous contamination from a wide range of Explosive Ordnance such as: rocket-propelled grenades, artillery shells, mortar Bombs, air-dropped weapons, and grenades that did not explode or have been abandoned. Although the full scope and scale of contamination is unknown, previous studies provide an indication that contamination is considerable . Most of those munitions that were fired did not explode on impact, resulting in unexploded ordnance contaminating communities. This has contributed to the rise of the direct risk of accidents with Explosive Ordnance, especially to the IDP population in the BAY states. An undetermined scale of contamination from a wide range of ammunition that have been fired, used, launched and propelled but not exploded plus others abandoned during this campaign remain in the ground as a risk for host communities, IDPs and returnees. The Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020 reported that in populated areas, there were 27 incidents of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and IEDs with 50 civilians killed and 111 injured. The majority of the incidents are with improvised items, especially antivehicle and antipersonnel mines of an improvised nature and Person Borne IED though conventional weapons, including mortars, rockets and grenades, poses grave risks to civilians and are readily available to design IED. In addition, according to United Nation Mine Action Service (UNMAS) in 2020, and after ten years of violent conflict, explosive devices continue to put millions of people at risk of death and injuries in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. From January 2020 to 24 September 2020, 334 civilian and non-civilian fatalities and casualties have been recorded. The presence of the explosive devices not only endangers the lives of the population and hampers the return to normal life, but also impedes humanitarian access and recovery efforts. In addition to the direct risk to the population and the impact on humanitarian, early recovery, and livelihood activities, the presence of Explosive Ordnance is providing a readily available source of explosives to construct improvised explosive devices (IEDs), which compounds the threat to communities.DDG’s team on the ground, through their interaction with IDPs, returnees and host community members, continues to collect information on contaminated areas in BAY States. Numerous reports of UXOs found in populated areas, on farming areas and/or on roads, namely in Konduga, Ngala, Bama, Dikwa and Gwoza (Borno), Gujba, Gulani (Yobe), Michika, Madagali (Adamawa) are recorded. In connection to the above, it is possible to ascertain that the threat posed by explosive remnants of war needs to be addressed, especially as the patterns of displacements are changing and people are becoming more mobile, therefore putting themselves at risk of finding themselves in contaminated areas. People returning to their area of origin, people in situation of secondary displacement or those starting to settle in in areas of which they don’t know the history of fighting are at-risk of being victim of an accident caused by mine or UXOs.

2 - Activities

ActivitiesThe proposed intervention will have three broad activities as described below. 2.1 Explosive Ordnance Risk Education: Based on the delivery of explosive ordnance risk education focused on EO and Covid-19 in all three north-eastern states, DRC/DDG has learnt and implemented following key strategies which will continue to inform the design and delivery of its future mine action programming in the region:• Continue to collaborate and share information with Security Forces, and national and state authorities, e.g. NEMA and SEMA, as keeping them informed of DDG activities helps to facilitate access and acceptance. • Adaptation of EORE materials to include Covid-19 prevention messages • Before initiating implementation of activities, it is essential that key stakeholders and INGO, for example camp managers, are identified and their authorization is obtained in order to facilitate access and acceptance. • When delivering RE in host communities, target three homes at a time in order to keep numbers manageable, i.e. 10 people, and avoid drawing larger crowds, which could expose staff and the beneficiaries to the risk of COVID-19. Design and delivery of DDG’s risk education programme is also based on data collected through Knowledge, Attitude, Practice (KAP) surveys conducted in Yobe (August 2016), Borno and Adamawa states (March 2017), and Borno and Adamawa (July 2020) on adults and children’s knowledge on mines/ERW, risk-taking behaviours and beliefs as well as the most at-risk groups. The level of effectiveness of RE activities will be measured against these baseline surveys. More importantly, target will be divided a 60/40 between adults (aged 18+) and children (aged 6-17) as children make up the majority of the ERW related casualties according to DDG casualty data. Further, more females will be targeted than men given knowledge gap is almost always slightly higher for males than females according to KAP surveys. Under the intervention, the following key activities will be implemented with a Covid-19emphasis: • Explosive Ordnance Risk Education Sessions (IDPs/Returnees/Host Communities) • Explosive Ordnance Risk Education Sessions (INGOs/NGOs/Local Government Authorities) • Emergency Response Explosive Ordnance Risk Education Team • Public Service Announcements • EORE Messages through Drama. 2.2 Non-Technical Survey: DRC/DDG Nigeria will utilize its current structure, specifically its GIS specialist to identify areas of intervention that need to be targeted and prioritize needs from communities to mitigate the impact of EO. This will be done using DDG Nigeria’s current database of EO reports, Casualty Reports which will be updated regularly. It will also incorporate an open-source reporting, knowledge gained from local security providers and other INGOs and UN organizations. DDG Nigeria will keep the engagement with Federal and state level key stakeholder’s during the execution of the strategy. The surveys will then be conducted, and information mapped and recorded in the IMSMA database and shared other NGO when required. The proposed intervention will have the following key activities, namely: Desk Assessment, Planning, Deployment of Teams, Communities Needs assessment related to EO, Sharing findings with Communities/Authorities, and Reporting and Prioritization. 2.3 Explosive Ordnance Disposal Training: DRC/DDG will enhance the Police EOD capacity through provision of training as per the existing international standards to reduce the protection risks associated with the Explosive Ordnance (EO) in North East of Nigeria. To this effect, DRC/DDG will make use of 3D printed Inert Explosive Ordnance (EO) and Advanced Ordnance Teaching Materials (AOTM). This activity also includes the mentoring phase after each EOD Training. DRC/DDG will also liaise with DRC Emergency and Protection coordination teams to ensure the information is relayed to relevant sector working groups and organizations working in suspected contaminated areas. Findings will be shared thought the MASWG in Maiduguri.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC/DDG will use an inclusive method during the need assessment where at risk communities are involved. A participatory approach shall be used to generate interest and ownership at the community level from the beginning of the project. Further, the needs assessment shall address the different needs, vulnerabilities and expectations of various groups and shall be sensitive to culture, gender, disability, and age. A review of existing community social networks, key community opinion leaders (bothmale and female) and local development committees shall be included in the needs assessment. The information collected through participatory methods shall be validated by the different segment of the community members. In addition, regular meetings will be held with different segment of the community to get feedback throughout the life of the project. These meetings sometime require to hold separate meetings for women and girls due to cultural sensitivities.
