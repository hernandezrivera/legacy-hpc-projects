Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to reduce IDPs risk of gender-based violence, child-rights violations, and other protection concerns. By delivering a number of interrelated activities, AVORD will also ensure that survivors of GBV and other forms of violence have access to comprehensive services, safe and protective environment.

Needs

In Somalia, as many as 2.6 million Internally Displaced People (IDPs) live in makeshift shelters where living conditions are extremely poor and protection risks are huge. In the context of weak government structures and unprotected shelters with no lighting, instances of Sexual and Gender-based Violence (SGBV) are common, especially in IDP settings. As per the protection cluster mid-year review, up to 76% of recorded GBV cases in the first half of 2018 countrywide have happened in IDP settlements. Victims don't not always seek support due to stigma and the lack of information about available support systems.Besides women, children remain one of the most vulnerable IDP groups facing protection risks. From January to June 2018, the Country Taskforce on Monitoring and Reporting (CTMR) has documented and verified 2,877 grave violations affecting 2,316 children (1,987 boys; 329 girls), compared to 1,249 violations reported in the first half of 2017. Instances of child recruitment have also increased by 83% in 2018 compared to 2017 whereby children and their families were forced to flee their homes to escape forced recruitment by armed groups. In many cases, children have abandoned their families for fear of recruitment. According to a recent protection cluster report, as many as 4,755 unaccompanied and separated children (2,048 girls & 2,707 boys) have been identified from January to June 2018.Forced evictions is on the increase which heighten protection risks for IDPs. Statistics by the Housing Land and Property (HLP) sub-cluster show that over 204,000 people have been evicted in 2018, which is equal to the number of people evicted in all of 2017. On average, up to 11,000 IDPs are evicted every month, which indicate a worrisome phenomenon. Evictions are often carried out with little or no notice leading to huge loss of properties, and at times, lives.

Activities

AVORD will undertake a series of activities to enhance protection levels for 11,550 most vulnerable IDPs in Middle Shabelle. These activities include training of key local stakeholders on a range of protection issues including peer to peer counselling, GBV/CPiE, documentation and reporting of protection concerns and violations, principled referrals (sensitive questioning, confidentiality, informed consent, etc), child protection, forced displacement, arbitrary detention, forced recruitment, among other topics. In tandem with these workshops, GBV crisis centers in target locations will be mapped and referral pathways established to ensure that survivors get the psycho-social support they need within the shortest time possible. Persons at elevated risk for protection violations, especially women and girls and vulnerable minors at risk for child-rights violations will receive targeted education/sensitization efforts including self-protection training, and awareness of referral pathways. AVORD will advocate for a durable solution to eviction and creation of joint protection plans in IDP settlements identified by protection officers to face increased risk of violence. AVORD will also seek integration with shelter partners to reduce exposure of IDPs in such IDP settlements through provision of portable solar lights and lockable shelters and supply of material support in the form of NFIs and dignity kits. Where feasible, child friendly spaces will be provided to at-risk children and effort will be made to place unaccompanied minors in foster care or reunite with families.A systematic approach to beneficiary selection will be adapted with a clear criterion to prioritize the most vulnerable including un-accompanied children, women, and those with disabilities. Data collected to inform site selection will be desegregated by gender to ensure that no group is left-out. Target beneficiaries will be involved in all phases and an elaborate complaint mechanism established.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This proposal is humanitarian in nature focusing on the protection of IDPs in humanitarian crisis. However, it also contributes to building the resilience of targeted IDPs in a number of ways. On the one hand, enhancing protection levels for at-risk IDPs translates into overall improvement in their living conditions and chances to access livelihoods, leaving IDPs more resilient to future shocks and able to cope with the grim reality of displacement. On the other hand, the project advocates for durable solutions to address forced eviction which is a key protection concerns for the IDPs. To maximize impact and sustain project outcome, AVORD will seek integration with other clusters providing response in areas where proposed activities will be implemented. Details pertaining to project activities will be shared through relevant clusters and 4W reporting matrix to avoid duplication of effort. Local communities will play a key role in different phases of the project to create sense of ownership which will contribute to sustaining of project outcomes. AVORD will strive to build local capacities thus ensuring that various community leaders and committees are able to sustainably maintain resources following project completion and handover.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IDPs may face exclusion from gate-keepers who try to hamper their access to the assistance provided by humanitarian actors. Besides, certain power dynamics within IDP settlements may sometimes lead to the exclusion of IDPs from marginalized communities. AVORD will prevent this by undertaking direct identification and registration of beneficiaries without use of middle men or gate keepers. AVORD will also apply systematic beneficiary identification and collection of adequate household profile before the intervention to ensure that only the most vulnerable and marginalized IDPs are registered for assistance. Forced eviction remains a constant threat facing the IDPs in Somalia. To mitigate this, AVORD will advocate for the construction of permanent shelters only in public land or in areas where land tenure is secured for a minimum of 10 years and obtain relevant legal documents e.g. land titles. AVORD will also work closely with local authorities to address the impact of eviction on IDPs while identifying the groups who could be more prone to eviction during need assessments.Finally, there is a likelihood of IDP and host community tensions that may result from the intervention. This will be mitigated by being transparent with the target communities and providing them with timely information on the selection criteria, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. Joint IDP-host community committees will be established to participate in project design and implementation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Exclusion based on gender and age could happen in IDP settings if adequate preventive measures are not taken. For example, in an environment of male dominance, women and girls could be easily left-out or disproportionately registered during beneficiary identification and selection processes. To prevent that, AVORD will collect and use data dis-aggregated by sex, age and diversity to inform beneficiary selection and maintain gender parity. Activities will also be designed in a manner that can address the unique needs of women, men, boys, girls and those with special needs.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Repetitive flare-ups of armed conflicts in Somalia may hinder access to areas targeted by this project. AVORD will mitigate that by continuously assessing local dynamics and making plans accordingly. Where necessary, relief items will be pre-positioned in strategic locations where feasible to respond to humanitarian emergencies in the event of restricted access. To prevent exposing beneficiaries to risk, AVORD will ensure that access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly and safe distribution. The Do-No-Harm principals will be observed at all times to prevent un-intended negative consequences.
