Project Summary

As part of SAMS mandates in the Health sector, SAMS 2024 programs will be in line with the HRP's 1st, 2nd, and 3rd strategic objectives, and as SAMS runs 30 HFs in Idleb, Aleppo, Al Hasakeh, and Ar Raqqa governorates, SAMS will continue to provide health support for population in 2024 through continuing implementing activities of strengthen health capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver a timely response to disease outbreaks, including Cholera and strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements. In this program, SAMS will prioritize the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improving access to quality health care (primary and secondary, specialized services, MCs, and an ambulatory system, C19 and Cholera response) in 33 HFs and MCs, they are:

SAMS will support facilities on different levels including coverage of staff incentives, running costs, provision of pharmaceuticals and other medical and non-medical commodities, and rehabilitation/maintenance of the HFs. SAMS will provide recruitment support, capacity building, and technical supervision to ensure the HFs are staffed with ed personnel and to enhance compliance with standard treatment protocols. SAMSwill provide the following services at the primary healthcare level: SRH services (antenatal care, postnatal care, family planning, normal deliveries, gynecological consultations, and clinical management of rape), EPI, communicable and non-communicable diseases, Nutrition, MHPSS, health awareness, and basic laboratory and radiology services. Services at the secondary health care level will include gynecologic surgeries and cesarean sections, High-risk pregnancies trauma management, major surgeries, hospitalization services, blood transfusion, hemodialysis, and ICUs, in addition to pandemic response (CM, Triage, PPE/IPC, CHWs). This program will also provide cancer treatment to Breast, colon testicular and ovarian, ALL, and Lymphoma patients (Hodgkin, and non-Hodgkin); Cath Lab, Pathology, GI endoscopy, Dental services, Paediatric ALL, ICU, NICU, PICU, Kidney transplant medications. Additionally, SAMS will support the ambulatory system (5 ambulances). In addition, as part of SAMS' strategy to build the capacity of the health care professional in NWS through advancing SAMS’ investment in its medical education program: Suppor Residents' Doctors; Support Midwifery Nursing Education; and Mental Health Education. SAMS will coordinate the Health Cluster through 4Ws and HeRAMS and constant participation and active membership in TWG SRH, MHPSS, Non-Communicable Diseases, SIG, MidwiferyCapacity Building Strategy for NWS, Secondary Health Care, and Drug Supply Line, in addition, to coordinating with other NGOs on the referral, and the contribution to DHIS2, notwithstanding its membership in the NGO Forum, SNA, and others. It is important to mention that both Qah and Idleb Maternity Hospitals are being supported by UNFPA within this period.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In Syria, the humanitarian situation, outlined in the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and HeRAMS, Q2 2023, highlights critical needs. The HNO 2023 reports 8.5 million people in need, with a gender imbalance of 93% female and 7% male. Violence against women and girls persists, aggravated by child marriage issues. Civil documentation shortages affect 51% of households, limiting movement, especially for women. The Health Cluster 2024 HNO Chapter reveals over 83% of residents with unmet health service needs, emphasizing the urgent demand for mental health and psychosocial support. Compounded emergencies, including an earthquake, have increased psychological distress, requiring enhanced mental health services, trauma management, and psychiatric support.Intervention activities include staff recruitment, HF rehabilitation, capacity building, and the provision of PHC services. Services cover pediatric, SRH, mental health, surgeries, and in-patient care across specialized centers. Other activities involve procurement, awareness sessions, health promotion through CHWs, and ambulatory and referral services. Implementation includes medical waste management, sustaining immunization programs, and medical education. Monitoring involves quarterly field visits, patient satisfaction surveys, and checklists/spot checks.Protection Risk Analysis (PRA):1. Safe Closure of Facilities and Data Protection: Low possibility, high impact. Mitigation: Adherence to SOPs and electronic data protection.2. Women and Girls Attendance: Medium possibility, high impact. Mitigation: Coordination with community leaders and outreach visits.3. Persons with Disabilities and Older Persons Access: High possibility, high impact. Mitigation: Allocation of outreach teams and transportation support.PSEA Strategy: SAMS prioritizes Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism accessibility through hotlines, WhatsApp, SMS, email, and face-to-face interviews. Information dissemination involves diverse channels like social media, facility screens, CHW campaigns, and posters. Community engagement includes in-person messaging, leaflets, and brochures. SAMS conducts networking and coordination meetings, visits key community members, and considers suggestions on staff recruitment. Staff are sensitized and trained on protection principles, PSEA prevention, and reporting mechanisms. Stringent policies, MEAL toolkits, protection mainstreaming in assessments, and PSEA posters contribute to a transparent reporting system.Normal Risks:1. Control by Unaccepted Groups: Mitigation involves Security SOPs, activity relocation, and coordination with civil authorities.2. UNSC XB Non-Renewal: Mitigation includes multiple funding channels, cross-border access, and contingency plans.3. High Turnover of Staff: Mitigation involves regular training, appropriate salary scales, and retention efforts.4. Theft: Mitigation includes security upgrades, CCTV cameras, photo verification, and inventory record reviews
