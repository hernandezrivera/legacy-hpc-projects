Project Summary

The global outbreak of COVID-19 and recurrent escalations including the May hostilities are putting further pressure to the already overstretched health system in the Gaza Strip. UNRWA continues to provide essential primary health care services to 1.4 million Palestine refugees in Gaza, adjusting its approach to integrate COVID-19 risk mitigation measures. The public health system is already overstretched responding to COVID-19 emergency, and expanding the capacity of the UNRWA health services to absorb increasing needs is necessary to ensure access of vulnerable population to health services. UNRWA’s ability to respond to emergency health needs in Gaza is greatly constrained by the tight funding situation as well as the increasing needs, and ensuring the availability of this intervention is crucial.
In light of these conditions, this proposed intervention aims to support the provision of critical healthcare needs for Palestine refugees in the Gaza Strip, while alleviating the pressure on public health system, through: (1) ensuring access to secondary and tertiary health services for poor refugees in response to MoH’s long-waiting list, (2) providing life-saving medicines especially for NCD patients, (3) hiring emergency surge staff to cope with increasing needs and new interventions designed in response of COVID-19, (4) providing healthcare services adjusting COVID-19 emerged needs including telemedicine, the triage system in all health centers and enhanced measures including strict use of PPE, (5) providing necessary assistive tools to children with special needs, and (6) providing mental health and psychosocial support especially to women, vulnerable groups and people affected by the hostilities and COVID-19 emergency.

1 - Needs

With more than 1.45 million Palestinians across the oPt identified as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2021, of which two-thirds of them in the Gaza Strip (HNO 2022), Gaza remains under protracted humanitarian crisis. The process of gradual deterioration under the blockade has left essential services in Gaza barely able to function, thus directly impacting people’s basic right to health access, which added further strain from the COVID-19 outbreak since March 2020 and the recurrent hostilities including the May escalation in 2021. The health system is facing challenges after over a year of responding to the COVID-19 pandemic while continuing to deliver other essential services to respond to both existing and emerging healthcare needs. The MSNA data shows that on average 47% of surveyed households in Gaza have at least one member with chronic disease. The Health Cluster also identified that about 110,000 people with non-communicable diseases and some of these in need of palliative care remain with very limited available support, affecting especially those with cancer, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease (HNO 2022). Other MSNA data shows that among the households accessing health services, 23 per cent reported facing access barriers in the three months prior to data collection that took place in July 2021. Of these, 64 per cent reported the high cost of services and medicines as a major barrier. The need for COVID-19 infection prevention, vaccination and risk communication remains a humanitarian priority (HNO 2022). As a part of the national COVID-19 vaccination campaign, UNRWA opened 18 vaccination points within its health centres accessible regardless of refugee status. While the MSNA (July 2021) data shows that 72 per cent of surveyed households in the Gaza Strip reported that not all members are willing to be vaccinated against COVID-19, more than 76,000 individuals received at least one dose and more than 42,000 received two doses at UNRWA’s vaccination points as of end of June 2021. Furthermore, the May escalation and ongoing COVID-19 emergency highlighted the continuous need for mental health and psychosocial services, including GBV survivors, adolescent with problematic behavior, and patients and other affected with COVID-19, among others. According to MSNA, 20 to 38 per cent of surveyed households have at least one member showing signs of psychological distress in the 30 days before the data was collected in July 2021, and the UN Women Gender Alert (2021) highlighted that woman are disproportionately more affected by the mental health situation (HNO 2022). While UNRWA continues to struggle with a financial crisis compounded with the COVID-19 emergency, the health demand continues to increase in a deteriorating socioeconomic situation. Alleviating the pressure on the public health sector and strengthening the health services already provided by UNRWA to approximately two thirds of the population across the Gaza Strip are immediate needs in order to cope with the on-going crisis.

2 - Activities

• UNRWA provides primary healthcare services to 1.4 million Palestine refugees across its 22 health centres in the Gaza Strip. In order to respond to existing and emerging needs, the Agency aims to provide further support to poor refugees facing the burden of high cost of healthcare to ensure their access to secondary and tertiary health services, also alleviating the burden on the public health system. UNRWA will refer vulnerable refugees who are not able to afford private hospital treatment to a network of partnered hospitals partially covering the hospitalization costs of medical treatment or reimbursing the patients for the medical treatment costs incurred. Hospitalization treatments covered include procedures considered lifesaving, urgent and those with long waiting lists at the MoH hospitals that have an impact on the quality of life of the patients. UNRWA Health Programme will work jointly with the MoH and WHO to determine the procedures that are identified as priority needs and will be referred to UNRWA contracted hospitals. This support is in addition to UNRWA’s regular operation that referring patients to its contracted hospitals for cases identified within UNRWA health services. In addition, UNRWA will procure life-saving medicines to support refugees’ access to essential health services, mainly for patients with non-communicable diseases (NCDs) and chronic diseases including different kind of insulins which are not available at UNRWA health centers as they are not included in UNRWA’s essential medicine list that are funded by UNRWA’s core-fund for primary healthcare. UNRWA will also expand its response capacity within its primary health care services by hiring emergency surged staff in order to cope with continuing demand and emerging needs due to COVID-19 such as telemedicine and a triage system in all UNRWA health centers to separate patients with respiratory disease symptoms from others. Furthermore, as a part of a coordinated approach, UNRWA will continue to contribute to the local national vaccination campaign. In 2021, UNRWA opened 18 vaccination points within its health centres for refugees and non-refugees requiring additional staff to continue supporting the national vaccination effort. UNRWA ensures the gender balance within the UNRWA health center staffing to ensure that both males and females have adequate access to services. Following COVID-19 emergency, UNRWA implemented some adjustments to prevent further spread of the virus, such as use of personal protection equipment (PPE) to protect the health staff, triage care and protocols to separate the patients with respiratory symptoms, and telemedicine service including the home delivery of medicine to limit the footprints at health centres as well as to maintain the access to primary healthcare services during lockdown. These measures will be maintained to continue to ensure appropriate risk mitigation. Furthermore, UNRWA will also support children with special needs, providing proper medical screening of school children and necessary assistive devices that vulnerable families are not able to afford otherwise. Finally, UNRWA’s mental health and psychosocial support at health centres as well as tele-counselling will continue to respond to increasing demand targeting vulnerable Palestine refugees identified as in need of support, including women of reproductive age and children, elderlies, people with disabilities and GBV cases. UNRWA has a number of female health staff, including health counsellors stationed in each health centre, creating a safe space for women to address their needs in confidential manner. As a member of PSEA network, UNRWA commits to all PSEA mandatory activities across its programmes, and the interventions included in this proposal are not exception. UNRWA adopts a safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on consultations with the beneficiaries and accessible to different groups. The PSEA focal points are trained to receive complaints in multitude of ways, including a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints, and a centralized confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints are in place. To ensure that the beneficiaries are aware of PSEA mechanism, PSEA awareness raising material will be disseminated to all project recipients. UNRWA regularly engage with HCT oPt PSEA network, and project staff will engage with UNRWA’s representative to this body via training and through project monitoring. UNRWA is mandated to provide primary healthcare services to Palestine refugees. Interventions included in HRP are outside of primary healthcare but supporting poor refugees who have been living under the prolonged blockade and would not have needed life-saving health care services otherwise (hospitalization, life-saving medicine, etc). Other intervention related to COVID-19 are responding to the emergencies.

Accountability to Affected Populations
9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback is sent to the management for information, evaluation and corrective action as required. The beneficiaries’ feedback contributes to continuously improve UNRWA’s health system at different levels.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,b - Project hotline,c - Complaint box,d - Satisfaction survey,e - Field visit,f - Other

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

Every UNRWA health centre have a Women committee, Youth committee and Health Center Friendship committee that are composed by community members to provide guidance and feedback on needs and the services provided by UNRWA. In normal circumstances, satisfaction surveys and exit interviews are conducted on a regular basis by UNRWA health officers to ensure optimal delivery of health services to Palestine refugees. If in-person collection of feedback can’t be performed, UNRWA counts on telemedicine services and hotlines, through which the beneficiaries can convey any feedback or concern. UNRWA also has a dedicated portal through which beneficiaries can raise any general query including feedback on the quality of services delivered. All staff are required to take mandatory PSEA training. UNRWA is represented in the National PSEA committee and the PSEA taskforce, while all UNRWA departments have representatives within the Agency composing well-established complaint mechanism to follow up.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNRWA has a multitude of options to receive feedback and ensure accountability to the affected population, including in-person reception in all UNRWA installations, a targeted online and telephone complaints portal (nominal and anonymous), a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints; a centralized (Amman) confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints. Direct (in person) and indirect (press, social media, etc) outreach is further undertaken to disseminate the information on the above mechanisms.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a dedicated Protection and Neutrality Oﬃce to guarantee the provision of its services in an impartial and non-discriminatory way. UNRWA regularly assess the level of compliance with Protection standards of the services provided to refugees through a Protection Audit. Staff in health centres, whom are regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection mainstreaming and gender, are able to refer speciﬁc protection cases to MHPSS counsellors for follow up. In addition, a new mechanism is developed to support critical and urgent cases of GBV and protection cases. In normal circumstances, the crowd control system is in place and a speciﬁc calendar is set up to avoid and limit potential incidents and/or disturbance. A complaint response mechanism is accessible in health centres and mechanism to provide timely feedback is available through speciﬁc response committees.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is targeting all Palestinian refugees in need for healthcare through its 22 health centres across the Gaza Strip. Location of health centres takes into consideration safety concerns as well as catchment area, distance and potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. In case of lockdown and movement restriction, people in need of primary health care are still able to access UNRWA’s health services through telemedicine. The health centres take into consideration speciﬁc vulnerability concerns and adopted speciﬁc SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize healthcare to vulnerable groups (abandoned women, pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow speciﬁc concerns and potential protection cases. Further, health centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. All UNRWA health centres have an elevator to ease the access for people with movement disabilities, and some health centers are piloted to have posters and guidance with sign language as well as tactile paving floor tile for people with visual impairment.
