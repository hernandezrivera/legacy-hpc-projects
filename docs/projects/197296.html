Project Summary

International Medical Corps (IMC) will work alongside the International Rescue Committee to implement a three-year integrated protection intervention focusing on mitigating and responding to child protection and gender-based violence risks in the Far North region. The project will aim at providing a safe and secure environment for women and children among Nigerian refugees and vulnerable households from host communities to both reinforce the community protective environment and strengthen access to basic services for the most vulnerable and at-risk individuals. Building on IMC & IRC’s child protection experience, the two organizations will focus on two components. The first component will aim at strengthening the protective environment for children, through building local capacities of key actors including government actors, community members and caregivers on protection including mental and psychosocial impact, to facilitate behaviour change in communities and with caregivers. Considering that behaviour change can only be achieved through a sustained intervention, these activities will be implemented over a three-year period and the strategy for Y2 and Y3 will be adapted according to the progress reached in Y1 and Y2, respectively. Capacity building will be scaled up throughout the three years of the project with increased support in Y2 and Y3 of local structures (government and community-based actors) to lead and coordinate activities. Youth empowerment activities for vulnerable youth will be integrated into the protection response aiming to strengthen their resilience and facilitate the development of life skills. The second component will focus on emergency response to child protection risks faced by vulnerable and at-risk children affected by the conflict and will be implemented through case management, development of safe learning and healing spaces and support for civil documentation.
The proposed project will target communities affected by the Lake Chad Crisis in Logone and Chari Divisions of the Far North. The project will target in 10,695 refugees (32% of the total refugee population). While the project is intended to target locations with high numbers of refugees, child protection and GBV services will be provided to all vulnerable women and children with identified needs including refugees, returnees, and host community members. GBV activities will primarily target survivors of GBV and vulnerable women and adolescent girls, while child protection activities will primarily target vulnerable children, including unaccompanied and separated children, and children without birth certificates. The selection of members of community-based structures, where they do not exist, will prioritize women and trusted members of the community and will strive to achieve a diverse balance of ages, ethnicity, and refugee status.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Ongoing violence in the Far North has led to a steady rise of displacement since 2014. As of November 2021, a total of 608,000 people (358,000 IDPs, 115,000 Nigerian refugees, and 135,000 returnees) , were displaced, representing an increase of 48,000 people since November 2020. 54% of the Nigerian refugees in Cameroon are female, 60% are children, 4.6% are over the age of 60 years, and 21% are people with special needs. In the Logone et Chari and Mayo-Sava divisions, the internally displaced population represents 45% and 48% of the total population, respectively. As of July 2021, 90% of the internal displacement in the Far North (since 2015) has been related to armed conflict, while 10% is due to natural disasters and flooding and 1% is due to intercommunal violence. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains widespread and one of the main rights violated that affects women and girls in the Far North region due to the crisis as well as the patriarchal system and cultural barriers. GBV partners have reported 1,668 GBV cases to specialized GBV service providers in through Y1 of BPRM implementation. Among the survivors, 96% are female, 41% are children, 78% are IDPs, 18% are host community members and 4% are returnees. Reported incidents included emotional abuse (31%), forced marriage (28%), physical assault (16%), sexual assault (10%), denial of resources or opportunities (10%), and rape (5%) . From this, a total of 379 GBV cases were reported by women and girls in Mayo-Tsanaga (66), Mayo-Sava (66), and Logone-et-Chari (180) during August 2021 to March 2022 by IMC and IRC within their intervention areas. While International Medical Corps (IMC) and International Rescue Committee (IRC) have attained major achievements in the Far North with PRM support and that of other donors, there are significant protection needs that remain and are emerging, particularly for refugees living outside camps. Additionally, due to the COVID-19 pandemic and heightened violence which continue to shape and damage the protective environment for women and children.
