Project Summary

Humanitarian situation remains critical in south Sudan in 2024 due to multiple shocks. The cumulative effects of years of conflict, sub-national violence, unprecedented flooding, high levels of insecurity, health outbreaks and destroyed livelihoods have left more than 8.5 million people (about two thirds of the population) in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection according to South Sudan Humanitarian Response Monitoring Report 2024. Twic and Pibor GPAA Counties are among the hard to reach counties in South Sudan as stipulated by the WASH Cluster and UNOCHA with demanding WASH needs particularly among host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. Peace Corps Organization target a total of 11,000 beneficiaries from the Twic, and Pibor GPAA Counties in providing of integrated equitable access to sufficient safe water, sanitation and hygiene services among the malnutrition GBV at risk/affected and food insecure communities of Warrap and Jonglei States. Peace Corps Organization intends to scale up water sanitation and hygiene services to these vulnerable group of people through an integrated approach in Nutrition, Health and GBV sectors. Provision of safe, reliable and sustainable water will be addressed through rehabilitating water points and developing new protected hand dug wells both at the community and institution level so that the affected population can have access to safe and adequate amount (15L/person/day) of water for drinking and other domestic uses. The rehabilitation of water points will be done after a GBV risk analysis have been conducted in the focus groups discussions with women and girls, conducted by female staffs and co-chaired by a GBV partner active on ground considering the safety and best location thus reducing the risk of occurrence of GBV cases and sexual exploitation of women and girls since they are the primary beneficiary of water. Nutrition facilities such as OTP/TSFP/SC, Education facilities such as schools, health facilities such as PHCC/PHCU and protection sites such as women and girl’s friendly spaces and child friendly spaces will be prioritized by PCO as part of integration among the various sectors. The water point’s rehabilitation will be done by trained hand pump mechanics selected among the locals in the community and the spares from the WASH core-pipeline hence ensuring acceptability, ownership and sustainability. Water management Committees will take care of these water points and will teach the community on best practices to ensure apron is clean and also fencing the water points to prevent animals from defecating at the aprons.
Sanitation situation will be addressed through rehabilitation of latrines and hand washing facilities to ensure safe disposal of human excreta and hand washing at critical times. The rehabilitation will be done after consultation through FGDs on best siting which is safe and will have doors and locks to ensure privacy, safety and dignity on usage and will prevent occurrence of GBV and other forms of abuse associated with the use of latrines. This project will ensure communities are taught by WASH staffs and Nutrition/Health staffs (trained on WASH minimum package) on how to improve hygiene situation through household visits, hygiene creation and awareness campaigns with emphasis on hand-washing with soap at critical times, safe disposal of human wastes, treatment and storage of drinking water to prevent occurrence and transmission of Cholera and other water borne diseases.

1. Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)-2024 estimated that more than two-thirds of south Sudan’s population, 8.5 million people, are in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection in South Sudan. The HNO, estimated 5.5 million people’s (excluding refugees) living standards their wellbeing are in dire need of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation and hygiene services in 2024. Many of these affected population have the highest proportion of HHs that are continually dependent on unprotected water sources, and have limited or no access to latrines and WASH NFIs. According to REACH South Sudan situation overview 2019, high proportion of HHs (74%) reporting having a water borne related diseases, with malaria the most commonly reported (74%), fever (56%) and then by AWD (20%). For the households that can access safe water, a lot of time is spent in the water points with an average of 1200 households per hand pump spending more than 30 minutes and walking more than 500 meters which is below the sphere standards which may be a source of conflict between the host and the visiting community. The operational hand pumps in Twic and Pibor GPAA Counties are overused and has led to more breakdown of pumps and increased queuing time at the viable water sources causing reduced per capita water consumption (under 15 L/person/day), hence increased consumption from unprotected water sources like untreated rivers and hand dug wells and also reduced time for other essential tasks at their homes. Inadequate access to clean water, improved sanitation and acceptable hygiene practices within the counties of Twic and Pibor GPAA contributes greatly to the high levels of malnutrition, disease burdens, food insecurity as well as WASH related protection issues. Access to sanitation facilities is equally low, Access to safe and gender sensitive latrines remains a big challenge during both wet and dry seasons and thus open defecation is predominant, leading to cycles of water contamination, resulting in acute watery diarrhea, cholera, eye infections, and skin diseases. The likelihood of WASH-related diseases is extremely high. Girls and women face gender-based violence (GBV) whilst trying to access scarce water supplies, and their dignity is compromised by poor sanitation and hygiene practices in communities and households. Limited access to clean water and sanitation services contributes to the high prevalence of malnutrition, water-borne diseases, poor menstrual and hygiene management, risk of GBV at water points, absenteeism from school, and school dropout. Women and girls may face increased risk of GBV when collecting water and using communal latrines: This competition and pressure on the scarce water and sanitation is a driver to conflict between the host communities, the IDPs and even animals. Climate change impacts are being felt every year now in South Sudan, with major flooding occurring annually since 2019. The impact of droughts and floods, linked to the effects of climate change, are impacting the gains made in expanding WASH infrastructureThe sanitation and hygiene situation is further worsened by the fact that most of the community does not have access to a hand washing facility, poor knowledge and practices on personal hygiene and environmental conservation practices. Hand washing is equally low, with socio-cultural practices unfavourable to achieving acceptable WASH standards, Outbreak of Ebola (EVD) in the neighbouring Countries risking the life of the people in South Sudan. Women have also been excluded and side-lined from leadership roles and opportunities which are associated with a little stipend or incentives. Adolescent school girls are likely to be inconsistent in school attendance, due to lack of menstrual management supplies, thereby making them more susceptible to teenage marriages (Protection Cluster – UNHCR, 2019)

2. Project activities

The proposed activities under the current South Sudan Humanitarian •Community mobilization to introduce the project clearly stating the objectives, duration and project implementing period, activities to be carried out, target population to the community and other stakeholders. • Training of enumerators and hand pump mechanics who will help the community in borehole mapping and repair of the boreholes and protected hand dug wells to ensure sustainability of the project. • Rehabilitation and repair of boreholes and protected hand dug wells- This will be done by trained hand pump mechanics after a GBV risk analysis through FGDs with women and girls using the in-kind supplies requested from the core pipeline. • Development of new protected hand dug wells where there is no existing borehole to provide safe water to the vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and the hosts. • Rehabilitation of latrines will also be done in or near institutions after a GBV risk analysis has been done and the latrines will have locks and doors to enhance privacy, dignity and security and hence reducing the risk of exploitation, such as sexual exploitation and abuse. • Establishment of hand washing stations and facilities at the rehabilitated/constructed sanitation facilities to ensure communities wash their hand after defecation. •Chlorination and water testing to all water points including nutrition facilities •Establishing and training water management committees on best ways of managing water points including guiding the elderly, sick, people living with disabilities, animals and children at the water points to ensure sustainability of the project. •Transport and distribution of WASH NFIs requested from the WASH core pipeline to under 5 children SAM/MAM and PLWs and monitoring their use. This will be done by WASH staff and also Nutrition/ GBV/Health staffs trained on WASH minimum packages including demonstration on usage. • Selection and training of community hygiene and sanitation promoters/ volunteers to visit households with messages of improved WASH which will help influence positive behavior change to the community. . Awareness creation and sensitization on Ebola virus prevention measures. • In schools, WASH club will be established with student members and teachers as patrons. School PTA members will also be formed and trained so as to train children in schools on WASH through Child to Child (C to C) approach • Streamlining GBV Training to WASH staffs after being trained by the protection teams active on ground. .Distribution of water treatment product (PUR, aqua tab, filters) • Training of Adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene Management both in schools and community level including demonstration on usage of menstrual hygiene management kits. • Training of Nutrition and Health staffs on WASH minimum package as part of integration among the different sectors.
