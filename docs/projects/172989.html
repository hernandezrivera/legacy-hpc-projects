Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen the resilience of vulnerable HHs and community-based assets. Individual capacities will be built across three sectors to improve people knowledge and skills to engage in livelihoods and social service activities. HH capacity will be strengthened through improvement of livelihoods opportunities and access to different services (WASH/Protection).
IDPs, returnees, resident farmers, pastoralists, and fisherfolk in conflict-affected areas are impacted by underdevelopment - They also face risks of exposure to water-borne diseases and vector-borne diseases due to limited access to safe and clean water resources and poor environmental sanitation and hygiene. Conflict is common over natural resources (water, pasture), especially between farmers and pastoralists. Existing veterinary facilities are non-functional and need rehabilitation. All of the already-vulnerable people in target areas are affected by implications of political instability and economic deterioration, To increase resilience to economic, environmental, social, and political shocks, this project intends to foster capacity building, self-help initiatives, and technical assistance for individuals and HHs to adopt resilient livelihood approaches; to build the capacity of local institutions including line ministries, DRR/NRM committees, CBRMSs, etc. to contribute to the transition from relief to development. The project will conduct conflict risk analysis at the planning stage and will reinforce its conflict-sensitive approach throughout; meaning that the activities will not be conflict triggers, but rather will act as unifiers to promote peace and social cohesion.VSF-G has already implemented protection interventions in Blue Nile State south kordofan and west Darfur through GBV capacity building to government counterpart, stakeholder and capacity building for NNGOs as well as trained more than,6000 women and girls and build them capacity in GBV prevention and responses through the establishment and rehabilitation of more than 12 women and youth multipurpose centres through this interventions had been supported vulnerable women and girls and GBV victims through IGAs activities sometimes with distributed start-up kits, more than this support social worker and medical assistances which are working in target areas in BN by provide them monthly incentive to improve responses to GBV access in most affected localities in BN, GBV camping awareness were conducted in different target areas. With collaboration with UNFPa and through fund from USAID/ OFDA VSF-G were distributed dignity kits for more than 1500 vulnerable women and girls. VSFG will implement GBV prevention response through the construction of 2 permanent women centres in Gharb Algabal and Habila the centre will be a place for GBV awareness promotion, information sharing, and linking protection activities with capacity building and economic empowerment of women. The center will be equipped with a small solar unit, furniture, training materials, audiovisual equipment and food processing equipment. 1 - Needs

Clashes between Arab militia and Masalit tribe in Krinding 1, 2 and Al Sultan IDP camps in Geneina town and neighboring localities has led to large population displacement (approximately 46,000). People from the three IDP camps were displaced inside Krenik, Habila and Kulbas taking refuge in government institutions, while another 40 villages were displaced either to neighboring safe villages. These incidents and the gathering of people in government institutions has increased tension and placed further strain on the existing facilities in targeted localities, creating gaps in basic services such as FSL, health and WASH. The negotiations for return were obstructed by many demands from the affected communities. Besides this, the risk of COVID-19 in the state is very high, as it is already spread in neighboring states and Chad. The food security situation in West Darfur continues to be affected by man-made and natural calamities. The 2019/2020 post-harvest assessment carried out by state Ministry of Production and Economic Resources, shows huge gap between production and consumption. The total cereal production in 2019 agriculture season was 149,559 MT while the consumption need is 242,102.2 MT with a deficit of (-41238.3 MT) after adding surplus from last year production.In Central Darfur, there are critical gaps in FSL and WASH sectors, especially in Wast,Gharab &Shamal Algabal localities. Agriculture is the predominant livelihood activity and the main source of food for over 90% of the residents. The main constraints to agricultural production include access to arable land, availability of improved seeds and access to affordable and sustainable veterinary services, particularly in remote areas. Hence, pastoralists are facing high risk of livestock morbidity and mortality due to vulnerability to disease outbreaks.Lessons from previous programs to guide future interventions to address current needs and gaps include:In Central Darfur, water supply remains one of the major challenges in the target areas. The influx of new IDPs, particularly in Shamal G locality, the pressure on and malfunctions of existing water sources, as well as the distance in some cases are all factors which push people to use water from unsafe sources (27% of the respondents). Whilst there is notable improvement in hygiene awareness as a result of the hygiene promotion activities, proliferation of hygiene messages is important through different means including child-friendly and mass media to reach more people, especially to avoid gatherings as it is a requirement during COVID19 crisis. The voucher system through CAHWs to offer veterinary services to vulnerable households, is more effective in reaching those in need, easy to monitor, provides more accountability measures, as well as contributes in keeping CAHWs active, and promotes their link to local veterinary authorities. There is still limited awareness among pastoralists, and therefore awareness creation campaigns to reach wider community groups and extension trainings to pastoralists targeting self-treatment, veterinary drug abuse, zoonosis and other malpractices would be more impactful.Introduction of group-based income generating activities with humanitarian interventions is reported to promote recovery and to have more sustainable impacts on affected people.According to HRP2020 for GBV SS West Darfur and Central Darfur states are still of high risk level and its need for emergency intervention about ( 14912 person in Wast algabal , 5447 in Gharab algabal , 21555 in Shamal Algabal peopersonneed for GBV interventions in CD, about 8080 in Habila , 81099 in Keriank and 2551 in Kulbas persons needs in WD, and still responses very week to GBV services the sector highlighting that still there is gap in dignity kits, and GBV services need to improve through strengthening referral system and increase awareness.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF Germany will apply different standards and best practices in emergency response including SPHERE standards, LEGS, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Natural Resource Management (NRM) and "Do No Harm'' approaches in the implementation of this project activities. It will be taken into consideration that the transfer of resources (project inputs and services including agricultural commodities and cash) into a resource-scarce environment can trigger conflict in the communities. Careful assessment of the local context, examining how humanitarian assistance would interact with the current situation in the target areas, and the creation of options for project revision to improve its quality will be carried out. this assessment will include analysis of: (i) dividers and sources of tensions between groups: systems and institutions; attitudes and actions; values and interests; experiences; symbols and occasions; etc.; (ii) connectors across sub-groups and the local capacities for peace; (iii) the project in terms of why; where; what; when; with whom; by whom and how and its impact on dividers/tensions and connectors/local capacities for peace. The partners will work together to ensure that the project is implemented in a way that can strengthen local capacities for peace, build on connectors that bring communities together, and reduce the divisions and sources of tensions that can lead to destructive conflict. To ensure that no negative impact should result from this project the following will be undertaken: just distribution of resources, creating or strengthening networks of relationships across divisions, strengthening good governance, the use of participatory processes for decision making, supporting traditional or indigenous mechanisms for conflict resolution and reconciliation, inclusion of diversity of ethnic or religious groups, gender, and youth in project activities and leadership structures. Selection of beneficiaries will be performed with due consideration of “Do No Harm” principles, ensuring fair representation of different vulnerable groups regardless of their ethnicity, religion or gender. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be socialized with communities and ample opportunity will be provided to raise concerns or complaints. Selection criteria for all project activities will focus on female-headed HHs, child-headed HHs, orphans, elders and persons with disabilities. The population of the targeted localities; Nertiti, Golo and Rokero, and Kereinik, Sirba and Habila localities includes a high proportion of vulnerable individuals; targeted HHs might have lost a family members during the conflict, mainly men, which explains the considerable number of women-headed HHs among the targeted communities; those women have to act as the breadwinners for their children and often their elders on their own. 7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

VSF Germany will adopt Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in setting standards, building capacity, communication, participation, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. IASC commitments with regard to AAP are incorporated in this plan including governance and integration of feedback and accountability mechanisms into program design, maintaining transparency and dialogue between the partners and the affected population, participation and involvement of the target population in design, monitoring and evaluation of this project, establishment of. Community participation will be promoted throughout all the stages of the program; communities’ feedback was significant element for the needs assessments conducted before proposing this action. Community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure that the project is well understood by target communities and raise the communities’ awareness about their participatory roles and responsibilities. Participation will be promoted through the involvement of beneficiaries in the formation of community committees and planning the implementation schedule. The community committees will be created to organize and mobilize the beneficiaries, participate in the development of beneficiary selection criteria, distribution of inputs and monitoring and evaluation of the intervention. Representatives from a cross-section (concerning age, gender, and diversity) will be ensured to participate as community committee members and in the monitoring and evaluation of the project. The consortium partners have intensive experience working with the target communities and established relationships and inclusive community structures which will facilitate the establishment of effective feedback mechanisms. The most appropriate mechanisms will be established in consultation with community members, taking into consideration literacy rates, accessibility to communication devices and the presence of suitable sites for establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response. Good internal procedures and confidential reporting systems will be implemented to ensure the confidentiality and safety of all community members. The communities’ feedback will be acquired and verified through different approaches including regular monitoring activities and feedback, complaints and response mechanisms including focus group discussions, semi structured interviews, key informant interviews, community meetings, establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes in project sites.
