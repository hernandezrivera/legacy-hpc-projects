1 - Needs

General food security and livelihood situation, just like any other counties of South Sudan, has in the recent years, deteriorated in greater Kajo Keji and Lainya Counties of Central Equatoria State, as a result of the protracted conflict that started in December 2013. The IPC August 2019 report, estimates 6.35 million people facing acute food insecurity. The outbreak of the 2016 conflict greatly affected the Equatorial region, including Kajo keji and Lainya Counties. Lives, properties, farms and livelihoods were destroyed at the height of the crisis in 2016. This caused a widespread displacement of people from Kajo Keji and Lainya, with a large number of affected people fleeing to neighboring Uganda to live as refugees while others internally displaced within Kajo Keji. (OCHA August 2019 reports estimate the number of South Sudanese refugees in the neighboring Countries to have reached 2.32 million people with 60% children and women while IDPs at 1.5 million people). This displacement has caused untold suffering, misery, distress and hunger to refugees/returnees, IDPs and host communities in one of the worst humanitarian situation ever witnessed in the two counties on record. In 2018, the signing of the revitalized peace agreement on September 12, followed by reports of expected return of SPLA/IO Opposition Leader, Dr. Riek Machar in November, 12 2019 and the long awaited formation of the National Unity Government, has caused mixed feeling and hope amongst South Sudanese including refugees, IDPs and host communities from Kajo Keji and Lainya Counties of Central Equatoria. The recent events of development in South Sudan, has further attracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities to return and settle in Kajo keji and Lainya Counties in 2019 and widely expected to attract returnees and IDPs further in 2020. IRNA inter agency report, June 2019, estimate total number of returnees to have reached to 86,799 people in June compared to 14,742 people in March 2019 whereas for IDPs 36,559 people in June compared to 17,447 people in march 2019.With destruction of lives, farms, properties and livelihood, in addition to limited inadequate humanitarian assistance in the two Counties, influx of returnees and IDPs are expected to put further pressure on the already strained and limited humanitarian assistance and services in Kajo Keji and Lainya Counties in 2020. In order to address the humanitarian situation in 2020 and to provide culturally acceptable remedies on how best to provide livelihood emergency support in Kajo Keji and Lainya, SCA proposes to support the FSL Cluster, through delivery of an integrated emergency livelihood support package to include projects that support and respond to livestock vaccination and treatment and food production with particular focus on distribution of vegetable and crop seeds and tools. SCA will also use this FSL project to build on the existing livelihood and Child Protection projects that it has been implementing in the two Counties since 2017 as part of its multi sectorial integrated strategy to provide a durable lasting solution to the lives of the most vulnerable in Kajo Keji and Lainya Counties of Central, Equatoria State by 2020.

2 - Activities

To provide integrated emergency livelihood support to address urgent and robust food security challenges to most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in Kajo Keji and Lainya Counties of Central Equatoria State, SCA proposes two interlinked implementation strategies; a) Animal vaccination and treatment and b) Crop production. Through strategy (a), SCA will; 1. Mobilize, select, train and support Community animal health workers (CAHWs) on basic animal healthcare skills and management 2. Distribute vaccines and livestock kits to selected farmers 3. Support construction of selected animal crushes 4. Identify, vaccinate and treat livestock, while through strategy (b); SCA will, 1. Mobilize, select and registration of most vulnerable beneficiaries 2. Support provision of fast growing vegetable and crop seeds. 3. Provide basic farming tools 4. Train farmers’ groups on better methods of farming through good agronomics practices 5. Conduct post-harvest monitoring to selected farmers. This project will largely build on current emergency livestock vaccination and treatment project funded by UNFAO and Protection (Child Protection and Gender Based Violence) related projects funded by IRRM-IOM and UNICEF, SCA has implemented in Kajo Keji and Lainya Counties since 2017. These activities are part and partial of an integrated emergency livelihood support to affected people linked to FSL Cluster Objective 2 HRP 2020, aimed at enhancing emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and livestock support. These activities, if implemented concurrently, will establish a nexus between humanitarian and resilience programming and provide a durable solution to food security situations to the affected people. By the end of the project, 2,000 most vulnerable Households in Lainya and Kajo Keji will benefit from this integrated emergency livelihood support project.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

All SCA stakeholders can give feedback and make complaints, including direct and indirect beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, and group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the SCA program activities. Complaints are collected through paper based system at the field level, phones calls, emails, complaint boxes, during monitoring visits, established help desk, coordination/review meetings, and recipient/complaint committees. Possible courses of action for resolving disputes and complaints include: Apologize and accept the complaints when genuine and consider all relevant information and arguments; Refer/forward to alternative agencies if the complaint/issue raised is not within SCA mandate and is unable to assist with services; Explain the complaint process and the steps that will be followed complaint submission, if possible, resolve the complaint immediately or commit to a time frame for investigating the complaint further; Refer if the complaint requires additional information and further investigation/analysis by management for decision making; Referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention. SCA has also evaluation forms especially during trainings which will enable any of the participant to respond/rate and comment on the paper about any issue that does not please or which provides room for improvement.SCA will ensure planning and programming are gender and age responsive. Children, women and girls will be consulted through Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) and other methods on issues that affect them and decision taken to support them will fully reflect their thoughts, ideas and suggestions to achieve results.
