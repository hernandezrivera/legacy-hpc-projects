Project Summary

This project seeks to prevent, respond to, and mitigate the effects of SGBV with regard to UNHCR persons of concern in West Region of Libya. The project provides prevention and awareness-raising activities, SGBV case management and referral to essential services, supports psychosocial support services, builds community capacity, and supports linkage and direct service provision to ensure provision of SGBV related health care.

1 - Needs

While the extent of SGBV remains underestimated and unknown, it is believed that it is endemic in Libya and refugees and asylum seekers, including women, girls, men and boys are specifically vulnerable and at risk. Many of them may have been exposed to SGBV in the country of origin, during the flight (in particular when crossing the desert and at the mercy of smugglers and traffickers), and in Libya, in particular when held in detention (including the official detention centers and the detentions run by smugglers and militia groups) but also remain vulnerable in urban areas. Forms of SGBV include sexual violence, rape, sexual exploitation and forced prostitution/survival sex, trafficking, domestic violence and early/force marriage. Men and boys are exposed to SGBV, including rape and sexual exploitation. In the urban context, refugees and asylum seekers face extreme stress associated with displacement, insecurity, loss of livelihoods and other challenges related to living in a conflict environment which often results in negative coping mechanisms, including early marriage, survival sex, and widespread domestic violence. However, due to fear of retaliation, stigmatization and forced marriage to the perpetrator in case of rape as well as the lack of action from the legal system in country (impunity) which leaves survivors unprotected, SGBV remains to be notoriously underreported. Lack of awareness and information regarding SGBV services, result further in most survivors of rape and sexual assault not having access to emergency medical assistance, including clinical management of rape. Coupled with all the above mentioned, SGBV remains to be a very sensitive topic due to the traditional environment and social context. These factors, in particular the lack of awareness and information also impede on the development of effective and quality multi-sectoral response mechanisms, based on a survivor-centered approach

2 - Activities

Information dissemination and awareness raising: Information dissemination will be provided to all PWSN accessing the CDC including women, girls, boys and men. Information dissemination topics will be determined on the basis of routinely updated service mapping, and will focus on basic rights and how to access available services. PSS services for women and girls: Organize structured activities for women and/or adolescent girls based on an adapted version of the PSS IRC curricula. The PSS activities will follow a women-, girl-, and survivor-centred approach and will include group safety discussions, relaxation techniques, recreational activities (picnics, drawing, sewing, traditional events, henna, 16 days of activism, etc.), as well as information dissemination. Emergency case management for men and boys: Men and boys with specific needs including disabled people will have access to emergency Risk Mitigation: Community mapping and assessments in a participatory approach with girls, boys women and men in the community.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Given the volatility of the context, violence or a security event could make it impossible for staff and supplies to access intervention sites as well as for targeted population . Escalation of conflict may result in temporary evacuation of international staff from the country, and temporary suspension of activities to certain areas. Other known operational challenges are the recruitment of staff and the availability of pharmaceuticals in Libya. Cultural attitudes pose obstacles in the dissemination of gender-related messages, posing risks to staff and beneficiaries. Liquidity remains a concern in the Libyan environment. Refugees and asylum seekers, in addition to a limited knowledge of services available also face a lack of financial means, discrimination and threats to their physical and legal safety when attempting to access public health services. A UNFPA survey indicated that 87% of female respondents needed permission and at times an escort from a male family member, spouse or parents to access health services. Other data suggests women don’t access health services out of fear of stigma, gossip, or lack of confidentiality. An estimated 70% of respondents were not aware of any community based organization providing services in their community. Hardship resulting from multiple displacements and loss of a protective environment puts children in Libya at higher risk of abuse, exploitation and violence. The HNO reports that out-of-school adolescents and unemployed young men are particularly at risk of being forced or coerced into joining armed groups or militias. Although there is little data about the specific needs of older migrants and people with physical and mental disabilities, these groups are among the most vulnerable due to lack of financial resources and discrimination.

5 - Mitigation measures

The security situation in Libya is monitored closely. Contingency measures are adopted including low visibility, curfew hours and restrictions of movement to insecure areas, and relocation procedures in place. Considering that protection activities will be based at the community level, they will be subject to specific security monitoring and clearance to ensure the safety of the team. Should the location participating agencies will consult on the best course of action. Agencies continuously engage with the POC community to understand access barriers, allowing for a rapid identification of potential access challenges. Should challenges arise, participants will identify further potential mitigation measures, including relocation if needed.
