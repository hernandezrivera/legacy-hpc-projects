Project Summary

This project aims to provide integrated emergency food security and livelihoods assistance to 60,000 (10,000HHs) vulnerable women, men, boys and girls including IDPs/host communities/ returnees in the IPC crisis and emergency phases in Jonglei and Upper Nile States. This project is in line the South Sudan Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster Objectives, CO2 and CO3. By providing emergency food security and livelihoods assistance through direct distribution of agricultural inputs, livestock vaccination and treatment services and training on improved agricultural production techniques, this project shall improve access to food, increase vulnerable households food production, strengthen households and community livelihoods and coping capacities, and, consequently, support early recovery and resilience among the targeted households in Jonglei and Upper Nile States. African Leadership Skills Initiative (ALSI) has been operational in the Greater Upper Nile for over a decade with competent and experienced staff that will be rapidly deployed to respond to the needs. The organization currently has implemented FAO-funded food security and livelihoods project (Emergency Livelihood Response Programme-ELRP) in Ayod County for more than five years. ALSI has built very good rapport and excellent community goodwill, local state ministries linkages, strong local networks and coordination with the FSL Cluster. ALSI shall employ multi-sectoral approach to promote vegetable and crop production, reduction in post-harvest losses and safe guarding animal health, restoration and support of fishing activities, focus on restoring and strengthening livelihoods (IPC 4 and IPC 3) alongside more multi sector integrated actions (e.g. WASH, Nutrition & Health), advocacy for women’s needs, voice and rights, capacity building and skills transfers, livestock vaccinations and treatments, and increase efficiency and informed assistance through robust assessment and post distribution monitoring. Through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, protection including Gender Based Violence/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, Disability inclusion, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

1 - Needs

The severity of the food security situation in South Sudan is still very high and, according to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis for Dec. 2020, an estimated 6.35 million people (52.6% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 2.1 million people faced Emergency (IPC 4) acute food insecurity. An estimated 5.8 million people (48% of population) are projected to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC phase 3 or above) between December 2020 and March 2021. Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with all counties in Jonglei State projected to be in Emergency (IPC phase 4 and above) within the projected period of December 2020 and March 2021. Nearly all the counties in Jonglei and Upper Nile states are projected to face increased levels of acute food insecurity (IPC phase 3 or above) between April 2021 and July 2021 with more than 1.63 M people (85% of the population) in Jonglei and Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) in IPC phase 3+ while 1.03M people (71 %) in Upper Nile State projected to be in IPC phase 3+ within the same period. Jonglei and Upper Nile States are also facing critical levels of acute malnutrition with the recent IPC report classifying as IPC AMN Phase 4 (GAM Prevalence of 15.0-29.9%). The direct and indirect impact of COVID-19 and related pressure put on markets and the movement of goods; an upsurge in sub-national violence, especially in Jonglei; desert locust invasions in the east of the country; the unprecedented flooding experienced in Jonglei and Upper Niles States in the year 2020; a protracted macro-economic crisis; higher food prices; and a devaluating local currency are all threatening the food security of people. Other factors that influenced and continue to impact negatively the food security situation include the seasonality of livelihoods, grain stocks, prices, climate, movement of livestock, nutrition status of people, and ongoing conflict. Jonglei and Upper Nile State face the greatest food insecurity severity due to compounded shocks exceptionally amplified by intensified inter-communal violence, two consecutive years of widespread flooding, indirect effects of COVID-19, and a protracted macro-economic crisis resulting in record high food price spikes severely impacting people’s livelihoods and access to food from markets, livestock and farming. The depreciation of the South Sudan Pound (SSP) resulted in low purchasing power of household. The local currency weakened and touched the record low rate at 750 per US$ on 14 Oct 2020 leading to the above normal increase in prices of food items across markets, especially for exclusively imported items such as wheat flour, sugar and vegetable oil. Low crop production caused by prolonged dry spells, floods, crop pests and diseases, and shortage of seeds and agricultural tools as well reduced access to livestock products as animals migrate in search of water and pasture. Chronic pattern of food insecurity has eroded livelihoods, disrupted social safety-nets and negatively affected the resilience of affected populations. This is compounded by already existing factors of low seed and tools access, monoculture, flooding, dry spells, low area under production, lack of land use planning, weak state institutions and poor governance. Deterrents associated with limited and lack of means for income generation and limited access to markets, have been worsened now due to insecurity and poor road conditions. As a way to cope, households (mainly female-headed HHs) engage in negative strategies such as reducing number of meals, eating less preferred foods, sending children to beg and selling livelihood assets. Income generating options are scarce and unprofitable due to inflation with most vulnerable HHs engaging in petty trade and selling firewood/charcoal further adding to environmental degradation.

2 - Project activities

Support vulnerable and food insecure households with crop and vegetable kits, and agricultural tools and fishing kits; Train community leaders, lead farmers and extension staffs on improved agricultural production techniques including the management of Fall Army Warm(FAW); Train fisher folks on improved fishing and fish preservation techniques; Train farmers on vegetable utilization and preservation; Vaccinate livestocks against endemic livestock diseases; Treat livestocks heads against endemic diseases; Provide refresher training for CAHWs; Provide new training for new CAHWs; Train new stock persons/AHAs; Provide refresher training to existing SP/AHAs on cold chain managements and monitoring and CAAP; Conduct Participatory Disease Search sessions for emerging livestock diseases; Install and maintain solar fridges for vaccine handling at the field; Procure and distribute new CAHW kits; Capacity Building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production training( farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation(business skills), vocational training etc., among targeted beneficiaries; Support value chain addition e.g. through milk and fish preservation, and marketing among targeted beneficiaries; Train communities on improved natural resources management practices; Identify and support DRR projects which target youth; Conduct post-distribution, post-planting and post-harvest surveys; Conduct monitoring sessions and compile reports.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project beneficiaries will be informed of their right to file complaints, including reports of SEA and sexual harassment through established reporting mechanisms before commencing the project activities. ALSI highly safeguards safety, confidentiality, and dignity of the reporting beneficiary and handles the matter with utmost respect and in a non-discriminatory manner. Project-specific complaints are directed to the PSEA focal point in the head office. Any report of SEA or sexual harassment at the field level is reported immediately to the Head of Field Office who will communicate with ALSI’s Project Manager and Executive Director, per the ALSI’s Whistleblower Policy, for an appropriate response and/or investigation. According to ALSI’s Whistleblower Policy, the PSEA focal person will enlist support from various individuals to ensure a prompt, diligent, and confidential investigation appropriate to the complaint.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ALSI will put in place a number of mechanisms through which feedback and complaints from all project participants, including vulnerable members, can be collected. These include suggestion boxes installed at project meeting venues/distribution points, complaints desk manned by a trained officer at each input distribution sites, and AAP committees, through which beneficiaries will be able to submit their complaints or feedback about the services provided by ALSI. Vulnerable members such as persons with disabilities, those who are not literate, women, and girls will be educated on how best they can share feedback using any of the above channels without fear of their information being disclosed.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ALSI will seek feedback from affected populations in order to address any problems that may arise during program implementation and to improve how activities are designed and carried out. Beneficiaries will be informed about how they can register complaints or provide feedback in a variety of ways. For example, during distributions, a helpdesk will be set up where beneficiaries can provide feedback or register complaints with a trained staff member. Beneficiaries are then notified about availability of such services. The staff member at the help desk will be a member of the monitoring and evaluation (M&E) team, rather than the distribution team, in order to ensure adequate separation of duties. The helpdesk staff member will have a standard form for receiving and recording feedback/complaints. At each of the distribution sites, there will also be a complaint box where community members can submit written complaints if they prefer. In addition, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) committees will be established and trained in each community to receive and submit to ALSI any complaints/feedback community members bring them. Beneficiaries will be informed about the availability of these complaint and feedback channels on a regular basis (i.e. during community meetings, training sessions, before and during distribution exercises) so that they are well informed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback submitted in boxes will be regularly collected and combined with the feedback received by the committees during distributions. Non-sensitive feedback will be summarized and shared with beneficiaries and staff at the field level. The information generated via feedback and complaints system will be used to continually improve program implementation. In particular, program teams will meet at least quarterly to discuss general program feedback received and to agree on action plans to address any concerns raised. Program teams will then provide feedback to beneficiaries about what will be done to address their concerns, and will then carry out the agreed action steps. Program staff will provide feedback to beneficiaries at the start of program activities to inform them about steps taken to address their concerns (e.g. through pre-distribution messaging for distribution activities, at the start of trainings for training activities, etc.). Where applicable, project manager will also inform the relevant local authorities about plans to address general concerns beneficiaries have raised. Non-sensitive complaints that do not involve general program feedback, but which are specific to an individual or individuals, will be reviewed by the head of field offices, presented to the project manager, and shared with executive director in monthly reporting. Where such feedback necessitates a response, head of field offices, in consultation with the project manager, will agree on action steps and the individual(s) who raised the complaint will be notified about what will be done to address their concerns. Feedback involving critical or sensitive protection issues, however, will be reported to protection officers. The field offices will also notify the head office. In such cases, an action plan will then be created to address the issues raised and a team unconnected with the complaint will investigate.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

ALSI has identified the following protection related risks and analysis: Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services (Likelihood is low and impact is high). To mitigate this risk, ALSI will ensure that registration is conducted and assistance provided as close to the beneficiaries as possible. ALSI will work with existing community structures and local authorities to ensure that appropriate locations are identified for project activities so that beneficiaries do not walk for long distances for registration or to receive assistance. Activities will be implemented in line with the seasonal calendar to ensure accessibility to project locations. Inputs will be distributed and trainings conducted prior to the rainy season during which some locations are either cut off or inaccessible. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities (likelihood is low, impact is high). As a mitigation measure, ALSI will adopt participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches throughout the project life-cycle from the onset of the project including consultations during identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. During the identification and registration of beneficiaries, ALSI’s project team will adopt the FAO’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms (likelihood is low, impact is medium). To mitigate this risk, ALSI will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns. ALSI will strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria and how to provide feedback or complains to ALSI. This will be done through regular meetings with the target communities and use of IEC materials to disseminate information about the CRM. ALSI upholds a code of conduct that is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Beneficiaries will be informed of their right to file complaints, including reports of SEA and sexual harassment, through established reporting mechanisms including setting up hotlines. ALSI highly safeguards safety, confidentiality and dignity of the reporting beneficiary and handles the matter with utmost respect and in a non-discriminatory manner. Program-specific complaints are directed to the PSEA focal point at the head office. Any report of SEA or sexual harassment is reported immediately to the Executive Director who will communicate with programs team, per the whistleblower policy, for an appropriate response and/or investigation. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources (likelihood is low, impact is high). ALSI's mitigation measure for this risk entails developing and sensitizing the local authorities and community groups (youth and women groups) on the beneficiary selection criteria. The beneficiary selection process will be monitored by ALSI staff to ensure objectivity and impartiality. PDMs will be conducted to ascertain the objectivity of the beneficiary selection process and appropriate corrective measures will be implemented in case of any issues identified.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The program will provide emergency assistance to beneficiaries with a particular focus on those in IPC 3, 5 and 5 living in Jonglei and Upper Nile States. The assistance will ensure that critical inputs are available, accessible, and utilized in a manner that reduces the suffering and vulnerability of those affected by conflict and natural flooding. They will be provided with emergency food production assistance including main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution and fishing, vegetable, and crop kits. With this type of support, affected HHs will be empowered to produce sufficient food to last them through the lean months when food is normally scarce. Dry season response kits, fishing kits and vegetable kits, which will be provided just before the onset of the dry season, will supplement this. Women and youth will be targeted as they have a key role in ensuring food security in the household.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)
12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project will adopt an integrated multisectoral approach with interventions occurring in other sectors (WASH, Health, agriculture/food security, economic recovery, etc.) that directly contribute towards the reduction of global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates among targeted HHs. For example, by encouraging beneficiaries (women) to join crop and vegetable production groups, the project will offer a platform for additional group-based activities and information sharing on topics such as nutrition and health. Field supervisors and village agents will be trained to layer these topics into farmer group meetings. ALSI will conduct cooking demonstrations with a focus on the vegetables promoted by the project. The purpose of conducting the cooking demonstrations will be to enable the mothers/caregivers to gain new food preparation skills, develop their confidence in preparing new dishes, and create awareness of underutilized locally available vegetable/staple crops. All HHs participating in dry season vegetable production activities will be instructed on how to prepare nutritious meals in addition to other topics (e.g. washing or cutting vegetables, food hygiene, meal frequency, etc.). In addition, through the provision of fishing kits and training on fish harvesting and fish processing, the targeted beneficiaries will further be provided with another layer of support through which they will be able to improve household food security and nutrition.
