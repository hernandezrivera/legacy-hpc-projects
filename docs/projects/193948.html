Project Summary

Plan International Sudan will target 50,182 of most vulnerable and marginalized girls and boys at risk of exclusion from education of the newly displaced and vulnerable host communities (27,600 gils and 22,582 boys) in 5 states of Aj Jazirah, Gedaref, White Nile, South Kordofan, and Kassala. Those target states areas receiving significant inflows of people fleeing violence, as well state like South Kordofan host protracted displaced populations, with current waves of IDP arrivals further straining limited basic services, and exacerbating humanitarian conditions. This response aim to assist the target children affected by conflict and crisis have access to safe and protective learning environments as well have enhance structured lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being within a protective environment. The children targeted detailed as follows:
- Aj Jazirah: 15,000 internally displaced and vulnerable children (8,250 girls and 6,750 boys) in primary schools in 2 localities of Al Hasahisa, and Medani Al Kubra.
- Gedaref,: 4,000 internally displaced and vulnerable children (2,800 girls and 1,200 boys) in primary schools in 3 localities of Al Fao, and Al Fashaga.
- White Nile: 15,432 internally displaced and vulnerable children (8,488 girls and 6,944 boys) in primary schools in 4 localities of Aj Jabalain, As Salam / Ar Rawat, Kosti and Tendalti
- South Kordofan: 7,750 internally displaced and vulnerable children (4,262 girls and 3,488 boys) of primary education in 3 localities of Abu Jubayhah, Kadugli and Talawdi.
- Kassala State : 8,000 vulnerable children (4,400 girls and 3,600 boys) in basic schools of 5 localities of Madeinat Kassala, Reifi Aroma, Reifi Hamashkureib, Reifi Shamal Ad Delta and Reifi Telkok.
The response’s objective will be achieved through two outcomes. Firstly: Improved access to safe and protective learning environments for vulnerable children including children with disabilities by through support of enrolment of target children in child-friendly learning spaces, provision of recreational supplies and hygiene kits for schools’ adolescent girls as well improved learning environment through provision of safe drinking, rehabilitation of construction of gender responsive latrines , establishing temporary learning spaces provision of psychosocial support activities and establishing referral pathways for protection services and provide children access Alternative Basic Education. Secondly Improved structured quality of lifesaving education for crisis affected children, through provision of teaching and learning materials. This is beside capacity building of teachers to prevent and respond to child protection and safety in learning environment by training them on child protection, provision of psychosocial support, and prevention and response to gender-based violence (GBV), positive discipline, and building resilience. This will be further supported by establishing child protection referral pathways.

1 - Needs

The situation in Sudan has significantly worsened since the last update on humanitarian needs was released in November 2022 . Starting on 15 April 2023, fighting between the SAF and the RSF has killed hundreds of people, forced more than 218,000 to flee across borders and displaced over 730,000 people inside the country in the first four weeks of the conflict. Education has been severely affected, with schools and educational institutions remaining closed in conflict-affected areas, namely Khartoum, North Darfur, South Darfur, West Darfur, Central Darfur, East Darfur, North Kordofan, South Kordofan and West Kordofan.With approximately 6.9 million children not attending school before the conflict, the learning crisis has deepened with higher levels of risk of physical and mental threats, including recruitment into armed groups. As of 11 May, schools and educational institutions have started to reopen in areas not affected by hostilities, preparing for the final academic year examinations.Vulnerable Children in the newly displaced and vulnerable host communities in the states of Aj Jazirah, Gedaref, White Nile, South Kordofan, and Kassala have prioritize immediate critical life-saving education needs and continuity of access to education services and retention. The highlighted needs include need for safe and child-friendly learning spaces, equipped with both learning and recreational supplies, distribution of Education in Emergency supplies to both schools and safe learning spaces. Furthermore, providing psycho-social support through counselling and other interventions is highly needed to equip children with the skills they need to cope with trauma and menstrual hygiene management for adolescent girls.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will improve access to safe and protective learning environment for vulnerable children including children with disabilities through implementing a set of activities including:• support enrollment of 50,182 child (27,600 gils and 22,582 boys) in a child friendly learning spaces • provision of 114 kits of recreational materials for 39,900 child • provision of hygiene kits for 5520 adolescent girls at primary schools.• Improving school WASH by provide safe drinking water for 5250 child in 15 leaning spaces, establishing 50 temporary handwashing stations and construction and rehabilitation of 30 gender responsive school latrines. • establishing 100 temporary learning Spaces.• Providing psychosocial Support (PSS) for 40000 child in target schools through different activities • Establishing child protection referral pathways in 72 primary schools• support 300 children access Alternative Basic Educationin addition the project will contribute to improved structured quality of lifesaving education for target crisis affected children through:• Provide 50,182 child with emergency teaching and learning materials.• Training of 537 teachers (337 females , 841 males) on child protection, PSS, GBV, and positive discipline.This response is targeting 50,182 vulnerable children (ages 6-18) including 17,455 IDPs (girls 9,600 and 7,855 boys) and vulnerable residents 32,727 (girls 18,000 and 14,727 boys).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Plan International maintains high standards of Safeguarding and PSHEA (preventingsexual harassment, exploitation and abuse), more-so in emergency situations. Plan’sSafeguarding Policy and procedures are implemented during the design andimplementation of project interventions with additional vigilance and we adapt andstrengthen existing interventions to identify and reduce risks to girls, boys, children,young people and beneficiaries. Safeguarding and PSHEA risk analysis andassessment of project interventions will be conducted with mitigationmeasures/contingency plans identified for each risk. Plan will work to ensure staff,partners, and beneficiaries and communities are aware of the safeguarding policy, itsminimum codes of behavior, and requirements to uphold high standards ofprofessional behavior. The implementing partners were assessed to ensure theadequacy of the partner’s safeguarding and PSHEA policies and guidelines. Mitigationstrategies take account of particular risks or vulnerabilities associated with girls, boys,children and young people. Children and young people are respected and their safetyand protection is not compromised when obtaining and using information on andimages of them.
