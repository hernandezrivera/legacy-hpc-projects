Project Summary

The economic and social situation in Syria continues to decline and severely affect everybody especially those who have been displaced from or returned to their homes. The humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2022-2023 as several factors still worsen the economy situation that make people in front of a big daily challenge to secure their basic food needs. The Economic pressures are key drivers of need, with large groups stating that the most widespread challenges are inability to afford needed food items and following negative coping strategies.
The purpose of this project is to provide food parcel to most vulnerable Syrian people in Rural areas, based on HNO 2023 the food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent of the total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity, an alarming increase of people at risk who could shift to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels.
Our contribution to this objective is to reduce the vulnerability of affected population through the provision of general food assistance (which its content designed based on SO1 response package) in targeted areas of this project by distributing food parcels during project life, with a purpose of assisting the very vulnerable population in these areas. As well as to re-establish livelihoods and boost purchasing power. In rural areas this through out a range of food production and processing activities such as restoring services (including irrigation; land preparation or harvesting; dairy production; extension ; and advice), and the supply of inputs to farming communities (including all value chain phases production, processing and marketing through providing seeds; agrochemical inputs; fuel and/or funding; livestock inputs, equipment; and power to support processing; and cold chain activities).
The food parcels (including wheat, rice, pasta, raw chickpeas, beans, tea, brughul, vegetable oil, sugar, salt, canned tuna ,canned sardin, halawa, canned meat and lentil)
As an outcome for this project The well being of 2,000 vulnerable families (around 10,000 individuals as an average of five members in each family) in need who will receive 16,000 essential food items during the 2023 project for eight rounds. Also 600 H.H will be supported to re-establish their own livelihood activities during 2023.

3 - Needs

The food insecurity PiN has risen by 45 percent in the last two years of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure. Food prices in Syria have increased dramatically in 2020-2021 and WFP’s national standard reference food basket price in November 2020 was 251 per cent higher when compared to November 2019. The aim of this project is to provide assistance through in-kind, to those in need of assistance and scale up/expand to the newly food insecure caseloads including those impacted socioeconomically by the protracted crisis and COVID-19This FAS project will target geographical areas with severity 4 with food assistance and early recovery resources based on the severity of needs. So our contribution to this objective is to reduce the vulnerability of affected population through the provision of essential food items. GOPA's mobile team conducted needs assessment visits in these areas at the last two months from 2021year in order to better identify the humanitarian needs and priorities of families affected by the crisis, so that it can design a program that better meets these needs.based on HNO 2022 context in the targeted areas, 90 per cent of the population is estimated to live below the poverty line, the socioeconomic deterioration is expected to trigger further increases in extreme poverty, and to aggravate already alarming food insecurity and malnutrition rates and protection concerns.

4 - Implementation of activities

These project activities was planned based on the sector priority which will be food assistance and emergency agriculture and livelihoods assistance and resilience.The distribution process will complete within three phases:- GOPA-DERD team will arrange with the local community leadersFGD meeting in order to explain the project objective and selection criteria to the community.- Community leaders in each area will provide beneficiaries list in coordination with GOPA team.- Beneficiaries will invited to register their personal data.- The distribution and registration location will be in familiar, public and accessible places that take into account the social environment of the target area (GOPA centers, mosque square, school, or municipality building,...)- On the appointed day the commodities that will be distributed to the target areas will shipped in order to be distributed to the families who were registered in the first step.This project proposes to continue with a scaled-up approach to combining lifesaving and life sustaining assistance with livelihoods activities and promote early recovery and resilience through irrigation, land preparation or harvesting, dairy production, extension and advice. All distributing commodities will be procure from local market based on GOPA-DERD procurement policy.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All project activities will use a rights-based approach to provide a protective environment that promotes equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical and social specification of targeted populations.Also, all our volunteers have already received a package of training including: psychological first aid, non-violent communication, protection induction, basics of SGBV, first aid, PSEA and risk management in case of any emergency, as well as other basic information that enables them to act towards any person in need with total respect and most importantly to deliver assistance while protecting the dignity of the person.GOPA will ensure the “Do No Harm” principle by carrying out a Protection Risk Analysis as part of its strategy development. In some urban and crowded areas, humanitarian and beneficiaries may face a GBV abuse and Sexual exploitation, GOPA will make sure that its staff is well trained on SGBV basics. Moreover, GOPA had developed a misconduct internal policy to be fit with the humanitarian works in all sectors and locations.It is very common that the children would be exposed to any kind of harassment or abuse, GOPA will ensure providing gender-segregated facilities to avoid such cases.
