Project Summary

"WaSH project is continuing to ensure inclusive and equitable provision of life saving water and sanitation services building on infrastructure already constructed in the previous phase in 2019 (in the Camp no,14 and 15 and host community) and strengthening behaviour change communication (BCC) by encouraging community-based collective measures.
There are two main routes of transmission of COVID-19: respiratory droplets and direct contact. Respiratory droplets are generated when an infected person coughs or sneezes. Droplets may also land on surfaces where the virus could remain viable and thus, the immediate environment of an infected individual can serve as a source of transmission (contact transmission). The provision of safe water, sanitation, hygienic living and environmental conditions are essential to protect human health during all infectious disease outbreaks, including the COVID-19outbreak. Hand hygiene, personal and social hygiene are key elements in preventing this disease to spread. As of today, a lot of aspects of COVID-19 prevention and control are still unclear, including implications related to WASH interventions (like virus persistence on surfaces or sewage).
The previous phase of the intervention has contributed to an increase in the appropriate usage of handwashing. Almost 79% of the respondents from the end of project evaluation reported that handwashing points installed under the project were utilized for hygiene maintenance. In terms of safe and inclusive access to water, the water point accessibility for PWD, pregnant women, adolescent girls and aged persons has been enhanced. The increase in ease of accessibility for pregnant women rose from 6% in baseline to 48%, similarly, for adolescent girls, it improved from 24% to 83%. So, maintenance of these essential WaSH facilities and services are vital to reduce the risk of disease outbreak.
Bear in mind the gravity of the issue, CAID will continue to ensure lifesaving water and sanitation services to the Rohingya and host communities in 2020. Focus will be provided to address the key challenges facing WaSH service such as congestion at water points, unequal distribution, inclusive access and protection concerns in bathing cubicles and latrines."
