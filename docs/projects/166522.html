Project Summary

COVID related: WFP will provide unconditional food-assistance to the most food-insecure, crisis-affected
populations. Life-saving general food assistance (in-kind food and cash-based transfers)
will be delivered to IDPs, returnees and host populations in North-East Nigeria (Borno,
Yobe and Adamawa States). Early recovery and resilience to shocks will be promoted
through asset creation and preservation, increased livelihoods opportunities, and
enhanced agricultural value chains. Joint multi-sectoral assessments and Emergency
Food Security Analysis will be conducted regularly to monitor the nutrition, food security,
and markets situation in crisis-affected states.
A gender-transformative approach that reflects the needs of women, men, girls and boys
will be adopted. WFP will mainstream protection throughout its work, supported by a
solid understanding of the operational context and protection analysis. Training and
capacity building of staff and partners in protection and humanitarian principles will
include on-the-job coaching. WFP will ensure that affected populations (women and men)
will be consulted throughout the programme cycle; beneficiary feedback mechanisms will
be tailored to their preferred means of communication. Beneficiaries will continue to be
empowered with knowledge on their food assistance entitlements and their rights,
including WFP’s zero tolerance policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Protection
against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be embedded in M&E tools, complaint
feedback mechanisms and training of partners and staff.
The WFP protection and AAP unit is made up of two national protection officers and two
international protection officers. This would need to be correct as it currently states 6
national protection officers and three international protection officers.
WFP will continue the collection of key information on beneficiaries and assistance
provided as part of its M&E activities. Monthly monitoring will include distribution and
activity site management; beneficiary and entitlement verification; and ensuring adequate
protection and gender measures. Lessons will be regularly drawn from monthly process
and biannual outcome monitoring and reporting, and periodic reviews and evaluation to
inform strategic and operational decisions.
WFP will further consolidate partnerships to enhance national and state-level emergency
preparedness and response systems, including in food security and nutrition assessment
and monitoring, early warning, protection, gender analysis, targeting and registration
processes, safety nets, cash-based transfer capacities and supply chain.
Where security and access to land allows, increasing livelihood activities will complement
food assistance to support access to productive assets, income generating activities and
self-reliance in the long-term. Assistance will be coordinated with government
distributions, to ensure food assistance is complementary and fills gaps. Through an

1 - Needs

COVID related: The persistent and widespread humanitarian crisis in the northeast of Nigeria continuesto cause repeated displacement, protection concerns and unrelenting food and nutritioninsecurity. Millions of children, women and men bear the brunt of the conflict, which hasdecimated livelihoods, crops, livestock, infrastructure, education and basic services.According to the October 2019 Cadre Harmonise analysis (projection for June-August2020), approximately 3.8 million people in the BAY states are projected to be foodinsecure and considered to be facing crisis or emergency situations. The October 2019Cadre Harmonise confirmed a worsening of the food security situation compared tofindings of the CH analysis for the same period in 2018. The marked deterioration in thefood security situation is linked to the upsurge in insecurity as evident in renewed attacksby non-state armed groups which have led to a new wave of displacements, limitedaccess to farming and grazing land essential for livelihood opportunities. This situationhas stretched communal resources due to increased dependency from IDPs andreturnees. Of the 1.832 million people that remain displaced in the three most affectedstates (Adamawa, Borno and Yobe), 79 percent are women and children. In recentmonths, thousands of people in dire need of assistance have newly arrived at areas ofrelative safety, placing increasing pressure on humanitarian actors to meet their mostbasic food, nutrition and other needs. Notwithstanding, the continued assistance by theGovernment of Nigeria and the humanitarian community to deliver food and nutritionassistance as well as livelihoods support, access to farming/grazing land, labor market,productive assets and livelihood activities remains limited. Given the current levels offood and nutrition insecurity, below-average crop production, disrupted livelihoods, andhigh staple food prices, food security and nutrition situation remains extremely fragile.Large populations in the northeast remain dependent on humanitarian assistance tomeet their food and nutrition needs. WFP regularly conducts protection analysis includingDo No Harm, protection risk assessments and protection monitoring within the context ofthe provision of food assistance. Protection and gender specific questions have alsobeen integrated into vulnerability food assessment tools and rapid intersectoralassessments to ensure that protection and gender is captured in food securityassessments. Depending on the context, one or more of the following targeting criteriacan be considered in agreement with the communities: people with disabilities with nosupport[1]; heads of household with a disability; households headed by older people withno support; child headed households, separated and unaccompanied children; heads ofhousehold taking care of a large number of children or relatives; households headed byfemales with no support; households headed by widows with no livelihoodsource/support; households with a large number of dependents; households with nolivelihood activity/source; households reliant mainly on risky income generating activitiessuch as firewood gathering; newly displaced individuals (less than 3 to 6 months)residing in a camp and not integrated with communities; households headed by peoplewho are chronically sick; households found to be adopting negative coping strategiessuch as beggars (when considered by the community as an abnormal consequence ofshock).

2 - Activities

COVID related: (1) Provide unconditional food assistance and income-generating activities to food insecureIDPs, returnees and host communities affected by crises [contributes to FSSObjective 1]. In 2020, it is planned under this activity to reach up to 1,984,593 beneficiarieswith GFD (in-kind food, CBT) in BAY States. Food assistance will be maximized from thebeginning of the year up to the lean season and will decrease during the final quarter ofthe year (post-lean season and harvest period). (2) Provide conditional transfers to food insecurepersons, including women, young people and smallholders [contributes to FSSObjective 2]. In 2020, it is planned under this activity to reach up to 250,000 beneficiarieswith conditional food assistance, increased livelihoods opportunities, asset creation andpreservation, and natural resource management in BAY States. In 2020/21, a plannedgradual increase in the number of people assisted to reach 200,000 and 250,000beneficiaries, respectively. (3) Support the technical capacity of the federal, state andlocal actors in IMS, vulnerability assessment and mapping, M&E, safety net management,food technology and fortification, supply chains, nutrition and EPRP, integrating gender[contributes to FSS Objective 1, 2, 3]. To continue focused support to the most vulnerableconflict-affected communities and LGAs, WFP leverages evidence from the CadreHarmonise and other supporting information such as the EFSA and market and livelihoodassessments carried out by WFP. Multiple factors are considered with priority given to themost food-insecure LGAs (in CH Phase 3 and 4), including the most vulnerable peopleliving in camps and fully dependent on WFP support, and accessibility and coverage bypartners. The criteria below are used to classify beneficiaries that receive lifesaving andlivelihood support in order of priority: IDPs beneficiaries living in camps; newly arriveddisplaced people; IDPs with local communities returnees and host communities. WFP willprovide livelihood and income generating support to IDPs and host communities usingfood assistance for assets (in-kind food and cash based transfers). The activitiesundertaken shall build resilience and be shock responsive in nature falling under therange of WFP’s livelihood typology e.g. agri. based, infrastructural rehabilitation, VSL...etc.The selection of the activity type shall be informed by contextual analysis and communitybased participation. Targeting through a community based approach shall enable furtherprioritisation of the most vulnerable. The type of businesses for which cash grants aretargeted are informed by seasonal livelihood programming that entails an analysis of theseasonal activities that are unique to the operational context and thereby, appropriate forselection. In short, it forms the basis for various businesses that are appropriate for thecontext. The activity selection process, targeting and prioritization criteria, and transfermodality selection ensures social cohesion aligned with the “do no harm” principle. Theselection of both IDPs and host communities to receive resilience building shockresponsive support assures overall community integration in the end. The selection ofactivities and transfer modalities are informed by WFP’s operational context, beneficiarychoice and community-based targeting/prioritization. Gender and protection analysisalso informs the above mentioned selection/approach. The appropriate geographicaltargeting is also informed through the FSS and the CH results, complemented by WFPfood security assessments and IOM DTM. Targeting and prioritization of beneficiariesand activities are collectively derived through the participation of Partners and thecommunities to avoid duplications and minimize assistance gaps. WFP collaborates withIOM through a data sharing agreement assures a harmonized registration approach viaan electronic data exchange.
