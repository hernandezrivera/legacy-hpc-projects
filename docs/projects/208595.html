Project Summary

TCDI 2023 HPR project is designed to addressed the most pressing lifesaving WASH needs identified in the targeted locations. This project will contribute to the overall WASH objective and ensure populations affected by Northeast Humanitarian crisis have reduced risk of exposure to WASH related diseases through provision of access to lifesaving water services, sanitation and hygiene. TCDI will response to the severe cholera outbreak as a result of the recent floods.
The project has a contingency plan for the projected outbreak of flooding during the rainy season of 2023. These will be addressed through drainage clearing, construction of local drainages as well as rehabilitation of broken drainages. Cholera crisis will be addressed through the new cholera response approach, implementation of Case Area Targeted Intervention and as well ensure distribution of WASH supplies.

1 - Needs

According to the 2024 humanitarian, 5 million people have been identified in need of humanitarian WASH assistance across the BAY States with a significant deterioration of the severity compared to last year, from 27 to 34 LGAs in critical situation (phase 4). Women and girls accounting for 52% of the population in need, often are tasked with fetching water far away, potentially exposing them to protection issues. Children accounting for 60% of population in need are highly impacted making them susceptible to WASH related diseases and malnutrition. Persons with disability require adaptive WASH infrastructures-water supply and sanitation, through which their dignity and hygiene are upheld. The WASH needs within the BAY states are expected to increase contributed by multifaceted drivers into foreseeable future. Given the sustained nature of the conflict interspersed with periods of lull in hostilities with a chance of sporadic escalations, the number of internally displaced people may increase across the camps and in some instance due to potential threats and actual closure of camps population movements are expected from one or several camps to other camps resulting in overcrowding and mushrooming of informal settlements. This would result in the continuous stretch and strain on the existing already limited WASH facilities in the IDP camps and potential host communities.Climatic shifts and its effects on the availability of water resources are also expected to increase/more frequent following global changes in climate and weather patterns. These factors could result in droughts and flash floods, which could affect the availability, quantity, and quality of water sources with periodic displacement. Increased demand for and challenges with FSM could lead to sanitation problems and increase the risk of waterborne diseases propagated by flooding. Although humanitarian intervention can help close the gaps, the combination of factors such as conflict, population movements, overcrowding in camps, and climatic changes affecting weather pattern (drought and flash floods) and providing suitable environment for epidemics such as Cholera, Malaria, and Dengue fever, suggests a continued protracted WASH needs in the BAY states in the most likely scenario. Internally displaced population accounting 31% of the population in need across different IDP camps especially in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe are primarily mostly affected and in need of WASH services through routine operation and maintenance or construction and expansion of WASH infrastructure across the camps. Sanitation fecal sludge management (FSM) remain one the major needs and challenge across all the camps. Returnee Population accounting for 27% of the population in need either in IDP camps or in host communities are in need of water supply sanitation and hygiene promotion including WASH supplies such as hygiene kits to prevent disease outbreaks due to lack or use of contaminated water sources and instances of open defecation due to lack of sanitation facilities. Host communities accounting for the remaining 42% of the population in need, reel from lack/limited of basic services worsened by insecurity and other natural disasters such as floods and food insecurity, adding to this in some areas such as Pulka, Damboa and Monguno in Borno as well as Maiha, where host communities have had to share limited water resources with IDPs and or their water supply infrastructure- destroyed during the conflict or affected by changes in climate.

2 - Activities

1. Emergency trucking of chlorinated water before sustainable water supply sources are developed. 2. Provision of safe drinking water at community and/or household level, through new construction and rehabilitation of water supply systems including handpumps, boreholes rehabilitation or construction, water systems and networks rehabilitation. 3. Ensuring access to safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene through maintenance/repair and replacement of water system parts (including Handpumps, Solar Systems, Generator Systems, and Electric pumps), operation and maintenance of existing water systems, batch chlorination, bucket chlorination at the points of use, water treatment tablets and provision of fuel for water supply. 4. Provision of gender-segregated sanitation facilities and services (latrines, showers, handwashing stations) to protect the health and dignity of the affected population also supporting vector control campaigns through the development of solid waste and fecal sludge management systems. 5. Desludging of emergency and institutional latrines as well as related decommissioning of sanitation facilities. 6. Affected people are reached with community-informed hygiene messages promoting hygienic attitudes, behaviors and practices, including, household water treatment, proper handwashing with soap at critical times and Menstrual Hygiene Management. 7. Provision and prepositioning of WASH NFIs to the people in need (hygiene kits, replenishment hygiene kits, WASH cholera kits, WASH Nutrition kits, sanitation kits and MHM kits).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Beneficiaries are involved in all stages of the project from needs assessment to project evaluation. Before the start of the implementation of the project, TCDI will organize an inception meeting to inform all beneficiaries and key stakeholders about the purpose of the project, TCDI’s identity and mission, and the importance of raising feedback and complaints about TCDI’s work. To ensure the project complies with Do No Harm principles, TCDI continuously and proactively engages with local communities during community meetings and project activities. This is particularly important before the start of the project. All staff is trained in best accountability practices in the field. Beneficiaries are directly involved in hardware activities (e.g. construction & rehabilitation of water points, latrines, and bathing shelters) through decision-making regarding site selection. Hygiene promoters are recruited from within the local community. Those hygiene promoters will act as stepping stones for the community to provide additional insight, understanding, and feedback from the beneficiary community. A specific budget line is included for accountability costs, which includes materials and training for staff. Materials will be tailor-made to beneficiaries, meaning information will be translated into local languages and visual materials are used.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

TCDI actively commits to Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), and pro-actively informs and guides all TCDI staff on why and how to be accountable to beneficiaries. Beneficiary accountability is one of TCDI's quality standards and priorities across all humanitarian interventions. TCDI handles all complaints and feedback through its Complaints & Response Mechanism (CRM) system. Each project has a complaint/feedback focal point to coordinate complaints and feedback received. Beneficiaries have access to this mechanism verbally (phone, face-to-face) or non-verbally (email, SMS). Different channels are being used, but experience shows community committees are the most effective way to collect feedback and complaints from the community since most beneficiaries are illiterate or do not have a phone. All feedback is registered by the MEAL officer and reviewed by the Programme Manager and Country Director. He/she deals with all complaints and feedback according to TCDI Complaint Handling Procedures.
