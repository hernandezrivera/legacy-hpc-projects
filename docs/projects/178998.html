Project Summary

As part of its education response, PUI is targeting vulnerable and underserved communities with a bundle of activities, with the overall objective of enhancing the access to quality education provided in protective and suitable learning environment for affected children, to ensure that they don’t become a lost generation. Indeed, with the protracted crisis approaching its tenth year in Syria, an entire generation of children and youth is being subject to persistent lack of opportunities to access quality education. This situation could have long-term negative consequences, affecting children’s mental health and wellbeing and preventing them from developing in good and safe conditions, as well as gaining essential life skills and knowledge.
The proposed project in brief:
- Will target locations where high education needs are documented by Education Sector. 63 % of the locations have a severity score of 4, while 31 % have a score of 3. PUI also will target Al Haffa which has as score of 2 as per above mentioned document; however, according to PUI observations the needs are high and proposed intervention will complement PUI interventions under other sectors.
- The cost per beneficiary is 27.24 USD.
- It is estimated that 353 individuals of targeted beneficiaries may have disabilities.
- Total funding the organization received in 2020 for education is about 4.5 USD from non UN donors. These funds are for two years, which means it will also cover 2021 activities PUI plans to implement. Additionally, PUI is currently in the process of fund raising, thus 2021 budget might increase.
This project aims at encouraging out of school students and dropped-out students to resume their education through tailored back to learning campaigns and empowering those children with self-learning program conceived as transition programs to enable them to catch-up and reintegrate the formal education system after this transition period. In addition, PUI will assist children in their education by providing them with kits, education supplies and remedial classes to cover for any gaps and ensure every child’s educational needs are met and to prevent them from dropping out of school. Furthermore, PUI will contribute to enhancing the quality of education by providing capacity building activities to teachers and MoE staff, training them on active learning, PSS, and life skills. PUI will also initiate an awareness campaign for 11,400 children and their parents on health and hygiene, with a specific focus on COVID-19 protection. Lastly, in parallel to the above-mentioned activities, PUI will ensure that children have access to safe and protective learning environment where they can access quality education by rehabilitating damaged schools in targeted locations.

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has forced many children out of school or put them at risk of dropping out. Due to the crisis, many schools have been damaged or are no longer accessible. The shortage of functional schools resulted in overcrowded classes that negatively affect the education performance and undermines the school attendance of a lot of children. At the same time, children face complex barriers to accessing education including displacements, family economic difficulties, child labor, and early marriage as well as the cumulative psychological effects of the protracted crisis. Lastly, the education system is also suffering from a shortage of qualified education staff.As per the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) of 2021, an estimated 6.9 million people (97% are below 18 years old) need humanitarian education assistance, with about 94% of school-age children living in areas with really poor education conditions. In 2020, an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out; numbers for 2021 should even increase due to the worsening socio economic-situation in light of the Covid-19 pandemic.Through field visits, PUI and local stakeholders identified that education was often interrupted/limited due to security issues and displacements. Furthermore, school damage, lack of education staff, and the pressing economic situation is leading to an increased risk of dropping out of school. Additionally, many girls are deprived of education due to early marriage. Furthermore, youth and young adults have been particularly affected as the hostilities forced them to leave school before reaching a certain level of proficiency in professional skills or accredited educational degrees.

5 - Implementation of activities

In terms of organizational capacity, working in Syria since 2008 , PUI directly implements integrated programs, including education and protection, livelihood, WaSH, shelter and NFI components, with the overall objective of bringing the Population of Syria back to their initial standards of living, to foster self-sufficiency and to promote social cohesion. Through the implementation of this integrated response, PUI gained good acceptance in its areas of intervention and increased the positive impact of the interventions implemented. PUI always tends to create synergies between its interventions; the proposed intervention will therefore complement other interventions implemented in targeted areas. The design of the response is based on consultation with key informants from local stakeholders and the targeted communities to design the best way of implementation without leaving any one behind. Also, PUI introduced several tools to ensure monitoring of implementation at all stages of project. Moreover, coordination at field level with local stakeholder and humanitarian partners and Education sector working group is ensured by the Local representative officers to share knowledge and to avoid duplication of provided services .

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Acceptance of local population for PUI´s interventions remain high; Humanitarian access to the proposed locations continues; Approvals for the proposed activities are obtained; Security situation remains stable; No mass movements of populations into/out of intervention areas and No major fluctuation in exchange rates. Risks:1)COVID-19 new waves and lockdown as a mitigation 1- PUI will keep good coordination with the concerned stakeholders to anticipate such measures. 2- full adherence to WHO recommendations for all PUI Staff, partners and contractors. Also PUI will adapt a remote modality (blended or online trainings and classes) to ensure safe delivery of services. .2) Aids is diverted to armed groups > Mitigation measures: 1- Intervention areas are assessed and perceived safe by PUI and its stakeholders; 2- PUI has the capacity to follow the full process of activities implementation.3)Limited facilitation by partners contributes to a delay in implementation > Mitigation measures: 1- PUI maintains regular formal and informal contact with all partners at field and mission level; 2-PUI continuously advocate to further expend the presence of staff in the field.4)Variations in exchange rate negatively impact local procurement > Mitigation measures: PUI times procurements in line with overall exchange rate trends, and will delay or re-schedule purchases as needed; exchange rate terms are also elaborated in contracts with suppliers.5)Independent access to beneficiaries is restricted during monitoring stages > Mitigation measures: PUI relies on standard methodologies, including photography, field monitors and data management systems to confirm implementation and continuously monitor progress.
