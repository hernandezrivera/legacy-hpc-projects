Project Summary

After 12 years of unyielding conflict, Syria faces a persistent humanitarian crisis, with 12.7 million people requiring assistance in 2023. In NWS, a significant portion of the population encounters severe needs. The assessment underscores necessities in CCCM. MWL's efforts are geared towards strengthening camp management committees, pinpointing rehabilitation activities, and providing income-generating activities and business start-up grants to boost livelihoods. Camp infrastructure facilities need repairs due to bombings and natural disasters. There's a pressing need for risk management and mitigation training, along with teams ready to tackle emergencies. Initiatives to enhance livelihoods are vital for improving living conditions and promoting income-generating ventures within the camps, incorporating vocational training programs and start-up grants for women and PWDs to better livelihood conditions in the camps.
Life-saving aid for individuals in camps includes: Mitigating the vulnerability of displaced families (620 families) by offering secure shelter units equipped with essential humanitarian infrastructure and support for a duration of 12 months, including enhanced measures and basic humanitarian infrastructure. The camps' governance and management mechanisms are to be effectively implemented. Establishing robust data and information management to ensure inclusivity for women, men, boys, and girls. Setting up an inclusive and accessible Help Desk, alongside a confidential complaints and feedback mechanism for reporting issues such as intimidation, coercion, violence, sexual exploitation, and abuse when receiving aid.
The primary activities involve providing 6 camps with necessary administrative and humanitarian infrastructure repairs (fire points (12), administrative offices (6), and WASH facilities 18 latrines, 100 lighting poles. Conduct 6 IVT sessions for 100 beneficiaries. Organize 5 SME training for 90 beneficiaries. Offer 80 start-up grants. MWL will establish community committees within the target sites to foster community engagement, participation, and social cohesion.
PRA: Collate data on prevalent protection risks. Identify key risks and vulnerabilities. Evaluate potential project interactions. Conduct safety audits to address protection-related risks, aiming to mitigate or address them. For instance, many camp roads and sectors lack adequate lighting, including toilet facilities, particularly for women, posing significant protection threats to IDPs that necessitate immediate service provider intervention at the camp level per sector.
PSEA strategy involves all MWL staff and beneficiaries signing the PSEA and CSG policy. PSEA sessions for all personnel are planned. Assumptions include the targeted individuals' willingness to engage in Informal Vocational Training (IVT) activities, the capability of PWDs to access training facilities safely, a work environment protected from PSEA and Child Safeguarding (CSG) issues, and obtaining necessary permissions from local authorities. To counteract trainee dropout from IVTs due to extended durations, MWL will leverage experiences and lessons from past IVTs to create concise, intensive training materials that impart essential skills within two months. Clear communication about the IVT will manage expectations and formalize agreements. A CRM system will ensure comprehensive complaint resolution. Potential tensions between IDPs, returnees, and the host community will be addressed through effective community engagement and mobilization. To boost female participation, the project will ensure learning environments are safe, private, and welcoming, with measures like female trainers and staff and easily accessible centers, alongside maintaining gender balance within the project staff, drawing on past successes in inclusive initiatives for women and PWDs.
