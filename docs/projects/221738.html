Project Summary

Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) is proposing this project to reach 18,000 vulnerable population (Host, Returnees, IDPs) in IPC phase 3+ in Pibor, Aweil Centre, Rubkona, and Bor South counties. The Project interventions will aim to prevent famine and improve food production and consumption, dietary diversity and improve household coping strategies for the target population in IPC phase 5, IPC phase 4 and IPC phase 3 localities. The project further seeks to enhance community driven livelihood interventions so as to reduce over reliance on food and agricultural inputs by working with communities using a group model approach and supporting them with microgrants.
CRSF shall deliver and distribute main season crop, vegetable and fishing kits; and dry season fast maturing crop, vegetable kits and fishing kits for immediate benefits. This is anticipated to improve food production, nutrition status of vulnerable beneficiaries at household level; and improving household income for a period of 12 months from January 2024 to December 2024.
CRSF will coordinate with FAO Pipeline for in kind support of crop, fishing and vegetable kits to benefit 18,000 (3,000 HHs) vulnerable population (Host Communities, returnees and IDPs): (10,000 women, 4000 men, 1,500 boys and 2,500 girls).
CRSF will establish 5 large community demonstration gardens (one in each locality state) to serve as farmer field school for training farmers groups, and also set up kitchen garden in the targeted nutrition, health and schools facilities identified in coordination with stakeholders, nutrition, health and education partners as well as the line county and state ministries with in the targeted counties. In addition, CRSF will adopt a combination of intervention approaches including complementary livelihood support, capacity building of farmers on modern agronomic practices, Integrated Pest Management, Natural Resource Management), and resilience building at the household level. Further integration ad complementarity of FSL interventions into WASH and protection will be at cornerstone of this project, while ensuring strict coordination with the efforts of other humanitarian actors and in alignment with line ministries, and FSL sector objectives. To ensure inclusivity, the project will also focus to benefit people with disability (PWDs) and capacity build them on improved crop and vegetable production, proper fishing methods and onsite routine practical trainings for the farmers (affected beneficiaries at Boma and payam level). To ensure adequate agricultural extension services for beneficiary farmers, CRSF will reach the target beneficiaries through a team of trained agricultural extension agents (FSL extension workers) who will undergo a TOT training for sustainability purposes in all targeted project localities. CRSF will ensure that youth and women are trained on income generating activities for instance VSLAs to improve household income that will finally lead to their livelihood improvement. In this project, CRSF anticipates to work with affected population and community stakeholders who will partake of the project AAP committees to oversee and monitor implementation of the project in the target locations. CRSF will also take note of the climate sensitive agricultural practices for instance planting of fruit trees like Oranges, Pineapples which are key to improving the nutrition status of the vulnerable population in project target population.
Overall the project aims to achieve improved food security and livelihoods at household level for the vulnerable population at risk of famine and malnutrition. To ensure that implementation of FSL planned response is aligned with 2025 HNRP county prioritization, CRSF will ensure the FSL response for Aweil Centre is implemented for 6 months.

1. Needs

The current trajectory of the food security situation in South Sudan indicates an alarming trend with new food security data showing 57% of the country’s population will be acutely food insecure through the 2025 lean season amid high influx of returnees fleeing the war in Sudan, children facing the highest levels of hunger and malnutrition, economic pressures, climate shocks worsening hunger.According to IPC Report (Nov 18th, 2024), 5.83 M people (46.3%) of the country’s population face high levels of acute food insecurity and classified in IPC 3 or above (Crisis or worse) . The most food insecure populations in counties including Pibor, Bor South, Rubkona and Aweil Centre have been significantly affected by frequent climatic shocks, economic crisis, conflict and insecurity including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and reduced humanitarian assistance.Further, Between September to November 2024, 53% of the populations in Jonglei, 51% in Northern Barhrel Ghazel, and 57% Unity state currently face IPC 3 or above with many of the vulnerable children under five and PLWs in these household classified as AMN IPC phase 4. During the lean season period in the first and second projection from December 2024 – March 2025 and from April to June 2025, the severity of acute malnutrition is likely to deteriorate in 61 out of the 80 counties . Children Under the age of five, Pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and elderly are perceived to be at greatest risk of severity of acute malnutrition and emergency food insecurity.The IPC report further reveals that, food consumption gaps still exist at extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. Multi-year flooding has limited agricultural production and access to markets, while the depreciation of South Sudanese Pound and the global impacts of the multiple crises including; Israel-Gaza and Sudan crises are contributing to rising prices of fuel and basic commodities. There is a need for immediate support and scale-up of integrated interventions to save lives and protect their livelihoods and reduce household-level food consumption gaps. The Khartoum/Sudan crisis continue to affect South Sudan's economic outlook, especially for Northern States that rely heavily on imports from Sudan, leading to an increase in food prices and fuel costs. This could negatively exacerbate protection risks and vulnerabilities of the population in these localities including returnees from Sudan and thus increased food insecurity, negative coping mechanisms and acute humanitarian needs. This is happening amid the protracted conflict and frequent natural disasters including flooding in the country which has resulted into displacement, increased food insecurity and exposure of the population to the risk of asset loss and destitution. Highly vulnerable returnees are settling into areas that are already facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity and a shortage of available livelihoods due to years of compounding shocks and stressors. The majority South Sudanese returnees have endured long, arduous journeys, are traumatized, and many have experienced GBV. People are arriving in South Sudan in extremely bad health, nutrition, and physical conditions, almost all have demonstrated food insecurity and the inability to meet basic needs. Host communities are strained by increasing demand for resources and services by returnees. CRSF has presence in project sites with funding from its partners (Roddenberry foundation, UNDP, recreational project and FAO and aims to improve food production through access to livelihood kits (crop, vegetable and fishing kits) and practicing proper agricultural practices and supporting longterm resilience through working with microgrants for income generating activities that contribute positively to improving food security outcomes.

2. Project activities

CRSF will deliver its planned FSL response activities aligned to the 2025 HRP and FSL cluster objectives as follows;CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5). Under this objective, CRSF will provide the following activities;Activity 1.1: Conduct cash and voucher distributions; conditional food transfers like Food for Assets.Activity 1.2: Conduct Food security and Nutrition education as well as sensitization in the project localitiesCO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. Under this objective, CRSF will provide the following activitiesActivitiesActivity 2:1: Distribution of main season agricultural kits (CK, VK, FK): CRSF will conduct distribution of emergency livelihood kits; fishing, vegetable & crop kits to increase emergency food production during the main crop season. This activity will focus on crop production specifically sorghum, maize and legumes.Activity 2:2: Distribution of dry season agricultural kits (VK, FK): CRSF will conduct distribution of Dry season agricultural kits which will include Emergency food & livelihood support through provision of fishing kits/ vegetable kits; and at least three variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds. Activity 2:3: Conduct establishment of community kitchen gardens at an identified locality that is accessible by the farmers for proper agricultural trainingsActivity 2:4: Establish of kitchen gardens at Nutrition sites targeting mothers and caregivers of children enrolled in nutrition program with training and input support.Dry season farming will focus on water efficient farming methods to produce sufficient and nutritious vegetables for consumption and sell the excess vegetable for cash and savings in VSLAs. This will ensure increased availability of food during the lean season where availability of vegetables becomes a challenge and therefore complicates the food insecurity and malnutrition situation. Fisheries activities will cut across the main season and dry season as there are sufficient fishing grounds for both seasons.CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. The main activity will be Training and capacity building on both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production targeting, farmers through field schools and on demonstration plots as well as the agropastoralists. CRSF will conduct the following activitiesActivitiesActivity 3.1: Conduct training of individual household farmers on modern agronomical practices Activity 3.2: Establish and train farmers groups on modern agronomical practices in project localitiesActivity 3.3: Conduct training of farmers groups on value addition and providing equipment in agricultural value chains (fish, meat & poultry, vegetables & fruits, milk, honey, etc.)Activity 3.4: Conduct Training of farmers on VSLAs and IGA activitiesActivity 3.6: Conduct training of farmers on Integrated Pest Management (e.g. FAW, Desert locusts, Pesticides etc) Activity 3.7: Training of farmers on post-harvest handling techniques including Storage granaries (using locally available resources as much as possible)Activity 3.8: Mobilize farmers to construct dykes around farms to prevent perennial flooding, excavation of drainage channelsActivity 3.9: Mobilize farmers to construct and rehabilitate access roads to markets during the rainy season
