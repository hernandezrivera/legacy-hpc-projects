Project Summary

As the crisis in Syria protracts, the importance of community-based interventions and mainstreaming is more acknowledged.
The goal of this project is the provision and enhancement of community-based protection assistance in Dana, Atareb, Azaz and Afrin subdistricts. The project will integrate protection services within UDER’s existing health and community-support project in Dana and Atareb, and will establish new services in Afrin and Azaz to cover the needs of the IDPs displaced in early 2020. This approach will beneﬁt from the existence of this program in Dana and Atareb since 2018, and the community acceptance and trust built in UDER’s community teams.
Speciﬁcally, UDER will seek to achieve 3 components:
1- Provision of psychosocial support to improve the wellbeing, resilience and coping of crisis affected population. This will be achieved through mobile psychosocial support teams linked with UDER’s exiting community network, and supported by facility-based councilors to provide private, individual sessions of cases in need at UDER’s community centers. The targeted population will also have access to UDER’s community centers to provide safe spaces for socializing and connecting with protection and support groups. The team will also provide psychoeducation and awareness sessions on protection issues and concepts to the targeted communities including care givers, educators and other individuals.
2- Monitoring of protection threats and mainstreaming of protection principles through retained and enhanced outreach activities targeting IDP locations and areas with limited access to services with a focus on groups with hindered access to services. This will be achieved by dedicating PSWs teams to ensure that UDER’s health and community services are inclusive of vulnerable individuals, and that the targeted communities are empowered to participate in identifying their protection needs including barriers to public services with support from outreach teams.
3- Community-based case-management for cases of concern including survivors of gender-based violence, children at risk, and people with disability. This will be achieved through teams of social workers and educators as part of a community-based MHPSS network to assess select cases and link them with service providers. The teams will further be supported by GBV specialists and health professionals operating at UDER’s existing health centers to receive cases that need structured care, including cases of sexual violence.
The intended methodology is that CHWs will properly identify expressed / suspected protection issues, and refer them to the accompanying mobile PSWs. Those mobile PSWs will be distributed geographically just like the CH teams so each one of the mobile PSWs will be responsible for a certain number of households. They will assess the protection-related issues detected by the CHWs and put intervention plans launching from the basis of social support activities. If the mobile PSW anticipates the case should receive counselling, the case will be referred to the Counsellors in the PHC and/or community centers the CH/PSS mobile teams are linked to. The counselor will draw intervention and follow up plans. If the case needs interventions / services from one or more sector other than protection, the Counselor will refer the case to the Comprehensive Case Manager, who will in turn put the necessary intervention plans for proper engagement with services from other humanitarian sectors.
As they do for Health Referral System, the CHWs, accompanied with PSWs, will play a key role in Atareb and Dana in fostering the Protection Referral System and reaching out the most vulnerable populations including Elderly, women, boys and girls, and people with disabilities to ensure better coverage and the continuity of care. Our teams focus on IDPs concentrations and remote rural communities, given the fact that Dana has received the largest IDP inﬂuxes.

4 - Needs

According to REACH, HNAP and CCCM, between 1 Dec 2019 and 2 Feb 2020, over 560,000 IDPs moved from southern and eastern Idleb towards Turkish boarders, where Dana, Azaz, Afrin and Atareb sub-districts are amongst the ﬁrst intended areas for IDP inﬂuxes. Over 80% of IDPs are women and children. Patient exit interviews conducted at UDER-supported facilities show that transportation presents a signiﬁcant challenge to accessing static services for vulnerable people especially for elderlies, PWDs, women and young children. In many cases they must be escorted by a male relative in order to access static service providers which limits them from accessing these services. Primary data collected by UDER in Atareb and Dana (involving 41,839 HHs in late 2019) shows that 19% of pregnant women were under 18, and UDER’s community teams reported divorce after child marriage to be increasingly observed. This data is supported by a SMART survey in NW Syria showing that 46% of all interviewed women were married before 18, demonstrating that households are resorting to negative coping mechanisms to survive. GBV, particularly in forms such as domestic and family violence, forced marriage, sexual harassment and violence (including rape), denial of resources, emotional/psychological violence and physical abuse continue to threaten the lives of women and girls, resulting in very few spaces where they feel safe. Women and girls are disproportionately affected by all forms of GBV in Syria. Widowed and divorced women and girls and those with disabilities continue to face stigmatization, discrimination, sexual violence, and forced marriages. An MSNA conducted by the Protection Cluster partners of WoS, shows that Child labor and Child marriage are reported in 84% and 69% respectively of assessed communities. Psychosocial distress is reported in 42% of surveyed HHs demonstrating the cumulative toll on child and adult mental and physical wellbeing with immediate and life term consequences.

5 - Implementation of activities

UDER has already 120 CHWs from Atareb and Dana sub-districts targeting over 13,000 families, and more CHWs will be hired for Afrin and Azaz. PSWs will be added to the CH teams based on clear selection criteria in consultation with the community to give special focus on protection issues while conducting HH visits.CHWs are trained on PFA besides protection cases identiﬁcation and referral to protection team. Outreach PSWs will identify protection risks, provide psycho-education and community PSS activities, educate about available services, and mainstream protection principles. While counselors in UDER’s PHC and Community centers will provide specialized MHPSS counseling, the Comprehensive Case Managers will mainly provide specialized CP & GBV services including case management, awareness raising, structured and semi-structured PSS sessions, parenting skills, as well as referrals for services of other sectors.Data is collected by CHWs/PSWs through Kobo surveys using tablets. Team Leaders and Supervisors analyze the data and report to the Program & MEAL Managers. For quality assurance and burn-out prevention purposes, no counselor nor case manager will manage > 25 cases at once, considering each case requires three months in average. MhGAP doctors who visit UDER centers once a week will provide technical supervision. However, UDER is keen to secure more effective supervision by trying to recruit PSWs who are themselves technical supervisors, partner with agencies of such technical expertise, and to provide remote supervision. All data will be documented and stored in a safe conﬁdential place. E-data will be coded anonymously and reported to the technical supervisors, the program and MEAL managers, and TPM parties. The entire processes is ultimately at the survivor hands who owns the decision of continuing or withdrawing from the treatment plan, or keeping, sharing or destroying the information. This will be reﬂected through informed consents for every necessary step

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions-Permission for UDER to operate in Idleb and Aleppo continues-Current conflict doesn't affect program activities-Donor funds continue to be provided for NW Syria-Community acceptance for this project continuesRisks and mitigation measures1-Continuum of violence escalation in Idleb and Aleppo2-Continuous massive IDP movement to northern Idleb and Aleppo might put increased pressure on the humanitarian needs in northern Syria. This requires maximizing the coverage3-Mobile teams may be exposed to harassment or rejection by the community or local authorities.Measures-UDER’s chosen locations for this project (Atareb and Dana) are located near to the Turkish border and away from potentially targeted locations-UDER will equip the mobile teams with communication devices (walkie talkies or local mobile phone subscriptions) to ensure that they can contact with each other, provide information about their location, and receive immediate guidance from their supervisors and facility professionals as needed. Team Leaders will be primarily responsible for handling and reporting security incidents related to their teams, and they will receive support on safety and security measures, including proper walkie talkie communication practices-Close communication will be maintained with Health Directorates, local councils and community leaders to ensure that our teams are covering the communities with the highest concentration of IDPs-Mobile teams’ members are largely recruited from the local community to make sure trust is exchanged between them and communities they serve-The project is backed by local health authorities (independent Health Directorates) and community leaders through ongoing communication and trust-building behaviors. They oversee and support the implementation to ensure the team protection and prevent armed groups interference-Mobile teams have visible ID cards and permissions from local councils to implement the project in targeted locations
