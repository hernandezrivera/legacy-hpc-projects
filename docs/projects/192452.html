Project Summary

Aligned with ERP Health SO1, SAMS will support in-patient hospital care for at least 1,350 vulnerable Lebanese citizens and non-refugee migrants, whose ability to access life-saving treatment has been eroded as a result of the pandemic and Lebanon’s deteriorating socio-economic situation.
The decision to include Lebanese citizens, in addition to migrants, stems from the fact that increasing numbers of Lebanese are unable to meet their basic needs. As stated in a report by the UN Special Rapporteur on Extreme Poverty and Human Rights (11 April 2022), since 2019, over 80 per cent of Lebanon’s population has been pushed into poverty, the currency has lost 95 per cent of its value, and prices have increased by more than 200 per cent. Diesel prices, up by 2,000 per cent in only one year, have made it prohibitively costly for people to travel to work, school, and to receive health care. Nine in10 people are finding it difficult to get by on their income. Moreover, according to findings shared in health sector coordination meetings held in April 2022, due to fuel shortages that lead to electricity cuts, some hospitals have reduced operating capacity by as much as 50 per cent. Availability of hospital beds has also become more limited because of dwindling financial support from the MoPH. Today because of delayed payments or lack thereof, some of the biggest private hospitals are not accepting patients insured by schemes such as the following: the MoPH, NSSF and COOP, and they only admit private patients. In addition, some advanced medical conditions are not approved for financial support by the ministry. For these reasons, SAMS will support life-saving hospital care for the most vulnerable patients on an as-need basis, regardless of whether or not they are covered by an insurance scheme.
SAMS will provide patients with either full financial coverage or top-up contributions to support hospital admission and treatment for urgent, life-saving care. SAMS will screen referred cases against vulnerability criteria to determine whether or not they qualify for full or partial coverage. This criteria is based on prior coordination with MoPH, MoSA, IOM, ICRC, Caritas and other key stakeholders. With regard to payment modality, there will be no transfer of funds to patients. Rather, a network of around 50 contracted hospitals across the country that have been previously vetted by SAMS will submit invoices to SAMS for review and payment of bills of up to $3,000 per patient. The cost per patient was determined in coordination with Health Sector leads, IOM, and based on SAMS’ prior experience. In case patients with catastrophic illness require more support with uncovered costs such as orthopedic material, medical supplies and advanced laboratory and radiology tests (ie. complications/longer-term support), SAMS will strive to cover the cost in full, negotiating also with contracted hospitals.
As an immediate result: 1,000 patients experiencing severe economic hardship will access medical care that they could not otherwise afford, while being alleviated of any protection concerns associated with hospital admissions (such as document confiscation and refusal to admit/detention in hospitals due to a lack of payment). While only an estimate, the anticipated disaggregation is as follows: 625 Lebanese and 375 migrants.
As a long term result: Hospitals will benefit from additional income and patient bills paid to avoid further MOPH deficits.
Having implemented hospital referral programming for refugees, Lebanese, and non-refugee migrants over the past four years, SAMS has built relationships with relevant ministries, UN agencies, and other key stakeholders, alongside a network of contracted hospitals across the country. SAMS has also developed a robust outreach plan that has involved reaching out to community centers, leaders and volunteers to increase access to local communities and supplement its work with regular privately funded medical missions.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

In coordination with relevant working groups and key stakeholders to establish medical/vulnerability criteria and raise awareness of available support, SAMS will target 1,350 vulnerable Lebanese and non-refugee migrants across Lebanon to support with hospitalization costs, allowing patients who may not otherwise afford the remaining bill to access life-saving surgeries and urgent care. SAMS will receive referrals for five types of cases: 1) Lebanese unable to cover the full cost of interventions (covered partially by MoPH, NSSF and/or COOP) will be supported with top-up contributions up to $500 per patient; 2) migrants unable to cover the full cost of interventions will be supported with top-up contributions between $800 and $1,000 per patient (in coordination with IOM and relevant agencies); 3) Lebanese patients who are unable to cover the cost of interventions(who are admitted as private patients without any support from governmental entities)whose hospitalization/admission is fully covered by SAMS, up to a maximum threshold of $3,000; 4) migrants who are unable to cover the cost of interventions (who are admitted as private patients without any support from governmental entities) whose hospitalization/admission is fully covered by SAMS up to a maximum threshold of $1,500; and 5) psychiatric patients (Lebanese and Migrants) who are unable to cover the cost of care (who are admitted without any other support) will be fully covered by SAMS up to $600 per patient.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

During program design, SAMS consulted with the PSEA Network and SGBV WGs to ensure top-up contributions are equally accessible to men and women, including persons with special needs. The project will decrease protection concerns for patients, including the confiscation of ID and legal documents, the long separation of patients from their caregivers, refusal of the hospital to admit or discharge the patient because of medical debt, and the refusal to release the bodies of deceased patients through MOUs with hospitals that name SAMS as the responsible party for payment rather than the patient. Specifically with regard to PSEA, trained Safeguarding Focal Points will be responsible for ensuring that hospitals display reporting mechanisms, and will be ready to support survivors with consent-based referrals, as needed. All project staff are also trained by a Country Security Officer on SAMS Safety and Security Policy.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

In-keeping with the commitments of the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability, SAMS will welcome feedback from beneficiaries and will react constructively to concerns in a timely manner. Recognizing that individuals have access to different means of communication, SAMS utilizes various methods for receiving confidential feedback/concerns through the following methods:1. Receiving feedback over the phone or through hotline numbers, which are advertised in PHCs, flyers, WGs, and other places SAMS accesses beneficiaries2. Receiving feedback through satisfaction surveys/key informant interviews3. Advertising the SAMS services are free of charge SAMS will ensure consistent and effective communication with patients through pre-procedurephone calls, post- procedure calls, and satisfaction surveys. A project monitoring plan will be overseen by the MEAL Coordinator, including field visits, patient service verification, monthly quantitative reports, document audit, etc.
