Basic Info

Project Name

Provision of Water Sanitation and Hygiene services to mitigate cases of WASH related diseases, malnutrition and GBV among 40,000 IDPs, returnees and host communities in Juba, Terekeka and Kajo Keji Counties of Central Equatoria State.

Start Date

01/01/2021

End Date

31/12/2021

Project Summary


NSDO’s intervention in 2021 will focus on the components of improving WASH semi-permanent or permanent services in Nutrition, Health and mitigating WASH GBV as well as building WASH resilience through the introduction of community-centred sustainability. This project will address the present acute WASH needs in Juba IDPs camp by strengthening WASH capacities and promoting positive WASH coping mechanisms for sustainable access to services. 41 Water Management committees will be established in Juba within and beyond the Juba IDPs camps to strengthen community capacities for improving sustainability after end of funds.
Keeping in mind that before transitioning from UNMISS-protection sites (POC1 and POC3) previous interventions were purely emergency. Sanitation and hygiene promotion was funded by UNICEF, and water supply is the prospective component due to the pipeline from the treatment plant being at the completion stage. However, distribution challenges and maintenance remain unaddressed for 9,109 households. Without a comprehensive WASH intervention, the current vulnerabilities of IDPs will increase.
Beyond access to water, the aim will be to control open defections, and mitigation of conflicts due to disputes over access to WASH facilities, vandalism and reduction of COVID-19/other WASH-related diseases risks and persuade healthy coping mechanisms.
NSDO will address sustainability challenges by the establishment of WASH Management Committees, training assistant water technicians to maintain facilities during breakdowns. Support for the WASH Management Committees will be through rehabilitation of sanitation and hygiene facilities and commissioned for sustainability.
NSDO will also work with UNICEF to ensure planned costs recovery from the water pipeline through the introduction of SCOPE vouchers system is acceptable and purposeful. Water Collection points (kiosks with taps) will be established timely access at households, health care units and nutrition points. Considering effectiveness, participatory hygiene promotion will be created at the household level and places accessed by sick persons and persons receiving nutrition services. NSDO will support the WASH committees to adapt to community contributions for sanitation sustainability through making self-arrangements for water collection, dislodging of latrines and garbage and hygiene promotion.
At the community level and displacement sites outside the Juba camps, target locations are those feared for cholera and other WASH-related disease outbreaks, especially during the rainy season. NSDO will enhance and formalize WASH committee groups that will be trained by NSDO to help community members during their attempt to meet WASH needs. The intervention will address the water quality assurance by promoting appropriate cost-effective hygiene promotion, chlorination and related-training for the monitoring at both collective points of use and household level, providing in a first step the WASH supplies for the WASH Cluster Core Pipeline.
Repairs will be done to improve 361 stances sanitation facilities (266 latrines and 95 bathing shelters; and hand-washing facilities will be improved) at IDPs sites, places for accessing health services and nutrition services. The quantity of available water for displaced persons and households at risk will be improved by the rehabilitation of 30 handpumps and 3 mini-water yards and upgrading of 3 water points and establish 5 new ones as per assessment results.
As a result of joint needs analysis with beneficiary members, WASH non-food items will be distributed to 9000 households promote water quality and protection concerns related to accessing sanitation and hygiene services.
The Project will contribute to resilience building at the community level in close collaboration with humanitarian and development actors. NSDO intends to work with nutrition, health and protection partners to mitigate acute watery diarrhoea, COVID-19, malnutrition.

1 - Needs

After South Sudan signed the revitalized peace agreement with its implementation period ongoing, humanitarian needs are on increase especially health and protection across the country. Natural disasters and existence conflicts caused by cattle raiding and inter-communal fights are major drivers. Same conflicts are now being on the increase in Juba and surrounding counties. Years of conflicts affected investment in basic services for the people which hinders development and stability. The majority of displaced people are unable to return to their homes due to unresolved property concerns. The COVID-19 pandemic affected the functionality system of the private sector in service provision that has left coping mechanisms of vulnerable communities without access to service. Poor living conditions, lack of access to clean water increases the likelihood for the outbreak of waterborne diseases, increase malnutrition and protection risks. Most affected persons are newly displaced persons, returnees and communities with large numbers of IDPs. More affected persons are living in single-parent headed households, elderly people and persons with disabilities. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that 50% of malnutrition is associated with repeated diarrhoea or intestinal worm infections as a result of unsafe water, inadequate sanitation or insufficient hygiene. The HNO estimated 1.4 million children under the age of 5 and another 483,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021. Recurrent Ebola cases in the neighbouring country (DRC) continue to pose a significant public health risk. In the target counties of Juba, Terekeka and Kajo Keji, the specific population of focus are those challenged by weak health systems and lack of access to sanitation services (70% open defecation). In these areas, protection concerns and conflicts over insufficient resources for survival is alarming and is expected to worsen the current status of extreme levels of gender-based violence (GBV) at the community level. Already households are adopting negative coping mechanisms that are harmful to the majority of children. Displaced people sheltering in UNMISS-protected Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites are currently experiencing a reduction in services provision due to transitioning to conventional IDP sites under the jurisdiction of the government. Assessments show that 25% to 75% of people did not feel safe most of the time. This project will give solutions to the challenges of an increasing trend affecting access to WASH needs at the most affected sites. 40,000 Individuals with 12000 Boys, 11200 Girls, 8400 Men and 8400 Women are targeted. 58% are children under the age of 18 years while 23% are under 5 years of age. 3% of the population are elderly persons.Targets were based on analysis of needs documented from field visits and assessment conducted by NSDO and participation in Multi-sectorial needs finding missions organized by OCHA. The persons in needs include the IDPs camps (formerly POC1 and POC3) with 29,659 people (15,008 Males and 14,651 Females) in Juba County. Additional 3804 people with 1141 Boys, 1065 Girls, 799 men and 799 Women in Rejaf Payam and Northern Bari Payam. Most of them are displaced living in camps but not registered, living in not served sites and host communities.In Terekeka County, the focus is on Terekeka Payam and Tali targeting 3678 people of communities living without IDPs and temporary sites accommodating displaced persons (1103 Boys, 1030 Girls, 772 Men and 772 Women). Meanwhile 2859 people with 858 Boys, 801 Girls, 600 Men and 600 Women in Mere Payam of Kajo Keji County. These are returnees, communities living with returnees and temporary places accommodating displaced persons. Alongside addressing WASH GAPs in improving health, nutrition and protection, the project will further contribute to better living standards through participating in the resilience-building.

2 - Project activities

Activity 1:Improve access to water at IDPs sites, health care facilities, and nutrition centers and at the household/community level. Upgrade handpumps to mini-water yards at sites registering rapid population increase. Rehabilitation of non-functional water points. Construct new water collection points both for IDPs and communities. Train pump technicians and provide standard took-kits to support WASH management Committees in repair/maintenance. Activity 2:Rehabilitate existing communal latrines stances and bathing facilities targeting public health safety for most vulnerable populations. The repairs will focus on improving safe access to appropriate sanitation for women/girls, elderly, men and children. Repair will re-enforce protection by fixing doors, locks, replacing broken timbers, damaged floors and replacement of worn out structures. Repair will include upgrading sanitation facilities for persons living with disabilities and the elderly. Upon completion latrines will be commissioned for daily latrine cleaning and maintenance by WASH management committees. Activity 3:Conduct participatory hygiene promotion sessions at the Household level, Primary Health Care Units, public places and site for nutrition services. Target children and mothers and other community members at risk of getting WASH-related diseases. Provide basic items for disinfection of water, hand washing, water storage and IEC materials as per WASH Cluster kits. Emphasize on disease prevention (WASH related diseases and COVID-19), safe excreta management and disinfection of hands and water. Conduct hygiene promotion training and PSEA risk management to community mobilised. Activity 4:Install handwashing facilities to improve habits of handwashing at critical times for mitigation of WASH related diseases. This includes provision of soap for handwashing or chlorine (0.5%) solution or chlorination for disinfection of hands. Handwashing targets go beyond public places to household level especially those beneficiating from nutrition services and those facing sudden WASH emergency due to displacement, health risks or flooding. Activity 5:Form and train of WASH Management Committees to facilitate better coping mechanisms for WASH services provision among IDPs and community members.This activity is to build resilience and enhance participatory approach for sharing WASH responsibilities among users, action planning for self-arrangements water collection, communal latrine cleaning, safety audits and monitoring. Activity 6:Conduct Focused Group Discussions (FGDs) with beneficiaries at WASH Management Committees levels to discuss self-arrangements water collection, latrine maintenance of sanitation and water facilities and participatory hygiene promotions. This will include needs analysis of beneficiaries and audit of protection concerns by beneficiaries.Engagement of beneficiaries (women and girls, elderly, persons with disabilities etc) in setting up of renovation of facilities focusing on safety and timely access. Arrange joint field monitoring sessions with beneficiaries, partners on the ground and local authorities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

NSDO has a protection and PSEA Coordinator who will conduct sensitization to the beneficiaries on SEA using existing mechanisms of communication.NSDO will collaborate with partners to create and use existing feedback mechanisms that beneficiaries will access to report SEA. All project staff, volunteers and contractors will be trained in PSEA. NSDO has a children protection policy where each child has a right to protection and basic needs; NSDO works towards achieving that in every activity implemented.NSDO will ensure that project staff are well oriented on mainstreaming protection within the project and know their Code of Conduct. All staff are acquainted and understand the ZERO TOLERANCE for sexual exploitation and abuse. Beneficiaries will be introduced to the organization’s devised and accountable reporting and response procedures for beneficiaries facing exploitation or abuse by humanitarian workers. NSDO has two focal points (one at the senior management level and one at the field level) to monitor protection issues and deal with reports and promote good humanitarian assistance given without conditionality.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During community engagements, NSDO will ensure that information sharing platforms are inclusive. The focal person at information desks will be persons speaking the local languages and sites will be suggested by beneficiaries.The information desks will be functional by times suggested by women and girls; and additionally, separate FGDs will be conducted for women and girls, the elderly and persons living with disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The needs assessment report will be jointly reviewed with beneficiary groups and NSDO will provide hard copies of the project document before the implementation of activities through established focal persons accessible by the beneficiaries and their leaders. NSDO focal person will read through and make an explanation for community representatives to understand the project objectives and expected results. Jointly with WASH management committees, information desks will be established at suitable sites based on suggestion and analysis by beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will be given the opportunity through established complaints desks to provide feedback or raise issues during the provision of services.A suitable information sharing environment will be created through the suggestion box, informing beneficiaries about the contact details/telephone numbers to call or focal persons to report complains to. Additionally, NSDO will gather complaints and feedback through the weekly Focused Group Discussion activities. NSDO will continue to observe its open-door policy where community members are welcomed to give feedback; and will create a platform for community members to give their feed-backs anytime to be shared to monitor project progress.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Information obtained through feedback mechanism such as complaints desks, will be used to improve programming.NSDO will ensure for any piece of information received from beneficiaries of each activity, the response is given to the beneficiaries during FGDs. NSDO Officers and beneficiary groups will review if there is a need to revise existing approaches to meet needs and achieve the project objectives. Information collected from beneficiaries will be compiled for analysis. Results will be shared for decision making. NSDO will utilize existing humanitarian coordination platforms to share gaps with partners so that project beneficiaries are assisted.
