Project Summary

The project is designed to meet the needs of vulnerable populations previously trapped under war zone and located in hard to reach Counties with high food Insecurity, high morbidity due to preventable diseases, high GAM rates, Malnutrition, depleted community assets and poor WASH infrastructure.
The targeted counties are; Canal / Pigi (Jonglei State); Panyikang (Upper Nile State); Fashoda (Upper Nile); Morobo (Central Equatoria); and, Jur River (WBeG).
The HRP 2020 targets IPC 5, 4 is 100% and 77% IPC3. These Counties are within this IPC3,4&5. Out of the 301,130 Persons HRP target cumulatively in these counties; our Intervention will focus on 15% of the HRP target (45,170) persons translated to 7525HHs using denominator 6persons per HHs. These beneficiaries are disaggregated; Men elderly 14003, Boys> 5years 6098, Boys <5years 2033, Female elderly 14906, Girls >5 years 6098 and girls<5years 2033. The Project is designed to meet the needs of the specific age groups. IDPs 60%, IDPs-returnees 10% and Host community 30%
Situation analysis:
Canal Pigi County lie within SPLA-IO held areas with population that often experiences armed fighting (between rebels & SPLA, and inter-tribal conflicts), causing routine displacement, life threats and destruction of community assets. The Nutrition and Mortality SMART survey (IMC April 2019) report records A GAM rate of 24.3% and SAM rate of 6.7%. Access to food remains a major concern. More than 14,832 HHs affected by flood between Jul –Sept, capital assets are depleted; (Canal pigi PHSA HAA Sept 2019). In Upper Nile (UN), IDPs returns continue to increase from 50% in Dec 18 to 77% in Mar 2019, Fashoda and Panyikang host these returnees IDPs from the Sudan (Situation Overview Upper Nile REACH Mar 2019). Poor rains, high market prices for staples, lack of seeds resulted to poor access to food across the counties. Protection concerns still persists especially SGBV. No water treatment. There is an increase IDP and refugee return (IoM DTM Oct 2019) in Kuajena and Rocrodong Payams of Jur River County. There is long term livelihood depletion, poor food access leading to negative copying mechanism wild food gathering. Protection, WASH and Health remained constant concerns. SGB increased including rape, forced marriage, child soldiers’ recruitment, inter communal violence, child headed Households and separated children from their parents were not re-united. Limited humanitarian access (REACH Jan-Mar 2019 Situation Over view WBeG).
In the Equatorias (Morobo) suffered sporadic armed conflicts since 2016. Basic infrastructure is destroyed, limited humanitarian support including food assistance, lack of complementary livelihood support (cropping, Veg, Fish and livestock. return movement appeared to be primarily Household returns (2019 returns scenario brief for HCT).
The project over-arching objectives are two-fold:
a) In line with CO2: provide complementary livelihood support (cropping/veg/fish/livestock) to support targeted vulnerable populations and strengthen their basic livelihood activities that have been disrupted and/or destroyed by the multi-hazards.
b) CO3: Ability to recover and absorb shocks (resilience). This is to reverse the food insecurity situation by providing access to food security inputs to the targeted groups and beneficiaries.
Activities:
1) Mapping and validating the vulnerable households for their eligibility under the agreed criteria
2) Systematic distribution of on-farm inputs (fast maturing crops and vegetable seeds and associated agro-implements) to enhance access to food production technologies.
3) Systematic distribution of off-farm inputs/kits (fisheries’ hooks and fishing nets) to enhance access to off-farm food production inputs.
4) Link the beneficiaries to already HAA supported health facilities and referral pathway for effective case management and above all Basic water treatment for HH consumption (WASH)

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

In-spite-of the prevailing socio­ethnic and clan/tribal barriers to physical access, to benefit humanitarian assistance, all project activities will be carried out impartially in the target areas; most of all, regardless of whether the selected beneficiaries are IDPs, returnees and/or host community. This will mitigate the potential for escalated conflict over access to emergency assistance amongst the vulnerable groups. HAA will ensure that gender disaggregated data is collected and priority for support will be accorded to elderly women, female­headed households, widows, pregnant and lactating mothers. Women will be separately consulted in identification of needs and distribution mechanisms, and there will be gender parity in the beneficiaries committees. Men and women, girls and boys will be mobilized for the validation of the final food security and livelihoods intervention beneficiary list. Specific attention will be given to victims of sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) and women­led structures will be assisted to link with specialized agencies which will play an important role in identification of and provision of psychosocial support to victims of SGBV. In addition, women will be engaged as food and production inputs distributors or social mobilisers during the planning and actual distribution of the food parcels, hygiene kits and agro and fishing inputs. Distributions will be tailored (e.g. timing, location, prioritization, etc.). Throughout the project HAA M&E teams will be gender­balanced. For example, seeds and tools will be distributed equally to both men and women, with both genders represented in the distribution committees. The location of farmland for vegetables’ seed beds will be decided by both men and women to ensure it is in well­protected areas, close to their homes

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

In line with a Do­No­Harm and conflict sensitive approaches, HAA staff in collaboration with the county and community leaders carefully engage all relevant stakeholders (including the IDPs, returnees, and host communities) in ensuring participatory approach to the emergency service delivery. Community input will inform locations of agricultural demonstration points, agro-inputs distribution points, days and time of convergence. Safe access to the inputs (seeds, fishing kits, goats) and distribution locations will be assessed to take into consideration all attendant potential risks to beneficiaries as well as implementing mitigating measures. HAA staff will be well inducted/ trained, on the job, in order for them to rollout the ‘do harm approach’ by frontline workers (extension workers/inputs distributers) in the field. The locating food parcels and agro-inputs kits’ distribution sites, timing of distributions and distribution procedures will also be informed by safety considerations for women (including lactating and pregnant mothers), boys, girls and special categories e.g. the elderly and persons with disabilities to ensure that distributions are inclusive and have no biases. Safe programming will also involve downward accountability through complaint handling and feedback case management mechanisms. 6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The FSL proposal will go hand-in-hand with the cluster objectives of access to food as a stop-gap measure and also strengthen livelihoods of the vulnerable population at risk of hunger and malnutrition. The emergency response will ensure vulnerable population access to livelihood kits through distribution of food parcels, hygiene kits, crop seeds, and vegetable seeds thereby securing access to sufficient food all the time for active, healthy life, access to food defined by entitlement to produce and therefore complement the nutrition status of the community. In addition, the fisheries resources will provide a sources of proteins’ food for complementary diet during the lean season as farmers continue with their production activities. The project will integrate livelihood intervention into HAA existing programming to ensure holistic responses. Complement to the livelihood efforts and strengthened relationships of various targeted communities especially the vulnerable groups in the project areas will be a priority of the intervention. This intervention is in line with the Ministry of Disaster preparedness Humanitarian affairs strategic plan 2020
