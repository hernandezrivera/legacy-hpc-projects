Project Summary

An estimated 4.8 million people across Somalia are expected to face a humanitarian crisis, of these 2.6 are internally displaced people in various part of the country. Moreover, an estimated one million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through mid-2020. Current high levels of food insecurity are driven by drought conditions from late 2018 through mid-May 2019, exacerbated by below-average and erratically distributed rainfall during the 2019 Gu season (April-June), which began late and finished early. In the absence of assistance during the October- December period, food security is expected to deteriorate in most of northern and central Somalia and some parts of the South (FSNAU sept 2019)
Decades of conflict have destroyed infrastructure, on which lives, and livelihoods depend, such as water, soil, land, and forest resources, making Somalis increasingly vulnerable to recurrent climatic shocks. Restoring the farmland productive capacity of Somalis to better prepare for, respond to and recover from shocks is a precursor to improving food security and nutrition, as well as reducing future humanitarian needs. In the project area of Buloburte and Jalalaqsi districts, food security and nutrition among agro-pastoral and riverine communities are critical and considered hotspots in need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions. They currently have or are projected to have a Critical GAM prevalence, indicated by a weight-for-height z-score of 15-29.9% or by a Mid-Upper Arm Circumference below 125 mm of ≥10%
The poor nutrition situation in Buloburte and jalalaqsi is mainly attributed to poor household food security in addition to a lack of access to adequate health and humanitarian interventions due to the blockage of insurgents. This project is therefore aimed at enhancing the capacity of the rainfed and riverine farmers to rebuild their productive assets and reconstruct their capacity to absorb the recurrent climatic shocks. The project provides farm inputs, animal-drawn tools, water pumps, and extension services to the affected farmers. It also provides training on soil fertility amendments and good farming practices for ensuring sustainable food production in agro-pastoral and riverine livelihood zones of Buloburte and Jalalaqsi districts. Direct target beneficiaries of the project are 6500 households of agro-pastoral and 7500 riverine communities in Buloburte and Jalalaqsi districts and will be provided farm inputs to 6500 HHs of agropastoral (15kg sorghum +5kg cowpea) and 7500 HHs of riverine (20kg maize+5kg cowpea) farmers. it will also provide vegetable seeds to 2500 HHs of riverine farmers ( 20grm tomato, 20gram pepper, 20grm onion, and 20gr of kale) as well as training of 2500 farmers on composting, and water harvesting techniques for enhancing farmland productivity and restoration of farmland ecosystems. the project will support indirectly to pastoral communities and Livestock within the target communities will have access to crop residues and pasture of the restored ecosystem. SOADO will address the underlying causes of food insecurity and restoring farmland productivity of target areas for building the adaptive capacity of the project area.

Project Activities

1. Restore productive capacity of the agro-pastoral and riverine community through the provision of farm inputs to 6500 HHs of agro-pastoral (15kg sorghum +5kg cowpea) and 7500 HHs of riverine (20kg maize+5kg cowpea) farmers in the project target areas (14000 HHS)2. Provide vegetable seeds to selected 2500HH of riverine farmers (20grm tomato, 20gram pepper, 20grm onion, 20gr of kale) for the diversification of their livelihoods.3. Train farmers of 1000 agropastoral and 1500 riverine farmers on manure composting & water harvesting techniques for improvement of soil fertility management and ecosystem restoration.
