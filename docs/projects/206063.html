Project Summary

From the time of attainment of independence in July 2011, South Sudan has be having a protracted conflict that has put its entire population from extreme to catastrophic need to a minimum essential humanitarian services. The two targeted states have experienced decades of conflict both as the frontline in the national level conflict between the government and opposition as well as the inter-communal conflict over resources and rivalry amongst ethnic groups. Protracted conflict, corrupted with endemic natural shocks, have triggered a humanitarian crisis that has severely damaged WASH, FSL and Nutrition infrastructure, services and practices.
According to HNO 2023 (Inter-sectoral analysis), the limited access to WASH infrastructure coverage alone, such as water points and latrines, poses multisectoral impacts. Poor access to WASH services and goods, combined with high levels of food insecurity, has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as evidenced by the high prevalence of malnutrition in the country. At the national level, the GAM prevalence for children between 0 and 59 months is at 16 per cent, while SAM was at 5.9 per cent according to the preliminary FSNMS and 2022 SMART surveys. The presence of water-borne diseases such as diarrhoea in children under age 5 is also indicative of poor WASH conditions. The WASH situation in certain locations is projected to worsen before the end of 6 months due to heavy flooding, which has caused further disruption to livelihoods, displacement and damage to WASH infrastructure while limiting humanitarian access. Limited sustainability of the WASH response in the IDP sites due to funding shortage will increase the likelihood of waterborne disease and outbreaks, including Hepatitis E, AWD and cholera.
The project will complement the newly secured 3 years German Federal Foreign Office (GFFO) funding for implementation of Integrated WASH, Food Security and Nutrition services to conflict and disaster-affected persons in the selected priority Counties in Lakes and Jonglei States. The strategy of integrating WASH in Nutrition is to tackle some of the key acute malnutrition drivers, as poor hygiene and access to clean water, limited food availability knowledge.
This project will mainly target SAM/caretakers at nutrition sites, nutrition facilities with poor WASH conditions and communities with high GAM and no access to safe water and all work will be done in line with the WaSH Cluster strategy in close coordination with line ministries, WASH Cluster and international guidelines for implementing WaSH. The project has 5 Main Strategic Objectives (SOs); CLWSH/SO1) Contribute to reducing prevalence of malnutrition; CLWSH/SO2) Contribute to reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality; CLWSH/SO3) Provide access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster; CLWSH/SO4) Disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facility; CLWSH/SO5) Provide durable access to services in long-term IDP sites and communities chronically affected by disaster. The following activities will be carried; (Re)Construction of flood resilient water points with hand pumps and motorized systems; Water quality analysis, Training of Community hand pump mechanics, Training of Water Management Committees, (Re)construction of flood resilient latrines, Hygiene promotion, Distribution of WASH NFIs distribution, solid and liquid waste management at Health facilities and communities, and incorporation of PSEA and GBV into Protection mainstreaming training.

1. Needs

Inadequate access to clean water, improved sanitation and acceptable hygiene practices within Lakes and Jonglei states contributes greatly to the high levels of malnutrition, disease burdens, food insecurity as well as WASH related protection/GBV issues for the most vulnerable (e.g. CU5, PLW, adolescent girls, people with disabilities and the elderly). Open defecation is widely practiced, leading to cycles of water contamination, resulting in acute watery diarrhoea, cholera and HEV diseases. Hand washing is equally low, with socio-cultural practices unfavourable to achieving acceptable WASH standards. Women are exposed to a series of protection risks due to distance and time taken at water points.The most food in secured state between September and November 2023 with 61.3 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) are Jonglei State (61.3 percent) with Lakes State (52.8 percent) taking 4th position. Furthermore, An estimated 72 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. The Contributing Factors for Acute Malnutrition; A high prevalence of diseases such as diarrhoea, malaria/fever and respiratory illnesses occur with 50 out of 80 counties reported illness greater than 50 percent among children, All Infant and Young child feeding (IYCF) indicators remain suboptimal at national level with only 5 children out of 100 are getting the recommended quality and frequency of food required for optimal growth, Poor access to improved drinking water and sanitation compromises hygiene practices and increases the risk of high incidences of illness among children and Stock-out of essential supplies for management of acute malnutrition and sub-optimal coverage of health and nutrition programs.An increased number of IDPs Mingkamman and other regions in Lakes State as they run from intercommunal conflicts and flooding that destroyed their homes. In Twic East and Ayod, the floods destroyed their homes and farmland, displaced thousands and paralyzed important services such as hospitals, local markets and schools. Thousands of people, including the population in these places and in Jonglei in total, were affected by the floods. Twic East and Ayod are inundated by floodwaters when communities from surrounding villages were displaced and sought shelter in neighboring centers and cities.Household food insecurity and poor hygiene habits are all underlying causes of malnutrition, making food security, water, sanitation and hygiene services critical to improving nutrition outcomes. People also travel great distances to access basic services, further exacerbating food and personal insecurity. Women and adolescent girls are disproportionately impacted by the lack of basic services and infrastructure, including women-friendly WASH facilities and MHM kits. Help-Hilfe Zur Selbsthilfe has been implementing WASH projects in greater Yirol and Awerial Counties since 2013, and in 2016 and 2022, the inception of Nutrition and FSL in Yirol East and Awerial Counties respectively and this is after constantly monitoring the vulnerability and needs trends on Nutrition & FSL data of the population. The 3 years funding secured in July 2023 from the Germany Federal and Foreign Office (GFFO) for Integrated WASH, Food Security and Nutrition services to conflict and disaster-affected persons in the selected priority Counties in Lakes and Jonglei States. In Jonglei the Project for WASH & FSL is being implemented together with a local partner Christian Mission for Development (CMD). This project will complement and target SAM/caretakers at nutrition sites, nutrition facilities with poor WASH conditions and communities with high GAM and no access to safe water. This project will be done in line with the WaSH Cluster strategy in close coordination with line ministries, WASH Cluster and international guidelines for implementing WaSH.

2. Project activities

The project activities will be based on 5 Main Strategic Objectives (SOs); CLWSH/SO1) Contribute to reducing prevalence of malnutrition through access to safe drinking water by drilling and rehabilitation of water points, construction of rain water harvesting tanks; provision of sanitation by construction and rehabilitation of latrines; CLWSH/SO2) Contribute to reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality through access to safe drinking water by drilling and rehabilitation of water points, construction of rain water harvesting tanks, water quality analysis, provision of water purification tablets, shock chlorination of water points, free Residual Chlorine (FRC) measurement; provision of latrines, hygiene awareness promotion, promotion of hand washing with soap and personal hygiene; CLWSH/SO3) Provide access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster through the (Re)construction of disasters resistant water points, distribution of water purification tablets, water trucking, water quality analysis, shock chlorination of water points, FRC measurements, distribution of Water containers, rehabilitation/construction of water points. Sanitation – distribution of latrine digging tools, construction of household latrines by the community. Installation of handwashing points. (Re)constructio of latrines in health facilities/schools, Hygiene promotion – hygiene awareness in health facilities/schools/community on use of soap and good hygiene practices, distribution of soap, hygiene kits and MHM; CLWSH/SO4) Disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facility by provision of water and sanitation facilities that are friendly users for persons with disability and minimum risks GBV to girls and women, trainings on GBV and PSEA in protection mainstreaming, distribution of sanitary cloth (reuseable), solid waste management (inclusive of sanitary cloth) , (Re)construction of bathing shelters; CLWSH/SO5) Provide durable access to services in long-term IDP sites and communities chronically affected by disaster through rehabilitation of water points, latrines, bathing shelters, hygiene promotion through a proactive approach to the beneficiaries, distribution of water carrying containers, distribution of soap, training water management committee on sustainability of water points, distribution of water treatment tablets, water quality analysis.
