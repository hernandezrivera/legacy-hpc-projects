Project Summary

- The October 17 (2019) protests and their aftermath, the August 4 (2020) Beirut Port explosion, the economic crisis, and the COVID-19 pandemic have made life in Lebanon difficult for most residents, Lebanese, refugees, and asylum seekers, but also migrants from Sudan, Ethiopia, Bangladesh, Philippines, Sri Lanka, and other Asian and African countries. They are facing hunger, homelessness, and severe living conditions, while at increased risk of becoming victims of violence and exploitation. Many migrants also face micro-aggressions, discrimination and racism daily, all while being vulnerable to abuses from security apparatuses. (Frontiers in .org,2020)
Some migrants who face additional vulnerabilities in Lebanon, such as lack of documentation and legal status in the country (e.g., lack of birth registration, marriage registration, and a national ID).
To give an example, if an Ethiopian migrant domestic worker gives birth to a child in Lebanon, the child will most likely not receive legal status or documentation. Lack of documentation prevents the child from accessing education (e.g., public, and private schools, daddy day care). And should the mother wish to return to her country of origin and bring her child with her, she will not be able to receive a passport or a laissez-passé without legal representation, which is not easy for migrants to obtain. Children of migrant mothers and Lebanese fathers are eligible to obtain citizenship but lack official documentation to claim this and need instead a DNA test to prove their identity.I n addition, children of migrants often live in insecure environments. Most migrants with children are live-out migrants, of which many do not have a legal status in the country. Areas with a large migrant population, such as the Beirut neighborhoods of Karantina, Sultan Ibrahim or Burj Hammoud, often have a lower standard than other areas, with homes lacking security, proper heating, or hygiene facilities. Many migrant families are food insecure, unable to provide their children with enough healthy food cover into cover their nutritional needs.
Justice Support to minor children in judicial proceedings and works specifically with those at highest risk, including such children in need of protection. UPEL is in charge of assisting children throughout the justice protection process, from opening of the protection file to court proceedings, follow-up, referral, and reintegration. As such, it is an important player in Lebanon's national child protection system. The support provided to UPEL through this partnership aims to continue to allow UPEL to fulfil its mandate by providing children in protection with the needed support. UPEL case workers and managers are using the National SOPs during the case management process. Through the case plan and the assessment of each case, UPEL team will refer the children and their families to the needed services after getting the approval from the judges. UPEL team will advocate for the children in need of protection in front of the judicial system taking into consideration the best interest of the child.
The project aims at helping these migrant communities by providing them with in-kind and cash assistance, and legal support, as needed. By buying these services addressing some of the vulnerabilities mentioned above, the program seeks to enable migrant families and children to have a better life and a new start.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Migrants are to be protected against what stands between them and between the good conditions of their development whether it is food, health, education, housing, playgrounds, or any other condition of a respected lifestyle, and right to exist. And in the same perspective, they should be protected against threats that jeopardize their physical or mental integrity. Our program will be addressing those challenges and problems through our specialized services using a holistic case management approach. The project primarily aims at enhancing the migrant resilience by trying to solve the problems they are passing through in times of crisis and emergency situations, To this objective needs assessment will be conducted within home visits, and awareness sessions on community problems will be delivered for a target number of 80 children. The screening process will tackle more than 100 children to choose the most vulnerable. Moreover, UPEL has been keen to strengthen the youth’s role in times of crisis and emergency situations. To this objective, UPEL will provide case management services, legal counseling, 5-month cash assistance, diapers, winterization, clothes, rental support, medication etc. That is not all, UPEL will work closely with these families to solve their legal problems, opening the protection file to court proceedings, follow-up, and referral. The project will include awareness raising for front liners within the CP sectors on how to work with migrant children.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Risk Mitigation: Following the country’s situation, we are expecting an increase in violent cases including sexual exploitation and abuse. There are many steps we can take to mitigate the increase in violence, below are some we aim to follow: 1. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: UPEL will ensure that all staff involved in the project are aware of the Secretary-General Bulletin Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The UNICEF Code of Conduct on PSEA has been translated and disseminated to all staff. A focal point on PSEA will be designated. 2. Safeguarding: UPEL uses the typical 5 R’s approach which is Recognize, Respond, Report, Record, and Refer. UPEL’s staff will be working on ensuring the children’s safety above all and will be reporting any concerns to the police in case of any suspicion. Our staff is highly knowledgeable and well-trained when it comes to these matters and they follow internationally recognized protocols.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

To ensure accountability I must take into measure these three aspects: Gender, equity, and sustainability:The proposed project includes the provision of gender-sensitive services to girls and boys at risk and those in conflict with the law. Through its technical capacity-building initiatives, it aims to strengthen actors’ knowledge of gender-based inequalities and rights violations present in the Lebanese context, as well as how to address them through proper case management and advocacy. Paying attention to gender issues is about acknowledging the different needs, capacities, and contributions of women, men, girls, and boys. Ignoring or being blind to these different needs can have serious implications for the protection and survival of people caught up in humanitarian crises. Women, men, girls, and boys play different roles within the family and community and have different levels of access to power and resources.
