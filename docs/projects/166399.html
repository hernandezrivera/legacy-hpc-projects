Project Summary

The project seeks to contribute to reducing morbidity and mortality from COVID-19 by building up on the currently implemented health programmes. Three facilities will be established (Ukhia, camp 5, Sadar hospital), equipped and operated for the treatment of up to 212 women, men boys and girls at a time. Quarantine centres will be established to serve up to 1,100 refugees for the quarantine of contacts of suspected/ confirmed patients. Existing 22 primary care facilities (camps 1E/W, 2E/W, 3, 4, 4ext, 5, 6, 8W, 11, 12, NYP RC, KTP RC), will be strengthened to identify suspected COVID-19 patients through triage, establishment of separate holding areas, referral and strengthened IPC measures. Risk communication will be strengthened through CHWs and multimedia. Health sector coordination will be supported through CHWG, MHPSS WG as well as field coordination (UNHCR AoR) and other sector coordination forums.

Needs

The 859,000 Rohingya Refugees currently residing in 34 camps in Cox’s Bazar are highly vulnerable to COVID-19 due to overcrowded living conditions, poor WASH infrastructure. Rohingya refugee camps are among the most densely populated place on earth with more than 42,000 people/ km2 and physical distancing measures are expected to have a limited impact. Poor food consumption scores and underlying malnutrition contribute to an weakened immune status of individuals. Elderly persons above the age of 60 and those with underlying health conditions are at higher risk of developing serious disease and will be affected disproportionally. Men and women ar similarly affected by the diesease while younger children appear to be less suspectible to developing the disease. There is an urgent need to rapidly detect, diagnose, and prevent spread of the virus to minimize human-to-human transmission of the virus and care with dignity for those who will be affected. In the likely event that the virus spreads to the camps, all efforts must be made to minimize morbidity and mortality. The current capacity of 168 isolation beds is insufficient to meet the expected demand to treat even all expected severe cases of COVID-19. Moreover, facilities are not equipped with the needed equipment including oxygen and personal protective equipment (PPE) to treat COVID-19 patients. No facilities exist in the camps nor in the host community to treat those in need of critical care. Homebased care concepts need to be developed to provide care and support to those who can’t be accommodated in health facilities at the peak of the outbreak. Special considerations will need to be given to the protection needs of women and girls as they will take traditionally the role of homecare for the sick. While upscaling facility and homebased care, significant investments will need to be made in recruiting and training additional health care providers and community volunteers. Awareness campaigns on COVID-19 and prevention messages are being conducted but widespread rumors and misperceptions prevail and will need to be addressed to ensure that those infected are detected early and receive appropriate care. Recent analysis done by different partners indicate a need for more targeted information to women and girls as well as the elderly. Current delays in health seeking due to misconceptions about the care received at health facilities need to be addressed to enable early care seeking. Physical distancing and IPC measures will need to be enhanced in all facilities.

Activities or outputs

Objective 1:- Remodel existing facilities to establish and operate three quarantine facilities for up to 1,100 people. Facilities are designed as family quarantine with appropriate segregation to ensure privacy and include with gender-segregated WASH facilities. Facilities will be linked with other sectors for the provision of non-health services, incl. protection.- Construct, equip and operate two SARI ITCs with 50 and 144 beds respectively for refugees and host communities, seperate wards will be established for men and women. - Establish and equip an Intensive Care Unit (10 ICU beds, 8 HDU beds) in District Sadar hospital, provision of HR for min. 6 months- Train health care workers on COVID case definitions, treatment protocols etc- Train CHWs and health care workers and establish homebased care for mild and moderate cases - Procurement of PPE and medical supplies for SARI ITCs and DGHS Objective 2:- Establish triage and holding areas for suspected cases at all currently supported health facilities - Establish hand washing stations and train health staff on IPC measures, strengthen waste management protocols - Procurement of PPE and medical supplies for 22 PHCs and HPs- Regularly train CHW supervisors and 300 CHWs and other sector volunteers on approved COVID-19 - Conduct small group or individual awareness sessions with men and women and community leaders to maintain two-way communication. Special emphasis will be given to the needs of vulnerable groups such as the elderly. - Recruit additional CHWs to cover emerging gaps Objective 3:- Chair Community Health WG in support of the health sector in cooperation with PHD- Provide regularly training to all CHW supervisors on COVID messaging and new concepts - 5 dedicated Camp Health Focal point will support camp-level coordination and monitoring Objective 4:UNHCR will continue to operate health facilities as outline in the JRP submission. Special emphasis will be on maintaining preventive health services (SRH, EPI) to avoid excess morbidity and mortality through other causes. Related budgets are not included under this submission.
