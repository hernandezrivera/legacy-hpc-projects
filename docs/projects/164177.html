Project Summary

The proposed project aims to address emergency and more sustainable shelter and NFI needs of vulnerable households with acute needs (both IDPs and host community members) in Northeast and Northwest Syria. Specifically, ACTED will rehabilitate, through contractors, 850 households; 6000 in Ar-Raqqa and 250 in Idleb governorates. 1,150 households in Ar-Raqqa governorate will also be targeted with Cash for repairs interventions to conduct small rehabilitation works under ACTED supervision and support. In addition, ACTED will address the needs of newly displaced households through the provision of tents, emergency shelter kits and tents insulation kits to guarantee a shelter and dignified living conditions to these newly displaced individuals. Under this intervention, 1,175 households will be provided with tents across Al-Hasake, Ar-Raqqa, and Idleb governorates; while 5,600 emergency shelter kits will be distributed in Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates. Idleb governorate will also be targeted with the distribution of 38,329 insulation kits across the eight camp clusters, to better equip the residents for winter. In addition, 2,650 NFI kits, composed of tarpaulin, jerry can, mattresses/sleeping mats, blankets (single and double), solar lamps, floor mat, stove, cooking pot, bowl for food, and cutlery, will be distributed across Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates. 300 households will also be provided with cooking kerose stoves in Areesheh camp, Al-Hasake governorate. The intervention will target a total of 62,160 individuals, including 23,000 in Northeast and 39,160 in Northwest Syria; 13,763 men, 11,724 women, 19,804 boys, and 16,869 girls.

4 - Needs

With ongoing displacements in Northwest Syria, NFI and Shelter assistance continues to be essential and life-saving. According to the last estimation (December 2019) of the Shelter/NFI sector; just over 3 million people are in need of assistance in Northwest and Northeast Syria. The situation is particularly critical for individuals residing in tents and makeshift shelters, as the Situational Overview of Shelter Conditions in Northwest Syria report highlighted. The report showed severe shelter inadequacy issues for 83% of the households residing in informal and formal camps across Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates, where shelters rehabilitation and insulation works are required to guarantee safe and dignified living conditions; especially for women, more exposed to protection risks. Winterization support, particularly fuel vouchers for heating, also emerged as a pressing needs for individuals in Northwest Syria, facing increasing barriers in accessing fuel on the market, whose price constantly increased since Novermber 2019 due the depreciation of the Syrian pound. Similar needs arose also in Northeast Syria where, according to OCHA, 70,590 individuals remain displaced in Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa governorates. Individuals who, in the majority, found shelter in camps and informal settlements posing a great challenge on the already stretched camps and settlements’ capabilities and services, which are currently struggling to cover the shelter needs of the new arrivals. As the perspective of these individuals to return home is limited, interventions are required to guarantee the coverage of basic shelter needs, namely provision of tents, to all the new arrivals in camps along with the provision of the necessary means, particularly fuel and insulation material, to enable them to better face harhs winter conditions. Lastly, basic shelter repairs works will be also required to ensure safe and dignified living conditions.

5 - Implementation of activities

ACTED will implement a multi-faced intervention through the following activities:Result 1: Shelter and settlement assistance is provided1)Distribution of emergency shelter kits: -5,600 ESKs in Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates 2)Winterization support, through NFI and tent insulation kits distribution: -2,650 NFI kits (containing tarpaulin, jerry can, mattresses/sleeping mats, blankets (single and double), solar lamps, floor mat, stove, cooking pot, bowl for food, and cutlery) in Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates-38,329 insulation kits across the 8 camp clusters in Idleb governorate-300 HHs will be provided with kerosene cooking stoves in Areesheh camp, Al-Hasake governorate-2,760 HHs will be provided with fuel vouchers and heaters in Areesheh camp, Al-Hasake governorate3) Rehabilitation of private shelters:- 250 HHs across Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates through contractors-600 private HHs through contractors, and 1,150 through cash-for repairs in Ar-Raqqa governorateThrough Cash for repairs activity, ACTED will provide beneficiaries with grants to be used for small works. The grants will be disbursed in two instalments, upon beneficiaries’s adherence to the workplan established at the commencement of the activity.For all the shelter rehabilitation, ACTED will mitigate the risk of beneficiaries’ eviction from the HH upon the completion of the rehabilitation activities by putting a strong HLP due diligence process in place.4) Tents distribution to the new arrivals:-1,175 HHs across Al-Hasake, Deir-ez-Zor, Ar-Raqqa, and Idleb, governorates.Gender vulnerabilities will be taken into account across all the interventions. Particularly, for shelter rehabilitation activities, ACTED will take into account needs in terms of accessibility, privacy and safety of most vulnerable individuals. All items and technical support will aim at reducing protection concerns (lighting, lock, door, partition, ramps and handrails, etc.)

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementation Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach. ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneﬁciary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects.Risk 2: Eviction of beneficiaries from the household upon the completion of the rehabilitation works. Likelihood: Medium – Impact: High. Mitigation Measure: A strong HLP due diligence process and MoUs with the rehabilitated buildings' owners will mitigate against expulsion risks. This will reduce the risk of eviction or conflict erupting from a lack of clarity and ensure that the protection standards on HLP are respected. During the selection process, ACTED will ensure the following: if the beneficiary household is the owner, ownership is verified by community verification and documentation if available; if beneficiary household is the tenant/hosted, the beneficiary household is the rightful tenant with rental contract or written hosting agreement; the ownership claim of the landlord is verified by community verification and documentation if available; and the landlord provides written consent for repairs and tenancy duration of at least 1 year from completion of repairs. Where possible, ACTED will intend to extend the duration further to ensure sustainability of the assistance, by piloting tailored design of MoU with landowner in order to reinforce sustainability of the agreement.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

.SNFI related risks: -SNFI kits procured for the project could be stolen in transit to or from warehouses: ground level storage spaces below residential properties are used as and when possible, warehouses are protected by a security guard 24 hours a day and keys to warehouses are solely in the possession of the Storekeeper who is responsible for all stock movements. Warehouse locations are carefully considered and when possible located outside of the town perimeter and near checkpoints for additional security.-Diversion of assistance: ACTED has robust and established SOP for its activities to track the assistance to ensure it reaches the targeted beneficiaries. In the planning of distributions, ACTED and partners will consult actively with key community leaders and local authorities to define an appropriate time and location for the distributions. ACTED and partners’ security teams will conduct thorough security assessments in each target area to identify secure distribution sites. Distribution schedules will be set up to ensure that there is only a small number of beneficiaries per day and the location of distribution site is close to the area where beneficiaries are. AMEU also conduct 100% verification and extensive Post-Distribution Monitoring to track the assistance provided. The PDM also records beneficiary satisfaction and provides beneficiaries with the opportunity to report any instances of aid diversion or theft.Gender-based violence related risk:-Sexual Abuse and Exploitation or Gender-Based Violence: ACTED will ensure that all its national and international staff will receive an induction training session on PSEA. All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to SEA/GBV. Lastly, dedicated accountability staff members in the AMEU are in charge of undertaking all beneficiaries’ complaints, to establish their degree of sensitivity, their truthfulness, relation to implemented activities and to staff from ACTED and contracto
