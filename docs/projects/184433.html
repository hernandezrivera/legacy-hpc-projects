Project Summary

Acute malnutrition is a major public health problem in Somalia and disproportionately affects children under the age of 5, as well as Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). Malnutrition is the result of years of conflict and violence, disease outbreaks, drought, locust infestation, recurrent flooding and population displacements. In addition, other factors such as high morbidity, low immunization, low vitamin-A supplementation, reduced access to milk, food insecurity, low access to health services, poor access to water and sanitation, poor maternal nutrition, and high disease burden contribute to malnutrition rates. Poor feeding practices for infants and young children are also strongly linked with undernutrition.
According to the latest findings of 2021 post Gu results of FSNAU, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) at the national level has remained at a serious level of 11.1 per cent weight-for-height z-score. Based on this analysis and the latest population figures, the cluster estimates that in 2022 around 1.2 million children, under the age of 5 year will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will therefore ned emergency nutrition services urgently. Out of this, 295,515 children under the age of 5 are likely to be severely malnourished and around 986,000 children will be moderately acute malnourished (MAM)
To respond and address the critical nutrition needs of the extremely 34,650 vulnerable children and women in three target districts in Bandir, lower and middle shabelle regions, SOS CV Somalia proposes to provide a sustained holistic urgent support to recovery and prevention of deteriorations in the nutrition situations in particular for children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women who will likely face acute malnutrition in Heliwa, Daynile, afgoi corridor, and Kahda, Balcad and Jowhar districts through existing SOS Managed health/nutrition facilities in the proposed districts.
The propose emergency nutrition interventions will provide integrated lifesaving treatment for acute malnutrition in the targeted districts through facility and community- based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) targeting children under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The proposed project aims to increase coverage and access to the management of both moderate and severe acute malnutrition (MAM/SAM), vitamin A supplementation and deworming as well as promotion of optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices. SOS will adopt an expansive IYCF in Emergencies (IYCF-E) strategy aimed at increasing reach beyond mothers raising infants but also fathers, grandfathers, community leaders, religious leaders, teachers, etc.
All the proposed nutrition interventions will improve early identification and prevention of malnutrition and therefore reduce excess morbidity and mortality in the target districts.

1. - Projects activities

The proposed project aims at providing integrated Nutrition treatment and prevention services for displaced and non-displaced under five children and pregnant and Lactating Women for a period of 12 months. It will contribute to reduction of nutrition related morbidity and mortality rates to below emergency threshold through scale up of therapeutic feeding and supplementary programs. The project will support treatment of severe acute malnutrition cases through admission to Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTP), Supplementary Feeding Programs (TSFP) and establishing referral linkages to stabilization centers supported by other Implementing partners in target locations. For moderately and severely malnourished under five children identified through community screening by community health and nutrition workers, they will be referred to respective interventions. Besides Nutrition treatment services, the project will offer Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) promotion through group and individual counselling sessions, mother to mother support groups to sensitize pregnant and lactating mothers on IYCF. The activities will be implemented through 6 fixed facilities and, 3 mobile teams 1 stabilization center. A network of trained community volunteers will help with provision of services at community level

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

- Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.- Identify central locations - beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points.- Avoid placing distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points.Consider the frequency of distributions – the greater the time between distributions the larger the quantities distributed, which puts beneficiaries at risk of attacks and pillage.Consider separate waiting areas for males and females. -Have a proportionate number of female health staff. If female doctors/nurses are not available, consider a female doctor rotation between locations. In this case, women must be adequately informed of which days a female doctor will be available.
