Project Summary

The project will provide emergency agriculture and livestock-based livelihoods interventions and resilience building support for vulnerable farming and pastoral IDPs, returnees, refugees and hosting communities in South, North, West, East, and Central Darfur states. Vulnerability of the target populations stems from the protracted crisis, the ongoing economic hardships at the macro and micro-levels and recent poor harvest because of localized droughts and pest infestations. This situation has led to high rates of acute and chronic food insecurity and malnutrition. FAO prioritized in a participatory manner 28 localities in the five states of Darfur based on the high degree of vulnerability to food insecurity and malnutrition.
This project will target 741,300 people among the most vulnerable communities including 185,325 returnees, 222,390 IDPs, 259,455 residents and 74,130 South Sudanese refugees. The focus will be on benefiting 226,850 women, 151,200 girls (less than 18 year of age), 217, 950 men and 145,300 boys(less than 18 year of age) from the proposed interventions based on their basic and specific needs taking into account the factors of age and gender. The project will target about 59,305 elderly people among the adult population including persons with disabilities.
The project will follow the FSL Sector Strategy that follows the Multi-year Humanitarian Strategy adopted by the Humanitarian Country Team in Sudan for the period 2017-2020. The FSL Sector Strategy entails providing livelihoods-saving interventions and addressing the root causes of food insecurity and malnutrition to tackle all aspects of food security, including physical and economic access, availability and utilization of food and the stability of food systems through complimentary emergency life-saving and resilience building through agricultural and livestock-based livelihood interventions and income generating activities.
The project objectives are aligned with the FSL Sector objectives 1, which includes addressing immediate lifesaving humanitarian needs through emergency agricultural assistance including crop, livestock and veterinary inputs and extension services targeting 300,000 people. This will include as well building the capacity of governmental institutions and sector partners to improve preparedness and response for new emergencies. Under FSL Sector objective 2, the sector aims to restore and improve food and nutrition security and enhance self-reliance of the most vulnerable communities to cope with current and future shocks and stresses. Agricultural production will be improved through provision of climate-smart crop production, livestock and veterinary inputs and support to services. Similarly, small scale farmers will be targeted for reduced food losses and strengthened post-harvest techniques through improved food and crop storage and processing facilities. On and off farm livelihood assistance will be diversified and managed and coordinated to respond to food chain threats including animal and plant pests and diseases; In addition, communities will be involved in building, restoring and maintaining productive assets through cash and/or food for assets schemes. About 387,000 people will be targeted under Sector objective 2. Under Sector objective 3, the sector will strengthen nutrition, water and energy sensitive agricultural, livestock and natural resources management- based livelihoods inputs, kits, and capacity building, and increased resilience of 54,300 vulnerable people.
Protection of people, specifically women and girls, against GBV; accountability to affected populations and environmental concerns will be mainstreamed into the proposed activities to reduce risk to GBV, respond to the needs of the target population, promote climate change adaptation measures and ensure implementation of environment-friendly interventions.

Needs

According to the latest IPC results, 5.67 million people in Sudan are classified as food insecure in 2019. The FSL sector partners plan to target 4.39 million of the most vulnerable people that fall under IPC Phase 3 and above. As macro and micro-economic challenges continue to erode household purchasing power, the food insecurity of IDPs, host communities, refugees and returnees is expected to worsen. Based on FEWS NET’s 12/2019 report, people’s access to food during March-May 2019 is expected to be less than in normal years and the most vulnerable households will deplete household food stocks and become even more dependent on markets. Prioritized localities and communities was informed by both the IPC latest result and the Post-harvest Assessments which indicated high food and nutrition insecurity due to food deficits and accessibility issues. With below-average purchasing power, poor households will face difficulty in purchasing adequate levels of food and non-food supplies, particularly, farming and pastoral supplies for the next season. The prioritized response was identified by FAO Rapid Needs Assessments conducted in West, Central, South and North Darfur states in January 2019. Lack of certified, improved seeds, extension activities, and veterinary and livestock services are among the most serious challenges responsible for low crop and livestock production in the traditional system. Identified needs included certified crop seeds, improved vegetable and legume seeds, hand tools, ploughs, veterinary drugs and vaccines, animal feed, mineral licks, IGAs and live animals. Water for livestock use and farming, fuel efficient stoves and training activities on improved crop and livestock production were also on high demand. Agricultural productivity cannot be sustained without proper natural resources management. The interviewed families mentioned the need for building community assets such as grain stores, hafirs, shallow wells and climate smart agricultural structures for soil and water management.Rural women represent more than 50% of the farming population in the rain fed sector in Darfur. They participate in many agricultural activities. Women are also responsible for rearing small animals such as goats and poultry and active in processing and marketing of livestock products. Men are in control of large animals and cereal cropping. This project will respond to the basic and specific needs of women, girls, men and boys taking into account the factors of age and gender. The project will support environment-friendly interventions including climate change adaptation measures. GBV risk reduction measures will be mainstreamed. The project will introduce veterinary voucher system to improve access to veterinary services by vulnerable HHs so as to safeguard private service delivery. Cash intervention for community asset creation before the main crop season will be considered after intensive market assessments.

Activities

1. Provision of life-saving emergency agriculture and livestock inputs and services including combined cash interventions; procurement of seeds, tools, vaccines, drugs, animal feed, and mineral licks. 2. Building the capacity of governmental institutions and sector partners to improve preparedness and response for new emergencies;3. Provision of inputs and capacity building for climate-smart crop production, livestock and veterinary services including community seed production and cost recovery veterinary services;4. Provision of income generating support and vocational training;5. Support building, restoring and maintaining productive assets through cash and/or food for assets schemes;6. Provision of nutrition, energy and water sensitive agricultural and livestock and natural resources interventions including input and kit distribution and capacity building7. Reduction of food losses through post-harvest techniques, plant pest and animal disease control;8. Implementation of natural resources management interventions including climate change adaptation measures;The proposed interventions will be implemented by FAO in collaboration with implementing partners that are operating currently in Darfur taking into account age and gender needs. State line ministries will be involved in implementation as well. Roles and responsibilities of each party will be clearly stated under the Letters of Agreement with IPs. Women, girls, men and boys are to be consulted about their specific needs, concerns, protection risks and solution to key issues. Mechanisms for exchange of information with the targeted population will be established and functioning. Procurement, testing and pre-positioning of inputs will be made by FAO according to its regulations. IPs will be responsible for distribution of inputs, training, and monitoring of activities and reporting. FAO will ensure that the project activities are technically sound and monitored both at output and outcome levels.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Like many other countries, Sudan in general and Darfur in particular, is experiencing more frequent droughts, floods and storms causing damage to the environment and people's livelihoods. Poorly manged livestock is much more implicated in GHG emissions and its intensity varies between regions causing climate change phenomena. The project will contribute effectively to the efforts that can build resilience and prepare for and adapt to climate change through some mitigation measures that are explained below:  Increasing livestock productivity through supplementary feeding interventions will help in reducing the number of nonproductive animals which bear full responsibility in increasing emissions; Reducing the prevalence of diseases and parasites through the animal health component would eventually reduce emissions intensity as healthier animals are more productive and thus produce lower emissions per unit of output; Adoption of climate smart agriculture techniques by targeted trained farmers will result in incorporation of manure into soils which can reduce emissions while maintaining farm productivity; Pasture rehabilitation and tree plantation activities will store more carbon into the soils; Training the target groups on sound disposal of carcasses would reduce environmental pollution due to flooding
