Project Summary

The project will be operating in IRC’s 5 existing WGSS’s known as Integrated Women’s Center (IWC) and 2 BEmONC centers to ensure women and girls are protected from and treated for the consequences of GBV and have access to comprehensive, quality GBV response and prevention services in a safe, dignified and confidential manner. All activities are implemented in close consultation with women and girls, supported by strong community engagement through community volunteers.
IRC will provide GBV case-management including PSS and referral service as lifesaving support to GBV survivors. Besides that, IRC will train service providers to strengthen adherence to regularly updated, confidential and timely interagency referral systems. IRC will also continue to provide integrated SRH services in the IWCs including FP and voluntary contraception; safe MR including post abortion care; and management of STIs and CMR. In addition, IRC will have a resident legal officer who will provide legal counselling to GBV survivors.
IRC will deliver prevention and empowerment activities to reduce acceptance and prevalence of GBV through awareness raising; listening sessions; community-led activism; and dialogue sessions with community leaders using the SASA! model to address GBV-driving norms. EMAP program which works constructively with men to examine the gendered impact of conflict and power dynamics in household will be introduced. Women and girls will regularly benefit from life skills and empowerment activities. Girl Shine curriculum will be used to provide adolescent girls with skills and knowledge. EA$E (Economic and Social Empowerment) will be implemented to challenge gender norms about financial decision making.
IRC will conduct safety audits which will document protection risks informing advocacy with community leaders and other stakeholders. IRC will set up GBVIMS to harmonize data collection, data storage and analysis, safe and ethical sharing of reported GBV incident data.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya women and girls face many challenges in the community as they navigate within the deeply patriarchal society where they have defined gender roles. The Covid 19 pandemic has brought new challenges as has brought direct and indirect social, economic and psychosocial impacts on women and girls and others at risk and left behind communities. According to a Rapid Gender Analysis conducted by ISCG Gender Hub, Care, Oxfam, ACAPS-NPM and UN Women in October 2020, with lack of security, a sense of impunity among perpetrators, inaccessibility to or lack of justice for survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), especially in the camps, has gives rise to a vicious cycle of harassment, abuse and exploitation. IRC conducted a Safety Audit in July 2020 through KIIs and qualitative data collection. The report conducted interviews with 40 refugee/displaced and host community women living in 16 Rohingya and host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilla of Cox’s Bazar. The interviewees mentioned that women’s leadership is crucial to raise the voice of women and girls and ensure their active engagement as decision making in the household is controlled by men. The report shows that GBV increased during COVID 19. However, cases were not reported during the period due to movement restrictions and being at home with abusers. Despite all the progress made on increasing awareness of women and girls, the Safety Audit showed that women and girls did not know about available services during Covid 19. Therefore, there is a need to do more community outreach with information on available services for GBV and ways to mitigate GBV. The Safety Audit also shows that women and girls are sexually assaulted while accessing basic needs (food distribution points, water points and toilets) and those are often under reported. IRC’s GBVIMS data and the Safety Audit shows that the percentage of reported intimate partner violence (IPV) is always high in the Rohingya context which is around 90% but it has increased more during Covid 19. The IRC GBVIMS data trend analysis shows that the percentage of physical assault is high due to IPV. Therefore, there is a need of health including CMR, legal and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services. Interviewees of the Safety Audit reported that the percentage of marital rape has continued to increase during Covid 19. The high rate of GBV has consequences on the health and emotional wellbeing of women and girls but there are additional consequences of marital rape such as unwanted pregnancy, abortion, and denial of resources. Despite low reporting on forced and early child marriage in GBVIMS data, it remains a problem in the Rohingya camps as seen in the Safety Audit. The GBVIMS data trend analysis shows that the percentage unemployed perpetrators of GBV is the highest. The interviewees of the Safety Audit shared that the frustration of lack of employment is one of the driving forces of GBV. Therefore, there is an urgent need to engage men in economic activities and in empowering women and girls as mentioned by the Safety Audit interviewees. Moreover, there should be structured behavioral change programs for men and boys to reduce GBV.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Provide quality case management services including PSS and referral services for survivors of GBV through a survivor centered approach ensuring do no harm. Provide need base dignity kits to survivors. Ensure facilities and services are accessible to survivors with multiple intersectional identities to ensure no one is left behind. Ensure complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place. Capacitate GBV and non GBV partners on confidential referral pathways to ensure proper and confidential documentation of GBV referrals and enhance appropriate referrals by non GBV partners. Ensure coordinated efforts to improve case management services by delivering integrated capacity development of IRC staff, local partners and other relevant stakeholders on PSEA, case management, gender and inclusion mainstreaming. Activity 2: Ensure GBVIMS data collection, storage, analyses and share the data and report while maintaining information sharing protocol (ISP) to understand the overall GBV situation and adapt the project accordingly to support the most vulnerable members of the community. Activity 3: Conduct service mapping of actors. Conduct safety audits to identify, document and develop strategies and advocacy initiatives to address identified issues around women’s protection and empowerment. Activity 4: Provide adolescent girls with skills and knowledge to identify types of GBV and seek services if they experience or are at risk of GBV through Covid 19 adapted Girl Shine curriculum. Activity 5: Empower women by enabling them to challenge existing gender norms by focusing on their economic and social empowerment through Covid 19 adapted EA$E program. Activity 6: Conduct awareness raising; listening sessions; community-led activism; and dialogue sessions with community leaders and activists using the an adapted version of Covid 19 SASA! model to address GBV-driving norms. Activity 7: Conduct door-to-door and community awareness raising for women, girls, men and boys with intersectional identities on life saving messages including GBV risk mitigation, available GBV services, COVID 19, PSEA and cyclone, landslide and flood preparedness using specifically designed messages for the most vulnerable (people with disability, elderly, pregnant). Capacitate the protection team on emergency response to respond immediately after any disaster following a well-developed contingency plan. Activity 8: Conduct awareness raising with men using an adapted Covid 19 version of EMAP model to examine the gendered impact of conflict and power dynamics in household.
