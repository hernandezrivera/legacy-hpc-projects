Project Summary

PIN will target the most vulnerable persons/HH through its programming notably IDPs, the most vulnerable among hosting populations, people in-hard-to-reach areas, people with chronic illness, people with disabilities and people facing extreme socio-economic hardship- all possibly facing further and various level of protection risks and vulnerabilities. A specific attention will be pay to IDPs as being 6.1 million in-country, while some result from protracted displacement and presently living in last resort and makeshift settlements. Therefore, PIN will monitor the conflict and population movement to be able to assist newly displaced persons in the timeliest manner with appropriate response both across North East and North West Syria.
The main goal of the project is to reduce morbidity and mortality and improve living conditions and dignity of affected populations in Northern Syria, through coordinated, timely emergency and support to WASH services to meet the most acute humanitarian needs.
PIN strategy will be focusing on two main pillars:
- The emergency response for the IDPs in the camps and the new arrivals and provide them with the basic WASH needs from drinking water, hygiene needs and appropriate sanitation systems.
- Building resilience in the communities to support the returnees and the settled IDPs with the WASH services that ensure the safety and sustainability of these services and prevent any health outbreaks related with the water, sewage, and waste management systems.
Likewise, this emergency response is complemented by other sectors notably covering Food Security, Livelihoods and Education in host communities receiving IDPs, in order to maximize “do no harm’”considerations and ensure that vulnerable host populations also receive appropriate assistance. In addition, the complementary of implemented sectors permits, when possible to direct beneficiaries from emergency to regular food assistance and early recovery.
Constant M&E monitoring act as activities backbone through beneficiary selection and verification, need assessment, market monitoring, post-implementation (such as distribution) monitoring, routine monitoring, Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM).
It should be noted that activities are designed to be in line with the relevant coordination system such as Sphere standards and meet WASH cluster recommendations (length of assistance, contents of kits etc.).
Lastly, all activities will be carried out in coordination with local councils (LC), ensuring to share information on needs and response gaps at the village level as well as involved in beneficiary selection process; local associations, and IDP representatives at village level will be considered, if any.

Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018, there are 6.1 million IDPs living inside Syria. HNO data highlights that 13.1 million people across Syria are in urgent need of assistance, including 5.6 million with acute needs. HRP 2018 highlights that of the above vulnerable populations, 14.6 million people are in need notably accessing safe and sufficient WASH services (water, sewage, solid waste management) due to war damaged infrastructure/equipment, neglect because of lost funding from the Syrian Government, or significant increase of population as a result of hosting IDPs. The dire humanitarian needs are expected to remain high over the course of 2019 as part of the unpredictable ongoing conflict. Specific sectors, regardless of the current level of hostilities, will be further damaged by the ongoing conflict such as infrastructures, availability of services, livelihood opportunities. As part of these water destruction, in some areas, HH are forced to resort to costly water resources, hygiene supply and sanitation facilities putting a further strain on HH spending. Across the affected areas six population groups were identified as particularly vulnerable: people living in UN-declared besieged and hard-to-reach areas; people exposed to high intensity of hostilities and areas contaminated with explosive hazards; IDPs in last resort camps/sites/collective centers; newly-displaced people; spontaneous returnees; and over-burdened communities (where over 30% of the population comprises IDPs and/or spontaneous returnees). IDPs fleeing sudden-onset crises, who often flee at short notice and leave behind essential assets, are very vulnerable. Long-term IDPs, who often struggle to access income and services in their host, are also extremely vulnerable (HRP, Dec 2017). Therefore, some groups and some areas remain particularly critical to reach regarding the severity of needs they face such as IDPs in makeshift camps or populations living in hard-to-reach areas. A number of at-risk groups with specific needs were highlighted during needs assessments, including Female-headed HH (FHH), children, disabled and elderly groups. FHH were consistently found to be most vulnerable, with baseline data highlighting that 82% FHH in NWS had no income, compared to 44% of male headed households (44%). In November 2018, PIN conducted KII in Northern Syria with 79 local council and municipal administration representatives. All assessed NES locations in Ar-Raqqa and Aleppo asked for assistance in water supply system rehabilitation, which included interventions such as rehabilitation or extension of water networks and water stations, provision of chlorinators and pumps, and construction of water towers. Dire need is observed in solid waste sector; 53% reporting the need in landfill rehabilitation. In NWS in Aleppo and Idlib, 83 % of the interviewed local councils expressed perceived needs for rehabilitation and the extension of some parts of the water networks at village level. 64% said that the water stations in their communities require rehabilitation works, including the rehabilitation of the water wells, installation of submersible pumps and generator and construction of elevated water tanks. 97% of local councils interviewed confirmed the need for the rehabilitation and extension of the sewage networks in their villages as part as contributing to prevent water borne diseases. Needs for WASH facilitates in schools was found in 80% and 48% of KII in NES and NWS respectively. PIN’S programming include the construction or rehabilitation of the latrines inside the schools, connection of the sewage system in schools with the main sewer line, provision of water tanks and/or connection of the schools to the main water network. In addition PIN is working closely with the WASH cluster and other partners to observe the movement of IDPs and new arrivals to ensure their basic WASH needs are fulfilled in camps, settlements and reception centres.

Implementation of activities

The response is grounded in needs assessment data, contextual understanding and scenario planning. Target locations have been identified based on needs assessment data, and population movement, highlighting the most critical needs, vulnerabilities and gaps. Objective: deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and service for most vulnerable people to meet their WASH needs.Output : Vulnerable conflict affected households have increased access to basic, safe and dignified WASH emergency solutions.Activities• Water supply.Description: PIN plans to provide water supply for a maximum of 3 months in response to sudden onset shocks. This will be achieved through water trucking and provision of water points (water storage tanks with taps) in camps and informal IDPs settlements. At each water point, a chemical analysis will be conducted to ensure that the water points meet the national water quality and SPHERE standards.• WASH promotion and WASH NFI kits.Description: emergency Hygiene Kits are utilised in response to sudden onset emergencies in support of newly displaced HH complementing other emergency response implemented by PIN. Hygiene kits are designed to provide an initial kit containing both consumable and durable hygiene items for essential personal and household hygiene. Kits content has been adapted by PIN to be appropriate for a family of six with basic hygiene and dignity items, the composition might be altered based on feedback from PDMs on current hygiene kit distribution. To complement the kits distribution, PIN will also undertake hygiene awareness activities and specific messages tailored to the context such as identification, prevention and treatment of diarrhea; proper hand washing procedure; potable water safety; human/solid/household waste disposal; Leishmaniosis identification and prevention; and oral hygiene.• WASH sanitation. Description: PIN will support the rehabilitation of the sanitation systems and rehabilitation/construction the sanitation infrastructure in the camps and IDPs settlements in response to shocks and sudden population influxes through external contractor, with PIN maintaining a supervisory role.Design of bathing facilities and latrines will take into account cluster guidelines in addition to the needs of all users, including women and girls, particularly seeking to minimise protection risks, such as SGBV. Measures include ensuring the locations of bathing facilities are recognised by women and girls as safe places where they are not at risk of sexual violence, facilities are well-lit and can be locked from the inside, separated entrances for females/males, placement of shelves and sinks near latrines to encourage their use for hand washing (especially during menstruation). Objective: support to water, sanitation and sewage systems to ensure continuous service for conflict-affected people in Syria.Output : water infrastructures are rehabilitated in target areas for conflict-affected communities’ enhanced access to vital community WASH services.Activities• WASH infrastructure rehabilitation.Description: this activity is aim to increase access to critical WASH infrastructures in communities affected by the chronic impact of the conflict, such as rehabilitate of latrines in Schools. Pre-selection criteria will include considerations related to: quality of evidence presented on severity and scale of needs met with the projects, most severe conflict-related damage, coordination with other cluster partners, sustainability. • Training.Description: administrations in target areas will be provided with trainings and follow-up training over the course of the project covering topics including sustainable operation and maintenance of WASH infrastructure, environmental health with a significant emphasize on sustainability of the project. The detailed training plan will be tailored to the specific need of a selected service department or school administration.

Protection Risk Analysis

PIN programming in Northern Syria are inherently driven by protection considerations. Within implementation and regarding the specific activities related to the WASH sector, the following measures are taken to reduce protection risks: • PDM, awareness and transparency, and inclusion in the design of targeting criteria will contribute to decrease tensions between host communities/IDPs- beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries • Door-to-door distribution, gender separated queue lines and gender awareness will be opted for for beneficiaries attending distribution subjected to harassment and unsafe environment • The facilities developed and/or rehabilitated throughout the project, especially in IDP settlements, will taken into account security, vulnerability, gender and cultural-sensitive issues.• PIN holds community stakeholder meetings to provide an open forum and opportunity for the community and individual households to raise concerns, ask questions, and ensure greater decision making in programming. These meetings include representatives from all groups in order to understand the different feedback for each group. • Water and Sanitation facilities will be equipped with lightning devices (notably solar-based solutions), and will be separated for male and female users. • The design of emergency latrines should consider the diversity of population (age, gender, disability). As much as required, accessibility features will be installed in proportion with the population with specific needs related to mobility and/or sensorial impairment – ramps, railing, commode). • Hygiene Promotion messages will be delivered through various medias to ensure that they reach all – posters, oral messages, written and illustrated etc.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and mitigating measures: 1. Inaccessibility to target areas due to insecurity, large scale military offensives, or change in territorialcontrol.PIN would suspend, relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; shift program resources to otherareas; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. 2. Safety and security of staff and beneficiaries is compromised.PIN uses rigorous security monitoring mechanism carried out by PIN staff and triangulated with information available through other sources,including the International NGO Safety Organisation (INSO). 3. Borders are temporarily closed. PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA and local authorities to facilitate access and operations; purchase locally if possible; eventually resort to remote Cash-based programming in extreme cases. 4. Legal/administrative constraints – including refusal of required operational permissions and visas – delay or prevent aid delivery in countries from where cross-border operations are conducted adopt a phased approaches to withdrawal 5. Inflation of the Syrian pound / Exchange rate fluctuations / Local markets are not able to meet the increased demand for goods use available in-kind commodities through food basket/bread/RTER provisions; switch allocations to increase in-kind assistance provision, reshape programming by revising targets in coordination with ECHO, switch to food basket distributions with items procured in neighbouring countries. 6. Restricted CTA liquidity inside Syria/International Bank Transfers rejections.Cash modalities would be suspended whilst the finance department assess and monitor market situation.If the situation persisted, as a final option, PIN would explore switching to in-kind modalities. PIN would engage with back up CTA. 7. Syria offices/warehouses/assets are raided PIN would temporarily suspend activities; carry out internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors; inform relevant counter-terrorism stakeholders; seek to relocate the intervention to other areas. 8. Local Councils or other relevant local stakeholders stop collaborating with partners or fail to conduct targeting according to agreed methodologyPIN would temporarily suspend activities; engage dialogue to re-sensitize on humanitarian principles of engagement/PIN policies, relocate resources to other target areas. 9. Aid is perceived as politically motivated and/or PIN are subject to intimidation from military groups or local authorities.Activities would be suspended; re-sensitization/dialogue, reallocate funding to alternative target areas.11. Aid is diverted for personal or political gains. PIN mitigates aid diversion through a comprehensive set of rigorous management policiesPIN would suspend activities; carry out investigations, advocate for dialogue for return/replacement of diverted aid; re-sensitize actors; reallocate funding to alternative target areas.
