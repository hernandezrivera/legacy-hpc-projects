Project Summary

This project will prioritize the needs of men, women, adolescent girls and boys including people with disabilities and other diversified people by targeting the practical issues of addressing gender and social norms and GBV related different barriers through gender transformative approaches at 20 Unions of Bangladeshi Host Communities and 3 Rohingya camps, where women and girls will be able to access first level PSS, referral coordinated survivor centered case management, MHPSS, SRHR services and skills development. Women and adolescent girls including those living with disability will be encouraged to get those services of Plan International Bangladesh (PIB) with partnership of Mukti Cox’s Bazar, SKUS, PHD and Hope Foundation. Men and boys including other marginalized people will engage with different activities and transformational change approaches like, Male Engagement and faith leader engagement models to reduce GBV and encourage them to support women and girls to seek GBV services. Our approach will community-based intervention which will be implemented by empowering the existing community leadership structures, local CSO, Government stakeholders through trainings and GBV sensitization, who will be sustained as agent of us for those communities. The partner staff will be trained through ToT on different GBV programming standards. According to the GBVSS facility service mapping October 2021, at camp 3 has 2 women and 2 men facility gap and camp 4 has 2 women and 3 men facilities gap. After carefully analysis and considering the service gap and partners strength, we have selected camp 3 (Block A, B, D, E) & 4 (Block B, D, F, G), and Teknaf & Subrang Union of Teknaf, Jaliapalong & Haldiapalong of Ukhiya for our project interventions. PIB will integrate child marriage prevention specialized interventions. PIB has dedicated Gender Advisor for the overall technical support and Case Management Advisor and their technical team to build capacity of partners staff and quality GBV interventions as per global standard. For better coordination with GBVSS, PIB representative join all the meeting of GBVSS and its different working group and task teams regularly and comply with all action points. As government has special commitment to reduce SGBV and promote gender equality and women’s empowerment, the project will strongly coordinate with government counterpart. There a number of workshops, consultation and capacity building activities with government and local government stakeholders. The project will integrate environment and disaster response and environmental hazards during the disaster and COVID-19 pandemic. 4% of total proposed budget will be kept for emergency disaster response like cyclone, flood, fire etc. 8,000 dignity kit also kept to support reproductive aged girls and women in normal and emergency period. GBV will be mainstreaming through GBV component and dedicated Gender and Inclusion staff among all sectors of PIB at camps and Host Communities. All staff and stakeholders will be oriented on complaints and community feedback, AAP and PSEA and those issues will be gathered on a continuous basis and responded to promptly to ensure accountability to the beneficiaries. Hotline number, compliant box installation at every center and email will be used to get feedback about need based services and effectiveness of their life and any other complain on service quality and PSHEA. Internal Complaint, Feedback and Response will be tracked and reported monthly basis. Beneficiaries will be involved and consulted in the different stages of the project cycle management like need assessment, strategic planning, Resource Mobilization, Implementation and monitoring and evaluation and program management team adapt program intervention and take necessary actions accordingly. The program interventions will be implemented by maintaining global GBV standards and guiding principles with priorities the vulnerable people’s interest.

1 - Identified Needs

SGBV is a key area of work to improve long term protection in the Bangladeshi host and Rohingya communities as there has been significant improvement in the humanitarian situation over the preceding four years to meet the immediate lifesaving needs of the population. But, protection risks and related unmet needs specially GBV remain at the core of this humanitarian response till now. In June 2021, research reported a significant increase in GBV and IPV incidents post pandemic in both host and refugee communities. The incidence of child marriage has also increased due to the stoppage of education, unemployment and rising food prices. There are still gaps in the outreach of safe and quality GBV services and referral mechanisms for host communities (“Doing right by women and girls in Cox’s Bazar: Gendering perspectives on social cohesion”, SAFERWORLD, June 2021). Boys and young men grow up internalizing violence, including intimate partner violence, condoning negative and harmful attitudes towards women and girls and learn to perpetuate gender discriminatory norms and behaviours. Despite the presence of government run services such as hospital based One Stop Crisis Centers (OCCs), women police desks, and legal aid services, as well as GBV service entry points set up by NGOs, gaps are still in ensuring adequate coverage of safe and quality GBV services for host communities. In addition, low awareness about available services and deep-rooted socio-cultural barriers, limit the ability of survivors to access services. Access to justice for women is still of concern due to cost and long waiting times. Furthermore, due to the COVID 19 pandemic situation, GBV partners are reporting an increase in GBV. The stay home restrictions have affected the livelihoods of poor families, straining household finances as most people have lost their income sources. This has increased tensions within families, worsening vulnerabilities for women, with various forms of GBV as the resultant consequence. Findings of a Rapid Gender Analysis 2020 conducted among the Rohingya and Bangladeshi host communities, indicate that COVID-19 pandemic, has increased gendered safety and security risks for the women, girls, and boys. The findings show increases in intimate partner violence (IPV), polygamy, transphobic violence, and violence against sex workers, as well as a rise in both girls and boy's child marriages. GBVIMS incidence reports of the second quarter 2021 shows, 3% Rape, 2% Sexual Assault, 53% Physical Assault, 1% Forced Marriage, 20% Denial of Resources, 22% Psychosocial/Emotional Abuse and 84% intimate partner violence. Though the actual incidents are higher than the reported cases. The increased focus on the COVID 19 pandemic, has overwhelmed the service delivery system, diverting both attention and resources away from the ever- present threat of GBV and other reproductive health needs of women and girls.
