Project Summary

The infrastructure in Syria requires substantial repair and operational support, including training for O&M (Operations and Maintenance) of rehabilitation sites and on-grid electricity supply in areas of high need. The proposed intervention will provide life-saving WASH interventions at both the community and HH levels to increase water supply that is in most cases below the Sphere Standard. Furthermore, Medair’s activities will continue to address community sanitation through appropriate waste management, both of sewage and solid waste. The proposed response will reach vulnerable communities for the next 2 years, an approximate 100,000 beneficiaries, with life-saving interventions through the following interventions.

1. Water Network Rehabilitation
WASH systems suffered additional damages due to years of functioning at high capacity, lack of maintenance, displacement of technical staff, climate change and poor water resource management. Medair will rehabilitate up to 9 community level water systems. Activities will include: upgrade/installation of water pumping equipment; (re)equipment of wells; generator replacement; repair of water distribution lines; and development of water purification stations. Alongside these activities, Medair will promote water conservation awareness, and teach proper operation and maintenance (O&M) techniques to Water Economic Unit (WEU), the water system operators responsible for systems after they are rehabilitated. Medair will further promote environmental sustainability by identifying water stations that have the capacity and/or need to have solar panels installed.

2. Sewage Network Rehabilitation
A large portion of sewage is presently discharged untreated and many of the necessary sewage systems are completely non-functional, in disrepair, or nonexistent. In order to address life threatening health risks posed by open sewage, Medair will rehabilitate two sewage networks. The intervention will ensure that waste can be safely transported away from the HH and public spaces and that the systems will continue to be monitored sustainably. This will be done through pipe networks free from both leaks and blockages with the goal of connectivity to the main sewage line. This will prevent further discharge of raw sewage and eliminate associated health risks.

3. Solid Waste Solutions
Local councils remain ill equipped to appropriately manage solid waste throughout communities in Syria. Due to the lack of waste receptables, solid waste is left in the streets, burnt or buried. Furthermore, the available spaces for dumping are unsuited to the volume and frequently poorly managed which in turn increases vector breeding hazards among other health risks. Medair will provide community-sized solid waste bins for communities where poor solid waste management is identified.

Medair’s proposed intervention will target vulnerable households and communities in Government Controlled areas within Homs, Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor, Idlib, Quneitra or Rural Damascus, Hama, and Ar-Raqqa. At present, Medair has a presence within these areas, subject to approvals and identified needs.
In terms of resilience, Medair will insure the sustainability of all interventions. Also, Medair conducts Operations and Maintenance trainings for operators at the project locations and conducts community awareness for safe water and water conservation. Medair‘s staff meet with the community and local authorities, asking about their highest perceived needs and involving them in the design. Additionally, to enhance resilience, sustainability and to reduce the impact on the environment, each rehabilitated water system will be fitted with solar power as a renewable energy operating modality. Water systems are generally connected to a city power source; however, for situations where this is not the case, a generator will be provided in addition. This combination of power sources should power the water point at all hours.

4 - Needs

An estimated 13.2 million people are in need of life-saving WASH interventions. The majority of communities assessed by Medair last year did not meet Sphere standards for water and at least 47% of the population are relying on alternatives to piped water (often unsafe modalities) to reach their water needs. In addition, 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and half of the sewage systems are non-functioning, causing life-threatening health risks. Areas of concern remain ill equipped for IDPs and/or returnees. Around 5.6 million people are in need to solid waste management services (HNO, 2022). The COVID-19 pandemic continues to exacerbate hygiene associated risks. COVID-19 cases continue to spread, reporting a peak in October 2021 (WHO, Syrian Arab Republic COVID-19 Dashboard). Further, Syria remains economically stagnant after years 2011-2016, during which GDP losses are estimated at $226 billion (Toll of War, World Bank, 2017). An acute water crisis also continues to affect Northern and Northeastern Syria (NES). Water levels remain low, severely impacting the assessed region of DEZ, HAS, RAQ and Northeastern ALP governorate (WASH sector Monthly Monitoring Report – October 2021). Given the demand, water trucking prices have increased by 36% between January and June 2021 (REACH, Market Monitoring Northwest and Northeast Syria, June 2021) diverting household income away from critical expenditures (REACH, Market Monitoring Northwest and Northeast Syria, June 2021).

5 - Implementation of activities

Medair implements an integrated response, targeting areas where they can provide a package of both health and WASH programming in the same community. Medair aims to reach around 50,000 beneficiaries with the following activities. Medair will rehabilitate 15 water systems to restore adequate water supply to communities. Installation and repairs include various water pumps, air compressors, PLC systems, electrical service panels, ventilation devices, pipelines, filter basins and other purification components. These activities will bring each individual above 35 L each day according to Sphere standards. After rehabilitations, projects are handed back to the Water Establishment Units with Operation and Maintenance training as a sustainability measure. Wherever possible, Community Awareness-Raising workshops on water conservation and safety will be organized for key local leaders. Medair will rehabilitate three community level sewage networks. Selection of the project will happen strictly based on vulnerability criteria and predicted public health impact. After the rehabilitation, the system is handed back to the sewage company and local Municipality. Medair will also identify communities with solid waste health risks. Medair will provide 100 community-sized solid waste bins for those communities. The removal of waste will involve municipalities to a municipal-identified landfill using municipal garbage trucks.
