Project Summary

Currently Rescate team is conducting visits to beneficiaries’ houses for rehabilitation in Al-Shaar, East Aleppo. There, Rescate team found that more than 80% of families were with infants, PwDs, and or elderly. This meant that a big number of people were in need of diapers, but their parents and caregivers are unable to afford them! From this exercise, Rescate did a field assessment, results showed a dire need for various types of diaper distribution in Aleppo city and countryside, i.e., Jabal Samaan (city and rural). The case load identified 90,000 elderly and PwDs, and 124,500 children under 2 years of age.
Due to non-tolerable cost, Rescate will prioritize the elderly in this intervention. Rescate will target three Elderly Care Centers under the project for diapers distributions to 500 elderlies in Dar al-Safa (Al-Mashatia), Al-Mbara al-Islamiya and St. Mansour. Rescate has coordinated with acting partners, mainly Syria Trust and SARC for the distribution to the targeted centers in six rounds every two months for one year.
Lack of diapers for elderly bears high risks of skin diseases and infections, not to mention psychological distress for them and their families.
Each beneficiary will need 4 diapers/day so in 2 months will require 24 Kgs of diapers. Six rounds of distribution every two months will be conducted during 12 months in the targeted centers to reach to the targeted beneficiaries.
According to SARC, the last quantity of diapers distribution was in December 2020 in cooperation with ICRC, therefore an intervention like this is lifesaving. The project will carry out activities in full coordination with the protection sector. Trainings will include:
Train centers staff to raise awareness, address their own attitudes and build skills to design and deliver age-oriented programmes, Identify and manage chronic and non-communicable diseases among older people, and to provide accessible information on healthy ageing and self-management of chronic diseases, paying attention to the different health needs of older women and older men with and without disabilities.
Support volunteers and caregivers with information, advice, and appropriate equipment, for example, use of gloves or masks when changing diapers assistive products, checklists for assessing needs and progress, and supervise their activities.
Prevent elder abuse specially those who are in need: Emotional abuse – inflicting psychological pain, anguish, or distress on an older person by means of verbal or non-verbal acts that are humiliating, intimidating or threatening, Abandonment – desertion of an older person by anyone who has assumed the responsibility for care or custody of that person. Prevention measures: Consistently raise awareness in communities and families and among older persons about what constitutes elder abuse, sexual abuse and GBV against older women and men and other forms of violence. Train staff and community volunteers on the warning signs of elder abuse and how to respond (through established procedures of referrals to others services that RESCATE does not provide.) and respecting the wishes of the survivor. Work with GBV survivor support services to respond to older persons who seek their services. Include elder abuse, including GBV, within protection strategies and pay attention to the protection of widows. This project will impact positively on the wellbeing of 500 direct elderlies and their families and caregivers. It will enhance their resilience, dignity and positive relation with themselves and community.
Rescate is committed to submit 4Ws and reports to cover progress and achievements, M&E, financial and narrative reports to the sector and donors. Rescate coordinates with DINGO to ensure no duplication of services amongst the acting partners.

4 - Needs

Comprehensive assessments helped Rescate design tailored approach, particularly for NFI items outside of the standardized kits and thus diversify needed inputs. Rescate as an active partner will provide assistance to vulnerable underserved communities, by increasing the scope and invest in providing the most needed assistance for elderlies. Rescate performed several assessments in rural and Aleppo city, found that crisis affected families who are facing serious difficulties obtaining needs for cleanliness and to maintain their health. Cost of diapers is increased due to spiking prices under the current escalated socio-economic crisis, unprecedented currency devaluation, rising inflation, record unemployment and a Covid-19 pandemic captivating community who are trying to recuperate from a decade of conflict. Rescate based its intervention on the need and response activities with full consideration to the health implications on elderlies who are left behind and are in dire need for protection and care. If they are not supported with this activity to distribute diapers, they will be prone to high health deterioration risks. Rescate was in direct contact with the elderly centers managements and received the numbers of individuals in need for this support. The caregivers to elderlies expressed the situation under the current increased prices including detergents hence it is still difficult to maintain elderly residents cleanliness while taking into consideration that disposable diapers are much more safe and healthy. The numbers reached via actual registration 500 elderly residents in need at the 3 centers Mashatia, Mbara,Islamiya, St Mansor Plan to provide 3 distribution rounds, each covers 2 months needs for 12 months.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Occurrence of gender-based violence, sexual exploitation & abuse including of minors: Rescate staff, volunteers and partners are child protection gender and GBV aware; GBV is strongly mainstreamed in this project; Safeguarding accountability and reporting mechanisms are in place in Rescate and partners. • Women may be harassed to get access to the services, hence; during the project implementation Rescate will conduct awareness sessions on GBV and the role of women in their communities, besides to the extent possible Rescate will ensure women participation in the activities to ensure their support when beneficiaries are females• The project could be perceived as taking a side in the conflict due to favoring specific communities or social groups: for every project Rescate will perform conflict sensitivity analysis in order to make sure that the project is enhancing connecting factors and minimizing dividing factors in the targeted locations.Activities such as, claim mechanism will be introduced. A dedicated M&E officer will be with the team to monitor the implementation and to prepare reports about progress, maintain track of works vs. budget and action plan, and about beneficiaries’ segregation (gender, age) and social impact. A questionnaire will be prepared to assess beneficiaries’ satisfaction during and after the project implementation. ONG Rescate maintains a schedule for financial and narrative reporting to the donor and shares information under the sector 4Ws. Full coordination with Protection Sector will be ensured during project activities.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk of sexual exploitation: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: All Rescate staff and partners, and contractors, are required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, Rescate is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its project to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on Rescate work, as well as the contractors’ work, and to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issues. All PSEA and/or GBV issues will be addressed first by the Project coordinator, who will share the concern with the Head of Mission based in Damascus, in order to establish a solution or way to proceed to enhance the protection of the PSEA/GBV survivor. When needed, the Head of Mission will scale the issue to ONG Rescate Headquarters in Spain, where the Director, in consultation with the gender specialists’ team, will look for potential solutions and/or an appropriate way to proceed.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

During the implementation of the project unattended people may feel discriminated and tensions arise. There is a mid-risk that this could happened, and the impact again would be grave. For mitigation measures, Rescate will: 1) do follow up visits to the site of intervention, in the visits will remind the targeted population, and local stakeholders about the selection of the location rationale being highly populated and serving other adjacent areas as well; As soon as Rescate learns of any tension that may be arising, Rescate will immediately take action according to the situation, informing stakeholders & donor. Assumption that Covid-19 will not disrupt the project. It is difficult to assess beforehand the probability for this to happen and its consequences. Rescate will take prevention measures since the project launch to avoid contagion: PPE will be distributed for the staff & volunteer. Assumption is that there will be stability of prices for the project implementation, there is a high risk that this will not be the case, and could have grave consequences. Mitigation will be to pay down-payment to the local contractor to purchase all the needed materials for the project. In terms of support, the assumption is that stakeholders, Sector, and local authorities and maintain a strong collaboration with Rescate. There is a low risk this would not be the case, as previous projects have been successfully implemented under this line. In case of a lack of support, the impact would be grave. For mitigation, Rescate will inform at all times to the public authorities of the results being achieved, and will keep the targeted population of the intervention, and clarify any question might be raised.
