Project Summary

Conflict, natural hazards, epidemics, and the recent Sudan Crisis have severely impacted South Sudan's health systems. The International Rescue Committee (IRC) proposes a project to address the immediate lifesaving healthcare needs of 243703 people in need, including the elderly and persons living with disabilities. The project will provide essential outpatient care, child health care, safe motherhood, sexual and reproductive health, mental health care, gender-based violence response, and emergency preparedness and response in Rumbek East (Lakes State), Aweil East (Northern Bahr El Ghazal State), Koch, Mayendit, Panyijar & Rubkona Counties (Unity State).
The project will support 10 static health facilities, and 10 Rapid Response mobile (RRM) outreach posts and strengthen community health services by deploying 80 Boma Health Workers each in the targeted counties. Communities and County Health Departments will be engaged to map out underserved populations and prioritize displaced populations without access to health services. The project will also support rehabilitating structures, qualified staffing, pharmaceutical supplies, and health management information systems.
The integration of services will be prioritized through multi-sector collaboration with food security, livelihoods, nutrition, and protection mainstreaming. Providers will have capacity strengthening on mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), and infection prevention and control (IPC) measures. The IRC will staff, resource, and facilitate early deployment of RRM teams to locations pre-identified by the community and CHDs, factoring in the health priorities of affected populations.
Coordination through local duty bearers and partners, as well as national and subnational coordination mechanisms, will be an integral component of the response. The project will consider the perspective of affected people through IRC’s Client Responsiveness Measurement Framework as part of its AAP, and people with disability will be prioritized in its response. A joint support supervision for health staff will be done with CHDs and partners to reinforce capacity building and project quality.

1. Needs

South Sudan inherited a weak health system at independence in 2011, following systemic underfunding and neglect. The health system further deteriorated to near collapse following the disruptions caused during the 2013 and 2016 crises and subsequent transitional government arrangements. Limited health facility functionality, capacity, and poor coverage including the recent closure of health facilities due to reduced funding, have made it difficult to meet health needs worsened by floods, food insecurity, and frequent conflicts. Women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare. The major barriers to access and utilization of care services were related to lack of medicines, staff and equipment transportation costs, insecurity, floods, long distances to health facilities, and high cost of healthcare (mentioned by host communities, IDPs, and returnees). Only 49% of the population can access health facilities in one hour (ISNA 2022), further compounded by frequent conflicts, natural hazards, food insecurity, and epidemics including measles, and cholera. 755185 persons (171052 Returnees, 306332 IDPs, and 277801 host communities) in the targeted counties need health services (PIN target 2024). Displacement of populations due to subnational conflicts, floods, and food insecurity often leads to disrupted access to health services. Overcrowding as populations seek shelter and safety results in public health problems like increases in cases of Acute watery Diarrhea (AWD). Extremely low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3 coverage 43%- Measles). Confirmed Measles outbreaks in over 30 counties in 2023 have been reported in the context of low immunization coverage (Penta 3 coverage at 49%- Measles nationally (Measles situation report Nov 2023). Further, Hepatitis E cases significantly rose to over 3,473 cases with 26 deaths and remained above epidemic thresholds in 2022 (DHIS2 Data, 2020) and Cholera outbreak was declared in Rubkona County, Unity State. Only 10% of households have access to toilets (worse in Greater Upper Nile regions) with open defecation the norm across the Country (Multisectoral Needs Assessment 2022).Health services functionality is low with around 8% of health facilities providing the full package of the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services. 12% of women and girls reported avoiding water points due to GBV concerns, increasing to more than 40% of households feeling unsafe while collecting water in areas with frequent conflicts. Cases of SGBV are driven by protection issues resulting from overcrowding in displacement areas, inadequate shelter, and long distances to WASH sources leading to an increase need for provision of clinical management of rape (CMR) services in health facilities. Insecurity affects access to health services as access corridors are impacted or facilities closed during acts of violence. Women and girls, especially in IDP settings, are disproportionately affected (ISNA 2022). Only 59% of the population reportedly access health services within 1 hour. Under 5 mortality rate is high at 96.2 per 1000 live births. 75% of all child deaths are from malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia. Quality of health services provided varies widely across the country. An analysis of service delivery bottlenecks identified only about 39.8% of approved midwifery positions in supported health facilities as filled based on maximum staffing norms, while 14.8% of mother of children 0-11 months received at least 4 antenatal care (ANC) visits during the last pregnancy. Stockouts of essential supplies including pharmaceutical supplies, infection prevention and control supplies, and immunization supplies affect the continuum of care and basic services provision. Logistic challenges due to floods have impacted supply side and the overall Maternal Mortality Rate is as high as 1,223 per 100,000 live births (report 2022) despite efforts.

2. Project activities

The proposed project seeks to improve the health status of vulnerable populations in need across 6 Counties in South Sudan (Aweil East, Aweil South, Koch, Mayendit, Panyijar, and Rubkona Counties), targeting 243703 persons (116523 Men, 93790 Women, 18267 Boys, and 15123 Girls. Objective 1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations.To improve equitable access to lifesaving health care services the project will support 2 (PHCCs) each in Aweil East, Rubkona, Panyijar, Koch, and a PHCC each in Aweil South and Mayendit. Additionally, 2 Rapid Response Mobile (RRM) Teams each will be supported in Panyijar, Mayendit, and Koch, and 1each RRM in Aweil East and Aweil South Counties. The IRC will recruit qualified healthcare providers in the PHCCs and RRM sites, providing 24-hour services. Facilities to provide all BEmONC signal functions to ensure safe motherhood services, postnatal care is availed and referral strengthening from community and outreach sites. The facilities will provide both preventive and curative health services for communicable diseases linked with community-level demand-creation services through tailored health, nutrition, and hygiene promotion messaging. The project will actively seek opportunities for multi-sector integrated approaches to strengthen linkages and promote synergies, particularly for nutrition, immunization, food security and livelihoods, GBV, and child protection. Boma Health Workers will be selected from community members (preferably women) and trained on screening and diagnoses for common illnesses affecting children under 5 years (malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhea) including community nutrition screening and referral. Providers will have access to capacity strengthening on mental health GAP action program (mhGAP), clinical management of rape (CMR), and Integrated management of newborn & childhood illnesses (IMNCI). Pharmaceuticals, medical commodities, and other supplies will ensure that facilities are fully equipped to provide quality health services. Treatment of Non-communicable diseases, mental health, and trauma clients will be provided while strengthening referral linkages to existing secondary care facilities. Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases. The project will ensure timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases through a strengthened community system, and coordination with local authorities, boma health committees, and the health facility in charge. Response activities will be coordinated, looping in actors (national and subnational) to ensure zero duplication of efforts. Data will be stored in the District Health Information System (DHIS) including Integrated disease surveillance and response (IDSR) and Early Warning, Alert and Response System (EWARS). The project will develop and update integrated health and nutrition micro-plans in collaboration with local authorities and the Ministry of Health, aiming at comprehensive service coordination and enhancing Emergency Preparedness and Response. Objective 3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. As AAP, the project will enhance resilience and promote humanitarian development linkages through engaging communities in the delivery of health care. Community members will be selected to join the facility health committees to strengthen accountability. Technical and operational oversight through monthly support supervision visits to health facilities will be conducted. This will result in decreased disease prevalence, improved health-seeking behavior, and ultimately a more robust and resilient community health ecosystem.
