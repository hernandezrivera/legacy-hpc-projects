Project Summary

The proposed intervention sets to alleviate the vulnerable status of Lebanese and migrants in Lebanon, due to the ongoing socio-economic crisis which has highly affected and impeded the health sector. Through this project, Caritas is set to meet the SO1: ensuring access to life-saving and life-sustaining health services for those most vulnerable and in need in the context of worsening socio-economic conditions
The intervention will focus on persons with low and middle incomes. Older people, people with disabilities, and those with serious medical issues living in Akkar, North Lebanon, Beirut and Mount Lebanon.
CL has been implementing health-related interventions and programming since 1972, providing primary and secondary healthcare services to vulnerable communities across the country. In 2022, CL delivered more than 350.000 healthcare services to 81,597 beneficiaries. Over 170 physicians from different specializations are operating through CL’s Primary HealthCare Centers (PHCC), dispensaries and Medical Mobile Units (MMU) on a daily basis. Likewise, CL has existing contracts in place with over 100 hospitals and laboratories in Lebanon detailing the means of engagement.
This program will be a direct continuation of an already existing program set to finish in mid March 2023, and that has reached the maximum targets, and still have a great number of patients in need. As part of a visibility plan, Caritas had set out a social media campaign to advertise the service being provided and had coordinated with municipalities and other department within CL for referrals. A high number of cases were assessed but many were rejected due to the fact that the budgets provided are not proportional to the needs. As such, this project aims to increase the cost to cover migrants, and Lebanese beneficiaries.
After the case is assessed and is found eligible and authorized, the support will be provided either through reimbursement to the patient or direct payment to the hospital. Moreover, during an emergency, the MoU will act as a safeguard, ensuring that the patient receives lifesaving assistance after confirmation from the CL focal point, even before any funds are transferred.
As a result, through this intervention, CL will target 200 vulnerable patients for inpatient hospital coverage and diagnostic tests. In addition, 50 patients will be supported with treatment for other conditions (including dialysis, cancer, catastrophic illness).
Vulnerable beneficiaries will be identified from those who approach the CL PHCCs on their own initiative after learning about CL’s services in the area. Regarding the outreach modality, CL will inform the HSWG during the interagency meeting about the project for referrals, as well as all hospitals we’re in agreement with. In addition, the social workers in our 36 sectors across the country will also be informed about this activity so that they refer patients who are in need of IP assistance. In addition, further identification will be done through internal referrals to PHCCs by the social workers (at social centers) and other departments within CL.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Caritas Lebanon prioritizes preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), promoting protection, gender, and social inclusion in all its projects. A comprehensive PSEA policy outlines its zero-tolerance approach, and staff and volunteers undergo background checks and regular PSEA training. Reporting mechanisms are safe, confidential, and accessible to all staff and beneficiaries, with investigations conducted promptly and impartially. Moreover, CL strictly follows the Do-No-Harm methodology and acknowledges that project beneficiaries may be among the most vulnerable groups, requiring special consideration in project design and monitoring, and therefore designs projects in a way that prioritizes their needs. Additionally, CL has a set of policies and procedures to mitigate operational risks and maintain ethical behaviour, including a Code of Conduct, Child Protection Policy, Safeguarding Statement of Commitment, Anti-Harassment Policy, and Anti-fraud and Corruption Policy.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

CL is a member of the CHS alliance and a strong advocate of Accountability to beneficiaries and donors. CL has a number of well-practiced quality assurance mechanisms and accountability strategies in place. Constant communication between the project coordination team and the medical staff and follow up with the hospitals and beneficiaries ensures good interaction between all stakeholders involved in the project. This allows feedback for rapid responses to changing dynamics. Additionally, this program has a hotline for SHC and a separate hotline for feedback and complaints. The Do-No-Harm principles are embedded in the project design and implementation, and social stability factors are taken into account. The project builds on existing deep-rooted relationships developed with Lebanese and migrant communities. Services have been updated by engaging with different communities and lessons learned from previous years.
