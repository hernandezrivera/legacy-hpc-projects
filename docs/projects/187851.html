Project Summary

The HALO Trust will continue to deliver an integrated Mine Action approach in northwest Syria involving Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE), Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD), Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Victim Assistance (VA), delivered both directly and through implementing partners.
HALO and its implementing partners will provide EORE to men, women, boys and girls, with a focus on community members most at risk of accidents (including children, teenagers, IDPs, agricultural workers and rubble removal workers). HALO will also continue delivering ‘Training of Trainer’ (ToT) workshops to NGO workers and Community Focal Points to improve the relevance, integration and reach of EORE messaging across all communities and relevant humanitarian sectors. Incorporation of COVID-19 messaging to EORE sessions will continue and COVID hygiene measures will be observed.
Following the completion in 2020 of HALO's contamination impact survey work in all accessible communities in NW Syria, capturing the scope of explosive ordnance (EO) contamination in the region, in 2022 HALO is conducting detailed non-technical survey to define precisely the types of hazards and technical requirements for disposal. This will enable the training, equipping and deployment of a clearance and disposal capacity to reduce the presence and impact of EO in highly vulnerable communities in a part of the country where HALO is the only INGO able to provide these highly specialised services supported by a sector-leading global EOD advisory team. NTS also provides local authorities, humanitarian actors, the Gaziantep cluster network, and the donor community with a greater understanding of EO threats, including specific locations.
Through its Turkish implementing partner, iMFAD (Turkey's only humanitarian demining NGO) HALO will also conduct Mine Action activities as and where authorised in accessible districts of Turkish-administered northern Aleppo, including Afrin.
The support to survivors of EO accidents will continue through a Victim Assistance (VA) component implemented by HALO and its partners. This will include the provision of prosthetics and orthotics, physiotherapy, nursing, psychosocial services and multi-purpose assistance delivered by rehabilitation centres. HALO and its partners will also provide victim assistance case management services, providing referrals to specialised services for survivors of EO accidents.
HALO acknowledges the requirement to avoid activity duplication within the MASC as well as addressing gaps in providing EORE (to children, adults, CFPs, and NGO Focal Points), NTS and EOD. This will be achieved through ongoing and regular coordination with other members of the MASC, as well as engagement with local authorities, communities, and other NGOs throughout the implementation of the project. Both HALO and its implementing partners are active members of the MASC (of which HALO is co-chair) and regularly submit 4Ws to the cluster.

4 - Needs

There is widespread and ongoing contamination of communities and agricultural land by landmines, cluster munitions, rockets, mortars, artillery projectiles and IEDs. The 2021 Mine Action Review reports that 50% of people are at risk and over 33% of communities are possibly contaminated, notably Aleppo, Idleb, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-Ez-Zor and Quneitra. At least 4 cluster munition attacks by Syrian and Russian forces in 2020 targeted non-government areas in the northwest. HALO’s survey from 2018 to 2020 confirmed the presence of explosive contamination in 41% of accessible communities surveyed, with approx. 1.6 million people living at risk from EO in the northwest, where very limited clearance has taken place. By contrast, iMMAP reports that in the northeast, over 25km² of contaminated ground has been released. HALO’s assessments indicate 3,500 EO-related accidents in NW Syria, resulting in approx. 4,500 casualties, over 600 in 2020 alone. This puts pressure on agricultural land and natural resources, further increasing risk of accidents. UN OCHA reports that 25% of recorded casualties in 2020 were children (40% fatal). This agrees with HALOs finding from 2021 FGDs and KIIs where the majority of participants stated that children are most at-risk. The protracted nature of the conflict was mentioned as a barrier to behaviour change, as people feel less wary of EO, seeking scrap metal from it and treat it as a negative coping mechanism in the absence of other livelihood opportunities.

5 - Implementation of activities

HALO will conduct activities both directly and through partners. EORE activities will involve the delivery of EORE with vaccinated staff through community-based sessions, applying precautions like distributing masks and sanitizers, and maintaining distance in sessions, with flexible options including interactive school-based sessions, social media campaigns, and usage of cartoons and comic books. Considering the different risks that certain gender, age, and occupation groups are exposed to, materials tailored for specific groups will be used during sessions. NGO workers and CFP will be trained through ToT. HALO will prioritise PwDs as ToT participants and continue to explore ways to guarantee inclusion of PwDs in EORE. NTS will confirm hazardous areas by validating previous EO survey data with an emphasis on suspected minefields, IED fields, and multiple EO-contaminated areas. Collected data will be shared with UNMAS. The information on disposal and clearance operations would inform where Mine Action resources should be prioritised. EOD will dispose of accessible surface hazards, in accordance with priorities determined and agreed upon by HALO and partner technical staff. VA will include provision of services, such as physical rehabilitation, in-kind assistance, and referral to specialised services. MEAL Activities: HALO MEAL staff will compile monitoring reports & feedback forms, while HALO’s FM spot checks, KIIs, and FGDs will validate and inform project implementation

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Familiarisation may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident to threat of EO (Likelihood: medium; Impact: high): Emphasis on “Stop, Warn and Report” messaging in EORE and introducing EOD team hotline for further interventions. Difficulties accessing girls and women (Likelihood: medium; Impact: high): Current teams already include female staff. HALO will seek to achieve gender-balanced recruitment for field staff to facilitate the consideration/implementation of appropriate activities for inclusion of girls and women. Trained local EOD capacity may become overconfident and injury to team members &/or local community members during survey/disposal tasks (Likelihood: medium; Impact: medium): Staff trained to IMAS standards and supervised by Technical EOD Advisor through regular quality assurance checks. Teams will deploy with an ambulance, first aid equipment, and communications. Risk that local authorities may dictate mine action priorities are not in line with humanitarian objectives (Likelihood: high; Impact: medium): Recruit local staff to build trust and to promote community participation. Highlight humanitarian priorities by using contamination data while engaging with local authorities. Teams perceived as having a biased affiliation, exposing them to harm (Likelihood: low; Impact: high): Partners are well known/trusted in their areas of operation and coordinate with local authorities to facilitate safe access. .


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

HALO and its implementing partners have zero tolerance of abuse of human rights in any part of the organisation or its supply chain and partner organisations. HALO conducts due diligence checks on partners, contractors and suppliers to ensure that correct processes and practices, values and standards are in place to ensure the protection and safeguarding of staff and communities. Gender-balanced recruitment, training, and code of conduct requirements for project staff will be applied to ensure appropriate protection-sensitive interventions. Staff exposed to, or guilty of, GBV, sexual exploitation, and abuse (Likelihood: low; Impact: high): Mitigation - all project staff are trained in PSEA, gender mainstreaming, and 'Do No Harm'. Activities are designed to consider the needs of men, women, boys, and girls. Each beneficiary has access to feedback and complaint mechanisms. Ongoing M&E through field visits. Safeguarding incident in particular gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse during activities (Likelihood: low; Level of impact: High): All project field staff will receive initial and refresher safeguarding training which includes PSEA, GBV, gender mainstreaming, and the principles of 'Do No Harm'. EORE activities are designed, taking into consideration the needs of children and adults. A leaflet including the feedback mechanism (WhatsApp number and an email address) will be provided to each beneficiary.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access. Regular contact made with local, governorate and national authorities ensures concerns are addressed quickly and avoids withdrawal of approvals. Agreements in place partners and Turkish governor offices. If COVID restrictions prevent standard EORE activities in Idleb, HALO will conduct indirect activities, including videos via social media and adjusted community engagement. Under performance against project activities. A mobilisation phase builds relationships with local authorities and refresher training is provided. Conservative beneficiary targets are established for project activities. Project design is based on experience in NW and S Syria. Official agreements established between HALO and partners. Formal project kick-off meetings and monthly project reviews and strong MEAL mechanisms will be in place. Recruitment & retention of essential field personnel. HALO and partners have demonstrated capacity to recruit good quality staff. The maximum number of suitable staff from any concluding projects are recruited for the next project and competitive salaries offered. Security situation deteriorates preventing/restricting project activities. Daily security situation monitoring informs work plans. Activities can be quickly diverted to respond to changes in the security environment, with the principal challenge being widespread security deterioration. HALO and partners will then suspend activities and review to identify areas where work can be safely conducted.
