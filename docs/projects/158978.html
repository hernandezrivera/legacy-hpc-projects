Project Summary

The main objectives of this project is seeking accountability by providing legal interventions on behalf of victims of Israeli attacks occurred in the context of the great march of return that is taking place in the access restricted areas and also to strengthen monitoring, verification and documentation of the possible IHR/IHRL violations including attacks on health staff and other protected and/or vulnerable groups in the Gaza borders in addition to enhance access to medical care for the victims outside Gaza and in the special context of peaceful demonstrations in the Gaza Borders.
Those objectives are directly linked to Humanitarian Response Plan Country Level Strategic Objective 1: The rights of Palestinians under occupation are protected in accordance with International Humanitarian Law, IHL, and International Human Rights Law, IHRL, and linked to strategic result statement 1 of the Protection Cluster: 1. Increased respect for International Humanitarian Law and Human Rights Law and Accountability for Violations
The project will be implemented by the Palestinian Center for Human Rights –PCHR, and it will last for 12 months starting from 1st January, 2019.
The activities of the project will vary between legal aid for the victims of IHR/IHRL violations in ARA, Monitoring and documentation, reporting of the violations in the border areas and also providing legal aid to address the freedom of movement restrictions impeding injuries and patients from receiving treatment outside Gaza.

1 - Needs

While concerns over excessive use of force in the oPt are longstanding, the staggering loss of life and injury – including serious wounds with long-term health, disability and psychosocial implication – recorded in Gaza in the ‘Great March of Return’ (GMR) demonstrations since March 2018, remains a key concern as well as a significant driver of need for the Protection Cluster, with projected needs extending beyond 2020. PCHR documented 214 killings by Israel since the outbreak of the protests on 30 March 2018, including 46 children, 2 women, 9 persons with disabilities, 4 paramedics and 2 journalists. Additionally, 14,251 were wounded, including 3,501 children, 380 women, 245 paramedics and 215 journalists, noting that many of those injured had sustained multiple injuries on separate occasions.The large number of casualties among protestors, in particular the high number (over 7,381) injured by live ammunition, in circumstances that did not appear to constitute a threat to life to Israeli Security Forces (ISF), raises serious concerns about excessive use of force. PCHR have had to intensify its presence and work on documentation of violations and provision of legal aid. Furthermore, based on the numbers of victims who raised cases or complaints through PCHR to the Israeli ministry of defense in the aftermath of these peaceful demonstrations, it is estimated that a case load of 175 individuals will require legal support including counselling, assistance and representation. As the nature of this intervention is of timely manner, it is estimated that the 175 cases for victims/individuals will require to continue their proceedings and will continue to be in need of legal support in 2020, this ensures the engagement of the beneficiaries across the whole project cycle where they asked for the service, updated about the whole progress during the implementation and five groups discussions will be conducted to measure their satisfaction at the end of the service. Israel not only used lethal force against Palestinian protesters in the field but also denied passage for medical treatment to critically wounded protestors. where GMR injured persons continued to experience delays and denials to exit Gaza via the Israeli-controlled Erez crossing to access health care and are therefore in need for legal aid. According to Gaza’s Coordination and Liaison Office, as of 31 August 2019, of the 585 applications to Israeli authorities by those injured in demonstrations since March 2018, 17 per cent were approved, 27 per cent were denied and 56 per cent were delayed. According to PCHR database, 327 patients asked for legal aid interventions to secure permits. 40% of them will continue to be in need of legal assistance to get the permits in the project period and new 140 patients is expected to ask for the legal aid in 2020. PCHR emphasizes that continuously inflicting casualties, either killed or wounded, and also denying injuries from receiving the medical treatment outside Gaza is unjustified, and using lethal force to target and kill civilians, who exercise their right to peaceful assembly or while carrying out their humanitarian duty, is a serious violation of the rules of international law, international humanitarian law, the ICC Rome Statute and Fourth Geneva Convention. Thus, PCHR proposed through this project to hold accountable all those applying or involved in issuing orders within the Israeli Forces at the security and political echelons , It is so important to continue and pursue accountability avenues for Israeli violations within the Israeli mechanism of justice and within the accessible international avenues in order to break the cycle of Israeli impunity

2 - Activities

The main objectives of this project is seeking accountability by providing legal interventions on behalf of victims of Israeli attacks occurred in the context of the great march of return that is taking place in the access restricted areas and also to strengthen monitoring, verification and documentation of the possible IHR/IHRL violations including attacks on health staff and other protected and/or vulnerable groups in the Gaza borders in addition to enhance access to medical care for the victims outside Gaza and in the special context of peaceful demonstrations in the Gaza Borders. This will be achieved through a well- designed package of activities as follows: 1. Providing Legal aid to Palestinian victims of Israeli HR/IHL violations during the great march of return through engagement in the Israeli justice mechanisms 1.1 Building Legal Files (preparing new files and following up old files) 1.2 Filing Complaints Demanding Criminal Investigations 1.3 Sending Notifications to the Compensation Officer at the Israeli Ministry of Defense 1.4 Filing Compensation Cases before Israeli Courts 1.5 Providing Legal Consultations to Victims and their Relatives 2. Monitoring, documentation and reporting of violations of IHL and IHRL, including of protected and/or vulnerable groups (women, children, health staff, journalists, PWDs) Independent verification of reported incidents of grave violations against children Gender based analysis of violations 2.1 Conducting field visits 2.2 conducting personal interviews with the victims and/or their families and eyewitnesses 2.3 collecting testimonies 2.4 Filling out incident forms 2.5 Taking photos 2.6 Collecting documents 2.7 Writing field reports 2.8 conducting meetings to verify the collected information 2.9 Feeding database 2.10 Issuing press releases 2.11 issuing weekly reports 2.12 one conference highlighting the consequences of the great march of return 3. Providing Legal Aid to Protect the Right to Health and address freedom of movement 3.1 Filing Complaints to Medical Referrals Department at the Ministry of Health and Coordinating with Organizations Working on the Right to Health 3.2 Filing complaints to the Israeli Humanitarian Center at Beit Hanoun (Erez) crossing in addition to sending dozens of reminding letters 3.3 Providing Legal Consultations on the Right to Health3.3 conducting a conference to highlight the consequences of the Great March of Return . In terms or reporting, PCHR is committed to report on the indicators to HRP periodic reporting and financial tracking.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

To achieve touchable, reliable results, PCHR follows and monitor the violations perpetrated by the Israel year after a year, and document the violations contentiously and precisely in the most effected areas like the access restricted areas. which always reflect the demand for accountability for violations. Gaza Strip witnessed a significant increase in the casualties in the context of mass demonstrations taking place along the Israel fence with Gaza, increased violations against the civilians including the health staff, journalists, women, children necessitated to continue the efforts of documentation, reporting and legal aid by PCHR which will enhance the accountability to affected populations in the border areas. PCHR carry out focus group discussions with various stakeholders, including beneficiaries, women, journalists, lawyers, politicians, civil society organizations, human rights organizations, fishermen, etc. in order to determine needs, strategic priorities, appropriate interventions and programs' focus areas. PCHR has a complaint system for beneficiaries, and its managements maintains accessibility for all beneficiaries and counterparts to discuss any issues. Furthermore, they conduct periodic reviews and assessments of its interventions and services through focus groups with beneficiaries. PCHR internal guidelines and statues ensures best practice in line with international standards and the principles of Do No Harm.In terms of monitoring and documentation, the victims are informed about the whole process and their privacy is prioritized by using letters of their names in the issued documentation materials if they prefer that.in terms of providing legal aid for victims of human rights violations, PCHR has to obtain informed consent to initiate any legal action. During the legal aid , the lawyers inform the victims about any updates in their cases in addition to any possible ramifications of the legal actions.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

1.1 % of the beneficiaries seek PCHR services by coming to its offices themselves. PCHR is one of the leading human rights organizations known in Gaza by its excellent reputation in providing services to victims of human rights violations which motivate the victims and their families to seek the services. Legal aid unit aim is to accept all requests for legal assistance (100% acceptance rate). This is made possible through the design of the programme where all violations of human rights on the Israeli and Palestinian agendas are included.1.2 % of the beneficiaries are engaged through outreach by PCHR’s fieldworkers or lawyers.1.3 In the context of cooperation with partners, % of beneficiaries are referred to us by partners, CBOs and other civil society organizations.
