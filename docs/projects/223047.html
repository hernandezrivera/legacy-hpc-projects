Project Summary

This project will provide survivor-centered support services to GBV survivors and those at high risk in Rohingya refugee Camp-13 (Blocks A, B, G), Camp-15 (Blocks B, C, E), Camp-19 (Blocks A, B, C, D), and the host community in Pallongkhali Union adjacent to Camp-19.
The project aims to strengthen sustainable community-based mechanisms to combat gender-based violence in targeted locations. This will be achieved by building the capacity of community leaders, women leaders, youth leaders, religious leaders, and GBV stakeholders and non GBV stakeholders, including police and Armed Police Battalion (APBN), through specialized training, orientation, and basic GBV knowledge. The project will also conduct psychosocial support activities and life skills training to empower women and girls and build participant resilience.
In alignment with JRP-2025, World Vision focuses on Objective 5: "Enhance access to survivor-centered services by responding to individual needs, preventing and mitigating GBV risks, and supporting survivors of GBV in the Rohingya refugee/FDMN camps and targeted areas in host communities." To uphold this objective, World Vision will implement interventions that both respond to GBV and create an enabling environment for prevention and response and risk mitigation
To provide intensive support to survivors, the project will offer case management and referral services through 4 Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS), following inter-agency guidelines. These centers will provide comprehensive support through skills training in sewing, handicrafts, cooking, and life skills. Quarterly safety audits will be conducted to align and ensure GBV related sectoral standards. Women and girls will receive awareness sessions and training on domestic violence, child marriage, early marriage, forced marriage, denial of resources, physical assault, and emotional abuse etc. The Girls Shine module will be implemented at these WGSS.
World Vision's GBV awareness raising and risk mitigation strategies include promoting gender equality and women's empowerment, providing education on consent and healthy relationships, supporting protective laws and policies, and ensuring access to survivor support services. Risk mitigation efforts will address root causes of GBV through risk mapping, conducting Focus Group Discussions with Rohingya and host community members, addressing gender inequality, challenging social norms that condone violence, and creating economic opportunities for women.
Capacity building will include training and monthly follow-up meetings for volunteers and staff. World Vision will provide comprehensive training on Code of Humanitarian Practice, Code of Conduct, GBV Case Management, GBVIMS+Primero, Psychosocial Support and Psychological First Aid, life skills curriculum, and specialized case management for child and male survivors, LGBTQI individuals, and mental health support. Additional capacity building will be provided for non-protection staff, mediators, local representatives, health workers, legal professionals, Camp-in-Charge officials, government representatives, and women-led organizations on protection mainstreaming.
To transform social norms, community leaders and youth leaders will receive intensive training and awareness sessions. The Champion of Change module will be implemented to facilitate social behavioral change. The program will promote women leaders to ensure sustainable transformation.
For male engagement activities 10 peoples corner will be runned under this project in addition to community outreach activities The project will organize multiple awareness events throughout the year, including during the 16 Days of Activism, International Mother's Day, International Father's Day, International Women's Day, Youth Day, and Suicide Prevention Day.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to GBVIMS factsheet and GBVSS quarterly bulletin for Q2 2024, the identified needs in Cox's Bazar reveal a complex humanitarian crisis where gender-based violence intersects with severe protection concerns and systemic challenges. The data shows that intimate partner violence (IPV) remains a critical issue, constituting 73.8% of reported GBV incidents, highlighting the urgent need for targeted interventions in domestic settings. This is compounded by the fact that 64.6% of camps report women and girls feeling unsafe or very unsafe, particularly due to increased threats from organized groups engaging in kidnapping, abduction, and sexual violence.The service provision landscape shows significant gaps, with particularly concerning trends in service uptake. Despite the availability of services, 80% of survivors decline legal assistance, and an alarming 99% decline safe shelter options. Mental health support services face similar challenges, with 86.6% of recommended cases declining MHPSS support. These statistics point to urgent needs in improving service accessibility, confidentiality, and cultural appropriateness. Additionally, there are critical gaps in service availability during nights and weekends, leaving survivors vulnerable during these periods. For persons with disabilities, the need for accessible facilities, including water points and latrines, remains a pressing concern. As per ISNA, access to services remains a significant challenge, with 13% lacking access to specialized reproductive health services. Despite high awareness of GBV services (94%), several barriers impede access, including movement restrictions at night due to poor lighting (26%), discrimination/stigmatization (14%), and distance to services (14%). Service utilization is further hampered by increased insecurity limiting mobility, stigma, cultural norms, physical accessibility issues, and concerns about service quality. The intersection of these barriers creates particular challenges for vulnerable populations, including women, girls, and marginalized groups.The assessment indicate that Safety and protection concerns present another crucial area of need, with 46% reporting increased safety concerns for women/girls and 52% for men/boys. The nature of these safety concerns varies significantly by gender: women and girls face risks of sexual assault, harassment, domestic violence, and exploitation, while men and boys confront threats of forced recruitment, physical violence, and exploitation by criminal groups. Women and girls particularly identify streets/pathways (29%), public toilets/latrines (25%), and market/shopping areas (15%) as unsafe locations, primarily due to inadequate lighting and the presence of criminal groups.Resource constraints and capacity issues significantly impact the quality and consistency of GBV response. There is an urgent need for sustained funding to maintain adequate staffing for essential "firewalled" activities like GBV case management. The coordination between field administration staff and service providers also requires strengthening to ensure effective response to GBV incidents.Specific population groups face heightened vulnerabilities that require targeted interventions. Adolescent girls and younger women are at increased risk of forced marriage and sexual violence, while female-headed households face particular vulnerability to organized crime and violence when male partners are absent. Persons living with disabilities experience higher rates of abuse, discrimination, and face additional barriers in accessing services. These layered vulnerabilities require a comprehensive approach that combines immediate protection measures with longer-term structural changes to address the root causes of gender-based violence in the refugee and host communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project ensures protection mainstreaming throughout its interventions by prioritizing safety, dignity, and equitable access for all beneficiaries. It addresses the specific needs of GBV survivors and at-risk individuals in targeted locations and communities by providing survivor-centered services through WGSS, ensuring confidentiality, safety, and quality support. The project emphasizes meaningful access by identifying and addressing barriers, such as cultural norms, that limit the participation of women and marginalized groups. Through targeted capacity-building initiatives for community leaders, youth, women leaders, and GBV and non-GBV stakeholders, it ensures that diverse actors are equipped to uphold humanitarian principles, reduce risks, and support sustainable community-based mechanisms.A participatory approach underpins all activities, with communities actively engaged in risk mapping, FGDs and social norm transformation initiatives. The project’s behavioral change components, such as the "COC" and "Girls Shine" modules, empower women and girls while fostering male engagement to challenge harmful norms. By establishing robust feedback and complaints mechanisms, the project ensures accountability and continuous improvement.Protection principles are further upheld through collaboration with local authorities, Camp-in-Charge officials, and service providers to strengthen referral pathways and ensure access to life-saving services. Specialized training on GBV case management, psychosocial support, and protection mainstreaming for staff and volunteers promotes adherence to humanitarian standards. By conducting quarterly safety audits, the project monitors and mitigates risks, ensuring interventions align with inter-agency protection standards. This comprehensive approach ensures that protection is integrated across all project components, fostering an inclusive and safe environment for all beneficiaries.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project implements World Vision's Humanitarian Program Accountability Framework and Accountability Operating Guidelines, aligned with global standards including CHS and IASC commitments. Building on WV Bangladesh's annual MSNA and accountability & safeguarding assessment findings, the project prioritizes affected people's needs without discrimination. A comprehensive AAP plan and progress tracker guides implementation, supported by SOP developed in consultation with key stakeholders. The project ensures accountability through seveal key activities: 1) strengthening capacity of all staff, volunteers, and community leaders on AAP through targeted training; 2) implementing inclusive beneficiary selection through community engagement following agreed criteria; 3) providing comprehensive project and AAP-related information to community members; 4) conducting regular consultation meetings with all relevant stakeholders including beneficiaries; 5) promoting community participation in implementation, monitoring and maintenance through volunteerism and community platforms like women watch groups and community protection groups; 6) establishing diverse community-preferred feedback channels including feedback boxes, help desks, face-to-face interviews, and hotline services. 7) organizing learning workshops with all relevant stakeholders to share experiences and improve practices; 8) conducting joint monitoring to ensure transparency and effectiveness; 9) developing and distributing community-friendly IEC/BCC, project activities, selection criteria, free assistance, and feedback mechanisms; 10) actively disseminating AAP-related messages through multiple channels. Through these systematic approaches, the project ensures meaningful community participation, transparent communication, and responsive feedback mechanisms while maintaining accountability to affected populations.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project is designed to proactively address Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) as a fundamental component of its implementation strategy, particularly in the context of the Rohingya refugee camps and the surrounding host community. By aligning with the objective of the Joint Response Plan (JRP) 2025, the project seeks to create an enabling environment for preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse, ensuring that survivor-centered services are effectively delivered.To achieve this, the project integrates PSEA principles into its capacity-building initiatives, targeting community leaders, women and youth leaders, and various stakeholders, including police and Armed Police Battalion personnel. Through specialized training sessions that emphasize PSEA awareness, participants will gain the knowledge and skills necessary to recognize and respond to potential SEA incidents. This training will also include the importance of reporting mechanisms and the ethical responsibilities of aid workers, thereby promoting a culture of accountability within the community. The establishment of WGSS and peoples corner is a crucial aspect of the project, providing confidential and secure environments where survivors and project participants can access different services. These safe spaces will follow inter-agency guidelines, ensuring that the reporting and referral processes for SEA incidents are handled with the utmost care and sensitivity. Awareness sessions conducted in these spaces will educate women and girls , men and boys about PSEA and its reporting channels. Additionally, the project emphasizes community engagement through awareness-raising events that will highlight the risks associated with GBV and SEA. Through this comprehensive approach, the project not only addresses immediate PSEA concerns but also fosters long-term cultural change that prioritizes the safety and dignity of all community members.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project is equipped with a comprehensive ERP that incorporates a robust continuity plan, detailed emergency response standard operating procedures, and a dedicated contingency fund. This strategic approach ensures the project's long-term sustainability and provides the necessary resources to respond effectively to emergencies, particularly those impacting vulnerable populations such as women and girls. The continuity plan outlines strategies to maintain critical operations during disruptions, while the standard operating procedures provide step-by-step guidance for responding to emergencies. The contingency fund serves as a financial safety net, enabling the project to allocate resources to support affected communities and implement emergency response measures promptly.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this project actively engages youth (15-24 year-olds) through comprehensive and targeted interventions designed to empower young people as agents of change in their communities. The Champion of Change module serves as a cornerstone program, providing intensive training and awareness sessions for youth leaders to facilitate social behavioral change and transform community norms. For young women and girls, the Girls Shine program is implemented through Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS), focusing on building essential life skills, confidence, and resilience. The project offers various skill development opportunities including vocational training in sewing, handicrafts, and cooking, complemented by comprehensive life skills education. Youth participants receive focused awareness sessions on critical issues such as domestic violence prevention, child marriage awareness, forced marriage prevention, understanding consent and healthy relationships, and self-care practices. The program particularly emphasizes youth leadership development through dedicated training and engagement in community outreach activities. Young people are actively involved in various international observances throughout the year, including International Youth Day and the 16 Days of Activism campaign, providing platforms for youth voices and participation. Through this multi-faceted approach, the project ensures meaningful youth engagement while building their capacity to prevent and respond to GBV in their communities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees through various targeted interventions. By providing skills training in sewing, handicrafts, cooking, and other life skills within the Women and Girls’ Safe Spaces (WGSS), the project empowers women and girls to develop sustainable livelihoods. This enhances their economic independence and ability to support themselves. Additionally, psychosocial support activities and life skills training help build emotional resilience and coping skills, strengthening participants' capacity to manage the challenges they face. Community engagement and leadership development among women, youth, and community leaders further foster self-reliance by encouraging local advocacy and support networks. These efforts are complemented by male engagement activities and social norm transformation, which promote supportive environments and lasting resilience for refugee and host communities.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project’s activities in Palongkhali Union align closely with Cox’s Bazar's District Development and Growth Priorities (DDGP), particularly in advancing gender equity, social safety, and economic empowerment. By providing safe spaces, skill-building, and support for GBV survivors, the project contributes to district goals focused on gender equality and reducing violence against women. Training in sewing, handicrafts, and life skills aligns with DDGP’s emphasis on economic empowerment, enabling women to achieve financial independence, which supports poverty reduction and sustainable development. Furthermore, community engagement initiatives build social cohesion between refugees and host residents, supporting DDGP's focus on resilience and harmonious coexistence. Through these targeted efforts, the project addresses both immediate protection needs and long-term development goals, enhancing the safety, dignity, and economic stability of vulnerable populations in Cox's Bazar.
