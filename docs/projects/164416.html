Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen protection and resilience of Syrian IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities to enjoy rights, legal and physical protection and access to services through advocacy, identification of risks and response, coordination and provision of community-based protection services. It will capitalize on UNHCR’s community- centers network in Syria to help identify protection needs and facilitate response to people in need of protection and access to services to enhance their legal and physical protection. As such protection interventions including legal counselling and assistance, protection monitoring, referrals and information sharing, social protection through livelihoods support to most vulnerable people, psychosocial support, GBV prevention and response, child protection, case management, in- kind assistance covering basic needs of elderly and persons with disabilities, education support to vulnerable children, campaigns and awareness including on mine education for communities targeting women, men, boys and girls and capacity building of first responders working in communities, volunteers, partners and government counterparts will be provided through the project. Outreach to communities will be convened through static and mobile facilities including outreach volunteers, through which people in need of protection services in areas of SGBV, CP, civil documentation, psychosocial support and community empowerment will be provided and enhanced. Activities on community initiatives will also be implemented engaging communities to address own protection concerns and enhance participation, inclusion, ownership and social cohesion. Legal aid interventions will enhance access to civil/HLP documentation of IDPs and refugee returnees and provide support in legal matters related to GBV, CP and other protection risks. Accountability to affected people will be realized through meaningful participation of communities, structured focus group discussions and enhancement of community- based complaints mechanism to receive feedback with regards to programmes delivered through the project and complains including regarding sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and the redressal of incidents.
Intervention to refugee returnees will focus on humanitarian assistance, legal aid, coordination with government counterparts and UN and NGO stakeholders, information management and return tracking systems and sub – district profiling to better inform return programming and interventions. In addition, the project will support coordination fora between protection actors ensuring coordinated protection interventions as well as providing technical training to local stakeholders and HLP Technical Working Group. UNHCR Syria will continue to lead Protection Sector in coordination of response plans of IDPs and affected people as well it will execute leadership roles in refugee return programmes in collaboration with the Government. In North West Syria, it will lead Protection Monitoring Task Force (PMTF).
Through the project intervention approximately 3 million IDPs, returnees and affected host communities (60 %, 25 % and 15% respectively) will be reached in government and non-government-controlled areas redressing their protection concerns. 3,000 humanitarian workers and local authority staff will be trained, and 2,700 communities consulted through FGDs and 2,310 communities through protection monitoring. 30% of the project beneficiaries are girls, 25% are boys, 24% are women, and 15% are men and 3% elderly women and 3% elderly men. 840 humanitarian workers and local authority staff are to be trained and 120 communities will be assessed through protection monitoring activities.
The project will ensure GBV prevention through community-based mechanisms, such as women committees.

4 - Needs

Complex and inter-linked protection needs continue to exist in many areas in Syria. According to the 2019 HNO, 11.7 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, including 6.2 million IDPs. Some 5 million are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, direct exposure to hostilities, conditions in sites/collective shelters, returns to destroyed areas and limited access to basic needs and services. In 2019, displacement of people in Northeast Syria has induced massive influx to Al- Hol Camp causing various humanitarian, legal and protection challenges. Northwest Syria continues to experience ongoing conflicts exposing civilians to direct hostilities and grave violations of International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL). The humanitarian challenges are aggravated by lack of access and limitations of protection actors to reach out to the communities in protection and humanitarian aid needs. At the end of 2019, 99 UNHCR Syria community centers recorded in IM database a total of 589,794 individuals with vulnerabilities, of whom many have more than one vulnerability, ranging from chronic medical conditions, psychological distress, denial of basic rights including freedom of movement due to absence of civil statues documentations and limitation to access to adequate protection services, education and basic needs particularly for people residing in collective shelters/camps to children, women and adolescent girls and boys suffering from extreme socio economic hardship, sexual and gender based violence, child labour and child marriages. Women headed households, children, youth and adolescents, older persons and persons with disabilities, especially those without adequate family or community support were found to be particularly at heightened risks. Self- organized return continues and requires specific assistance for reintegration (1.7 million IDPs and 96,000 refugees returnees in 2019).

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with the identified needs, the project will be implemented through integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including 1) targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and the elderly; 2) legal counselling and legal- presentation on civil statues documentation; 3) advocacy and support to government on HLP related matters, 4) provision of quality and life- saving response services to GBV and CP including through case management; 5) education support , 6) psychosocial support; 7) community based child protection and women’s committees; 8) PSEA awareness raising and response to incidence, 9) mine risk education; and 10) community empowerment through community led- initiatives and proactive engagement of community based grassroot organizations to build resilience and solidary. UNHCR will strengthen its outreach to people in need in 12 governorates through capitalization of community outreach volunteers, community and satellite centers and mobile units. Capacity of first responders working in the communities, volunteers and partner personnel will be built to provide better quality protection services. Prevention campaigns and awareness sessions will be undertaken to reduce harmful behaviors and promote gender equality. Response services to GBV and CP cases primarily will include individual case management and referrals to other services. Prevention of child protection issues will be also carried out through community-based structures /child welfare committees and children clubs. Likewise, women committees will be expanded to assist in GBV prevention and response activities. Children engaged in worse forum of child labour will be withdrawn and supported through a specific CP activity implemented in collaboration with ILO. UNHCR Syria will continue to lead the Protection Sector/Cluster to ensure coordinated protection interventions and spearhead coordination and programming for safe return of refugees.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1.Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and service. Mitigation: Raise awareness among staff and community about SEA and that humanitarian aid and services are rights and provided free. 100% of UNHCR staff and 30% of partners staff to be trained on PSEA and code of conduct. Apply the mandatory SEA screening when recruiting staff members. Design specific complaint mechanisms and ensure adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality. 2.Risk of inter community tension if returnees are provided statues based benefits or if some communities in the catchment areas of the community centres or areas reached by mobile units are served while others are not or; and risk to increase tensions or damage relationships/trust between communities and humanitarian actors for continuing to assess. Mitigation: Provide needs- based assistance and not statues based to all crisis affected population including IDPs, host communities and returnees. Conduct an in depth assessment of the envisaged locations for community centres, existing populations groups and needs and provide information to communities on scope and expectation of assessment, triangulate information, train assessors, provide feedback, ensure that staff and outreach volunteers in community centres reflect age, gender and diversity principles. 3.Lack of involvement and technical capacity of national stakeholders in areas where the response to needs require their involvement, e.g. as regard to access to civil documentation and HLP documents, specific child protection interventions (family reunification, alternative care arrangements). Mitigation measures: Advocate with duty bearers on identified needs; support national actors and community-based groups to ensure sustainability of protection interventions; support technical capacity of duty bearers; and establish coordination mechanisms with duty bearers.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Risk: Fluid context, shifting frontlines and unstable political situation in North Syria are affecting response.Mitigating measure: UNHCR has in place policies, manuals and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for risk assessment and risk management. Its risk management approach is specifically tailored to suit UNHCR's needs in giving visibility to risk considerations in planning and decision-making. The risk management process helps managers to identify, analyze and evaluate risks, and develop and implement appropriate risk treatments relevant to the specific context. 2.Risk: Lack of direct monitoring/control mechanisms on partners' activities due to inaccessibility.Mitigating measure; advocacy to increase access to monitoring activities. Establishment of a third-party monitoring mechanism for physical verification and adequate monitoring 3.Risk: Limited number of partners that have the experience and/or capacity to implement the interventions that address the identified needs of persons of concern. Mitigating measure: Ensuring training packages are in line with standards, providing technical support and conducting refresher sessions periodically for partner staff. 4.Risk: Lack of direct access by UNHCR to persons of concern inside non-GoS-controlled areas of northwest Syria as well limited access and requirements for approvals to access in Government controlled areas. Mitigating measure: Work through partners who employ Syrian staff inside Syria for NES operation. Ensure that partners have staff who have GoT permission to cross from Turkey to Syria to conduct face-to-face trainings and spot checks on activities. Establish a monitoring mechanism through a third-party contractor with researchers inside Syria for physical verification and evaluation of activities and advocacy with GoS to grant UNHCR access and facilitate timely approval of access requests in Government controlled areas.
