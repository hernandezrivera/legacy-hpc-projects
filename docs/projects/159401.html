Project Summary

Populations in Belet Xawa rely only on open rainwater catchments, as a result, they are now depending on water trucking from vendors who provide water that is contaminated, leading to high public health concerns. Between February and 31 May 2019, 334 cases of AWD/Cholera suspected cases were recorded in Belet Xawa hospital, including one death of under-five. In the month of June and July 2019, Belet xawa town was one of the hardest hit areas by failed rains resulting in localized conflict between livelihoods groups (pastoralist/agriculturalist) and clans for access to meagre water resources. Concern conducted a sanitary survey and bacteriological tests at four water sources in the month of August 2019. The survey indicated contamination risk levels to be very high. This was confirmed by a bacteriological test results which tested positive for the E.coli. In all the 4 wells used by water vendors in Malkariyey.
In the same month, Concern conducted a sanitation assessment in nine camps in Baidoa town. The findings of the survey reported the crude latrine coverage of the population to be at 1:487 meaning 487 people using one latrine. Many IDPs are practicing open defecation. This is not only a public health risk but also a protection risk of particularly for young women and girls at risk of rape or physical assault while using the open fields at night. Proposed activities will be aimed at providing sustainable water sources that will be shared by targeted community members for both domestic and livelihood use. To address protection issues, constructed latrines will be fitted with lockable doors and households will be provided with handheld solar lights to be used at night while using the toilet facilities. The water situation is dire, however Concern is planning to drill two boreholes funded by DFID. The need to get support to implement hygiene and sanitation activities in the affected areas to avoid possible outbreak of diseases remains a priority. Some of the key activities needed to address the gaps are construction of latrine installed with hand washing facilities aimed at improving the latrine coverage and hygiene promotion activities to improve knowledge on potential risks that can cause AWD/Cholera and mitigating barriers aimed to improve good hygiene practices
Displacement from Lower Shabelle due to conflict is increasing. Weydo village in Daynile district has been particularly affected, with 700HHs newly arrived last week of September. Lack of sanitation and water are a primary Concerns. The Banadir Joint Inter-Cluster Rapid Assessment Report from April 2019 reported the main source of drinking water in Garasbaley and Kahda to be private water pipes where IDPs have to pay 1000 SoSh per 20l jerry can of water. Some of the IDPs, mainly the new arrivals have no access to safe drinking water at all due to poverty. Open defecation was being practiced in some settlements due to lack of latrines. Water quality in Kahda was very poor. New arrivals in Kahda reported that they did not have water containers to collect or store water, poor hygiene conditions among the new arrivals was also reported in Kahda. In Daynille, IDPs did not have access to free water and latrines, which increased the risk of health problems, including AWD/Cholera outbreak. There is a private water source in the area that charged 1,000 S.Sh per 20l jerry can which can’t be afforded by IDPs faced critical water problem. The assessment team observed that latrines were very few and open defecation was a common practice in the area.

Project Activities

Construction of six water systems comprising of shallow well installed with hybrid pumping system, elevated tank, and distribution systems: the activity will be preceded by water source assessments to identify sites with good groundwater potential and quality. The survey will be undertaken by Concern Engineers. Priority will be given to good potential wells that require upgrade/development. The wells will be fitted with submersible pump powered by a hybrid system (comprising genset and solar). An automatic chlorine dosing system will be fitted to chlorinate water produced. The system will be developed in Belet xawa in Gedo, as per consultations with local authorities and is expected to benefit 4,800 people. Construction of the shallow well will be contracted locally through a competitive tender process and will entail supervision by qualified engineers in line with Concern’s guide to development of well supervision. The beneficiaries comprise of 800hhs. Water quality will be tested upon completion of work to ensure physical, biological and chemical parameters are suitable for human consumption. Two water distribution systems to be constructed each with a water point at strategic locations in Belet xawa. Each tap stand shall be designed for 250 people based on a flow rate of 7.5litre/minute. The well will be equipped with a hybrid pumping system comprising solar and electric power. The two (Hybrid) systems (Electric from the Generator and Solar from the solar panels) will be separated using a changeover switch. Making it only possible to use one system at a time. The submersible pump procured will use both Alternating Current (from the Generator) and Direct Current (from the Solar panels). Once drilling is complete and the borehole operational, the project will be handed over to a trained water management committee formed in collaboration with the ministry of water department at the onset of the project to manage and operate the system. The water management committee will comprise eight members, (5 men and 3 women ) democratically nominated from the community to operate and maintain the system. These members will be taken through a three-day training on the management, operation and maintenance of the system after which the committee will fully manage the system. Concern will access the affected IDP sits in Banadir with an aim of providing clean water through pipeline. Water will be provided through pipeline extension and every water point will have a smart meter. Concern will make payment to water consumed through as indicated based on the meter readings. 24,000 (12,240 Male, 11,760 Female) individuals will be targeted in Banadir. Construction of 300 new Emergency Latrine and 100 Latrines for the disabled equipped with handwashing facilities.: Concern conducted a sanitation assessment in nine camps in Baidoa town. The findings of the survey reported the crude latrine coverage of the population to be at 1:487. Households will be provided with a handheld solar light to be used at night. The latrines for people with special needs will be designed with a large access door and installed with a ramp and support rails. The latrines is will benefit 6,200 (3,038 M, 3162F) beneficiaries. Distribution of 3000 Hygiene Kits as part of the AWD/Cholera preparedness and response and as a way of promoting hygiene among the target population. A total of 3000 Kits will be procured and distributed. The kit comprises 2-Jerricans of 20 liters each, and 12 bars of soap each 200g will be distributed to selected household for 3,000 households. Distribution will be done based on the assessment and needs on ground. 118 Community Hygiene Promoters will disseminate hygiene messages through house to house visits and community gatherings. Hygiene messages will focus on transmission routes and key barriers to prevent AWD/diseases among the community. The UNICEF/WASH cluster manual will be adopted for dissemination.

Mitigation Measures

2b) Pervasive protection risks include exclusion and discrimination especially based on clan affiliation, gender and age due to societal power structures, through the recruitment of women and individuals from different clans, Concern seeks to counter the discrimination against them. CWW will, as much as possible, ensure to have an equal number of men and women among the staff it hires. All assessments will be conducted in a participatory way by consulting women, girls, men and boys of all backgrounds, including persons with specific needs and from different groups. Concern will ensure that committees include female representatives and representatives from a broad age-range and from different groups to ensure balance and a range of perspectives in community-level targeting and verification. 3a) Concern has also been acknowledged by the government of Somalia, through its election to the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), selection as a Grant Agent (GA) for GPE, awarding of an EU funded lead consortium role for durable solutions and support in the position of lead for the 2018 ECHO funded cash consortium. The country program’s relationship with the federal government has not only been maintained but expanded in recent years; with continued interaction with the Ministry of Education (MoE), new and growing alignment with the Ministry of Health (MoH). Concern has a strong history of working through locally embedded implementing partners, committees and groups which greatly improve Concern’s presence and acceptance among targeted communities as well as helping to reach communities that might otherwise be inaccessible. 3d) Concern Somalia is currently involved in 9 consortia programmes, across multiple sectors and locations. Concern is a well-respected member of these consortia and they serve a important fora for sharing learning, best practices and challenges as well as a stronger collective voice for evidence-based influencing of donor and Govt policy. This coordination through the sharing of lessons learned and joint assessments including assesments for conflict sensitivity which allows Concern to develop and form a complete picture of the communities we work and serve. Deep and longstanding connections with local community groups and committees allow Concern to better understand community dynamics and avoid causing possible areas of conflict through our programming. The constant community involvement in consultations and the programme develop similarly helps to prevent such conflicts or issues.
