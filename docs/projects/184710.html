Project Summary

According to the Somalia Humanitarian needs overview 2022, 7.7 million people are in need, out of which 4 million people are in need of protection interventions. Gender-Based Violence continues to be an issue of concern in Somalia due to increased displacements and evictions caused by armed conflicts, seasonal floods, and droughts. Women and adolescent girls from 18 – 50 years old (including those living with disabilities) are subjected to Intimate Partner Violence, rape, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment, and abuse. Women and girls living in IDP Camps are more prone to sexual violence and abuse than those living in host communities with better housing and service sites.
The SHNO report showed that 2.1 million IDPs are in need, lack of access to essential services is a core protection concern for IDPs. From January to August 2021, the Protection Return Monitoring Network (PRMN) reported over 413,000 displacements country-wide, mainly due to conflicts. The PRMN recorded 15,980 IDPs who moved lacked humanitarian assistance, others because are in hard-to-reach areas. GBV service provision remains low compared to the needs and response. Also, limited specialized services are major hindrances to delivering provision timely, confidential, and quality GBV services.
The impact of the crisis is aggravated due to an unconducive protection environment since Somalia is a challenging environment to provide and improve protection for displaced and other vulnerable men, women, boys, and girls.
AWI will work towards reducing the vulnerability of the community and the newly displaced by providing timely and efficient referrals to GBV survivors and those at risk, expanding the existing GBV services, and building the capacity of the service providers and the community.
The proposed interventions have been designed to enable beneficiaries to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety, and dignity. AWIs’ interventions with the help of community workers will focus on reaching vulnerable groups such as women-headed households, children, the elderly, and the disabled. In order to have equal and active participation of all vulnerable groups will be involved in identifying their needs and how to strengthen the coping mechanisms.
The project will strengthen case management for 1000 survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV). The project staff who will be providing immediate assistance to the survivors and vulnerable population will be trained on case management and a survivor-centered approach.1000 survivors, persons with disabilities, and the vulnerable persons at risk of GBV will be provided with tailored material assistance, Psychosocial support, medical referrals, legal assistance referrals, and follow-up on the services provided.
AWI will ensure the protective environment needs are improved and this can be done by providing solar lanterns to IDP’s. Survivors and people at risk of sexual violence. AWI will be providing 3000 solar lanterns to reduce risks to the most vulnerable members of the community and also increase outreach and build networks by awareness-raising to 5000 community members male and female youth, adults, and elders; to create and promote awareness and dialogue on the GBV risks, consequences, and services available for survivors and ways to mitigate exposure to Gender-based violence. AWI will conduct refresher training for the protection team on case management and existing referral pathways, 5 GBV caseworkers and 10 community support workers will be trained. AWI will also create and train Committees comprised of 30 members. Whereby the committee formed will be meeting once a month to share their views and challenges faced by the IDPs. The committee will be meeting every month for the whole project duration. A total of 60 service providers, teachers, community leaders, and religious leaders will be trained on GBV risks and referral pathways.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Displacements affect individuals in different ways and most of them are marginalized within communities and are often overlooked when assessing needs of the community or even in programme planning. In ensuring these marginalized groups are included AWI will ensure that we work with all the relevant stakeholders to prevent excluding affected groups. In assessing the needs and programme planning AWI will ensure the most affected in the community that’s is IDPS, Returnees and even the vulnerable host communities are targeted as they are most affected and discriminated. AWI will focus on strengthening of gender inclusive community-based protection mechanisms and robust information campaigns to ensure impartial and timely access to protection services, also in situations of displacement. AWI will work closely with local leaders and government officials to ensure safety and stop any possible threats. The proposed intervention has been designed in a way to enable beneficiaries to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety, and dignity, while at the same time avoiding harm. To ensure that none of the marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance /activities as mitigation measure AWI will do community engagement of all groups as a key requirement in the response mobilization phase and ensuring that marginalized groups are by default among the prioritized groups. Also with the help of community support workers AWI will carry out inclusive community and authority consultations to identify vulnerable, under-represented HHs, with specific attention to age, gender, minorities, disability and clan representation. – Again with the help of the community workers, AWI will train community members on how to use and access the complaint and feedback mechanisms so as to get feedback from the beneficiaries. AWI will ensure all beneficiaries are informed of the available complaints and feedback channels. Ensure complaints and feedback provided are acted upon and feedback provided to the community.
