Project Summary

Zimbabwe is experiencing one of its worst food security crises in recent years with typically high humanitarian food assistance needs. The El Nino induced drought negatively impacted water sources, resulted in livestock poverty deaths, inadequate pasture, poor agricultural activity. This resulted in most households relying on the market for source of food in a country where more than 60% rely on rainfed agriculture for food. This is coming at a time the country is faced with hyperinflation, high unemployment, and reduced buying power. The poor macroeconomic environment continues to impact livelihoods and remains a key driver of the rural and urban food insecurity. The high parallel market exchange rates for foreign currency still largely influence the pricing of most goods and services further reducing buying power for most households worsening the already compromised food security situation in the country.
According to the ZIMVAC report, about 5.5 million people are currently facing food insecurity, as poor rains and erratic weather patterns impacted harvests and livelihoods. In urban areas, an estimated 2.2 million people are food-insecure and lack access to minimum public services, including health and safe water. According to WFP assessments, about 30% of the households are cereal insecure and in need of urgent assistance, 77% of the households are below the Food Poverty Line and unable to meet basic needs and approximately 93% of the households are below the Total Consumption Poverty Line.
A poor start of the 2019/20 rainfall season is likely to result in a bad farming season and reduced cereal stocks. Despite most districts having received sufficient rainfall for planting, high prices for seeds and fertilizer is likely to negatively impact the 2019/20 harvests. In addition, anecdotal and secondary sources are suggesting Government’s inability to import grain and its capacity to continue with the grain scheme under the social safety net program.

1 - Activities

1. Provide monthly food baskets through either in-kind or voucher transfers to acutely food insecure populations in rural and peri urban areas. The transfer is $9 dollars per person per month which has been informed by the food security cluster and meets 62% of the minimum expenditure basket. Oxfam will carry out a market assessment to assess the feasibility of cash or GIK as a transfer modality. Issues that will be considered include functionality of the markets, availability of cash from banks for cash in transit, network coverage for mobile money and availability of food commodities on the market.The country is undergoing economic uncertainties which affects the choice of the transfer modality. Where markets are functioning and food commodities are available but cash is not available like in the current situation, Oxfam plans to engage traders for the supply of food commodities. It will carry out trader assessment to assess capacity of the traders and conduct a due diligence exercise. Payments of the suppliers will be done directly to the traders for the vouchers redeemed. beneficiaries will be given electronic vouchers to redeem at selected retailers. 2. Provide gender and protection awareness raising sessions to the targeted groups3. Provide vegetable seed packs and tools for household, community and school vegetable gardens. 4. Provide drought-tolerant seeds and fertilizers. 5. Provide training, extension and advisory services to extension officers and farmers focusing on good agriculture practices, climate-smart agriculture. crop pest management, post-harvest handling, storage and processing. We will adopt the lead farmer approach and group the farmers into farming clusters. The selection criteria for the farmers to be trained will focus on access to productive land, willingness to farm, food security need among other agreed vulnerability indicators. Oxfam will work closely with Agritex in the establishment of demonstration plots. The farmers will be trained on the following topics, farming as a business, basic agronomic practises which include land preparation using the conservation farming technique, weed control, post and preplanting sessions as well as post harvest handling and storage to reduce post harvest losses.6. Promote fodder production and supplementary feeding of cattle. 7. Support livestock vaccination, disease surveillance and management through the community paravet model. 8. Rehabilitate dip tanks and provide dipping chemicals. 9. Enhance access to water for productive purposes. 10. Irrigation schemes and community garden rehabilitations.11. Promote establishment of school vegetable gardens12. Awareness raising on food preparation through the healthy harvest module Agriculture support is training in climate change adaptation, inputs support and demo plots.- Seed varieties for region 5 will be as follows 5 kgs sorghum, 5 kgs millet, 50 kgs basal fertiliser and 50 kgs ammonium nitrate and 5 kgs cow peas. For regions 1-3 the pulse ration will be as follows 10 kgs groundnuts, 50 kgs gypsum, 50 kgs compound L fertiliser. For the same region cereal ration will include 10kgs maize seed, 50 kgs basal and 50 kgs ammonium nitrate.- Livestock support is farmer training on good animal husbandry practices as well as practical demos for forage and fodder production.- We are targeting the same beneficiaries under both objective 1 and 2.
