Project Summary

Through this project, FAO will protect the lives and livelihoods of 8,049 people (3,130 households) in hard-to-reach rural GCA of Donetsk and Luhansk. Under two outputs, the intervention will ensure that rural families can meet their immediate food needs while protecting and restoring their own food production (through Cash+ interventions) and prevent further losses of their essential livestock through the delivery of critical supplies.

Needs

The prolonged conflict continues to have a detrimental impact on the socio-economic status of the population, especially in rural areas, where people are more vulnerable because of higher unemployment rates. This is also due to the loss of access to many sources of livelihoods that were available before the conflict. Analysis of Humanitarian Trends in Luhansk and Donetsk Oblast, conducted by REACH in 2017 has revealed that the food consumption pattern of the rural population is also characterized by a higher share of roots, vegetables, and fruits, which means that backyard farming continues to be a significant source of food for many.Nevertheless, rural families’ access to farmland is severely curtailed due to widespread mine contamination. Almost five years after the beginning of the conflict, vulnerable people are increasingly forced to buy food on credit, cut health expenditures, or resort to begging. Loss of income and lack of livelihood opportunities are among the main concerns of conflict-affected communities. People living close to the ‘contact line’ have a more pessimistic outlook regarding job opportunities and feel isolated and even abandoned due to the poor state of the transportation network and the lack of several basic public services.Over 30 percent of the population in the area close to the ‘contact line’ is aged over 60, majority being women, whose access to food and livelihoods have been limited by ongoing insecurity. While being distant to the main urban centers to access food markets, rural households become vulnerable and rely only on their own backyard agricultural production to cover their basic food security and nutrition needs.Rural residents practice subsistence farming on small land plots and in their back gardens varying from 0.05 to 0.5 hectares. They grow potatoes and other vegetables, as well as fruit trees, and keep a few heads of livestock (mainly cattle, pigs, goats and poultry). On average, each rural household owns two heads of cattle, up to five pigs and around 15-20 heads of poultry. Such subsistence farming mainly provides households with means to meet their food security and nutrition, however, occasional sales of surpluses may also generate cash for the families, in particular through the sale of eggs, meat and milk products.According to the latest FAO Socioeconomic Impact and Needs Assessment, conducted in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts, livestock production remains a very significant contributor to food security and to incomes in rural Ukraine. Almost one third of the assessed rural households in the Government Controlled Areas (GCA) close to the contact line owns cattle. However, with the ongoing military conflict, pastoral households have no more access to grazing land due to the constant shelling and the presence of land mines that threaten not only the animals but also their own lives. Furthermore, the inability to buy feed makes it even more difficult for the households to keep their animals alive, resulting into slaughtering and subsequent loss of their stock.Limited availability of transportation makes it almost impossible for rural population to replenish their agricultural inputs even locally. Operating prior to the conflict markets are now at the Non-Government Controlled Area (NGCA) and currently available at the GCA markets are distant, located in more secure areas of the region and inaccessible for rural population living at the line of contact. This results in further destocking and in the deterioration of the already precarious food and nutrition security situation at household level. In this regard, provision of assistance in the form of agricultural inputs is urgently needed in order to ensure the survival of the most essential livestock assets and to sustain agricultural and food production, thus protecting the main source of nutrition of the affected and most vulnerable population residing in the rural villages close to the ‘contact line’.

Activities & outcomes

The proposed project is aimed at covering the gap in the response, assisting hard-to-reach rural households residing in the close proximity to the line of contact, in particular within the range of 0 to 5 km zone. Monitoring and reportingA monitoring and evaluation system will will focus on the selection of beneficiaries, distribution of inputs, and post-distribution survey. Monitoring will be against the identified indicators. Further, FAO will facilitate establishment of phone complaint communication system to handle feedback in real time.The main monitoring / reporting tools at field level will include:- Post-distribution surveys based on physical verification and on beneficiary interviews/focus group discussions;- Technical monitoring reports;- Reports from counterparts,- Partner reports,- Organization of separate phone line as a part of communication compliant system, and- Terminal report.In addition, FAO staff will undertake periodic field visits to monitor field activities and will meet regularly with implementing partners. Project objective: To increase immediate access to food, sustain own agricultural production and ensure survival of essential livestock assets.
