Project Summary

Somalia continues to rank second worst country in the world after South Sudan according to the Fragile State Index of 2018, which is attributed to continued insecurity and armed conflict, recurrent and extreme climatic shocks, fragile governance structure, human rights violations and lack of infrastructure with over a third of the population requiring humanitarian assistance through 2019-2020. Access to healthcare continues to worsen due to widespread violence, and the health system remains fragmented, under-resourced and ill-equipped to provide lifesaving and preventative services. The public health services are mainly delivered by private providers and NGO actors with limited government oversight. As a result, the service delivery package has not been standardized leading to limited oversight on quality. Somalia has less than 40% of its target of two health facilities per 10,000 populations, and has only 19% of its target skilled health workforce. Maternity services are lacking in 74% of IDP settlements. In particular, displaced and marginalized groups face barriers to healthcare and higher exposure to disease. Somalia has a high under 5 yrs. mortality rate (U5MR) at 27.2 per 1000 live births; the leading causes are reported to be pneumonia (24%), diarrhea (19%) and measles (12%), as well as neonatal disorders (17%), which are predisposed by malnutrition.
Proposed Intervention
The proposed intervention is aimed at reducing morbidity and mortality among host and displaced conflict-affected people in Somalia. It will be implemented at community and health facility levels
At community level - A large network of Community Health Workers (CHW) currently at over 1200 will link beneficiary populations with catchment health facilities using the behavior change model of care group. The support includes a heavy emphasis on community health care, working with community health volunteers in Care Groups, or in areas far from primary health care facilities, with integrated community case management (iCCM) models. These volunteers increase nutrition screening, active case finding, encourage appropriate care seeking for vaccines and early management of the common childhood illnesses, increase utilization of reproductive healthcare services, and empower communities to adapt health social and behavioral norms.
Supervision of volunteers is conducted at three levels: the community health supervisors who supervise the work of the promoters using a check list in accordance with the iCCM guidelines, the promoters who supervise the volunteers through household visits using a checklist in accordance with the guidelines and lastly the promoters who conduct household visits to supervise work done by the volunteers. Clinical supervision is the heart of quality assurance in all Medair iCCM programme
At primary healthcare level
At primary healthcare facilities, Medair will continue to work with trusted local NGO partners to ensure that outpatient services, including community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) services; reproductive health services—ANC, delivery, PNC, and FP; consultations for communicable disease; basic management and referrals for NCDs, mental health, and trauma; support to EPI personnel; pediatric care; participation in the early warning system; and outbreak response. Medair will support the operation of ten (9) Primary health care facilities in Afgooye (1), Badhadhe (1), Balcad (1), Cadale (1), Daynille (1) , Karaan (2) and Kismaayo districts (2) through provision of Pharmaceutical and related medical commodities as well as supporting the payment of partner staff salaries. The integrated multisectoral activities will include outpatient consultations for both the under and Over 5 years with an emphasis on Integrated Management of Childhood Infections (IMCI), immunization.

Project Activities

•Support epidemic preparedness by ensuring preposition of essential AWD outbreak drugs and supplies at a ware house near field level for quick outbreak response.•Support an Emergency Response Team (ERT), these teams will be rapidly deployed to outbreak sites/areas for assessment and response including case management. •Conduct public health promotion and education using Behavior Change Communication (BCC) methodology via community networks and raise awareness to potential risks.•Participate in the Early Warning Alert and Reporting Network EWARN (system) and in cluster coordination to report early and advocate for coordinated responses to any new instance of notifiable disease.•Support operation and management of communicable disease outbreaks and malnutrition emergencies for 9 PHC facilities (Dameraale in Afgooye, Caawiye in Daynille, Galooley in Balcad, Bulo Karame in Cadale, Shagalaha in Kismayo Urban, Abdala Biroole in Kismayo Rural, Burgabo in Badhadhe through: payment of PHC staff salaries, procurement, transportation and stocking of essential PHC pharmaceutical and other medical supplies•MHPSS consultation at PHC facility; diagnosis and management of mental health conditions according to mhGAP-HIG guidelines Training of clinical staff in mhGAP-HIG and PFA.•Training of community members in PFA, Supportive Communication Skills and basic MHPSS course (e.g. stress management, grief, depression).•Provision of community based psychosocial support through Care Groups, using an agreed curriculum based on assessment outcomes but will also address common issues such as understanding and coping with stress, grief, loss and depression.•Raise awareness of mental health and psycho-social well-being and how to access MHPSS services in the clinic and communities (e.g. by involving key community members/leaders, posters in clinics, messaging through Care Groups)•Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI) supplies, including vaccines and cold chain (EPI fridges), are provided to all 9 supported health facilities. Immunization services will be provided in all fixed health facilities and at selected mobile/outreach units•Support and strengthen 24 hour Reproductive Health services in (Dameraale, Karan, Cawiye, Galooley, Shagalaha and Burgabo, Abdala Biroole and Bulo Karame) PHC facilities, working towards provision of reproductive health services (ANC, PNC, BEmONC) through recruitment of additional midwives, training on Basic Emergency and Obstetric Care (BEmONC) and ensure effective timely referral system.•Train Care Group Volunteers and CHV (Male and Female) to promote the importance of timely care seeking especially for childhood illnesses and reproductive health services • Implement an integrated Community Health program using the Care Group and iCCM models by training CHWs and carrying out a behavior change program on immunization, prevention of common causes of childhood mortalities, community management of common childhood illnesses.•Ensure availability of WHO approved placenta pits all health facilities with delivery services.

Mitigation Measures

Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance - Medair is committed to ensuring that members of groups who are internally displaced OR minorities persons are routinely involved at all levels of project cycle from inception to closure. They will also be engaged individually at their households and community level to better understand their unique needs and how best to respond to them. Dialogue meetings with local leaders will be enhanced to promote local ownership of project activities. Health workers will be trained and support-supervised to ensure they practice humanitarian core principles. Health services will be offered round the clock to all including host and displaced persons at no cost. Hot line number, which is toll free will be provided to community members so they provide feedback on ay form of exclusion or marginalization to services delivered. Community leaders will be encouraged to advocate for inclusion of minority in decision making roles Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance - Medair has zero tolerance on sexual exploitation and abuse and has strict policies to prevent and protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Medair will continue to provide staff training on physical sexual exploitation and abuse mainstreaming and ensure they sign code of conducts on physical sexual exploitation and abuse, all employees have signed a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards including the rights of beneficiaries to access humanitarian services without undue exploitation. Medair has recruited a safe guarding office at all bases in Somalia to ensure continued sensitization, awareness and follow up on any allegation or even rumors about sexual exploitation and abuse to the beneficiaries. The Care group volunteers and health/nutrition workers are also trained to identify passive cases in the community and counsel them to seek professional care at supported facilities. Where these incidences have happened, trauma counselling and first aid will be provided to the survivors. A clear referral pathway will be established in coordination with other actors with capacity for long-term psychological and legal support for affected persons.Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms - Transparency will be done in sharing all the information related to the project to include Project period, where necessary targeted beneficiaries and their entitlement. Service charter in local language posters will be published, staffs will create awareness on the rights to safety, access, dignity, privacy and confidentiality Medair has an efficient and effective feedback mechanism in place for suggestions and complaint that is accessible, confidential and well understood to beneficiaries with a view to feedback to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Timely responses are provided to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. Ongoing awareness and sensitization sessions at the facility through the monitoring and evaluation officer/nutrition facility workers and the community through the care group volunteers and community health workers where they are informed on the mechanism in place and how it works. All Medair and the partners’ staffs/nutrition staffs, and community volunteers they will be trained on Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients.Exit interviews will be conducted on the targeted beneficiaries to understand their need and concerns on the quality of services they received and address whenever possible.

Please provide further explanation

Medair’s humanitarian interventions are aligned with the Federal Government of Somalia’s National Development Plan, Somalia health sector strategic plan and the Essential Package of Health Services (EPHS). The program strategy is aligned with the broader organizational and health cluster aims of improving equitable and safe access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for crisis affected aimed at reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality.The integrated multi-sectoral interventions that are both lifesaving (emergency) and medium-term (recovery and early rehabilitation) helps promote resilience and sustainability of project activitiesWorking with four local NGO partners as well as over 1200 CHW’s ensure resourceful persons will remain on ground even after Medair exit thus ensuring strengthened communities with capacity to respond appropriately to emerging needs. Similarly, the focus on capacity building and equipping health facilities to ensures a stronger local response to outbreaks and health emergencies in the future. In addition to health and nutrition services, Medair will incorporate sanitation and access to clean water activities into the humanitarian responseMedair has developed an exit and expansion strategy linked to its middle- and long-term vision, it sets indicators and minimum capacity criteria that local partners, health facilities and locations must meet in order for Medair to exit/ transition and move to other vulnerable locations. This strategy also defines partner agency selection with matrixes that are based on organizational values for accountabilityAll the affected population, whether host or IDP’s will be involved in the design, implementation and evaluation phases of the programme. The unique challenges facing each group of beneficiaries will be put into attention to ensure that affected persons have access to project benefits
