Project Summary

DRC will be targeting 24,050 individuals with income-generating activities that include agriculture and livelihoods support through the provision of business and agriculture skills, provision of cash grants for agricultural inputs (purchase of seeds, livestock, etc) of approximately 200 USD for each household, and provision of training on Climate Smart Agriculture practices through the Ministry of Agriculture departments and agencies. These activities will depend on the existing skills, preferences, and motivation of each individual. The project promotes livestock re-stocking and seed inputs while value chain analysis is conducted for agricultural markets to support smallholder farmers to increase livestock and crop production through capacity building and empowerment with improved seeds and Agro-inputs to enhance Agricultural productivity and profitability for both dry and wet season farmers in the project targeted locations. The smallholder farmers will be introduced and trained to climate-smart agricultural practices.
The project aims to provide support to vulnerable conflict-affected small farmholders with greater access to microfinance, and business skills as part of the livelihoods development scheme. The project will provide the crisis-affected people with livelihood opportunities while promoting the local market, and enhancing the self-reliance and resilience of the participants. DRC will be working and coordinating closely with different stakeholders including local authorities, agricultural and livelihoods working group, and humanitarian actors to avoid duplication and ensure a harmonized approach.

1 - Needs

The continued deterioration in the food security situation is linked to protracted insecurity as evident in renewed attacks by non-state armed groups which have led to a new wave of displacements, limited access to farming and grazing land essential for livelihood opportunities, and further exacerbated by the economic fallout from the COVID-19 pandemic and high food prices. In recent months, the closure of camps in some LGAs in Borno state has added to the number of people in dire need and this has placed increased pressure on humanitarian actors to meet their most basic food, nutrition, and other needs. Notwithstanding, the continued assistance by the Government of Nigeria and the humanitarian community to deliver food and nutrition assistance as well as livelihood support, access to farming/grazing land, labor market, productive assets, and livelihood activities remains limited. Given the current levels of food and nutrition insecurity, below-average crop production disrupted livelihoods, and high staple food prices, the food security, and nutrition situation remain extremely fragile. Large populations in the northeast remain dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their food and nutrition needs. The impact of inflation and other shocks will further undermine coping mechanisms that are already weakened by lack of access to livelihoods. Diversifying livelihoods, including reducing the dependence on agriculture, is key to improving resilience among IDPs. Nigeria’s entrepreneurial spirit is visible even in its poor northeast, underlining the potential to improve livelihoods. Generally, the evolution of livelihoods ranged from Stress (Phase 2) to Emergency (Phase 4) FNI situation across the states. Fuel subsidy removal, the volatile naira/ dollar exchange and monetary policy rates which rose by 50 points (16.5%) to reach 27.5% between August and September 2024 were amongst the major setbacks to HHs access to stable income generating sources and livelihoodsopportunities. Nonetheless, the unstable and rising inflation rate estimated as 32.7% in September 2024 as against 26.7% same period in 2023 have continued to impact negatively on the prices of agricultural farm inputs (fertilizers and seeds) and overall cost of business operations. During the lean season, agricultural livelihoods activities may continue to be threatened by the rising inflation and unstable naira-exchange rates, high cost of production inputs and business operational costs, including poor access to investment capital. This may reduce the sources and opportunities for HHs to access stable income generating activities. In the agricultural sub-sector, the possibility of HHs engaging in dry season livelihood activities may also be stiffen as the rising cost of fertilizers, irrigation facilities and overall cost of labour may out weight the financial capacity of smallholder farmers who constitute over 80% of the farming populations in states with huge potential for dry season farming.

2 - Activities

DRC will select 4,810 HH (24,050 individuals) for the emergency agriculture - livelihoods that include provision of cash for agricultural inputs. DRC will target small farmholders affected by the crisis who received or receiving food assistance and have access to land for farming and have experience in farming as a graduation scheme. DRC will conduct a rapid seed market assessment in the areas of intervention before the implementation of to identify the gaps and availability of seeds in the markets. DRC will conduct value chain analysis of the seed market to identify the markets that might need support and linkages with its participants.
