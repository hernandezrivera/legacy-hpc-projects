Project Summary

The project aims to improve the well-being and health through integrated healthcare support to the Rohingya community living in the camps and to host communities in Ukhiya in Cox’s Bazar as continuation of previous project phases. The project includes operation of 1 Primary Healthcare Centre (PHC) and the general consultation, dental, eye and limited surgery section of Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU, a secondary Healthcare center). One primary Healthcare Centre (PHC) in camp 7 will provide services in affiliation with Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS). For the Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU), the maternity part (CEMoNC) is funded by IOM, the GBV services are funded by DanChurchAid, the family planning services are supported by IPAS and rest of the services including general consultation services, eye and dental units are funded by the Friendship Luxemburg and provide us the required flexibility to strengthen and consolidate services across the facilities. The Maternity part (CEMoNC) of the Friendship Hospital Ukhiya (FHU) is identified by the health sector as a referral point to provide higher level maternal healthcare services including Caesarean sections for both host communities and Rohingya. The project focuses on protection against illness and equitable access to (Health Sector Indicator 2) health services without any discrimination and specially targeting the most vulnerable groups (women, children, pregnant/lactating women, people with disabilities, the elderly, GBV and conflict-affected survivors). The project also planning to collaboration with the Ukhia Specialized Hospital (UNHCR) by sharing the expertise, accepting as referral point, receiving maternal patient from USH until the maternity part is started there. Friendship will conduct following activities following the MESP:
- Provide essential health services to both host and Rohingya community through 1 primary healthcare center in camp 7 and one 24/7 Secondary level health care services named as Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU).
- Strengthen the facility level preparedness for combating COVID-19 infection among the service providers and beneficiaries at Friendship health facilities (FHU and Primary Health Centres).
- Provide SRH services including safe deliveries (assisting Normal Vaginal Delivery ) and providing basic neonatal care, Ante Natal Care (ANC) and Post Natal Care (PNC) services to both host and Rohingya community.
- Conduct normal vaginal delivery through the PHC and C-section through FHU to ensure safe institutional delivery.
- Provide Family Planning, Menstrual Regulation, management of STIs and Post abortive care services.
- Screen and refer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Medium Acute Malnutrition (MAM) children (6 to 59 months) to Outpatient Therapeutic feeding Program (OTPs).
- Provide Mental Health and Psychosocial Support and Gender Based Violence services including Clinical Management of Rape services and clinical management of Gender Based Violence cases.
- Support Expanded Program on Immunization services in alignment with national program.
- Provide laboratory investigation facility services for the beneficiaries at the health facility level.
- Provide essential medicine and supplements including Vitamin C and Zinc tablet to increase immunity of the beneficiaries
- Coordinate with District and Upazila level COVID-19 Task Force, Health and Family Planning Department, and other stakeholders.
- Conduct awareness-raising sessions including IPC at facility.
- Strengthen feedback mechanism by developing and implementing action plan based on feedback received from camp level health coordination and health committee meeting.
- Community Health Volunteers are identified, trained, and supervised to identify community members in need of referral for PHC services, EPI, and SRH including ANC/PNC with special attention to term mothers for skilled birth attendant deliveries with the support of outreach Midwives.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya crisis has become one of the largest humanitarian crises in recent times, requiring relief and support at an unprecedented speed and scale and causing immense pressure. After fleeing mass-scale violence in Myanmar in August 2017, as of 30 April 2022, 925,380 Rohingya refugees have been living in the world’s largest and most densely populated refugee camp in Cox’s Bazar– one of the poorest districts of Bangladesh (UNHCR Population Factsheet), which is putting a lot of pressure on the already limited and strained infrastructure, services, and environment currently existing. Host communities and local authorities are struggling to cope with this reality as detrimental social, economic and environmental effects of the prolonged displacement continue to increase their sufferings. The Rohingya population in Cox’s Bazar is highly vulnerable, after generations of statelessness and no access to health or education before the severe traumas inflicted by the Myanmar Army, Police and local Rakhine Buddhist mobs. Especially, the people who arrived in Bangladesh since 25 August 2017 came with very few possessions and are still almost entirely reliant on humanitarian assistance for food, shelter, healthcare, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and other lifesaving needs while living in difficult conditions. Of the 33 refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar District , 26 (79%) of them are in Ukhia Upazila, hosting 83% of the total refugee population (902,578) currently residing in different camps in Cox’s Bazar. Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG), led by UNHCR and IOM, continues to work with Bangladesh government and coordinate Rohingya response efforts of all local, national and international NGOs and UN agencies in Cox’s Bazar to arrange essential shelter, food, water, medicine, sanitation, and protection. As in 2021, ensuring adherence to quality, and respectful implementation of the minimum essential service package for health services remains a top priority for 2022. However, the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic in the camp setting continues to create additional challenges to service provision. According to ISCG’s (Inter Sector Coordination Group) Need Analysis and Response Plan, a substantial gap remains in accessing quality healthcare services in the Rohingya camps and host communities, despite four-year continuous interventions by NGOs and donors. The prioritized sector objectives for health by ISCG JRP 2022 was1. Support equitable access to essential primary and secondary healthcare services for Rohingya refugees/FDMN and the host community. (SO3, SO4) 2. Prepare for, prevent, and respond to outbreaks of communicable disease and other health related hazards, including for periods of increased risk during the monsoon and cyclone seasons. (SO3, SO4, SO5) 3. Promote health and wellbeing at individual and community level. (SO1, SO3, SO4, SO5)For continues support to the objectives, quality healthcare services through health facilities for Rohingya and host community is still highly required and these facilities are already recommended by the Health Sector’s rationalization process and health facility gap analysis.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

As per rationalization principles, Friendship is already providing healthcare services from the mentioned facilities (1 PHC at camp 7, eye, dental, limited surgery units and general patient consultation of FHU) to both Host and Rohingya community with equal approach. An integrated approach is essential for fulfilment any project goal as it brings on board all the key stakeholders including the beneficiaries themselves. Sectoral coordination between Health, WASH, Nutrition, site development etc. is of the essence for the development of the community at large. The current Cluster coordination meetings, while efficient, might need further scrutiny in terms of their effectiveness, timeliness and relevance. Friendship has collaboration with other Health facilities inside and outside the camp including Gov Upazila hospital and Sadar Hospital. And also planning to collaborate with UNHCR- Ukhia Specialized Hospital by sharing skilled medical staff including doctor and gynecologist, receiving patient until their maternity center is functional, send patient to USH as referral point etc. Friendship is an important part of the camp coordination and collaborating with other sectors. Also participating to the camp coordination meeting regularly. One of the key stakeholders for the smooth running of operations are the local authorities. Strong government liaison at both the RRRC as well is the Upazila level is crucial for the implementation and sustainability of any project in the Rohingya context. CIC office oversees the appropriate execution and actual effectiveness of proposed plans and to better understand and address gaps in services. Dedicated manpower to coordinate and improve communications for the alignment and development of policy for the benefit of all stakeholders must be ensured. Friendship is providing. Regular updating the CiC offices and communicating for any problem facing by the facilities are the core part of implementing the project into a camp for Friendship

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

-Health interventions are designed to address the health needs of special age groups and gender groups -In collaboration with Handicap International, Friendship staff receive training on disability mainstreaming. -A special cell comprising of facility coordinators at field and head quarter level will be formed to monitor the activities and address any such violations conducted by any service providers across the health service networks like gender issues, protection, misconduct, and forgery etc. A direct phone number and email provided for recording any violations. The human resource department of Friendship take the overall lead in this. -All healthcare facilities of Friendship are disabled friendly, and features added to provide easy access to the vulnerable population, -Proper security ensured to avoid any kind of violence in the premises. At the same time, frontline workers will receive basic training to recognize and safely refer cases of sexual and gender-based violence

3.2 - Gender

Gender have been mainstreamed through below considerations: - Gender sensitive services (ensuring confidentiality, women heath care providers, breast feeding corner, unisex latrine etc.) being ensured. - Special psychosocial counselling will be made available for gender-based violence cases. - In collaboration with Action Against Hunger (ACF), Norwegian Church Aid (NCA)and WHO, training on gender awareness, sensitivity, Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) is provided to Friendship staff with implementation of different relevant organizational policies such as PESA, GBV policies, child protection policy, gender policy, and “Do No Harm Policy”. Regular orientation provided to the staff by human resource department and proper recording for regular follow-up. - The initial induction for the project staff will discuss all the policies (Friendship and GBV and PSEA sector) against gender-based violence and distribute materials mentioning the guidelines to access help.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Friendship has own child safeguarding policy to promote good practice, providing children and young people with appropriate safety/protection whilst in the care of Friendship. - the welfare of the child is paramount - all children, whatever their age, culture, ability, gender, language, racial origin, religious belief and/or sexual identity should be able to participate in a fun and safe environment - taking all reasonable steps to protect children from harm, discrimination and degrading treatment and to respect their rights, wishes and feelings - all suspicions and allegations of poor practice or abuse will be taken seriously and responded to swiftly and appropriately - working in partnership with parents and children is essential for the protection of children - all Friendship employees who work with children will be recruited with regard to their suitability for that responsibility, and will be provided with guidance and/or training in good practice and child protection procedure.

3.4 - Diversity

Friendship will be mainstreaming the diversity including disability to ensure have access to assistance and support on equal access to the support provided by the Friendship Health Facilities. . All the Friendship Health centres were developed as disability friendly centres. The 6-item Washington Group Short Set of Disability Questions (WGQ) was used to identify people with a disability. Ramp, wheel chairs, disability friendly toilet and priority care is intervened in all Friendship health facilities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Community Involvement: The crisis-affected communities have been involved through community consultation and periodic survey findings. Community involvement is a major part of all of Friendship’s interventions. The beneficiaries will be given adequate information during the community consultations about project activities, and the CHWs/CHW Supervisors will take into account their responses. Friendship involves community leaders (majhis) and camp authorities in the process through regular meetings. The leaders and authorities will be informed of planned activities, and their feedback will be analysed and incorporated into the programme as possible. Formation of Health Committee & Monthly Meeting: The health committee’s monthly meeting at camp level is one of the most effective ways of involving beneficiaries throughout the development of the programme and implementation. The member of this committee consists of all sectors working in the camp and government official and FDMN leaders.
