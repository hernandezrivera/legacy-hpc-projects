Project Summary

This project aims to enhance the accessibility of conflict-affected children to quality, safe, and equitable non-formal education in NES in DEZ governorate in Kisreh, Hajin, and Susat sub districts and in NWS in Aleppo governorate in Jabel Saman district and in Idelb governorate in Idelb and Harem sub-districts targeting in total 8500 children and educational personnel in both NES & NWS. The project targets children (7-17 years) dropped out or at risk of dropping out of schools with NFE programs and children (3-7 years) with ECE.
The implementation approach is in-person, and in case of imposing curfew by the authorities in the area/s of implementation due any diseases’ outbreaks, the modality will be shifted to remote learning.
The project has 3 objectives, that are aligned with the HRP Education sector objectives, which are:
1- Increase safe and equitable availability and access to non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth.
2- Enhanced quality of non-formal education for children and youth.
3- Education system is better able to support the education response.
The activities that will be implemented in both NES/ DEZ and NWS/Aleppo & Idelb are quite similar and including the following:
1- Establishing and running 4 TLSs, 1 in each of Jabel Saman, Kisreh, Hajin, and Susat where the NFE, PSS, ECE, and parenting programs will be implemented.
2- Undertaking light rehabilitation to 20 educational facilities where NFE and PSS programs will be implemented. The locations of these schools will be in the following sub districts: Kisreh, Atareb, Daret Azza, Idleb, Bennsh, Armanaz, Hajin, and Susat in Aleppo & Idelb governorates in NWS and DEZ governorate in NES.
3- Support the 20 educational facilities with operational cost, IT equipment, solar system for lighting, sterilizers, and PPE for educational staff and beneficiaries.
4- Conducting 10-days Back to Learning (BTL) Campaigns in each sub district in order to introduce the project activities and invite the children’s caregivers to register their children to the services available, note that the BTL campaign will include conducting diagnostic tests for children registered in order to be able to select the most in need children for NFE programs.
5- Implementing ALP, remedial, and SLP NFE programs for children dropped out of schools and these children will undertake pre and post-tests to measure the impact of the NFE programs implemented and assess their educational improvement.
In average, 11 classrooms will be utilized in each of the 20 schools, 10 for the implementation of NFE programs, and 1 classroom for admin; therefore, 10 teachers (1 teacher per classroom) will be providing NFE in each of the 20 schools during the afternoon shift.
While in the 4 TLSs, the space is smaller, so 4 rooms in each TLS will be used to implement the NFE programs but during the morning and afternoon shift, so 8 teachers will be recruited for each of the TLSs, 4 for the morning shift and 4 for the afternoon shift. Total number of teachers for TLSs is 8*4=32 teachers.
6- Targeting children attending the NFE with structured and sustained PSS programs in schools and TLSs such as I DEAL and Little Fellow.
7- Implementing ECE for children (3-7 years) using 2 approaches: static-based in the TLSs and schools, and through community-based outreach approach in order to reach a wider scope of beneficiaries geographically (those living in distant locations from the TLSs/schools) and physically (EX: people with disabilities facing barriers to come to the TLSs/schools).
8- Providing transportation for children with disabilities (CwD) from and to the educational facilities to attend the activities in the schools and TLSs.
9- Distributing monthly incentives and building the capacity of 252 Educational personnel on EIE and INEE, school and classroom management, SEL, PSS, and PFA.
10- Providing the children attending the NFE & PSS programs with school bags, stationery, and curriculums.

LH 1: Med-IMP 1: High-MM 1: Bahar will ensure all learning facilities have Covid-19 safety protocols and practiced resources in place, and will use NES EWG C19 guide. Protocols include identification, prevention measures, awareness-raising and referral system to the health center. Resources include distribution of PPEs i.e. hand sanitizer, masks, and regular ventilation of schools 2-Insufficient water and sanitation facilities result in an unwillingness or inability to use WASH facilities or mixing of sexes and ages. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Bahar has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that usually is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying all the related procedures. Bahar will ensure the field teams attend PSEA\safeguarding training and refreshing sessions provided by Bahar internally or externally. Bahar’s PSEA focal points responsibility is to ensure training is delivered to the project staff, informative materials (either soft copies in case of online or in-place approach of implementation) are distributed to both staff and beneficiaries, and updating and develop the PSEA policies including the complaints pathway/reporting mechanism regularly. The MEAL team will monitor if any danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings in order to take the appropriate measures. Bahar team will also inform the community, particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and its aim in order to mitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: COVID-19 and other health pandemics/infections imposes lack of students’ access to schools due to curfew restrictions, home lockdown etc... Mitigation: Bahar adopts other implementation modalities, including distant learning or SLP programs, which require less physical contact between students and children.Risk 2: Bab Alhawa border is closed due to security reasons which cause lack of materials in the market. Mitigation: Sending the needed resources when the border is functioning., Coordinating with authorities to be informed before the closing of the border., Checking for alternatives inside Syria, Using another border crossing.Risk 3: Host community rejects Bahar’s presence in schools where NFE programs are conducted (due to a lack of understanding of selection criteria, or other factors). Mitigation: regular discussion with parents, teachers, social counsellors; clearly communicated and transparent selection criteria for NFE education, as well as the objectives of the NFE programs; close coordination and engagement with local educational authorities.Risk 4: students might not go to school due to long distances. Mitigation: ensure BTLs are implemented in communities’ levels and they include good information sharing, ensure that residency information is collected in the registration process and distance is one of the selection criteria, consider having transportations service as a project activity.
