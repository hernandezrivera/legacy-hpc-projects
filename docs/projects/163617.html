Project Summary

This project will support the most vulnerable households and individuals impacted by the ongoing conflict including newly displaced, survivors of rescue at sea, refugees and migrants to meet their urgent basic needs through the provision of unrestricted multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA). For households, multi-month MPCA will be provided to ensure that the most vulnerable will be able to meet their basic needs without relying on negative coping strategies such assistance will be delivered in areas where markets are functioning, accessible, and safe delivery of cash will be possible. MPCA can be deployed rapidly to respond to the basic needs of socioeconomically vulnerable households in a manner that allows choice and dignity.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict – which has escalated again since April 2019 - has resulted in an increase in the scale of displacement and a protracted humanitarian crisis. In turn this has driven irregular migration, disrupted socioeconomic infrastructures and generated a significant financial liquidity crisis. Combined, this has resulted in substantial limitations of Libyans and non-Libyans access to basic services including financial services. These are expected to continue to prevent the most vulnerable groups within the conflict-affected populations from accessing basic goods and services including food, shelter, medicine, transportation etc. in 2020.According to the 2019 MSNA preliminary findings of households who have enough money available to meet 30 days of needs in Libya are as high as 42% and this is even higher for some regions such the South with 68% and some Mantikas/districts in South Region such as Murzuq are as high as 89%. Some of these challenges include salary/wage not being paid regularly, low salary/wage, households who are unable to withdraw enough money from banks, and the lack of work opportunity to mention a few. In addition, most of the Mantikas in the South and West are currently using crisis level coping strategies such as taking up an additional jobs (32%), borrowing money (48%), selling the productive assets of the household (32%) reducingand reduced expense on health care or medicine (21%).Access to finance to meet basic needs is critical in some of the locations and for some of the most vulnerable individuals. For instance, Sebha Mantika in the South currently hosts 21,100 IDPs and 2,085 returnees according to the IOM-Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) R26. According to MSNA findings, 91% of the interviewed households in Sebha mentioned access to cash among their top priories where this was similarly high for IDPs (96%), returnees (80% and even non-displaced (90%). According to the same report, accessing food was the most frequently-reported reason for using livelihoods coping mechanisms, among HHs that used at least one such coping mechanism in the 30 days prior to data collection (95%) IDP(97%) Returnee(100%) Non-Displaced (94%).

2 - Activities

IOM will provide unconditional cash assistance packages (one-off) to support the vulnerable populations to meet their basic needs. The project will prioritize for assistance vulnerable Libyans (IDPs and returnees) as well as vulnerable migrants. This project will support 2,904 Households - 976 IDPs, 195 returnees, 1,733 migrants (total of 11,195 individuals).

4 - Protection risk analysis

Proper identification and prioritization of needs of vulnerable individuals for targeting may be very critical for cash- based assistance as this may pose some potential risks including stigma and discrimination. In the case of migrants, this may be a serious concern involving potential harm from locals witnessing such assistance to migrants. Funds in the bank are insufficient to accommodate the request from the beneficiaries due to liquidity shortage. (IDPs) as well as delays of funds in the banks causing long queues. (IDPs)

5 - Mitigation measures

In order to mitigate such protection risks this project will apply usage of Vulnerability Scorecard recommended and developed by the Cash and Market Working Group (CMWG) as well as application of protection monitoring interviews and protection and financial risk mitigation matrices shared by UNHCR.IOM will ensure adequate crowd control measures are in place during assistance. IOM will collaborate with the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI), through which markets and good are being monitored on monthly basis, CMWG are setting thresholds at which the transferred values should change meet the changing market conditions. IOM will implement vulnerability verification criteria through vulnerability scorecards. It will also implement internal controls that promote transparency and accountability (for example: Penalties/ information verification/supervision of interviews). IOM will implement appropriate monitoring and reporting procedures that would enable faster shift from cash to in-kind in the event of high inflation. IOM will ensure real time monitoring of cash withdrawals and timely cash call forward IOM will ensure signing data protection agreements with service providers. IOM will also consider to reduce visibility at time of selection, distribution, monitoring to prevent stigmatization. In order to reduce potential tensions IOM will complement to community support projects, inclusion of a proportion of hosting vulnerable (IDPs/Returnees) families in the assistance scheme. IOM will ensure timely information sharing on transfer dates and amounts within the minimum notice working days. IOM will also collaborate with the CMWG on creating contingency distribution days post arrival of PoCs when needed and also explore opportunities to apply hybrid interventions and modality changes where applicable

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

IOM will engage beneficiaries during needs identification, planning, implementation and monitoring of this project to promote beneficiaries’ participation, ownership and decision making. The implementation of this project will be guided by vulnerability assessments to identify highly vulnerable households and individuals who need support to meet their urgent basic needs. The vulnerability of the household will be determined based on the vulnerability score card harmonized by the CMWG. IOM will also conduct post-distribution monitoring to assess beneficiary satisfaction with assistance. In coordination with CMWG as may be required, IOM will periodically revise the cash transfer value based on periodic joint expenditure and income monitoring. IOM will also take into consideration beneficiaries’ preferences with modalities and transfer mechanisms. IOM will also engage the CMWG to apply the comment feedback mechanisms adopted by the working group to receive suggestions and complaints from beneficiaries to make timely adjustments.
