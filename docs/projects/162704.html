Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to the reduction of malnutrition cases and preventing malnutrition depletion of vulnerable communities through responding to immediate nutrition needs of the target population in South Darfur,East Darfur , South Kordofan and Blue Nile States for both male and female. The main objective of the project is to contribute to improve Nutrition and health status of vulnerable conflict-affected populations including Children under five years Old boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women as well as general wellbeing in South Darfur,East Darfur , South Kordofan and Blue Nile States through providing quality CMAM full packages to malnourished PLW and U5 During project period of December January 2020 to December 2020, World Vision Sudan plans to continue lifesaving Nutrition activities in the above stated areas as well as expand community based management of Acute malnutrition through prevention, integrated Community case management and improved access to quality treatment .
World Vision will work in collaboration with State Ministry of Health and Federal Ministry of Health at state and national level respectively.
In addition, World Vision Sudan is also working towards empowerment of families and communities through strengthening & capacity building of community health workers/networks in health promotion and prevention for both male and female
In all of its activities, World Vision Sudan works to promote adherence to the national standards and policy frameworks established by the Ministry of Health.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition constitutes a significant public health challenge in Sudan where an estimated 2.7 million children under 5 suffer from acute malnutrition. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate is 14.1 percent, including a severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate of 4.39% and a moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) rate of 9.7%. The percentage of underweight children is 28.9% while those suffering with chronic malnutrition (stunting) is 36.5%. Where pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are concerned, approximately 805,000 women suffer with acute malnutrition (HNO/HRP Nutrition sector 2020)South Kordufan GAM rate is 15.41, SAM rate is 7.28 and MAM rate is 8.13, South Darfur GAM rate is 30.03, SAM rate is 16.43 and MAM rate is 13.6. Blue Nile State GAM rate is 11.14, SAM rate is 6.4 and MAM rate is 5.74 and East Darfur GAM rate is 30, SAM rate is 16.88 and MAM rate is 13.12, which are above the WHO threshold in all targeted states (S3M II, 2018).An estimated 2.7 million children under 5 suffer from acute malnutrition and 5.7 million are food insecure. An estimated 57 percent of children with acute malnutrition (SAM or MAM) fail to receive nutrition treatment through the therapeutic and supplementary nutrition interventions, due to lack of funding, inadequate resources and a lack of trained health staff (HRP/HNO 2020)By August 2019, 5.8 million people were food insecure and in need of food and livelihood assistance. The 5.8 million people included 1 million in IPC phase 4 (humanitarian emergency) and 4.8 million in IPC phase 3 (acute livelihood and food insecurity crises). Many families were forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms including reduced food intake and smaller food portions thereby decreasing their energy and nutrient intake. Other practices that families resorted to included, liquidation of livelihood assets, early marriage or school drop-out to engage in work. Where children under 2 were concerned, many states failed to achieve age-appropriate dietary diversity for example, the rate of children in East Darfur who failed to achieve age-appropriate dietary diversity was 89.5 percent (S3M II, 2018). Stunting (low height-for-age) among children under 5 is also high. According to the S3M II (2018) survey, approximately 805,000 PLW need nutrition support due to acute malnutrition. Regarding coverage of nutrition treatment services, access to nutrition facilities is challenging as nutrition sites are unevenly distributed geographically with some localities lacking services; 30 percent of the population lives more than 5 KM from a health/nutrition facility. Consequently, many women are challenged with traveling long distances to nutrition facilities due to absence of affordable transport (HRP/HNO 2020) The targeted localities have a GAM and SAM rate ranking of either very high or severe as well as nutrition specific and sensitive indicators in access to food, hygiene, sanitation, safe water and social protection coverage. Most localities are within severity ranking 4, these include, Al Gezeira, Blue Nile, all Darfur states, Gedaref, Kassala, Khartoum, River Nile, Northern State, Sennar, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, White Nile. Most of these states also experience drivers of food insecurity including, civil unrest, local conflict, deterioration in the macroeconomic situation and flooding (HNO/HRP 2020)It is anticipated that malnutrition rates in 2020 among children under 5 and PLW will increase and an estimated 3.2 million people will need nutrition assistance. Approximately 2.7 million children under 5 will need treatment for acute malnutrition out of which an estimated 472,000 will likely develop SAM and of these an anticipated 15 percent will develop complications and require inpatient treatment. An estimated 3.8 million PLW will require IYCF counselling services to support adequate maternal diet along with appropriate IYCF practices. A further 805,000 PLW will require treatment for acute malnutrition (S3M II, 2018).

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will apply different standards and best practices in emergency response including SPHERE standards, LEGS, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Natural Resource Management (NRM) and "Do No Harm'' approaches in the implementation of this project activities. Careful assessment of the local context, examining how humanitarian assistance would interact with the current situation in the target areas, and creation of options for project revision to improve its quality will be carried out. this assessment will include analysis of: (i) dividers and sources of tensions between groups: systems and institutions; attitudes and actions; values and interests; experiences; symbols and occasions; etc.; (ii) connectors across sub-groups and the local capacities for peace; (iii) the project in terms of why; where; what; when; with whom; by whom and how and its impact on dividers/tensions and connectors/local capacities for peace. The partners will work together to ensure that the project is implemented in a way that can strengthen local capacities for peace, build on connectors that bring communities together, and reduce the divisions and sources of tensions that can lead to destructive conflict. To ensure that no negative impact should result from this project the following will be undertaken: just distribution of resources, creating or strengthening networks of relationships across divisions, strengthening good governance, the use of participatory processes for decision making, supporting traditional or indigenous mechanisms for conflict resolution and reconciliation, inclusion of diversity of ethnic or religious groups, gender, and youth in project activities and leadership structures. Selection of beneficiaries will be performed with due consideration of “Do No Harm” principles, ensuring fair representation of different vulnerable groups regardless of their ethnicity, religion or gender. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be socialized with communities and ample opportunity will be provided to raise concerns or complaints. Selection criteria for all project activities will focus on female-headed HHs, child-headed HHs, orphans, elders and persons with disabilities. The population of the targeted localities includes a high proportion of vulnerable individuals; targeted HHs might have lost a family members during the conflict, mainly men, which explains the considerable number of women-headed HHs among the targeted communities; those women have to act as the breadwinners for their children and often their elders on their own. Enabling them a safe and dignified access to basic services is part of the protection mainstreaming in this program. In this action, efforts will be made to secure maximum care for mothers and children through ensuring better access to nutrition and health service potable water and quality sanitation. Water committees, water quality monitors and hygiene and sanitation committees will also be aware of protection protocol.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

This will be through Participation and involvement of the female and male targeted affected population in design, monitoring and evaluation of this project, establishment of strong communication systems to ensure feedback is received, processed and responded to timely and effectively. The project approach focuses on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elders, persons with disabilities among other vulnerable and or marginalized groups. Community participation will be promoted throughout all the stages of the program; communities’ feedback was the most significant element for the needs assessments conducted prior to proposing this action- the proposed intervention is based on communities’ needs. Community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure that the project is well understood by target communities and raise the communities’ awareness with regard to their participatory roles and responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the program partners toward them. Participation will be promoted through the involvement of beneficiaries in the formation of community committees and planning the implementation schedule. The community committees will be created from female and male to organize and mobilize the beneficiaries, participate in the development of beneficiary selection criteria, distribution of inputs and monitoring and evaluation of the intervention. The project partners will work with the target communities and established relationships and inclusive community structures which will facilitate the establishment of effective feedback mechanisms. In consultation with community members, taking into consideration literacy rates, accessibility to communication devices and the presence of suitable sites for establishment of complaint / suggestion boxes; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response. Good internal procedures and confidential reporting systems will be implemented to ensure confidentiality and safety of all community members. The communities’ feedback will be acquired and verified through different approaches including regular monitoring activities and feedback, complaints and response mechanisms including focus group discussions, semi-structured interviews, key informant interviews, community meetings, establishment of complaint / suggestion boxes in project sites and communicating the partners’ contacts and addresses with community members and committees. Feedback and complaints would be reflected by the field staff /project management to the attention of program management, partners or the donor . Information collected and analyzed through monitoring and feedback mechanisms will be communicated to and discussed with the communities as appropriate.
