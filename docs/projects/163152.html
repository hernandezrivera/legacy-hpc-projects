Project Summary

This project will improve access to clean water for 30 critically isolated communities of Ekondo-Titi, Bamusso, and Mbonge Sub-Divisions with water point rehabilitation, hygiene promotion and distribution of WASH kits. These were among the first communities to be affected by the crisis but their extreme remoteness has prevented them from receiving humanitarian support.
Activities:
1) Access mediation
Introductory meetings will take place at community level to ensure acceptance for Reach Out’s activities and respect for neutrality and safety during distribution.
2) WASH Assessment
600 KAP surveys will be carried out in the 2 Divisions, at baseline and end line, at least 45 water sources will be analyzed and documented with support from the WASH cluster at each step of the process. The study will inform the water points to be carried out by the project. Community structures and capacities will also be analyzed in order to inform partners who can tap into local knowledge to carry out WASH projects.
3) Creation of Hygiene Committees
Village assemblies will be called to present the project and create Hygiene Committees. This committee is a voluntary unit that supports targeting and distribution of WASH kits, Hygiene promotion, and carries out surveillance and maintenance of sanitation facilities and water points in the community.
These first village meetings also serve to identify the different WASH challenges of men, women, boys, girls, and the elderly, and how they should be appropriately served by the project.
4) Creation of Monitoring Committees (MCs)
Monitoring Committees receive and review complaints and carry out validation of beneficiary lists.
Gender and ethnic balance between the FDC and MC will be requested.
5) Targeting
• Geographical targeting, sites are selected based on objective data (remoteness, level of displacement, population data).
• Distribution Committees collect beneficiary data.
• Validation is carried out by Reach Out and the Monitoring Committee by visiting a sample of prospective beneficiaries and checking consistency.
• Validated data is typed into a scoring database that gives out the priority list.
• Community validates final list.
6) WASH Kit Distribution (4,000 kits, targeting 20,000 persons at least)
1) Market Assessments
2) Call for tenders
3) Review Panel and selection of service providers
4) Purchase of commodities
5) Quality control
6) Packaging and labeling
7) Transportation to Ekondo-Titi
8) Warehousing
9) Secondary transportation to Distribution sites.
Each family will receive a basic WASH Kit, with 2 15L buckets, 2 20L jerrycans, 10 cubes of soap, 15 reams of aquatabs, 2 1 L jars, and 5 plastic cups.
7) Habilitation of Water Points
30 water points will be developed, these will mostly be habilitation works to improve access to water from the local springs.
8) Monitoring and evaluation.
Embedded in all activities, independent calls, verification rounds and focus group discussions are also organized.
Integration with other sectors:
1) Reach Out will identify sources of funding for mosquito nets and try to accompany the WASH kits with 2-3 mosquito nets per family, as this is a critical priority of hard-to-reach areas that is also life-saving at a low cost. Reach Out will set up a crowdfunding project at GlobalGiving.org, a platform through which the organization has raised more than 110,000 USD in the past years.
2) To ensure cases of abuse are reported and attended, distribution staff and Monitoring Committees will be trained on Child Protection, GBV guidelines, Psychosocial First Aid and the local referral pathways.

Besoins / Needs

The international Office for Migration carried out a Multi-Sector Needs Assessment in the South-West and North-West, aiming to extract key needs of all communities of these two regions. Within Ekondo-Titi, Mbonge, and Bamusso Sub-Divisions, 92% of communities identified access to clean water as their main priority, and only 1.5% acknowledged having received any sort of WASH support. Moreover, 73% of communities expressed having no access to jerry cans, and 26% no access to soap. Displaced persons have created self-settled communities in the forest, where they access water from natural springs. Water coming out of these natural springs is fairly clean, but has insufficient flow, gets contaminated before collection, and is hard to collect in a clean manner. Also, most sources are often very hard to access. Natural springs are the principal source of water in Bamusso, Ekondo-Titi, and Mbonge, 70% of communities have natural springs as their main source of water. Village councils of targeted communities have openly requested support from Reach Out in several occasions since the beginning of the crisis, including WASH kits and water point rehabilitation. During the elaboration of the Council Development plan of Bamusso, in 2017, 75% of communities requested the development of a water point as a priority project. With more than 2 years into the crisis, it seems evident self-settled communities are no longer a temporary arrangement, but rather permanent settlements that still lack any sort of infrastructure or services. It is therefore of capital importance to guarantee sustained access to water in all the communities of the South-West. The on-going cholera outbreak in Bakassi Health District, with 278 reported cases, threatens to worsen the situation. Bamusso, Ekondo-Titi and Mbonge, are all risk-zones surrounding Bakassi, and their critically low WASH standards are breeding ground for water-borne diseases. This project will increase the resilience of displaced communities of these three Sub-Divisions, therefore contributing to the prevention and mitigation of a cholera outbreak spreading through Ndian and Meme. Lastly, this project aims to contribute to the acute lack of comprehensive data on WASH environment in the South-West. The WASH Cluster settled in the South-West and North-West in February 2019, in this period, numerous assessments have been conducted in localized communities, mostly of Fako. But most of these fail to be comprehensive, capture detailed information about water sources, and reach the hardest to reach communities. Reach Out has identified more than 50 cases of GBV in Ekondo-Titi in the year 2019, mostly physical violence. About 40% of the female adult population is illiterate, as a result of the prioritization of male education. Women marry early and have an average of 5 children. They access land, but they do not have legal ownership, limiting their access to capital. Women don´t farm the more lucrative cash crops (cocoa and palm) but rather focus on subsistence agriculture and kitchen gardening, and use unprofessional methods with insufficient inputs. It has been observed that females of all ages are hosting displaced children, adding to their responsibilities. Children and women are most often in charge of collecting and transporting water from water points to the households, and often are put at danger of injury by moving through the steep roads that lead to water points. Youths are suffering of massive unemployment and teenage pregnancy, and need support in order to be included in community power structures. The elderly are also having specific needs and conditions, literacy is not common among elderly persons of Ndian, particularly women.
