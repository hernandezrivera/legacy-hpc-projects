Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to improve the efficacy and accuracy of
targeted humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations (focusing on new and
protracted IDPs, returnees and refugees) by providing humanitarian partners with
accurate and timely information (number of affected population, vulnerability status,
demographic data, place of origin, place of displacement).
The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) is IOM’s primary set of tools used to track
and monitor displacement and population mobility. It is designed to regularly and
systematically capture, process and disseminate information to enable a better
understanding of movements and evolving needs of populations on the move, and to
provide the information needed by humanitarian partners to adequately address the
life saving and protection needs of mobile populations. Detailed information will be
provided on the availability and gaps of basic services within settlements, and will
alert relevant responsible parties on where the most acute needs are. Information on
population movement will also be provided through the early warning system that
will be established through this project.
The information will be disseminated through the DTM global website on a quarterly basis and coordinated through IMWG and ISCG.
This project contributes to the Multi Year Humanitarian Response Plan Outcome 1:
“Populations affected by natural or man made disasters receive timely assistance
during and in the aftermath of the shock."

Needs

Ongoing conflict between government armed forces and rebel groups continues to cause widespread displacement in Sudan, while at the same time challenging climatic and socio cultural conditions have led to high levels of food insecurity and malnutrition encouraging high internal mobility. According to the Government of Sudan (GoS), there are a total of 2.2 million IDPs in government controlled, conflict affected areas across Sudan. IOM has registered over a 365,406 returnees in Sudan since 2015. Most of the returnees had fled their homes in 2013 and were returning from Chad and from IDP camps within the state. IOM has been registering this population in South Kordofan and East Darfur, and the information collected allows humanitarian actors to understand the impact of this population on IDP and host communities, and to plan and advocate to meet these additional needs.In 2018, IOM were able to register and verify 66,227 households composed of 397,159 displaced individuals / affected population, returnees, South Sudanese refugees and other migrants across North, South, East, Central, and West Darfur, South and West Kordofan, and Abyei, enabling the humanitarian community to provide well targeted humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations. The situation for IDPs, returnees and affected populations continues to be critical and reveals a serious need to ensure that data is collected through the DTM. More specifically, the sustained high levels of displacement coupled with reduced levels of funding in Sudan require the timely identification of vulnerable populations and their needs to ensure that emergency response is delivered in an accurate and strategic manner.In 2018, IOM established over 32 emergency tracking hubs and flow monitoring points with focal persons in a variety of strategic locations, where high levels of mobility were identified. These tracking hubs / focal points worked as early warning systems, informing humanitarian actors to prepare for potential displacements. The tracking hubs also enabled ‘deregistration’ of IDPs after an emergency by identifying populations that returned shortly after a crisis has ended. It is expected that in 2019, there will be continued returns to areas of origin requiring DTM to collect information on movements, needs and vulnerabilities of IDP returnees to be shared with the humanitarian community to provide support for sustainable returns and durable solutions.The situation for IDPs, returnees and affected populations continues to be critical and reveals a serious need to ensure that data is collected through the DTM. Activities

Main Output:Accurate and reliable DTM registration information on affected populations is disseminated to the humanitarian agencies and sectors. Activities:1. Use of DTM’s emergency tracking system to monitor ongoing emergencies, or known high risk locations where population movements occur regularly/are predicted to occur.2. Flow monitoring in ongoing emergency situations by conducting headcounts of affected populations or displaced persons, in transit or upon arrival in secured areas, via use of emergency and temporary flow monitoring points established as a result of emergency tracking.3. Registration and verification of affected populations, to collect, analyze and share accurate, disaggregated data on displaced population at locations identified via emergency tracking or reported to IOM by A/HCT, GoS counterparts, or other partners.4. Registration and verification of returnees, to collect, analyze and share accurate, disaggregated data on previously displaced people who have now returned, at their places of origin.5. Site assessment at the site of the displacement or return as a follow up to registration/verification, fashioned around the principle of rapid needs assessment to identify the urgent needs of the affected population and gaps in the availability of vital services at the site. 6. Comprehensive site/camp assessments to support programming of life sustaining assistance in recovery and transition context. 7. Trainings and refreshers on standard DTM tools and protection practices for IOM enumerators. 8. Regular DTM statistical reports to be shared with GoS, humanitarian actors, relevant sectors and sector partners.9.Collect data on malnutrition through Middle and Upper Arm Circumference screenings in order to determine malnutrition levels in children and send results to UNICEF.10. Undertake information campaigns and sensitization meetings on the aim and modalities of DTM prior to data collection exercises.
