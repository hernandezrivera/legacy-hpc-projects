Objectives

World Concern plans to continue running quality emergency and preventative nutrition programming in the 3 established TSFP/BSFP nutrition sites in coordination with the existing OTP partners in these catchment areas. These nutrition sites were established in 2018 in partnership with WFP as part of the scale up to improve coverage in locations with no or limited access to TSFP/BSFP. These nutrition sites are in Camp 3 (which serves a catchment population across the northern part of Camp 3 and the northern part of Camp 1W), Camp 6 and Camp 16 (covering the entire camps in these two locations) with a total catchment population of 70,073 individuals. In addition to the TSFP and BSFP at these sites World Concern is piloting Community Management of At-Risk Mothers and Infants (C-MAMI). World Concern will also continue with a mobile CMAM team that is able to provide capacity building support to other nutrition partners as well as support with emergency preparedness prior to the 2019 monsoon/cyclone season as well as assist with mobile responses if needed during this season. 1) To reduce excess mortality and morbidity among boys and girls under 5 years old, PLW and other vulnerable groups through provision of life-saving interventions to treat Severe and Moderate Acute Malnutrition.2) To reduce the burden of malnutrition among boys, girls, PLWs and other vulnerable groups through the strengthening and scale up of malnutrition prevention interventions.

Needs

Within the Rohingya Kutupulong makeshift camps the overall situation has stablized however refugees within the camps are dependent on general food distributations and nutrition programming as they have limited options for livelihoods. Although there are now more services available many are still clustered along the main roads resulting in poor access to services for populations living in camps that are not along these main roads.The most recent SMART survey conducted in April/May 2018 found that the acute malnutrition rates decreased since 2017 but with GAM at 12% (2% SAM, 10% MAM) within the makeshift camps combined with aggravating factors such as outbreaks, displacements and inadequate shelter along with poor infant and young child feeding (ICYF) practices (only 50% reported exclusive breastfeeding of infants under 6 months) there is still need for emergency and preventative nutrition services. The recent coverage survey conducted in June/July 2018 found that access to nutrition services in the makeshift camps is poor with low coverage of CMAM nutrition services (<60% for OTP and <40% for TSFP across the camps). There are challenges in referral and coordination as CMAM services in most locations are not integrated and different partners are providing OTP and others providing TSFP/BSFP.

Activities or outputs

Outputs and activities:Output 1: Functional screening and referral pathways established and operational for admission to nutrition services through integrated services at nutrition sites for children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating womenOutput 1 activities:• Continue running TSFP/BSFP nutrition sites in coordination with the OTP partners within Camps 3/1W, 6 and 16 and scale up to integrated CMAM services as per Nutrition sector strategy as feasible within 2019• Screen and enroll 14,997 children 6-59 months (7,648 boys and 7,349 girls) into TSFP or BSFP based on weight for height and MUAC and refer cases of SAM to the referral OTP • Screen and enroll 2,372 PLW into TSFP or BSFP based on MUAC• Screen infants 0-6 months and their mothers (1,927) and enroll into C-MAMI based on C-MAMI guideline criteria • Strengthen existing referral mechanisms between OTP partners to refer and receive cases and with health, MHPSS and protection partners• Train and provide on the job training for the national nutrition staff to provide quality nutrition services (i.e. CMAM principles and understanding of malnutrition, anthropometric measurements, treatment guidelines, key messaging, humanitarian standards, psychosocial and GBV awareness and referrals)• Train and provide on the job training for C-MAMI staff• Continue with established mobile CMAM team with the capacity to support other nutrition partners with additional support or training for key components of CMAM and/or plans for emergency preparedness and the capacity to support continuity of CMAM services following a natural disaster Output 2: Increased access to knowledge and support to carry out best practices in maternal nutrition, infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and key health and hygiene practicesOutput 2 activities:• Provide key nutrition and IYCF messaging and IYCF counselling to mothers of children from 0-23 months based on IYCF guidelines both in the facility and at household level• Provide key nutrition IYCF messages during group site sessions in the community and at the household• Train and provide on the job training for IYCF counsellors• Train community outreach volunteers (CNVs) and nutrition staff in contextualized approaches for delivering IYCF messages • Conduct community based participatory research to target BCC
