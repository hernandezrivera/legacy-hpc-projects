Project Summary

The project will focus on expanding the provision of protection services in Asharfieh, Mar Mkhayel, Qarantina, Sin El Fil and Msaytbeh and strengthening mechanisms for the protection against sexual exploitation and abuse in affected zones in Beirut and Mount Lebanon in response to the Beirut explosions. The trauma of the Beirut blasts necessitates a holistic response that includes customized protection services, including immediate free and high quality therapy services to support the mental health and psycho-social needs of persons affected, provided in spaces that are safe and accessible. UN Women in partnership with ABAAD will provide protection services. Specialized psycho-social support (both in-person and virtual sessions) and psychological first aid will be provided to a total of 2,354 women and girls affected by the explosion, with GBV case management services to at least 80 women and girls. Cash assistance for those affected by the explosion will be streamlined through the Food Security and Shelter sectors. However, bearing in mind multidimensional nature of needs, protection partners will be undertaking a significant part of the identification of the people in need and ensuring relevant services are provided through CPM and referrals. Therefore, through the work proposed UN Women and Abaad will be coordinating closely between sectors and partners in vulnerability assessments, identification and referrals to the relevant protection and food security partners – specifically ACTED in Basta 66-69 and Karm el Zeytun.

1 - Needs

In the immediate term, women face heightened risks in accessing humanitarian assistance. Displacement, overcrowded temporary shelters, lack of privacy, lack of lighting, limited and unsegregated wash facilities, and other factors will contribute to violence against women and hinder women’s access to necessary services. The trauma of the Beirut blasts necessitates a holistic response that includes customized protection services, including immediate free and high quality therapy services to support the mental health and psycho-social needs of persons affected, provided in spaces that are safe and accessible. An analysis of the first set of raw data collected via the MSNA led by LRC shows that there is a large number of female headed households (52% FHH to 48% MHH) residing in the affected areas and that many women especially elderly are living alone, 12% of women reported living alone, of which three quarters were over the age of 60. The data also shows that female headed households are more likely than to report physical and mental disabilities (59% vs 41%). Protection mechanisms are currently severely limited and safe spaces have been partially destroyed by the explosion. All these issues show the urgent need to ensure that safe spaces are available and that protection services are accessible for those affected by the explosions. In addition, there is an urgent need for an Inter-Agency PSEA reporting hotline, given the proliferation of humanitarian actors, limited accountability mechanisms for abuse, and rising risks of exploitation amidst sky-rocketing poverty. In a country where in 2019 23% of women stated that they had either experienced ‘sextortion’ (coerced sex in exchange for public services) or knew someone who had, the threats of exploitation and abuse are severe, especially in light of increasing economic vulnerabilities. This will be particularly true for female headed households and domestic workers and will be exacerbated by law enforcement mechanisms being overwhelmed by the disaster.

2 - Activities

1. Infrastructural support to three Social Development Centers in Beirut to enable women to access safe spaces and specialist services (5000 women and girls) 2. Specialized in-person psycho-social support (PSS) consisting of 6-8 individual and group sessions for 250 women and girls and virtual PSS consisting of 4 sessions for 480 as well as Psychological First Aid (PFA) for 1000 women and girls, reaching a total of 1600 women and girls caught in the blast and at risk/surviving GBV3. Case management to women and girls at risk or survivors of GBV (80 women and girls) 4. In coordination with the In-Country Network on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA ICN) support to establishment and staffing of an inter-agency PSEA hotline (500 persons reaching the hotline) 5. PSEA awareness raising (of hotline and support services) for affected women, men, girls and boys and for front-line actors (15000)
