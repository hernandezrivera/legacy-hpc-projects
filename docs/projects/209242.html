Project Summary

This project aims to improve the food security and livelihoods of 45,700 Sudanese (50 % female, 50 % male) who have been affected by the ongoing conflict in the country. Specifically, the project target areas include North Darfur, North Kordofan, South Kordofan, White Nile, and Kassala States. These areas have experienced significant conflict and displacement, making it challenging for communities to sustain themselves.
The project will provide life-saving food assistance to vulnerable populations and emergency agricultural and non-agricultural support to affected households to protect and enhance their productive assets. This will be achieved by providing cash or vouchers for full rations to 3,100 individuals, supplying agricultural support such as seeds and tools to 15,000 farmers, providing livestock support to 3,000 small-scale producers, offering veterinary support to 3,000 project participants, distributing livelihood cash or voucher support to 17,100 households, and providing skills development and on- and off-farm income-generating livelihoods support to 4,500 individuals.
The project will prioritize the most vulnerable groups within these communities, including women, children, women-headed households, youth, elders, people with disabilities, and internally displaced persons (IDPs). These activities are designed to provide targeted assistance to those who require it the most and help establish resilience and self-sufficiency in affected communities.
Moreover, the project is designed to contribute to gender equality, including across age groups significantly including children, youth, adolescent girls, people with disabilities, women-headed households, IDPs, and Returnees. It also aims to have a medium environmental impact while implementing mitigation measures. Specific interventions include providing treatment and livestock vaccination campaigns to protect the animal assets of households in selected localities.
To ensure protection mainstreaming during the project's design and implementation, Plan International Sudan is committed to ensuring the safety and dignity of all individuals involved in its programs and interventions. The organization has a 'Do No Harm' policy in place to protect the safety and dignity of children and young people, with a zero-tolerance approach to abuse and exploitation. High standards of safeguarding and prevention of sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse are rigorously implemented, especially in emergencies. Plan International Sudan also communicates safeguarding policies through various means to ensure widespread awareness, accounting for different literacy levels.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Plan International Sudan is an organization that is committed to ensuring the safety and dignity of all individuals who come into contact with its programs and interventions. The organization places a high priority on identifying, preventing, and mitigating any potential risks or negative effects that may arise during its interventions, with a particular focus on girls, boys, children, and young people who may be vulnerable.To achieve this, Plan International Sudan has put in place a 'Do no Harm' policy that is designed to ensure that the safety and dignity of children and young people are protected at all times. This policy includes a zero-tolerance approach to abuse and exploitation, which is articulated in the organization's safeguarding policies for children and young people.Plan International Sudan works closely with local authorities to ensure that its activities are context-appropriate and adhering to human rights principles. The organization also maintains high standards of safeguarding and preventing sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse, especially in emergency situations.To ensure that all staff, partners, project participants, and communities are aware of the safeguarding policies, Plan International Sudan communicates information through various means to reach a wider audience, accounting for different literacy levels. The organization is committed to respecting project participants, particularly children and young people, and their safety and protection when obtaining and using information on them.Plan International Sudan identifies groups that are at risk of unsafe livelihood practices and prioritizes the provision of alternatives. The organization also monitors and responds to potential exploitative practices or abuses by identifying power dynamics and implementing appropriate measures.To promote a sense of ownership and build self-esteem, Plan International Sudan encourages communities to play an active role in identifying solutions and participating in decision-making processes. The organization also promotes collective livelihood projects that contribute to community protection and resilience.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will use community feedback to improve and adapt activities through the implementation of Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRMs). These mechanisms are designed to be accessible to all community members through various entry points such as telephone calls, suggestion boxes, feedback forms, and community meetings. The information gathered through these channels is treated confidentially and analyzed to identify areas for improvement and adaptation. The feedback received from the community will be considered seriously and incorporated into decision-making processes and programming decisions. Plan International is committed to promoting accountability throughout its interventions and actively involves the target groups and communities in providing feedback through CFRMs. This ensures that the voices and perspectives of the community are heard and acted upon, leading to more responsive and effective project activities. Any feedback or complaints received is followed up promptly to ensure that they are resolved effectively, demonstrating the organization's dedication to using community feedback for continuous improvement and adaptation.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Plan International is a leading humanitarian organization, dedicated to improving the lives of children and their families around the world. As a member of the AAP/PSEA network, Plan International is committed to upholding high standards of safeguarding and prevention of sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse (PSHEA), particularly in emergency situations.The organization takes safeguarding very seriously and rigorously implements these standards during the planning and execution of project interventions. They conduct thorough risk analyses and assessments to identify measures for risk mitigation and contingency planning. Plan International works tirelessly to ensure that its staff, partners, beneficiaries, and communities are well-informed about the safeguarding and PSEA policies. They provide comprehensive training to all stakeholders on the minimum codes of behavior and professional conduct requirements.Plan International collaborates with UNCT/UNOPS call centers and AAP focal points in other organizations to enhance technical standards and AAP and PSHEA mechanisms based on the organization's global experience and lessons learned. They work closely with these organizations to develop and implement effective safeguarding policies and procedures.In addition to safeguarding, Plan International is committed to promoting accountability throughout its interventions. They do this in accordance with the Core Humanitarian Standard on Accountability and Quality Management. To achieve this, the organization ensures that target groups and communities have access to relevant information. They actively involve them in programming decisions and enable them to provide feedback through the Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRMs).These CFRMs are designed to be accessible to all community members, providing multiple entry points such as telephone calls, suggestion boxes, feedback forms, and community meetings. The information gathered is treated confidentially and analyzed, and responses are provided in line with Plan International's internal procedures.Furthermore, any feedback or complaints concerning other project stakeholders working in the same areas are appropriately referred to their respective senior management for action. Plan International takes all feedback and complaints very seriously and follows up on them promptly to ensure that they are resolved effectively.
