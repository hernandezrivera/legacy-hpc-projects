Project Summary

Blumont will continue providing CCCM to ensure equitable access to assistance, protection, and services to improve the quality of life and dignity for approximately 91,000 IDPs living in Newroz, Al-Hol and Roj camps and 42 Collective Centers (CC) in Al-Hasakeh Governorate, Tuwayhinah, Mahmoudly, Tel al-Samen camps, and 26 CCs in Ar-Raqqa Governorate.

4 - Needs

As of December 31, 2021, in NES 135,537 individuals residing in planned (87,299) and informal (38,238) camps and 149,338 individuals residing in 304 informal sites are most affected by the deteriorating economic and security conditions, as they rely exclusively on humanitarian assistance for all their needs. Of the informal sites, 24,788 individuals live in 141 collective centers (CCs) and 124,550 individuals in 163 informal settlements. This represents a 7% population increase since last year. Camp (planned and informal), informal settlement and CC populations include 130,155 individuals in 116 sites in Al-Hasakeh, 116,963 individuals in 117 sites in Ar-Raqqa, and 31,306 individuals in 41 sites in DEZ, with Al-Hasakah hosting the largest number of CCs (88) and Ar-Raqqa the highest number of informal settlements (77) throughout NES. As noted above, site residents rely exclusively on external humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs. The proposed project will meet CCCM needs in targeted camps.

5 - Implementation of activities

1) Beneficiary Registration: Blumont will serve as the primary focal point for registration of all new arrivals in the camps and updating the camp population database. 2) Information Management: Blumont will collect, optimize, and share data for coordination and decision-making within NES, helping to inform a rapid, effective, and principled humanitarian service delivery in planned camps, informal sites, and CCs among all humanitarian actors and donors. 3) Camp Governance Structure and Community Leadership Support: Central to Blumont’s CCCM rights- and community-based approach is the assurance of accountability through site governance structures and committees to support beneficiaries' active engagement in how CCCM and assistance are provided. 4) Conduct Community Mobilization: Blumont will conduct a range of community-based activities to encourage participation of camp residents, particularly youth, women, and children to help residents adjust to their new lives and foster a sense of social responsibility and cohesion. 5) Maintain and rehabilitate site infrastructure: Blumont conducts general maintenance and operations of site infrastructure to improve the functionality and cleanliness of site facilities. 6) Heating and Cooking NFIs: Blumont will provide fuel (per S/NFI WG and UNHCR guidelines) and heaters during winter to all HHs and cooking stoves and fuel in all camps except Newroz where communal kitchens exist.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Persons in camp settings may have specific needs due not only to the drastic change of life opportunities caused by the crisis, but also stemming from vulnerabilities that existed prior. In our commitment to protect all persons with special needs (PWSNs), Blumont undertakes to identify such persons as early as possible in accordance with a threshold approved by the UNHCR protection team for all vulnerabilities associated with camp residents. Blumont’s registration and protection volunteers work to ensure all vulnerable persons with special needs are registered and appropriately referred. Blumont properly screens each new arrival HH as part of the registration process. Cases of specific vulnerability and those at risk are referred to UNHCR and protection actors within the Protection WG. Blumont works closely with the beneficiaries, relevant WGs, CA and LAs, and highlights any protection needs or concerns. Blumont follows up with protection partners to ensure activities are implemented and solve any challenges that may be preventing beneficiaries from accessing protection services. When identifying any case of gender or protection needs, it is referred to the proper service provider partners. Blumont coordinates with all stakeholders and ensures concerns are raised and needs addressed when possible.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Blumont understands and accepts the responsibility to help ensure SEA does not take place through the proposed project. Blumont operates with a dedicated PSEA strategy which covers the following elements: recruitment policy; induction and training; performance management; whistleblowing; discipline and grievance/complaints; partnership agreements; policy implementation, monitoring and review; and promotes an organizational culture concerned about these issues coupled with top-down leadership from its Board of Directors and Executive Management Team. Blumont also sensitizes vendors on standards for Code of Conduct and intolerance for gender discrimination and sexual harassment or abuse.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Blumont has identified the following risks, assumptions and mitigating measures: 1) COVID-19: The pandemic continues into its third year and is still a threat to all staff and program beneficiaries, especially those still unvaccinated. Blumont will continue to monitor developments in relation to COVID-19 through advice being published by WHO and the U.S. Government. Blumont staff wear gloves and masks, use hand sanitizer frequently and practice physical distancing by maintaining two-meter distance between themselves and camp residents. 2) Civil unrest: Blumont continuously monitors for tensions within the camps to address with relevant stakeholders, monitors national news, media and social media, and maintains low profile and avoid areas of demonstration or protest. 3) Physical Security & Crime: Program staff or objectives may be impacted through physical violence and theft of project commodities. Blumont’s security team continuously monitors the on-the-ground context and Blumont Global Safety and Security Policy, and Security SOPs are in place.
