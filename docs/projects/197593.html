Project Summary

Through this project HRO aims to enhance the accessibility of conflict-affected children in NWS in Aleppo and Idleb to quality non-formal education. The project will target 5000 children (B:2500,G:2500) (7-17 years) who have dropped out or at risk of dropping out of schools with NFE programs and 600 (B:300,G:300 ) children (3-5 years) with (ECE).
The planned approach of implementation is face-to-face.
The project has 2 objectives, that are aligned with the HRP Education sector objectives ,which are:
1- Increase safe and equitable availability and access to in-person non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth.
2- Enhanced quality of non-formal education for children and youth.
The activities that will be implemented including the following:
1- Establishing and running 2 TLSs, 1 in each of Aleppo and Idleb communities, where the NFE, ECE, and parenting programs/PTAs meeting will be implemented.
2- Undertaking light rehabilitation to 10 educational facilities where NFE and PTAs will be implemented. These 10 facilities include 2 TLSs and 8 schools. 7 of these educational facilities out of 10 will be Aleppo and 3 will be in Idleb :
- In Aleppo: 1 TLS and 6 schools
- In Idleb: there will be 1 TLS and 2 schools
3- Support the 10 educational facilities with solar system for lighting, sterilizers, and PPE for educational staff and beneficiaries.
4- Conducting 5 Back to Learning (BTL) to 500 (B:250, G:250 ) children Campaigns in each of Aleppo and Idleb .
5- Implementing ALP and remedial NFE programs for children dropped out of schools and these children will undertake pre and post-tests to measure the impact of the ALP and remedial program implemented and assess their educational improvement.
20 teachers will be providing NFE in each of the schools during the afternoon shift.
While in the 2 TLSs, 8 teachers will be recruited for each of the TLSs, 4 for the morning shift and 4 for the afternoon shift.
6- Implementing ECE for 600 ( B:300,G:300) children (3-5 years) using 2 approaches: static-based in the 2,and through community-based outreach approach .
7- Providing transportation for children with disabilities (CwD) from and to the educational facilities in order to attend the NFE (ALP & remedial) and other activities in the school sand TLSs.
8- Establishing 2 PTAs, 1 in each of Aleppo and Idleb communities, and conducting regular meetings with these PTAs.
9- Distributing monthly incentives and building the capacity of 100 ( M:50,W:50) Educational personnel on EIE and INEE, school and classroom management, SEL, PSS, and PFA in addition to identification and referral for CP cases
10- Providing the children attending the NFE programs with school bags, stationery, and curriculums.
11- conducting awareness raising sessions about Cholera and PSEA for all children.

3 - Needs

In reference to HNO 2023, There has been a 4 per cent increase in the sector PIN, with 6.9M children and education personnel needing emergency education services,Attendance rates are similar for boys and girls up to 11 years, after which there is gender disparity with more boys not attending. Transitioning to secondary school remains a challenge, only six governorates had <10% of children attending secondary school. This is further exacerbated by the lack of vocational schools. Only 11 per cent of four-year-old and 36 per cent of five-year-old attended any type of learning, limiting school readiness. Rates of non-attendance was highest in Aleppo (16%) and Idleb (15%). Data shows that if a child is displaced more than three times, there is a higher chance of not attending education (20%), with 42% less likely to attend in the first three years of displacement. Dropout due to marriage is more of barrier for IDPs in camps (5%) than other population groups. Additional data sources estimate that there are over 2 million children (6-17) that are out of school. The action will address needs that will support the elimination of barriers to access to education. Those barriers include: poverty not allowing families to afford education costs, displacement, security due to distance to safe learning spaces/schools, child labour, unavailability of number of learning spaces, and disabilities hindering access to school and at school if buildings are not equipped to facilitate access.

Attacks on Learning facilities by parties so facilities can be de-conflicted using the de- confliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA.1 De-confliction does not guarantee that a learning facility will not be attacked. Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying so enforced code of conduct against all forms of violence and unacceptable conduct, including bullying, corporal punishment, and physical, verbal and sexual abuse. Teachers and school personnel should be trained on prevention and reporting of issues related to, violence, physical punishment and bullying and the use of positive discipline Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities so HRO polices and systems in place by the organization should be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving so PSEA policies should be in place, practiced and monitored Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.) or from public health risks including Covid-19 and other communicable diseases so : improvement to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

HRO has PSEA Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure all effort is made to protect all Program participants. HRO PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising, Prevention, Reporting, and Response. In addition, all forms of sexual harassment and exploitation by staff who work in and for HRO are unacceptable, both during and outside of work. In addition to PSEA, HRO reporting system in place to prevent andtake actions against such behaviors, which clearly stating zero tolerance for sexual harassment. All staff ,contractors, cleaners, etc. must adhere to these, and HRO offices have been already trained on the policy.
