Project Summary

(CAO) will provide child protection services targeting 42,000 benificiaries including ( 15,000 Girls and 15,000 Boys ) Children, adolescent children and (6,000 men and 6,000 Women ) in Fangak 8,000, Canal/Pigi 9,000, Uror 9,000 Counties in Jonglei State and Ulang 8,000, and Nasir/Luakpiny 8,000 in Upper Nile State, aimed to ensure the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience caused by the conflict and flood, through provision of timely comprehensive case management and referrals including CBI, Mobile and static protection monitoring and assessment, Psychosocial Support Services, protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging and CBP preparedness and protection risk mitigation.
CAO, with community participation, will involve the beneficiaries, local authorities, CBCPN and other stakeholders in project implementation and capacities building on the three approaches that will use to achieve more results as the Current flood and conflicts significantly impact the children’s psycho-social well-being and development, which exposes them to violence, loss of lives, or separation from, family members and friends, deterioration in living conditions, inability to provide for one’s self and family, and lack of access to services that can all have immediate and long-term consequences on children, families, and communities and impair their ability to function and be fulfilled.
CAO will provide crucial psychosocial support for children during emergency situations to help them overcome such difficult experiences. These efforts include culturally and age-appropriate, safe and stimulating activities such as games and sports to develop life skills, coping mechanisms, and support resiliency,
CAO will also use extensive comprehensive case management forms to document all cases in accordance to the CP principles into the database and report it to the relevant services, this will be done in conjunction with the community-based child protection committees or Networks (CBCPC/N, which will be recruited and trained to provide quality case management and proper coordination of the FTR in the local context. The project will also strengthen the ability of community members to support and provide comprehensive quality of services to the most vulnerable children, UASC, communities and families with their neighbors with keys messages on how to cope with this situations through a variety of channels including the existing community structures, youth centers and use of child friendly spaces (CFS), organize activities to the children and adolescent youths in a safe and stimulating environment where affected communities and children can be supported, provide specialized referral pathways to access the services base on the need, or who may need extra support, so that appropriate networks of care may be engaged.
CAO will recruit and train the CP staff on the child protection concerns in order to speed the implementation to provide quality activities and services to support the, identification, registration, documentation of the Unaccompanied and separated children in the selected counties and conduct assessment, planning, implementation follow up and reporting in accordance with the IASC, SOPs and ISPs to improve FTR and case management in line with minimum standards of Child protection in emergencies services.

1 - Needs

Floods caused by torrential rains have worsened the living conditions of South Sudanese people in many parts of the country in the past few months. Some of the areas affected include; Ayod, Akobo, Bor South, Duk, Twic East, Pibor, Pochalla and Uror counties in Jonglei; Ulang and Nasir/Luakpiny in Upper Nile; The heavy rains have inundated shelters, water sources, crops, and crucial productive assets in many areas. It also increases the vulnerabilities of the children especially UASC and other vulnerable children, they have also damaged essential service infrastructure like schools/TLS CFS and health facilities, impacting women and children in particular. The floods have rendered many roads impassable, constraining people’s access to markets schools, CFS Centers and other community existing centers. Humanitarians’ ability to move across affected areas. Thousands of families have temporarily relocated from worst-affected to safer areas. The flooding has increased the risk of diseases among the Children and other community members especially elderly people. The most commonly reported ones include acute watery diarrhea, respiratory infections, and malaria.Across the country, authorities and aid organizations have reported that an estimated 234,800 people have been affected by the floods in the past months including about 132,800 people in Jonglei, 52,000 people in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, nearly 37,000 people in Unity, some 8,000 people in Eastern Equatoria and 5,000 people in Upper Nile. a joint needs assessment by partners to assess needs and ascertain the number of people affected by floods in Jonglei, Upper Nile Tonj North, Gogrial East, Gogrial East and Tonj North counties commenced on 11 September and October. Local leaders continue to report new affected areas and people daily.The prolonged conflict in the listed counties of Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Uror in Jonglei State and Ulang, and Nasir/Luakpiny Counties in Upper Nile State where it has had a catastrophic effect on the population especially children being the most affected due to increased exposures to protection risks which include separation from caregivers, widespread of GBV concerns, loss of immediate caregivers, forced recruitment and marriage, massive destruction of schools and TLS, abuse, exploitation of the vulnerable children among other protection concerns.CAO intends to continue addressing protection risks issues through a response strategy which includes: Provision of comprehensive case management and referrals including Cash Base Intervention to children under support with material and other medical concerns. CAO will increase its Static and Mobile respond to reach hard to reach areas with protection monitoring and assessment, strengthening the Psycho-social Support Services and Protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging to the affected communities within the selected counties, strengthening community-based child protection Committee Network (CBCPC/N), increase capacity building program to staff and communities to support the children during the exit of the organization, to continue improving the well-being of the children that have gone through difficulty and in the community’s centers in order to complement the current multi-sectoral interventions with mainstream CP and GBV into Education, WASH, Nutrition, Health, and FSL Referral pathways will also be established to strengthen and support implementation of case management and link children to other services and address the gaps in coordination within the targeted counties were CAO operates.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Community Action Organization (CAO) has been running a WASH, Nutrition, child protection and GBV project in most of the target Counties funded by UNICEF for one year. SSHF will complement UNICEF funds and allow CAO to expand and implement child protection activities. This project is expected to be community-based and participatory. Community members will be involved from the beginning and CAO staff will emphasize that they contribute many skills and valuable knowledge, thereby encouraging more community participation. The IDP and host community will be engaged and encouraged to buy into the project on the understanding that CAO will only manage it to the end of the project period but that should not be the end of the activities. As long as the needs of the community are there, the activities will have to continue with minimum financial support but rather with community support since it is for the communities’ benefit. Through the trainings, community members will have the knowledge and also have seen the impact of the project on the lives of the beneficiaries, both direct and indirect. The different referral pathways will eventually run systematically without much outside resources needed once the community has adopted the system. CAO will remain in the communities to provide any technical support or advice for the community and encourage them to continue activities after project closure.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: CAO will ensure that services are designed to prevent as much as possible any unintended negative effects which can increase children’s vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks. In particular, all staff of CAO and community volunteers will sign and adhere to CAO’s Child Protection Policy to protect children from any eventual abuse and advocate for viable solutions.Meaningful Access:CAO will ensure that all children including vulnerable children in the community have access to CP services in the CFS or direct support without any discrimination. In particular, children living with disabilities, children living with HIV/AIDS, child-headed households, child mothers, children demobilized from armed forces/groups, SGBV survivors, etc. will be accorded with necessary assistance and services. Equal participation and empowerment:Adolescent girls and boys (peer educators) will be trained on life skills education to protect themselves and also help support other small children. Adolescents and child rights clubs will be formed in schools to help children develop self-protection capacities to enable them to claim their rights, including rights to education, health, sanitation, Food/NFIs, shelter, etc.The team will conduct throughout an in-depth protection analysis using the protection risk equation, and will utilize the use of the cluster assessment template and the report will be shared with the national cluster and Sub-Cluster coordination team. CAO will insure do no harm’ approach is observe for the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries as it is the core of the CAO Policy, the protection team will assess the distribution sites and will advise the project team on adjustments if needed. Traveling routes and time to the distribution sites will be taken into consideration as well.
