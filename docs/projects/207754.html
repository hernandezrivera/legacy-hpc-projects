Project Summary

The “Empowering Refugee and Host Communities in Cox's Bazar through Inclusive WASH Initiatives, Collaboratively Streamlined with Local Partners for Sustainable Impact” project aims to align with strategic priorities of WASH sector for 2024-25. The focus is on enhancing sustainable and inclusive WASH services for both refugees and the host community in Teknaf. Oxfam, in collaboration with implementing partners DSK and NGOF, will coordinate efforts with relevant agencies to ensure equitable access to quality water and sanitation.
OXFAM has already phased out from the direct camp operation since 2021 , transferring O&M responsibilities to local partners to grow and access direct funding from UN’s and other institutional donors in 2024.
1.Oxfam's WASH interventions in Teknaf's Camp 22 and surrounding communities (Hnila and Whykong) prioritize inclusivity. Using a 'Community-Led Total Sanitation' model, the project upgrades existing latrines and constructs new accessible facilities for women, girls, and individuals with disabilities. In Whykong ,addressing water scarcity, the initiative includes borehole construction and maintenance, alongside solid waste management efforts. Long-term sustainability is emphasized through host community tariff collection models and local operator transition.
2.Oxfam's WASH innovation in Cox's Bazar focuses on transitioning from emergency to sustainable hygiene promotion for Rohingya camps and host communities. The ongoing project started from June 23 will continue till mid-2024 , a scoping and feasibility study will identify cost-effective, context-specific solutions. Key criteria include cost efficiency, tailored responses to public health risks, gender-responsive community engagement, and capacity-building for self-management of WASH facilities.
3.Oxfam will be conducting different studies , assessment , generate case studies from past projects to look on their efficiency after 6 years of response, few of the planned activities are – a comprehensive assessment of two surface water treatment plants established in 2017 and 2018, delivering 450,000 liters per day to drought-affected camps. During the rainy season, turbidity in canal water exceeds 1000 NTU, necessitating aluminum dosing, impacting taste and color. This study can contribute to enhancing the Surface Water Treatment (SWAT) system, beyond sedimentation and chlorination. Based on the recommendation A. OXFAM will advocate with relevant AFA’s to allocate funding for upgrading existing SWAT & also raise fund from OXFAM’s funding channels. B. Support WASH sector and AFA’s to assess existing FSTP’s and FS collection systems in terms of their efficiency and financial analysis , support decision making process for future investment .
4.Expert human resources is likely to leave response due to lack of funds which is showing declining trends further in coming years . Capacity building of new & existing incoming staffs in FSM ,SWM, Safe water chain will be crucial , OXFAM will support WASH sector organizing trainings, workshops to ensure projects and activities are designed by local partners in efficient and most suitable manner. OXFAM WASH in Cox’s Bazar excelling in water and FSM infrastructures through community-driven solutions. OXFAM will be maintaining a strategic footprint, builds local partner capacity, supports the WASH sector, and copes with reduced funding. Partner-led interventions ensure effective service delivery, fostering harmony between Rohingya and host communities through this project.
5.Hygiene promotion priorities include functioning CBO’s and linked with ward and union WATSAN committees in host community , CLTS approach with community Based Action Groups ,community-oriented solid waste management, cost-effective and sustainable hygiene promotion, adoption of the Community Perception Tracker, and active participation of representatives from different levels in decision-making.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In line with the JMSNA findings in 2024, addressing water-related challenges is crucial in the host communities of Whykong, Hnila, and Teknaf. The water crisis persists, with pollution from solid waste and contamination from effluents and wastewater ranking as second-priority concerns. Sustainable solid waste management (SWM) and fecal sludge management (FSM) are also imperative across the response area, emphasizing quality, equality, and inclusive services.Specifically, in Whykong and Hnila, a water crisis and the lack of menstrual hygiene support for adolescent girls have been identified as significant gaps. WASH sector funding gaps represent beneficiaries are receiving almost two times less than SPHERE standard ; essential WASH hygiene items as per recommendations for the ‘Menstrual Hygiene Kits’ and ‘Soap’. Also new WASH infrastructures are required as some of them are deteriorating, leading to drastic programmatic choices due to budget limitation (e.g., only 33% of latrine blocks in camps have functional handwashing stations). Girls and women reported irritation resorting to makeshift materials for menstrual hygiene. Though 160 FSTPs are operational in camps , but most of them are struggling to respect effluent discharge standard which is become more evident in 2023 as FSTP's are passing their lifetimes, they will require major upgradation, replacement of filter medias ,otherwise agencies can result negatively in FSTP troubleshooting objective. Faecal sludge collection and treatment systems are absent in Whykong, Baharchara, and the adjacent host community of Teknaf camps, relying heavily on surface water sources vulnerable to fecal contamination. A host community faecal sludge collection modelling is a gap which can be connected to the newly built 120m3/day capacity FSTP in Teknaf. The host communities integration to the FSTP is a big challenge to be addressed. Centralized fecal sludge treatment plants aim to ensure safe sanitation, reduce pollution, and mitigate contamination risks to natural water bodies and the environment. Despite notable gains in handwashing practices during the peak of the COVID-19 pandemic, a decline in functions, use, and practices has been observed post-pandemic.From the Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) Strategy for Rohingya Response it is being highlighted to focus on camps and recommendations for camps does not necessary apply to host’s communities where context it totally different. Cost’s recovery system needs to be put in place for host’s communities and could be more effective.After withdrawal of UNDP from the Teknaf host community's SWM project in June 2022 created gaps in transitioning to a sustainable operating model. OXFAM continued the intervention through 2023.But still the absence of potential compost production from organic waste in Whykong and Hnila persists. A market-based tariff collection is a need that has to be addressed to make the intervention sustainable.The impact of decreased funding on water supply and sanitation services is evident through shrinking budgets for current facilities and recent reductions in soap allocation to refugees. Challenges include severe water scarcity in Teknaf during dry periods, unsustainable water solutions for host communities and camps, and stress on the aquifer in Kutupalong mega camp. Funding shortages are affecting latrine desludging, fecal sludge treatment, and solid waste management. Reduced focus on the host community may lead to tensions between refugees and locals. Frequent emergencies, including disease outbreaks and natural hazards, further complicate the situation.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

In the 2024/25 fiscal year, OXFAM's primary focus will be on capacity building and conducting high-level technical studies aligned with WASH sector and its stakeholders. OXFAM aims to address the gap in camp-level infrastructures by collaborating closely with specific UN/AFAs, thereby preventing any duplication in the JRP rationalization process. Despite facing a gradual decrease in funding, OXFAM remains committed to its fundraising efforts for the Rohingya Response. The organization will actively engage with local partners to effectively meet the critical WASH needs of refugees, while ensuring coordination within the WASH sector."Adopt an integrated approach," is exemplified in the project's design through its emphasis on collaboration and coordination with various stakeholders. Oxfam, alongside implementing partners DSK and NGOF, is actively engaged in coordinating efforts with relevant agencies to ensure an integrated approach to improve inter-sectoral camp-based coordination. By leveraging technical expertise to tackle water scarcity, inadequate sanitation, and fecal sludge management, the project fosters relations with the government and other partners, particularly with the CiCs."Ensure predictability and continuity," is reflected in the project's focus on efficient operation and maintenance of existing WASH services. The project's consideration of long-term sustainability through host community tariff collection models, local operator transition, and the incorporation of community-led total sanitation models also contributes to stability and predictability in service delivery, aligning with the principle's objectives. Through these measures, the project not only addresses the immediate WASH needs but also establishes a framework for sustained impact and collaboration, reinforcing predictability and continuity in the refugee operation.

3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

By prioritizing the safety and dignity of individuals and actively supporting affected populations in accessing and enjoying their rights, beneficiaries will be able to safely avail themselves of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services without encountering harm. The implementation of Community Perception Trackers, and the establishment of mechanisms such as Listening groups, consultations, referrals, and feedback gathering. Additionally, staff training will be conducted to enhance participation and empowerment, ensuring a comprehensive approach to safeguarding the well-being of individuals while accessing essential services. Assessment regarding the meaning full access to WASH facilities can uphold the protection mainstreaming approach.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project is centered around ensuring inclusive access to Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services, with a focus on all age individuals, while also integrating child protection measures. The primary goal is to prevent discrimination, abuse, neglect, violence, and exploitation. Staff members undergo training to effectively identify and refer at-risk children to community-level child protection systems.Moreover, the program adopts a twin-track approach to disability inclusion, incorporating it into all outcome areas and implementing targeted activities for individuals with disabilities. Following the guidelines set by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC), the project places a strong emphasis on meaningful participation, barrier removal, empowerment, and data disaggregation for monitoring. Inclusion within the WASH components, people with disabilities actively participate in discussions and contribute to various committees. This ensures their inclusion in decision-making processes and accessibility initiatives.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to affected populations is a fundamental pillar of this response. Implementing partners commit to practicing downward accountability by involving communities and local authorities in all aspects of project design and implementation. Key strategies for ensuring accountability and involving communities in design and implementation will include:• Community Consultation and Feedback: The project design process has involved and will continue to engage affected populations in constructive dialogues, using mixed, disability, age and sex-disaggregated focus groups and interviews. This approach ensures all voices within the community are heard, fostering safe environments for people to voice their priorities and concerns. • Community perception tracker in WASH interventions can uphold accountability to affected populations• Community Ownership: To support greater community ownership in host areas , OXFAM and its partner will hand over the management of small-scale infrastructure to local communities, including women's groups and WASH structures. By entrusting these responsibilities to local populations, the project aims to promote local investment and engagement in project outcomes as well as challenging gender norms where appropriate.• Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM): This mechanism provides safe and secure channels for community members to raise issues and concerns. FCM channels include project engagement, hotline telephone numbers, and feedback and information centers, including mobile outreach and household visits to ensure access for marginalized and vulnerable groups in camp and host communities.Monthly reports on the FCM (disaggregated by disability, sex and age) will aid in identifying and addressing gaps in feedback and complaints.All of these strategies will work in synergy to ensure that the project is responsive to the needs and concerns of the affected populations.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the OXFAM project incorporates Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) elements as part of its strategy. Oxfam employs the Social Architecture approach, involving communities in designing resilient Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) infrastructures. This approach integrates DRR/DRM by empowering communities to actively participate in decision-making, particularly involving women in households. The community's responsibility includes developing preparedness plans and mitigation measures for recurring disasters such as monsoons, cyclones, droughts, and Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) seasons.The project emphasizes community involvement in building resilient WASH infrastructures using locally available materials. The long-term repair and maintenance of these infrastructures, including addressing post-disaster impacts, are the community's responsibility. This approach instills a sense of ownership and resilience among affected people, enabling them to address their needs using existing capacities when disasters strike.Furthermore, the project demonstrates consideration for environmental issues by promoting the use of locally available materials, which contributes to sustainability and climate change mitigation. The focus on community empowerment aligns with climate change adaptation measures, fostering self-reliance and preparedness for environmental challenges. The project will work further on resource recovery from FSTP’s ( Co -composting / biosolid ) , assessing product values in host communities, FSM and SWM inputs, biogas digester , scrubbing H2S and utilization for electricity generation at small communal digester.
