Project Summary

Medair’s project aims to reduce preventable morbidity and mortality among IDPs and highly vulnerable, conflict affected populations and host communities in Iraq through provision of essential Primary Health Care, including integrated mental health and psychosocial support. Medair will support PHC in camp and non camp settings, handing over services at established PHCCs to the DOH where possible and ensuring timely EWARN reporting for surveillance and response to communicable disease. In Ninewa, target districts include Al Mosul, Sinjar, Tal Afar and Tilkaef. In Kirkuk governorate, Medair will target locations in Al-Hawiga and in Salah-al-Din, Medair will support Primary Health Care in Tuz and Tikrit. Medair has already secured funding for current projects which will run into 2019.

Needs

More than 1.9 million IDPs remain displaced in Iraq. The rate of return has slowed. Just 18% of IDPs returned so far in 2018 (OCHA Sept 2018) and more than half of these IDPs have been displaced for more than three years. OCHA estimates 71% of IDPs are outside camps, and largely within Kurdistan Region and Ninewa. Of these, only 10% have been reached with humanitarian assistance. Of the 1.9 million still displaced, 65% reported they have no intention to return to their areas of origin in the next 12 months. Existing support for basic health services in camp settings is reducing. Therefore, even as displaced camps are being consolidated and de-commissioned, provision of essential PHC services for IDPs both in and out of camp settings is critical.Existing capacity of DOH facilities is insufficient to meet demand for PHC services, highlighted in 2018 HeRAMS data for Ninewa governorate which found only 43% of PHCCs fully functioning, 34% partially functioning and 13% health facilities remain completed destroyed. Support to rehabilitate and re-establish PHC services remains significant need, impacting both men and women in affected communities and all age groups; young children, children, adolescents, women of childbearing age, and older men and women. Access for people living with physical disabilities also needs addressed with protection actors and community leaders to identify vulnerable families and individuals who need support to access PHC services and referral for physical rehabilitation. There is disparity in access to Reproductive Health services for affected communities, in both in availability and quality of service provision, impacting young children, adolescents and women. Ninewa HeRAMS 2018 data indicate only 23% PHC facilities provide PNC, and 22% ANC. For example, Medair recent assessment to Qadia camp, Sumel District identified gaps in provision of ANC, PNC and family planning. Although referral for skilled delivery is available (in Qadia), other locations do not have referral systems in place and options are extremely limited, particularly in more remote areas of displacement or return. This gap is related to lack of female medical staff at PHC level. In Hawiga, needs assessments suggest many women deliver at home without skilled assistance or travel over 1 hour to hospitals in Kirkuk or Shirqat. In Salah al Din governorate, access to RH is also limited; DOH delivery care is limited to hospitals and NGOs provide part time services in key PHCC locations in Baiji PHCC and in Shirqat camp. In Sudeira, returnees reported giving birth at home with assistance of untrained traditional birth assistants, and incidence of neonatal deaths. In Nawjul, Tooz district, IDPs also reported delivering at home without skilled assistance, and routine PNC was not available. Recent health cluster Gender Based Violence (GBV) service mapping highlights limited access to GBV services, especially provision of MHPSS and medical care for GBV survivors. It remains underreported. In Medair focus group discussions (FGD), women disclosed that economic stressors and effects of displacement have led to increased domestic violence within the home. During a women's FGD in Quraishiya, women reported intimate partner violence, forced marriage, and sleep disturbances. Female IDPs in focus groups held in Salah-al-Din, reported severe protection violations by members of armed groups including rape and subsequent suicide of some survivors. Protracted displacement increases risk of morbidity and mortality, including increased mental health and psychosocial needs across all age groups. Ninewa HeRAMs found just 15% PHC facilities providing mental health services. The REACH MCNA found extreme stress reactions were common across all population groups: 44% of host community, 35% of out-of-camp IDP, and 30% of returnee HHs. Access to specialist and non specialist MH services is very limited. Medair KPC survey (July 2018) found only 40% of respondents were aware of any MHPSS services, despite 37% of respondents experiencing psychological stressors in the preceding 6 months. Risk of outbreak disease remains a concern in areas of displacement and return. A recent assessment in Golat, Sinjar district (Medair RNA Sept 2018), found lack of vaccination coverage for children under 5. In other areas such as Ain Al-Hasan (Medair RNA Oct 2018), vaccination campaigns for measles and polio have been completed but routine immunization has not resumed. Medair KPC July 2018, found 18% of mothers with children under 5 reported that their child had not received any vaccinations. Of those who did, only 61% received measles vaccination. Routine EWARN reporting from DOH PHCC facilities needs re-established enabling effective disease surveillance. Displacement has contributed to spread of other communicable diseases such as cutaneous leishmaniasis, requiring improved case management and prevention at facility and community level.

Activities

Conduct health needs assessments in IDP (camp and non camp) and returnee locations and regularly monitor population movements and shifting security context• Support health coordination at local and national level and liaise with key stakeholders in all locations to ensure appropriate gap identification and response, including preventing duplication of services• Provide essential Primary Health Care services, in camp and out-of- camp settings, including RH, NCDs and MHPSS, for IDP and vulnerable communities through support to DOH PHCCs and with medical teams where there is a lack of DOH staff or facility • Strengthen PHCC systems and quality of care through training of DOH staff and provision of essential medicines, medical supplies and equipment• Establish and support patient referral systems from communities to Primary Health Care facilities to secondary and tertiary care facilities• Establish or strengthen EWARN at facility level, ensure submission of weekly and monthly reports to DOH, EWARN, health cluster and facilitate appropriate investigation and response • Provide rehabilitation of PHC facilities where appropriate to ensure health care space fit for purpose• Provide integrated psychosocial support groups for men and women, including adolescents and the elderly, including referral into PSS groups from PHCC and to specialist services if indicated • Provide community health program focusing on increasing demand for Reproductive Health services for women of childbearing age, and facilitate community health groups such as Baby friendly Spaces, targeting pregnant and lactating women and children under 2 years• Develop clear exit strategies and timelines, in partnership with the DOH to ensure continuation of essential primary health care services

Protection Risk Analysis

In areas of intervention, protection risks will be analyzed and mitigation measures will be developed to incorporate into project design and implementation. Overall protection issues may include unequal access to benefits and services due to gender, age, ethnicity, and religion (among others) and other associated discrimination by authorities and armed groups. Beneficiaries may have difficulties with identification documents, forced evictions and returns, camp closures, lack of services, restricted movements and insecurity. Health intervention-specific risks may include inequality of PHC access related to IDP status, financial capacity, age, gender and disability. Protection risks to staff may include insecurity in accessing project sites and in delivering services.

Mitigation Measures

One of the risks identified is inequality of PHC access related to IDP status, financial capacity, age, gender and disability. Medair will coordinate with and continue to advocate to DOH for free provision of PHC, for waiver of user fees and cost of essential medication for vulnerable groups to remove financial barrier to accessing quality lifesaving services. Equality of access will be monitored through analysis of daily PHCC consultation numbers, disaggregated male and female, as well as all age groups designated by DOH, including young children, children, adolescents, adults and the elderly. Medair Community Health programme will facilitate home visits to identify older persons or people living with physical disabilities, who may not be able to independently access PHC facilities. Medair will promote and encourage use of the Iraq IDP Call Centre, for confidential reporting of sensitive protection concerns, including allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse. Referrals for emergency and specialist services will be reviewed to monitor access and identify potential barriers. Medair will engage with community leaders and health and protection cluster to identify people with physical disabilities within affected communities to facilitate PHC access and referrals including for MHPSS services. Through PSS groups and the Community Health programme, Medair will increase awareness of GBV and mental health services to reduce associated stigma and encourage uptake of services. Medair will follow agreed case treatment and referral protocols, to ensure patient privacy and confidentiality of information about vulnerable individuals, such as are in place for GBV. Medair will support health cluster advocacy on the issue of mandatory reporting of GBV. Medair will work with DOH, health cluster and partners for timely handover and exit of PHC services at all locations to ensure continuation of services. How is your project accountable to affected population

Medair multi-sector rapid assessments of new locations include identifying health needs of men and women, as well as age specific health needs related to young children, children, adolescents, adults and older people. Wherever possible Medair will include protection actors in RNAs, and ensure protection assessment is included in the RNA tool. Medair holds regular consultation with community leaders and FGDs in the community with men and women to identify multi-sector needs, assess access and acceptability of services. Medair health facilities have comment boxes for feedback, which are reviewed monthly. Liaison or health officers are available on site to receive and respond to verbal feedback regarding patient concerns or difficulties. In addition project locations have formal feedback systems, such as Kirkuk, where Medair conducts patient satisfaction questionnaires each month to identify potential barriers to access or dissatisfaction with services provided. As mentioned under mitigation of protection risks, Medair will share the number for the IDP Call Centre to enable confidential follow up of reports by the appropriate agency using established referral pathways. Regular stakeholder meetings will be held with community leaders to discuss service provision and identify vulnerable families within a community and individuals with a disability, to enable access and refer to Community Health team and/or protection actors. Where a change, or provision of new services is anticipated, Medair will liaise with community leaders to ensure they are informed and to hear and respond to their input. Protection concerns raised will be addressed and/or referred as appropriate and shared with protection cluster. Medair activities are community centered and adhere to a “Do No Harm” approach in selection of project locations, beneficiary selection, taking into account vulnerable population groups, and to uphold social cohesion. Quantitative beneficiary data is anonymized but will be coded where possible, to enable follow up of patients attending RH or MHPSS services. PHC clinics monitor patient numbers and disaggregate male/ female and for age groups as designated by DOH, covering young children, children, adolescents, adults and older people. Medair has a detailed plan to monitor activity-level progress and will measure this progress against project results and indicators by utilizing data collection tools and methods already in place. Data collection tools will be reviewed and modified as needed to ensure that all necessary data to measure project progress and quality is collected. Qualitative data will be collected through clinical observation and supervision/monitoring visits as a means of ensuring that internationally recognized quality standards of care are followed and training needs identified. PHCC supervision/monitoring visits will be carried out on a monthly basis, in collaboration with the DOH where feasible. Clinical observation and supervision/monitoring visits, including exit interviews, will be conducted by health managers and officers. A transition timeline for phased exit will be planned for each supported location in consultation with DoH. The timeline will be dependent on achievement of quality benchmarks and DoH capacity to take over required services. Medair will implement a quarterly review of progress against transition strategy for each location. Phased exits include tracking provision of the package of services as appropriate to the level of the facility and ensuring contingency is made for patients to access NCDs, MHPSS and RH services if DOH staff capacity is still limited on Medair exit.
