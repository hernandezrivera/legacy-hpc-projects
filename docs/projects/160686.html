Project Summary

The number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan stands at 7 million, or well over half the population. On-going fighting and surges of violence in new areas have forced people to flee their homes, many of them multiple times. The number of people uprooted since the start of the conflict in 2013 has reached more than 4 million, including 1.9 million internally displaced people (IDPs), with up to 85% estimated to be children and women.
Violence and rights violations continue unchecked and have become a persistent reality for civilians. Internally displaced people’s access to services has eroded with insecurity and economic decline. The population is highly susceptible to disease and conflict-related injuries. Disease outbreaks have lasted longer than ever and reached previously unaffected areas, weakening already vulnerable people’s ability to cope with multiple shocks. In 2017, South Sudan has seen the longest-running cholera outbreak in its history, which began in June 2016 and is expected to continue into 2019. Destruction of health-care facilities (only 22%of health facilities are fully operational), attacks on health workers, and shortages of drugs and skilled professionals mean access to health care is increasingly limited. The health facilities which are operational face challenges of delivering the complete basic package of health-care services. The absence of services means that cases needing emergency obstetric care, as well as tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS and mental health issues go largely untreated, causing increased morbidity and mortality.
Through the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan, World Vision International South Sudan will provide emergency lifesaving health interventions which will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, protection and FSL activities. Additionally, the emergency health intervention World Vision South Sudan intends to implement will focus on sexual gender based violence (SGBV), clinical management of rape (CMR) and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Owing to the high mortality rate of under 5 children (90.7/1000), maternal mortality rate (789/100,000) in South Sudan, World Vision will provide also maternal and child health interventions, mainly skilled birth attendance, prevention of post -partum hemorrhage, ante natal care, post-natal care, basic and comprehensive emergency neonatal and obstetrical care (EMNOC), Sexual and Reproductive Health/ family planning service and Expanded Programme Immunization (EPI) and quality management of Severe Acute Malnutrition with medical complication. World Vision will ensure that there is continuum of case for under 5 with complicated SAM so that they have access to OTP and TSF facilities run by nutrition partners, including World Vision who is a provider of Community Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) and Maternal Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practice.
World Vision will ensure that people with disabilities and mental disorders have priority access to emergency health services and are included as vulnerable groups owing to their high risk during emergencies. The response plan will be prioritized based on epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, cholera hot spot locations, measles outbreaks, malaria seasonality.
The emergency lifesaving response World Vision will provide will be done in line with the recommended health cluster emergency clinical services package for primary healthcare units, primary healthcare centers, county hospital, mobile medical team, and Integrated Community Case Management (ICCM). These interventions will be in line with the South Sudan Ministry of Health’s policies and guidelines, namely basic package of health and nutrition services, Boma Health Initiative, sexual and reproductive health, immunization guidelines and health policy of the Republic of South Sudan.

1 - Needs

The protracted armed conflict since December 2013 has resulted in multiple, acute, and chronic needs among the conflict affected population in South Sudan, further stretching their coping capacity. The security situation in many states, as well as the deteriorating economic state across the South Sudan is affecting the provision of basic, lifesaving services, such as WASH, to displaced and vulnerable populations. Nearly 4.8 million of the most vulnerable people across South Sudan need support to access safe water and basic sanitation facilities, with some of the IDPs and host communities, resorting to alternative water sources such as unprotected streams and wells. The number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan stands at 7 million, or well over half the population. On-going fighting and surges of violence in new areas have forced people to flee their homes, many of them multiple times. The number of people uprooted since the start of the conflict in 2013 has reached more than 4 million, including 1.9 million internally displaced people (IDPs), with up to 85% estimated to be children and women. Violence and rights violations continue unchecked and have become a persistent reality for civilians. Internally displaced people’s access to services has eroded with insecurity and economic decline. Particularly vulnerable groups such as children, people with disabilities and older people, suffer the most intense consequences of sustained displacement, violence and lack of access to services. Continued economic decline has undermined people’s access to basic resources. Long gaps and inconsistency in salary payments to public sector employees have impacted the provision of health-care and other services.Hunger and malnutrition have escalated, with about 1.1 million children under age five estimated to be acutely malnourished and in need of lifesaving services. Although localized famine was stopped in 2017, severe food insecurity continued to increase for the fifth consecutive year. Severe food insecurity is expected to continue in 2018, with the worst-case scenario of a return to famine in multiple locations across the country. The population is highly susceptible to disease and conflict-related injuries. Disease outbreaks have lasted longer than ever and reached previously unaffected areas, weakening already vulnerable people’s ability to cope with multiple shocks. In 2017, South Sudan has seen the longest-running cholera outbreak in its history, which began in June 2016 and is expected to continue into 2019. Destruction of health-care facilities (only 22%of health facilities are fully operational), attacks on health workers, and shortages of drugs and skilled professionals mean access to health care is increasingly limited. The health facilities which are operational face challenges of delivering the complete basic package of health-care services. The absence of services means that cases needing emergency obstetric care, as well as tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS and mental health issues go largely untreated, causing increased morbidity and mortality. Inadequate infection control and health-care waste management, combined with lack of water quality monitoring in health-care facilities, pose a significant public health threat. Preventable diseases like measles spread unchecked, and cases of kala-azar and meningitis are on the rise. The mental health and psychosocial burden increases each day that the conflict is prolonged. Through the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan, World Vision will provide emergency lifesaving health interventions which will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, protection and FSL activities. Additionally, the emergency health intervention World Vision intends to implement will focus on sexual gender based violence (SGBV), clinical management of rape (CMR) and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS).

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, the project will recognize legally the rights of beneficiaries. Being a child-focused organization, World Vision will mainstream child protection in all components of the project. World Vision supports the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child; children have the right to be protected "from all forms of physical or mental violence, injury or abuse, neglect or negligent treatment, maltreatment or exploitation, including sexual abuse". World Vision works to uphold this right through working with local communities and national and international governments to address both the causes and manifestations of violence against children and will incorporate child protection into this program through ensuring that all beneficiaries are protected and their rights are upheld during the project implementation. Project staff members will receive training in protection, child protection, and humanitarian standards to uphold accountability. The special concerns of physically and mentally disabled people will be addressed through careful targeting and active project participation. To promote peace building, project stakeholders will be involved in a Local Capacities for Peace/Do No Harm analysis at the initial stages of this project. World Vision also recognizes that all organizations that come into contact with children have a fundamental duty of care towards them. As such, World Vision acknowledges the level of responsibility to keep children safe in both relief and development interventions, and is working with a number of other agencies to develop and promote good practice in child protection among international organizations that come into contact with children.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

World Vision will ensure that all the target beneficiaries, in the four states, have access to nutrition specific and sensitive interventions through both fixed and outreaches modalities. World Vision International South Sudan will use its Fragile Context Programming Approach ( FCPA) which allows flexibility in the modalities of the lifesaving nutrition interventions. With such approach, World Vision will move from provision of health intervention of health facilities to Rapid response mechanisms in case where Rapid Response can be the only option which can permit accessibility to beneficiaries. Depending on the humanitarian context and the needs of the target populations, World Vision will either used one approach plus integrated outreaches; or World Vision can use the rapid response mechanism modality or use a combined approach: fix - outreaches - RRM.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

World Vision will implement this project in collaboration with the MoH at national level; the state ministry of health at state level and the county health department at the county level. In ensuring a strong integration of the health project, World Vision will work in collaboration with the Ministry of irrigation and infrastructure, the Ministry of Agriculture, and other line ministries.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The health project will be implemented in line with the South Sudan health policy, Basic package of health and nutrition services, and CMAM guidelines. The CMAM will help in provide guidance onthe management of SAM with medical complications.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficiently safe/ stable. Safety measures will be taken in the disposal of waste in the health facilities by use of an incinerator and health staff will be sensitized on proper and appropriate disposal measures. Environment and natural resource being a critical, target beneficiaries will be encouraged to conserve the environment and avoid practices that contaminate or affect the nature during the project activities. Hygiene promotion will help to promote messages about the importance of water conservation in addition to basic hygiene and sanitation practices. It is predicted that these sanitation messages will also have a positive impact on the surrounding environment as communities are encouraged to avoid water contamination through keeping their area clean and clear of possible diseases or infections. As all the interventions planned to protect and further promote environmental sensitivity, this project will positively impact on the environment in the project location.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

World Vision will strengthen community support structures to improve community resilience and create an enabling locally based prevention and response mechanism. World Vision will build the capacity of health workers and the health department to prevent, identify and treat disease outbreak and in proper management of patients in line with MoH guidelines such IMNCI, BHI, EPI, etc. With the involvement of the HHPs and the County Health Departments, there will be sustainability of the project when World Vision exits the target locations. World Vision will handover the project to the SMOH when it is time to exit.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

World Vision being a child-focused organization has a child protection policy. This policy is especially important to the nutrition department, as the staff in this department are in regular contact with children, and other vulnerable groups in the community. It is mandatory for all World Vision staff to adhere to the child protection policy, or otherwise face disciplinary action, which includes termination and prosecution. All the project staff and volunteers will sign the code of conduct and child protection policy, in order to promote child safety and prevent sexual exploitation. The health project will be based on the principal of social inclusion so that emergency health services reach people of special needs, the marginalized groups, the youth. The area of GBV will be given special attention as health workers will be trained on GBV and referral pathways, on clinical management of rape and pyscho social support.
