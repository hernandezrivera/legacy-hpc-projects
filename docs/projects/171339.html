Project Summary

Children 66% of Somalia’s population in need are children accounting for 1.88M of whom 10% are living with disabilities and are vulnerable to multiple child protection risks including but not limited to; exposure to threats of recruitment, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation and violence. These children are in need of immediate child protection services.
HNO 2021 reveals that Boys and girls of all ages in Somalia continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights including family separation, sexual and physical violence, killing and maiming, psychosocial distress, worst forms of child labor, widespread recruitment and use of children, forced and protracted displacements, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/or discriminatory practices and exploitation. Despite interventions put in place the government, to reduce recruitment of children, Somalia has the highest total number of grave violations in the world.
From January to June 2020, the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) verified 1,774 grave violations affecting 1,613 children. These violations ranged from recruitment/use to abductions. Protracted conflicts in Somalia has exposed children to mental health and psychosocial risks with some ending in distress. According to a perception survey conduct by the Child Protection Area of Responsibility in April/May 2020; reveals 71% of child protection partners reported that children were experiencing psychological distress. Partners have reported increased fear and anxiety amongst children due to disruption of their daily routines. Significant progress been made in the provision of stabilizing and generalized psychosocial support to children in need but there is still need to scale up more structured psychosocial support for most severely affected.
Further exacerbating the already dire situation is entrenched harmful traditional practices disproportionately affecting girls and women. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of early marriage and other forms of GBV. About one in 10 girls marry before the age of 15, and about 5 in 10 girls before the age of 18 . Nearly all (99 per cent) of women 15-49 years have undergone female genital mutilation (FGM), with 71 per cent circumcised between ages five to nine (HNO 2021)
Separation from caregivers is another area of concern. According CP AoR, 48 per cent of IDP families and 44 per cent in host communities reported separation from caregivers with death of caregivers/losing parents, child marriage (mostly adolescent girls) and child recruitment (mostly adolescent boys) reported as the main cause. As at July 2020, 10,655 unaccompanied and separated children have been registered, most of whom report experiencing emotional distress. These groups of children are at heightened risk of child trafficking, abuse and exploitation in the camps and communities. Child labour is reported in 55.5 per cent of camp and urban communities with child recruitment and use by parties to the conflict; children are made to beg in extreme heat, shine shoes, sell goods in the market being the most common.
This project seeks to address the myriad protection challenges children are faced with through setting up of child friendly centres, provision of PSS/PFA to crisis affected distressed children, reunification of separated children with parents/ caregivers, establishment of toll free-line at children’s desk to community reporting, establishment of referral pathway for children with specialised needs, establishment of inclusive community child protection structures and provision of dignity and menstrual hygiene kits for adolescent girls affected by crisis.

Project Activities

Output 1: Timely and quality support to children at protection risk in Balcad through expansion of Community based Child Protection services, establishment of referral pathways, and provision of medical / clinical services, psycho-social support, material, legal assistance and awareness creation. Activity 1.1.1: Provision of dignity kits to displaced girls who are affected by conflict and floods in Balcad district middle Shabelle region, Shangani and Garasbaley (Khada) Districts of Banadir. The kits will contribute to psychosocial and emotional wellbeing of recipient girls, particularly the component of sanitary towels which will improve their mobility and confidence. Other clothing items such as dirac (Somali dress), scarfs, and petticoats also play important role building confidence and improving girls and women mobility and reducing protection risks. Activity 1.1.2 Provision of qualified quality counseling services to survivors of child abuse and exploitation and other distress associated with emergency crisis. The survivors will have requisite information on available services and ways to access. The counselor is trained on survivor/child centered approach which will guide service provision to allow survivors to access the information they need and decisions made will be based on consent of caretakers/parents for minors at each stage in the process. Children who need specialized services that are not available at the centers will be linked with mapped service providers. It is expected all the 900 survivors to receive one on one psycho-social counseling session Activity 1.1.3: Establish and capacity build inclusive community based child protection committees (CBCPCs) in each of the targeted districts Output 1.2: Prevention and response to child abuse, including early Identification, documentation and reintegration of separateActivity 1.2.1 Establish child protection desks at each of the three targeted district for timely reporting, processing CP cases and creation of awareness on child protection risks.Activity 1.2.2: Identification of unaccompanied minors and separated girls and boys and reuniting them with their families. Activity 1.2.3: Establish and equip two child friendly centers providing recreation and PSS/PFA services in the targeted districts

Mitigation Measures

Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups who are not often included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be deliberately targeted to partake in decisions. Clear stakeholder analysis will be undertaken to ensure inclusivity at decision making levels of the project. Women, girls and boys are often the ones left out in consultations and decision-making processes because of their age due cultural barriers. The project will ensure community decision makers will be representatives of all, young and old, men and women, boys and girls to ensure the specific needs of each are adequately listened to and addressed. The project will address the risk of exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. This will be done through: staff and vendors signing code of conduct that expressly prohibits the above violations and makes consequences of the same clear. Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services will be addressed through scheduling of distributions, meetings and consultative forums in such a way that they do not pose risks to beneficiaries, particularly girls and women. The distances covered by girls and women to seek services will also be compliant to Sphere Minimum Standards and other best protection best practices. Synergies and collaborations will be developed with other partners in the field shelter and camp coordination to ensure there is complementarity in addressing needs of IDPs in holistic manner. Aid Vision runs health and nutrition projects in Garasbaley of Kahda district and Shangani District. This will complement the proposed protection project.
