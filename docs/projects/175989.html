Project Summary

This project will be implemented in Counties of Wau and Raga in Western Bhar el Ghazal, Budi in Eastern Equatoria and Terekeka in Central Equatoria states respectively targeting total of 43,346 (7224 households) beneficiaries. AFOD intends to reach 43,346 beneficiaries with food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers; 43,346 beneficiaries with agricultural inputs (vegetable, crop & fishing kits), 3,440 people benefit from capacity building & training, asset building, pest management & value addition and set 45 kitchen gardens at nutrition facilities
This will be achieved through a combined approach of distribution of unconditional cash to households in critical need of food and provision of productive inputs, trainings and capacity building to support self-reliance and resilience. This approach will Include the identification and registration of beneficiaries, distributions of complementary livelihood inputs (seeds, vegetable kits, tools fishing), formations of small farmer groups, trainings and capacity building (postharvest management , fish productions and preservations, use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves) and promotion of the diversification & restoration of livelihoods; small grants for small businesses; village savings & loan schemes & micro finance support.
AFOD is proposing to reach a total of 186,976 beneficiaries in 4 Counties by scaling up the food security and livelihood interventions to improve access to food security to avert hunger, malnutrition and destitution of vulnerable populations including Internally displaced persons (IDPs). An estimated budgeted of $ 458,450 will be required to support the vulnerable communities in Budi, Wau, Budi and Terekeka Counties in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is still faced with increased violence, political uncertainty and a deepening economic crisis, worsened by the displacement of the population, the recent floods, and COVID-19 pandemic pushing more people into extreme hunger and are in dire need of humanitarian Assistance. Their livelihoods are at risk of collapsing if nothing is done to save their lives and avert the alarming situations. According the IPC report (December 2020), it’s estimated up to 6.5 million people are faced with severe food insecurity in South Sudan however, this figure is expected to increase to 7.24 million between April and July 2021 leaving 60 per cent of the South Sudanese population in severe acute food insecurity, and at this time many of the families are anticipated to have exhausted their emergency coping strategies. Similarly, around 1.4 million children under age 5 are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021, which is the highest number in three years and will need lifesaving treatment (IPC, Dec, 2020). An estimated 1.9 million children will need treatment for acute malnutrition in 2021 mostly children (74%), pregnant and lactating women 26% (HNO January 2021). In Western Barh el Gazal, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate in the lean season period of April to July 2021 with an estimated 280,000 people (43.3% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, and 54,000 people will likely be in emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, all counties (Raja, Jur River, and Wau) will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. From April to July 2021 in CES, deterioration in the food security situation is expected with an estimated 778,000 people (51.6% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute insecurity, and 227,000 people will likely be in emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, Terekeka county may be classified in emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity while the rest of the countries will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurityIn Eastern Equatoria State, From April to July 2021, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate as the lean season progresses, with an estimated 507,000 people (46.2% of the population) likely to be in crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, and 140,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, Budi county will be classified in emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, while the rest of the countries will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurityThe deteriorating food insecurity is mainly driven by typical lean season factors such as depletion of harvest stocks, high food prices, degraded road conditions that affect market access and functionality, likelihood of insecurity in some counties such as Terekeka (tribal conflicts), Budi (isolated attacks), Raga and Wau (continue conflicts), the economic crisis and associated currency devaluation and diminished household purchasing power, and the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on supply chain systems, markets and household livelihoods and incomes. To avert the alarming food insecurity situations, AFOD proposes to scale up food security interventions in the 4 locations (Raja, Wau in WEBG, Terekeka in CES and Budi in EES to minimize the deterioration of situation into Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) through; provision of food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies, enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock and reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs by vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3);

2 - Project activities

• Sensitization on modern agricultural practices, land rights, peace & reconciliation, child abuse & women rights, HIV/AIDS etc. • Training on good sustainable farming practices to strengthen the existing extension service mechanism to increase agricultural production. • Train local farmers on preparation of organic manure using crop and animal wastes. • Distribution of complementary livelihood support of crop and vegetable kits, tools and fishing kits • Provision of nutrition vouchers (poultry and goats) • Establishment of kitchen gardens demos at nutrition facilities • Conduct cooking demonstration for households. • Support formation of small farmer groups. • Conduct distribution and post distribution Monitoring.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AFOD will ensure through the complaint feedback mechanisms, relevant information on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse is disseminated to the beneficiaries to ensure exploitation of beneficiaries is reported on PSEA. Community leaders, staff and beneficiaries will also be trained on PSEA matters to be able to identify and report exploitation between the beneficiaries and any authority or partners. AFOD shall roll out its whistle blower policy or guidelines, conflict of interest policy and ensure all staff sign and adhere to the organization code of conduct. Hence, no tolerance to exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

A transparent complain feedback mechanisms will be established and strengthened in all project sites. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable persons to provide feedback on the services they receive AFOD two-way feedback platforms will promote beneficiaries to share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. AFOD will continue to strengthen the complaint feedback mechanisms/desk to receive feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries about the services, and the feedback and comments of the beneficiaries managed appropriately. During Community engagement, mobilization and sensitization, any feedback shall be transmitted to the beneficiaries, the project will promote harmony among the beneficiaries and services will be provided to every beneficiary regardless of status (physical state, race, tribe and ethnic dimensions). The community leaders and beneficiaries will be empowered to monitor, report and refer cases to and participate in coordination mechanisms with other agencies.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Transparent feedback and/or complaint mechanism system will be established and strengthened, and AFOD shall ensure two-way feedback platforms are created/improved so that beneficiaries can share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. AFOD will continue to strengthen the current complaint feedback mechanisms/desk to receive feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries about the services, and the feedback and comments of the beneficiaries shall be analyzed and used for decision making. During Community engagement, mobilization and sensitization to increase service uptake, and issues identified during the feedback shall be transmitted to the beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

AFOD will ensure feedback from beneficiaries is compiled and analyzed to inform decision making to improve project delivery. The analysis of the feedback from beneficiaries will be used to inform or guide any changes or improvement required in the project. The beneficiaries will be continuously encouraged to use the feedback mechanism established to give feedback about the services provided and through their community leaders and groups, appropriate solutions will be sought collectively. Project changes or modification will be done with reference to the feedback received from the beneficiaries.
