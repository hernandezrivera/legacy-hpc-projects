Project Summary

Première Urgence Internationale (PUI) undertakes around 180 projects per year supporting nearly 5 million people in 21 countries. PUI has been present in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) since April 2016, and has developed a strong expertise to address the needs of crisis-affected populations through a community based comprehensive integrated response. PUI seeks to implement live-saving activities, strengthen the local capacities, provide an appropriate response and give a primary place to the accountability to affected population in all implemented programs.
The proposed project was designed as an area-based approach, ensuring strong community involvement from the onset in order to tailor the interventions in each targeted area, with the overall objective of improving access to basic services through an integrated health, nutrition and community-based protection response.
The project aim at preventing, mitigating and responding to protection risks and concerns, PUI will contribute to strengthening the overall protection environment in targeted areas through:
Protection Monitoring, Information sharing and awareness sessions, Community Outreach, Individual assistance, one-off cash assistance (IPA) or in-kind support to beneficiaries at high-risk of protection incidence or early survivors, and GBV case management for GBV survivors. The purpose of the intervention is to Strengthen the protection environment for conflict and displacement-affected persons through community-based mechanisms and provision of individual and community protection response for vulnerable persons in displacement affected areas.
PUI will provide Psychosocial Support activities in Health Facilities and Safe Spaces in MMC and in Monguno targeting IDPs and Host Communities. These activities include awareness sessions, individual and support group sessions for adults as well as referrals to specialized services and recreational activities for children and adolescents.

1 - Needs

To date, in North-east Nigeria (Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States) the crisis has affected over 7.1 Million people, representing 53% of the population of the three states combined. The crisis is mainly characterized by attacks against civilians, internal displacement, sexual violence, and the use of children to carry attacks. In 2018 alone, an average of 2,400 individuals per week were displaced within Borno State, which is hosting an estimated 80% of all displacements. Between November 2018 and early January 2019 over 25,000 people have arrived into Maiduguri, and an estimated number of 10,000 into Monguno. Despite the rapid scale-up of humanitarian assistance since 2016, significant needs remain, both in accessible and inaccessible areas. After a decade in crisis, the HRP 2019-2021 still highlights the main forms of need as life-saving, and basic: 60% of the IDPs are living in host communities, making it harder to access them for assistance and putting additional pressure on the already stretched resources; 25% of the IDPs are under five, and 80% are women and children; an estimated 41% of health facilities within Borno State are not functioning; over ½ of the deaths in Borno State were attributed to malaria coupled with malnutrition in 2018; pre-existing and cultural gender inequalities have contributed to limited resources and skills amongst women and girls – increasing their exposure to sexual violence, exploitation and trafficking; General protection concerns (GBV, forced, multiple, and prolonged displacement, lack of access to basic services etc.), major child protection concerns (child recruitment, SGBV, early marriage, child labor and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) ) continue to largely threaten conflict-affected populations in Borno State.According to a Multi Sectorial Needs Assessment made by PUI in March 2020, both experiences of displacement and current living conditions are increasing psychological distress and the need for MHPSS support. Since December 2018, PUI is providing MHPSS services in Bolori II, MMC. From August 2019 to March 2020, more than 1,958 individual consultations have been conducted at PUI supported facility level. PUI will maintain its in-facility MHPSS services in Maiduguri and in Monguno, with individual consultations (aimed at women, men and children), which include counselling, basic behavioral activation and problem management, identification and strengthening of resources. These activities will become even more essential if we consider the possible effects of a pandemic, including social isolation and lack of activity (indirect consequences of Covid-19 preventative measures on mental health). Some of these effects could include increased symptoms of anxiety, depression, increased fear and stigmatization, increased risk of violence and misuse of substances.From PUI protection monitoring data compiled in the previous months, access to basic services remains a main protection concern. People in the current IDP settlements are in need of basic supplies, such as food and non-food items. These provisions are currently unavailable or financially inaccessible. Therefore, the most vulnerable people in the communities will most likely develop potentially harmful coping strategies, further increasing their vulnerability. Also, with the continued population influx, protection issues are likely to increase, including familial and inter-communal violence.Furthermore, the poor living conditions, such as the lack of shelter, the collapse of protective community networks and the erosion of law and order increase the risk of GBV to occur. Domestic violence and intimate partner violence remains one of the most persisting types of violence that is affecting the wellbeing of families, particularly women and children in areas of displacement.

2 - Activities

PUI will contribute to strengthening the overall protection environment in targeted areas through:Protection monitoring: regular monitoring and identification of protection risks and concerns, analysis and identification of trends to inform current and future programming and provide evidence for local advocacy purposesInformation sharing and awareness sessions: development and maintenance of a comprehensive service mapping in targeted areas, dissemination of key messages to the targeted communities through awareness and sensitization campaigns on general protection, child protection, GBV, available services, and health and hygiene promotion etc. Enhancing Community Outreach approach impact through strengthening protection knowledge and self-protection capacities of targeted communities by providing training sessions for the community leaders, structures, and committeesIndividual Assistance: vulnerability screening, timely identification, and effective referral of individuals with specific needs primarily to specialized protection services, but also to others specialized service providers including health, MHPSS, food security and nutritionIn the case of non-assistance available by partners or government, PUI will provide one-off cash assistance (IPA) or in-kind support to beneficiaries at high-risk of protection incidence or early survivors.GBV case management: case management will be available for GBV survivors. It will follow stringent documentation, assessment and supervisory processes. Case workers will follow the standard case management steps of identification- assessment- case planning- implementation- follow up and review- case closure.Provision of Food assistance to individuals with protection acute concerns: 11,600 individuals IDPs/Host community members with adverse protection conditions (rape, sexual abuse, early marriage, forced eviction, child headed household, child labor) referred by protection team for food assistance will receive 12 months of disbursements.PUI will continue to provide psychosocial support activities, directed towards all age groups within the general population with services like:- Community awareness sessions: psychosocial workers and psychosocial awareness volunteers will provide awareness sessions to the general public in the waiting areas of health facilities every morning but also in community. These sessions highlight some important reactions to stress, important warning signs, and how to get help. - Individual counselling sessions: psychosocial workers will provide lay counselling sessions. The aim on these sessions is to provide a safe structured environment for patients to express their feelings, provide good basic listening and communications skills, focus on coping skills of the patients, address the social and psychological needs of patients, enhance the ability of patients to bounce back and restore normality after adverse experiences through exploring support networks and individual resources. - Group support sessions: psychosocial workers will conduct support groups to some patients. The aim of the support groups is share experiences, and activate support networks. - Recreational activities for children and adolescents: psychosocial workers and/or psychosocial awareness volunteers will facilitate sessions for sports and handcraft activities for age-appropriate groups. Proposed activities will include but not be limited to: art, storytelling, group games, sports, group discussions. The aim of these sessions is to provide a safe space where learning, experimenting and playing are encouraged and can be done safely According to the current context, all of the above activities (except some recreational ones with children and young people) can also be adapted and implemented in reduced groups in order to respect social distancing measures, if deemed necessary give restrictions for COVID-19.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The main activities implemented by PUI will continue to include Community protection with Information sharing through service mapping and awareness raising and sensitization campaigns, capacity building (strengthening community’s self-protection capacity); the provision of tailored referrals (including for GBV survivors) ; and general protection with protection monitoring and evidence-based local advocacy.Through the community-based methodology, populations will be aware of and directly contribute to the tailored design and implementation of the proposed protection response within each of the targeted locations, including with beneficiary identification process. Furthermore, targeted communities will be informed about PUI’s Complaint and feedback mechanism through which complaints can be reported to PUI’s MEAL department and Senior Management team.
