Project Summary

The socio-political crisis in the Northwest and Southwest regions has led to a massive population displacement. According to the August Multisectoral Need Assessment (MSNA) 2022, the conflict has forced approximatively 562,807 persons to be displaced in the Northwest, southwest, Littoral and west regions. The NWSW also count 419,734 IDPs returnees and 43,027 Cameroonians refugee returnees. HNO reports for April 2022 some 562,000 people in need of protection. Civilian population continues to face severe human rights violations, unlawful arrests and detention, destruction and extortion of property, threats to life, physical assaults, sexual assaults, rape and child abuse. From January to November 2022, 4,190 priority protection incidents have been reported by Protection Monitoring actors IRC/UNHCR and DRC. This number is an estimate given the geographical limitations of Protection Monitoring projects. 6 out of 13 divisions are covered by IRC and DRC.
The North-West and South-West regions continued to be marked by high levels of insecurity in 2022 with armed conflict, terrorism and crimes beside the imposed lockdowns, and roadblocks.
UNHCR identified 4 key priorities in 2023 and beyond to contribute to the protection response and play its role as last resort provider and the Protection Cluster lead: 1) strengthen the centrality of protection, 2) coordination of lead clusters, 3) emergency preparedness and response and 4) securing solutions. UNHCR will cover the Northwest, Southwest Regions, West (Mifi and Menoua divisions) and Littoral regions (Wouri and Moungo divisions).
Through a community-based approach, UNHCR intends to secure protection and provide solutions through reinforcing protection monitoring and empowering communities to contribute to their own protection, respond to protection incidents as much as possible and search for solutions, and ensure peaceful coexistence among their members. The intervention will respond to critical child protection and GBV cases including communities and women empowerment, legal assistance, support for civil documentation and psychosocial services. Cash-based interventions as a modality, and referrals to possible available services will be an integral part of the response. In kind support will be done where cash intervention is not feasible.
An area-based approach will consist of selecting specific areas especially those with limited or no humanitarian response in the regions affected by the displacement and work with all stakeholders in the priority locations including displaced, host community, IDPs and returnees (refugees’ returnees and IDPs returnees), local authorities, civil society, agencies working in the areas to increase protection outreach and support to the most vulnerable; mitigate tensions, empower local authorities; increase local coordination. The approach will contribute to build sustainability of key services and prevent displacement as well as guarantee a greater accountability to affected populations (AAP). UNHCR will ensure the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and accountability to affected populations through the implementation of zero-tolerance policy. UNHCR will conduct sensitization sessions in the targeted communities on their rights and reporting mechanisms, capacitate implementing partners whose PSEA assessment report proved to be not fully capacitated, and setting up various confidential reporting channels: a toll-free helpline in collaboration with WFP and protection desks during all protection activities will be ensured.
UNHCR will targets 28 communities in 6 divisions 3 (Fako, Meme and Manyu) in the South West Region and 3 (Mezam, Momo and Menchum) in the North West Region, and 2 divisions (Wouri and Moungo) in the Littoral and 2 (Mifi and Menoua) in the West region with an estimated reach of 350,000 persons with the following specific targets:
• 28 Community-Based Protection Committees supported to contribute to protection solution

These monitoring mechanisms and systems will be regularly used for advocacy, interventions, programming, resource mobilization and communications. In addition to monitoring, protection responses will involve protection services for IDPs including activities that prevent, respond to, and mitigate protection risks, including protection against GBV, child protection, family separation, case management and referrals for persons with specific needs or victims of protection incidents, mental health and psychosocial support. UNHCR will establish protection monitoring mechanism in 28 communities in 6 divisions of the Northwest and Southwest regions (Fako, Meme, Manyu divisions in SW, Mezam, Menchum, Momo divisions in NW), 2 divisions (Wouri and Moungo) in the Littoral and 2 (Mifi and Menoua) in the West region with an estimated reach of 13,000 persons in need covered by protection monitoring monthly. Civil documentation capacity building for the support in the facilitation of the establishment 4000 civil documents of children and adults identified through the protection monitoring or referred by others will be provided including disability cards for vulnerable persons living with disabilities. UNHCR intervention will include legal assistance and counselling to 3,800 individuals whose rights were violated. Targeted protection assistance will be provided to identified 11,300 most vulnerable people. Communities will be empowered through 28 Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPCs) involved in protection, GBV prevention and response, and child protection related activities. 12 community empowerment initiatives towards protection solutions will be supported with direct support to approximately 1680 individuals. Both IDPs, returnees and host communities will be increasingly involved in protection monitoring and their capacity build to address and prevent the protection incidents occurring in their areas will be boosted. The capacity building of community-based protection committees will also contribute to build peaceful coexistence and a sense of ownership and responsibility of their own protection, Respond to GBV and child protection incidents. UNHCR will provide support to victims of human right violations including GBV survivors and children affected by conflicts and will strengthen the community-based approach while ensuring that interventions focus on the protection of victims of human right violations. 2,600 survivors and persons with specific needs including children care givers will be provided with multi-purpose cash-based interventions to mitigate protection risks and respond to their immediate protection needs, 3000 persons with specific needs will benefit from individual protection assistance through case management including case management for children and GBV survivors. Legal assistance will be provided to victims of human rights violations including GBV survivors as per need and their consent. 4000 persons of concern including children will be supported to obtain civil documentation including disability cards. Communities will be involved in all the programming process (from needs assessment to monitoring & evaluation of the protection intervention, including planning and implementation). UNHCR will strengthen referral mechanism to available services in the identified communities and increase collaboration to cater for existing operational gaps. All identified persons with specific needs that are not covered by UNHCR support will be referred to existing structures and programs for the access to basic social services for their inclusion to increase their socio-economic well-being,
