Project Summary

Over the years, Zimbabwe has been experiencing recurring economic and humanitarian challenges resulting from various factors which include erratic weather patterns, shrinking economy and changes in monetary policies especially the introduction of SI 142 financial instrument. These factors have compounded severe cash shortages, devaluation of the Zimbabwean dollar over other currencies such as the United States Dollar, price hikes and ultimately food shortages in urban and peri-urban areas. FEWSNET’s food security outlook report of November 2019 revealed that the foreign currency parallel market exchange rates are impacting severely to a large proportion of people in urban areas who are earning the weaker Zimbabwean Dollar. The Food Security Monitoring Outlook report by WFP VAM department in November 2019 revealed an increase in prices of basic commodities by an average of 40% which is eroding the purchasing power of the Zimbabwean Dollar for the vulnerable households.
It is estimated by Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) report for urban food security that 2.2 million people in urban areas will be food insecure during the peak hunger period up to March 2020. Vulnerable households in urban area are founding it difficult to provide for their families, especially during the peak hunger period from January to March 2020 due to high poverty (estimated by World Bank to be at 72.3%) and unemployment rates. According to ZimVAC urban assessment report, Norton urban area had the highest proportion of food insecure people estimated to be at 55% (over 32,132 people) hence is targeted by the project.
The proposed project in Norton urban area will be implemented over 4 months from January to March 2020. The main purpose of the project is to improve the availability of food through scaling up emergency cash assistance to support 3,500 acute food insecure women, men, girls and boys in need including people living with disabilities, illnesses, child headed and the widowed in Norton by April 2020.
The project will target beneficiaries in IPC 3 and 4 through community-based selection process that will be agreed between all the stakeholders and the community. The community will define food insecurity indicators of own choice that matches with IPC 3 and 4 characteristics. Special considerations will be given to marginalised groups such as people living with disabilities, chronical illness, the elderly, widowed, orphans and child headed. Verifications of beneficiaries will be conducted through purposive and random sampling to reduce inclusion and exclusion errors.
Since its establishment in 1967, Christian Care has implemented emergency projects in urban areas of Mashonaland West Province and it has created good reputation with local authorities and the government.

1 - Activities

The project will provide monthly food baskets through mobile cash based transfers. Each beneficiary will be entitled to receive Zimbabwean Dollar equivalent to US$13.20 at the prevailing bank rate. Thus a household with 5 members will receive Zimbabwean Dollar equivalent to US$66. Monthly post distribution monitoring will be conducted 2 weeks after the cash disbursements to determine the outcomes of the cash distributions. Furthermore, the project will conduct weekly market monitoring assessments to determine the market response to the project in terms of price changes and availability of food commodities. Proposed activities will be implemented in coordination with government departments, the local authority of Norton, local leadership and other humanitarian partners that will be operating in the area. The organisation will attend coordination meetings at district, provincial and national level organised by government departments, other organisations and the food security cluster.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured through determining the percentage of women, men, girls and boys in need including people living with disabilities, illnesses, child headed and the widowed who received emergency food assistance through mobile cash-based transfers. The success of the project will be determined through comparisons of the baseline and endline situations. The monthly post distributions monitoring will inform the success of the project on monthly bases by tracking on the outcomes of the project to the targeted households. Baseline and endline data will be collected using a combination of qualitative and quantitative techniques consisting of focus group discussions with communities, key informant interviews with line government ministries, household questionnaires and review of relevant food security documents prior to the start of activities to establish benchmarks. The post distribution monitoring will be undertaken 2 weeks after the cash distributions to gather information on outputs and outcomes of the project as food consumption scores, coping strategy index, food consumption patterns, and food utilization trends through focus group discussions.
