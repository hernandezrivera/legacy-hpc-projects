Project Summary

2021 Post Gu FSNAU nutrition analysis shows that at the national level the median prevalence of GAM has still remained at a serious level of 11.1%. Based on the nutrition cluster figures estimation in 2022 around 1.2 million children <5, will be at risk of acute malnutrition. In addition to this, around 245,909 PLWs will be at risk of acute malnutrition. As per the last Somalia 2021 Post Gu Nutrition Map (Projection, Sept-Nov 2021) published by FSNAU, the GAM level for the 5 target districts of this Action is between serious (10-14.9%) and critical (15-29.9%) and IPC level is between 2 and 3 as per the Somalia 2021 Post Gu Nutrition Map (Projection, Oct-Dec 2021). In 2022 food insecurity and nutritional levels are expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr season rainfall and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season. It is therefore estimated worse outcomes to be in place between October and December 2021 and along 2022. COVID-19 continues to be another major challenge as it has affected the nutrition service delivery modalities. The nutritional status of children under 5 years of age continues to deteriorate in most parts of Somalia thereby increasing their vulnerabilities and predisposing them to morbidity and mortality. The above factors compounded by the poor health service delivery system, high prevalence of AWDs, poverty and reduced purchasing power further predispose the Somali children to all forms of malnutrition including micronutrient deficiencies and therefore require preventive services. This action will be implemented in Beletweyne, Hobyo, Banadir and Afgooye districts targeting vulnerable populations affected by different cyclic crises. The main purpose of this intervention is to mitigate and prevent malnutrition trends in the aforementioned districts with focus on children < 5 years and PLWs. This will be done through provision of quality curative and preventive nutrition services. In order to achieve the Nutrition cluster objectives, the action will be implemented in the 5 target districts where CESVI has a strong operational presence implementing a broad range of interventions across different sectors: Health, Nutrition, WASH and FSL. This Action targets 6 static Health facilities (Elgula. Galbarwaaqo, Elasha, Jazeera, Wadajiir, Helane) and 4 mobile clinics (Hobyo, Beletweyne, Elasha and Jazeera) delivering both lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition services. Both static health facilities and mobile clinics will provide screening and treatment of children aged between 6-59 months with SAM condition without medical complications. Children with SAM condition with medical complications will be referred to the nearest SC run by different NGOs. Children aged between 6-59 months together with PLWs with MAM condition will be treated in the TSFP centers in both mobile and static Health centers. In order to provide quality services, this action will train Health workers and CHWs on revised IMAM guidelines and nutrition assessments. The trained staff will then conduct screening of children aged below 5 years using MUAC tapes. The trained staff will be also expected to undertake IYCF counselling for PLWs and caretakers. The Action will also train mothers on Mother MUAC Screening. There will also be provision of micronutrient supplements in both static and mobile health facilities. This includes, supplementary nutrition products for children aged 6-23 months, PLWs, Iron folate supplements for pregnant mothers attending ANC clinics, vitamin A supplements for children aged between 6-59 months. Inclusion of beneficiaries with disabilities will be taken into consideration during the design, implementation and reporting phases of the activities. Nutrition service is integrated with the health services implemented in the same target locations

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.2 - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance.4.4 - Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors.The project target mainly children and women for the provision of nutrition services. In order to mitigate exposure of beneficiaries to violence during the activities as a result of the distance or location of humanitarian assistance and services, this action as a mitigation measure will mainly deploy mobile clinics to the villages without static health facilities within their vicinity. Services is provided in a safe and protective environment and beneficiaries will be able to access the humanitarian assistance without being exposed to the risk of sexual violence. These mobile clinics will be equipped with trained staff and nutrition supplies to deliver both quality curative and preventive nutrition services to the affected populations. This is to be sure that location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe and both risk sexual violence and attacks from armed sector are minimized. Accountability to the affected population is guarantee through a CFM system in place for patient feedback and to Identify marginalized groups and barriers to accessAs a second mitigation measure and as part of efforts to reduce the risk and strengthening the awareness of protection assistance / referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.), this action will mainstream protection components within the projects ‘s grain. The mobile clinics will also be used on a need base to transfer protection cases to various service provision points. The project will ensure that all staff is trained on PSEA (and subsequently sign CESVI PSEA policy). CESVI ill ensure that the “Do not Harm” approach is in place and the staff have knowledge and skills to safely care for patients. This measure will ensure that any staff in contact with beneficiaries of this project does not engage in any form of sexual exploitation and abuse when handling beneficiaries. This action will put in place measures to ensure that all marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, take part in consultations or decision-making processes affecting the project and disaggregated data are collected. This will be done through discussions with their leaders through various open fora where their concerns and needs will be factored in during project implementation.
