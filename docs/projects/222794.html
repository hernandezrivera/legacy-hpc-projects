Project Summary

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) will implement this project in Jur River County to address the food security gap by reaching 12,600 beneficiaries (1000 boys, 1000 girls, 5,300 men, and 5,300 women) between 01/01/2025 to 30/06/2025. SEM currently has a static presence in Jur River County.
We shall target community members whose access to food is at risk, likely resulting in their households adopting coping strategies like eating less nutritious food, reducing consumption, and going for days without food.
We shall contribute to achieving the FSL cluster strategy 2024-2026 by integrating food assistance (CO1), emergency livelihood support (CO2), and resilience building (CO3).
This project seeks to enhance food production in Jur River County by conducting training and capacity building for farmers. We shall utilize practical, hands-on, participatory training. Establishing demonstration plots at the village level will maximize comprehension, learning, and efficiency, deliver knowledge and facilitate skills transfer. Vulnerable men, women, boys and girls will learn agronomic practices, including land selection and preparation, planting, tending, harvesting and post-harvesting handling to reduce post-harvest losses.
This project will also contribute to the Global Food Security Cluster (gFSC) strategic plan 2023-2025 as we have greener and climate-adapted responses that make us more accountable to communities to reduce the Food Security Cluster climate footprint by training farmers on climate-smart technologies and utilization of climate-smart crop varieties. This will be achieved through smart agricultural practices where farmers in Jur River County will practice multi-cropping, using fast-maturing cassava varieties and establishing kitchen gardens.
We shall also collect beneficiary feedback to inform the implementation of this project and carry out adaptations to best suit the needs of the project participants. We shall utilize our community complaint and feedback form to collect feedback from the participants as it provides information without having to use telephones and can easily be used by those who are not literate. By using structured face-to-face feedback channels, we shall have an interactive process that will enable us to gather information from vulnerable community members like persons with disabilities and individuals who are not literate.

1. Needs

According to the South Sudan IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition Snapshot report published on 18th November 2024 (covering the period between September 2024 and July 2025), the acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating due to the economic crisis repeated climatic shocks – primarily widespread flooding – and conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation – putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 per cent of the population analysed) were classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). A further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The report places Jur River County under Acute Food Insecurity Phase Classification Phase 3- Crisis. The report recommended a scale-up of livelihood support, such as providing seeds and tools (farm inputs) to support agricultural production and return it to surplus levels to reduce dependence on food imports and increase self-sufficiency. Farmers must be supported in adapting to climate-induced environmental changes by training them on climate-smart agricultural practices and distributing climate-adapted (flood/drought resistant) crop varieties. This project has a component that will train farmers on adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills) for them to produce food for their domestic consumption and supply the surplus to their local markets to build their resilience to economic shocks.Therefore, this project's action of providing seeds and fishing kits is well-informed, according to the needs, and will contribute to improving the food security situation in Jur River County, where this project will be implemented. The report also recommends that existing prevention and treatment programs be continued. Prevention efforts should address the major driving factors of acute malnutrition, including improving the quality of diet consumed by children, improved access to safe water and sanitation services, and prevention and treatment of childhood illnesses. This project will implement this recommendation by establishing 120 kitchen gardens to provide nutritious vegetables at home and in schools to improve the diet consumed by children in Jur River County.

2. Project activities

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) proposes to undertake the following activities that are aligned to achieve the set FSL cluster objectives of Integration of food assistance (CO1), emergency livelihood support (CO2) and resilience building (CO3) through carrying out the following activities:1) Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity, and coping strategies for 3,000 (1500 male and 1500 female) project participants to support vulnerable populations in IPC 3 (indicator - number of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers);2) Provide emergency livelihood support that includes fast-maturing vegetable seed & crop kits to 1,200 (600 male, 600 female) households to supplement dietary needs; climate smart field crop seeds to 1,200 (600 male, 600 female) households; and fishing kits to 1,200 (600 male, 600 female) households to enable them to improve their access to food, provide them opportunities for additional income, promote sustainability and to guarantee their long term economic stability (indicator - number of households/people receiving agricultural inputs/vegetable & fishing kits);3) Establish 120 kitchen gardens to increase access to fresh and nutritious vegetables among households and schools in Jur River County (indicator- number of kitchen gardens set at Nutrition facilities/Schools/Household);4) Conduct training and capacity building for 6,000 (1000 boys, 1000 girls, 2000 male, 2000 female) project participants. The project participants will be trained on adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc. This project shall focus on restoring food security and strengthening the livelihood capacity building of vulnerable households. Capacity building, skills & knowledge development, and community engagement in agriculture will be practical, hands-on training and participatory delivered in village-level demonstration plots for both main crops and vegetables in real-time to maximize comprehension, learning, efficiency, and impact delivered at the community level by our Field Officers. To reduce postharvest losses, vulnerable households will learn agronomic practices, including land selection and preparation, planting, tending, harvesting, and post-harvesting handling. We shall also sensitize men to play active roles in agriculture to address long-held stereotypes on gendered labour or desist from adding to existing labour burdens placed on women, specifically related to agricultural work and nutrition management. We will, therefore, train households together and - in educating men and women and boys and girls together - encourage the joint responsibility of food security at the household level (indicator- number of people benefiting from capacity building & training, improved livelihood productivity, market development, Anticipatory Actions).
