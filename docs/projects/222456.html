Project Summary

Humanitarian needs in most parts of South Sudan are at record levels. Access to essential services has been severely disrupted due to triple emergencies of flooding, conflicts and economic decline. According to OCHA, Flooding Situation Flash Update No. 3 (As of 12 September 2024), flooding has affected over 735,000 people across 38 of South Sudan’s 78 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area. (IOM) Displacement Tracking Matrix also indicated that 65,000 people have been displaced, with over 41,000 in Warrap State alone. This needs humanitarian actors to increase their support to civilian victims of clashes in Warrap State and advocate for protection services by addressing needs of women, children, disabled and other vulnerable groups in a dignified and gender sensitive manner through the implementation of resilience solutions to reduce drivers of needs by providing lifesaving child protection and gender-based violence respond services to people facing severe crisis in Warrap State.
In Warrap State, Twic, Tonj North, and Tonj East counties have been disproportionately affected by both inter communal violence and seasonal flooding, exacerbating vulnerabilities in access to safe shelter and essential non-food items. Several report including IRNA and IPC Report highlights these areas as having alarming levels of displacement, with many households residing in temporary shelters or makeshift settlements that expose them to health risks, insecurity, and harsh climatic conditions. The market supply chain disruptions because of the conflict in Sudan, the seasonal deterioration of road networks during the rainy season and associated impacts on market functionality, floods, and cattle-related conflicts and inter communal conflicts and violence
The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024 further underscores the rising vulnerability of these counties, noting the impact of the Sudan crisis and the potential disruption from upcoming national elections, which may intensify displacement and humanitarian needs. Flooding in these counties has destroyed homes and livelihoods, leading to additional displacement, while access constraints and delays in the humanitarian supply chain continue to hinder rapid responses.
Based on the severity of needs as shown by the 2025 HNRP, TOCH intends to respond in order to address the interconnected challenges of child protection, gender-based violence (GBV), and food insecurity in conflict-affected regions of South Sudan. Through an integrated approach, the project will enhance community-based protection mechanisms, deliver lifesaving Child protection, GBV and psychosocial support services through the provision of child protection case management services, delivery of GBV response services, and coordination with other sectors for a comprehensive support services. The intervention will target women, children, and marginalized populations, fostering resilience, safety, and well-being in the face of ongoing humanitarian crises stemming from the triple emergencies. This project will directly contribute to HRP objectives by addressing protection concerns of vulnerable populations, especially women and children by emphasizing multi-sectoral coordination, community engagement, and resilience-building in conflict and disaster-prone areas in the two states of Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal States.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces overlapping crises of conflict, flooding, economic volatility and human rights violations, and exacerbating risks for women and children. The Child Protection Risks include high rates of child abuse, child marriage, and recruitment into armed groups. Breakdown of family structures and lack of access to protective services. Women and girls face systematic sexual violence, with limited access to psychosocial support or safe spaces. Harmful cultural norms perpetuate perpetrate sexual violence against women and girls especially in rural areas where protection services are limited and traditional beliefs and practices override protection laws and policies. TOCH identified a case of a 15 year-old girls who was beaten to near death in Tonj East in August 2024 by her relatives as she was being forced to marry a suitor against her will despite the fact that she was still very young. According to Protection Monitoring Spotlight, September 2024 Weekly Reports by subnational cluster coordination, mechanisms in Warrap State, inter-communal clashes over resources such as grazing lands intensified in Tonj North and Tonj East resulting into unlawful and extrajudicial killings, attacks on civilians, destruction of property and displacements. Incidents of cattle raiding, revenge killings, forced marriages, and rape are widespread, fostering dire violations of human rights and undermining the protection, safety and dignity of children, women and girls. Most rural economies are dependent on cattle and the competition for grazing lands. Displacement and floods disrupt livelihoods, leaving households unable to meet basic food needs, this often resulted to negative coping mechanisms such as early and forced marriages, alcohol abuse and gender-based violence against women, girls and children. The war in Sudan has displaced more than 800,000 people into South Sudan. Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) state, which shares a border with Sudan’s East Darfur and West Kordofan states, has become one of the primary areas of settlement for both Sudanese and South Sudanese fleeing the violence (UN-OCHA. “Sudan Humanitarian Update.” January 25, 2024.). Although severe humanitarian needs are widespread among all population groups in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, both returnees and host community members agreed that newly arrived households from Sudan were particularly vulnerable. Some returnees, though South Sudanese nationals, were displaced to Sudan as young children, were born there, or married into Sudanese families, leaving them with little or no social support in their settlement areas. Some do not speak local languages, and many lack the knowledge to forage for wild foods, fish, or the connections necessary to find casual work. The war in Sudan has directly affected the local economy of NBeG, driving up prices, restricting income opportunities, and reducing access to food at markets. Both returnees and the host community frequently cited volatile prices and reduced income opportunities as major barriers to accessing food and basic commodities. This exposes the population to high protection risks such as child labour, family separation, psychological trauma, and GBV. Building on our existing programming in Warrap and northern Bahr el Ghazal State, TOCH intends to strengthen and expand the protective environment for children, women and girls through "Strengthening Child Protection and Gender-Based Violence Prevention and Response Mechanisms in Crisis-Affected Communities of Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal States" in line with the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) priorities and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) of zero hunger, gender equality, and child well-being.

2. Project activities

On Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Deliver GBV case management and psychosocial support services to 3,000 women and girls. Construct at least 5 women and girls friendly spaces with standard VIP in each of the facility for protection purposeswith integrated psychosocial support (PSS) groups, Conduct community dialogues and awareness sessions to shift harmful norms affecting women and girls and Distribution of dignity kits to 2,500 women and girls at risk of GBV. GP/GBV Coordination and updating of referral pathways through; Updating GBV referral pathways, Commemoration of International Women’s Day, Commemoration of 16 Days of activism against GBV.General Protection Case Management, will be Provided through tailored case management services for vulnerable individuals, including identification, assessment, care planning, and referrals, address cases related to family separation, GBV survivors, and persons with disabilities, elderly individuals, and people at risk of exploitation or abuse. Psychosocial Support (PSS) through Establishing and supporting psychosocial support groups for affected individuals and communities to offer individual and group counseling to address trauma and promote mental well-being.Community-Based Protection Mechanisms will be established to Strengthen and support community-based protection committees to enhance local protective capacities by training the committees on monitoring, reporting, and responding to protection concerns. Conduct community sensitization sessions on rights, legal frameworks, and protection mechanisms, advocate for the prevention of harmful practices, such as child marriage, GBV, and discrimination. These mechanisms will undertake awareness-raising and Advocacy for protection. Establishment of Safe Spaces and Support Services through setting up safe spaces for women, children, and vulnerable groups to access services in a secure environment and provide life skills training and recreational activities in these spaces. Family Tracing and Reunification FTR programs will be implement for separated and unaccompanied children by collaborate with relevant agencies to ensure safe reunification or alternative care arrangements.Capacity Building through training of local stakeholders, including government, community leaders, and service providers, on protection principles and humanitarian standards to build capacity for case management, referral pathways, and protection monitoring. On protection Monitoring and Reporting, this will be done to monitor and document protection risks, trends, and gaps through regular assessments and field visits and share findings with coordination platforms to improve advocacy and response.
