Project Summary

This project will be implemented in three target states (Blue Nile, White Nile, and South Kordofan). The overall aim of the project is to contribute to reduction of mortality and morbidity among the affected persons (internally displaced persons, returnees, and host community members) through provision of integrated Nutrition, Health, and WASH emergency and recovery interventions to vulnerable men, women, boys, and girls in the selected states during the year 2022. The total population targeted by the project is
estimated at 74,732 individuals including children under five years of age, pregnant and lactating women. The nutrition proposed interventions aim at improving the nutrition status of the target population and reducing the nutrition-related mortality and morbidity rates among the most vulnerable groups of the population, especially children under five years of age, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) at the selected geographical areas (Blue Nile, White Nile and South Kordofan) through provision of timely and comprehensive preventive and curative nutritional services. The main activities of this project include: 1.
Supporting nutrition screening for children under five and PLW, 2. Supporting management of MAM cases in the TSFP, 3. Supporting referral of malnourished children from the community to the TSFP, 4. Following up the MAM cases and defaulters tracing. 5. Providing micro-nutrients supplementation to the children in need. 6. Supporting nutrition counseling on infant and young child feeding. 7. Supporting capacity development of the care providers, community-based and civil society organization staffs.
The total budget required to implement this project is estimated at 267,500 USD.

1 - Needs

According to Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) 2021, the total number of people estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance is more than 13.4 million (29% of the population) with an increase of 44% in the year. This increase in number can be justified by several factors such as large scale population displacement, influx of refugees and climate and socioeconomic conditions across the country. Vulnerable host communities and residents in need are estimated to be 9.8 million which represent about 71% of the total people in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan. Considering the type of the need, there are 9.2 million people are in need for basic health services; 57% of them are female, 55% are children and 45% are older people, >18 years of age. It is estimated that 2.3 million are women of reproductive age who are in need for comprehensive reproductive health services. In White Nile The average level of global acute malnutrition (GAM) in the target communities of White Nile is 10.10 (HNO 2022) these communities have very limited access to nutrition services. According' to the IA assessment, this is largely due to the influx of refugees out camp. The inability of these households to produce food has made it a top household expense. The cost of food caused by escalated inflation, exacerbated by the current macroeconomic crisis, and the majority of vulnerable households are unable to engage in wage labor. The lack of access to food has had direct effects on children. The SMoH WN reported> 30% of children under five (CU5) were stunted and 8.55% moderated wasted and 1.75% severe wasted in 2018 (S3MII), while the under five mortality 65.8 (MICS 2104) Households are engaging in negative coping mechanisms, including consuming low quality and less expensive food, decreasing the portion sizes at meals, eating fewer meals in a day, borrowing food from neighbors, and, to a lesser extent, harvesting immature crops and collecting wild foods. In the proposed HNO 2022, the WN state is projected to have more then 203,000 children under five and more than 75,000 pregnant/ lactating women with malnutrition and in need for assistance aid. In Blue Nile, the average level of global acute malnutrition (GAM) in the target communities of Blue Nile is 10.00 (HNO, 2022). ADD proposed in 2022 to target 15 localities in South Kordofan, Blue Nile, and White Nile. This project is part of a multi-sectoral program (WASH, Health & Nutrition) provided to beneficiaries by ADD in the targeted areas. This approach ensures synergistic effect to maximize impact and hence achieve the aim of the project, and based on nutrition needs & gaps the project will focus on IYCF and nutrition treatment of SAM & MAM cases at both health facilities and community levels.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The proposed activities are in line with the Strategic Response Plan and the Nutrition sector objectives. These activities are the following: The project aims to respond to the nutritional needs in the selected areas through provision of comprehensive package of both preventive and curative nutrition services through the following activities: 1. Strengthening the Case management of moderate acute malnourished (MAM) girls and boys (6- 59 months) and PLW through improved outreach and referral services. 2. Supporting routine screening and periodic MUAC screening campaigns for active case finding. 3. Supporting referral of defaulted cases to treatment centers 4. Training and capacity building of Ministry of health staff on CMAM, IYCF, GBV, Monitoring, Supervising and Reporting. 5. Support GBV activities at nutrition centers and provide a special support to disabled persons. 6. Providing IYCF education and counseling for mothers/care takers. 7. Conducting meeting with key influencers at sub-national levels to raise awareness and support their engagement and development and reviewing of the key nutrition documents. 8. Organizing meeting and training for community based organizations at locality level to contribute to sub national plan and processing, and to increasing their capacity on nutrition aspect, 9. Supporting consultation workshop for CSA members at national and sub-national level to ensure that the civil society organizations are able to contribute to deceiving the nutrition goals and targets. 10. Hosting SUN-CSAs planning and review meetings. at national level (4 meetings).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ADD staff was trained on prevention of sexual exploitation of beneficiaries by aid workers will orient the field staff on the subject matter as a part of ADD’s commitment to ADD’s values and Humanitarian Accountability Framework. Envisioned complaint response mechanism for ADD shall have adequate scope to address the protection concerns of the target population. Wherever applicable, ADD will develop selection criteria to choose individuals/households for assistance with dignity kits, soap,mosquito nets, blankets, latrines, etc (in collaboration with other sectors). Relatively vulnerable individuals such as severely ill, children under 5 years and pregnant women shall be prioritized, when the services are crowded by implementing triage system. ADD will also mainstream protection concern in activities like men and women patient privacy during consultation, gender designated latrine sites inside the facilities. ADD will ensure that the women have a say in when and where the health coordination meetings are conducted.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Feedback and Complaint Mechanism: add will consult communities on establishing a viable feedback or complaint mechanism. Most possible options are use of mobile feedback and complaint/suggestion boxes and/or a telephone hotline system to collect feedback from beneficiaries. Complaints will be validated through initial screening processes; an investigation will take place in case of valid and serious cases. ADD has a pool of senior staff with experience in handling complaints during the project cycle, ADD will make necessary arrangement to ensure that staff in field locations are well trained in handing complaints. ADD will train its CBOs in Feedback and Complaint Mechanism. Information sharing: ADD will organize sessions with beneficiaries and HCs to communicate relevant details about the project, especially related to the activity to be implemented in their respective community. ADD’s mission, presence of feedback mechanism, entitlement of the community (if applicable) is few other elements that will be shared. Do no Harm: ADD will follow principles of ‘Do no harm’ prior to and during the intervention of the project. One of the necessary things is to be aware of ‘connectors’ and dividers. In order to avoid confrontation between host communities and refugees, a portion of activities will be allocated to adjacent host community. Some health non-food items like dignity/hygiene kits, soap, some FSL items will be provided also to vulnerable host community members as part of integrated activities. ADD will also work towards boosting availability of nutrition services in nutrition sites. ADD understands that many communities in the project areas are diverse and will explore ways to unite the community; one of the ways is to promote participation of people from various ethnic backgrounds among IDPs and host communities together as members of HCs. ADD hopes that serving in a social platform like HCs can be a way to increase coherence and connectedness. ADD may include the refugees and host communities around target in its intervention related to conflict resolution through creation of economic opportunities
