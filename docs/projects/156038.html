Project Summary

The education in Syria is under severe threat with almost 2.10 million children and youth without access to the educational system and 1 in 3 schools damaged or serving other purposes. The situation is quite severe in Afrin, Jandairis, Atareb and Ar-Raqqa sub-districts which score 4 and 5 on the severity scale while 220,000 children from these areas are in need of education, especially in the case of IDPs and returnees. With its unique access to these areas, some of them hard to reach, Bahar is prepared to support the integration of children in school while increasing the quality of education and providing teachers with necessary training to strengthen their skills. The toll of actions is in line with the 2019 HRP responding to the priority regarding the inclusion of students with disabilities and rehabilitation of learning spaces to make them suitable to accommodate such cases. Through its aim, the project contributes to the two objectives of the sector by scaling up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth (aged 5-17 years) while enhancing the quality of formal and non-formal education within a protective environment. As a result of the project, 18,000 children (9900 girls and 8100 boys) will benefit of formal and non-formal education in 17 schools and 500 teachers (275 women and 225 men) will receive dedicated training to transform school in a more-friendly and safe space. The approach is integrative as it targets to fill in the current gaps within these schools.
Project aim: Enhancing education’s quality and inclusiveness by securing safe and equitable access to education for vulnerable students. 18,000 children (9,900 girls and 8,100 boys) along with 500 teachers (275 women and 225 men) will be targeted by non-formal education classes and courses.
The aim is to be attained through a cluster of activities encompassing:
• Rehabilitation of existing infrastructure to accommodate students with disabilities (17 schools will be targeted in formal settings) – Raqqa (7 schools), Afrin (4 schools), Atareb (6 schools)
• Provision of fuel and heaters to ensure proper conditions for teaching during winter for 13 schools in Ar Raqqa and Atareb in formal settings (for 3 months)
• Provision of learning materials and school bags for 18,000 students in formal settings engaged in formal and non-formal activities (coming from 17 Schools)
• Provision of school transportation for 100 students with disabilities in all locations
• Delivery of professional development to education personnel on child-centred and protective pedagogy in formal settings in Atareb mainly
• Covering incentives for 200 teachers engaged in formal setting for non-formal activities in Atareb’s schools
• Provision of literacy and numeracy classes in non-formal settings for 6000 students in 6 schools in Alatareb.

Needs

Based on the draft Education HNO 2019, the vulnerability of school-aged children with no access to learning opportunities has increased over the eight years of the crisis, putting children at high risk of being exploited, abused and have their rights violated. The intensification of hostilities in some areas has negatively impacted thousands of children who lost access to education opportunities due to forced displacement, schools closure and lack of teaching personnel. One of the most affected governorates regarding the educational crisis were Ar Raqqa and Aleppo. In Ar-Raqqa, the education was disrupted for more than one year and half, with deep consequences over students and teachers. This situation associated with protection risks, made children even more vulnerable to integrate in schools. Aleppo was confronted with large waves of IDPs in 2018 making impossible the educational attendance of all children, especially in informal settlements. While Afrin is considered to be more stable due to the Turkish administration, children are hard to be accommodated in schools because of their high number and poor infrastructure. 2019 HNO disclose that almost 1,540,000 people are in need of education in Aleppo and Ar-Raqqa counting for 25% of WoS needs of this sector.To contribute to the efforts made by donors and implementing partners to the current crisis, Bahar has selected four sub-districts and 17 schools for intervention. The gaps in the schools are:• Poor infrastructure (deteriorated conditions of classrooms, lack of school furniture, lack of gender based WASH facilities)• Lack of fuel for heating in winter which affects the participation of students in classes• Lack of learning materials (at all levels) and school bags• Lack of means of transportation for children with disabilities to reach up the school• Lack of incentive for teachers engaged in non-formal education

Implementation of activities

The activities are implemented in parallel in all schools. The approach is constructive and integrative. The project builds on the existing system covering the costs of formal and non-formal activities which complement and close the chain for students’ integration in school. The activities to be supported are:- Rehabilitation of 17 schools in all locations: minimum 100 classes with their ceiling, doors windows, walls repaired, walls repainted, electric installation repaired; fence installation for the yard; provision of school furniture and repairing of damaged furniture; WASH gender based facilities restored.- Provision of fuel for 3 months and of heaters for 13 schools operating in Ar Raqqa and Atareb in formal settings (for 3 months). 9000 children with benefit of it.- Provision of learning materials and school bags for 18,000 students in formal settings engaged in formal and non-formal activities (coming from 17 Schools). There will be one time delivery of the materials at the beginning of the project.- Provision of school transportation for 100 students with disabilities in all locations. The transportation will include daily stipend needed to travel/ special car to ensure travel from home to school and back for children/- Delivery of professional development to education personnel on child-centred and protective pedagogy in formal settings in Atareb mainly. The training will be based on Education in Emergency curriculum and will last around 8 h each. Groups of 15 teachers will get engaged in the activities. Follow up on the content will be ensured through dedicated monitoring team and Education specialist.- Covering incentives for 200 teachers engaged in formal setting for non-formal activities in Atareb’s schools. The incentives will cover a period of 9 months of activity.- Provision of literacy and numeracy classes in non-formal settings for 6000 students. The courses will take place during the afternoons and it will aim at reintegrating to school children which drop out of it or never attend it.- The duration of activities will be 12 months covering the whole educational year. The activities will be closely monitor though specific dedicated instruments. The M&E team will conduct monthly random visits to each school and interview minimum 2 teachers per location and 4 students (2 girls and 2 boys) to catch their satisfaction regarding the implemented activities to document progress along with remedial actions. The conclusions will be shared with the programme team for lessons learn exercise and future strategic planning. Complaint mechanism will be in place for each location. Reporting will be made accordingly to the donor’s provision. 4W will be submitted to Education cluster.

Protection Risk Analysis

Bahar is adopting a steady attitude towards all categories of risks, compiling a plan for protection associated risks to education sector. The focus will be to secure the safety and integrity of children in schools. In this respect, security plans and evacuation training will be delivered to children and their teachers. Security situation will be closely monitor to cease activities whenever the danger is imminent or most likely to happen. Emergency focal points will be named in each establishment. Schools are located in the vicinity decreasing the risk of long travel of children. In addition, the protective environment in schools will be strengthen by educating children and teacher against bullying and other types of violence. Special attention shall be paid to girls, more vulnerable to drop out due to early marriage. Teachers for non-formal activities will dedicate time to speak to parents and increase their awareness about the importance of education. Incentives received by teachers in non-formal education will be in line with the salaries of the teachers working in the public sector. The team will organize households visits to explain the importance of formal education and avoid children migrating from formal to non-formal activities. The learning materials and bags will be provided to all children attending the school to avoid conflict and in pursuit of the “do not harm” approach. All staff, Bahar and teachers, will be trained on PSEA and Child Sexual Exploitation Policy and will have to obey with the Code of Conduct, reporting immediately sensitive cases. Monitoring of Child safety as part of regular monitoring will be provided.The risk of the community to reject the project is low since Bahar is present in the community for long time and has strong relations with the Local Authorities. In addition, these relations will be nurture through constant information sharing. Community leaders will be involved and consulted to avoid any opposition towards gender equality. The team from the field will be empowered to be able to manage the project in case the borders are closed and the management team is unable to access the locations.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Risk 1: Changes in the security situation. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation Risk 1: Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct regular security assessments. Staff members and teachers will be trained in safety and security best practices, including mine risk education. 2- Risk 2: Fluctuating of fuel prices. Likelihood: Medium Impact: Medium Mitigation Risk 2: The prices will be identified at the early moment as possible and a contract will be signed with the vendor to secure the reserve. 3-Risk 3: Limited number of beneficiaries accepted in the program, which might cause inter-community tension and pressure on the implementing staff. Likelihood: Medium Impact: low Mitigation Risk 3: Set a clear-cut selection criterion and inform the community of the limited resources and selection criteria. Support all children from a school.4-Risk 4: Regarding capacity-building, there is a risk of competition to attend due to inadequate coverage and different approaches. Likelihood: Medium Impact: low Mitigation Risk 4: Clear selection criteria will be advertised based on needs and further impact that the teachers might have on the children. Assumptions:1- The community will get involved and support the implementation of the project. 2- The Ministry of Education/Local authorities will continue to support the formal schools with salaries for teachers and curriculum for children.3- The educational year will not be disrupted for long period of time allowing children to graduate the class.4- The resources will cover all needs.
