Project Summary

The project will promote Access to Basic, Quality, Protective and Inclusive education to 10,400 children (5,304 G; 5,096 B) aged 3 to 18 living in the PoC of Bentiu, Malakal, Bor and Juba 1 and 3. The program will seek to incentivized enrollment and attendance to learning, reduce risks of cognitive underdevelopment while providing access to safe and protective learning environments through a wash and protection build-in components.
The intervention seeks to build on the good practices and results achieved through the UNICEF education project implemented by INTERSOS between 2018 and 2020, where over 75, 000 children including 28,000 girls were enrolled in pre-primary, primary, secondary and accelerated learning schools in 5 different states including Upper Nile, Jonglei, Unity, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria. The project aims at addressing holistically the needs of schools to ensure a protective environment and will include three different components of WASH, protection and education. Through the WASH components it is expected the spread of COVID19 and cholera as well as other water born diseases will be reduced in the school environment. The education component will seek to ensure the return of all children to school after COVID19 trough the rehabilitation of TLS to ensure the respect of social distancing and the provision of PPE for learners and teachers. Additionally to responde to the generalized problem of school dropout, learners in South Sudan face due mainly to displacement, general poverty and early marriage/ teen pregnancy for girl that has been exacerbated during the pandeminc the project foresees the payment of teachers’ incentives, the provision of education material and the provision of remedial classes for children having dropped out. Furthermore, support to accelerate learning programs to ensure older teenagers have access to educational opportunities is also foreseen. This will be done in close collaboration with Juba University given the overall good results of the partnership in the former project.
Finally, the project hopes to enhance the protection environment of learners through the presence of caseworkers in schools identifying and directly observation vulnerable cases at risk of drop out or other child protection threats while closely leasing with general protection services for a complete response. Additionally the case worker in the school will support the training of teacher in live-skills, PSS and PSEA mechanism.

1 - Needs

In 2021, 3.4 million children, representing 51% of all school aged children need educational support. A figure even more dramatic when compared against the 3.1 million children in need of 2020 or the 2.9 million of 2019. These numbers of show an increasing trend of educational needs across the country exacerbated by multiple and simultaneous shocks. In 2020, South Sudanese population were negatively impacted by an increase in inter-communal violence, unprecedented flooding that submerged entire counties and the negative impact of COVID19. The education sector was amongst the most severely affected by COVID19 measures that imposed the closure of schools since March 2020 until October 2020, for candidate classes and February 2021 for the rest. The closure of schools significantly reduced the protective environment of children, numbers of cases of child labor, early marriage and teen pregnancy significantly increasing when compared to the years before. IDP Children leaving in PoC sites in Bentiu, Juba and Malakal and Bor were forced to stay at home and wonder the streets of the sites without access to any other safe spaces. Now, the education partners face the challenge to reopen school ensuring the safety of learners and teach regarding COVID19. An enormous challenge when considering 17% of primary school lack access to drinking water and 31% do not have functional latrines. In the PoC the challenge also relates to space since schools have no space to ensure the social distancing of learners during classes. Beyond the physical barriers, COVID19 and its impact in the general economy of the country has exacerbated economic barriers to education. It is estimated that the main barriers to access education in 2020 include: lack of books (22%) lack of materials (21%), school fees (16%) and uniform (7%.) The other challenge will be to ensure girls return to school. According to data from FSNMS+, before COVID19, only about 17% of girls where enrolled in pre-primary, 58% on primary and only 25% in secondary education. After COVID19 this low numbers will be difficult to increase given the general trend of sending only boys to school, exacerbated by the increase of early marriage from girls during the COVID19 period with 27 % respondents to the unpublished report on COVID19 impact reporting early pregnancy and 5% early marriage as the main barrier to access school. Involving the entire community and working closely with School management committees and Parent-teachers associations remains the only sustainable way to raise awareness on need of sending girls to school. Furthermore, the number of schoolteachers, already low before COVID19 is likely to decrease during 2021 due to the lack of payment of teacher’s incentives during the pandemic that forced them to find other livelihood opportunities. Creating new teaching opportunities paying particular attention to the training of women teachers will be paramount to ensure access to education in South Sudan in the next year. Finally, specific attention will also have to pay to ensure children with disabilities have access to specific and adequate educational opportunities. It is estimated that 15% of school aged children suffer from some cognitive or physical disability. Promoting inclusion will therefore remain a priority to ensure quality access to education.

2 - Project activities

The project will adapt a holistic aimed at increasing access, to quality, inclusive and safe learning, while ensuring that the spread of COVID-19 is significantly reduced in the PoC sites of Bentiu, Malakal, Bor and Juba 1 and 3. To achieve its major objective the project will implement three different components: 1.Improve WASH in schools with the aim to reduce the risk of COVID19, cholera and other water borne diseases through: 1.1 Establishment of handwashing facilities with soap and water and water tanks in schools 1.2 Provision of PPE for teachers and learners. 1.3 Provision of dignity kits for schoolgirls 1.4Training of teachers on water chlorination, social distancing measures and ways to implement them in the classroom and hygiene promotion. 1.5 Establishment of hygiene clubs in schools 1.6 Hygiene promotion messaging. 2. Increase educational capacity through: 2.1 Rehabilitation/ construction of TLS to guarantee respect of social distancing 2.2 Enrollment and retention of children in pre-primary, primary and secondary schools in the PoC 2.3 Establishment of remedial classes for children having drop-out of school during COVID19 or in school delay. 2.4 Provision of ECD (Early Child Development) kits 2.5 Provision of teacher’s incentives 2.6Training of school staff teachers in: school management, EiE (Education in Emergency) pedagogical skill and child-centered methodologies to improve learning process and early child development 2.8 Promote enrollment and recruitment through “back to school” and “back to teaching” campaigns. 2.9 Establishment of school clubs and strengthening of PTA to guarantee the participation of the community. 2.10 Adaptation of standard school SoP to COVID-19 19 restrictions ( organization of the physical space, teachers protocols, etc.) 3. Ensure a protective environment in school through: 3.1 Provision of case management in school aimed at identified and refer the most vulnerable cases. 3.2 School safety audits aimed at identifying and mitigating protection risks on the way to school and inside school 3.3 Establishment of PSEA mechanisms in schools through training of School staff and teachers 3.4 Training of teachers on life-skills, psychological first aid and disaster risk reduction. 3.5 Establishment of peer to peer learning workshops for teachers and learners.

3 - Project indicators

Measuring the impact of the education intervention will be done by a robust MEAL plan developed to measure both outcome and outputs of the project in a quantitative and qualitative way. The measure of outcome results will include the collection of a baseline, to have a base against which to measure the success of the intervention. Additionally, indicators measuring overall satisfaction of beneficiary will be measured on the regular basis through PDM exercises and quality surveys performed quarterly in all locations. The MEAL plan will include above all the following indicators: Outcome 1. % of school population targeted reporting satisfaction on WASH facilities in schools -Number of learners and teachers with access to enhanced wash facilities in schools.-Number of handwashing facilities constructed / rehabilitated.Number of water tanks rehabilitated. -Number of children and teachers with access to PPE -Number of teachers trained on hygiene promotion focus on COVID19 preventive measures Outcome 2. % of school aged children living in the PoC sites enrolled in school - Number of targeted children enrolled in formal education services-Number of children receiving cash for school-Number of teachers receiving incentives-Number of TLS rehabilitated -Number of teachers trained -Number of school committees created -Number of schools with code of conducts in place-Number of schools with operational SOP adapted to COVID19Outcome 3. % of children reporting an improved feeling of safety in school at the end of the intervention in comparison to the beginning-Number of children receiving case management-Number of safety audits in schools performed-Number of teachers trained on life-skills, PFA and DDR

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

For complaints related to PSEA and CP policy issues, two members of each CRM committee (one men and one women) will be selected by the community as PSEA / CP focal points. They will receive specific sensitivity training and direct communication line (phone and airtime) with the PSEA focal point in mission in Juba. The focal point will then follow INTERSOS PSEA protocols and liaise directly with the internal audit.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Each CRM committee will have one representative of the most vulnerable groups of the community including among others PWD, elderly, youth, and women. The CRM committee mechanisms was set up to ensure most vulnerable members had access to the feedback and complain mechanisms after other feedback mechanisms, including complaint boxes showed their inadaptation to the context precisely by the important number of illiterate individuals. It is expected that through this inclusive mechanism feedback of the most vulnerable will be collected on a regular basis.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IEC material will be provided in all schools informing learners, teachers and school staff about the feedback mechanisms established. The messaging will be tailored by age to ensure child friendliness. In each school a CRM committee will be established composed by school management staff, teachers, parents and child representatives since the age of 7. Additionally, complaint boxes will be established in each school supported. Finally, the help desk staff will be trained to receive complaints. On a monthly basis, each CRM will provide a matrix with all feedback receive through all the mechanisms.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

On a monthly basis, program staff on the ground will meet the CRM committee and add to a pre-established matrix all feedback received by the beneficiaries will the measures to be taken to address said feedback. This matrix will be shared with INTERSOS coordination team in Juba that will do a strict follow up of the measures taken during field visits. For the PSEA and CP complaints, a separated matrix will be kept by the focal point where all feedback receive will be stored as well as the measures taken. This matrix will be shared on a regular basis with the internal audit.
