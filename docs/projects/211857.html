Project Summary

The 2023 SMART survey conducted by the Nutrition Cluster in July and August 2023 in NWS shows a slight increase of the combined GAM rate, referring to 5.6% (in Aleppo the rate was higher 6.5%), while the stunting prevalence showed no change compared to 2022 data. Maternal wasting reduced slightly in both Women in Reproductive Age (WRA) (9.8%) and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) (9.9%). The 2024 HNO highlights that 1.48 million PLWs, adolescents’ girls and children under 5 years (CU5) are in need of nutrition services in NWS with increased rates across malnutrition parameters compared with 2023. The number of acutely malnourished CU5 in need increased by 36% while the children in need for preventive activities also increased by 12%.35,061 PLWs in need for treatment and 175,375 PLWs in need for BSFP and IFA supplements have also been highlighted across Northwest Syria. Cluster data shows that the total number of functional nutrition services facilities have started decreasing from January 2024 due to lack of funding.
RI’s nutrition project includes 4 components:
1. Nutrition Surveillance through nutrition screening for CU5 and PLW. In addition, RI CHWs will conduct MUAC screening in the target community.
2. Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM): RI will implement outpatient CMAM program in 8 HFs in target governorates. The program includes OTP for SAM children, Targeted Supplementary Program (TSFP) for children with MAM and PLW with acute malnutrition.
3. IYCF Counselling through nutritionists and midwives conducting one- to-one IYCF-E counselling to PLW visiting the HFs.
4. Micronutrient Supplementation distribution for pregnant women at least for three months during pregnancy, children 6-24 months, and CU5 at least for four months.
Protection Risk Analysis:
R1: lack access to HFs for women and girls; as mitigation measure, RI will ensure community mobilization with CHWs reaching out to PWL at community level, conducting home visits and facilitating tracking defaulters under the CMAM.
R2: risk of GBV for women/girls while accessing HFs; as a mitigation measure, RI will provide separate spaces for women and girls to access nutrition services and strengthen the link with protection preventive services.
R3: increase risk of intimate partner violence due to stressors related to food insecurity, will be mitigated by integrating GBV prevention and response services into nutrition programs, including training staff on GBV identification and referral pathways to the required services.
In terms of safeguarding, RI has zero tolerance towards safeguarding incidents with a robust system, enabling the safe submission and handling of sensitive information, and assuring that service-users/staff are aware of how to report a violation of safeguarding or Code of Conduct (CoC). To prevent and mitigate SEA risks, RI will: provide HR orientation/refresh sessions for staff on CoC and Safeguarding mechanisms; deploying a PSEA focal point; develop and disseminate informational materials for program participants, outlining their rights, available support services, and avenues for reporting concerns; conduct surveys to assess the effectiveness of safeguarding measures.
Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures: Assumptions: Unimpeded and safe access to the targeted health facility for all populations, including women, girls and people with disabilities; Border crossing remain open for humanitarian access; Targeted communities positively accept proposed intervention R1: Increased military activities leading to a surge in trauma cases, disruption of healthcare services, and displacement of people. Mitigation measures: Preparedness measures such as establishing temporary caravan healthcare facilities. R2 Natural disasters, compounded by limited sanitation facilities, pose significant safety and health risks. Mitigation measures: RI constantly monitor the context and re-adapt the programming, if necessary.
