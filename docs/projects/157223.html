Project Summary

The aim of the project is to respond to the life saving needs and improve the resilience of affected populations by providing essential life saving primary health care
This project is in line with Health Sector Objectives: “Providing primary health services including referral services for vulnerable population affected by natural and manmade emergencies." The MBHP includes outpatient treatment of communicable and non communicable diseases, maternal and child health services, facilitated referrals and health promotion activities to ensure aid reaches people affected by crises such as conflicts, outbreaks of disease and natural disasters. “Strengthening the capacities to prepare, detect and respond promptly to public health risks or events at federal, state and locality level.” Developing institutional capacities to address public health threats while strengthening preparedness through staff training on topics to improve prevention and response protocols, surveillance systems and close monitoring of disease trends. “Contribute to the reduction of maternal and child morbidity and mortality among vulnerable populations” by focusing on the needs of children under 5 years, including outpatient treatment, immunization services and comprehensive maternal care.
The project is in line with Sudan's Multi Year Humanitarian Response Plan Outcome 1: “Populations affected by natural or man made disasters receive timely assistance during and in the aftermath of the shock.” Outcome 2: "Displaced populations, refugees, returnees and host communities meet their basic needs and/or access essential basic services while increasing their self reliance.”

Needs

The health needs are large especially among the most vulnerable populations such as IDPs and returnees because of a decade long crisis which disrupted public health sector that was already lacking. In addition the rise of population influxes into Sudan, increases of disease outbreaks and food insecurity and lack of nutritional knowledge has further strained health capacity. According to the latest HERAMS (Health Resources Availability Mapping System), only 44% of health facilities provide the MBHP, and this percentage is even lower in remote areas in areas of new displacement. Some of the Primary Health Care (PHC) facilities that are available are only partially functional; many are in remote areas and can only provide limited services due to lack of supplies and dilapidated infrastructure. The situation is exacerbated even further due to the reduction of funding and withdrawal of non governmental organizations (NGOs) and humanitarian organizations providing assistance in these areas. Amongst crises affected populations, Women and children are especially at risk of malnutrition, and gender based violence (GBV) and high maternal mortality rates due to lack of care during pregnancy and delivery. To improve delivery of health assistance and strengthen the overall health systems, resources need to be allocated to establish functional PHC facilities that offer MBHP, referral systems to secondary and tertiary care facilities and support health worker training (especially in preparedness/early warning and response protocols). Regarding sustainability and resilience, the project initiatives include reinforcement of basic infrastructure of health facilities, health promotion and disease prevention. Since 2014, IOM has been supporting the Ministry of Health (MOH) to strengthen health services and rehabilitate PHC facilities in Sudan and has the experience in providing health related services to affected populations. In 2016, IOM provided lifesaving health assistance through PHCs and mobile clinics in North, East, West and South Darfur to over 55,000 vulnerable people and trained 220 health workers to conduct health promotion on several health topics.

Activities

All proposed activities will be coordinated with the MoH, WHO and with health partners working in the same areas. Some of the implemented activities will also be implemented though national NGOs who will be selected through a competitive bidding process advertised through the local health sector at the state level. Planned activities include: 1. Rehabilitation of selected PHC facilities - clinics to be selected in coordination with the MoH. 2. Provide a complete range of the MBHP encompassing: a. General clinical and trauma care; b. Child health, including immunization (routine and campaigns) c. Management of communicable diseases, including support to the prevailing disease surveillance, early warning and response system; d. Sexual and reproductive health, including maternal and newborn health, management of Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) and HIV/AIDS; e. Management of non communicable diseases and mental health; f. Environmental health, including waste management and vector control.3. Facilitate referrals from community to primary, primary to secondary/tertiary emphasizing adequate continuity of care. 4. Facilitate community led initiatives for health promotion, including training of Community Health Workers (CHWs) on the topics of common public health risks, infection prevention and control, reproductive health and other positive health practices. 5. Support the MoH by running clinics and mobile clinics to provide the following services: outpatient treatment for communicable diseases (such as influenza, malaria, Acute Water Diarrhea (AWD), and non-communicable diseases (such as diabetes, high blood pressure), child care, mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) screening for malnutrition and referrals to nutritional program, immunization, maternal care, antenatal care (ANC), promotion of good reproductive health and food general health promotion. 6. Integration of nutrition activities with livelihood interventions

Impact on environment/natural resources

Medical facilities will have proper medical waste management according to WHO and MOH standards. IOM will also provide health programs that raise awareness among IDPs about environmental health risks. The awareness campaigns will focus on issues like preventing the spread of infectious disease through vector control, proper waste disposal, and personal hygiene.
