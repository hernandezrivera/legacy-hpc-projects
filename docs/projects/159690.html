Project Summary

The current context in the oPt is marked by imposed closures and the stop of peace negotiations between the Israelis and Palestinians. In the Gaza Strip, blockades and political divide continue to marginalize the population. In the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the Education cluster estimates that children with disabilities are one the most affected by the protracted and acute crisis: they lack basic assistive devices that would facilitate their learning in the classroom and require psychosocial support (PSS) and remedial education. Since 2014, Handicap International (hereafter as HI) has strived to improve the access of children with disabilities to education in Gaza. In 2017, HI started directly supporting the capacity building of educational staff and implemented activities aiming to make schools more accessible. HI also provided specialized educational materials and developed technical guidelines on the inclusion of children with disabilities in education and developed an inclusive education practical manual. Besides, HI is supporting 3 special schools and 11 pre and primary mainstream schools in Gaza to develop child-friendly and accessible education environments, and to improve the delivery of adapted education services responding to children’s individual needs. In Gaza, the Middle Area and Rafah, HI develop emergency response plans providing psycho-social support, and organizes inclusive summer camps. Furthermore, HI closely collaborates with mainstream education service providers to build teachers’ capacities to adopt inclusive education strategies. Building on a two-fold strategy, the project’s objective is to increase the access to quality inclusive education for children with disabilities in improved education facilities. The first component focuses on community actors to identify and provide tailored services for out-of-school children with disabilities and their families to support children’s enrolment and continued attendance in school. The second component will aim at building the capacities of schools and teachers to respond to the needs of affected children. The project falls under the Humanitarian Response Plan’s (HRP) strategic objectives 2 and 3 and Education Cluster’s objectives 1) Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children and 2) Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to potential emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school-based psychosocial services. HI will financially and technically support three local implementing partners for the proposed project. The project will respond to the education cluster objectives through the identification of out-of-school children, the provision of personal support for children with disabilities: enhance their mobility, provide visual and hearing assistive devices, transportation, school kits, education materials and stationaries, school-based structured individual, and group psychosocial services. HI will also support the provision of holistic services through referrals to other stakeholders. Furthermore, HI will aim to develop an enabling educational environment through the provision of technical support for mainstream and special education service providers. These will be supported to respond to the needs of affected children through the development of adapted processes and tools benefiting both children with and without disabilities. HI will further provide educational materials, tools, and resources and create a welcoming and accessible educational environment for children with and without disabilities. The project will be implemented in partnerships with 3 local organizations in Gaza.

1 - Needs

According to the Education Cluster, 497,000 (236,000F, 250,000M) school and kindergarten (KG) age children and teachers, of whom 20,500 are children with disabilities, are affected due to blockade and recurrent conflict in the Gaza Strip. The PA fiscal crisis and the financial cuts from major donors will likely affect the PA’s ability to provide adequate education services in Area C, East Jerusalem, and Gaza. Services such as counseling, school transportation programs and rehabilitation may downsize significantly, causing further deterioration in the humanitarian situation. The 2017 census of the Palestinian Census Bureau of Statistics indicated that 35,675 children with disabilities live in the oPt, 27.4% of which have never accessed education. According to UNICEF Out-of-School Children (OoSC) report published in 2018, 1 in 3 children with disabilities aged 6 to 9 and about 1 in 2 children with multiple disabilities of the same age do not attend school. Also, 32.5 % of children with disabilities are out of school. Girls with disabilities aged 10-15 are out of school at higher rates (36.6 %) than boys (26.3%). The study also shows that those enrolled are likely to drop out of school as their education progresses, especially children with disabilities aged 15-18 (48.5%) and those aged 12-14 (29.1%). Girls with disabilities face additional barriers due to the cultural and traditional norms in their communities, which result in them being mostly hidden. In addition to these elements, the Palestine Center for Democracy and Conflict Resolution (PCDCR, 2019) indicated that there are gaps in support to students with injuries due to the Great March of Return demonstration related to home adaptation and inclusion within the education system. 52.4% of injured students were dropped out from schools and stopped receiving any type of education inside or outside the school due to their injury and health condition, the distance of the school, school accessibility and the financial needs for transportation and remedial education. According to EducAid (2018), none of the assessed public-schools principals or teachers received training related to inclusive education, and they rely on special education teachers to interact with children with disabilities and children with learning difficulties. The focus group discussions held with children highlightedthat access to pre-school, primary and secondary education is easier for boys with disabilities than for girls with disabilities, as the latter are more likely to face sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse while growing-up. Male parents, therefore, prefer to keep them at home and restrict their movements.

2 - Activities

Building on a two-fold strategy, the project’s objective is to increase the access to quality inclusive education for children with disabilities in improved education facilities. The project will target the Gaza Strip. The first component (output 1) focuses on community actors to identify and provide tailored services for out-of-school children with disabilities and their families to support children’s enrolment and continued attendance in school. This will be achieved through a sequence of activities, which are:1.1 Provide technical and financial support to local partners and CBR programs to identify boys and girls with disabilities who are out of schools through awareness raising and initiatives (Environmental building); 1.2 Support CBR programs and local partners to develop family social support plan for identified out-of-school girls and boys with disabilities (Remedial); 1.3 Support referral pathways to identified boys and girls with disabilities to specialized rehabilitation services, special education services and mainstreaming schools in collaboration with the existing referral networks of HI partners, that will improve their inclusion in the regular schools according to their social support plan (Remedial); 1.4 Provide mobility, hearing, visual and educational assistive devices and technologies for boys and girls with disabilities in need to overcome environmental barriers preventing equitable access to education (Remedial for the education cluster); 1.5 Provide transportation for vulnerable boys and girls with disabilities to enable their access to schools and reduce their risk to dropout from schools (Remedial); 1.6 Donate educational materials and stationery to girls and boys of poor and vulnerable households to maintain their access to education (Responsive); 1.7 Support home reasonable accommodation for boys and girls with disabilities to facilitate their access inside their homes (Remedial); 1.8 Provide inclusive open days and recreational activities for boys and girls with and without disabilities with a particular focus on girls and women due to emotional, cultural and social barriers they are facing (Responsive); 1.9 Provide school-based psycho-social and outreach psychoeducation for boys and girls with disabilities and their parents (Responsive); 1.10 Provide educational counselling support for boys and girls with disabilities (Responsive). The second component (output 2) will aim to build the capacities of schools and teachers to respond to the needs of affected children. This will be pursued through a series of interlinked activities: 2.1 Support targeted school to develop inclusion action plans through the use of School Inclusiveness Assessment Tool (SIAT) (Remedial); 2.2 Support the development of adapted and active offline and online education resource platform that can be used by girls and boys during emergency and non-emergency situations (Responsive); 2.3 Support technically and financially preschools, mainstream and special schools to develop an accessible child-friendly environment for boys and girls with and without disabilities through school accessibility (Remedial); 2.4 Provide technical support to male and female teachers of pre-schools and schools to respond to the personalized needs of boys and girls with disabilities (Remedial); 2.5 Donate educational materials and stationery for female and male teachers in pre-schools, mainstream and special schools in order to provide inclusive education (Responsive); 2.6 Provide educational counseling support for male and female teachers to respond to the individual needs of children through regular technical support and documentation of good practices (Responsive).

3 - Indicators

HI will ensure that the project is monitored and evaluated based on HI’s Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation (PME) policy. In line with the policy, the following system will be established: Planning: set up of project steering committees, project steering tools, and quarterly risk assessments. Monitoring: project quarterly reviews, monthly data collection, beneficiary database, administrative and financial tools, and regular self-assessments. Evaluation: satisfaction monitoring visits, and final participatory evaluations. Project managers and technical teams will be responsible for the implementation and monitoring of the project. They will provide direct technical support to the partners and teachers. HI’s regional technical unit and inclusive education technical advisors will monitor and guide for the project to be in line with the national, international and HI standards through site field visits and technical support missions, in addition to remote support. In terms of indicators, the following will measure the progress and impact of the project: -Outcome:60% of 500 (225 girls and 275 boys) with disabilities in Gaza Strip respectively enroll in pre and primary schools; -Output 1: 1.1 500 (225 girls and 275 boys) in Gaza out-of-school children with disabilities are identified; 1.2 85% of trained CBR workers and local partners’ staff are able to identify and develop family social support plan for out-of-school girls and boys; 1.3 70% of identified out-of-school children with disabilities receive special and mainstream services based on their specific needs through referrals; 1.4 100 (45 girls and 55 boys) children with disabilities in Gaza benefit from assistive devices; 1.5 25 (12 girls and 13 boys) with disabilities in Gaza benefit from home adaptation; 1.6 450 (225 girls and 225 boys) children with and without disabilities in Gaza participate to inclusive open days and extra-curricular activities; 1.7 500 (275 girls and 225 boys) students and 500 (100 males and 400 females) parents receive school-based and outreach psychoeducation and psychosocial support services; -Output 2: 2.1 6 pre- and primary schools are more accessible and equipped with educational equipment/materials;2.2 1 accessible digital educational resource platform to be used during emergency and non-emergency situations has been developed; 2.3 60 (45 females and 15 males) preschool and primary school staff improve their capacities to respond to the personal psychosocial and educational needs of girls and boys with disabilities.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

HI is implementing a safeguarding policy including an internal code of conduct, a policy on protection from Sexual Exploitation, a Child Protection policy and an Abuse and Anti-fraud, bribery and corruption policy. To enable beneficiaries, affected populations and partners to provide complaints, different mechanisms are in place including the availability of HI’s Safeguarding Focal Points. Focusing on partners, training and awareness sessions will be provided for the selected local partners’ staff. In addition, HI supports its local partners in having complains mechanisms in their entities as complains box. Partners’ Child Protection workers are entitled to inform beneficiaries through information and awareness sessions about the complaints mechanisms, the safeguarding policy and their rights to report about any suspicion or any inappropriate behavior. These mechanisms develop safe ways of working and promote beneficiaries’ well-being, including the protection of children from maltreatment, abuse and violence, and deterioration or drop in children’s health or development.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In line with its strategy of intervention, HI will implement the activities of this proposed project in partnership with local organizations representing the local communities, both civil society organizations and mainstream and special schools. In a partnership relation defined into a signed agreement, HI will be responsible for the implementation of specific activities and for the support to partners, while local partners (CSOs and mainstream and special schools) will implement other activities such as services provision to girls and boys with disabilities and their parents. The beneficiaries will be active participants and contributors to the implementation and monitoring of project activities. At the level of monitoring and evaluation of the project, each project in HI has a steering committee, which includes parents’ representatives, partner representatives and project teams.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Accessibility is at the core of the project and will be ensured to all beneficiaries considering theirs needs and gender, through physical access to services and through consideration of their specificity by professionals –especially interacting with children with disabilities. Additionally, all project and technical tools/materials will be tailored to suit the specificity of the Gaza Strip context and needs of children with focus on children with disabilities.
