Project Summary

Palestinian children have been facing many challenges due to different crises and recently the actual world-wide pandemic COVID-19 which have a lot of impacts on child rights especially on the right to Education, child protection and Health. The purpose of this project is to foster access to safe quality and inclusive education to 10,895 (51% girls & 5% CWD) children affected with COVID-19 in 5 ARA locations in Gaza Strip. To reach this goal, different interventions will be carried out during the implementation phase of this project.
The majority of the school children in ARA have no reliable connection to the internet or IT devices. To reach these learners during the period of the COVID-19 emergency, we propose to identify adapted solutions for alternative and home-based education that do not require a high performing and permanent internet connection through engaging school children in the local CBOs to attend remedial/catch up classes and benefit from the established IT lap. In addition, support MoE to allocate hotline to guide/support parents of children in grades 1-4 in Arabic, Math, and English through answering their phone calls by MoE teachers.
Besides, to support distance learning and the continuation of education in hard to reach and rural communities, and following the MoE and UNRWA policies, the project deigned to develop radio lessons and audio creative self-learning materials to be disseminated during COVID-19 which lead to massive school closure and education interruption . In the period following the crises, these radio/audio and other self-learning materials can be adjusted to serve as a complement, rather than an alternative, to classroom teaching. Such lessons can be designed in an interactive manner whereby students pose questions via phone text messages to be answered by a moderating teacher. prior to this, Educaid will provide standard technical guideline for developing interactive and inclusive summary lessons for radio, TV and inclusive self-learning materials using Diamond Kite Project*
Educaid will support back to school campaign through the distribution of assistive devices in favour of children with disabilities inside 25 targeted schools.
The project will be based on the Do No Harm framework with a gender transformative approach to empower girls and mothers ARA in the Gaza Strip
* The DKP is Educaid's toolkit for education in emergencies. it designed at aim of fostering active and inclusive education based on the Universal Design for Learning and centered on the four main access of experiential learning: I AM; I CAN; I SHARE; and I CARE. According to the DKP methodology and tools, CBOs teachers with support from Educaid educators will be able to respond to the psychosocial needs of targeted children, developed their life skills and learning process, increase the level of resilience in emergency and vulnerable context, and in the same time, promote the inclusion of CWDs.

1 - Needs

Around (83,453) school children access the E-learning in the governmental schools and the percentage is only 30% among UNRWA school's children (Education cluster meeting conducted on 29 Sep 2020). The percentage of individuals (10 years and above) who used the internet from anywhere reached 65% in Gaza Strip, also, data showed that the percentage of male internet users reached 72% compared to 69% of females (PCBS, 2020). This imposes the necessity to find alternatives and complementary modules to E-learning program. Given that 43.1% of families don't have computer meanwhile 96.6% of families have a mobile (PCBS, 2017), which brings ideas to reach students who are drop out or at high risk to drop out through phone calls or broadcast products. On the other hand, 49.1% of female are with educational attainment of secondary and above (PCBS, 2017), which means that half of mothers are not qualified to play the teacher role. With 47% unemployment rate in Gaza and 64% among youth which considered evidence of increased resorting to negative and harmful coping mechanisms on the part of vulnerable groups, particularly children, as shown in the rise in the school dropout rates, child labor and child marriage(Education Custer Strategy, 2020-2021)With. Prior to COVID-19, 57.5% of schools working on double or triple shifts which overloaded teachers and reduce teaching hours which mean little support for slow learners and CWD and increase the rate of drop out. (Statistical Yearbook 2019-2020)Recently, COVID-19 hit oPt in early March 2020 which triggered the declaration of a state of emergency and cause closure of all education facilities. The obstacles to e-learning in Gaza mainly involve infrastructure, weak internet networks, power outage, and insufficient awareness among students and their families. Accordingly, MoEHE has requested to provide more support to the low achievers, boys and girls to enable them mastering the basic skills on reading and writing in addition to the numerical skills in Math.There is (1,907 Male, 1,347 Female) children with disabilities are registered in the academic year 2019-2020 (MoE, 2020). EducaAid conducted a multi-sectorial assessment (annex). the main findings are explained as next: • 75% of children with disabilities are facing lack of accessibility to E-learning platforms • 45% of children with disabilities are facing lack of accessibility to curriculum materials, since the materials are not adopted to different disability types. • 65% of children with disabilities, who have accessibility to E-learning platforms, face additional difficulties mainly noise, lack of privacy, electricity cut, not adopted desk and furniture, and unsuitable home atmosphereEducaid also conducted an opinion poll for 26 female teachers of grades 1-4 in targeted schools (7 Governmental and 8 UNRWA across Gaza Strip) in the ongoing "I CAN project". The main findings are:• Low engagement percentage among students to E-learning• The best ways to transfer information to children are: summaries of lessons, interactive learning games, offline application on mobiles• Some mothers teach their children wrongly, since they are not qualified. • It's very difficult for teachers to conduct both: group counseling sessions for mothers and classes for children, in addition they daily receive hundreds of whatsapp messages and calls• Fresh graduated teachers considered good alternative to support teachers after school since they have innovative ideas and tools; they are more familiar with technology and IT solutions; conduct psychosocial support; and Could be an alternative to parents rather than teachers• CBOs is a good choice to complete the school role, since CBOs can support both, parents and children not only children like schools.

2 - Activities

In line with Education cluster SO2: Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children and SO3: Children and school staff in vulnerable schools are supported in preparing and responding to emergencies. In 2021, Educaid will focus on increasing remote access to quality and inclusive basic distance education for 10,895 (51% girls & 5%CWD) school-aged COVID-19 affected mainly OOSC children in the targeted five locations in ARA (Al Shujaeya, Beit Hanoun, Juhr El Deik, Al Burij, and Rafah ). 5 basic schools per area will be targeted. The specific activities include the following: QUALITY: Activtiy1.1 Conduct capacity building to five local CBOs in ARA on fostering humanitarian response in emergency to increase access to quality inclusive educationThe actions includes: A1.1.1 select 5 CBOs in ARA based on selection criteria(established before at least 5 years, located in ARA, accessible, have at least two core staff); A1.1.2: recruit three fresh graduated teachers per CBO (2 female, 1 male); A1.2.3 conduct capacity building to selected fresh graduated teachers in COVID-19 prevention, mitigation, Diamond Kite Project-DKP, remote learning and safe classroom management; A1.1.4: Equipping targeted CBOs with 10 laptops, electricity, and internet connection per each CBO in order to facilitate the engagement and strength participation of school children in the distance learning platforms, A1.1.5:conduct face to face remedial/catch up classes to dropout school children or who are at high risk to drop out (reach 750 school children, 150 children per each CBO, 51% girls). A1.1.6: train 750 parents (at least 51% mothers) in COVID-19 prevention, mitigation, and safe remote – learning for students during this pandemic, including a focus on the importance of girls' education and safe return to school measures once school re-open indicators.QUALITY: Activity1.2: Provide standard technical guideline for developing interactive and inclusive summary lessons for radio, TV and inclusive self-learning materials using DKP (Joint activity by Educaid and the local partner SDF)ACCESS: Activty2.1: Support MoE in development and distribute remote/distance learning materials mainly radio lessons and inclusive self-learning materials using the DKP (reaching 10000 school children, at least 51% girls & 5% CWD). (Joint activity by Educaid and the local partner SDF) With close coordination with MoE and UNRWA, Educaid will select 25 boys and girls primary schools (10 UNRWA, 15 Government) to be targeted with the distribution of self-learning materials on 400 school children from grades 1-4 per each school, means in total targeting (10,000) children including 500 children with disability to receive adapted self-learning materials, that adapted by the partner SDF. Prior to this, and in compliance with MoEHE and UNRWA policy, Educaid will support the MoE and UNRWA to develop self-learning materials following the Diamond Kite Project methodology in order to enrich the content with more updated materials. Besides, 50 (51% female) MoE teacher will develop audio and video lessons using the studio that establish by Educiad in June 2020. The activity includes support MoE and UNRWA in development and broadcasting standard messages on COVID-19 with standard WHO/MoHCC/MoPSE messaging for broadcasting during radio lessons. ACCESS: Activtiy2.2 Support MoE to allocate hotline for guiding and following up school children and their mothers in distance learning (activity of the local partner SDF)In coordination with MoE and UNRWA, Educaid will implement a pilot technique targeting parents of school children over Gaza Strip in grades 1-4 in the subjects Arabic, English, and Math, 30 graduated teachers will be available from 9am to 4 pm to support, guide, and follow up parents mainly mothers in emergency situations and during distance learning. The graduated teachers provide the service inside the selected number of MoE and UNRWA schools (in close coordination the MoE and UNRWA) to answer phone calls and support parents. INCLUSIVE: Activtiy3.1: Distribute assistive devices in favour of students with disability (reach 50 CWD)In coordination with MoE and UNRWA, Educaid will support back to school campaign through distribution assistive devices to support students with disabilities to better access the school and engaged in the education process effectively as their peers. In this regards, Educaid specialists will conduct a specific assessment on the individual level in order to identify the specific needs of children with disabilities. According to the results of conducted assessments, Educaid specialist will identify the suitable assistive device. The specific assessment will be conducted in strong cooperation with the child school staff and family in order to guarantee to match the specific needs of the child to ensure better access to school life and promoting the engagement in the education process

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Educaid is regularly conducting capacity building to the staff at aim of increasing knowledge about core humanitarian standards, sphere project, human rights approach, IHL, IHRL, Gender, disability and protection mainstreaming and children rights EducAid is ensuring Do No Harm in two levels:1- At strategic levelThe project activities identifying and responding to the most at risk beneficiaries (vulnerable school children and their mothers including children with disabilities). The Activities not create any unintended negative consequences affecting beneficiaries or the community mainly during COVID-19 through setting different scenarios of implementation to ensure commitment to delivering services with full respect to government instructions during emergency. The project activities designed to promote meaningful access ensuring safety and dignity of beneficiaries through participatory approach that engaging them besides direct key actors (mainly MoE, UNRWA, Education Cluster, Teachers, student with disabilities) in order to design the intervention based to their needs and with full respect to right to education2- At operational levelThe implementation methodology ensure that the four protection mainstreaming principles mainly Do No Harm are integrated and it became part of each phase of the project life-cycle. the activities prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: with close consultation with MoE and UNRWA mainly during the catch up/remedial classes and the distribution of printed materials also during the implementation of capacity building programs. All activities take to consideration the social distance, prevention measures, appropriate distribution times and locations. Secure meaningful access: the project ensure assistance and services are delivered according to needs without discrimination. Services and assistance are provided in good quantity and quality, within safe and easy to reach locations, are known by the beneficiaries and are accessible by students with disabilities. Ensure accountability through enable the affected beneficiaries and communities to play an active role in the measurement of the quality of interventions that affect them, and put in place effective and easily accessible mechanisms for suggestions and complaints. there will be a complaint box in the CBOs in addition to hotline managed by Educaid M&E officer. Beneficiaries will be updated about the progress of dialing with their complaints or suggestions. Ensure participation and empowerment through fostering inclusive and effective participation of beneficiaries through building and maintaining a direct communication channel between project staff and beneficiaries in order to encourage them to raise voices, speak out and to claim their rights and empower themselves. the project related staff will be requested to conduct regular field visits (with respecting to government instructions) to listen to beneficiaries and collect their feedback then back to them with a response. A complaint response mechanism will be put in place to ensure that feedbacks and complaints from the beneficiaries are reported confidentially, and that complaints are properly addressed

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Educaid will ensure engaging and participation of beneficiaries through conducting pre-assessment to group of representative sample of direct beneficiaries also through conducting interviews with community representatives and CBOs focal points to know in depth about the characteristics and the culture of the community in early stages of the project mainly during breakdown activities. A clear image will be formed to design the implementation methodologies to ensure equal and impartial access to services. For example, the time of implementing mothers workshop will be suitable to them through avoiding the time of doing house works, may we will do separate workshops for fathers and mothers; the engagement of girls will be increased through selecting well-known and trusted and adapted CBOs while selecting the morning time to conduct the remedial classes, a special attention will be paid to prevention measures to protect participants from COVID-19. A special attention will be paid to children with disabilities and their mothers through disseminating adapted key messages, and adapted printed self-learning materials. During the project implementation phase, monitoring visits will be conducted to make sure that the activities are on the right track, also a feedback and complaint mechanism will be accessible and adapted for all through allocating the complaint box and a direct call number who will be written in braille and large print to be accessible for all. Responding to the complaints, corrective actions will be taken if needed to ensure inclusion of all.At the end of the project, an evaluation will be conducted and learned lessons will be collected for the future projects. Additionally, the M&E officer will make sure to collect disaggregated data by age, gender, disability, vulnerability, refugees, and others to ensure taking to consideration the different needs of different targeted groups
