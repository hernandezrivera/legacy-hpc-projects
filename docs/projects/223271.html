Project Summary

Leer and Rubkona Counties stands a risky areas for flooding, communual conflicts and more especially influx of returnees in the past few months from Sudan. And so meeting the daily food and other basics needs remains a huge challenge. Local population continues to suffer including the returnees from Sudan conflicts and the displace population by flooding and conflict. Local markets are down with limited supplies. This is due to distance and the cost of the transportations. Children and women are on the high risks, therefore Cash Voucher Assistance is significant scope for increasing the use of Cash and voucher assistance in humanitarian responses. In appropriate contexts, this approach allows beneficiaries a wider and more dignified choice of assistance, based on their preferences; and it empowers vulnerable groups. The overall objectives of this project is to; 1) Provide lifesaving multi-purpose cash transfer to vulnerable households to meet their urgent basic needs, 2) Provide timely and flexible cash transfer to vulnerable affected individuals and households, ensuring that assistance is responsive to their immediate needs and priorities, 3) Implement MPCA that empowers vulnerable individuals and households to enhance their capacity to withstand shocks and crisis.
At the end result; 1. Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs.
2. Vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action informed by communities’ priorities.
3. Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand the impact of shock is increased and resilience-building opportunities are optimized. Greater Upper Nile Organization (GUNO) will use, Cash transfers as the only aid modality designed to offer people affected by crisis a maximum degree of flexibility, dignity and efficiency equal with their diverse needs in this project. It will encourage GUNO to better understand the local economy and market dynamics in a holistic way.
GUNO will therefore Conduct inception meetings to the local stakeholders for smooth operationalization of the project,Empower project staffs in order to create an enabling environment where staff can contribute their best and develop their potentials, conduct sessions with relevant agencies and enumerators to orient them on the available tools, provide instructions on data collection methods, Conduct clear and detailed cash feasibility study as well as financial service providers’ mapping in the area conduct Cash feasibility study, risk assessment, and Delivery mechanism assessment to assess the feasibility of cash and voucher assistance (CVA), consider the applicability of CVA to meet a range of needs (i.e., use for different sectoral outcomes) and populations (e.g., Returnees, host population) and incorporate the feasibility of non-traditional delivery mechanisms, such as mobile money, block chain-based digital currencies, cryptocurrencies, or other token-based systems, into the overall assessment in high-priority areas in Leer and Rubkona Counties, Review and analyze secondary data to update the risk register and identify data gaps in Leer and Rubkona on CVA, identify and register vulnerable beneficiaries for cash voucher assistance, identify and collaborate with the CVA actors in Leer and Rubkona to identify concrete pilot opportunities to implement new digital CVA delivery mechanisms, with at least one pilot focused on an identified ‘innovative’ technology solution (if feasible), provide timely and flexible cash transfer to vulnerable affected individuals and households, provide assistance that is responsive to their immediate needs and priorities, and implement MPCA that empowers and raise awareness of vulnerable individuals and households to enhance their capacity to withstand shocks and crisis.

2. Project activities

Panyijiar, Leer and Rubkona Counties remains one of the most fragile counties in the Unity State. In 2023-2024, Greater Upper Nile Organization (GUNO) participated in a series of assessments on CVA especially in Rubkona and Mayom Counties supported by IOM. Although a revitalized peace agreement was signed in 2018, the implementation of key aspects remains partial. The areas are facing with multiple interrelated crises. Conflict, subnational and localized violence, climatic shocks, and the ripple effects of the global food crisis continue to push hundreds of the locals into dire hunger, disrupt livelihoods, and aggravate rooted vulnerabilities, threatening peace and stability. Limited access to basic services reinforces marginalization and rooted inequity, especially for the most vulnerable groups in hard-to-reach areas. Four years of consecutive floods and drought have also brought Panyijiar, Leer and Rubkona Counties to the forefront of the climate crisis, displacing over 15,000 people. As a result, humanitarian needs remain alarmingly high, far above available resources; in 2023, over 9.4 million people are in need of urgent assistance and protection, a shocking 76 percent of South Sudan's population, increased by over 854,000 people from 2025. The continuing Sudan crisis and related cross-border population movements continue to worsen an already dire situation, since the outbreak of fighting in mid-April, 2023, the influx of people fleeing Sudan has increased exponentially. As of July 14, an estimated 171,181 people – 91 percent of whom are South Sudanese nationals – have fled South Sudan as returnees, requiring immediate humanitarian and protection assistance. The actual numbers are likely to be higher as crisis-affected people entered South Sudan without registration.GUNO is aware that, Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) demonstrated to be effective for the 2022 humanitarian response in South Sudan. More than $7 million of humanitarian assistance was delivered through CVA, reaching over 1 million households through food security and livelihood interventions. Multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) became important when responding to the immediate life-saving needs in South Sudan. MPCA will empower vulnerable households to make independent decisions on their urgent, basic needs with dignity and freedom. Protection, Shelter and Non-Food Items (S/NFI), and WASH clusters commonly use cash-for-work and cash-for-assets modalities as part of their response. The counties relies heavily on imports of food items and non-food items. GUNO will use market-based programs, including CVA, as a response modality in their strategies. Cash will be used to ensure gender equality and disability inclusion as part of the GBV AoR response strategy. Aware that S/NFI Cluster consider to use CVA to meet the NFI and health needs in emergencies when markets support the demand and to increase access to services.To overcome some of the encounters, GUNO will promote the use of MPCA in a multi-sectoral approach to meet people’s basic needs. The approach will be linked to safety net initiatives to ensure their access by persons with disabilities and to mitigate unintended consequences, such as mitigation measures for women and girls receiving cash support. GUNO will use Joint Monitoring Market Initiatives (JMMI) to monitors the market situation, prices, and currency exchange rate to support CVA programming by humanitarian actors and informs the minimum expenditure basket. GUNO will use the JMMI for situational analysis of the exchange rate and evolving market trends to inform advocacy strategies and exchange rate policy reforms. GUNO as a CWG member is represented in the national- and state-level to support cash as a modality in humanitarian responses.
