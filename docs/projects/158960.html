Project Summary

According to the education 2020 HNO, there will be an increase in the need for humanitarian assistance in 2020. This is based on an analysis of trends over recent years which revealed a marked deterioration in the overall humanitarian situation in oPt, which has pushed more families into poverty, undermining their ability to afford basic education supplies and transportation costs for girls and boys, and increasing the risk of drop out. This project responds to Cluster Objective 2: Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, catch-up classes and school based psychosocial services. Arabic and Math classes will be provided to 5,000 boys and girls between 8-12 years old and enrolled in grades 3, 4, 5, and 6, who are at risk of dropping out in vulnerable public schools in the Gaza strip. The out of school report (2017) showed that more boys than girls are at risk of dropping out of school. Low achievement is given as one of the main reasons for dropping out of school.
The project will enhance their basic skills in Arabic language and Maths as a way to strengthen their abilities both in these and other subjects. A training will be provided to around 210 teachers (50% females) on remedial education. Learning materials and stationery will be provided to learners and teachers to run the classes. These activities are categorized under the responsive and remedial objectives of the HRP education cluster.
This intervention has been developed in consultation with relevant stakeholders, targeted teachers, students and parents. All of them showed great needs for this intervention as it provides the children with a safe access to education and protecting them from dropping out. Moreover, a consultation was made with the counseling team from relevant stakeholders and UNICEF child protection to provide the targeted children with a safe access to counselling services provided by relevant stakeholders or family centres supported by UNICEF. In addition, several assessments were conducted in this regard and showed the great impact of remedial education on the students’ performance such as the evictions in the Gaza Strip- Needs Assessment Report conducted by NRC in October 2018 which stated that “[t]he need for support in covering basic school-related expenses and paying for remedial classes for their children to positively impact their academic achievements.”
Project Locations: The criteria of schools’ selection will include the following - located in 116 vulnerable localities identified by the vulnerability mapping conducted by Islamic Relief, UK, 2017 and considered by relevant stakeholders with low learning outcomes. In order to mainstream child protection, targeted schools will be those close to the family centres to allow vulnerable children in need for protection or those who were injured in the “Great Return March” to be referred to the family centres for protection. The 60 vulnerable and marginalized public schools located in the directorates with low achievements amongst children and these are: Beit Lahia and Jabalia in North Gaza Directorate, Gaza city in West and East Gaza Directorates, Al Maghazi, Deir Al Balah, Al Zawayda in the Middle Area Directorate, Khan Younis city in Khan Younis Directorate, Abasan, Bani Suhaila, Khuza’ah, Al Qararah, East Khan Younis Directorate, and Rafah Directorate.

1 - Needs

The already dire living conditions for children across the Gaza Strip further deteriorated since 30th of March 2018. The Gaza Strip faces a chronic humanitarian crisis, impacting the livelihoods and access to essential services by its two million residents. This crisis has been driven by over 12 years of an Israeli blockade and an unsolved internal Palestinian divide, exacerbated since March 2018 by the massive increase in Palestinian casualties in the context of demonstrations taking place near fence between Gaza and Israel, as well as escalations in hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups. Children participating in these protests are particularly exposed to violence. Between 30 March 2018 and 20 September 2019, 2,636 children have been hospitalised at the fence, by a combination of injuries including those hit by live ammunition, rubber bullets and tear gas. An additional 33 children have been killed. According to WHO, 2,980 children were so badly wounded that they required hospital care. Many children are deeply traumatized from injuries and distressed at having witnessed first-hand egregious injuries on family and community members. Mental health and psychosocial support issues resulting from this exposure to violence, contribute to children experiencing learning difficulties and falling behind in school. Moreover, the education system is facing pressures exacerbated by the humanitarian situation. For school year 2019/2020, the Ministry of Education (MoE) has enrolled an additional 22,868 students whose parents could no longer afford private school fees or transportation to UNRWA schools. There are 241 public schools operating in Gaza of which 58.2 per cent operate on a double or triple shift system and with student density of up to 50 per classroom, which reduces instruction time to about four hours a day. This limits the time available to reinforce learning, support slow learners, remedial education programmes, and prevents extracurricular activities. According to the education cluster revised framework for Gaza, 2019, there are 18,989 school children (50% females) in need for remedial classes and 280,360 school children (48%) females in need for school-based psychosocial support services in both UNRWA and public schools. Thus, these boys and girls are in need for structured play activities that help them learn, recover from distressing experiences, and develop social and emotional skills, according to the INEE Guidance Note on Psychosocial Support. In addition, the Ministry is suffering from a shortage of teachers due to limited recruitment of teachers in the last three years which is another factor affecting student learning. According to MoE, there is currently a shortage of 585 teachers and education staff (419 teachers and 166 education staff). Additionally, new teachers are lacking experience and is negatively affecting the quality of teaching which in turn could increase school drop-out particularly in low grades. Poor learning environments also contribute to the risk of children dropping out of school as indicated in the Education Needs Assessments conducted by Educaid, “based on parents and school personnel the poor educational environment including the poor facilities, unsafe furniture resulted in huge psychosocial stress on students due to the financial burden and losing hope in future, more willing to drop out.”

2 - Activities

The remedial education programmes are of essence to help students cope and overcome learning difficulties and ensure no student is left behind by recovering the lost school time. This project addresses the Strategic Objective 2 of the Humanitarian Response Plan that identifies as critical intervention remedial education of children who are at risk of dropping out in Gaza. The project will ensure provision of remedial classes and tutoring for 5,000 basic school children (age 8 to 12; around 50 per cent females) in Gaza who are experiencing difficulties in learning and in mastering foundational skills of reading and writing of Arabic language and Math and who are at risk of dropping-out. The selection of the schools will be based on the following criteria; located in 116 vulnerable localitiesidentified by the vulnerability mapping conducted by Islamic Relief, UK, 2017 and considered as low learning outcomes. Schools close to the family centres will be targeted to allow vulnerable children in need for protection or those who were injured in the “Great March of Return” to be referred to the family centres to receive any needed child protection services. A multi-sectorial approach will be adopted with close coordination with the child protection section to strength the protection mechanism and the referral methodologies for students in need. Children will be selected through the examination of the learning achievement records in most vulnerable schools and selection of those who have shown continuous or rapid drop in learning achievement over the past 5 months. The project will focus on foundational skills in the Arabic language and Math as key priority among core subjects where students have been falling behind. The project willtarget particularly those who are failing to meet minimal normative standards in the subjects. Therefore, special customized tests will determine which students will need support. The project will build on catch-up / remedial programmes that were implemented by UNICEF between 2015 and 2019 and will benefit from materials that have already been developed and tested in partnership with relevant stakeholders. These materials considered the recent changes in the curriculum and have been completely updated as of 2019. UNICEF will use methodology already proven effective through the on-going and previous interventions implemented with relevant stakeholders. Children will be instructed in each school by three teachers, one teacher being from the targeted school itself and the other two support teachers who are university graduates who obtained high scores in thefinal university exams. The project includes in-service training to teachers (50% females) in their role as remedial education teachers as well as mentoring/on the job training for the university graduates to enhance their teaching capacities. This modality was recommended by the relevant stakeholders based on feedback received and the evaluation Findings of remedial programmes carried-out in previous years. UNICEF will work with relevant stakeholders and NGOs to support these interventions in schools; 1. Conduct remedial classes benefiting 5,000 students (around 50 per cent girls) from grades 3, 4, 5, to 6 through arrangements made at the school level and during the school day or through provision of extra tutoring after school. 2. Train and mentor 180 teachers (50% females) in 60 schools in Gaza in the year. 3. Conduct an assessment of the effectiveness of remedial education on children's achievement following implementation.
