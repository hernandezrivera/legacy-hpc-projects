Project Summary

In North Darfur, severe gaps in basic, lifesaving goods and services have resulted in heightened risks to food security, acute malnutrition and outbreaks of communicable and non-communicable disease.
In 2018, over 980,000 people in North Darfur are classified in IPC phase 3 and 4. While the 2018 seasonal rainfall across Sudan is reported to be on average (2018 Seasonal Monitor & JJAS Forecast), increasing prices of seeds and livelihood assets as well as long dry mid-season spells are exacerbating Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) food security outcomes, requiring urgent humanitarian interventions to protect livelihood assets and strengthen self-reliance of vulnerable HHs.
Compared to the previous year, cereal crop productivity is significantly reduced across all North Darfur (2018 State Ministry of Agriculture’s post-harvest assessment report).The current economic crises in Sudan (limited foreign currency, fuel shortages, high inflation rate) is projected to persist and will continue to reduce household’s purchasing power and limit their access to food. WFP Monthly Market Updates (January - August 2018) reports that average prices of sorghum, goats and groundnuts are steadily increasing, all prices are over 130% compared to 2017. Livestock diseases including PPR, Sheep Pox and HS are affecting gravely the livelihood of the purely pastoral and agro-pastoral communities (COOPI - livestock vaccination campaign report). Reduction in the animal production contributes to the deterioration of food security. Water for production is scarce with few functional structures, often shared between humans and livestock.

Needs

The cost of key agricultural inputs—including seeds, machinery, fertilizers, pesticides, water, fuel, and electricity— has significantly increased and further increases are projected. With the rising impact of the continuous economic crisis, the purchasing power of the most vulnerable farmers continues to weaken, making it harder on farmers to access to required inputs for the next harvest season in May. This could impact the harvest of the upcoming season, creating a shortage in the availability of certain foods on the market, pushing prices even higher while at the same time impacting the access to food sources from own production by family farming communities. While the current harvest at the national level appears to be relatively strong – it has been identified that several localities have witnessed limited harvest. Reduced livelihoods opportunities, also increases protection risks, including gender-based violence, and the risk of negative coping mechanisms

Activities

1) Agricultural Input Provision to selected 3000 farming HHs;2) Training of 300 farmers HHs on selected agronomic practices and post-harvest handling;3) Conduct Livestock Vaccination and treatment service to 300,000 livestock head owned by 4,000 household;4) On job refreshment training and equipping of 36 CAHWs; and5) Rehabilitation/construction of two haffirs.
