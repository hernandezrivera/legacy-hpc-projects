Project Summary

The proposed intervention will contribute to the HRP 2023 Strategic Objectives:1) Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe, and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs, 2) Vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action informed by communities’ priorities. The main objective of the proposed intervention will contribute to the Protection Cluster's objective, to ensure the mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. To achieve this, the proposed intervention has the following specific objectives:1) To undertake effective and regular protection monitoring in targeted areas for timely and robust humanitarian and protection decisions, influence, and actions. 2) To strengthen community-based protection mechanisms for risk reduction and promotion of local peace and security for women and youths;3) To increase access to safe and dignified protection and GVB emergency services for crisis-affected populations, especially the most vulnerable groups including PWDs and GBV survivors.
This project will support 5110 conflict-affected people from Returnees and Host communities in Renk, Upper Nile State by strengthening community-based protection mechanisms that prevent, mitigate, and respond to protection issues and providing specialized assistance to people with specific protection needs. Oxfam will establish three Information and Protection desks to share protection messages, information about the available services, and guidance and support to Sudan crisis-affected communities in Renk. The three information desks will support the identification and referral process to the most vulnerable protection cases. Each of these will serve as a hub for the local community to provide protection services including services mapping pathway sheets and awareness-raising messages. The project will prioritize community participation and ownership and will strengthen partnerships with other key stakeholders. The project will address the immediate protection needs of highly vulnerable people by identifying community-level protection risks and referring people with needs, including women, children, the elderly, and people living with disabilities. Oxfam will ensure that safe referral mechanisms are in place, including specialized support for survivors or those at risk of gender-based violence.
The project will include a protection cash assistance component for persons facing urgent protection risks or victims of indiscriminate conflict. Oxfam will closely coordinate with the case management actors, Protection and other key stakeholders to ensure that the entire Cash for Protection process including identifications, referrals, and provisions of operations is safe, accountable, and inclusive.
Furthermore, Community-Based Protection Networks (CBPNs) will be established and strengthened. This effort will consider age, gender, and diversity groups of displaced and conflict-affected communities, to address and mitigate protection concerns, promote referral mechanisms, lead protection initiatives, and enhance community self-protection mechanisms. Furthermore, it will promote the acknowledgement of the need to monitor protection issues and advocate for it. The intervention will address this through conducting regular protection monitoring aiming to assess the pattern of abuse or repeated threats and their impact on vulnerable groups and the findings will be shared externally with the wider humanitarian community considering Safe Programming principles.

1. Needs

As of 27 October, 341,967 returnees (over 90 per cent), refugees and third-country nationals’ people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan since April 2023. Since the last weeks of October, an increased influx was recorded. Nearly 3,000 people arrive daily in Renk, which means more than 17,000 people every week, more than 10,000 people travelling in, as compared to the same period previously. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children under age 18 who are extremely in need of immediate assistance. An estimated 12,666 people are currently hosted in the Renk TC area who are facing various protection and GBV risks including early pregnancies, rape, physical violence, economic violence, and child labour. According to the recent Rapid Protection Assessment conducted by DRC in May 2023, over 60% of the people who arrived at Renk reported they had arrived without identification documents with 38% of those in possession of documents only possessing work IDs, 34% with nationality IDs and 28% possessing other documents like passport and birth certificate. However, this is also confirmed by the Oxfam Protection Monitoring Report - Sep 2023 which shows that people in transit camps have no access to documentation and cannot apply for and renew their civil status documents. 68% of those who were interviewed by Oxfam during protection monitoring mentioned that they did not approach authorities for missing documents due to various reasons including lack of information, lack of resources to cover expenses, lack of relevant authority in the location, and not being eligible to obtain the document. According to the Rapid Interagency Assessment - Aug 2023, individuals are encountering difficulties in obtaining information about available services. There is also a limitation in the regular provision of information regarding transportation and entitlements for cash assistance, as well as a noticeable absence of timely feedback. This situation is further confirmed by the Oxfam Protection Monitoring Sep 2023, which highlights the concerns about the gaps of information concerning legal documentation and the whereabouts of PSEA services. Additionally, the Oxfam Protection field report revealed that some individuals found themselves in Renk for extended periods, often four days or more, without access to information on transportation options, travel routes, or the necessary resources to continue their onward journeys.Although health interventions are being implemented at the Renk (TC), there is a concerning rise in reported cases of diseases such as malaria, measles, acute watery diarrhoea, and malnutrition. The centre currently faces limitations in its ability to effectively manage patients with non-communicable and chronic diseases. Oxfam's protection monitoring findings indicated that certain demographic groups remain particularly vulnerable to accessing health services. This vulnerability is particularly prevailing among women, GBV survivors, lactating mothers, children, elderly people, and persons with disabilities. In certain cases, individuals are at a critical point where their lives are at risk, making it imperative to access these services in Juba. However, individuals are still facing a lack of the financial means to access the necessary health services.Based on the Oxfam Protection field reports from July - September 2023, a distressing pattern has emerged. Women and girls are experiencing GBV and PSEA inflicted upon them during their journey from Sudan to Renk. Also, the lack of privacy and lighting in WASH facilities increases the GBV threats by Women and Girls. Furthermore, the provision of dignity kits remains a recurring recommendation emphasized during Oxfam's protection field reports and other various protection assessments.

2. Project activities

1. Provision of individual cash for protection to 435 persons. The project will include a protection cash assistance component for persons facing urgent protection risks or victims of indiscriminate conflict. Mainly the Cash for Protection assistance is proposed to enable at-risk individuals to meet their protection-related needs and avail services. This includes facilitating access to Durable solutions, Legal, Medical, and other protection services, etc. Oxfam will closely coordinate with the case management actors, Protection, and other related stakeholders to ensure that the entire Cash for Protection process including identifications, referrals, and provisions operations is safe, accountable, and inclusive.2. Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPC). Oxfam will work with the established community-based protection committees of 3 groups – 30 Members (Male and Female). Oxfam will train the CBPC members on GBV, Protection and Humanitarian Standards, protection monitoring, and referrals to equip them to prevent, respond, and mitigate the protection risks and threats. 3. Capacity-building training will be carried out for service providers on key general protection topics - including Referrals, safe programming, and PSEA. 20 participants from different service providers including Government and NGO providers will be trained. The training will aim to enhance the safe practices in the humanitarian response and strengthen the referral systems.4. As part of enhancing the Protective Environment and Referral systems, a day-long workshop will be organized to discuss and analyze the protection risks and mitigation measures. Oxfam`s Protection team will facilitate a one-day workshop, bringing together the protection actors, service providers, local authorities, protection clusters, and community representatives in Renk. During the workshop, participants will actively identify and advocate for the barriers, and associated risks related to referrals, emphasizing the importance of a coordinated, efficient, and safe process.5. Service mapping pathway sheet. Protection and emergency services accessible to Sudan Crisis-affected communities in Renk will be mapped and information on available services will be disseminated to the targeted communities. The mapping will include INGOs, NNGOs, governmental offices, and public and private service providers. In coordination with community-based protection committees and actors, the services mapping pathway sheet will be disseminated to targeted communities. 6. Monthly Protection Monitoring. Oxfam will conduct protection monitoring at the community level to identify human rights violations and protection risks encountered by refugees and returnees. 7. Referrals to publicly and privately provided protection services. Cases with GBV, Protection, Child Protection, and other concerns will be referred to the specialized services providers by Oxfam protection teams through static and mobile approaches. 8. Community-led initiatives. Vertical and horizontal dialogues within the community including Community-based Protection will be facilitated to identify the main protection concerns and later to come up with Community-led initiatives aimed at promoting protection and gender-based violence (GBV) practices. 9. Advocacy Dissemination Workshop on the protection Monitoring findings to Key Stakeholders, Donors including the Government.10. Conduct comprehensive and contextualized awareness-raising campaigns on the available services, returnee and refugees’ rights and entitlements, GBV, PSEA, protection, child protection, etc. 11. Information and Protection desk. Three Information Dissemination, protection and Referrals Desks will be constructed by Oxfam in Renk to inform the new arrivals about the available services, receive concerns from returned and refugees, and identify and refer the protection and GBV cases to the specialized services providers.
