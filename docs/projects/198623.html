Project Summary

The Shelter and NFI assistance project will target IDPs and returnees in three BAY states; Borno, Adamawa and Yobe with provision of new emergency and transitional shelters, emergency shelter repairs, shelter kits and Non-food Items (NFIs) to contribute to the physical well-being and dignity of PoCs and as protection assistance aligned with Shelter/ NFI/ CCCM Sector strategic objectives for the HRP 2023. The project will also ensure holistic durable solutions approach led by the Government, mainly to enable local integration of IDPs in Adamawa and Borno State, reintegration of refugee returnees and support to the host community in building resilience and ease the pressure on them.
Therefore in 2023, UNHCR shall build upon progress being made in 2022 in providing shelter and NFI support to respond to unmet needs in Adamawa (Fufore Mubi South, Madagali, Michika, Yola South and Yola north); Borno (Bama, Banki, Damasak, Damboa, Dikwa, Jere, konduga, Gwoza, Pulka, Maiduguri, Mobbar, Monguno, Rann and Ngala) and Yobe State (Damaturu and Gujba,) through provision of; (1) 10,000 emergency shelters (2) 550 Durable/Transitional, (3) 4,000 shelter kits materials (4 ) 25,000 NFIs kits and (5) 15,000 emergency shelter repairs targeting IDPs and Returnees households both in formal and informal settlements, suffering from protracted displacement, natural disaster and those returning home. To assess and ascertain the level of beneficiary satisfaction with regards to Shelter and NFI support, UNHR plans to carry out 4 post construction/distribution monitoring.

1 - Needs

In line with the strategic objective 1 of the 2019-2021 Humanitarian Response Strategy - Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and protection interventions to the most vulnerable, UNHCR intends to contribute to fostering a favorable protection environment as the crisis in the northeast, continues to unravel with no end in sight; Internally Displaced populations, particularly in the BAY states, continue to face immense needs regarding Shelter and NFIs. Based on the recent MSNA, DTM and Site Tracker assessment4 in July-September, approximately 672,050 Households representing 3,360,254 individuals remain in need of shelter and NFI support translating into 12% increase from 2022. Among them approximately 300,000 individuals are in acute need composed of those living in the open, living in public facilities, and sharing shelters in camps and host communities. Another one million individuals were identified as sheltering with other households (sharing) or in partially damaged shelter in return areas thereby lacking basic privacy raising protection concerns.Under the 2023 Shelter/NFI Sector Response Plan and based on the 2023 HNO analysis, the overall Shelter/NFI Needs for shelter and NFI interventions is 3,360,254 and the overall Shelter/NFI Targett 1,853,610 individuals.The displaced communities in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states living in out of camp, camps and camp-like settings still exist in a highly congested nature due to continuous population movements owing to insecurity related to the 12 years of insurgency by the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) and counter-repellant efforts by the State Government Forces. Majority (93%) of IDPs reported conflict as the main reason of displacement, which is in line with findings from previous rounds as highlighted by Displacement Tracking record (DTM) data collected during Round 41 completed in June 2022. Other factors that have contributed to the movement include government induced returns, inter-communal fighting, and effects of heavy rains. UNHCR and partners conducted assessments and field visits which have highlighted the need for lifesaving protection and assistance and promotion of community resilience and sustainable returns.The needs of IDPs in the Northeast for basic and domestic items is an indicator of PoCs vulnerabilities and this has continued to deteriorate with no abating. The combined multiple efforts by humanitarian actors have reduced the number of IDPs in need of NFIs, which, however, is still an immense representation of the brutal impact of suffering and disruption of normal life in the affected LGAs. Essential households’ items, which are a prerequisite for a minimum standard of living, continue to be a reported gap. Despite regular NFI distributions by humanitarian actors over the past years, 53% of the reported gaps are the essential items. Largest gaps are Blankets/Mats (52%), followed by kitchen sets representing 18%, and mosquito nets representing 11% of the reported gaps.Because of the large of number of makeshifts shelters and natural disaster across most displacement sites, coupled with congestion, severe climatic condition in Northeast Nigeria, torrential rains, strong windstorm have led to destruction of shelters, household items and critical facilities across IDP camps and host communities, there is need for continued rehabilitation of dilapidated shelters and structures

2 - Activities

2 - ActivitiesTo improve protection environment, access to dignified, safe, and secure settlements for internally displaced persons, Refugee returnees and some host communities in Borno and Adamawa, UNHCR will implement following activities:• Construction of 200 Durable shelters in Adamawa state to support the reintegration of IDPs and host communities. The shelters will be built using Hydra-Form Technology, Interlocking Stabilized Soil Bricks (ISSB). These will constructed where local authorities have provided land for the reintegration of IDPs. • Construction of 350 transitional shelters (Mudbrick walling and GCI sheets targeting 1750 individuals.• Construction of 10,000 new emergency shelters to replace damaged shelters and address new displacement needs.• Repairs for 15,000 emergency shelters for most vulnerable households in camps and camps like settings to address dilapidated shelters as a result of severe climatic conditions.• Procurement and distribution of 25,000 improved NFI kits to strengthen protection to crisis affected households. • Provision 4,000 shelter kits in kind and in cash grants for rehabilitation of shelters mainly for Returnees and IDPs leaving the camps and returning to their original place of residence.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: • Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need. • To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls. • If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks. • In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures. • Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy. • All along project implementation, UNHCR will monitor protection, age, and gender aspects and will address any arising issues. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors, with whom UNHCR will maintain close coordination.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will strengthen community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Child friendly complaint boxes will be introduced in child friendly areas as well as in educational structures. The existing tollfree number and email will be vulgarized and peer to peer approach will be established to support reporting of complaint. Focus group discussions with people from different age, gender and diverse origin will be conducted regularly to collect feedback from affected population on available services and as well as protection risks. UNHCR will ensure that committees of women, men, boys and girls are in place in order to participate fully in the implementation of activities. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention as well as to the attention of other partners, for intervention. UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the SG’s special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
