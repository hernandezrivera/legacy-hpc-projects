Project Summary

Oxfam is currently working in Somaliland and Puntland implementing essential lifesaving activities for drought affected people in Sool and Sanag and cyclone affected people in Awdal region of Somaliland and Nugaal region of Puntland. In 2019, Oxfam will continue working in these areas and deliver life saving water supply, by constructing new systems, rehabilitating existing water sources and improving quality of water sources; sanitation support through constructing emergency latrines for high concentration IDP camps, and hygiene awareness campaigns including on menstrual hygiene management. Oxfam will target the most vulnerable communities and IDPs in Oxfam interventions areas. To effectively respond to severe water scarcity in Sool and Sanaag, Oxfam will move from water trucking to Cash for Water (CfW) modality. The lessons learnt from previous WaSH response has led to Oxfam successfully piloting Cash for Water in place of water trucking, which is often costly and complex to manage. Previous CfW pilots show that locally established Water Committees can bargain better with water vendors, negotiating lower prices than Oxfam ever could. On average a barrel of water costs between 3$ - 5$. When purchasing water in bulk-a full 8m3 water truck-the price per barrel drops by an average of 25% to 30%. Water vendors without the guarantee of selling a full truckload, refuse to deliver. Thus, to ensure uninterrupted and safe access to water for the most vulnerable households, the Water Committees in each target community will be provided with Telesom Zaad accounts (mobile money transfer modality) restricted to the purchase of water from pre-selected water trucking companies. Oxfam will facilitate drafting of MoUs between the two parties, and between the Committees and Oxfam. The Committees will be responsible to purchase and distribute the water and keep a register listing all recipients. They will be supported by Oxfam staff members and by Community Health Volunteers in providing water to the most marginalized beneficiaries, distribution and vendor engagement. Each family (6 members/HH) will receive the minimum daily requirement of 7,5l./person/day, with an entitlement of 40l. of water a day (or the weekly equivalent). To ensure adequate water storage facilities at community and household levels, Oxfam will provide 10m3 water storage tanks for temporary bulk water storage, and each targeted household will receive 2 jerry-cans. It is imperceptive to note that periodic random water quality tests will be held through out the project life cycle.

Needs

Water: Majority of villages in both Somaliland and Puntland depends on rain water harvesting systems. 2018 Dayr rains have just started and actual performance and impact will be evaluated after December 2018. The existing barkads in intervention areas are expected to harvest some water but if rains do not perform as expected, this might not be enough to sustain communities until next rainy season (Gu, May 2019). During emergency, majority of the IDP areas rely on water trucking either from NGOs or from private trucks. There are a limited public birkads which can be utilized by the IDP’s for free. The existing ground water sources are either saline or contain highly mineralized content - hardwater. Oxfam conducted water quality assessment in 13 boreholes across Sool and Sanag and all wells have high mineral content that is higher than tolerable range. Further to this the Ministry of Water Development of Somaliland reports 60-70% of boreholes in Somaliland are currently non-functional. There is a bit gap in the information available on water access and availability in Somaliland, with the Ministry of Water Development needing support to map functionality more precisely, as well as develop policies that encourage better access and quality of water in Somaliland. Access to latrine is very limited in Somaliland and Puntland, communities are practicing open defecation and community contribution for latrine construction is very limited. Oxfam is working with Ministry of Health in Somaliland to revise the existing strategy for sanitation and find possibility of encouraging communities to contribute for latrine construction. In addition to this, the post-Gu multisectoral REACH assessment of Somalia (September 2018) has found that sanitation facilities are often poorly designed on the protection front, without lighting and locks to ensure women’ safety. Given the current awareness and practice for anal cleansing practices, improving the handwashing practices is critical.

Activities

• Conduct water and sanitation inventory in Oxfam supported areas to better understand the level of service delivery, coverage and reprioritize intervention. • Rehabilitation and Upgrading (e.g. solarisation, or construction of piped networks) of 15 strategic water sources including non-functioning boreholes, shallow wells (and communal Berkads only in area with no other source of water). • Support functionality of 30 strategic water wells through mobile maintenance teams and provision of fastmoving items • Conduct groundwater study, drill and construct 3 deep wells • Expansion of 12 existing water sources to IDP camps and nearby villages • Provide cash for Water for 6 000HH ($15 per HH) living in chronically water insecure areas. Cash for Water Committees will be established to purchase and distribute trucked water, resulting in each HH getting 40 litres per day. The action will purchase and distribute 24 x 10 000 litres water storage tanks. It will distribute jerry cans and household water treatment chemicals/reagents. • Purchase and install desalination plants at 4 strategic wells • Conduct periodic water quality surveillance • Train 60 WASH committees on basic maintenance and financial management and conflict management. • Construct 300 emergency shared household latrine for communities & IDP (4 HH per latrine) and provide 1 200 latrine cleaning kits.• Train 48 community health volunteers on public health promotion. • Provide menstrual hygiene kits to 13 000 females (6 500 women and 6 500 girls at reproductive age). The kit will composed of reusable sanitary pads, soap, underwear, skirt, so as to ensure proper MHM with dignity. • Construction of 24 solid waste pits and organize environmental cleaning campaigns. • Conduct baseline and end line survey to measure the impact of WASH interventions• Coordinate all activities with WASH cluster in Moqadisho, WASH working group in Hargiesa, Ministry of water, Ministry of Health and the local administration.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Oxfam intervention areas in Puntland and Somaliland are relatively stable except minor risk related with clan conflict and border conflict between the two above. Environmental protection is a key area of interest for Oxfam Somalia and Oxfam protection team are reviewing risk faced by women and girls. In IDP camps, some of the most vulnerable communities are practicing negative livelihood practices (charcoal production) in order to get cash for basic household needs including water. in addition, as the number of latrines are limited in IDP camps, open defecation is widely practiced. Oxfam wash team will be working closely with EFSVL team to include the most vulnerable communities in the multipurpose cash transfer and creation of income-generating activities. Additional latrines will be constructed in IDP camps to minimize open defecation practices, training and sensitization workshops will be given for WASH committees in the IDP camps in raising awareness on environmental protection and limit the impact of open defecations. Oxfam will conduct environmental cleaning campaigns around the IDP camps. In addition, special attention will be given during site selection for drilling and construction of new WASH facilities to reduce the impact on the environment to the lowest possible level. After completion of the project, Oxfam will handover all facilities to the local government and WASH committees representing the communities. In case a land ownership issues is raised in IDP camps, Oxfam will be working local government to ensure access to land is secured for IDP people. The establishment and capacity building of WASH committee will nurture community project ownership which will ensure project sustainability.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

RISK 1: Outbreak of diseases, particularly in IDP sites. MITIGATION MEASURES 1: Carry out regular disease surveillance and water quality monitoring, community sensitization in safe disposal of human waste, safe water chain and maintenance high standards of food, personal and environmental hygiene. RISK 2. Water shortage creating conflict among host and IDP communities, or different clans. MITIGATION MEASURES 2: Ensuring that both host and IDP’s are selected, and a conflict sensitive approach when working in multi-clan areas. Carry out risk analysis and monitor the risk register, before the intervention, ensure inclusiveness of different clans during the selection process, understand the context and conflict dynamics. RISK 3: Disputed ownership of constructed infrastructure. MITIGATION MEASURES 3: Use of community participatory approaches and government engagement. Ensure contribution and commitment to operation and maintenance of any infrastructure, and ensure MoU to ensure that communal structures are available to be shared. RISK 4: Opposed government approaches in proposed interventions; for example, authorities may refuse access temporary IDPs settlements. MITIGATION MEASURES 4: Ensure involvement of government during project design implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages, representation of government on various coordination forums and advocating amongst government (local, regional and national) on IDP rights. RISK 5: Conflict among Households that do not receive the support from Oxfam or households received by Oxfam assistance may targeted by others. MITIGATION MEASURES 5: Ensure inclusive participation during the selection process, target most vulnerable households. Objective beneficiary selection and explanation of criteria. Provision of blanket or communal approaches when needs are high amongst all population, explanation of these approaches and agreement from community.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

RISK 1. Location and distance of the water source is a burden on women and girls. MITIGATION MEASURES 1: Having community consultation during site selections involving all actors during the design; ensuring that where possible siting is done in a consultative manner and pipe network is put in place in accessible location. RISK 2: Exclusion of women representative from the water committee. Women lack influence decisions made by committee members. exclusion of people with disabilities and minority groups. MITIGATION MEASURES 2: Inclusion of equal men and women in all community committees, and separate consultation and feedback sessions with women.Mandatory requirement for water committees to have women representatives, explain to community leaders (e.g elders) rights of women to participate in decisions that affect their lives and how to set selection criteria that accommodate different parts of social groups e.g. disabilities and minorities. RISK 3: Misperceptions of menstrual hygiene kits, men may refuse women to be distributed menstrual hygiene kits. MITIGATION MEASURES 3: Engage men and elders in community mobilization, engage male and female field staff so groups can talk to whom they feel more comfortable with. Consultation with women on preferred menstrual hygiene products, raising awareness among beneficiaries about how to use the Menstrual hygiene kits. Cultural sensitivity in the hygiene promotion done around these issues, that takes a community engagement approach to improve hygiene practices in the household. RISK 4: If the latrine is poorly designed (e.g. visible to outsiders, not secure), it will expose people to sexual abuse. latrines may construct inaccessible locations for women and girls during the night time. MITIGATION MEASURES 4: Involvement of communities specifically women and girls during latrine design (e.g. lighting and locks) and siting, ensuring protection and gender mainstreaming. Consult with women and girls suitable location.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

RISK 1: Tribal/clan conflict revenge killings. MITIGATION MEASURES 1: Ensure targeting is not biased and equal participation for women, men, boys and girls of different clans. Ensure analysis of clan dynamics and integration of this analysis into site and beneficiary selection. Clear explanations to be given on selection criteria, and Oxfam’s principles as an NGO during initial assessment and community selection. RISK 2: State to state (Puntland Somaliland) conflict impacts on implementation. MITIGATION MEASURES 2: Monitor security situations and advisories about border regions, and where possible respond when there is large scale displacements in existing intervention areas of Sool and Sanag maintaining humanitarian principle of impartiality.
