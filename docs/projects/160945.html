1 - Needs

Despite the advent of the September 2019 revitalised peace agreement, South Sudan continues to experience unprecedented humanitarian need. Almost seven million individuals are in need of food assistance, nearly 1.5 million remain internally displaced, and human rights abuses continue to be perpetrated by all parties to the conflict with near impunity. It is within this context that an estimated 297,239 refugees, the majority of whom are from Sudan, have sought safety from insecurity and conflict in neighbouring countries. In Sudan, the outbreak of conflict in 2011 in South Kordofan and Blue Nile States, between the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement North (SPLM) and the Government of Sudan saw an estimated 250,000 Sudanese flee into Upper Nile and Unity States. Currently, Pariang County in Unity State hosts an estimated 119,104 refugees across Ajoung Thok and Pamir refugee camps, and Yida refugee settlement. While in Maban County, Upper Nile State, an estimated 145,705 Sudanese reside across Doro, Gedrassa, Batil, and Kaya refugee camps. The large influx of Sudanese refugees has placed the host community under pressure to share their existing scarce resources and has seen a rise in tension between refugee and host communities, particularly in Maban County. While the conflict in South Kordofan and Blue Nile States has stabilised, the deteriorating macroeconomic situation in Sudan are driving acute levels of food insecurity and 2019 has seen increasing political turmoil in Khartoum as former President Bashir was overthrown in a military coup. The current situation in Sudan suggests that the environment is not conducive to returns. As such there is a growing need to provide an multisectoral response to the Sudanese refugees and vulnerable host communities to contribute to the achievement of durable solutions. According to FEWNETS both Pariang and Maban Counties are experiencing IPC 3 (Crisis) food security outcomes. A continued cycle of protracted conflict, displacement, and underdevelopment have restricted livelihood opportunities and undermined households resilience to manage shocks and stressors. Women continue to be marginalised in formal employment opportunities, the situation exacerbated by social and cultural restrictions among Sudanese refugees on the role of women in the public sphere. Given that most of the refugee population reside in camp settings, there is a need to provide camp coordination and camp management services, with a need to continue to identify and support Persons with Specific Needs (PSN), and to support peaceful co existence initiatives between the refugee and host community. As households compete over resources, they are increasing forced to rely on unsustainable livelihoods, such as cutting firewood, which leads to environmental degradation. Both the host and refugee community are vulnerable to climatic shocks and stressors. In October 2019, Maban County was hit by large scale flooding that has left the refugee and host community devastated. While assessment data is still being gathered, it estimated that 90% of the host community and 80% of refugees have been affected by the flooding. Preliminary information suggests that many latrines and shelters have collapsed, households are in urgent need of food assistance, and there are reports of households living in the open elements. The deteriorating hygiene and sanitation conditions are serious potential health harzards, which increasing the spread of water borne diseases such as cholera. The disruption to basic services and destruction of shelter/household assets pose serious protection risks to women and girls, furthermore their is a risk exploitation and abuse as vulnerable women and girls turn to negative coping mechanisms, such as survival sex. Furthermore, it is anticipated that the flooding will have wiped out many households assets and destroyed the 2020 harvest, which will likely lead to a deterioration in food security outcomes.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention aligns with Strategic Objective (SO) 1: Reduce morbidity, mortality, and suffering from protection threats and incidences of most vulnerable in severity levels 3&4. The project also aligns with SO2: Ensure safe, equitable, and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations to meet their basic needs in locations of severity levels 3&4. Considering these objectives, DRC proposes an integrated multisectoral response, including camp coordination and camp management (CCCM), food security and livelihoods (FSL), protection, WASH, and the provision of Shelter and Non Food Items (S/NFI) for both vulnerable refugees and host community members in Maban County, Upper Nile, and Pariang County, Upper Nile State.FSL: Supporting food accessibility and availability by the distribution of agricultural inputs; supporting the establishment of home permaculture gardens; voucher system for PSNs to access basic food items; establishment of savings and loans groups; the provision of basic business skills and start up grants; natural resource management activities including planting of trees and rehabilitation of marram pits. CCCM: As the Camp management agency in Maban County (Doro, Gendrassa, Batil and Kaya refugee camps) and Jamjang County (Ajuong and Pamir Camps), DRC will continue providing essential CCCM services. These will include, strengthening services targeting PSNs, strengthening referral systems, material support to PSNs, ensuring that information is disseminated to the wider refugee community in order to bridge any protection and assistance related gaps. DRC will also strengthen community engagement so that refugees and the affected populations are empowered and self-reliant. Capacity building of the governance structures will also be a priority so that self-management capacities are enhanced and sustained. In addition to this, the complaints and feedback mechanisms will be strengthened so that vertical and horizontal accountability is promoted. In collaboration with UNHCR, DRC will also facilitate the regular Participatory Assessments (PAs) which inform programing from a broader perspective. Social cohesion initiatives contribute significantly to durable solutions. DRC will continue to facilitate these initiatives within the camps as well as with host communities. Working closely with camp administrators and the camp coordinator (UNHCR), DRC will facilitate forums that enhance these initiatives. WASH: Utilsing its rapid mobile response modality, DRC will out WASH needs assessments and response missions; distribute WASH NFIs; rehabilitate non-functional hand pumps; construct household and institutional latrines; carry out water quality testing and purification; carry community awareness raising on hygiene promotion activites. Shelter/NFIs: Conduct shelter/non-food item needs assessments; Distribution of emergency shelter kits; distribution of life saving NFI assistance, including mosquito nets, blankets, and kitchen sets. Protection: Protection assessments and monitoring; identification of PSNs; provision of individual protection assistance; establishing and supporting community based protection structures and networks; provision of assistance to individuals through referral pathways; provision of group and individual psychosocial support; provision of case management services for GBV survivors; operation of women and girls friendly spaces.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.14. Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources. Due to the risk of tension between the host and refugee community over resources and services. DRC will target both the host and refugee community in this response and all actions will be implemented through a conflict sensitivity lens. In addition, DRC will implement peaceful coexistence initiatives that support the development of positive relations between the host and refugee community. 8.10. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. As was observed in Bentiu, Unity State, late last year, women and girls face an increased risk of sexual violence when accessing humanitarian assistance. Considering this, DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming across its programming and seek to reduce/mitigate protection risks that may be incurred during the course of the project. Examples, of steps that will be taken by DRC include engagement of beneficiary members around selection of the locations for distributions and screening of locations with a GBV risk assessment. 8.1. Members of marginalised community’s/minority groups are not included in consultation and/or excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. DRC will carry out sensitisation among its own team members to raise their awareness about the need to engage members from all segments in the consultations/project implementation. DRC will also carry out home to home visits to ensure the inclusion of community members who may be unable to leave their household due to physical or cultural/social restrictions.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC adopts a community-based approach, where beneficiaries and local authorities are included in the prioritising, planning and implementation of activities as an important measure of rehabilitation and capacity building. DRC is a certified member of the Core humanitarian Standards (CHS) and as such fulfils the CHS benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. DRC has deployed beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism and operates complaints desks to enable community members to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC also has a feedback hotline Number (9686) that beneficiaries can call to register complaints, feedback or requests. Through its activities, DRC will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the F-CRM will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project.
