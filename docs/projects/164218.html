Project Summary

Vulnerable households are targeted with food assistance in Aleppo and Rural Damascus. The focus is on returnees, women headed households and households with people with disabilities, selected through community-based protection interventions or through civil society organisations in the locations. Households targeted by the food assistance intervention will receive food vouchers where the market is available and food parcels where the market isn't yet able to provide for voucher programming.

4 - Needs

Since the Syrian civil war started several million of civilians have been displaced, some have sought refuge in neighboring countries others have undertaken the journey to Europe in search for a saver home.Amongst the civilians that remained in Syria 6.2m have been displaced, some of them already several times due to changing front lines. Many families, especially the ones displaced, live in dire needs and parents struggle to provide basic needs including nutritional essentials, because of lost livelihoods or unemployment. Inflation of the Syrian pound further undermines the purchase power of Syrian families. Salaries in Syria are low and have lost a lot of the value due to inflation of the Syrian pound. So despite some people have work the capacity to procure daily needed items remain limited. On the other hand agricultural production in Syria has also not recovered and is not yet producing basic foods at the level needed to feed the own population. As a result there are high numbers of people in need. As for the target areas of Dorcas' food assistance interventions they are as follows: In Jebel Saman sub-district live an estimated 1,638,972 people, 39% or 643,579 of which count as people in need in the food security sector (HNO2020), 29,671 are critically at risk (HNO2020). In Rural Damascus, Jaramana sub-district 38% of the population, an estimated number of 597,423 people are people in need. 224,034 are reported and 16,972 are critically at risk of food insecurity.

5 - Implementation of activities

First of all market study will be carried out through which Dorcas determines the best suited implementation modality. If voucher programming is possible Dorcas will assess different shops and select them based on a procurement process through which the best provider will be selected. Through the support from local partners Dorcas will select the an targeted number of 825 households (4125 individuals - a majority (52%) of which are expected to be female) based on their vulnerability. Vulnerable households will be selected through outreach from community centers, specific assessments or input from civil society actors. Recipient families will be selected based on developed vulnerability criteria, that focus on a combination of social indicators, such as whether a household is women-headed, whether members of the household require support such as PwD or PwSN and whether their economic situation and their monthly income are low (poverty).Food assistance will be provided to the selected households for a period of 3 months.The intervention will be monitored through on-site visits and post distribution monitoring. One part of the intervention will be evaluated through a comparative project evaluation. Where the it will be compared to interventions of other actors.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff; Likelihood: high; Impact: high; Mitigation Measures: 1. Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and tools made available by the Food & Agriculture Sector, such as WFP’s new beneficiary targeting tool; 2. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonised as possible throughout protection service providers; 3. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to ensure that selected beneficiaries to which Dorcas just provides three months of assistance can be covered through other actors for the remaining months.Protection Risk: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Likelihood: medium; Impact: high Mitigation Measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended, 2.Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting, 3. Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/ assistance, through regular and targeted sessions, including separate sessions with different groups that may be more at risk, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 4.Implement specific complaint mechanism and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 5. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA, 6. Perform post-distribution monitoring.
