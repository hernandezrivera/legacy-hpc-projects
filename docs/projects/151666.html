Needs

Somalia is one of the top ten countries with the highest prevalence of malnutrition in the world, and the third highest in the eastern and southern Africa region. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) is at 17.4 per cent49 amongst children under five years, with 3.2 per cent being severely malnourished. Despite the large scale humanitarian assistance delivered, the FSNAU/FEWS NET) post-Gu assessment indicates sustained high-level risk of famine in some parts of Somalia by the end of the year. This is due to a combination of severe food insecurity, high acute malnutrition and high burden of disease. The projected number of children who are, or who will be acutely malnourished, has increased since the beginning of the year to 1.2 million,51 including over 231,829 (including IDPs) who have or will suffer life-threatening severe acute malnutrition (SAM) over the next one-year period from Sep 2018-Sep 2019. Severely malnourished children are nine times more likely to die of killer diseases such as AWD/cholera and measles, and the rising malnutrition outcomes are a direct manifestation of the combined impact of drought and conflict. According to 2018 post Gu nutrition burden by district estimate by nutrition cluster, Burdhubo/Garbaharey district pastoral livelihood zone with a total population of 20,750 indicated to critical GAM of 10.7% and 1.7% SAM case with an overall burden of 289 acutely malnutrition, The same was evidence on the riverine (Burdhubo) livelihood zone where a GAM of 13.6 % and 1.5% SAM with total 660 acutely malnourished. There is need to scale up the existing preventive health programs (routine vaccination program and vitamin A supplementation) in the regions with low levels of coverage of VIT A and Measles vaccination status. This critical situation shows urgent nutrition life-saving intervention to enable avert it to slip into greater measures.RASS organization will therefore, strive to ensure well coordinate with MOH,Nutrition cluster and other actors

Activities

1. Provide therapeutic treatment to acutely malnourished under five children 4000 boys,4000 girls and 2000 PLWs in Gedo region through linkage with health, WASH and livelihood project.2. Screen, admits and referred moderate malnourished boys, girls under five children and pregnant and lactating mothers in the project sites in coordination with MOH and other humanitarian actors in the project area.RAAS shall also attend the sub cluster and national cluster meeting to share the project progress and the gaps.3. Provide 4 IYCF counseling sessions for all caregivers both men and women admitted at OTP and SFP site in the target locations.4. Conduct community awareness campaign through OTP outreach services through CHW to provide NHHP to vulnerable 4000 boys, 4000 girls and 2000 pregnant and lactating women in coordination with Health and WASH cluster in the project areas. 5. Capacity build project staff (10 men and 15 women and CHWs/Volunteers and promoters (5 men and 5 women) on IMAM/BSNP/IYCF and NHHP.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Based on age and gender challenge that may most occurred during the staff hiring an/or to the beneficiaries, RASS will mitigate by ensuring staff recruitment take into consideration the female counter part and slat 40% of the position to women and disable population thus ensure be representation. Facilities and outreach/mobile team might be challenging too for some beneficiaries boys, girls and PLWs movement might be risky thus RASS will ensure closer or otherwise outreach/mobile.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Rass will ensure to trained its staff and community health workers on code of conducts as well as ensure proper feed back mechanism is in place to enable the beneficiaries concerns are taken care of.In case of fear of any military and militia confrontation RASS will ensure all its projects locations are far away from these areas believed to be high risk for beneficiaries and staff.
