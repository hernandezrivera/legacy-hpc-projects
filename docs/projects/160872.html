Project Summary

The proposed project will prioritize life-saving treatment services targeting children under five years of age (U5s) and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) vulnerable to malnutrition in Bentiu PoC, Koch, and New Fangak.
The activities will primarily focus on Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) to increase equitable access and utilization of lifesaving nutrition services: early detection through screening and the treatment of SAM and MAM cases. World Relief South Sudan (WRSS) currently operates 2 Nutrition Centers in Bentiu; 8 in Koch; and 6 in New Fangak. Two of the centers (one in Koch; one in New Fangak) also have Stabilization Centers. This project seeks to support outpatient therapeutic feeding program (U5-OTP), Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP – U5 MAM and PLW) and Mother, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) programming in these 16 sites by including community outreach and mobile interventions. The project will also increase the capacity of OTP/TSFP sites through improved infrastructure and maintain the number of Stabilization Centers in Koch and Fangak Counties.
Prevention activities will also be incorporated to address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition. This will be primarily through onsite nutrition education to mothers and caregivers on use of treatment nutrition supplies and promoting dietary practices will equip them with knowledge and skills to prevent malnutrition and make healthy food choices within their context. Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) will also promote improved MIYCN practices. Subject to funding available, additional community components, such as Mothers’ Support Groups, will be supported.
Program integration shall be ensured by establishing kitchen gardening in selected nutrition sites, PLWs beneficiaries linked to FSL to receive vegetable kits where possible and they will also benefit from cooking demonstrations. For WASH, proper sanitation facilities (latrines), safe drinking water, handwashing stations and ECDE materials will be mainstreamed across nutrition sites, towards Education. Towards Health, SAM children will be tested for malaria and other illness (URTI, UTI) and referred/linked to adjacent PHCC/Us for comprehensive treatment. The program shall be sensitive to the needs and safety of women, boys and girls by creating a friendly and sanitary atmosphere.

1 - Needs

With this project, WRSS will address nutritional needs of host communities, IDPs, returnees, and other affected women, girls and boys in Koch County, Bentiu PoC and Fangak County. In these areas, national and program assessments have shown deteriorating conditions arising from pockets of malnutrition for both women and children under 5 years. The targeted areas are staggering between “critical” GAM (WHZ) 15.0 – 29.9%) and ‘Serious’ GAM (WHZ) 10.0-14.9%) levels of acute malnutrition according the latest IPC key findings (August 2019 - April 2020). On the basis of poor quality, data that would be vital to indicate status of malnutrition in Koch and Fangak was dropped by the latest FSNMS Round 23. Notably however, the same source approximated GAM for Fangak and Koch to remain around 11.6 percent. By experience, this normally deteriorates around lean period which stretches long between January and August of every year. The current WR program data reveals severity of what is observed on-ground. In Koch and New Fangak, we have continued to receive a minimum of six SAM children with medical complications for treatment in our SC as new admissions per month. While low, these are remote locations that would otherwise leave large population groups with no access to such services. Currently, we have 329 OTP admissions, 932 U5 TSFP, and 693 PLW TSFP admissions in Koch. In Bentiu we have a total 220 OTP admissions, 336 U5 TSFP, and 257 PLW TSFP admissions. In Fangak, we have 111 U5 OTP, 668 U5 TSFP, and 405 PLW currently in the program. We have continued to receive between 10 - 21 U5 SAM cases, 25 – 50 U5 MAM and around 44 malnourished PLWs every week in Fangak, Bentiu, and Koch. All are receiving optimum care and treatment in our programs. Mother and Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices remain sub-optimal in most areas. WR conducted an MIYCN Knowledge Attitude Practice (KAP) survey in January 2019, interviewing 390 individuals (55 households) from Bentiu Sector 2 and Sector 5. The survey showed poor knowledge levels on the importance of exclusive breastfeeding and the continuation of breastfeeding a child to 24 months and beyond. The survey indicated that the provision of the recommended number of meals including at least four food groups were inadequate where food is scarce with most households dependent upon food assistance. In addition, access to other nutritious foods, (e.g. dairy products and eggs) was extremely low. The survey also showed that caregivers initiate complementary feeding too early (within the first 6 months of life) with these foods being of poor/low nutritional value. A common explanation for this occurrence is related to inadequate food at the household level, poverty and a general lack of information and knowledge on MIYCN practices. Poor MIYCN practices and food insecurity are recognized drivers for acute malnutrition. WRSS proposes to continue putting Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) at the forefront as a life-saving treatment for SAM and MAM children under five years and malnourished pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with children less than 6 months. Treatment at the centres will follow CMAM guidelines. An educative and preventive component, MIYCN will be continued to ensure proper utilization of nutrition supplies and mothers/caregivers are empowered with skill and knowledge for better feeding practices, both through activities at the facilities and in the communities. Appropriate integration with other sectors (WASH, Health, Education, FSL, and Protection) will be ensured. SMART Surveys are proposed for Koch, due to the lack of these since March 2018 and the resulting data gap in a location with demonstrated food insecurity and high levels of malnutrition, requiring increased surveillance. The proposed response is detailed below in the Activities section and the Indicators section.

2 - Activities

This project will seek to address the four front-line responses prioritized by the Nutrition Cluster for 2019. Towards increasing equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery:• Beneficiary education shall be provided especially for mothers and caregivers of children under 5 in the program on proper use of nutrition supplies. • CNVs will do defaulter tracing and home regular home visits to ensure adherence and reminders on next dates for site visits. • Health and nutrition staff will be trained on CMAM and MIYCN/IYCF-E Guidelines to have better understanding and skill as they work. • WR will work with mother to mother support groups in the community to identify and counsel mothers and primary caregivers of children 0-23 months on MIYCN/IYCF messages. Towards increasing equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services:• Operation of Stabilisation Centres (SC), OTP and TSFP Sites shall be maintained. • Screening will be done both at the community and at the nutrition sites on children under 5 and PLWs to identify signs of malnutrition. • Appropriate referrals shall be made to nutrition sites and PHCC/U for treatment. • Stabilization centres (SC) will target children under five with severe acute malnutrition with medical complications. The criteria for admission will include: Bilateral pitting Oedema + or ++ or MUAC <115mm WFH<-3Z-score with a medical complication. - OTP criteria of admission (Bilateral pitting oedema + and ++ or MUAC <115mm or WFH<-3Z-score with appetite and clinically alert) will be admitted in OTP. • TSFP will target boys and girls aged 0-59 months, pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerable groups. TSFP under-five criteria; MUAC >115mm<125mm or WFH>-3Z-score and <-2 z-score and appetite/clinically well. • PLW will be admitted with MUAC <23mm during third trimester and <23mm lactating women with infants < 6 months and receive treatment in the program. • Patients discharged as “non-cured” will be visited at their current place of residence to investigate compliance with the course of RUTF and other factors (hygiene practices, etc.). These patients will be referred to a health facility for evaluation. • Upon discharge, patients will be referred to the supplementary feeding program. The project will track children transferred between program components (PHCC, OTP and SFP). • Beneficiary education shall be provided especially for mothers and caregivers of children under 5 in the program on proper use of nutrition supplies. • CNVs will do defaulter tracing and home regular home visits to ensure adherence and reminders on next dates for site visits. Towards increasing equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from other sectors, WRSS will integrate nutrition activities with its own Health, FSL and Education services to enhance resilience of the beneficiaries. Latrines, safe drinking water, latrine and WASH NFI kits specifically soap will be provided to caregivers of children 0-23 months or at hand washing stations at the nutrition sites. PLWs will be encouraged to establish their own kitchen gardens at home. ECD will be established at nutrition sites and equipped with ECD kits for learning and play. Children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months will be tested for malaria and test positives for malaria referred for treatment in PHCC/U. Patients discharged as “non-cured” will be visited at their current place of residence to investigate compliance with the course of RUTF and other factors (hygiene practices, etc.). These patients will be referred to a health facility for evaluation. Upon discharge, patients will be referred to the supplementary feeding program. The project will track children transferred between program components (PHCC, OTP and SFP). Towards strengthening nutrition information, WRSS will conduct two Rapid SMART surveys in Koch (pre and post harvest), as the last survey was conducted March 2018.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiaries’ rights as citizens of South Sudan are recognized and respected in the implementation of all project activities.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Projects are planned in locations in which local populations are affected by conflict and which host IDPs (including camp settings). Activities are targeted at reaching displaced and host populations alike. Currently, despite much speculation about returns, populations in targeted areas remain very transient between home areas and areas of displacement or camps. With static presence and programming in all three locations, WRSS is in a unique position to monitor any influx of returnees and to respond to their humanitarian needs.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

WRSS maintains ongoing coordination with government line ministries, local Relief and Rehabilitation Authorities, the County Commissioners, and with camp coordination in Bentiu PoC. Capacity-building activities, especially for line ministry staff are key to program implementation and long-term sustainability of project activities. WRSS reaches communities directly through needs assessments and surveys as well as through community meetings and feedback mechanisms at project sites. Community members continue to stress the need for assistance with a commitment to participate in project activities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

All project activities are designed in accordance with national guidelines where they exist (particularly nutrition and health) as well as with UN agency guidelines where relevant. Anticipating additional IDP and returnee beneficiaries during the project period, the project will follow all relevant national strategies for these population groups.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The ongoing peace process within the country is expected to create a more stable environment for implementation of humanitarian activities. WRSS works closely with local authorities to ensure humanitarian access, negotiate safe access to project sites, and to report and address violations when they occur. WRSS also links with Protection actors in all project locations and trains staff on pathways through which to refer beneficiaries who are victims of SGBV or other violence.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

WRSS will link short-term relief activities to development as much as possible. WRSS will aim to make beneficiaries and communities more resilient through key inputs and emphasis on self-reliance, to the extent that it is possible, in each context. WRSS recognizes the need to involve and build community leadership and ownership of programs from the beginning in order to increase sustainability, as opposed to trying to quickly hand over responsibility at the end of a grant. WRSS will involve local leaders and beneficiaries through quarterly stakeholder meetings throughout the life of the project, with the intent of familiarizing stakeholders with the project management and easing the closeout and transition. • Collaboration and Capacity Building: WRSS will support partner organizations to handle the routine clinical activities by including CHD and MoH staff in relevant trainings. WRSS has promoted national staff into management positions to promote greater national and local leadership. WRSS will also work closely with GoSS Ministries and Departments and volunteers to design and implement activities and hand over after the project.• Full-user Participation: Community participation implies a proactive process in which the beneficiaries influence the development and management of projects rather than merely receiving a share of project benefits. Community participation creates an enabling environment for sustainability by allowing users, as a group, to select the level of services for which they are willing to pay (for example, the case of improved water sources), to guide key investment and management decisions, and to make choices and commit resources in support of these choices.• Coordination through Clusters: WRSS will participate with partners in all relevant humanitarian coordination mechanisms for information sharing, needs assessment, activity planning, and materials distributions to ensure complementarity and prevent overlap.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Protection will be mainstreamed across this project to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. Consultation with local authorities and line ministries as well as other stakeholders will determine the locations of service implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to the largest number of beneficiaries. Minimizing the distances that people have to travel to receive services minimizes the risk of beneficiaries, especially women and girls being targeted with sexual or physical violence by armed groups on the way to and from accessing services. Gender-based violence has unfortunately been increasing in South Sudan, having been sanctioned by certain armed groups as a tactic of war. WRSS is serious about the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), including the demands for sex in exchange for goods or services. All staff are trained on PSEA and must sign on to the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. Staff are also trained on referral pathways for beneficiaries who identify as survivors of gender-based violence.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

A variety of feedback mechanisms are used to give beneficiaries a choice in how to express concerns and to ensure that vulnerable community members have access to a forum for giving feedback. First, comment boxes and/or complaint desks are made available at implementation sites to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback directly to WRSS in a confidential manner. Secondly, volunteers are able to receive feedback from participants one-on-one during home visits in settings where participants may feel safer sharing their comments in the privacy of their own home. This also ensures that elderly, disabled, or other people confined to their homes or with difficulties visiting project sites have the opportunity to submit concerns. Thirdly, beneficiaries will be informed of their right to submit complaints during community-level meetings in case they feel more comfortable in a group setting or directly to a World Relief Area Coordinator at a project site.
