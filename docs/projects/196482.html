Project Summary

The objective of this project is to support vulnerable populations to rebuild their longer-term resilience. Mission East aims to facilitate access to improved and sustainable services and inputs in environmentally friendly ways within the governorate of Rural Damascus. Interventions will fall under the Food Security and Agriculture Sector (FSA) and target 27,600 crisis-affected individuals (IDPs, returnees, and host communities alike). From this amount, 26,800 individuals will benefit from the economic infrastructures rehabilitated, while a total of 800 individuals (160 households) will benefit from either agricultural or livestock inputs.
The agricultural and livestock industry, which was previously a cornerstone of the Syrian economy, has been affected greatly during this protracted crisis. Climate and crisis-induced agricultural production losses, economic decline and weakened local currency, price inflation, water scarcity, damaged infrastructure, and protracted hostilities in some areas of the country have led to farmers curtailing, or in some cases abandoning, their enterprises. For this reason, ME aims to provide agricultural or livestock inputs such as seeds, fertilizers, livestock, and animal feed to affected farmers and livestock owners, as well as to help restore agricultural services through the rehabilitation of irrigation systems, solar-powered wells and pumps, and other agricultural community assets. The assumption is as follows: (IF) crisis-affected communities are supported with sustainable agricultural services and inputs, (THEN) beneficiaries can increase their resilience and restore their household food and livelihood security. Ultimately, ME envisions that with the added livelihoods opportunities, households in rural areas will be able to better meet basic needs, thereby increasing their food and nutrition security.
The intervention itself will be designed with a participatory approach, allowing affected community members to highlight their needs and preferences. For the restoration of agricultural services and community assets, projects will be decided in coordination with community stakeholders, as well as relevant technical bodies based on the needs in the community. For agricultural and livestock support, KIIs will be carried out before the intervention kicks off to identify the needs in the community and targeted recipients will be able to select their preferred inputs from a list of context relevant items based on FSA recommendations and the recommendations of specialized veterinarians and agricultural engineers, that have been approved by the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA). For this reason, the specific inputs to be distributed will be identified after the start of the project and based on the information mentioned. This will ensure that recipients are receiving assistance based on their needs, while at the same time ensuring that the inputs provided are native to the area, are of the correct specifications, are standardized and in line with sector recommendations, and will ultimately do no harm to the environment. Inputs will be provided via the in-kind modality.
Activities will work to support small scale farmers (with land size between 5-10 dunums) already working in agriculture and/or livestock rearing. The intervention will prioritize the identification and selection of the most vulnerable and marginalized groups. Targeting criteria will be set that will guide the selection process and consider factors such as vulnerability (for example: women headed HHs or HHs with a large number of dependents, including PWDs), motivation, available skills and land, and commitment to climate and environmentally friendly practices. Rehabilitation of economic infrastructures such as irrigation canals, wells, and water and irrigation systems will aim to target households working in the agriculture and livestock rearing by selecting public works that will, either directly or indirectly, support their livelihoods

3 - Needs

After more than 10 years of conflict, the Syrian crisis remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies. The 2023 Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that 15.3 million people in Syria need humanitarian assistance, compared to 14.6 million in 2022, with 85% of households unable to meet basic needs and 12.1 million estimated to be food insecure. A broad range of factors has cost most Syrians their jobs or income-earning opportunities, affecting their ability to meet basic needs, including food. In addition to hyperinflation and the continued loss of value of the Syrian pound, other factors affecting the livelihoods of families include the protracted displacement and instability in certain areas, disruption to and limited funding towards agriculture and livelihoods, limited energy supply, water scarcity, significant agriculture production losses due to drought-like conditions, the destruction of important economic infrastructure especially water and irrigation systems, the continued financial crises in Lebanon, and the economic impact of the Ukraine crisis. The few families that are still able to maintain their main source of income or livelihoods cannot cope with the prevailing price inflation, now at 90%.

4 - Implementation of activities

Farmers and livestock owners targeted to receive inputs will select their preferred inputs from a list of context relevant items based on FSA recommendations. The list of items will be finalized once the project kicks off. The inputs will be provided using the in-kind modality and will be equivalent to 1300 USD. The recipients will also receive trainings that cover topics such as value chain analysis, how to utilize compost, improved climate-resilient farming techniques, etc. For the restoration of agricultural services, this will entail close coordination with community stakeholders and relevant technical bodies in order to highlight feasible rehabilitation projects that are aimed to support rural communities. The rehabilitation will be contracted through ME and its partners, while at the same time providing the community with the knowledge and trainings needed to ensure adequate upkeep and ownership. KIIs will be done before the start of the project to ensure a participatory approach and that needs are relevant. A MEAL plan will be developed at the start of the project and baseline, endline and PDM surveys will be conducted with recipients. In addition, site-visits and regular beneficiary follow-up will be conducted throughout implementation, as well as the mainstreaming of a feedback and complaints system in line with ME’s CHS commitments. Finally, at the end of the project, an external evaluation will be conducted to assess the project’s overall impact and sustainability

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ME aims to be an example of good practice in disaster relief and development assistance towards local populations, our staff, and other stakeholders. We strive to achieve this through our certification of the CHS, and commitment to the Red Cross Code of Conduct, Sphere Standards, and other standards as outlined in our Quality Commitments Framework. We also expect all ME representatives to commit to principles of good conduct throughout their work. In addition, ME has a strict Accountability, Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse Policy that applies to all ME staff (including all employees, volunteers and interns wherever they may be based), temporary agency workers, consultants, or board members.
