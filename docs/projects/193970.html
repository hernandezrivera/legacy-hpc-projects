1 - Needs

The armed clashes that erupted on 15th April 2023 between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) have had a devastating impact on people and infrastructure throughout Sudan. Most of Khartoum people (figures unknown and influx is continue) has displaced to many states where malaria and other mosquito borne arboviruses are reported increasing the need for more vector control. The displaced population and the host communities in Gazera and Sinnar require immediate coverage by core vector control interventions to ensure their protection from the upcoming malaria season. Access to safe water and sanitation, as well as the ability to maintain good personal and hand hygiene, are critical to prevent the faecal oral transmission of infectious diseases and for an individual’s dignity, safety, and privacy. With reference to 2022 situation, existing risk factors will deteriorate increasing the risk of cholera transmission with the high pressure in the infra structure and WASH services (WHO regular reports 2022). and collapse of the environmental health services at community and facility levels and transmission of the cholera and other water borne diseases is expected with the mass movement and creation of displacement with poor housing settlements and inadequate services resulting form the recent conflict in the high-risk areas. WASH at health care facilities and entry points is considered as a fundamental service to provide quality care, improve infection prevention, and control and reduce morbidity and mortality especially . Primary data from the national assessment of WASH at HCFs revealed that out of 335 heath facilities, only 34.3% of the HFs surveyed have Piped supply inside the building for water, 43% have flush / Pour-flush toilet connected to sewer and tanks, 30 % of HCF have a functional hand hygiene facility at the point of care, and 6.6 % applied the segregation of medical waste at the point of production (WHO 2022). The continuous fighting between the SAF and RSF in addition to the contamination with Un exploded Remnants will result into more casualties which require healthy and clean facilities to manage them and prevent infections among the war victims. Escalation of vector borne diseases continued in sudan with disruption of the health services and preventative programs, the country support to basic WASH programs remains neglected. Malaria continues to take a great toll on human health, and Sudan is contributing with ~56% of cases in the region (WMR 2020). The current situation and poor country capacity require an urgent scale up of interventions to prevent/control communicable diseases outbreaks among vulnerable population. 2 - Activities/Outputs

WHO will ensure quality activities for maximum protection of crisis affected population from exposure to communicable diseases through managing all risks by supporting entomological surveillance, Water quality surveillance, multiple Integrated Vector Management, WASH in HCFs, Points of entry, and community mobilization to apply effective measures for their own protection. Vigilant entomological surveillance sentinel sites will be supported with different collection methods, targeting different mosquito resting and breeding sites. A comprehensive package of vector control measures will be applied including environmental management, chemical control of aquatic and adult vectors, active surveys will also be conducted t map risks of vector borne diseases in affected areas and IDPs settlements. Training of 620 environmental health officers, CHW workers from MOH and partners on multiple environmental health packages including integrated vector management, vector surveillance, water quality Monitoring, WASH in Healthcare Facilities adopting the FIT tool in selected 77 localities/locations. The community engagement as critical part of the control of the emerging Aedes borne disease outbreaks will be improved. WHO During 2022 WHO procured different tools for vector surveillance and control for aedes mosquito, In 2023, WHO will continue providing new spraying pumps, compatible with the new type of insecticide adapted to resistance patterns in the country. WHO will provide technical support and work closely with Health and WASH sectors to improve water quality activities at state and locality level to ensure the access to safe WASH services through strengthening water quality surveillance system and enabling WASH partners to conduct regular water testing in prioritized high-risk locations. These activities will include training of environmental health staff, community volunteers and hygiene promoters on water quality surveillance, provision of operational cost for water quality surveillance missions, provision of WASH supplies and equipment. Enable early detection and intervention for water related heath emergencies through timely and regular information sharing and consultations with health sector. Beneficiaries will be involved in monitoring of water quality through implementation of community water safety plan Approach, awareness raising on best WASH practices, the dentification of vulnerable families in cholera and hepatitis E hot spots and promotion of water quality and safety at household and community level , in addition to conduct regular coordination and follow up meeting with water corporation at state and locality level to ensure the implementation of the recommended corrective measures based on the sanitary inspection and quality check at the water sources. WHO will continue to provide support for WASH &IPC services at Heath care facilities and COVID 19 isolation centers through , development of the national roadmap to improve WASH services at health care facilities and develop minimum standards, support missions to assess WASH and IPC services in order to identify the operational and technical gaps, provide training/orientation sessions for health workforce ,and provides operational cost to improve environmental cleaning in health facilities , WHO will support development and implementation of incident/outbreak specific IPC training programs at all levels for staff, hand hygiene (HH), personal protective equipment (PPE), disinfection supplies, waste management. WHO will provide printing materials to increase the technical knowledge of staff, promote best behaviors, increase the awareness of the staff and communities and provide minor rehabilitation for health care facilities and disease isolation centers. These activities will be implemented in different setting and different heath related emergencies (addressing Water quality, Sanitation, hygiene promotion, medical waste management and IPC).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Distinct attention to the protection of the environment and natural resources is given by WHO during the implementation of the project with a medium impact on the environment. The main hazard is the waste generated by the vector control chemical activities. This hazard is reduced with several strategies; promote alternative methods to the chemical management and usage of the insecticide such as the habitat and environmental control, expanding the use of the gravitraps for adults which contribute to the control of gravid adult mosquito while searching for egg-lay positions and reducing contact with the incriminated vectors. However chemical control is still considered as the most important element in the integrated control of vector-borne diseases especially during the outbreaks where the vector density exceeded the reference threshold. During such situation WHO has already developed a plan for the proper disposal of the chemical generated during the process and accordingly WHO will ensure that the staff is trained and the necessary supplies are made available. Management of the potentially infectious wastes from health activities will be segregated and dealt with in a way that prevents environmental contamination and WHO will reduce reliance on open waste incineration and install high-temperature quality incinerators with treatment units to reduce risks of air pollution. For medical waste management; preventing air pollution will be managed through selecting high quality, environmentally friendly treatment methods, with fumes control and high- temperature production to minimize, control the potential hazards from the waste and reduce its volume to the amount that will be easily controlled.

A series of Focus Group Discussions (FGD) will take place during all project phases for different gender and age groups. Joint field visit and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. WHO through its capacity building program is delivering key messages on increasing community ownership and leadership to ensure eficiency and sustainability of the project, and how this will be achieved through the collaboration of different entities and areas where the lead will be taken by communities and the role of WHO and other partners. This timely feedback is aiming to inform the community on the deficiencies, their impact on health, and the responsible body for undertaking corrective measures which sometimes include the communities themselves. With the WHO identified focal person for PSEA at each field ofice with the country team will provide orientation on prevention, reporting and management of SEA.
