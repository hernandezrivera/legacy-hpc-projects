Project Summary

The effects of conflict in South Sudan causing displacement and lack of basic services continue to be felt in the country as no state has been spared from severe humanitarian needs. People in need of assistance, are increasing due to high numbers of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees (IOM, July 2019). Based on the current IPC AMN, most counties including Eastern Equatoria are in critical levels of acute malnutrition; and according to FSNMS round (24), the results show that, children Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) for the country has increased to 16.2% (2019) compared to 13.3% in 2018. In Eastern Equatoria the GAM for children has increased from 11.8% (2018) to 13.6% (2019) and Western Bahr el Ghazal from 10.4% (2018) to 15.5% (2019) this increment is not different from what is noted in program data in Kapoeta East and Wau. The situation is expected to deteriorate further in the first quarter of 2020 similarly, over 1.2 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in the same year. IRW is planning to contribute to reduction of morbidity, mortality, and suffering from protection threats and incidences of most vulnerable in Wau and Kapoeta East. A total of 14,452 Vulnerable women, girls and boys including persons with disability will be supported through different approaches. In the first objective – Increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women. The preventive services will be through three delivery platforms (household, community and facility levels). IRW, will focus on conducting quality Growth Monitoring through trained community personnel that includes MTMSG, CNV and qualified staff (women; men). Work with partners to support preventive supplementary mainly on specialize food. Under-five girls and boys and PLWs will be provided with specialized nutritious foods to prevent malnutrition; likewise 10,200 (4200 primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months will receive Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition counselling and 6000 boys aged 6-59 months will receive vitamin A supplement in semester 1 through routine and during nutrition campaign. Women of reproductive age that includes the adolescents girls, pregnant women will be provided with iron and folic acid supplementation. In the second sector objective – IRW will contribute to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition to 4252 girls and boys under five years of age and 1300 pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in Kapoeta East and Wau. Through early detection and treatment, IRW will reach 4252 girls and boys under five years of age and 1300 pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition. The project will ensure community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery through accountability and feedback. Male and Female Staff will be trained and engaged for different tasks, provide with relevant working tools including treatment guidelines. The project is planning to reach 1128 girls and boys aged 6-59 months with SAM; 1824 aged 6-59 months with MAM; 1300 Pregnant and lactating women with MAM treated and rehabilitated from malnutrition. Dignified participation by women, girls, male and boys in the program activity in order to promote social behavioral change communication on Nutrition, Health, and WASH services will be ensured. This will involve and not limited to Promotion of EBF; complimentary nutrition advice to women of reproductive age/ and adolescents to encourage a positive change in knowledge, attitudes and practices that will improve the nutrition status of children, PLW. IRW will focus to address the underlying causes of malnutrition through integrating with other sectors that is Education, WASH, FSL and Protection as described in the activity section

1 - Needs

Reduced insecurity after the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan in September 2018, has led to better access in some areas. However, the slow process of peace implementation is leading to uncertainty in humanitarian response in the country. The government is struggling with funds and human resources to address the nutrition gaps which are more widened by the persistent conflict, and food insecurity in the country, Moreover responsibility of these challenges are shouldered by INGOs, NNGOs, the UN agencies and some private sectors yet challenged by a significant funding deficits to cover the population in urgent need. According to the recent IPC report (September 2019) an estimated 6.35 million people are facing crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity and this has remains widespread in all corner of the country. The projection for January to April 2020 by FSNMS 24 indicates that, the food security situation is likely deteriorating and about 1.2 million children (6-59 months) are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition. Prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) to children in the country has increased to 16.2% (2019) compared to 13.3% in 2018. In the selected planning project location; In WBeG, the current GAM for children between 6-59 months has increased from 10.4% (2018) to 15.5% (2019) and the projected cluster case load in 2020 selected county ( Wau - Under 5 SAM is 7000; MAM under 5 is 12495; MAM PLW is (5724); and children under BSFP is 21371). House hold food shortages, population displacements and additional needs from returnees has worsen the situation. There is poor geographical access as those displaced are 10-20Km and 30 kilometers away from the service centers. In the planning location of Eastern Equatoria; Kapoeta East, is a marginalized community surrounded with history of drought, inter-communal conflict due to animal raiding, resource conflict and disease outbreaks thus increasing their vulnerability to food insecurity and Undernutrition. The prevalence of GAM for children between 6-59 months in this area has increased from 11.8% (2018) to 13.6% (2019). The Projected case Load for Kapoeta East (SAM Under Five is 3692; MAM under 5 is 6335; MAMPLW is 9048; MIYCN is 15,457; VAS is 49950; BSFP U5 is 8518; and PLW 4035). Therefore signifying the need to address the causes of under-nutrition. Additionally, in the analysis of 2019 program achievement - the determinant of nutrition coverage is widely affected in Kapoeta East. For Instance, in areas of human resources the trained health workers (HWs) in health facilities had reduced from 62% to 55% in Kapoeta East and the utilization of this services has also reduce from 82% (2018) to 79% (2019). Similarly the continuity of care has also reduced from 65% in 2018 to 56% in 2019 all these factors causing reduction on quality of care. In 2020; there is still need to support the vulnerable women, children (boy and girls) through different approaches to improve on the situation. Furthermore, the nutrition situation is expected to deteriorate further in the first quarter of 2020 because of early onset of the lean season. Seasonal changes, climatic changes is expected to cause increased incidences of diseases such as malaria and acute diarrheal. Both factors (deplete of food stock and increase of rainy season communicable diseases); Chronic disease like HIV and TB are immediate causes of maternal and child Under-nutrition, while the Planned project locations are not exempted from the mentioned immediate causes of Under-nutrition.

2 - Activities

IRW is planning to undertake project activity in Lopua Kapoeta East and Bazia in Wau through established 3 static facility; and 3 out-reach sites. IRW will focus more on Nutrition preventive activities including integration of services. Staff will be recruited, i.e, community nutrition works/volunteers, MTM, qualified nutrition assistance and officer as per the standard. The provision of nutrition preventive services will be through three delivery platforms (household, community and facility levels): The staff will be trained on active case identification from the house hold and at the community level; provided with relevant working tool, trained on Growth Monitoring program (GMP). In Prevention of malnutrition through provision of specialized food. Kapoeta East has been in crisis and serious malnutrition level there are 8518 under 5 children and 4035 PLW targeted for Blanket Supplementary feeding by the cluster. However, IRW intend to reach 518 under-five girls and boys and 403 PLWs; through working with other partners within the area to ensure beneficiary access this services. At Community level the CNV, and Mother to Mother Support group (MTM) will be trained on Provision of nutrition education on use of all nutrition supplies the (e.g. RUTF/RUSF). In Prevention of malnutrition through behavior change communication the program activity will be ensure MIYCN/IYCF counselling- individual/group) including Counselling and nutrition advice to women of reproductive age/ and adolescents. disseminating of messages through IEC materials; drama pre-recorded messages for replaying at the site. Already mother support groups are in existence in Lopua and Bazia with each group having a lead mother that gives direction and guidance to the group members. IRW will involve community religious leaders to be part of Community. In Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies – The staff will also support Micronutrient Supplementation –Vit A (during NID campaign, salt iodization where eligible; provision of Iron and folic acid supplementation for pregnant women; women of reproductive age and the adolescents girls. IRW will continue to participate in the nutrition campaign. There will be treatment and rehabilitation of 1128 children 6 -59 months suffering from Severe Acute malnutrition through Out-patient therapeutic programme (OTP); Treatment and rehabilitation of 1824 children 6 -59 months and 1300 pregnant and lactating women suffering from Moderate Acute malnutrition through a Targeted Supplementary feeding programme (TSFP). The proposed program will be linked with the existing health, FSL and WASH services, mothers and care givers will be capacity built and empowered in different sectors; In Kapoeta Lopua, the mothers and care givers will be linked to Solar powered irrigation scheme garden and be trained through the vegetable gardening so as to promote diet diversity at the household. The project will continue to work closely with the State Ministry of Health to implement Nutrition in all these sites. All SAM cases will be screened for malaria and Positive cases treated according to the malaria guideline. All OTP activities will be integrated with other departments within the health facilities, which include OPD, ANC, PNC and EPI; clear referral system will be put in place where complete. health facilities will distribute Long-Lasting Insecticide treated mosquito Nets (LLIN) to children admitted for acute malnutrition and ensure referrals to health services. IRW will provide WASH to all SAM cases in the facilities; while in GBV - referral pathway will be used, Nutrition staff will trained on GBV and protection, in-order to ensure timely referral; Posters and other GBV messages will used for awareness and be placed in nutrition service delivery points (e.g. therapeutic feeding centres in ). IRW will contribute and participate in nutrition assessment within the area and routinely collect and submit the monthly data through NIS and DHIS.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The Project can be partially linked with durable solution. However the political stability in the country will play a major role for the complete sufficient safe protection environment to enable stable durable solutions, through creating enabling environment, execution of policies and better resources allocation to health and nutrition sector through the MOH who are a key player to sustainable durable solution. The proposed project location that is Kapoeta East and Wau portrays a mixed response towards the link to durable solution; Kapoeta East is a Medium risk environment affected by common trend of resources related inter-communal conflict, cattle rustling, drought and population movement in search for resources; it is generally marginalized community with strong cultural background. The lessons learnt through the prior project and previous interventions gives an impression of average safety for stable and durable solution related to nutrition intervention. Wau (Greater Baggari) has been a high risk area until late 2018, as an identified location for cantonment and political instability will increase tension by default. Ideally the basic services should be handled by the Ministry of Health; and therefore IRW Like any other partners advocates for stability in order the government to take their own responsibility. However due to the current situation IRW is focusing on integration to long term nutrition sensitivity program in Agriculture e.g IRW has established drip irrigation scheme in Kapoeta East Lopua Boma, and the direct beneficiaries are the MTMSG who are doing gardening in the area, this has enable them to plant vegetables that is expected to be available through-out the year with an aim to promote house hold dietary diversity and improve house hold food security. Integration with other sectors like education and FSL. The act of training staff and use of religious leaders also promotes the building of community owned resources persons.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Principally and morally the government is obliged to provide these services however, it is severely challenged by macro-economic factors making it extremely difficult for it to allocate adequate financial, material and human resources for provision of basic social services and overall development in the current context. However, this proposed project is a life-saving humanitarian response that will save lives, but also provide sustainable aspects whereby communities are able to continue supporting themselves even after project completion. IRW will be working with key stakeholders, including the community members and the government, throughout the project cycle. Stakeholders are consulted on a variety of elements, which enhances community participation and contributes to local ownership. The training of community nutrition staff will ensure skilled health staff are available within these communities even after the project is complete. Additionally, more behavioral aspects, such as sensitizing the communities on gender and social inclusion are expected to contribute to gender equality in the long-term. Integration of services with livelihood support, empowering of religious leaders to promote prevention of Under nutrition, linking the intervention with government structures and intensive community capacity building to the local staff will contribute to the future exit strategy.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

IRW has established a feedback and Complaints mechanisms and reporting procedures where all the beneficiaries are given the leeway to communicate their grievances directly.. A complaints and feedback mechanism focal point (CFM focal point) is available in each of the field offices with a dedicated hotline phone number where network is available. During the project implementation, accountability committees (community health and nutrition committee) that includes persons with disability (PLWD); women, men girls and boys will be formed on community need vulnerability criteria. The committee will be trained on the purpose of the complaints and feedback mechanisms as well as how to collect the information from the community and report through the established system. Visibility boards will also be installed at the project sites with the hotline telephone number put on it and the name of the CFM focal point. The CFM focal person therefore records grievances for each complainant in the complaints/feedback form while greatly considering the confidentially of the complainant and share with the management team at the country Once for appropriate decision to be taken. All the issues raised in the complaint will be addressed. In summary, below are the mechanisms IRW will conduct monitoring, focus group discussion, and tracking complaints/feedbacks • In person, i.e. verbal feedback or complaint to any staff member of IRW especially the CFM focal person. This is really beneficial as people with low literacy levels are also given a platform to express themselves and make recommendations on how IRW can further improve on the project. • By phone/Mobile (call or text SMS). Focal person is assigned to collect and record feedback and complaints • Via a ‘suggestion/ complain box/visibility boards erected at the project sites will be used as a means of project access to feedback and improvement where necessary.
