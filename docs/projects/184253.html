Project Summary

This project will respond to the need of the most vulnerable displaced and host communities of 20,000 individuals (women = 6,500, men=4,400, boys= 4,200, girls = 4,780, PWD= 120) in Diinsoor and Bardale to cope with worsening humanitarian situation in these regions that now compounded by the effects of Covid 19 droughts which has resulted in massive challenges pertaining to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene. communities from these districts are still in need due to insufficient access to portable water and inadequate sanitation services of the affected population in many districts, thus negatively affecting the region’s public health and the dignity of highly vulnerable and displaced communities.
Lack of enough WASH services to the population is a main cause of spread of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD) in IDPs in the project target regions, leading to malnutrition in the country and contributing to increasing the existing stunting rate of 25.3% in children below 5 years of age. It is also a cause of relapse (ADD) when treated patients and children returning to their communities where safe water and sanitation coverages are limited, resulting in limited sustainability of health programs.
IRW through this intervention will identify distribution locations through in-depth assessment, and Relief Distribution Committees (RDCs) will be established at each distribution site, to facilitate the identification of the most vulnerable households and their needs, through community participation. This activity will also imply training of the beneficiaries on household water treatment, which will be conducted house-to-house by trained community hygiene promoters. The project activities will also include Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with women and girls in order to identify collective water points with secure location and access. At the same time, Water Management Committees (WMCs) will be established and trained in each area, which will be responsible for the maintenance of water points identified.
Through this intervention will conduct rehabilitation/improvement of existing water points and construction/ rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, in order to make them secure, protected and gender-appropriate (gender-segregated latrines with provision of locks and doors). This will include other FGDs, 50% with women and 50% with men. These activities will be paired with community sensitization on the use and maintenance of safe water sources and appropriate sanitation facilities, and on the use of correct hygiene and sanitation practices, e.g. the need of washing hands at critical moments, food preservation and proper storage of water safe for drinking, and discourage defecation, especially near open water sources. Community sessions of hygiene promotion will be conducted in common spaces in various locations, accessible by all categories of marginalized persons, by trained hygiene promoters in the course of one year.
