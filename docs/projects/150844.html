Project Summary

Conflict-affected populations are of particular concern, including those in hard-to-reach areas and displaced and marginalized (elderly, people with injuries, chronic illness, widows, divorce, pregnant and lactating women, malnutrition children, physical disabilities and/or psychological distress, polygamous wife, minority clans and their families) populations. IDPs, particularly those settling on the private land, are at constant risk of forced eviction. Protection issues include pervasive eviction and gender-based violence (GBV); child recruitment and early marriage.
Over 204,000 people were forcibly removed from their homes this year, nearly double the same period last year, with many people made homeless multiple times . Forced evictions in Somalia are predominantly an urban phenomenon and are typically carried out with no notice. In their search for shelter, evicted IDPs have been forced to relocate to overcrowded settlements or became homeless. Evicted IDPs are at risk of being recruited into armed groups.

Needs

In the context of a crisis, people with disabilities, elderly, people with injuries, chronic illness, and/or psychological distress and their family members) are often “invisible”, social networks are disrupted by the collapse of family and community-structures as a result of separation. The United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities states that Persons with disabilities are vulnerable to violence and multiple layers of discrimination and stigma. The UNCRPD art 11: situations of risk and humanitarian emergencies states the following: “States Parties shall take, in accordance with their obligations under international law, including international humanitarian law and international human rights law, all necessary measures to ensure the protection and safety of persons with disabilities in situations of risk, including situations of armed conflict, humanitarian emergencies and the occurrence of natural disasters” Since Handicap International recognizes there is still a huge gap regarding the “necessary measures” it aims to lend its specific technical capacity in support. The Universal Declaration on Human Rights, the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the African Charter on Human People's Rights speak clear - and also with a gender lense - the importance of human rights for persons with disabilities . Under the current challenges conditions, HI will pay even closer attention to needs and human rights of persons with disabilities that are often overlooked during emergency.Inadequate data disaggregation on PSNs, has contributed to limited awareness of disability and age issues among policy makers, planners, community leaders, services providers and the general public. How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

HI will work in conjunction with, not in replace of, local authorities to provide services. HI also works in conjunction with the appropriate clusters to ensure that all strategies are aligned with cluster and local government priorities. In all capacity building efforts, HI will engage the local authorities and communities with the intention of ensuring that the knowledge is retained even after the program ends. Programming will include capacity building of local structures (e.g. DPO’s local women’s groups, community-based leadership and CPCs) as well as risk mitigation activities to help ensure maximum sustainability. Much of the intervention is designed to provide immediate life-saving support to women and girls in need in conflict-affected, hard to reach areas. To the extent possible, sustainability will come through trainings and technical support to HI staff, partners, community organizations, and structures so they are better positioned to respond to the health, psychosocial, and protection needs of women and girls. HI staff members are recruited from the local IDP and host communities to support programming and activities to enhance long-term sustainability.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Elderly, People with disabilities and injuries face many additional difficulties before, during and after displacement. It is not common practice to include elderly and people with disabilities among those who are considered as particularly vulnerable in disasters and displacement and who therefore require targeted response – yet statistics tell us that up to 10% of all displaced people will have a disability. Solutions, 1) in areas with recurrent displacement, collect data on the number of persons with disabilities, and type of disability, alongside age and gender data plus any special needs they may have, 2) prioritize persons with disabilities in reunification efforts, particularly those dependent on caregivers, 3) Ensure disability questions are included in rapid assessments and target households with disabled people during interviews, focus group discussions or other assessment activities

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Persons with disabilities are extremely vulnerable to protection violations ranging from physical, sexual and emotional abuse to lack of access to justice systems and documentation. Children with disabilities are three to four times more likely to be physically or emotionally abused. Solutions include, 1) Target male and female persons with disabilities of all ages in all protection monitoring initiatives, 2) Assist persons with disabilities with communication difficulties to complete relevant forms and help all persons with disabilities to replace lost documentation and access justice and other systems, 3) Provide all information in simple language, using at least two means of communication (oral and written) and ensuring it reaches people who cannot leave their shelters/temporary homes. 4) Include persons with disabilities, persons with injuries and caregivers in psychosocial initiatives.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Death, physical (GBV, separations, disability, etc) and psychological trauma. People with physical, sensory as well as intellectual and psycho-social disabilities also face barriers in escaping and surviving armed conflict. Women with disabilities, already at a disproportionate risk for sexual violence during peace, are at an even greater risk of violence due to the insecurity of conflict, HI is proposing a combination of methodologies in ensuring that the barriers to access services are eradicated and addressed by way of strengthening and working with community protection mechanisms and improving greater access to information and services through dissemination of information on available services, identification of the most vulnerable and provision of referrals are urgently needed to ensure these groups’ survival and strengthen knowledge and practice of humanitarian actors on isolated and most at risk groups’ inclusion. Also, HI shall continue ongoing protection monitoring to ensure that the humanitarian community has a timely, accurate and up-to-date understanding of the needs and risks the population is facing and will conduct protection mainstreaming to ensure safe, dignified and accessible services across sectors and agencies. The proposed action intends to provide multi-sectoral assistance (protection, psychosocial support, specialized support tailored to specific needs of persons with disabilities as well as facilitating identification and referral/linkage of most excluded ad isolated population to other humanitarian actors for meaningful and timely access to lifesaving assistance.
