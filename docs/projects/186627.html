Project Summary

Outcome:
To improve access to quality and inclusive education as well as through formal and non-formal approaches with a major focus on the reduction of Out-of-School Children (OOSC) as well as improve learning outcomes through continuous learning of children of all categories (conflict-affected children, children with disabilities/CwD and so on) in a safe, protective and healthy learning environment.
Outputs:
• Provide access to formal and non-formal education to 15,554 children in and outside the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) camps through the provision of scholastic materials.
• To improve the quality of education delivered by equipping around 26 teachers/volunteers.
• To improve inclusiveness in the learning environment in 2,000 girls by involving them in curricula and extra-curricular activities while encouraging the participation of parents and other active community members.

1 - Needs

The impact of the crisis from the onset till date in the north-east has been devastating, topped with the whole COVID-19 pandemic which has also disrupted the livelihood of over 8.7 million people 1 (5.1 million children, 3.2 million adults and 0.4 million elderly) in need of humanitarian assistance across the three most affected north-eastern states of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. Armed clashes between the military and insurgents is constantly escalating with impacts on civilian populations of various locality across the north-eastern states.The current state of the security, and the new way of living due to the COVID-19 pandemic in the north-east has affected the education system in various of ways despite progress made in the pasted couple of years. The closure of schools in March 2020 in order to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 has compound the situation, which has restricting access to education, psychosocial support and learning to conflict affected children in turn increasing the number of out-of-school children (OOSC), reduced attendance and learning outcomes and lower completion rates.

2 - Activities

Continued delivery of strong education in emergencies programming:TIMC will continue support to ensure the children, especially girls and children with disabilities in IDP camps and host community locations have access to secure, safe and protective learning environments through the construction of new learning centres and rehabilitation of existing ones to accommodate a large number of children in need of education and the provision of furniture. We plan to run a double-shift to reduce the class size to utilize the few facilities and human resources available so proper learning can take place.In addition, education within a humanitarian context shows the importance of teaching and learning materials in a learning centre. Learning materials are very important to increase student interest and motivation to want to learn. TIMC will purchase and distribute teaching and appropriate child-friendly educational materials to promote learning and also a continuous implementation of enrolment drive campaign to reduce out-of-school with more emphasis on increasing girls’ participation.Mainstreaming of learners into formal education:The presence of large numbers of OOSC in the northeast and the dwindling education system has shown low completion and transition rates. The average secondary completion rate for the country is about 44 per cent but for the northeast states, it’s between 18 - 25 per cent. In the early grades, only 40 per cent of children in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states are attending school, this number reduced drastically to 4 - 17 per cent at the secondary level where most of the most affected are girls. TIMC has established a school in Adamawa which will be used to absorb some of the children in non-formal learning centres but not overwhelm the current existing school system, TIMC has a partnership with neighbouring schools in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe to absorb these children with major support from their internal resources through their scholarship programme. Addressing key cross-cutting issues, with a special focus on gender, disability and mental health and psychosocial support:TIMC based on the Inter-agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE) standards there is a need for teachers and education personnel to be equipped with the right training needed with respect to the current context they are operating in, it is necessary for this training should be a child-centred focus and inclusive to reach the most vulnerable.TIMC will carry out a series of training of teachers/volunteers in IDP camps, and host communities locations. The training topics will be cover child rights, pedagogy, child-centred methodologies, disability inclusiveness and psychosocial support (PSS) activities to enable teachers to systematically handle traumatized children.TIMC will be involved in the provision of uniforms for children where the majority of them will be girls to reduce the financial constraints on their parents as this is one of the biggest barriers in educating the girl child because most parents give priority to boys because they feel they will become the breadwinners. This intervention from observation in previous intervention has increased girls enrolment and improved parents’ motivation and confidence in sending their girl child to school and most importantly it has made girls feel valued and very happy to come to school.
