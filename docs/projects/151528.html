Project Summary

The project entails to provide immediate access to equitable livelihood assistance to the most vulnerable population in needs in Galgadud and lower shabelle regions through provision of unconditional and conditional cash for work to enable restore the lost community asset and ensure community resilience enhancement through this project will be able to provide community capacity building more specifically these in agropastral livelihood or farmers by providing both the inputs and tools to carry out their farming with ease and as well improve their livelihood needs.

Needs

About 6.2 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, a marginal reduction from the 6.7 million reported at the beginning of the year. Over 3.4 million people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC 4) and will face acute food insecurity through the end of 2018. According to FSNAU, most of Somalia’s rural livelihood zones are now classified as Crisis or Emergency. The protracted drought has had a drastic impact on the already fragile agricultural and livestock assets and has further eroded natural resources and the resilience of affected rural communities. Large-scale livestock losses and low agricultural production have significantly impacted household access to food and income. Prices of food and non-food commodities have increased well above the five-year average, weakening household purchasing power. Marginalization and discrimination due to societal conditions and/or power dynamics continues to render some groups at risk of being affected by food insecurity and vulnerable to shocks. In addition, individuals or households may have limited access to food due to age or gender or disability based discrimination. In the central regions, 548,000 people are in IPC Phase 3 and 269,000 people are in IPC Phase 4, while in southern regions 850,000 people are in IPC Phase 3 and 169,000 people are in IPC Phase 4. Multiple seasons of low agricultural production have left poor farming and agro-pastoral households with little to no food stocks or income. Many households have resorted to negative coping mechanisms to sustain their food levels, such as borrowing money which increases their indebtedness, consuming less preferable foods or skipping meals, early marriage to reduce burden on the family, and engaging children in labor. The most pressing humanitarian needs in 2018 are, immediate access to food and nutrition rural livelihood support to restore own food access, prevent further displacement, and enable early return or local integration or settlement

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Based on the cluster objectives, the response strategy includes the need to deliver a core package of integrated livelihoods services, where priority will be given to the delivery of equitable access to basic emergency livelihoods for the restoration of the community asset, while simultaneously addressing underlying causes of exposure to the risk of community livelihood attacks, To increase community uptake it will provided services, community level demand creation will be strengthened. DEH will strengthen the community capacity through training of active community village committees who will in turn provide the required services as well as information sharing within the community hence creating ownership and sustainability of the program even after the end of the donor funds.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project areas have high number of mixed pastoral and IDPs as well as scattered rural areas in Ceelbur and Haradhere as well as coastal deh with different livelihood status, due the diverse in the nature of the target beneficiaries the rural communities displaced might not welcome positively to the IDPs and conflict between the previous establish IDPs managers and newly displaced might conflict hence creating community tension, DEH will ensure to provide better coordination and information sharing when such eventuality hits the target community, also on the employment issues there might be discrimination or disparities between the women and men and to address this DEH will ensure to competitively sources the local community with expertise for employment considering women in equal measures this will then reduce the risk of gender disparities and minimized community expectation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Location, distance and access routes to the OTP sites and services might be a challenge of preventing factors limiting the access of the livelihoods. DEH will enable its beneficiaries have accessible to all of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women DEH will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. To ensure equity, DEH will used diverse community to reach rural communities as well as the IDPs and host communities, limited information and communication might hinder the understanding between DEH teams in the field and community leaders, however, to mitigate any such eventuality DEH will create feedback mechanism and reasonable compliance officer within its office as well as hotline mobile services to ensure all beneficiary complains are addressed timely

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Project staff will be trained on the basic human right issues and approaches to enable them understand the principle of equity and ensure those fleeing communities were well attended, the project will consult the communities where treatment centers will be place in avoidance of near military settlement as well as to avoid the risk associate with landmines and explosive targets.
