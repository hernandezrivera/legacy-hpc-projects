Project Summary

The number of malnourished CU5 and PLW is expected to continue increasing due mainly to El Nino induced drought and macro-economic challenges that hinder access to balanced diets. This is compounded by inadequate knowledge among mothers and caregivers of appropriate and healthy diets, especially in the first 1,000 days of life and poor IYCF practices. In addition, 21.2% of children have Vit A deficiency while 62% of women of child bearing age are iron. These aspects necessitate the need for supplementary feeding and micronutrient supplementation. Furthermore, there are capacity gaps within MOHCC staff and community health workers to deliver high impact nutrition services to community members, which necessitate the need for training and mentorship on IYCF-E, IMAM and provision of job aides. There are also reporting and coordination gaps which leads to duplication, wastage of resources, slow responses to needy people
To address these needs, the project interventions will include strengthening active screening to ensure early identification of malnourished children and referral through community health workers and family led MUAC screening, complemented by increasing availability and access to therapeutic (RUTF) and complementary foods. Facilitate micro-nutrient supplementation to children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women; he project will facilitate distribution of micronutrient supplements for children, pregnant and lactating mothers (MNPs, VAS, Iron and Folate Supplements). In addition, strengthen Care Groups and PD Hearth implementation to rehabilitate underweight children, enhancing sustainable nutrition practices through home visits and counselling sessions by VHWs/Care Group Lead mothers and continued promotion of preparation of nutrition dense foods through Positive Deviance Hearth model. Strengthen capacity of MOHCC staff and Community Health workers on IYCF-E through capacity building trainings, mentorship and provision of job aides. Reporting and quality will be enhanced through supporting monthly review and reporting meetings.
Objective from July 2019 to April 2020:
To rehabilitate malnourished children To protect the nutrition status of vulnerable children under the age of five years, pregnant and lactating women from deterioration due to the impact of drought induced food insecurity.

Needs

Malnutrition is a major challenge in Zimbabwe, with many adverse consequences for vulnerable groups like children under 5 (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The number of malnourished CU5 and PLW is expected to continue increasing due mainly to El Nino induced drought and macro-economic challenges that hinder access to balanced diets. This is compounded by inadequate knowledge among mothers and caregivers of appropriate and healthy diets, especially in the first 1,000 days of life and poor IYCF practices. In addition, 21.2% of children have Vit A deficiency while 62% of women of child bearing age are iron. These aspects necessitate the need for supplementary feeding and micronutrient supplementation. Furthermore, there are capacity gaps within MOHCC staff and community health workers to deliver high impact nutrition services to community members, which necessitate the need for training and mentorship on IYCF-E, IMAM and provision of job aides. There are also reporting and coordination gaps which leads to duplication, wastage of resources, slow responses to needy people.

Activities

Active screening and identification of children under five years for malnutrition: Strengthening active screening to ensure early identification of malnourished children and referral through community health workers and family led MUAC screening, Complemented by increasing availability and access to therapeutic (RUTF) and complementary foods. Facilitate micro-nutrient supplementation to children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women: the project will facilitate distribution of micronutrient supplements for children, pregnant and lactating mothers (MNPs, VAS, Iron and Folate Supplements). Strengthen Care Groups and PD Hearth implementation to rehabilitate underweight children: Strengthening of sustainable nutrition practices through home visits and counselling sessions by VHWs/Care Group Lead mothers and continued promotion of preparation of nutrition dense foods through Positive Deviance Hearth model. Strengthen capacity of MOHCC staff and Community Health workers to on IYCF-E deliverables through capacity building trainings, mentorship and provision of job aides. Reporting and quality will be enhanced through supporting monthly review and reporting meetings.
