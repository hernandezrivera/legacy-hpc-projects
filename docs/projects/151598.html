Project Summary

Medair will implement an integrated multi-sectoral project with the aim of improving access to timely and quality lifesaving integrated services for IDPs and vulnerable host populations. The project will be implemented with four local partners; SomaliAid, DAWA, SAACID and Zamzam in order to build on their capacity and ensure sustainability of the interventions once the program ends.
Medair will support the operation of ten primary health care facilities in Afgooye (1), Badhaadhe (1), Balcad (1), Cadale (1), Daynille (1), Hawlwadag (1), Karaan (2) and Kismaayo (2) districts through provision of equipment and medical supplies as well as paying of staff salaries. If GAM rates still justify outreach activities, these will be integrated multi-sectoral activities, including health activities: consultation, vaccination, health and hygiene promotion. This can be done in up to thirteen mobile outreach teams in Afgooye (1), Balcad (12) and Kismaayo (1) districts
Community Health will be implemented in the targeted locations through Medair’s Community networks which currently have a total of 999 Volunteers. Both the Care Group and iCCM models will be implemented in this project, depending on the community’s proximity to health facilities.
Community and health staff will be trained on BEmONC, IMCI, IYCF and relevant emergency response topics. Medair’s senior Managers will also provide on-job trainings during supervision visits in order to improve the capacity of health facility and community staff.
Medair will establish a referral system (where none existed before) and continue to support existing ones. Continuous training of community and facility-based health workers on EWARNs for outbreak disease surveillance and reporting will strengthen local capacity for identification of outbreaks. Responses will be coordinated with MoH, the cluster and local partners. An Emergency Response Team (ERT) will provide rapid response to suspected outbreaks. This will be done in areas where Medair’s local partners have good connections with the communities. Training will be provided to Medair and local partner staff, as well as officials from MoH, to improve on the quality of the outbreak responses.
Medair has prepositioned two tented health facilities. Medair will store the tented facilities in central warehouses where the different partners will access them at times of need. The tented health facilities can be utilized by local partners upon request to provide emergency responses (e.g. as a CTU in the event of an AWD outbreak).
Medair will mainstream protection and gender in all program aspects and utilize gender and culture analyses to inform project development. The proposed project will enhance inclusivity of women, men, boys and girls in all its programming aspects, and will, whenever possible, collect and analyze disaggregated data to give specific information about sex, age and other demographic groupings (the elderly, people with disabilities, minorities, etc.) that may bring to light any possible barriers to accessing health care.
Medair will promote awareness building and education about the importance of reproductive and sexual rights and will target both men and women. Among the men who will be specifically targeted are local government officials and leaders who give opinion such as the religious leaders, who play an important leadership role and guide decisions on general societal practices. Extensive reach to communities through Care Group volunteers and CCM CHWs’ household health promotion visits will also target boys and men to discourage FGM and other harmful practices.
Medair will continue with coordination and collaboration with the Somalia Health clusters; the coordinating body for government and NGO collaboration. It will also maintain regular communication with the cluster through coordination meetings both in Mogadishu, Kismayo and in Nairobi. Medair also has discussions with clan elders and local authorities regularly to investigate any new concerns or problems and to get input on potential solutions in an acceptable way.
Medair will continue to align its policies and procedures with CHS commitments and the five IASC principles on being accountable to affected populations which include leadership and governance, transparency, feedback and complaints, participation and design, monitoring and evaluation. Medair will also continue to be accountable to affected populations by ensuring monitoring and evaluation activities are carried out in an ethical and legal manner. Medair does not record names of people seeking services from the facilities and communities in order to protect their identities. Data collection devices are encrypted and can only be reviewed by authorized personnel. The data is also stored in secure servers that can only be accessed by specific people in the organization.
Medair has developed an M&E system that will improve and facilitate the process of data collection and analysis from health facilities, from community workers and from the Medair supervisors – all working with one database. Whenever possible, data will be disaggregated by age and sex to allow for more careful analysis of how the program engages these groups. This will help Medair to monitor its progress against project objectives, activities and indicators by utilizing streamlined data collection tools in line with methods already in place at the health facilities. Data collection will include both qualitative and quantitative mechanisms such as observation, supervision checklists, health facility and HMIS reports, nutrition registers and databases. Community input will also be included through community meetings, and assessment of key public health indicators will occur in KPC household surveys utilizing LQAS methodology. At the end of the project cycle, Medair will carry out an external evaluation with a full review of achievement of indicators, implementation of activities, beneficiary feedback, lessons learned and recommendations for future projects. The purpose of the review will be to determine the outcomes of the project, and the effectiveness and the efficiency in achieving those objectives.

Needs

Despite large scale humanitarian interventions in 2017/18, ongoing conflicts, wide spread acute malnutrition, food insecurity and natural hazards continue to reduce the resilience of communities and trigger internal displacement hindering access to basic services. Access to quality health services continues to be a challenge, especially for IDPs and in rural areas. Main service providers are private individuals and NGOs with limited government oversight in most regions. Lower Shabelle has coverage of 23% (920,000 have no access); both Banadir (1,080,000 have no access) and Lower Juba have 35% coverage. In Middle Shabelle, the coverage is 37% where 320,000 do not have access (HNO 2018). Maternal and newborn mortality, malnutrition and communicable diseases are the cause of 67% of deaths for all ages as reported in the chronic disease report by WHO. A total of 6,464 AWD/cholera cases being reported (since Dec 2017), 7,119 cases of measles and 1,998 cases of whooping cough since January 2018 (WHO/Government of Somalia – Epi week 40). If left unattended these conditions have a high risk of death and of undermining any advancement in the health. Survey by FSNAU and partners in July 2018 revealed that Crude Death Rate (CDR) was Critical (1 to <2/10 000/day) in 3 out of 33 population groups surveyed, including Mogadishu IDPs. Morbidity rates were also high (>20 %) in many parts of the country. Findings from the WHO report (2017) on progress of non-communicable diseases in Somalia reported that 22% of the total mortalities were as a result of NCDs. The burden from NCDs is still high with 20% of people between the ages of 30 to 70 years at risk of dying. The IDP and host population in urban areas of Mogadishu are in protracted crisis while populations in the rural areas of Middle and Lower Shabelle have repeatedly experienced conflicts linked to floods, limiting access to the few available health facilities.

Activities

Provision of quality PHC services as guided by Somalia national guidelines, this includes EPI, reproductive health and integrated nutrition to 200,000 beneficiaries through 10 health facilities. Strengthen referral mechanism for obstetric emergencies from rural areas by providing taxi ambulances in Mogadishu, Afgooye, Kismayo and Balcad, and establish referral mechanism for Cadale (Target: 5) EWARN system supported at all 10 HFs and weekly surveillance data submitted to MoH. Provide community case management of Malaria, Pneumonia and Diarrheal through iCCM to remote and hard to reach areas of Cadale (Target: 12,500). Provide Behaviour Change Communication through community networks such as Care Groups, to sensitize and mobilize individuals, families and communities to adopt targeted health behaviour which improves health outcomes for women and children under 5 (Target: 35,000) With the support of a central ER team, provide emergency response in the event of disease outbreaks within their area of operation including setting up of Oral Rehydration Points (ORPs) or Cholera Treatment Units (CTU/CTC), depending on the context. Where access and security allows, take integrated multi-sectoral mobile outreach services (including EPI and health consultations) to the remote and hard to reach under-served communities. Provision of regular feedback on project implementation to Communities/CHWs/Health Authorities (Target: Quarterly). New and refresher trainings for health staff and CGVs/CHWs in line with MoH guidelines. Trainings on BEmONC and IMCI. Emergency response - support all planned districts in emergency response, preposition ERT supplies and provide 2 month buffer stock, preposition 2 tented facilities in central warehouses where they can easily be accessed by ERT Team or Partners and train communities/staff on relevant ERT topics – both new and refresher trainings.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Medair’s humanitarian interventions are aligned with the Federal Government of Somalia’s National Development Plan 2017 to improve capacity to deliver access to integrated health and nutrition services for the people of Somalia. The program strategy is aligned with the broader organizational aims to respond to the greatest humanitarian needs of communities in areas where little assistance has been provided, particularly locations that are difficult—but reasonably secure—to reach. Medair carries out integrated multi-sectoral interventions that are both lifesaving (emergency) and medium-term (recovery and early rehabilitation). Medair’s approach links relief and rehabilitation to development. Specifically, this integrated project promotes effective health promotion, and health-seeking behaviors to improve the community’s resilience to current and future public health emergencies. It also focuses on capacity building and equipping health facilities to ensure a stronger local response to outbreaks and nutritional emergencies in the future. In addition to health and nutrition services, Medair will incorporate sanitation and access to clean water activities into the humanitarian response in Banadir, Jubaland, Hirshabelle and South West States of Somalia, emphasing health and hygiene promotion, including IYCF. Medair will implement its programs in accordance with the DoH strategic plans, specifically the guidelines for the Essential Package of Health Services (EPHS). In areas identified for their needs and gaps, Medair supports primary health care facilities and their catchment areas alongside local partner NGOs. Medair’s strategy puts heavy emphasis on community health care, working with CHWs in Care Groups, or in rural areas, in integrated community case management (iCCM) models. Medair partners with trusted and reliable local NGOs who share a similar vision, building their capacity to ensure continuity and sustainability of current program results. Medair has developed an exit and expansion strategy linked to its middle- and long-term vision, linked to capacity building It sets indicators and minimum capacity criteria that local partners, health facilities and locations must meet in order for Medair to exit/ transition and move to other vulnerable locations. This strategy also defines partner agency selection with matrixes that are based on organizational values for accountability. For this project, a strong emphasis will be placed on developing local capacity as an exit strategy from the start. Medair aims to increase the capacity of the local authority in terms of regulation, control and setting up accountability mechanisms. Medair has endeavored to ensure sustainability of its program gains in Somalia by including the partners in all programmatic planning. One of the supported facilities (Shibis) successfully transitioned in June 2016 to the development phase after being incorporated into EPHS and handed over to another organization.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Medair recognizes women and children are among the most vulnerable groups during conflicts. Through the implementation of this project, Medair will ensure services are accessible to both the host and the displaced populations. No person will be denied services based on gender or any other characteristic, including ethnicity. This needs to be a very intentional action for some groups of IDPs. Many of the IDPs in the areas where Medair is working in Mogadishu have come from the areas where Medair is working in Lower and Middle Shabelle. This helps in awareness of their needs. In order to assess discrimination or corruption, Medair will give beneficiaries the opportunity to evaluate services received, primarily through exit interviews at health facilities; the results will be compiled into regular Beneficiary Accountability reports.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The main focus of activities is on women of reproductive age and children U5 Dignity and privacy will be promoted; health facilities are accessible to persons with disabilities. In consultation with local NGO partners, Somali staff and beneficiaries, Medair continues to engage men as major decision makers in Somali culture. In rural areas where women are often responsible for household chores and parenting, family finances are largely controlled by the male gender indicating decision-making power. Here, women are also tending the family’s livestock. This activity keeps them away and makes self-care and care for children very difficult. Consequently, women and children are at a greater risk for disease and malnutrition. To ensure privacy and confidentiality at the supported health facilities, work has been done on partitioning for privacy during patient screening and examination and also confidentiality in handling and storage of the patient’s information records. Medair’s approach is influenced by cultural observations, such as barriers for acceptation of family planning activities that are rooted in customs and traditions. Staffing in HF balances male and female clinicians. In the network of Care Group Volunteers, the majority are women, selected from within and by the communities where they live and work. They are trusted by their community members and can broach sensitive topics of conversation, such as family planning and other health topics as cultural insiders. They are also able to reach influential mothers-in-law, who impact on the decisions of fathers about health care seeking behavior and the use of family finances for health. Care Group Volunteers also help identify households of persons with disabilities and special needs through household visits. Volunteers are trained to include these in health, nutrition, and hygiene promotion and to assess for any specific health needs. Transportation to health facilities can be arranged as necessary.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Services will be provided as close as possible to the beneficiaries, which reduces the risks to beneficiaries who may have to travel to access services. The Care Group Model and iCCM further optimize protection by bringing services to the household level in a low-key pattern that can continue to function during periods of tension. Medair will support a taxi referral network to ensure that beneficiaries with complicated cases safely reach the appropriate level of care, if possible because of insecurity Medair recognizes women and children are among the most vulnerable groups during conflicts. Through the implementation of this project, Medair will ensure services are accessible to both the host and the displaced populations. The Community networks will provide assistance to women in the community through dissemination of behavior change communication. Medair has incorporated a mental health component to help the targeted populations deal with trauma following a crisis.
