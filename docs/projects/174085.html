Project Summary

The Nutrition Crisis response Project is a Sector in IHANN project funded by BHA/OFDA since 2019. The Sector have two components – Maternal, Infant and Young Feeding in emergency (MIYCF-E) and Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM). The beneficiaries are IDPs, Returnees and Refugees in Banki, Damasak, Dikwa and Ngala in Borno State. The Nutrition aimed at reaching about 56,211 (33,727 women, 22,484 men) in total.
MIYCF-E Services will promote behavior change activities for improved MIYCF-E in Banki, Damasak, Dikwa and Ngala. Using SBCC strategies and community structures, Community support groups based on the population of IDPs to include mother-to-mother and father-to-father groups. The support groups will be clustered, and their activities supervised by the community health workers (CHWs) designated as Support Group Promoters and MIYCF Assistants, who will receive training and be given job aids for group activities. Promotion of behavior change will be complemented by mass media and community dialogue activities, one-on-one skilled MIYCF-E counseling at MIYCF corners and breastfeeding spaces/mother-baby areas, during facility and community-based GMP, community food/cooking demonstration for complementary feeding, micronutrient supplementation to children aged 6-59 months and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW).
Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM)
FHI 360 will continue to operate its existing nine sites for SAM treatment (6 OTPs and 3 SCs) and add two more OTP sites in 2021. Active case finding in the community will be driven by Community Nutrition Mobilizers and CMAM Assistants trained in the use MUAC and referral and follow-up in all our locations. The SAM sites will be managed by Assistant Technical Officers (CMAM) trained in CMAM (both OTP and inpatient management) with clinical background and reside on site. Medical Doctors employed as Technical Officer (health and Nutrition) will perform oversight function at the Stabilization Centre. Caregivers of the SAM children admitted in SC will be fed and provided with MIYCF-E services during admission. The project will commence family led MUAC screening. UNICEF will continue to supply RUTF as part of the extended MoU with FHI360 while a back up stock would be purchased.
There will be capacity building for all the Staff and volunteers which include doctors, CHWs, Community Nutrition Mobilizers, CMAM Assistants, nurses and midwives in health facilities on MIYCF-E and CMAM activities The capacity building will include also government health workers in the LGAs of operation on relevant issues such as out- and inpatient management of SAM, community mobilization, case finding, ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) forecasting, data management, defaulter tracing and referral, among others, for sustainability.
Integration: The Nutrition Project is a Sector within IHANN Project and part of integration, Health, WASH and GBV/CP volunteers will receive some form orientation to support the conduct MUAC screening during outreach and hygiene promotion activities and at the health facilities and GBV safe spaces. The WASH sector will continue to provide safe water and rehabilitate boreholes and latrines at nutrition OTP sites and health facilities. Hygiene promotion messages will be incorporated in all MIYCF-E activities and Pregnant and Lactating Women that meet the established vulnerability criteria will benefit from hygiene kits distributed by the WASH sector.
FHI 360 will continue to coordinate with the Federal MOH, BMoH, UN Agencies, Nutrition Partners and other sectors and participate in technical and plenary sessions with other Nutrition stakeholders both internally and externally
FHI 360 will conduct KAP and SMART Survey to assess the outcome of the above activities and make use of monthly routine data to improve services. The coordination will be at the IHANN Project Office in Maiduguri lead by the Nutrition Coordinator under the Response Coordinator.

1 - Needs

Optimal child’s nutrition is essential, as malnutrition during the first 1000 days (from conception to 23 months) of life have dire short- and long-term consequences on the individual’s growth, development, and overall health status. Nutrition sector in the North East, Borno continues to work in an environment where as many as 87%, depend on food aids (87%, 44%, 73.4% & 64.5% in (Banki), Damboa, Dikwa and Ngala LGA respectively SMART 2019) In this kind of situation Vulnerability to malnutrition is still very high should be unexpected break in pipeline for food supplies from development partners as most children are marginally malnourished and high possibility that most of the caregiver have not received any kind of behavioral messages relating to malnutrition or feeding practice. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months is on the increase across the BAY states when compared year-on-year, with Borno state rate above the emergency threshold at 10.7 per cent as of September 2020. Borno constitutes significantly to about 300,000 children currently suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) requiring urgent treatment and over half a million children moderately malnourished. Without timely and adequate treatment, one in every five of these children could die, especially in situation where there are late presentation.Exclusive breastfeeding rate In Borno is 26% (0-5months), still short of the WHO recommended 50% EBF coverage to have significant effect on child morbidity and mortality and to promote optimal growth. Only 23% mothers practice early initiation of breastfeeding within 30 mins to one hour of delivery while 22% practiced mixed feeding practice for their infant (0 - 5 months). A real concern and need for IYCF Intervention. Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) for Banki, Damboa, Dikwa and Ngala was 10.9%, 11.9%, 4.0% and 21.3% respectively. Children who are severely malnourished are 12 times more likely to die (all-cause of mortality) compared to a well-nourished child. A non-breastfed infant under 6 months is 14 times more likely to die from pneumonia/diarrhea than an exclusively breastfed infant <6months. Furthermore, children and women of children bearing age with disability are 5 times more likely to be malnourished than those without disability. In addition, children of underage mother, female headed households, foster care, living in the streets, unaccompanied and separated children are at a higher risk of acute malnutrition IYCF practices are highly linked with malnutrition, morbidity, and mortality particularly in children under 5 years. In Nigeria, malnutrition accounts for over 50% of under-five mortality and women in Northern Nigeria. Improvement needed are mostly behavioural. Promotion of IYCF practices for improved IYCF practice is inevitable to upgrade knowledge and improve practice of child feeding. An exclusive breastfed child is 14 times less likely to die in the first 6 months than non-breastfed child. Most of Infant mortality takes place in early months of life, hence timely initiation of breastfeeding reduces neonatal mortality by 22%. An estimated 13% of child death could be averted if 90% of mothers exclusively breastfed their infants for the first 6 months of life, and with the same proportion practicing adequate and timely complementary feeding for their infant 6-23months, a further 6% of child deaths could be prevented. Improving Vitamin A status reduces mortality rates by 23% on the average. This is in addition to the fact that improved iron and folate status of pregnant mothers reduces maternal mortality as iron deficiency anaemia contribute to 20% of all maternal deaths. Micronutrient supplementation is of utmost importance. To reduce malnutrition related death, active case finding through MUAC screening, Growth Monitoring and Promotion, and community mobilization are essential since late presentation for treatment is a major cause of death for SAM children.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

FHI 360’s proposed program extension has taken into consideration the heightened risk of sexual and GBV in situations of conflict-related displacement, for women and girls as well as men and boys. Incidents of unaccompanied and separated children, child labor, and female-headed households often rise in such contexts, while children (especially girls) commonly drop out of school or are married off at a young age to cope with the loss of livelihoods. The current conflict and associated displacement has also increased the potential for intra-family violence due to individual and social trauma; the return of former combatants and wider availability of weapons in the community; changes in gender roles and notions of masculinity; generalized impunity and breakdown of the rule of law; and increased tensions and frustration caused by economic stress, challenging living conditions, and the lack of opportunities. FHI 360 recognizes the impact of the ongoing conflict on women, girls, boys, and men on protection-related issues and in relation to access to humanitarian services, nutrition, raising children, information and opportunities. Additionally, we acknowledge cultural factors that influence gender roles and can limit the ability of women to work or make household decisions. This project will further build upon FHI 360’s extensive SRH, GBV and child protection expertise and knowledge of gender-related and other vulnerabilities particularly within the Nigerian IDP and Borno state host community context. There will be special attention paid to protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), especially when beneficiaries are directly accessing services from FHI 360. To ensure protection against sexual exploitation and abuse and child safeguarding, FHI 360 has developed a code of conduct on Child Safeguarding and SEA specifically for community-based workers in addition to the already existing staff code of ethics and conduct and the FHI 360 Child Safeguarding guidelines. FHI 360 will provide training every six months for all staff, CVs and CHEWs on this code of conduct while supporting reporting of abuses and action through its community feedback mechanism (CFM). Child protection and GBV coordination will be reinforced through cross-referrals and regular meetings to determine the best course of action to meet the child’s need with the consent/assent from the child/caregiver while maintaining confidentiality, safety and security of the child/caregiver. Where required, case conference will be conducted to identify the cross-cutting needs of children/caregivers. Also, the need for GBV services for example in cases where children face neglect as a result of a parent having experienced GBV, the internal referral system will be activated. CP staff will be kept informed of the services that caregivers and/or adolescent mothers in the GBV subsector need and will be encouraged to screen for GBV and refer accordingly. FHI 360 consistently works with its staff, stakeholders, local authorities and other actors to ensure gender and protection mainstreaming into all activities by prioritizing consideration of the specific and often sensitive issues related to gender, disability, and protection among the beneficiary populations. FHI 360 will promote close community participation in the design, implementation, and evaluation (e.g. quality peer reviews) of all project activities to support inclusion based on gender, disability, and age. Special considerations of persons affected by GBV and child protection concerns will be considered (e.g. special sanitation for women and girls). Disability considerations, especially for physical access to services delivery points (latrines, health facilities, water points) will be paramount when designing and selecting locations for such sites.
