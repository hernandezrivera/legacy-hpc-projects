Project Summary

For over eight years now, the ongoing Anglophone crisis in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon has caused many individuals including women, men, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities (PWD) to be displaced to various locations in search for safety and security. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and their hosts especially in hard to reach areas have right to live in dignity, protection and security as well as receive humanitarian assistance on the basis of need, without discrimination and exposure to further harm. According to UNICEF reports from las MSNA (R9,2023 and R10, 2024) there is limited access to safe water, and sanitation facilities by affected persons especially IDPs in hard to reach areas in the Menchum and Mezam divisions of the North West Region.
This project aims at ensuring equitable access to WASH services by IDPs and their host communities in Menchum three subdvisions and thee sub divisions in Mezam divisions. Specifically the project targets affected women, men and children by carrying out hygiene and health promotion, rehabilitation of water points, construction of gender sensitive latrines and distribution of WASH kits. In the course of implementing this project, aspects of protection, gender based violence and child protection shall be mainstreamed.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The WASH needs assessed in Menchum and Santa Subdivision like other areas affected by the conflict in North West and South West Regions of Cameroon are very critical based the ongoing November 2024 WASH assessment by humanitarian actors, National Organizations and WASH partners using Kobo tool under the auspices of UNICEF. Some of the key needs identified on the field include access to clean and safe drinking water. There have been the Destruction of Infrastructure; Many WASH facilities have been destroyed or damaged due to the ongoing violence. This includes water supply systems, sanitation facilities, and hygiene infrastructure. Access Challenges: Insecurity and violence have made it difficult for humanitarian organizations to access affected areas and provide necessary WASH services. Health Risks; Waterborne Disease like cholera, diarrhea, dysentery etc. The destruction and disruption of WASH facilities have increased the lack of safe, clean water which causes to water related diseases like diarrhea, cholera, dysentery, typhoid and even covid. Almost 61 percent of households do not have access to functional latrines. In November, 12,000 people received WASH and hygiene kits, compared to 35,300 in October, while people who accessed improved sanitation facilities from WASH partners decreased from 9,000 in October to 6,400 in November. Access to improved water supply was lower in November with 5,900 people benefiting compared to 10,000 beneficiaries in October. Lack of funding remains a major setback in WASH partners’ ability to meet set targets. From the above statistics, IDPs and their host communities are in need of WASH facilities. As more IDPs settle in these localities, there is pressure on available WASH facilities such as water, and sanitation facilities (toilets). As a result of inadequacy, open defecation is rampant leading to the spread of diseases like cholera. The fact that there has been reported cholera outbreak in the SW is a reason to prevent as this disease can be transmitted through affected individuals traveling. The humanitarian response through this project will help address the needs of the IDPs and the host communities in the aforementioned localities in Ngoketunjia, Menchum, Mezam Divisions.
