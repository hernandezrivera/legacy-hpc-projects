Project Summary

The objective of the project is to facilitate increased community resilience and provide increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions. The project activities will focus on vulnerable communities in Aleppo and Ar Raqqa governorates.

Needs

Increasingly complex and inter-linked protection needs continue to prevail in Syria, resulting from a variety of situations ranging from direct exposure to hostilities, displacement, conditions in sites/collective shelters and protracted displacement. Girls and boys of all ages continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights on a daily basis. Continuous displacement, exposure to violence, deepening poverty and the persistent lack of access to services and even the most basic necessities, including in newly accessible areas, are taking a huge toll on children. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern. Qualitative sources in the 2019 Multi Sector Needs Assessment indicate that the conflict and its impact on children and families have fuelled negative coping mechanisms such as child labour, early marriage and child recruitment, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk, while straining the capacities of families and communities to protect children. The high occurrence of child labour and early marriage, both identified as coping mechanisms (in 80% and in 45% of assessed communities respectively), prevent school attendance. In addition, trauma and psychological distress still have a major impact on the population. The need for community based psychosocial support activities and awareness raising on child protection issues is acute.

Implementation of activities

Community-based child protection for girls and boys is available in targeted locations through the: * Provision of structured and sustained child protection programmes, including psychosocial support, to vulnerable boys and girls. Following a needs assessment, mixed-gender project staff will design a multiple week curriculum for boys and girls in two community centers. Each child will attend between 16 and 20 session. The activities will promote individual and collective support through art, music, drama, sports, and story-telling, and will employ culturally sensitive action methods, as well as games and activities aiming to develop a feeling of safety, trust, connection, communication, relationship, collaboration, play and imagination. Community centers provide a safe space for children and can reduce the impact of protracted conflict by reintroducing a routine into lives disrupted by war and school closures. The structured play activities and creative resources in the community centers will support children’s resilience and psychosocial wellbeing. * Provision of parenting programmes to adults, mainly women. Project staff will conduct Focus Group Discussions and design a curriculum for training on various good parenting techniques with parents.* Organize and facilitate awareness raising and community events to prevent and respond to child protection issues. The project teams will organize large psychosocial community events such as themed art exhibitions, music events and sports competitions. These will be large scale community activities aiming to facilitate the reestablishment of community and family networks, and to support social network building. * Support youth and adult groups to ensure the community's active participation in preventing and responding to child protection issues. Youth and adult groups meeting in the two community centers will receive training and mentoring, and will be encouraged to raise awareness among their peers on child protection issues.This project is in-line with the Syria HRP 2019 Strategic Objective 2: Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria, by promoting international law, IHL, HRL and through quality principled assistance. It is also in-line with the Protection Cluster Objective 5: Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk A: Risk of sexual exploitation or abuse by staff members or partner staff members. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation A: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. Risk B: Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighbouring community not served. Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation B: Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Risk C: Protection risks faced by girls and boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation C: Ensure consultation with communities and neighbouring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments. Risk D: Risk of existing traditional and religious practices increasing due to limited community engagement or community acknowledgement of certain traditional and religious practices as a protection concern. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation D: Liaise with "neutral" parties as much as possible and triangulate all information from all sources prior to decision making. Risk E: Risk of girls and boys exposure to protection risk due to the limited initiatives to enhance children's participation in their own protection. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation E: Linking protection intervention with existing services within targeted communities to enhance the system approach. Risk F: Risk of some members of the community not being able to physically access services due to disability. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation F: The community centers are on the ground floor with easy access. Should special transport be required, project staff will provide free shuttle service to the community centers.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk A: Safety and security risks project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation A: The organization has a designated security focal point whoactively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place.
