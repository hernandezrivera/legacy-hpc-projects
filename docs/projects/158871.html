Project Summary

This project is in line and contributes to the Protection Cluster objective that calls for putting in place protection response mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the effects of occupation and conflict-related violence. It will ensure the most vulnerable children (boys and girls aged 7-17 years old) exposed to conflict related violence are reached with protection and prevention services in the West Bank.
This project aims for boys and girls to be equally safe, protected, and secure from violence, exploitation and grave violations. It will provide support to caregivers (mothers, fathers and teachers) and children to access protection services including structured mental health and psycho social support in the worst affected areas in the West Bank. Capacities of legal counselling and representation providers will be built, and legal aid for children will be strengthened. This will be achieved through identifying possible areas of interventions, prioritization of locations with the highest gap of service provision, capacity building of staff and volunteers, supporting interventions based on existing partnerships or centres and strengthening of coordination and information mechanisms. The project will also support incident monitoring, data analysis and reporting on grave violations trends related to child protection concerns in Israel/Occupied Palestinian Territory (both Gaza and the WB) to provide evidence and inform advocacy. Data collection and monitoring will be enhanced through documentation of testimonies of children in military detention.

1 - Needs

The situation in oPt continues to deteriorate and impact the lives of children and their respective families affected by conflict-related violence. Since 30 March 2018, Gaza Strip has witnessed a high number of Palestinian injuries (35,280 cases) in the context of mass demonstrations at Israel’s perimeter fence with Gaza. The demonstrations have occurred as part of the “Great March of Return” (GMR), which continue up to date on a weekly basis. Since these demonstrations began until 27 September 2019, a cumulative total of 44 children were killed, including 11 boys in 2019. Among them, 38 children were killed by live ammunition and 4 children died after being shot in the head with tear gas canisters. Up to end of August 2019, 4,024 children were hospitalized due to injuries sustained in the context of GMR. The long-term nature of physical and psychological impacts of injuries combined with overwhelmed and limited service providers raise concerns about the capacity to address existing and new humanitarian needs for MHPSS needs of both injured children and their families. With the above situation, an urgent need has been identified for the scale-up of monitoring and documentation, to ensure data collection, verification and reporting and timely analysis to inform the humanitarian response. This was clear in light of the limited capacities of protection partners to conduct monitoring activities to the extent required in the context of the increasing casualty figures. Moreover, as oPt is a “Situation of Concern” on the agenda of the Security Council, it is required to submit inputs to the Secretary General’s annual report of the on Children and Armed Conflict (CAAC). As such, there is a need to rely on independent sources for emergency monitoring of incidents of CAAC or grave child rights violations. This requirement further supports the need to enhance monitoring capacities of independent protection partners to strengthen the credibility of the findings. The situation in the West Bank is not improving; the conﬂict related violence affects children’s lives in multiple ways. Since the beginning of 2019, 2 girls and 4 Palestinian boys were killed and 1,120 children were injured during conﬂict-related incidents. In the West Bank, the ongoing confrontations with the Israeli Forces (IF) have resulted in property damage, large numbers of arrests including children. From January 2019 to date 49,935 people were affected by demolitions and displacement with 580 people displaced and 93 donor-funded structured demolished. Restrictions on Palestinian freedom of movement has continued, and disruptions in the provision of services affecting Palestinians. Stone throwing, demonstrations and clashes are often reported between Palestinians and IF. Checkpoints and refugee camps remain friction points and tensions are often observed around schools at opening and closing time. Between the beginning of January 2019 and end of September 2019, more than 95% of the population including children affected by demolition and displacement reside in Area C and East Jerusalem, with an increasing number of Bedouin communities enduring multiple demolitions and threats of demolitions through court issued orders. Other protection issues such as the use of force, the arrest of children as well as settler violence remain a serious concern. This has led to hundreds of children requiring psychosocial support, child protection and legal supportservices. The need in both the West Bank and Gaza Strip to strengthen the systematic response to vulnerable children is very high. The decades-old occupation, and the inherent violence that it embodies, have created a perpetual state of insecurity for children and perpetuated a range of protection violations. Children are particularly vulnerable to the threats and violence.

2 - Activities

The project aims for children (aged 7-17) to be safe, protected and secure from violence, exploitation and grave violations. Its main goal is to reduce vulnerabilities of children and families in the West Bank (WB), including East Jerusalem (EJ), and to improve their resilience when in contact with security forces. It also aims to document CAAC incidents within the oPt/Israel as a 'Situation of Concern'. This will be achieved by focusing on the following: 1) Protecting children at-risk of conflict with security forces through coordinated community level prevention programmes and protective services in the WB, including EJ; 2) Responding to the needs of directly affected children and their families through the development of a case management system in the WB; and 3) Documenting and reporting on CAAC incidents. The project will be informed by the United Nations Response Strategies that highlights the needs of children in oPt: the findings and recommendations of the Rapid Appraisal and the consultations conducted by UNICEF with key stakeholders. In addition, UNICEF and its partners will involve children, adolescents and caregivers by conducting field observations, home visits, focus group discussions, and community outreach activities. Children will be consulted to review existing feedback mechanisms, to express their views and provide suggestions on how to make those mechanisms more accessible. Upon collection and receipt of complaints, responses and feedback, UNICEF and partners will process the feedback, responses and complaints in line with UNICEF Operational Guidance on Accountability to Affected Populations recently developed. Regarding the geographical scope, intervention in Gaza is only limited to activities 3.1, 3.2, and 3.3 for the achievement of Output 3. The above activities together with other activities under outputs 1, 2 and 4 will be also implemented respectively across the WB. The project will seek to achieve the following main outputs and their related activities: Output 1: Support caregivers and children to access protection services including mental health and psycho-social support in worst affected areas in the WB, including EJ:Activities:1.1 Provide child protection services including recreational activities, structured psychosocial support with linkages to case management referral pathways; 1.2 Provide protection services for caregivers including psychosocial support and positive parenting; 1.3 Outreach activities with parents and children on available child protection rights and services; Output 2: Capacity building of legal counselling and representation providers and strengthened legal aid for children prior to interrogation and during remand in the WB:Activities:2.1 Strengthen capacity of organizations’ legal counselling and representation; 2.2 Strengthen access to legal representation for children in contact with Security Forces;2.3 Strengthen awareness of children, and caregivers on children's rights when arrested and detained; Output 3: Support incident monitoring, data analysis and reporting on grave violations trends related to child protection concerns in oPt/Israel (including Gaza and the WB) to provide evidence and inform advocacy. Activities: 3.1 Capacity building support to partners in the WB and Gaza on documentation, monitoring and reporting of grave violations; 3.2 Strengthen CAAC database system for confidential compilation and analysis of violations;3.3 Engage with State and non-State authorities to advocate for increased protection of children in Israel and the oPt (including Gaza and the WB) from grave violations; 3.4 Support in house internal capacity for documenting, reporting and advocacy; Output 4: Support documentation of testimonies of children in military detention. Activities: 4.1 Collect testimonies of children's experiences in military detention; 4.2 Develop detention database for trends analysis of treatment of children in military detention.
