Project Summary

In Maiduguri Municipal Council (MMC) and Jere, NRC will provide a mobile site management response in 50 sites (47 informal and 3 formal), including 12 sites in addition to the initial 38 sites where NRC has been implementing MSM since March 2020. For this expansion of the mobile site management response, NRC will ensure a continuous focus on increasing provision of services through enhanced coordination of partners in those sites that are currently facing significant gaps. NRC will particularly ensure that these sites are being covered by health and WASH services, with regards to COVID-19 preparedness and response plans, and will advocate and coordinate with the relevant actors accordingly. The response will also aim at supporting community engagement and governance structures, in order to increase the meaningful participation of all community groups in decision making. Specific care and maintenance activities will also be undertaken to guarantee that site facilities and infrastructures are functional and safe, in particular to mitigate the impact of floods and winds during the rainy season. Furthermore, in accordance with the DMS/CCCM sector strategy, NRC also proposes to establish a community centre to address out–of-camp displacement gaps through an area-based approach, using the NRC’s Urban Displacement and Out of Camp (UDOC) response. The community centre will be a coordination platform where beneficiaries living out-camp would be able to access services and to be referred to specific service providers. NRC will also respond with a mobile outreach team that will deploy in the different settlements of the catchment area to conduct awareness campaigns, referrals, collect and receive feedback. NRC will particularly focus in building preparedness to prevent the spread of COVID19 in those sites where very little or no support has been provided.

1 - Needs

According to Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 33, there is a total of 143 formal and informal sites in Maiduguri Municipal Council (MMC) and Jere hosting 269,341 individuals, which makes them the LGAs with the highest number of IDPs living in formal and informal sites. Of these sites, 56 are not receiving CCCM services, while 87 are (including 50 sites covered by NRC respectively since March and November 2020, corresponding to 64,873 individuals). Within the sites receiving CCCM support, the shelter DMS/CCCM sector (according to the camp management bi-weekly tracker 1-15 November 2020) reports that gaps in service provision are still critical, including: 39% of the IDPs still living in emergency shelters and 32% in makeshift shelters, as well as 2,599 households (HH) sharing shelters across 29 sites in seven LGAs. Moreover, a total of 76,136HH are reported in need of complete NFI kits (including 13,367HH in Maiduguri, 11,145HH in Jere), and 14% of the total latrines and showers are reported to be damaged. In terms of food assistance, 34% of the IDP population did not received food assistance the previous month. Lastly, 18% of the total households are headed by vulnerable population with 17.6% being headed by women (>18 years) and 0.4% by children (<18 years). A joint rapid needs assessment (RNA) in February 2020 in Bolori II (MMC) highlighted the significant vulnerability of displaced populations in that “out-of-camp” (UDOC) urban area in terms of access to essential services. IDPs settled in Bolori II consist of protracted IDPs and of new arrivals who recently flew from conflict-affected areas, sometimes having suffered secondary displacements. These communities are usually living in vulnerable housing such as makeshift shelters, abandoned buildings, shared rooms with other families or rented houses. IDP settlements out of camp are scattered in the urban landscape, mixed with the host communities, and characterized by lack of planning and poor care and maintenance. The rapid needs assessment conducted in February 2020 highlighted the presence of few organisations working in those areas, with critical gaps identified in particular in WASH, access to food, shelter and legal assistance, including significant protection concerns related to housing, land and property (HLP), gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection (CP). For these reasons, UDOC IDPs are also particularly vulnerable to extreme weather conditions such as cold and floods or fire outbreaks, as well as spread of communicable diseases, including COVID-19. An additional assessment was conducted by REACH in July 2020 (COVID-19 Vulnerability and Multi-Sectoral Needs of Host Communities Bolori 1 & 2, Maiduguri LGA, Borno State, Nigeria). Key findings confirmed the vulnerability of out of camp locations in these 2 wards. 96% of key informants reported that their settlements had received new arrivals in the 30 days prior to data collection, in particular from Magumeri, Gubio and Monguno. 96% of key informants also reported that approximately half or more residents of their settlements did not have enough food to meet their daily needs, including 65% who reported that certain items have become unavailable or more difficult to find at the nearest market place in the 30 days prior to data collection. 30% of key informants reported that IDPs were residing in self-settled sites, including 17% confirming that makeshift shelters were the most common shelter type for residents. Lastly, 30% of key informants reported that eviction is a concern for most residents.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

As part of its MSM and UDOC components, the project will ensure access to assistance, meaningful participation and inclusion for all groups of the community. In particular, the response will aim at involving these groups in all phases of the project. NRC will build on the traditional or created community representative structures established and/or supported in and out of camps. These structures are representative of the different demographic groups (gender, age, cultural specificities, specific needs and other marginalized groups) and will serve as a coordination mechanism between the community and NRC, as well as a channel for feedbacks and referrals. NRC will also strengthen its “Complaint and Feedback mechanism” through the installation of mobile feedback Desks where beneficiaries will have the opportunity to share and to receive feedback from NRC staffs. NRC will also mainstream protection through its site improvement and care & maintenance activities. Based on the community groups feedback and requests (see above), NRC will implement site improvements in order to improve living conditions, site safety and reduce protection risks. Hence, NRC will encourage community groups (such as site safety committees, site maintenance committees, women committees, elderly committees, etc.) to identify needs and will provide technical and material support for site improvement activities. In addition to regular meetings with relevant community structures, NRC will also conduct safety audits and participatory safety mappings to identify risks and unsafe areas in the camps. Site improvement works could include, but not limited to: construction of a fence to protect latrine area, lightening of latrines, installation of a gate at the camp entrance, construction of drainages to prevent flooding risks, rehabilitation of communal building. Site improvement could also be implemented through the cash for work modality. This modality will ensure that beneficiary selection is gender balanced and based on vulnerability criteria, in accordance with the corresponding Early Recovery Working Group SOPs. Referrals to specific service providers, such as shelter, WASH, Protection/Legal assistance to address protection risks will also be a key aspect of both MSM and UDOC components. Referrals will also be conducted through gender-balanced mobile outreach teams, in particular in UDOC locations, in order to reach remote areas and identify vulnerable cases to be referred.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will develop a CFM mechanism based on the community preferred feedback channels, following a participatory survey. This mechanism should therefore be accessible and adapted to the different demographic groups, including the most vulnerable and marginalized groups. NRC will use both static and mobile approach in order to also reach beneficiaries that have less access to the CFM Desk (in camps) or to the UDOC centre (out of camp). NRC will also coordinate closely with the specific community structures, including representative committees (women, elderlies, persons with disabilities, leadership, etc.) to ensure adequate access to vulnerable population.
