Project Summary

The proposed project aims to focus on constructing, rehabilitating, and maintaining water supply facilities in priority counties, along with integrated WASH programming with Nutrition, demonstrates a holistic approach to tackling WASH-related challenges. The emphasis on reducing morbidity and mortality, providing emergency WASH services, addressing Gender-Based Violence, and enhancing community resilience to environmental hazards underscores the project's comprehensive scope. By setting specific targets for reaching households, ensuring safe water access, promoting hygiene, enhancing sanitation facilities, and rehabilitating infrastructure, the project aims to significantly improve WASH conditions and public health outcomes in South Sudan. 36% of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes with no protection concerns. The remaining 64% across the country rely on unimproved or surface water sources. Upper Nile has the highest proportion of households (66%) relying on surface water. Followed by Jonglei (43%) and Central Equatoria (41%). The quantity of water available per day is below standards in many locations, with 17% of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39% of people reporting insufficient access to water for hand washing. Access to sanitation remains low, with 17% of households report owning a latrine in their compound, 4% have access to a shared latrine, and 2% have access to a communal/institutional latrine, meaning 77% of people do not have access to a latrine. Nationally open defecation is practiced by 73% of the population. Regional access to sanitation varied greatly, from 75% in Western Bahr el Ghazal to 8% in Warrap, 9% in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and 10% in Jonglei.
The combination of poor access to Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services alongside heightened levels of food insecurity continues to pose a significant threat to the health of children, leading to elevated rates of malnutrition and waterborne diseases. Counties with pronounced rates of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) often coincide with areas experiencing substantial WASH deficiencies. Mothers and caregivers of malnourished children frequently lack fundamental hygiene knowledge and access to safe water, thereby jeopardizing the effectiveness of nutrition programs. World Vision (WV) aims to address these challenges by implementing an integrated WASH intervention targeting Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities in specific areas including Upper Nile (Melut, Renk, Malakal, Kodok, Baliet, Panikang), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil North and East), and Warrap (Twic, Tonj North, Gogrial West, and Gogrial East).
In line with the objectives of the WASH cluster, World Vision International - South Sudan plans to deliver WASH emergency interventions in the designated counties to enhance access to safe water, establish secure and gender-appropriate sanitation facilities, and promote hygiene practices. These interventions will be integrated with various sectors such as health, nutrition, food security and livelihoods, and child protection/Gender-Based Violence (GBV) to ensure a comprehensive and coordinated approach to addressing the urgent WASH needs in these communities.

1. Needs

In October 2024 according Humanitarian Snapshot, the humanitarian situation in South Sudan worsened due to a combination of factors including soaring food prices, a severe economic downturn, conflicts, disease outbreaks, movements of people from Sudan, and flooding. By the end of October, assessments conducted by multiple agencies revealed that approximately 1.4 million individuals in South Sudan had been impacted by floods. Among them, over 377,000 people were forced to flee their homes due to flooding and sought refuge in higher areas. Limited physical WASH infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring floods in many parts of the Country remain the main drivers for WASH needs. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also contributes an additional burden on existing WASH infrastructure at displacement sites and creates challenges for WASH partners when implementing activities. In South Sudan, only 36% of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64% of people across the country either rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41%), take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19%), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4%). The highest proportion of households relying on surface water were found in Upper Nile. Approximately 9 million people (24% women, 54% children, 7% elderly, 15% people with disability) in South Sudan are in need of humanitarian assistance as of 2024 and in WASH sector is 5.6 million people. The situation is particularly severe in Manyo, Upper Nile State. Living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 6 million people were negatively impacted by inadequate access to clean water and sanitation and poor hygiene practices in 2024. Increased displacement has been experienced in states such as Northern Bar El Gazel (Aweil East and Aweil West) due to influx of returnees (over 7000 individuals) fleeing the conflict in Sudan. Returnees are met with the challenge of limited water sources, lack of latrines in public places, hand washing facilities and floods. Access to basic WASH services in South Sudan is limited, with only 36% of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns (HNOs), Access to sanitation remains low. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. High open defecation rates also expose women, children and people living with disabilities to higher risk of violence and discrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements. The absence, presence or placement of WASH facilities is a driver of population movement and conflict over land and property in some areas and requires careful management to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach that strengthens social cohesion. Around 15% of households have at least one disabled family member, as after years of conflict, many civilians have a disability after being maimed, or had an amputation, damaged or destroyed sight and hearing are some the identified impairments. The situation is worsened by the outbreak of cholera in Upper Nile and Hepatitis E in Abyei Administrative areas. This public health risks are exacerbated by the limited access to WASH services in the country.

The proposed project will contribute to the WASH Sub Cluster's objective of Ensuring that Construct, rehabilitate, and maintain water supply facilities for people in the mention states, WVI will be implementing those activities in line with the Cluster Objective. CO1: Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition. Provide WASH integrated interventions starting from the OTP/SC will target the couple “mother /caretaker – malnourished children” until the household level aiming to break the vicious circle of diarrheal diseases /nematodes with malnutrition, addressing also small stock open defecation that is most affecting the young children.
