Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates number of crisis-affected people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is 14.2 million (48.7% male and 51.3% female), of which 6 million are in acute need. The above figure of people in need includes 6.7million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); estimated 320,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 75,000 refugees return; 438,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities. More than 70% of the population in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria.
Overall, 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure (including 1.27 million severely food insecure and 9.5 million moderately food insecure) and 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity. In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 75,830 small-scale herding and farming households (equivalent to 454,980 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to improve food security and strengthen the resilience of vulnerable herders through restocking, the provision of animal feed and capacity building. The project outputs include:
• Output 1: Production assets of 16,250 vulnerable, crisis-affected households restored through restocking.
This output aims at restoring the productive capacity of crisis-affected households through the provision of small ruminants (two female ewes/sheep per household, in line with sector response package recommendations for livelihoods)
• Output 2: Livestock productivity for 59,580 vulnerable crisis-affected breeders is maintained.
This output aims at improving the availability of animal feed for the purpose of asset protection for 59,580 vulnerable households (400 kg of feed per household, in line with sector recommendations) as well as boosting milk availability through enhanced livestock production.
• Output 3: Improved skills and livestock management capacity of 75,830 households
This output aims at supporting all project beneficiaries to enhance their livelihoods through strengthened skills in livestock production and management.
It is expected that the introduction of agriculture-based livelihood protection support in the current protracted crisis will increase the local availability of food, improve self-reliance and food and nutrition security of Syrians, especially the most vulnerable.

4 - Needs

Livestock are a vital element to the livelihoods of a large number of vulnerable rural households in Syria. However, the sector has suffered substantial losses during the crisis. Livestock and animal production represented around 40% of the agriculture sector and generated in excess of USD 400 million in foreign currency earnings since 46% of Syrian land areas constitutes rangeland and only 28% are arable land. Livestock supports a large portion of the poor, landless and marginalized farmers (Men and women) in Syria. The sector provides also key support for the food security and nutrition of women and children as they have traditionally a prominent role (female represent 63.3 % of the agriculture labor force) in the care of livestock, especially as to small ruminants. Moreover, farm animals are important in the provision of milk and meat for domestic consumption and their sales also contribute to support farmers’ livelihoods and other products, such as leather and wool, are valuable commodities. Since the beginning of the crisis, the livestock sector has been significantly affected by reduction in livestock numbers (mostly due to asset depletion); destruction of infrastructure and services (veterinary and roads); contamination and land degradation; livestock theft; markets disrupted; feed became unobtainable or available only at very high prices; insufficient coverage of veterinary services; affecting availability of pasture/fodder; migration and the loss of human resources for production. The resultant effect is significant reduction in livestock numbers as compared to last comprehensive livestock census in 2010. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population examples IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets. Mitigation measures: Establish a community based beneficiaries’ selection and community based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do no harm principles. To the maximum possible extent, all measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for the project. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from host communities will be included as well to limit possible frictions between the two groups, and special attention will be provided to returnees to help them in their re-settlement and resuming their production activities through protecting and rehabilitating their assets.. In addition to this, re stocking and provision of feed can help in sustaining food production (dairy and animal products) in the local community, so more food will be available and accessible to people.
