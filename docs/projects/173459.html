Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) will work closely to engage 1,500 refugee populations in small-scale agriculture activities that will foster self-reliance, dietary diversity, and access to fresh food. Currently, agriculture activities focus mainly on homestead vegetable gardening, with pilots in aquaculture and vertical gardening. SC plans to expand and diversify the agriculture component of self-reliance programme in refugee camps. All the proposed activities will have a training component to ensure participants get the full benefit of the inputs. Training will be implemented following the COVID-19 preparedness measures, to minimize the risk of spread amongst participants. SC will also promote livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities for the host communities in Ukhia and Teknaf, Cox's Bazar, through agricultural inputs, cash for work and skill development training for income generation. Through cash assistance, SC will provide economic support to 3,000 households from host communities, allowing them to purchase productive inputs and assets contributing to sustainable and resilient livelihoods. Empowering the parents and caregivers of the children, the project will strengthen the protective factors at all levels of the child’s environment in order to reinforce the child’s resilience and prevent and respond to threats to the wellbeing of children. The project will improve the sustainable livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities for the host communities in Ukhia and Teknaf, Cox's Bazar, through providing soft skills, vocational skills, job linkages support, entrepreneurship development, assets and cash transfer support. The project will also provide soft skills and awareness on nutrition, health hygiene, disaster reduction, problem solving, decisions making and others relevant topics to Rohingya communities. Accountability or feedback mechanisms, such as, community meetings, FGD/interviews, hotlines, helpdesk, children’s consultation and post distribution monitoring will be in place, and these are accessible to children and children with disabilities to raise their complaints in a safe and dignified manner.

1 - Identified Needs

Overcrowding, poor living conditions and lack of livelihoods opportunities and limited food diversity exacerbate these vulnerabilities as well as protection risks. A lack of livelihoods opportunities leads to an increase in incidents of domestic violence including SGBV against women and children which impacts individual and family mental and physical health and wellbeing. It also decreases access to education for children as they carry out informal labor and/or household chores. It is now the third year of displacement for the Rohingya refugees living in Cox’s Bazar, having fled persecution in Myanmar. While socio-economic impacts on Rohingya refugees are multi-dimensional, COVID-19 also threatens to exacerbate the already dire conditions for the communities. Limited livelihoods options, due to lockdown compounded by limited access to food, lead the vulnerable groups to increased negative coping mechanisms. Supporting most vulnerable groups with appropriate cash assistance is needed to ensure that they can meet minimum essential needs. There needs to be a provision of adding value for better food in camp settings, and enhancing resilience. Through skills development training and incentive-based activities, the refugees can create economic opportunities. In the context of the COVID-19 crisis, preliminary assessments (April 2020) have shown that every family in the camps is vulnerable to food insecurity. Due to COVID-19 related restrictions, these families have lost access to additional sources of income, such as compensation for public works. Already before the COVID-19 crisis, WFP’s REVA III study found that almost half of the refugee populations had a food consumption level below the Minimum Expenditure Basket, even with high levels of humanitarian assistance. Local families need access to more diverse and nutritious food. There needs to be a provision of adding value for better food in addition to existing general food assistance and immediate needs to enhance resilience. Unemployment remains rife throughout the camps, with at 57% of households without at least one household member “employed” in the last 30 days. SC has observed that the majority of this employment is considered “unskilled labour.” To take the construction sector as an example, unskilled work pays on average pays at least 33% less than skilled position. There is an increased need of portable skills development for youth (male and female who are not able to access learning activities) and women as well to generate informal income generation. Construction works (under WFP’s FFA/DRR and SMEP) throughout the camps are currently implemented through the cash-for-work schemes involving both refugees and host communities. Training on basic masonry and carpentry is appeared as one of the needs for the able-bodied male youth to mobilize a much needed skilled workforce. It will also enable the refugees to earn higher incentives, and to acquire durable skills. There are already signs of the desire to pursue economic opportunities. With the more active involvement of the national and international actors in bringing the basic facilities to the camps, the overall economic and social conditions have now improved since the initial influx. Independently, some Rohingya refugees have built shops within the camps, selling groceries, fruits and vegetables. Labour-incentive based activities have now opened opportunities for the refugees to partially meet the basic needs of their households (REVA 2020). The Rohingya refugees have created an enormous impact on the host communities, including market access, labor competition, deforestation, and inflation. Cox’s Bazar is already one of the lowest performing districts in the country in terms of livelihood and other indicators. Host community households are involved in farming, fishing, firewood collection and daily labor activities.

2 - Activities or outputs

(a) Self-reliance: Training on homestead vegetable gardens and distribution of agricultural inputs; specialized training on skill development for Rohingya refugees, mostly women and adolescent girls, to reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms; and incentives for training participants; (b) Livelihoods: Cash assistance to the host communities, mostly to women; assets transfer to the host communities, mostly to women and vulnerable women; support families from host communities for entrepreneurship development/business planning and income generation efforts by emphasizing on female members of households; orientation to build host communities’ awareness on existing government social protection schemes, e.g. safety nets, food for work, cash for work tailoring both genders and different age groups; providing soft skills and awareness on nutrition, health and hygiene, problem solving, decisions making and others relevant topics to Rohingya and host communities to both genders; training for families from host communities on DRR preparedness; skill development/vocational training, job linkages for host communities based on market demand covering all priority sectors
