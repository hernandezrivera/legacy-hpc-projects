Project Summary

The project aims to reduce the impact of explosive hazards through the following interventions:
1. Training of staff, volunteers and community networks: In order to enhance the capacity of mine actions staff in Aleppo and Idleb
, Training will be done on (communication skills, CPMS, GBV core concept, PFA, communication skills and Risk Education).
Staff will be equipped with the right skills through training, coaching and mentoring. And will implement through outreach activities, Awareness raising on mine action: UOSSM will use the harmonized massages developed by the mine action sub cluster
The Mine Action Sub-Cluster messages will be used to disseminate messages on dangers posed by UXOs. The target beneficiaries will be children, parents, caregivers and community leaders in schools, mosques and IDPs in camp. flyers and brochures on risk education will be distributed by outreach team.
knowledge on the community level on risk education by dissemination The Mine Action Sub-Cluster messages on dangers posed by UXOs.
2. Provide victim assistance services for people with disabilities, including survivors of explosive hazard incidents through physical therapy in Afrin:
Afrin is currently considered one of the most important safe areas in the north of Syria, because of its location nearby the Turkish border and under Turkish control in addition to the variety of new provided services there. It consists UOSSM PHC, Child Protection Center and other services.
This project aims to provide victim assistance services for people with disabilities, including survivors of explosive hazard incidents containing (physical rehabilitation)
This center will consist of:
• Physiotherapy Department: It accepts trauma and injured patients for physiotherapy sessions and provide sessions for neurogenic injuries patients. in addition to the mobile team in order to reach the most vulnerable persons in the community and remote areas).

Needs

According to HNO 2019,Increasingly complex and inter-linked protection needs continue to prevail in Syria, resulting from a variety of situations ranging from direct exposure to hostilities,displacement,conditions in sites/collective shelters and protracted displacement. According to interviews in the community with male and female from all categories (adults adolescents and children),they reported that IDP returns are increasingly reported, and they may not always be safe, due to high level of contamination, fully voluntary or dignified representing a durable solution.The impact of explosive hazards on communities is immediate and devastating.While the removal of these items is the only way to eliminate the threat, provision of education on these hazards is crucial to provide civilians with basic safety message to reduce the daily risk.The scale, severity,and complexity of the explosive hazard threat remains a major protection concern across Syria, compounding civilian vulnerability in at-risk areas and endangering or limiting access to humanitarian assistance.The physical threat from explosive hazard contamination cannot be mitigated until comprehensive action is taken to reduce the risks.1 in 2 people in Syria are potentially at risk from the threat of explosive hazards. Based on current population estimations,10.2 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 1,980 communities reporting explosive contamination in the last two years. These communities are spread throughout the country and correlate to areas that have seen most explosive incidents over the past 12 months.The impact of explosive hazards on communities is immediate and devastating, with the loss of community members, an increased number of people with permanent injuries and loss of access to properties, basic services and livelihoods. It also prevents safe delivery of humanitarian aid, affects population movement and limits the scope for recovery and reconstruction efforts.A wide range of infrastructure, such as private property, schools, hospitals, markets, agricultural land, wells and roads may be blocked by explosive hazards, impeding access to education, health services, marketplaces and safe livelihood opportunities.Respondents in the assessed communities reported that agricultural land, roads, and private property were the most contaminated,followed by other public infrastructure, schools, and hospitals.Provision of risk awareness on these hazards is crucial to provide civilians with basic safety messages to reduce the daily risks posed by contamination.The survey of suspected hazardous areas, followed by the marking and clearance of located items is the only way to remove perceived and confirmed threats.Explosive hazard accidents can result in serious injury and death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families.Based on available data from where collection has been possible, on average 1.5 people are killed and 2 are injured in explosive hazards accidents, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation.The number of casualties from explosive accidents has steadily increased the burden on the health sector, in particular,on the provision of specialized services including trauma care, physical rehabilitation,and psycho social support on the short and longer term. In addition to physical and psychological impact, explosive hazard contamination can have severe socio-economic implications on families and communities,depriving them of livelihoods and sources of income, increasing vulnerabilities.While all population groups are vulnerable to the threat of explosive hazards , certain groups are at a higher risk in correlation with gender and age,as well as social roles and responsibilities.Certain identified activities expose particularly children.

Implementation of activities

UOSSM will provide the following activities integrated into CP centers in both Afrin and Idleb: 1- Conduct risk education for at-risk groups:UOSSM will build the capacity of the Protection staff on the following training:- RE training - Communication skills - PFA- CPMS- GBV core concept.After the team will be able to conduct risk education campaigns in the community by visiting camps, mosques, homes and schools in order to reach the beneficiaries from all age groups(adults, adolescent and children under 18 age) both male and female in Urban areas, the team will involve male and female from the local community who have good experience in protection, at the end of each campaign the team will distribute brochures and flyer.2. Provide victim assistance services for people with disabilities, including survivors of explosive hazard incidentsUOSSM will respond to the victims of mines by establish rehabilitation center in Afrin which will consist of:• Nursing Department: It will receive referrals from health, education and other services and register, follow up with them.• Physiotherapy Department: It accepts trauma and injured people for physiotherapy sessions and provide men, women, boys’ and girls’ survivors of explosive hazard incidents the humanitarian assistance. It provides sessions for them in weekly basis, also it will contain mobile clinic which will ensure transportation for beneficiariesUOSSM has rehabilitation center in Sarmada which was established in 2016 and provide rehabilitation, physiotherapy and nursing services. In addition, it receives referrals from Bab Al-Hawa Hospital and other medical facilities in northern Syria, that’s will support the new Afrin center in building capacity, working mechanisms and supervision.UOSSM will provide supervision, coaching sessions and monitoring the activities in regular basis and will receive feedback from beneficiaries to adjust the activities to work with them Also, there is a mechanism to follow complaints mechanism by suggestion box, email and WhatsApp number.

Likelihood: mediumMitigating Measure: Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messaging in risk education. Activity: Provide victim assistance services for people with disabilities, including survivors of explosive hazard incidentsProtection risk: Local population may become reliant on victim assistance and medical capacity provided by humanitarian organisations.Likelihood: lowMitigating Measure: Coordinate with development organisations to ensure the establishment of local medical capacities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks that we may face in the project are:- The threat of poor security situation and in the areas.Assumption: The team will receive training in PFA, staff care.UOSSM will mitigate the risk by daily communication with the security and safety team, following security advisories, recommendations, updating contingency plans, provide PFA to the new IDPs and activate the mobile teams.- The risk of disruption of activities due to change of dominant forces on the groundThe activities of the project will be locally closed at the level of the communities benefiting from the services of the project and inform (local councils, .) about the time of closing the project.Activation of safe evacuation and destruction of documents and postersthe lessons learned and the challenges that have been faced with the implementation of the project will be shared with the concerned managements for future use in designing of similar projects.
