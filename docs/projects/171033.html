Project Summary

The project is based on assessed informational needs among the population at large and specific groups in particular, as well as gaps in communication between communities and responders. There is no national broadcaster covering all of Somalia because the local FM radios are largely centred on urban areas and often have small footprints in technical reach. This leaves many geographical areas uncovered and rural, pastoralist, and farming communities without adequate access to information. In terms of quality of information available, the local media provides a surfeit of political and current affairs news and far less consistent, reliable issue-based programming that informs and amplifies community voices. As well as the pastoralist and farming groups named above, in particular women, youth and children, IDPs, and the disabled, lack coverage and involvement in media content. Radio Ergo’s broadcast strategy is to reach as many people as possible usThe purpose of this project is two-fold: 1. to provide critical information through daily radio broadcasts on lifesaving and life-enhancing issues to Somalis across the country, to enable them to make better informed decisions; and 2. to amplify the voices of local communities, including the most marginalized and excluded, through a trusted feedback platform to enable communities to participate in humanitarian planning and response. 1. The daily radio broadcasts will be produced by Radio Ergo, a dedicated humanitarian radio with an established reputation among listeners and partners for quality and professionalism. Radio Ergo’s mission is to provide relevant humanitarian and public interest information for listeners (men, women, boys and girls) in rural as well as urban areas, and to facilitate a participatory communication cycle between the listeners and the humanitarian community and local/national governmental authorities. The broadcasts contain original programming content based on a number of complementary sources. First, Radio Ergo’s unique network of around 20 locally based Somali reporters gathering interviews, reports, features and information from communities in their areas. The local reportage will reflect needs, views, and developments from different genders, age groups, social backgrounds and livelihoods. Second, practical humanitarian information and advice, aligned with cluster key messaging and advocacy, is incorporated into the radio programming in various formats including interviews with experts, discussions, question and answer segments, dramas, and short advisories. The range of topics covered includes health, COVID19, agriculture, livestock, protection, displacement, etc. A wide range of speakers from governmental and non-governmental circles are included in the output.
2. The Radio Ergo audience feedback platform is the only independent voice-based channel open continually to callers free of charge from across all regions of Somalia. The platform has become a popular way for people living in rural and remote areas to voice their concerns on the key issues affecting their lives, hoping to connect with the wider world including responders. Many callers are pastoralists, farmers, displaced, returnee refugees, among others. Radio Ergo compiles weekly feedback analysis reports to share with the humanitarian clusters and others as information for action. Radio Ergo follows up some calls journalistically with its reporters, and responds to others in radio segments such as Radio Doctor, farming and livestock programmes. Relevant calls are referred to clusters and/or agencies for response. The feedback forms part of collective efforts in Community Engagement and Accountability (CEA). The project aims to harness the power of radio as the most popular means of mass communication in Somalia to save lives, raise awareness and enable informed decision-making; and to ensure the inclusion of voices that would not otherwise be heard in forums for service planning and provision.

Project Activities

The project has four main activity components: 1. Programme production; 2. Broadcasting; 3. Managing Community Feedback platform; 4. Building local partner capacity; 5. Monitoring & Evaluation.Indicative activities include the following:1.Humanitarian Reporting from across all regions of Somalia and Somaliland using Radio Ergo's local Somali journalist network and staff production team; 2. Production of daily radio programming on humanitarian issues including reports, features, interviews, discussions, advisories and messaging,drama, and feedback; 3. Development and production of informative life-saving and life enhancingprogramme segments in collaboration with clusters and other experts, on disaster risk reduction, health, COVID19, nutrition, food security & livelihoods, protection, resilience, etc; 4. Broadcasting of daily one hour programme on shortwave (national coverage)and rebroadcasting by 20 Somali FM radio partners; 5. Production of web stories, Newsletters, social media products for wider reach of humanitarian information; 6. Managing Ergo independent toll-free audience feedback platform, by collecting voice messages from all regions, and preparing and distributing weekly analysis reports; 7. Providing capacity support and training to local Somali FM radio partners and local reporters in the field; 8. Conducting special projects/co-productions with local FM radio partners and civil society groups to produce programming for specific groups e.g. women, children, farmers, IDPs; 9. Conduct localised research studies into aspects of media and audiences to assess impact and inform future programming.

Project Indicators

Output indicators: the following will be measured monthly on an ongoing basis throughout theproject cycle: Number of programmes broadcast nationally on shortwave and via local FM rebroadcasting; Number of female sources included in programming (gender indicator); Number of early warning/advisorymessages/programme segments aired on different humanitarian themes or topics; Listeners feedback gauged through the online platform; Number of local Somali journalists/radios trained; Number of community feedback calls received by gender/region/theme; Number of feedback analysis reports distributed. Outcome indicators: the following will be evaluated at mid-points and at the end of the project using largely qualitative methods such as small online surveys and focus group discussions. - What impact/s has the Radio Ergo programming had on the lives, livelihoods and decision-making processes of different audience groups (gender; age groups, geographical locations, disabled, IDPs, farmers, pastoralists, others). - How has the feedback platform been used by/helpedcommunity users in different categories? - What actions and responses (by communitiesand clusters/other partners) can be attributed to the feedback platform? - To what extenthas the project's capacity building impacted local radio partners and journalists' coverageof humanitarian and public interest issues
