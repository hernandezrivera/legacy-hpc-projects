Project Summary

In South Sudan, food security is still fragile. The main factors affecting food security are food availability, access, utilization, stability, and seasonality which all have significant effects on the dimensions of food security. Unity and Upper Nile States have been severely hit by multiple impediments and constraints, this worsened over the last four years by widespread flooding, recurrent intra state violence, localized dry spells, indirect effects on the supply chain and production, and high food prices, thus exacerbating people’s food security situation and livelihoods. Based on the IPC findings of September 2024, the most food insecure states between December 2024 and March 2024 where more than 50% of their populations are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity are Unity State (64%) and Upper Nile State (50%) and Lakes 40%. The severe food insecurity situation therefore calls for livelihood and food security support through the provision and distribution of Agriculture inputs of (crop seed, vegetable seeds, fishing kits, and agriculture hand tools). The intervention will be supplemented by training surpport to targeted households in agriculture; good agronomic practices, good fishing practices, i.e. seedbed and nursery preparation, best agricultural practices, fishing, and fish postharvest handling, preservation, nutrition, and preservation techniques. One thousand farmers will be involved in reinforcing or constructing dykes in the Flood prone areas and supported with CVA.
The Sudan crisis has caused the displacement of hundreds of thousands of persons from Sudan to South Sudan. The primary cause of movement of people, particularly women and girls, from Sudan to South Sudan is to find Safety/protection. However, in South Sudan, sub-national and localized violence is ever-present; manifesting in clashes between State and non-State armed groups, community-based armed militias, and groups of displaced civilians most especially in Upper Nile, Lakes and Unity States (HNO 2024). In alignment with the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) objectives of the Protection Cluster in South Sudan, HACO Proposes to Ensure that human rights are upheld and promoted for the persons of concern by strengthening coordination, advancing centrality of protection that places the meeting of basic needs such as food as a primary concern. Thus making Protection central to the response, by ensuring interventions safeguard human dignity and address vulnerabilities to identified shocks.
Further still, the Sudan crisis has been compounded by gaps in lifesaving GBV services in South Sudan including clinical management of rape (CMR) and GBV case management and psychosocial support (PSS). Overall, the capacity and coverage of multi-sectoral GBV responses including health, GBV case management and Psycho Social Support, higher-level mental health (MHPSS) and other services remain scarce in areas experiencing elevated levels of returnees in Unity, Upper Nile and Lakes States (UNICEF 2024). This proposal seeks to scale up GBV in emergencies; prevention, response and risk mitigation which is in line with the South Sudan’s HNO 2024 overview to provide safety, inclusion and dignity to Women and girls in all the Humanitarian response programs. The Proposal targets Returnees, Most Vulnerable Host Community and the IDPs in Upper Nile, Unity and Lakes States. Persons with Special needs will be prioritized in the General Protection and GBV response services. In Food security and Livelihoods, Families hosting Persons with special needs will be targeted for the Livelihoods response activities. HACO will report all project activities using the 5Ws platform and report all finance sources and amounts for the project activites on FTS.

The outbreak of fighting in Sudan on 15 April 2023 has caused an influx of people fleeing the country to the crisis affected South Sudan. More than 500,000 individuals have fled from Sudan into South Sudan. Nine out of ten of those arriving are South Sudanese; of those fleeing, 50 % of the new arrivals are women and girls, nearly half of the Women and girls fleeing are below the age of 18 (UNHCR 2024). The primary cause of movement of people, particularly women and girls, from Sudan to South Sudan is to find Safety/protection.
In South Sudan, sub-national and localized violence is ever-present; manifesting in clashes between State and non-State armed groups, community-based armed militias, and groups of displaced civilians (HNO 2024). Without political stability and peace, communities will continue to be affected by violence leading to multiple displacements. Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO), therefore, proposes to carry out protection monitoring and reporting, engaging communities to reconcile and building inter-communal synergies for peaceful resolution of conflict. Identification and assistance of women, men, girls, and boys with protection concerns during static and IPMT, general protection awareness-raising, giving protective cash-based assistance in connection with safe, dignified, and voluntary return of persons of concern, Conducting training on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation, and peace-building, carrying out outreaches to people of concern through peace-building awareness-raising activities, individual, group, and specialized PSS. Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO) intends to reach 10,000 individuals in Lakes, Upper Nile and Unity States.
Although the primary cause of movement of people, particularly women and girls, from Sudan to South Sudan is to find Safety/protection. However, the Women and girls fleeing conflict in Sudan continue to face multiple risks of GBV including rape, sexual exploitation and abuse, intimate partner violence and physical violence when they arrive in South Sudan. Adolescent girls are even more vulnerable with child marriage increasing in many locations across South Sudan, more so in Unity State. Incidents of conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) have been documented in the Sudan crisis and the impacts of this are compounded by gaps in lifesaving GBV services in South Sudan including clinical management of rape (CMR) and GBV case management and psychosocial support (PSS). Overall, the capacity and coverage of multi-sectoral GBV responses including health, GBV case management and Psycho Social Support, higher-level mental health (MHPSS) and other services remain scarce in areas experiencing elevated levels of returnees in Unity, Upper Nile and Lakes States (UNICEF 2024). This proposal seeks to scale up GBV in emergencies; prevention, response and risk mitigation which is in line with the South Sudan’s HNO 2024 overview to provide safety, inclusion and dignity to Women and girls in all the Humanitarian response programs.
Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO) is committed to scaling up Gender Based Violence in Emergencies (GBViE); response, prevention, and risk mitigation programming to address the new and emerging needs arising in Unity, Upper Nile and Lakes States from the Sudan crisis. HACO proposes to intervene to ensure that women and girls are protected and GBV survivors have improved access to lifesaving GBV services and through referral – GBV survivors get health and Mental Health and Psycho social surpport (MHPSS) and overall Women and girls face reduced GBV risks in their communities. The response will be premised on services for survivors of GBV at women and girl friendly spaces (WGFS) and through mobile teams with referrals for health and other services, risk mitigation across other programmatic sectors, and prevention activities targeting gender inequality and other harmful social norms that underpin GBV. This will encompass the provision of GBViE response interventions to improve help-seeking, strengthen referral pathways and ensure quality case management for GBV survivors, particularly displaced Persons on the move from Sudan at the transit sites and return locations in Unity, Upper Nile and Lakes States. Additionally, HACO will carry out Safety audits in camp, community setting or focused on a specific facility, advising other sectors on how to most effectively reach marginalized sub-groups within the community and carrying out Consultations with women and girls focused on their safety and access concerns related to programming in other sectors. HACO will also Provide those working in other sectors with up-to-date information about where GBV survivors can seek support and Conducting trainings for other sectors on GBV risk mitigation and how to safely and appropriately respond to survivor disclosures. HACO Program staff will through community dialogues and engagement challenge existing social norms that are the root cause of violence against women and girls, as well as a barrier to service seeking behavior.

2. Project activities

In order to reduce the food security crisis, Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO) proposes to enhance food production and carry out support training to 5,000 farmers. The livelihoods Project activities are meant to improve crop, vegetable production, livestock yields in order to reduce the food insecurity outlook in Renk and Malakal Counties in Upper Nile; Panyijiar and Rubkona Counties in Unity state and Cuiebet in Lakes State. The project activities will be the distribution of high quality Agricultural tools and inputs to 10,000 Households (57,000 individuals); vegetable and crop seeds, fishing kits, Agricultural tools/Implements, Vaccination of 100,000 livestock in order to improve the yield of the farmers. Other activities will be training of 2,500 farmers in best agronomic practices, best fishing, fish handling, and fish preservation and Training farmers in climatic change shocks and adaptation in order to help famers cope with climatic change to increase their resilience to; crops and livestock diseases, dry spells, floods and displacements. One thousand farmers will be involved in reinforcing or constructing dykes in Flood prone areas and supported with CVA.
