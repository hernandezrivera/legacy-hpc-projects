Project Summary

Unified Culture Promotion Foundation (UNICPF) proposes to strengthen the preventive and curativeNutrition status of under 5 children, pregnant, and breastfeeding mothers across the BAY states, with a focus on the project participant context. UNICPF will achieve this through both preventive and responsive program while prioritizing the integration of essential components such as organic growing of gardens, awareness raising on nutrition best practices, MIYCN , treatment of MAM with local nutritious supplement (Tom brown), treatment of SAM with RUTF and referral. UNICPF will integrate anthropology into nutrition programming which will enhance nutritional status of communities while also ensuring sustainability and households economic strengthening.

1 - Needs

According to IPC The 2024 IPC Acute Malnutrition Analysis projects a worsening nutrition situation across the BAY states indicating that the severity of acute malnutrition and number of children, pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls (PBWG) in need of nutrition assistance is expected to remain high through 2025. This is aggravated by increasingly prolonged lean seasons, worsening acute food insecurity, economic crisis, high prevalence of measles and acute watery diarrhoea (AWD), outbreak of diphtheria and flooding. During the lean season, the impact of all the deteriorated drivers of acute malnutrition will be at their peak and the nutrition situation in most of the LGAs will likely be worse than same period of 2024. 2024 IPC Analysis as critical (IPC phase 4) is anticipated in 17 LGAs including Mobbar and Damboa LGAs in Borno StateBased on the North-East Nigeria Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance conducted in August 2024, the global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates in Borno have seen an increase from10.2% in 2023 to 15.7% in 2024, while Yobe had also experienced an increase from 8.0% in 2023 to 11.9% in 2024. In Adamawa, the GAM rates have increased from 4.0% to 6.6% in 2024. These high prevalence of GAM signify an extremely stressed populationgrappling with food insecurity, limited water and sanitationaccess, and suboptimal health conditions as the primary drivers of acute malnutrition. In 2025, it is estimated that over 2.5 million children under five and over 309,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will suffer from acute malnutrition. This proposed project will be addressing some of the above gaps

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NICPF will train all project staff and volunteers in the community on protection mainstreaming, safeguarding, code of conduct, PSEA. UNICPF will ensure that a clear code of conduct on safeguarding and PSEA are signed by all staff and any persons who will be involved in the project. Moreso, there will be mandatory training on PSEAH for all staff and personnel including vendors, volunteers and contractors in the project. UNICPF as report mechanisms in place (Email: complaints@unicpf.org and hotline: 08062941842 ) which will be made available in all our project locations. UNICPF will also liase with interagency PSEA network for SEA issues that is beyond UNICPF. Furthermore, UNICPF will create awareness sessions on PSEA within the communities in local languages and infographics showing all the reporting mechanisms.Recruitment will be gender responsive where female and persons with disability will be prioritize for the job as 10 additional marks will given to them. All report, MoVs will be disaggregated by women, men boys, girls and persons with disability. 100% of caseworkers will be females.Furthermore, UNICPF will integrate GBV into nutrition program by training staff and volunteers on GBV and referral of survivors to GBV partners and other essential services survivors might need
