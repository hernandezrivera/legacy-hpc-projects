Project Summary

An estimated 4.77 million women, girls, men and boys will face protection risks and violations in 2021. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers in South Sudan are in need of protection services. Insecurity and violence have disproportionately affected vulnerable people including children, older persons, survivors of gender-based violence and conflict-related sexual violence, and people living with disabilities. Coupled with natural disasters such as flooding, which destroyed number of crops across the country and very limited capacity of the government to respond, this has contributed to a combination of protracted and cyclical IDP situations with ever-shifting patterns.
Despite recent positive developments on the implementation of the Peace Agreement, violence and insecurity remain a persistent reality. Disaggregated data analysis of armed incidents indicate that violence continues to affect civilians across the country. Sub-national organized violence has increased during the first six months of 2020 as compared to the same period in 2019, most notably in Lakes, Warrap and greater Jonglei region. In 23 out of 78 counties, over 60 per cent of households surveyed reported protection concerns in their area in the past three months (HNO 2021). In 15 counties, more than 60 civilians per 100,000 population were killed or injured by violence or other catastrophes in 2020. Population Movement Baseline (PMB) Report, December 2020.
This project will target 20,000 persons of concern (9,100 women, 6,600 men, 3,100 girls, 12,00 boys: including 15% PWDs) with integrated protection services such as protection assessment and monitoring; Protection by presence and protective accompaniment; Comprehensive case management inform of IPA with cash-based component; and Safe referrals to other specialized services including information dissemination on available services; Psychosocial support programming; Legal assistance and counselling for survivors integrated into case management. To maximize protection outcomes, CINA will undertake Protection awareness raising and risk prevention messaging; Community-based protection preparedness and risk mitigation.
CINA will use its current static presence in the priority locations while deploying mobile and outreach teams to deep fields. This mobile team will carry out interventions based on the needs assessment from concerned agencies received. The intervention will be weekly for 4 weeks and move to the new area. General protection will be integrated by enhancing inclusive access to critical cross-sectoral services, community-based protection and resilience initiatives, timely protection monitoring and assessment.

1 - Needs

Protection assessments in the hard-to-reach locations conducted by REACH in November 2020 and MUAC – screening at PRM sites January 2021 revealed that Bor South, Pibor, Duk, Yirol East and Twic East are among the top five counties where insecurity impedes people’s access to protection, health and education services. Similarly, these counties are facing acute food insecurity (IPC 5,4,3) moving into 2021 (HNO January 2021). South Sudan humanitarian situation snapshot OCHA, December 2021 indicated that a severe flooding hit Pibor, Duk, Twic East, Bor South, Yirol East and Awerial among other locations in South Sudan, affecting drinking water sources, impeding access to schools and health facilities, destroying shelters and limiting humanitarian ability to respond across the affected areas. Competition over already scarce resources has caused tension between displaced persons and host communities. Maintaining harmonious co-existence between the two groups is a necessity. Children constitute 61 per cent of the IDPs and returnees population and they continue to face protection risks including neglect, abuse, exploitation, child labour, forced recruitment, SGBV, and lack of access to services. Protection concerns are especially high for girls at risks for early marriage, early pregnancies, and denial of education. Other persons of concern affected by the crises include the older persons, widows, persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating mothers, persons with HLP issues, child mothers, etc.). Due to lack of solutions, caused by variety of factors, including physical safety and security concerns, non-availability of basic services and a conducive environment for return, the IDPs largely remain staying in IDP and refugee camps. The COVID-19 pandemic in South Sudan and its measures resulted into significant restriction of movements limiting humanitarian access to the most affected people. The COVID-19 measures taken raised the human rights and protection concerns for the most affected persons (South Sudan HNO 2021). While there is some progress in formation of unity government, there is still unpredictability concerning the return of IDPs and refugees. Moreover, populations displacement is expected to increase in areas already affected by protracted crises and protection needs will be heightened. When conflicts and crises ensue, the most vulnerable people including children, the persons with disabilities, elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers, etc. who are usually left behind during crises where they suffer greater risks and abuses. The persons with specific needs usually lack access to information, livelihood opportunities, food, medical care, shelter and NFIs and psychosocial support. Tensions over HLP issues and other community resources compound existing forms of intercommunal violence. GBV against women and girls become rampant. The child survivors of violence (including unaccompanied and separated children and other vulnerable children) usually lack appropriate responsive services to survive and preventative and resilience services to cope. This protection environment coupled with decline in community protection support systems and structures and existing protection caseload from ongoing protection response calls for immediate and unhindered protection intervention to provide critical protection services to affected persons and at-risk populations.

2 - Project activities

The proposed interventions in this project will include life-saving protection services through: 1. Provision of mobile protection assessment and monitoring targeting 10,000 people as well as ensuring protection by presence and protective accompaniment; 2. Provision of Comprehensive case management and safe referrals to 800 PSNs and their caregivers inform of individual protection assistance with cash support to 200 most vulnerable cases of PSNs. The Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) will follow existing CINA’s approaches including using Protection Cluster vulnerability criteria, (including cases of: SGBV, murder, disappearance/family separation, forced recruitment, underage recruitment, unlawful detention, land disputes and documentation issues, and cases of extreme vulnerability related to illness, poverty, number of dependents, etc.) will be prioritized for IPA case management services (CASH BASED INTERVENTION AND NFIs). IPA case package will include NFIs and cash assistance (for some selected cases); Safe referral pathways will be developed during protection monitoring and assessments and will be maintained. Beneficiaries will be provided with protection information and referred appropriately for services especially targeting survivors of violence including CMR services for SGBV survivors, legal aid for unlawful detention cases, referral for food assistance for those in extreme need, NFIs for the elderly and persons with disabilities. 3. Provision of safe referrals services including information dissemination on available services to 2,000 people; 4. Provision of psychosocial support programming to 1,000 vulnerable people including individualized and group PSS; 5. Provision of legal assistance and counselling for 200 survivors integrated into case management; 6. Undertake protection awareness raising and prevention messaging for 20,000 people; 7. Conduct community-based protection preparedness and risk mitigation; 8. Conduct Peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence activities, with possible Cash Assistance for durable solutions programming, especially targeting youth and returnees to 500 people.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

CINA Cash transfer programming inform of unrestricted and unconditional grants has been increasingly popular modalities for responses; however, the use been limited only to Protection and Livelihood but had not expanded as rapidly as it could have within the sectors. This project therefore seeks to ensure how market functionalities will be incorporated into design and coordinated with Cash Working Group, to increase the use of cash in protection. This will be supported by improved market analysis, an increased focus on income and impact measurement, absence of evidence of harming the market, functional and healthy market, increased availability of products in the market, increased for quality products in the market, resilience of households, Gender, protection and inclusion of vulnerable people, shifts in income and gender perception, decreased disease rate, quality occupancy of housing and increased labour capacity in construction.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CINA Cash assistance will strive to provide the most appropriate, effective and efficient responses tothe needs of people affected by crisis. It will be provided in many ways depending on needs and context. The choice of appropriate transfers (in-kind, cash or vouchers), or their combination, must be based on a thorough assessment and context-specific analysis, including cost effectiveness and efficiency, secondary market impacts, the flexibility of the transfer, local availability of goods and services, prioritization and targeting, gender and protection considerations, and risks of insecurity and corruption. CINA in the past has been delivering Cash Based assistance to PSNs to address their food needs, NFI needs such as soap and medicine, whereas now, it is increasingly providing cash to beneficiaries, often in combination with cash and vouchers in order to meet this fundamental change in the way assistance was delivered. Therefore, Cash-based intervention has the potential to respond quickly and efficiently to a variety of urgent needs offering dignity, flexibility and choice to beneficiaries and supporting local markets growth for long term development. Assistance covers both cash and vouchers that are provided to individuals, households or communities as emergency relief and which contribute to meeting their basic needs or to assisting them in protecting or re-establishing their livelihoods. Cash-based assistance, particularly when delivered in the form of unconditional cash is a people-centered approach.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CINA staff and volunteers will be trained to ensure adherence to IASC PSEA guidelines throughout the project cycle. Each will sign a Code of Conduct. All CINA staffs are subjected to enhanced reference checks up on taking job offer. A commitment statement on PSEA is found on CINA’s job adverts. CINA will ensure that staff are trained and uphold humanitarian principles including, of neutrality, impartiality and participation, and where feasible, CINA team will remain diverse as much as possible to achieve PSEA objective. CINA will ensure project beneficiaries are sensitized about the PSEA, and their role to identify and report it appropriately. CINA will display PSEA messages on the banner during awareness raising activities. A PSEA reporting form will be kept at Community centers, clubs and beneficiaries wellbe sensitized about it. They will use this to report cases directly to PSEA focal person at CINA's head Office. A hotline number and email address will also be displayed on the PSEA banner which beneficiaries can use to call and report PSEA cases in the community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will target 15% People with disabilities, women, men and caregivers. Their views and preferences will be used to realign provision of IPA case management support in terms of material support such as wheelchairs, eyeglasses, as well as incorporation of awareness messages such as non-discrimination, gender equality, equal play and learning opportunities for all, rights to women to education, ending forced marriages, etc. into protection risks messaging.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Beneficiary Complaint mechanisms will be established at the community level and feedback will be collected using a form, complaint box or hotline number. Banners on SEA awareness and reporting will also be displayed at the community sites. In particular, Clubs, distribution centers, community courts, CP desks in the schools will be used to receive feedback from community members. During the project inception at the community, beneficiaries will be informed about the project objectives, targets, activities, and their participation in the project. Community-based protection networks will be formed/strengthened to convey any project information to the beneficiaries during the implementation. Activities such as awareness raising, caregivers sessions, and PSS activities in CFS will be used to pass important information about the activities of the project. CINA will strengthen its existing community beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms through display of complaints box at CFS, schools, Helpdesks, and during awareness sessions. CINA will also use feedback forms during M&E activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiaries' feedback about services rendered by CINA in their location will be synthesized by CINA management and used during the project planning or implementation to adjust next phase of response.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

1. Conduct services mapping and establish/strengthen effective referral pathways to ensure the most vulnerable persons of concern are specifically targeted to directly and unconditionally get equal and unhindered access to essential services availability in the community. 2. Involve relevant stakeholders such as RRC, Payam Administrators, etc. (in staff recruitment, needs assessments, sites selection) and community members representing all groups in the community (clans, sections, IDPs,returnees, host community, chiefs, women, men, youth, persons with disability) in beneficiaries targeting and selection to promote social cohesion and avoid conflict among groups. 3. Work with local land authorities to apply both local and national laws governing land tenure and advocate with them to protect land rights of community members. 4. Heighten awareness raising to staff and project beneficiaries on dangers of explosive hazards before and during humanitarian activities. CINA will consult Women and girls and include their views into the CBI component, especially the GBV survivors, the pregnant and lactating women, elderly and child-mothers. This is for CINA to make decision about type of IPA services relevant to them and how they will use cash to prevent further occurrence of risks. Youth as well will be consulted and involved as stakeholders for community-based protection initiatives. They will be guided to design local protection initiatives and implement them. CINA will use its existing community-based complaint mechanisms using the complaint box, banners, and hotline. The CBM focal point (Project Manager) collects feedback on monthly basis and synthesizes it and get back to respective beneficiaries through leaders of CB protection networks. Safe referral pathways will be developed by CINA and maintained in the project locations and beneficiaries will be provided with information dissemination about availability of services. Information will be provided by community mobilisers in local languages, with simple and precise messages. Banners will also be displayed at community points (distribution sites, schools, churches, child friendly spaces) about availability of services and who to contact in case of emergency.
