Project Summary

Through the proposed humanitarian responses, International Medical Corps (IMC UK) will provide integrated lifesaving nutrition service to conflict affected and displaced communities in Juba IDP Camps, Panyijar, Malakal, Fashoda, Panyikang, Manyo and Canal Pigi Counties. With the aim to reduce suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable populations, IMC will increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition, which are essential for addressing key drivers of acute malnutrition affecting children and women in the aforesaid project locations. IMC UK will also continue to address the prevention of malnutrition in children under two years of age through promotion of appropriate Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition Feeding (MIYCN) practices. The service will be delivered through static and outreach sites for remote and hard to reach areas in the targeted project locations. The proposed response will include all components of Community Management of Acute Malnutrition, including Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP), Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP), Stabilization Center (SC) and community mobilization activities. International Medical Corps UK proposes to operate 38 OTPs, 38 Targeted supplementary feeding programs (TSFPs), and 5 SCs across the project sites. IMC UK will work with the Ministry of Health (MoH), UNICEF and WFP to secure timely provision of nutrition supplies, which are necessary for nutrition service delivery. IMC will reach approximately 180,712 individuals, of which 72,285 are IDPs. All project activities will be implemented in line with adapted guidelines for nutrition services delivery in the context of COVID-19 provided by the Nutrition Cluster.
IMC UK will monitor the nutrition status of the population and the nutrition response through routine nutrition program data, quarterly MUAC screening campaigns and population-based SMART Surveys. IMC UK, with its already established Nutrition Surveillance Team, will undertake nutrition assessments in its operational locations and support to undertake in nutrition assessments in Priority counties as well to provide robust information on the nutrition situation of a given population, this information will be key in responding effectively to nutrition needs in the project area.
This project aims to enhance nutrition-sensitive interventions that will ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services; to further decrease the prevalence of acute malnutrition, and ultimately, to contribute to the prevention and reduction of morbidity and mortality associated to malnutrition in South Sudan. Additionally, IMC UK will reinforce and promote access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH, protection and FSL services provision in the project target areas through geographical integration, activity integration, and integration through common approaches to reduce the burden of malnutrition and improve wholistic impact. IMC will also closely work with Nutrition partners in the target location to prevent overlap of the services and enhance services availability through referral and integration. IMC will also support hygiene interventions to improve nutrition outcomes and reduce risks of hygiene-related morbidities. Nutrition services will be considered as potential entry points for GBV survivors seeking assistance, with nutrition staff trained on GBV referral pathways and other key GBV concepts. The project protection focal points will perform safety audits and assessments of nutrition sites. Safety audits are one of the key approaches used to identify potential GBV related risks at the program sites using a tested tool. The project will conduct safety audit assessments bi-annually at the health and nutrition sites.

1 - Needs

South Sudan (SS) continues to face overlapping crises including floods, conflict, and food insecurity – all contributing to persisting high levels of malnutrition. High food insecurity, and lack of hygiene, health, and environmental sanitation services, along with inadequate maternal and childcare in SS continue to drive malnutrition levels higher and impact negatively on children and women’s health and well-being. According to 2022 IPC analysis, an estimated 6.6 million people (53% of the population) face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 2.2 million people face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and 61000 in catastrophic (phase 5). These figures are projected to increase in 2023 and about 7.76 million people (62.7% of the population) are likely to face acute food insecurity during the April-July 2023 lean season, while 1.4 million children will be malnourished. This includes a projected 2.9 million people on the brink of starvation (IPC Phase 4) and another 43,000 people who are expected to be living in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. Malnutrition remains a persistent problem that continues to adversely affect the well-being of women and children. Preliminary results from Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) Round 28 and the 2022 SMART surveys show that the proportion of children 6-59 months suffering from acute malnutrition remains above the emergency threshold of 15% (very high acute malnutrition level- WHO classification) at 16.0% (15.4 -16.7) in lean season. This indicates no improvement since 2019, when the GAM prevalence was 16.2% (13.8-18.5) in lean season. FSNMS Round 28 and the 2022 SMART survey results estimated that a total of 2,175,546 people (1,469,919 in the host community, 315,503 returnees, and 390,125 internally displaced people -IDPs), will suffer from acute malnutrition (AM) in South Sudan in 2023. These include a total of 1,437,735 million children under five years (733,245 girls, 704,490 boys) of which 353,606 will have SAM (180,339 girls and 173,267 boys), 1,084,129 (552,906 girls and 531,223 boys) will have MAM, and 737,812 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will have acute malnutrition (AM-PLW). More children and pregnant and lactating women are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2023 compared to 2022 (increased by 158, 201). A total of 31 counties in four states (Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, and Upper Nile) have Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15% (very high acute malnutrition level- WHO classification). From the 2022 IPC for Acute Malnutrition analysis (IPC- AM), 15 counties are classified in phase 3(Serious) and 30 classifieds in phase 4 (critical). Projection for 2023 lean season indicates a worsening situation with a total of 59 counties projected to be in phase 4. This represents the highest number classified in IPC phase 4 for the past 3 similar seasons. Additionally, food insecurity remains high across the same counties and states affected by high malnutrition rates (classified using the IPC for acute food insecurity – IPC -AFI Phase 4). Specifically, the GAM rate in targeted locations continue to be above WHO emergency threshold of >15%. In Panyjar, Pigi, Juba IDP, Malakal, Fashoda, Manyo and Panyikang counties continue to face high GAM prevalence which is a worrying situation. According to IMC SMART survey conducted in 2022 and FSNMS result, the GAM rate of the targeted counties reported to be 24.7%, 18.2% 19.0% 21%, 15.2%, 21% and 20% respectively. Urgent actions and investment are required to curb the impact of aggravating factors, and to safeguard the gains made and prevent any deterioration of the nutrition situation in targeted counties. Hence, immediate response is needed – both curative & preventive – to avert a possible further catastrophe and spike to IPC Phase 5 (famine).

2 - Project activities

IMC UK will continue to provide nutrition services in Malakal, Juba IDP, Panyjar and Pigi and scale up the activities in three new counties of Fashoda, Panyikang and Manyo; implement community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) services at 38 OTP/TSFP sites and 5 SC sites each serving a catchment area with several villages. Activities include.Prevention of MIYCN services. IMC will continue to support prevention of malnutrition through social and behavioral change (SBC) approach including promoting and supporting optimal MIYCN focusing on women of reproductive age, PLW, and their children in the first two years of life (the 1,000-day window of opportunity) at the community level, as well as at health and nutrition sites. Appropriate MIYCN counseling/SBCC materials developed by UNICEF will be printed and used during the counselling sessions. IMC will promote appropriate MIYCN key messages and good hygiene practices with the use of appropriate pictorial IEC materials, provide individual and group counselling on MIYCN for pregnant and mothers/caregivers of children 0-<23 months at nutrition sites and provide skills and knowledge to caregivers/mothers on nutritious food preparation through cooking demonstration sessions, with an emphasis on appropriate complementary feeding. Nutrition staff will be trained on optimal MIYCN practices to support mothers and caregivers on conducting sessions on optimal MIYCN practices with other MSG/FSG members. IMC will also support the provision of Vitamin A and deworming to children aged 6-59 during the National Immunization Days (NIDs). IMC will prioritize lifesaving treatment services targeting children under five years of age and PLW in targeted counties. The nutrition interventions under this strategy will include community mobilization to increase access and coverage of CMAM services, early detection and referral of malnourished beneficiaries, treatment of children aged 6-59 months and PLW affected by acute malnutrition through the management of SAM and MAM, and support CNVs and family MUAC to undertake home visits for case finding. CNVs and Family MUAC, identify acutely malnourished CU5 and PLW at the community and refer to the static nutrition sites or mobile clinics for appropriate treatment and management. Family MUAC approach will be established and strengthened through MtMSGs to ensure improved coverage of beneficiaries. 76,264 children and PLW with SAM and MAM with acute malnutrition will be treated in targeted counties. All children 6-59 month with SAM without medical complications will be treated in the established OTP sites while children 0-59month identified with medical complications will be treated at SC sites. Those children aged 6-59 month and PLW identified with MAM will be treated in the TSFP. Treatment will be provided in alignment with national CMAM guidelines and related SPHERE standards will be used to assess the performance indicators of the intervention (rates of cure, default, non-response, and death). IMC will ensure that all frontline nutrition staff and CHD staff will be provided with refresher training on CMAM and MIYCN using South Sudan CMAM guideline with the aim to improve their knowledge and skills. IMC will continuously monitor the nutrition status of the population and the nutrition response through the routine collection and analysis of nutrition program data, mass mid upper arm circumference (MUAC) screening and periodic SMART surveys in targeted counties. To ensure complementarity of interventions and wholistic impact, IMC will adopt a multi-sectoral approach in services delivery in all project locations. Enhanced multi-sectoral programming will be conducted through the establishment kitchen gardens at the nutrition sites, distribution of WASH Kits for caregivers with SAM children, and conduct of Safety Audits to improve the effectiveness of nutrition interventions in targeted counties.

During project implementation, the project will use different channels, such as AAP monthly meetings, suggestion boxes, hotlines, house-to-house visits, etc., to inform affected communities on their rights and entitlements, and mechanisms available to them to share feedback and complaints. 7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IMC will ensure accountability to affected populations during implementation of this project. Mechanisms such as community feedback and complaints handling systems will be in place to ensure accountability to the affected population. IMC nutrition response is designed to work closely with the communities from project inception through implementation and monitoring of results. The project is going to provide beneficiaries with information about the assistance to be provided. IMC will ensure active consultations with the beneficiaries or affected populations during onsite monitoring, which allows generation of feedback from the beneficiaries. At the onset of the project, IMC will conduct community consultations and sensitizations on the types of nutrition intervention needed to implement, which takes into consideration gender and age group needs. During the nutrition treatment days, mothers will receive explanation about the program, its importance, the distribution process of supplies, and the importance of using therapeutic supplies for its sole purpose. Beneficiary feedback will be received through already existing Quality Accountability to the Affected Population Committees (QAAPC) in all the project sites. Similarly, IMC will conduct beneficiary feedback assessments at the household level that will gauge how beneficiary feel about the project, and their overall satisfaction on the services they received from IMC. IMC will also involve the County Health Department in field visits, giving them chance to meet with beneficiaries and directly get feedback regarding the program. IMC will also organize meeting with Payam leaders on a quarterly basis to inform them on program achievements and challenges and seek support in community mobilization and awareness raising on nutrition-related matters. Additionally, IMC utilizes community-based feedback and response mechanisms (CBFRM) to solicit feedback and receive complaints. IMC’s CBFRM procedures provides guidance on: (1) effective community consultations – building upon community preferences on how to raise concerns and provide feedback; (2) major steps on managing feedback and complaints and how to explain our actions and decisions to the communities (including vulnerable and marginalized people); (3) Clear informed consent - IMC will provide all necessary information on how to manage feedback and complaint process, explain people’s rights and entitlements, as well as key steps that IMC will take to keep confidentiality of sensitive complaints, and (4) Routing and management of sensitive complaints, including SEA and safeguarding, including, protection measures and investigation pathways. CBFRM is aligned closely with the organization’s broader safeguarding and accountability policies; together, these procedures help ensure the systematic use and management of the feedback and complaint handling process across all field locations and programs. Building on the existing feedback and complaints mechanism, IMC will ultimately promote community participation on management and use of the feedback and complaints through community learning meetings, community Help desks, Post distribution Monitoring, AAP meetings and field registers as a safe and user friendly channels to collect, process and respond to the complaints according to IMC and community’ preferences, as well as disseminate information about the project.During program implementation, IMC will continue to enhance these already existing community accountability structures in the interventions guided by IMC South Sudan CBFRM country local procedures. IMC will share relevant program information including information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure meaningful participation. Regular information on the program performance and monitoring results, staff code of conduct and PSEA reporting lines staff will also be shared. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IMC in South Sudan has developed country-specific guidance to protect beneficiaries from exploitation and abuse in humanitarian crisis. This Guidance complements IMC’s global safeguarding policies. All IMC employees have to undergo a training on PSEA and sign a pre-employment commitment against sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as report any suspected cases. IMC will conduct regular awareness raising sessions with the community on PSEA through QAAPCs CNVs, CHWs and other community structures. The project team will incorporate PSEA messages into CBFRM activities, especially during monthly AAP meetings with the community. The project team will also ensure posting/distribution of IEC materials at strategic locations in the community, including how to report PSEA cases. Regular sessions at the health facilities and other services point will be done on a monthly basis. Community focal points will be empowered through trainings to be able to pass on key PSEA messages, jointly with IMC project teams. For sensitive cases that arise, communities will be sensitized to report to the field site manager/designated focal point. The field site manager is responsible to log in the complaints into IMC reporting system, Ethics point, for further investigation. Communities will be sensitized to report any sensitive cases directly to Ethics point reporting systems for investigation. Ethics point contacts will be shared during community sensitizations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Through this response, IMC will engage the affected population to participate fully in the project design and implementation to ensure communities have clarity about the project, and have their say on the project’s objectives, activities, location, and their entitlements. Feedback and Complaints arising from the communities will be processed, analyzed, and discussed during program management meetings. The meetings will develop responses and action plans to address the complaints and feedback raised. These responses will be provided through the AAP committees in each project location. In addition, the information will be keyed into a complaints and feedback response database for analysis and learning. During monthly/quarterly program review meetings, the information from the database will form part of the meeting reflections and will inform necessary programmatic corrections.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

• IMC will maintain alertness on the security situation in all its health facilities and project locations, in close coordination with the camp/community leaders, local and national authorities, and UN agencies. IMC will also maintain its 24/7 security guards across all its supported health facilities, which also help in assessing the daily security situation together with IMC Site Managers and Security Director. Particular attention will be given to children (boys and girls), women, and men with disabilities and old caregivers (both men and women) in service provision to reduce further suffering, as well in case they have other complications that need specialized care. Full access and preventive activities will also integrate persons with disabilities, elderly and youth. Special adjustments are being done by IMC to accommodate the special needs of PWDs and elderly, such as doing house-to-house visits/consultations to them; ensuring that health facilities are accessible to patients with movement constraints and physical disabilities, supporting rehabilitation where necessary such as ramps and grab bars, among others.• IMC will mitigate GBV risks by scaling up of safety audits at the nutrition centers to identify and address women and children’s protection/safety needs and ensure that nutrition services and centers are not avenues for GBV. IMC ensures that multi-sectoral referral pathways are in place in all its project locations, especially for survivors of protection incidents. Monthly safety audits are being conducted, which results are shared to and discussed with relevant national and local authorities and other NGOs to draw timely, actionable solutions, and provide targeted support to the survivors.• To avoid abnormal population movement for the purpose of getting assistance (humanitarian assistance as a pull factor), IMC will bring the services closer to the beneficiaries, such as setting clear criteria for selecting sites and at the same time increasing number of sites to increase coverage and access. The service provision to the beneficiaries will be on a weekly basis to reduce frequency of beneficiaries’ movement. Treatment will be conducted in health and nutrition facilities, free from military or armed personnel, away from military bases. In addition, the treatment will start in the morning hours and close before dark to allow mothers reach home safely. If allocation has many beneficiaries, then more days will be allocated to serve each section or block to allow enough time to reach and leave the site. • IMC will further strengthen existing and/or establish Quality Accountability of the affected population (QAAP) committees and other CBFRM modalities, which were selected by the community, to ensure that everyone has equitable and easy access to context-specific complaint and feedback mechanisms.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

IMC incorporates protection principles in its all its programs and its daily work by prioritizing the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries served. Consultations and other activities will take place in secure areas that are also easily accessible for groups who are extremely vulnerable and/or with limited mobility. IMC healthcare and nutrition workers will be trained with a focus on gender-based violence prevention and survivor-centered treatment core concepts (clinical management of rape), considering the challenging environment that poses high barriers to vulnerable people, especially women and girls, in terms of reporting GBV cases, as well to counter the general lack of information regarding this topic. IMC’s multi-sectoral health, nutrition and protection project will integrate GBV medical and MHPSS services to survivors, including counselling, management and proper referral for survivors of GBV, including rape survivors. All staff will be trained on protection, GBV Core Concepts and safe referrals. Service mapping and medical referral protocols are in place across IMC’s health facilities and project locations to ensure meaningful and safe referrals, including ensuring patient information shared with third-party service providers is only done so after informed consent of the patient is secured. Furthermore, IMC provides integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for both in- and out-patients in all its supported health facilities. Other PSS interventions that IMC provides include psychoeducation, support groups and peer support sessions for the most vulnerable groups, including community leaders, women, children, GBV survivors and PWDs of the displaced and conflict-affected population in the target sites. These are conducted through small group discussions, tea sessions and community outreaches with the aim of bringing vulnerable individuals and group of people together, create social opportunities for their families and care givers of persons with psychosocial disabilities, and the wider community structures, which are tailored to ensure promotion of psychosocial wellbeing is achieved and the human rights of socially disadvantaged vulnerable persons are promoted. In addition, IMC will implement/pilot Self-help -plus (SH+) in all its project locations. SH+ is a stress management intervention based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) principles, for use by persons and group of people experiencing psychosocial distress and other stress-related conditions.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Through an integrated and comprehensive health, nutrition, FSL,WASH and protection services delivery, and through working with partners, IMC will ensure optimal and sustainable service quality for acute malnutrition management, as well as improving MYCN practices through nutrition education, counseling, facilitation of support groups, complementary feeding and kitchen gardening. A sustained delivery of such interventions will then significantly contribute to the decrease of proportion of populations facing IPC 3, 4 and 5.
