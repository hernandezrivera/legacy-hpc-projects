Project Summary

Education services remain highly prioritized & are a critical bridge between relief & early recovery. Supporting education services under a protracted crisis necessitates simultaneously addressing immediate needs & fostering coherent service delivery especially for the most vulnerable people. Under SO1: Increased safe & equitable availability & access to in-person & home-based formal & non-formal education for crisis-affected boys, girls & youth according to Sub-district Severity 3&4, Idleb- Ma'arrat An Nu'man 3, Aleppo Jabal Saman 4, Dayr Hafir& Rasm Harma Al Imam & Eastren Kwairse 3.
Rescate as part of its responsibility towards the affected students in Aleppo, Rural Aleppo & Idlib submits this proposal to in Ma'arra rehabilitate 4 schools, wash facilities in Rural Aleppo, in addition to rehabilitate & equip one Girls vocational Institute in Aleppo, & TVET with ST.
In Ma'arra response is to NLG, data reveals that 28% are out of school & half of the children who are not in school are never enrolled, due to lack of sufficient number of schools & classrooms, this is reflected in the fact that over a quarter HHs with children did not return to their city because there was no school for their child/ren & 44% HHs with school aged children stating that opening more schools and/or increasing the classrooms in existing schools is a top priority to enable their return. Nontheless; lack of area services is the main reason for the delay in return. Increasing return to the targeted areas in 2022, concurred with government efforts to provide basic services in cooperation with SARC and ICRC who provided solar streetlights and large potable water tanks. Based on high risk of PSEA and GBV against children walking to school, many parents refrain from taking this risk & hence don’t register their children. On another issue, 25% HHs with school aged children informed that the later are not in school due to lack of operational or segregated WASH facilities that maintain children privacy, health& protection. Based on field visits and discussions with parents, Rescate intervention is an area-based approach as Rescate implemented the Public Market in Dayr Hafir. Sellers with children request this support in the 4 schools close to the market place. Rescate proposes to rehabilitate & segregate WASH facilities in schools in (Dayr Hafir-Rasm Harma Al-Imam-Eastren Kwairse) where classes are over-crowded 35-45 students/classroom, without functional wash facilities, urging students’ dropout. On another urgent need based on this situation analysis for youth & adolescents (15 to 24 years), representing over 30% of the entire population, who are struggling to access learning opportunities, vocations or to have meaningful engagement in their communities. Many adolescents had no access to schooling during the height of the crisis & are now unable to return to learning due to over age & are in desperate need of alternative pathways. Informal Vocational Institutes are the solution for them since they dropout of school to work, therefore; vocational institutes will support their learning & livelihood.
In efforts to find solutions to youth outside schooling & to enhance protection from GBV and PSEA risks as in some situations, girls & boys, including recruits & those with disabilities, are at risk of additional & multiple forms of violence due to their economic vulnerability. Life Skills & Citizenship Education (LSCE) will be provided to Youth (52% girls) to enhance critical thinking & creativity skills. Rescate contributed to ST Hanano Manara with renewable energy where vocational trainings are provided for youth (15-24) who are out of schooling since years. Subject to fund availability, Rescate will equip & support other community centers where curricula will include remedial classes in addition to vocational trainings mainly on vocations to fill the gap for youth boys & girls to be self-reliant& productive skilled workers.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Ma'arrat An Nu'man has suffered from conflicts for many years Learning facilities may be contained EWR as a result of conflict. In fact, the local authorities conducted field survey with the necessary equipment & Rescate will ask UNMAS support at the project launch. Risk for unregistered students due to lack of documents. Rescate team communicated with local authorities, who confirmed that the local community committee facilitates & expedite the procedures to obtain the dox. Risk in terms of inadequately safe learning facilities, the assumption after repair works is that the targeted population will accept the idea to return their children to school & prevent early marriage for females. There is a mid-risk that this will not be the case & the impact would be grave. The mitigation measures will be taken across the project identification: 1. Learning facilities rehabilitation improve the schools added to safe access to school. This includes solar power systems, repairs of manhole, add road signs. 2. Efficient water & sanitation facilities provided & maintained. WASH facilities will be sex & age-segregated & accessible to PWDs 3. Rescate will support courses for formal classes on some subjects for the students who failed exam to be able to cover the gap & graduate to next grade. This is subject to MoE consent that this intervention would save students from dropout & enable them to continue studies to secure a job & protect them from child labor.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of PSEA: High, Impact: High. Mitigation: Rehabilitation of schools including segregated WASH facilities & install streetlights to ensure safety. Rescate staff, partners, and contractors induction awareness session includes the definition of what is SEA and channels of reporting/whistleblowing are. Hence, they are required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of (PSEA). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, Rescate is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on Rescate work, as well as the contractors’ work, & to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA issues, will be addressed first by the Project Manager, who will share the concern with the HoM in Damascus, to establish a solution or way to proceed to enhance the protection of the PSEA survivor. When needed, the HoM will scale the issue to Rescate HQ in Spain, where the Director, in consultation with the gender specialists’ team, will look for potential solutions and/or an appropriate way to proceed.Rescate have PSEA and Code of Conduct in place, it is under annual review at HQ, and will be adopted when finalized in all projects, Staff, contractors and partners sign the PSEA and Code of Conduct after an awareness session at the launch of any project.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption is that Covid-19 will not disrupt the project. It is difficult to assess beforehand the probability for this to happen and its consequences. Rescate will take prevention measures since the beginning to avoid contagion: PPE will be distributed for the activities, staff, workers and beneficiaries will be informed about recommendations. In terms of support, the assumption is that the MOE, the local authorities maintain a strong collaboration with ONG Rescate. There is a low risk this would not be the case (as Rescate received full support from Stakeholders. • The devaluation of the Syrian pound due to the inflation may cause differences between the prices, in this context, Rescate will reflect the difference in exchange rate on the target of the project. • Regarding the security issues that may occur and access during the implementation of the M&E phases, RESCATE staff will commit to the safe access procedures that will be announced by UNDSS and other concerned partners in this context.
