Project Summary

DRC will provide MPCT to new arrivals, people affected by disasters and people referred with different protection needs in Adamawa and Borno states (Michika, Madagali, Mubi in Adamawa and MMC/Jere, Konduga, Bama, Ngala, Gowza and Askira Uba in Borno state. This modality will allow beneficiaries to focus on addressing their immediate needs and will prevent negative coping mechanisms. MPCT beneficiaries will subsequently be integrated into other longer-term assistance. By doing so, DRC will ensure that new arrivals and displaced women, girls and people with disabilities unable to cover their basic needs can start recovering and rebuilding their lives.

1 - Needs

In more than 14 years of conflict, affected people in north-east Nigeria have experienced violence, repeated displacement, loss of and/or separation from family members, destruction of property and livelihood assets and a deterioration in living conditions. Their resilience has been tested over and over, with children, women, female-headed households, people with disabilities and older persons enduring the most suffering and becoming even more vulnerable. Over the years, floods have become more severe across Nigeria, including in the BAY states, displacing people, destroying or damaging infrastructure, and heightening the risk of waterborne diseases such as cholera and increasing the incidence of malaria. As per UNICEF situation report dated on September 2024, 40% of Maiduguri, Borno State’s capital, in northeastern Nigeria, was flooded due to a breach of the nearby Alau Dam. According to OCHA, the deaths of 37 persons, and injuries of 58 persons were reported. The numbers of IDPs who registered in one of some 30 camps increased to close to 400,000 people within a week. At least 18 of these camps were schools, which contributed to the government’s decision to close some of the camps within a week’s time, to enable reopening of schools in late September2, it also contributed to the food insecurity projected to affect 4.4 million people during the 2024 lean season. The lack of economic opportunities has deteriorated food security and public health, negatively impacting the well-being of IDPs, returnees, and host communities. According to IOM DTM May 2024 updated data, Northeast has an estimated 2,271,987 displaced population, while Northwest has an estimated 1,302,443 displaced population. The Cadre Harmonise' projects a deterioration of the food security and nutrition situation in June - August 2025 in the Borno state with all LGAs falling in IPC3+. Gender roles are shifting in the BAY states, as evidenced by an increase in female-headed households, and increased numbers of women in caregiving roles (including to unaccompanied minors and extended family members). The protection situation for women and girls is also a concern, as adolescent girls are at an increasing risk of sexual and gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Traditional gender norms and power dynamics also contribute to girls’ and women's vulnerability, as they frequently face economic and social disparities compared to their male counterparts. As a result, vulnerable girls and women are often forced to engage in high-risk behaviours to meet their basic needs, such as transactional sex or marriage at a young age. Most returnees in the BAY states – approximately 1.7 million out of 2 million people – are unable to meet their basic needs. Women and children are disproportionately impacted, comprising a staggering 1.4 million (87%) of returnees. Individuals with disabilities (262,294 people) face compounding challenges, with 75 per cent of returnee households having at least one disabled member. In addition to insecurity, several factors including the current economic trends (persistently high inflation rate, depreciation of the naira, elevated fuel prices and lingering effects of cash shortages post the naira redesign policy in late 2022) and soaring prices of food and essential commodities and agricultural inputs are diminishing the purchasing power of vulnerable communities already grappling to meet basic needs. The devasting floods in 2024 that affected the BAY states and coincided with harvest season disrupted food production and impacted crop yields.

2 - Activities

The action will target vulnerable women, girls and people with disabilities living in conflict-affected communities, regardless of their status – Internally Displaced People (IDPs), host communities, returnees. However, while PiN will be identified through different channels based on their vulnerability and on their protection concerns with specific focus on households headed by women, children or PwD. Needs assessment/protection monitoring reports and evidence from feedback mechanisms will be used to determine the prioritization of communities in the targeted LGAs, while the collaboration with community structures and parasocial workers will constitute a first entry point to pre-identify potential PiN. The assessments will integrate strong protection mainstreaming to ensure the beneficiaries are selected based on their protection situation, and that the response provided contributes to improve it; especially, the joint assessments will evaluate the protection-sensitive negative coping strategies adopted by the beneficiaries to cope with the lack of access to basic needs. Activity 1: Households eligible for all sectoral assistance will be provided with a multipurpose cash transfer, when the market assessment confirms cash is the most adapted way to respond to humanitarian needs in the areas of intervention. In MMC, Jere, Monguno, Bama, Ngala, Gowza and Askira Uba LGAs in Borno state and Michika, Madagali and Mubi in Adamawa state, where cash is particularly adapted, MPCT will allow DRC to cover the gaps observed for protracted displaced people, as they are often deprioritized by other external partners, and to complete the in-kind assistance they already received through other grants. Activity 2: DRC will verify that minimum requirements are met as a precondition to deliver cash assistance; emergency needs in targeted communities, markets must be functional and have the capacity to provide the goods and services beneficiaries need to meet the identified needs, cash transfers are safely accessible and accepted modality for beneficiaries to receive assistance. DRC will align the cash transfer value of the MPCT with the latest Nigeria CWG guidance which is currently 69,312 NGN (approx. 43 EUR) for a household of 6 with an amount of 11,552 NGN per capita. DRC will disburse the cash transfers based on household size with household size 1 to 3 receiving a fixed rate of household of 3 while household size 8 or more will receive a fixed rate of HH size 8. Households size from 4 to 7 will receive cash transfer value per capita. The purpose of the financial support is to enable the MPCT recipients to cover their basic needs and reduce their use of negative coping strategies and therefore DRC will be providing four-month installments for each household while referring them to adequate service providers in the areas that inlude income generating activities, protection, WASH, Shelter etc... Activity 3: DRC will conduct a Rapid Market Assessment before and after the MPCT distribution to measure the impact on the market due to the cash inflow in the communities. The monthly Market monitoring of the main commodities in the Minimum Expenditure Basket will demonstrate that the market are accessible and able to absorb the increase in demand and goods are available. The monitoring of prices and its fluctuation trends will be shared with the Cash Working Group (CWG) and will be analyzed against the MEB can the cash transfer value. Activity 4: DRC MEAL team will conduct a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) 30 days after each cash distribution.
