Project Summary

The main objective is providing access to life-saving and resilient WASH-IWRM, Education and Protection services and advocacy including gender and age concerns, protection guarantees, and environmental enhancement for 61,488 (WASH), 3,000 (Protection), and 3,215 (Education) beneficiaries who are traumatized IDPS, refugees, returnees, host communities, those affected by unsafe water borne disease threats, conflict and/or natural recurring disasters in South Kordofan,Seirba West Darfur, Red Sea states. There will be a special emphasis in targeting vulnerable populations such as women, children, and the elderly. The inclusion of large percentages of women in project initiation, design, planning, execution (including monitoring and evaluation), and close-out activities, is a key objective that will not only save more lives, but improve the quality of services that alleviate suffering for the present situation, add valuable input, help mitigate negative impacts of future life threatening occurrences, and affect truly inclusive decision-making beyond the water maintenance committees. Again, gender, protection and environment will be major priorities.This will set the stage for newly emerging resilient, sustainable IWRM approach to overall water usage and conservation and all that this most basic natural resource impacts.
In order to reach this objective IAS will utilize an Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) approach. IAS has developed a practical field manual on IWRM that serves as guide in the implementation of its WASH programs. Practical examples include: Following hydro geological studies and community consultation, the most sustainable method for water provision will be selected. Drilling of new boreholes equipped with hand pumps are good options in most of the locales. In areas with high demand and adequate water supply solar powered mini water yards will be introduced. In the areas with rare to limited availability of ground water the best option will be solutions related to the rainfall capture schemes. Every water point implementation or rehabilitation will include beneficiary sanitation and hygiene promotion and training, including hardware mechanic training which will include women and girls, for recruited water maintenance committees, where we will communicate that the best prevention to disaster is maintaining resilient, accessible and safe water points.
Closeout activities will include helping beneficiary water maintenance committees determine the financial cost for actual spare parts so that they can target necessary budgets for ensuring continuous water availability when breakdowns occur.
IAS interventions in education sector will be in Seirba- West Darfur ,Rashad - South Kordofan and Haya in the red sea targeting 3215 boy and girls , as more areas became accessible. IAS interventions in the targeted localities aims to improve quality of education environment through activities prioritized in the HRP such as the provision of teaching, learning and recreational materials targeting 3215 in the 3 localities.capacity-building for teachers and PTAs members targeting 470 individuals in Seirba and Rashad to support community engagement and interaction with schools through training on school management , psychosocial support and disaster risk reduction in order to enhance their skills. TOT training will also be conducted for 80 teacher in Seirba and Rashad . More over IAS will contributes in improving of the healthy environment in the schools through the conduction of awareness campaigns in hygiene promotion, gender sensitive and child protection for 3000 in the 3 targeted localities.
Protection is ensured through hygiene awareness sessions to targeted communities and students at schools including children and people with disabilities. IAS will use multi-sector approaches to increase the effectiveness of programming and meet multiple needs of the targeted affected populations in the targeted localities

Needs

According to IAS' recent Needs Assessments in Red Sea State and South Kordofan and ongoing assessments in West and South Darfur, along with statistics published in OCHA's December 2018 Humanitarian Snapshot that there are 5.5 million people in Sudan in need of humanitarian services. There are 183,500 new South Sudanese refugees in 2017 bringing Also, the authorities are reporting newly emerging areas in South Kordofan where access has not been available for agencies to provide basic necessity services. Many from these localities are wandering searching for water,food and shelter. The implication is a tremendous need to help the Sudanese Government provide even the most basic life-saving services to these traumatized wandering IDPs, refugees and returnees, along with the increasingly over-stressed host communities.They cited the need for safe drinking water and water borne disease mitigation training and convenient sanitation locations as key priorities.WASH urgent needs:1.Rehabilitation and upgrading of existing water supplies and drilling new boreholes 2. Establishment of monitoring of water quality and treatment systems 3.Comprehensive disease prevention by improvement of sanitation facilities supporting health/Nutrition sector. 4. Systemic hygiene promotion 5. Establishment of solid waste management program 6. Ensure schools and market places have hand washing facilities and adequate latrines. AS is one of the very few organizations that have our own drilling rigs, geologists, survey equipment and CDOs to meet the needs of all targeted beneficiaries in WASH, protection, environment, and all age/gender needs. Girls are walking many hours in unprotected areas in order to wait in long lines to fetch, often, contaminated water for their families. This leaves no time to attend school. Women and the aged are marginalized and prevented from assuming more influential roles in beneficiary societies. Needs in the education sector continue to increase mainly in North and West Darfur. As more areas become accessible, the need for quality education is increasing across all areas with humanitarian. Children of displaced communities are adding pressure to existing education facilities which cannot accommodate new students due to limited space and resources. As a result, children are in risk of dropping out of school, becoming exposed to additional risks and violence and child labor.

Every care and attention to making the work environment safe. Beneficiaries are the major part of the planning , project execution and closeout. Beneficiary complaint mechanisms will be put in place for all interventions. Impact on environment/natural resources

The environmental impact for safe water provision is naturally very positive in terms of supporting a healthier lifestyles, better quality of lives, and supporting plants and agricultural projects, etc, but with the use of cement and construction materials, great care will be given to cleaning up after each project site; that each water point intervention area will be cleaner than before IAS commenced the project. Inclusive community/ main actors project design, initiation and planning meetings will be the stage for environmental protection and planning advocacy. Beneficiaries will be tasked with developing a long-term environmental plan and will sign-off and be accountable to the authorities on their progress.For every water point IAS will conduct Environment Impact Assessments (EIAs). Taking the data from these surveys, we educate targeted beneficiaries to take necessary steps to improve the environment. Communities will be involved in cleaning up water point and latrine construction sites. Fences and shrubs will be set up around the area. Conservation of natural resources, especially water, will be central themes in WMC meetings throughout the project, including close-out activities which will also include inspection ensuring that the environment activities were carried out and the environment enhancement plan was completed for the future in each beneficiary community where IAS is intervening. As well as budgeting for water point maintenance needs, WMCs will be requested to work with the community to budget environmental enhancement activities. Finally, those receiving environmental advocacy and planning training will be encouraged to bring their knowledge into the local schools.
