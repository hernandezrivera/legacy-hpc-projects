Project Summary

According to the Health Cluster Response Plan for 2025, 10.8 million South Sudanese are in need of Humanitarian Aid, with 5.6 million among them requiring essential lifesaving healthcare services. Insecurity, long distances, limited healthcare supplies, inaccessibility of healthcare facilities and inadequate healthcare workers have disproportionately affected access to healthcare, especially among women and children. Unity State counties such as Leer, Guit, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Pariang have been adversely affected by these emergencies and currently lie in severity 3 or 4, with an intersectoral prioritization of either 1 or 2.
In Leer, Mayendit, Guit, Panyijiar, Rubkona and Pariang, an estimated 425,663 persons are in need of emergency life saving healthcare, with Rubkona alone accounting for 211,802 persons. The rates of immunization are extremely low, malaria cases remain high, while acute watery and acute bloody diarrhea are endemic in multiple counties. Thousands of hepatitis cases have been reported due to a dire WASH situation, while infectious diseases continue spreading, with significant portions of the population depending on traditional healers as a coping mechanism. Returnees fleeing the Sudan crisis have further compounded the situation, with cholera outbreaks in entry points and transit counties being a reality.
The proposed project shall extend primary health care and reproductive health services to vulnerable persons, including IDPs, returnees and host populations in Leer, Mayendit, Guit, Panyijiar, Rubkona and Pariang.
This project is in line with the health cluster objectives and the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan which seek to:
1. Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations.
2. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases.
3. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
This project is targeting 45,400 beneficiaries and will support them with lifesaving emergency health care and reproductive healthcare services through Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs), Primary Health Care Centers, County hospitals and mobile clinics that CH has been supporting in the targeted counties. CH will work with the State Ministry of Health in Bentiu and the County Health Departments in Leer, Mayendit, Guit, Panyijiar, Rubkona and Pariang, as well as the nutrition cluster for nutritional supplies to PHCCs, Protection and GBV sub cluster for reproductive health supplies and clinical management of rape, and the WASH cluster for WASH in health facilities. CH is a strong member of these clusters.
The project requires USD 1,100,000 to reach 45,400 vulnerable individuals in Leer, Guit, Mayendit, Panyijiar, Rubkona and Pariang.

1. Needs

According the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2024, and the Health Cluster Response Plan for 2025, 5.6 million South Sudanese are in need of lifesaving healthcare services, with 3.09 million among them targeted by the health cluster. Only less than 2% of the government's budget goes to healthcare, far below the 15% recommended by the World Health Organization and the African Union Heads of State. Insecurity, long distances, limited healthcare supplies, inaccessibility of healthcare facilities and inadequate healthcare workers are some of the barriers to accessing healthcare. For example, there are only 3.5 healthcare workers per 10,000 population against the WHO recommended 44.5 per 10,000. Flooding in large areas of Unity state has further compounded the healthcare needs of 415,803 displaced persons, with thousands of returnees adding to the crisis. Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with WHO estimating that 1223 in every 100,000 live births ending in maternal death. Only 46.7% of pregnant women attend at least one antenatal care visit, while skilled birth attendants only assist 19% of deliveries. Teenage pregnancy in the country remains high, with 30% of deliveries occurring among girls aged 15-19 or younger. Only 19% of healthcare facilities are able to offer the full package of the basic package of health and nutrition services. The rates of immunization are extremely low, malaria cases remain high, while acute watery and acute bloody diarrhea are endemic in multiple counties. Thousands of hepatitis cases have been reported due to a dire WASH situation, while infectious diseases have continued spreading, with significant portions of the population depending on traditional healers as a coping mechanism. In the targeted counties, healthcare has been adversely affected by flooding and conflict, with healthcare facilities either dilapidated or completely destroyed. Facilities like Thonyor PHCC in Leer have lost whole inpatient wings, while Guat and Lual PHCUs in Leer, Madol and Pabuong PHCUs in Mayendit, and Boaw PHCC in Koch, have been completely destroyed, which has increased mortality and morbidity rates in the affected counties, especially among vulnerable women and children under 5. Thousands of returnees have been flocking into Unity State through Rotriak in Rubkona, Adok in Leer and Nyaruop in Koch, courtesy of the civil war in Sudan, further straining the healthcare needs in Guit, Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, Rubkona and Pariang. An estimated 20,390 people are in severe need of lifesaving primary healthcare and reproductive health services in Guit, 55,325 in Leer, 25757 in Mayendit, 32,948 in Panyijiar, 25975 in Pariang and 158,851 in Rubkona. CH is targeting 45,400 persons in the named counties.

2. Project activities

In line with health cluster objective 1, which is to improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations, and cluster objective 2, which is to reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases, CH will offer complementary services in the target locations through: 1. Refresher training of frontline healthcare workers in provision of life-saving emergency services such as IMNCI, ICCM, BEmONC, CMR and PMTCT - CH will target health workers and community health workers currently working in currently unsupported facilities. 2. Carrying out vaccination campaigns such as EPI - CH will offer refresher training for EPI vaccinators and enlist them in EPI campaigns in order to reduce morbidity and mortality attributed to vaccine preventable diseases. 3. Outpatient Consultation for endemic diseases such as malaria, acute watery and acute bloody diarrhea through outpatient services in targeted facilities - CH will rehabilitate 6 primary healthcare facilities and use them to provide outpatient services for endemic diseases. 4. Carrying out rapid response and risk communication in the outreach locations earmarked for the project activities in Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Panyijiar and Pariang - CH will enroll and train community health workers and use them in risk communication and community engagement (RCCE). 5. Providing antenatal care (ANC), Skilled Birth Attendance and Postnatal Care services at primary health care facilities that are not supported by the HSTP - CH intends to build the capacity of frontline healthcare workers in skilled birth attendance, ANC and PNC. Frontline health workers working in the 6 rehabilitated facilities will be targeted. 6. Conducting community-based outreach services in primary healthcare facilities and Mobile Clinics - CH will support mobile clinics and outreaches in hard to reach areas where the local population has no access to a hospital, a PHCC or PHCU. 7. Promoting family planning through distribution of pills and condoms - CH intends to train community health workers on reproductive health and family planning and enlist them in distribution of family planning options such as pills and condoms, while creating awareness in the community on Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH). 8. Minor rehabilitation of 6 flood affected primary healthcare facilities - CH intends to rehabilitate at least one flood affected primary healthcare facility from each of the counties listed. Only facilities that are not supported by HSTP will be rehabilitated and equipped. 9. Last mile transportation of medical supplies - CH will support the 6 rehabilitated primary health care facilities with last mile transportation of drugs and other medical supplies from WHO warehouses in Juba. Drug consumption reports from the six supported facilities will be shared with WHO and the health cluster.
