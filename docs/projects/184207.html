Project Summary

FSNAU and FEWS NET Post-Gu Assessment in September 2021 projected that nearly 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. The 2022 humanitarian needs overview (HNO) shows the continued political instability, population’s displacements, ongoing armed conflict, and longstanding environmental stress have rendered many of the country’s estimated 15.7 million people acutely food insecure, 2022 presents the potential for drought to be one of the primary drivers of food insecurity.After substantial deterioration since the beginning of the year, pasture, browse and water availability improved in many areas following intense rainfall between late April and mid-May. However, pasture and browse availability is below average in most parts of central Somalia, Bakool, Gedo, Middle Juba and Lower Juba regions. Pasture is also below average to poor in coastal parts of Bari and Nugaal regions.
WRRS in the proposed program within the targeted areas will be working towards improving the livelihood support and improvement of the livelihood to the beneficiaries that will be targeted in the area. WRRS is currently implementing the un-conditional cash transfer program under the support of SHF funding, this has seen the beneficiaries improve their livelihood due to the cash support. WRRS therefore in the same approach will roll out a more similar interventions in the area to ensure that the support provided respond to an immediate needs as well as the to ensure that there is sustainable programs that the beneficiaries can continuously survive on. this will involve, WRRS will address food insecurity through providing unconditional cash transfers based on values from the Somalia Cash Working Group and through providing temporary employment opportunities (cash-for-work) for residents of IDP settlements. Cash-for-work provides an excellent opportunity for efficiently using funds, as funds will provide cash support to families suffering food insecurity while also funding basic improvements to local infrastructure (feeder roads, paths, local market infrastructure, fencing of water points). WRRS will support the transfer of unconditional cash transfers through six months of mobile money transfers. WRRS will work closely with the clusters to ensure that the service provision through the interventions are in line with the cluster strategic interventions.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

WRRS has had experience of both mode of cash transfer and for easier accountability voucher can easily be accounted. Given in-kind’s inability to address the needs of communities facing crisis, cash transfers have become more preferable among beneficiaries with the flexibility offered. Furthermore, with the increase in mobile money penetration in Somalia, cash transfers have increasingly become more popular in the modern era, unlike in-kind transfers which involve more costs, are not as safe as mobile money transfers, and take longer to distribute. Markets in Somalia have remained functional and there is adequate infrastructure to enable this modality to work efficiently.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

WRRS current implemented project that will be related to the proposed intervention will be used as a baseline in considering the market functionality. WRRS will ensure that adequate market survey has been done to get clear pricing for accurate and realistic cash disbursement. This has been seen as a guide towards developing efficient and effective responses to crises, particularly for populations in IPC 3&4. Monitoring of the markets will be done regularly through local market price monitoring, the CWG and FSNAU, and this will be helpful in ascertaining if markets in target locations are sufficiently elastic and competitive in order to respond to increased demand with sufficient quantity and quality of goods without resulting in inflation.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Gender disparities are on the rise in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. WRRS through the project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes. A Do No Harm approach will be adopted in all our interventions. Contextual analysis is important in the implementation of this project considering the various potential sources of conflicts in the target locations both at leadership and community level. Regular assessments and community engagements will be done to learn if the project activities, approaches and methodologies are sensitive to the local context. Vulnerable community members generally refer to those disadvantaged with compromised access to resources to meet their daily basic needs. The project staff will ensure that during community mobilization and sensitization, all community members are given equal opportunity to participate. WRRS has been working in areas where security and conflict has been a major factor affecting the beneficiaries. Accountability for violations is limited, with traditional or formal mediation and justice mechanisms having been disrupted, or having become disrespected or inaccessible. Building community structures including local temporary learning space that will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities fleeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate food security and livelihood services without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence. Accountability to affected population is a key aspect in the trans-formative agenda. The project intends to involve the community in all stages of the program cycle, so that they understand the aim and the outcome of the entire project and embrace its as it rolls, they will also understand the guiding principle behind the decisions made.The project intends to use already existing community governance structures and use them as community committees. These will be involved in recruitment of staff, community mobilization and monitoring of the project.Their presence will represent the interest and role of the community in successful completion of the project. The project will also have a feedback mechanism , from which complaints and comments from the community will be collected, and fed back to the same. This will be done in a highly confidential manner with very few staff in the management allowed to interact with this process. WRRS will address the complains as they come and embrace the comments for the sustainability and acceptance of the services within the community since these are live saving interventions that will help the households have a sustainable livelihood income.
