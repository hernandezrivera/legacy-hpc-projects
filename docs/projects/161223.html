Project Summary

RI’s proposed 2020 Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) intervention is designed to contribute to improved health and nutrition outcomes, addressing key factors of wider community disease outbreaks, vicious circle of malnutrition, timely and equitable access to safe WASH and mitigation of related GBV, amongst 250,000 IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Upper Nile, Central and Western Equatoria states in South Sudan.
The proposed WASH interventions will be implemented in close coordination with RI’s health and nutrition programming and teams, applying an integrated approach to improve people’s lives and well-being, thus contributing to South Sudan WASH Cluster objectives and the 2020 Humanitarian Response. Recognising WASH services to be key drivers of health and malnutrition outcomes and women and girls to be the primary users of WASH services, the project aims to reduce GBV risk by ensuring adequate and needs based provision of WASH services that address key protection concerns. RI’s integrated approach will further address high levels of malnutrition by targeting SAM and MAM children and families and contribute to controlling disease outbreak.
Additionally, RI will ensure equitable, timely and sustainable access to improved safe and adequate water and sanitation facilities that address the vital WASH needs, public health and protection issues, respect sphere standards; engage communities in design and ownership, improve knowledge on appropriate hygiene practices to reduce and prevent WASH-related diseases including cholera, AWD, typhoid, skin diseases etc.
RI will construct gender-sensitive water supply systems, mainly solar powered, boreholes through manual drilling, extensions to bring water points closer to vulnerable households, rainwater harvesting systems at schools, health and nutrition facilities, rehabilitate damaged / nonfunctional boreholes, increase access to safe water and mitigate the risk of GBV at WASH facilities. Comprehensive training will be provided to female and male members of Water User Committees (WUCs) and Health & Nutrition facility staff. RI will work with existing WUCs to reinforce technical skills for water point O&M (operations and maintenance), establishing new WUCs where relevant. Solid waste management training will be provided to solid waste/hygiene committees to implement community-driven solid waste collection and disposal campaigns. RI will also construct incinerators for medical waste disposal at health facilities.
At the household level, RI will construct 1,000 household/family shared latrines, with owner contribution, amongst displaced, returnee, and conflict-affected communities to ensure that immediate and long-term sanitation needs are adequately met and facilities are gender and culturally appropriate.
Latrine construction will adhere to protection standards as elaborated in the guidelines for WASH Cluster partners in South Sudan. RI will also promote hygiene awareness aimed to support behavior change in hygiene practices through house-to-house hygiene education sessions, training of hygiene volunteers, and distribution of household packages for famine prevention which will include: one 20-liter bucket with tap for proper water storage, one 20-liter bucket without tap or jerry can for water collection, filter cloth, water purifier (PuR), and soap for six month consumption.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to experience the impact of years of war, violence and resultant economic crisis and food insecurity. In 2017, 2018 and 2019, conflict continued largely unabated in Upper Nile and Equatoria regions, adding to continued mass displacement with an estimated 4.2 million people across South Sudan forced to flee their homes. The signing of the revitalized peace agreement in September 2018 has led to a decrease in armed conflict. Although communities continue to experience the impact of years of war, violence, and resultant economic crisis and food insecurity, as violence decreases and confidence gradually grows, large numbers of spontaneous returnees are coming back to South Sudan. Greater Upper Nile hosts 60% of all IDPs in South Sudan with women and children representing 85%. The cumulative effects of the myriad drivers and multipliers of conflict, including insecurity and violence, sparse basic services, disease, climate shocks, ongoing displacement, and economic instability, have resulted in limited and insecure access to food and livelihoods, water, sanitation and hygiene and dangerously high levels of malnutrition. This is compounded by continued, limited access to health services and poor access to safe water, proper sanitation facilities, and awareness of safe hygiene practices. Access to water and sanitation (OD) is projected to remain a major issue in 2020, further aggravating the risk of malnutrition. The lack of safe drinking water, in combination with poor hygiene practices leaves a large proportion of South Sudan’s population at persistent risk of preventable waterborne diseases including cholera, AWD, etc. RI has conducted and participated several needs assessments and surveys in Upper Nile State through 2014 to 2019, including an interagency rapid multi-sector assessments conducted in April 2019, a base line assessment in Sept 2019, Longechuk Rapid Flood assessments on 18t Sept 2019, RI- KAP survey in June 2019 and flood assessments in Nasir and Ulang in Sept 2019. Assessments revealed the below findings: • homes are scattered and water points /sources are far from dwellings. Distance to water sources varied significantly across and within surveyed payams, ranging 10 minutes to 4 hours to fetch water, forcing people to drink and use contaminated river, swamp and stagnant water close to homes.•open defecation is prevalent, and latrines are not functional, especially in rural areas, except in households where latrine construction was supported by INGOs. Approx. 80%-85% people practice open defecation close to their homes. • unsafe hygiene practices prevail as does lack of handwashing facilities in homes and institutions. Children are particularly prone to water borne diseases since they play in open areas and bathe in stagnant water.• sanitation facilities in health and schools are not functional as they lack regular maintenance, resulting in deteriorating sanitation and environmental health amongst patients, health workers and pupils. Poor hygiene practices further aggravate health issues and heighten the risk of disease outbreak (including cholera and typhoid). Moreover, frequent flooding, displacement, lack of access due to poor road conditions and conflict have prevented rehabilitation of existing water supply structures. Hygiene education and promotion is limited due in part to limited access by NGOs and community actors. Increased awareness and education on water treatment methodology and spread of water-borne illnesses is critically needed to prevent further spread of diseases. Through proposed WASH interventions in water supply (water point rehabilitation, construction and water point disinfection), sanitation (latrine construction, waste removal) and hygiene promotion, RI further aims to improve and strengthen ongoing WASH initiatives and expand into further hard to reach locations in Greater Upper Nile, and Equatoria region to address WASH needs of IDPs, returnees and hosts

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The proposed RI WASH project is aligned with the South Sudan National objective and South Sudan WASH Cluster 2020 Strategic objectives and will directly contribute to the 2020 South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan. The integrated approach will address high levels of malnutrition in the target area by targeting SAM and MAM children and their families, control outbreak, acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver of poor health and malnutrition. The project will also aim to reduce the risk of GBV, since women and girls are the primary direct users of WASH services, at household and community levels by ensuring adequate and timely provision of WASH services.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is volatile and not sufficiently stable or safe, especially for girls, women and children as well as people with disabilities. Safety risks are expected from the distance open defecation areas and water sources that potentially expose children and women to violence and insecurity from people and wild animals. All these risks will be addressed through establishing water sources closer to home and constructing safe sanitation at household level. In addition, WASH facilities will be inclusive for differently abled persons, children and elderly - fetching water containers of smaller sizes, transportation and equipment. RI will mainstream protection by supporting integrity, dignity, and safety for all targeted populations. Women and children will be prioritized and seek counsel during the implementation process. Protection will be supported through women and youth consultations and representatives, targeting the most vulnerable, and support rights-based and participatory approaches. Awareness building exercise on protection concerns will be conducted before the needs assessment so that communities make informed decisions on location of water points Moreover; RI developed community feedback mechanism whereby protection issues will be collected and facilities, services and assistance will be used to mitigate and improve the protection environment.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

RI will make sure all WASH facilities that are constructed, established, rehabilitated and upgraded are properly handed over to the community and institutions, thus ensuring ownership. To ensure sustainability and proper O&M (Operations and maintenance), Water Users, Advisory committees and Technicians/Caretakers groups will be formed and trained for all constructed/rehabilitated/established boreholes, water supply schemes, Intuitional latrines and waste disposal sites will ensure long-term management of WASH facilities. Additionally, RI will conduct follow up visits to all WASH facilities committees / technicians / caretakers to identify areas for improvement and challenges which will then be incorporated in refresher trainings planned to cover the whole program period. Eventually, committees will be responsible for maintaining the repaired and established WASH facilities as well as promoting best practices for hygiene and water management for all users registered or the catchment population around facilities. RI will focus on renewable energy options. establishing / rehabilitated Solar powered water supply scheme operation and maintenance in targeted project areas. Once establishment/rehabilitation is completed, RI will support the water supply scheme user committees on O&M in collaboration with local authorities. After two to three months of operations, the water supply schemes will be handed over to the local authorities in consultation with the respective County Commissioners and WUCs. All handover process will be gradual and will only be completed after a strong base of technical knowledge and skills is established, and willingness among communities and local authorities to ensure facility sustainability. RI will continue empower committees to advocate for the sustainable management of water points, sanitation and waste disposal facilities as well as promotion of improved sanitation and hygiene practices.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

RI will make sure all beneficiaries are consulted and informed about RI interventions through meetings , especially through FGDs with women and girls conducted by RI trained female staff and volunteers to get their perception and contribute to WASH design, site selection and implementation. RI will also explain implementation modalities and intervention timelines to target beneficiaries, specifically those designed to mitigate exclusion of women and girls from consultation and decision-making process. There might be chances of forced taxation directly or indirectly by local authority on humanitarian assistance. RI has experienced tax imposition on humanitarians and clearance taxes for boats and vehicle movement from one county to another and tax imposed on local traders who supply goods and service to humanitarian organizations. RI will coordinate with all implementing partners on the ground, donors and UNOCHA to intervene and resolve emerging issues on the same. Provision of safe sanitation is expected to reduce exposure of women and girls to potential violence and insecurity while walking long distances for OD. RI female staff and volunteers will conduct registration, verification and HP session at the HH level to avoid that women and girls travel to distance venues for WASH assistance. RI will also establish multiple distribution points for WASH NFIs and HKs distribution to ensure women and girls can easily access without harassment or violence. As part of accountability to affected communities, RI will employ two different types of complainant and feedback mechanisms - responsive and proactive, permitting direct and indirect beneficiaries to provide feedback and/or lodge complaints. Moreover, PDM will be conducted to allow beneficiaries to provide their feedback on satisfaction with quality and quantity of goods and items included in the kit. All beneficiary feedbacks and ideas are taken into account to adjust and realign ongoing and future WASH interventions.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

RI will implement number of activities to ensure feedback from vulnerable community members specially individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls are received and considered for ongoing program implementations and future RI programming. RI’s beneficiaries are consulted on a regular basis for feedback on programming and service provision quality so as to improve and enhance programming in 2020. RI will collect information and segregation of beneficiaries’ details during the household’s registration and verification. RI will use this information’s to identify more marginalized, individuals living with disabilities and are not literate, women and girls to get their views and perception of RI’s programming. Information on assistance will be provided during sensitization and mobilization prior to start of project, through consultations with community leaders, both formal and informal. Chiefs, project management committees, water user’s committees and women/youth groups will be provided with full details of assistance including water, sanitation and hygiene promotion activities. RI existing feedback and complaint mechanisms will be strengthened in 2020. Water User Committees will receive feedback and complaints directly through the community including marginalized and vulnerable people, RI staff will additionally conduct monitoring visits to field sites to consult with beneficiaries on their satisfaction on RI programming, ways of working and ways to improve. Additionally, at all distribution sites, a complaint desk is present to address issues raised immediately. Moreover, RI will conduct house to house visit during the hygiene promotion session and other WASH implementation such as monitoring household latrine construction, conduct post distribution monitoring and obtain feedback and perception on RI interventions from marginalized and vulnerable disabled and elderly people so as to inform 2020 WASH programming.
