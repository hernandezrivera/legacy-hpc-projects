Project Summary

The project will address the immediate and long-term health needs with focus on mental health and psychosocial support, physical rehabilitation and disability inclusion needs of the most vulnerable persons that are the center of humanitarian response to cope with recurrent shock and build resilience.
The project will target returnees, IDPs and host population with specific focus on persons at risk of exclusion specifically people with disabilities, people with injuries and chronic diseases, people experiencing mental health and psychosocial problems, children, older persons; who are often invisible to mainstream humanitarian organizations. The project activities will target an estimate of 85,930 (25,779, Male, 60,151 Female among them will be 12,890 persons with disabilities, 1,780 elderly persons, 2000 children and 83,930 adults ) Vulnerable IDPS, returnees and host population. The focus will be on newly and protracted displaced households with a key attention to support positive coping mechanisms and resilience strengthening. HI will have static presence in the target project locations ; with support from local community structures, local organizations of persons with disabilities, health and protection partners; HI will identify the most vulnerable to MHPSS and physical rehabilitation needs and provide direct services. HI will critically focus on mainstreaming protection, gender and disability Inclusion issues at all stages of the intervention, identifying and giving priority to vulnerable female headed households and persons with disabilities. HI has one (01) MEAL manager and two (02) MEAL officers who will independently conduct all post distribution monitoring and share the outcomes with the team and cluster partners at state and national level.
The activities will be implemented by HI static team based in Pibor and Panyijiar with a mobile response to surrounding counties .

1. Needs

Eleven years after independence and four years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as measles and cholera outbreaks and climatic shocks resulting in extraordinary flooding and localized drought. These in turn have a severe impact on people’s all aspects of life. The health cluster estimates that in 2024, over 6.1 million people are in need of basic essential life-saving health services due to the protracted crisis in South Sudan; the health cluster have a target for 3,234,718 for health response in 2024. Statistics or comprehensive information on the number of people with disabilities in South Sudan is lacking. However, recent surveys and estimates suggest that it is likely to be even higher than the global estimate of 15% due to the protracted crisis situation and the limited access to basic services including health, high prevalence of disease outbreaks, and widespread displacement leading to higher prevalence of disabling conditions. HI’s 2023 anthropological study conducted in GPAA in January 2023, shows that people with disabilities who could not move through the water faced particular hurdles, but were mostly cared for to the degree that their struggling families could afford in a situation that was extreme for everyone. During an attack, all family members have to be taken to safety, much faster, fleeing from the fighting zone together with the cattle. Many people with disabilities remained behind, seeking a hiding place, but in a land under water there were few places left where to hide. Thus, due to wide-spread flooding, people with disabilities were left behind when conflict broke out, with di-minished probability to escape and with no place to hide, and therefore are among are among the several hundreds of people who were killed. There is currently no specific data on the number of people with disability in GPAA, however HI, in 2023, has identified more than 2500 people in GPAA alone, who need integrated assistance including unrestricted cash for protection. This number will likely increase, with increased access and response in the deeper lying hard to reach areas that this project aims to target. Floods, Conflict, displacement and instability have led to the deterioration of the health system across the country, and prevented access to essential care for a vast proportion of the population, particularly persons with disabilities. The health care system has suffered the effects of the protracted crisis that has affected service delivery in the areas of mental health and functional rehabilitation services for persons with disabilities . At present, limited knowledge on inclusive humanitarian action for persons with disabiliites in the target areas prevented sustainable and context relevant interventions, and exacerbates marginalization and stigmatization disability groups. Limited understanding and prevailing stigma related to mental health disorders and psychological distress also prevents people living with acute , mild, moderate or severe psychosocial difficulties from accessing appropriate services. Similarly, for persons with physical impairments, injuries and chronic conditions, the availability of functional rehabilitation services and assistive devices is limited, particularly in hard-to-reach areas. Despite the high rates of conflict related injury, and physical conditions linked to displacement, poor access to healthcare, nutrition and other basic services, the availability and coverage of functional rehabilitation services is severely limited. HI will support the persons with disabilities providing mobility assistive devices combined with functional rehabilitation services at the primary health care facilities.

2. Project activities

This project will specifically seek to address the gaps in the inclusive provision of inclusive mental health and functional rehabilitation services for persons with disabilities in Pibor and panyijar- South Sudan. The proposed action will meet further demand through capacity building inclusive humanitarian access and twin track approach, inclusive communication for frontline staff, OPDs community groups in the targeted implementation areas to ensure efficient service delivery and better sustainability of the intervention. This will include; supporting joint assessments and coordination on inclusive primary health care including mental health and disability specific risks and needs. Beneficiaries’ identification and assessment for functional rehabilitation services will be conducted at community level and at health facility. HI Rehab officer will provide mobility assistive devices and physiotherapy for person with disabilities. Based on the most critical needs and vulnerability criteria including among others: people with disabilities, people with injuries and chronic diseases, people experiencing psychosocial distress or mental health problems, children, older persons. For specific cases, HI in coordination with other partners will refer for specialized MHPSS and psychiatry services and prothesis and orthosis for functional rehabilitation. * Capacity-building of health workers on MhGAP-HIG, identification and referral of MHPSS cases * Capacity-building of health workers on physical and functional rehabilitation, capacity building on disability inclusion strategies to promote inclusive humanitarian action. The participants will be selected from all level at field including boma health initiatives. HI Rehabilitation and MHPSS activities will be conducted as facility level and HI staff will continue offering periodic support supervision to the health facility staff. *HI will also support Health cluster information management systems and 5W reporting to mainstream disability data collection within the information management system. These will include supporting integration of the Washington Group Questions and training of partners on how to collect and analyze disability data using the Washington group questions. HI will also focus on capacity building for health cluster members on Disability Inclusion and on Inclusive MHPSS across their response.
