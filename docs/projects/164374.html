Project Summary

During 2020, Violet is planning to Provide Hygiene kits to the most vulnerable HC and IDPs in Northern Syria as emergency assistance.
The project has been designed based on the assessment that has been done by the end of the year 2019 and January 2020 by Violet in cooperation with its partners and the needs of the people in Northern Syria for the basic humanitarian assistance especially the WASH NFIs through direct distribution of Hygiene kits as follows:
- Distribution of 9,899 Hygiene kits for 9,899 crises affected groups (3,599 Kits are secured).
Through the proposed project, Violet aims to contribute to covering the basic WASH needs of the newly displaced through hygiene kits distribution. Violet will target newly IDPs within the first week of their displacement. The Hygiene kits will be designed in accordance with the Cluster recommended package. while designing the Hygiene kits, Violet will take into account the beneficiaries' preference and their feedback through the latest PDMs conducted regarding the Hygiene kits distributed by Violet in partnership with a variety of organizations like SCI, CARE, and others.
The project implementation will be done through the following steps:
1. signing MOus with the community representative and receive the initial list of the beneficiaries
2. Conducting the verification of the beneficiaries in terms of matching the selection criteria and updating beneficiary lists accordingly, taking into account the displacement duration, WASH needs for female community members, children, elderly or persons with chronic illnesses
3. Distributing Hygiene kits for the selected beneficiaries according to the agreed criteria, special requirements or opportunities for women to strengthen their confidence and abilities are considered.
4. Conducting monitoring activities: check-list within the distribution and PDM, Gender-balance within VIOLET's MEAL team will be insured to allow culturally sensitive questions to be asked of women, by women, so that the program continues to be informed by and respond to the specific needs of women men boys and girls.
During the project implementation, Violet will constantly raise awareness for the scope of the feedback, expected staff behavior and selection criteria. It will be clarified how to raise a complaint through the Violet accountability mechanism and how it will be handled confidentially. During PMD, Violet will ask on satisfaction with the complaints mechanism.

4 - Needs

Since November 2019, the humanitarian situation has reached unprecedented levels of needs, with large-scale displacement from southern Idleb up to safer areas in the north. Between 01.12.2019 and 31.01.2020 approx. 520,000 people have been displaced. The majority of displaced (80%) are women and children, who are facing increased protection risks, combined without the ability to cover basic needs. Multiple displacements increase their vulnerability. It also forced them to leave behind belongings. The displaced are heading to northern Aleppo and northern Idleb, the heightened concentration of people puts additional burden on already overstretched, but also damaged infrastructure. Further 280,000 are at immediate risk of displacement. Most urgent is providing shelter to IDPs. However there is a shortage in accommodation, which is forcing them to stay out of camps, collective shelters, in rented houses, but also in unfinished or sub-standard shelters. The overcrowding and lack of WASH facilities has led to privacy and protection concerns for women, and is increasing forced early marriage among displaced girls as coping mechanism. Not only rehabilitation of WASH facilities is needed, but also distribution of hygiene kits. Due to market access, multipurpose cash vouchers are a preferred modality in certain areas. Child protection concerns are heightening, also due to drop out from schools. The winter conditions are increasing the vulnerability further with the need for NFIs, food, shelter, health and WASH. Basic services, such as health and education, are highly limited due to damaged and directly targeted infrastructure such as hospitals. Besides all lack of basic items and services, IDPs are in high need of psychosocial support and psychological first aid to accommodate them in their current situation. Protection concerns are high and thus require a sufficient referral system to address urgent cases of GBV, SEA and child protection.
