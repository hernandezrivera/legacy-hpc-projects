Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve the access of vulnerable and food-insecure households (HHs) to diversified food and support their basic livelihoods from further deterioration as a result of the protracted crisis, socio-economic conditions, and the impact of the COVID19 crisis. The project aligns with the HRP's Country Strategic Objective 3: "The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued." The project's specific objectives are to improve food security and nutrition of vulnerable HHs (SO1) and support basic livelihoods from further deterioration (SO2). The project will target 350 vulnerable HHs (1,925 individuals) with high levels of food insecurity and those on the Ministry of Social Development's (MoSD) waiting list. HHs affected by the last escalation, HHs with cancer patients needing urgent assistance, and HHs with people with disabilities (PWDs) will be prioritized. The project will provide targeted HHs with multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to cover their basic needs. The project aims to support the targeted HHs with basic livelihood by providing them with cash assistance. This will be achieved through providing MPCA for four months to cover their basic needs (food, NFI, health, hygiene, rent subsidies, etc.). The project is aligned with the Food Security Sector (FSS) specific objective 2 "Households suffering from limited access to food and income opportunities are able to meet their basic needs". The project considers the COVID19 pandemic setting taking into account associated risks that may affect beneficiaries and staff and all necessary precautionary measures. Oxfam aligns its programmes with safe-programming strategies and guidance that continue to guide its project implementation in all activities in emergency and in non-emergency situations to ensure communities and beneficiaries are safeguarded against sexual exploitation and abuse.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Gaza continues to face multi-layered stressors and prolonged restriction policies imposed by Israel on the highly vulnerable and fragile enclave. The 14 years of blockade, the intermittent military escalations and restrictions imposed on the Gaza strip exacerbated the humanitarian situation. According to the Palestinian Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) and quarter 2 of the 2021 labour force survey, the unemployment rate in the Gaza strip stood at 44.7%. Unemployment among females in Gaza reached 64% and up to 39% among males. According to the Palestinian Ministry of Labor (MoL), it is estimated that more than 19,000 workers were affected by direct damages to active facilities, 37% of them working in the agriculture sector. In the first quarter of 2021, poverty rates were above 50%. Approximately 80% of Palestinian HHs were receiving some form of social assistance enabling scarce cash resources to be spent on other essentials, preventing further deterioration of food security and livelihood status, and reducing the impact of negative coping mechanisms. In May 2021, following the hostilities, the poverty rate has pushed in Gaza to 59.3%, whereby 62% of its population (or 1.4 million people) were food insecure. From 6th August to 10th September, Oxfam and MA'AN have been addressing the needs of families affected by the last military aggression on the Strip. In coordination with the MoSD, 834 families who were affected by the hostilities in May and were nominated by the MoSD, were surveyed. The included 7.4% female-headed households, 11.0% elderly headed households, 3.5% people with disabilities (PWDs), and 5.7% pregnant/lactating women. 78.6 % of the families are still displaced with host families. The families whose livelihoods had been damaged accounted for 33.1% of the 834 families. Among the assessed population, 91.2% are abjectly poor, and the other 4.6% are poor. 22.4% of households reported no income and a complete dependency on aid. This has left them at risk of food insecurity, inability to have their children be ready for school, or even get support beyond temporary shelter arrangements. Despite the ad-hoc support provided by the MoSD and others the families received no cash In consultation with the surveyed families, reported priorities included food items, rental subsidies, and non-food items.

2 - Activities

The project activities will be implemented jointly by Oxfam and MA'AN teams as per the following: Output 1: 350 affected and vulnerable HHs received MPCA to meet their basic needs.Activity 1.1: Targeting and Selection of Households (HHs): this will include coordination, nomination and cross-checking of potential beneficiaries. Oxfam will contact the MoSD to nominate HHs with high food insecurity and poverty according to the Proxy means test formula (PMTF). Beneficiaries on the MoSD waiting list will be prioritised depending on gaps identified by MoSD at the time of the intervention. MA'AN will carry out a verification and vulnerability assessment survey through home visits using the Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT). However, it will be done via telephone if COVID-19 restrictions are activated, whereby Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs) will be adhered to, to minimise contact with HHs. Based on the survey, scoring will be done for all nominated HHs, prioritising families affected by the last escalation, families with cancer patients, families needing urgent assistance, families with PWDs, and female-headed HHs headed. The list of HHs will be cross-checked with FSS and FSS partners (i.e. IRPAL, CRS, Mercy Corps, and others.Activity 1.2 Provide 4 rounds of MPCA transfers to 350 Households (1,925 individuals): Four cash transfers will be delivered through local Financial Service Providers. Each beneficiary will receive the transferred amount according to the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) for 4 months. The transfer value is in line with the cash working group’s (CWG) recommendation reflecting the monthly income of targeted HHs prior to COVID-19. The cash assistance will support HHs to cover and access urgent basic needs (i.e. food, health, non-food, hygiene needs and rent subsidies) to reverse their negative coping strategies such as selling assets or incurring debt.Activity 1.3 Monitor cash disbursement and delivery (Payment Verification): cash disbursement will be monitored by Oxfam directly at the bank and through phone calls to 100% of beneficiaries to ensure that they received the entitled amount in a timely, safe and dignified manner.Activity 1.4: Post distribution monitoring (PDM): The PDM survey will be conducted after the third payment. Oxfam aims to use the vulnerability assessment data as baseline information and monitor them through its PDM of targeted beneficiaries, utilisation of cash and, anywhere possible, changes to the income status at the HH level. The sample size will be calculated based on the actual reached target per project using a 95% confidence level and only a 5% margin of error. For example, the representative sample size for 350 HHs is 184. Oxfam and partners' field staff will conduct PDM data collection either in person or remotely via phone calls depending on the COVID-19 situation and mitigation measures. The remote data collection will follow Oxfam's Remote Monitoring guidelines and use Oxfam's mobile case application designed for this purpose. The sample size of remote data collection will be 75% of the target group to ensure more accuracy Oxfam will collect disaggregated data by sex, age, and also by disability to all project beneficiaries. Oxfam has provided training on safe programming to its partner staff working in its humanitarian programme particularly in the field and those responsible for cash and voucher assistance. It will ensure that risks related to access of women to benefit from cash are continuously analyzed and mitigated. Oxfam and MA’AN’s field teams have joint experience in monitoring activities in the field and in providing adequate and appropriate orientation to beneficiaries at the project start in addition to the attention provided towards feedback mechanisms and safeguarding policies. . Oxfam will be support MA’AN’s capacity in aligning its data collection and monitoring activities towards gender aware and gender-sensitive approaches aiming at capturing risks, and benefits impacted women in the project.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Oxfam's Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) is an accountability mechanism that provides broader access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive online feedback through verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels such as helplines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam's MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud will be referred to Oxfam's Country Director and safeguarding team in line with Oxfam's confidentiality policies and reporting, thus will not be recorded by the system. Oxfam and MA'AN have adapted accountability and monitoring mechanisms to best address the needs of the people served in a changing environment due to the COVID-19. Oxfam's helpline will be primarily used to report any misconduct, fraud, exploitation or abuse incidents, which will be automatically transferred to the designated teams. Field supervisors will be handling inquiries and operational complaints from beneficiaries by making their numbers available to all beneficiaries. This mechanism will help resolve issues in the field, such as prices, access to shops, changing contact information, and others. At the beginning of the project, the beneficiaries will be invited to groups of 10-15 to receive an MoU that will include information about the project, the amount they will receive, and the project designated number to call in case of inquiries and complaints.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Oxfam premised its project design on the 'do no harm approach', ensuring that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to essential protection needs, which would have put them at risk, had it not been available. Technical assessments consider the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population without compromising the protection of data and humanitarian actors. The project will also be adaptive to the ongoing risks of COVID19 with regards to beneficiaries' access to food and cash. Before implementing activities, project partner staff will attend a training session facilitated by Oxfam's Safe programming focal point on safe programming principles to identify project risks and explore mitigation measures. The Gender and Protection Officer will carry out a refresher session for the project team (Oxfam and MA'AN) on the overall protection principles and how to detect GBV. This should equip them with knowledge and tools to conduct a risk analysis that identifies risks, risks level, likelihood and mitigation measures to consider in advance. All activities will be designed and implemented in close coordination and full engagement and consultation with the targeted communities. Examples of how Oxfam will ensure that the "do no harm" approach is mainstreamed in the project are as follows: • COVID19 related SoPs are already prepared by Oxfam and MA'AN teams that highlight different scenarios of beneficiaries' access to banks, their exposure to potential gatherings, and access to a continuously functioning feedback mechanism through cash. specific scenarios.• supporting women accessing cash by providing safe and appropriate bank locations, time and environment during disbursement and in orientation meetings. (). All activities will follow a quick risk assessment to identify any potential risks for women participating in the activity and identify means to support them within the project or beyond through a referral mechanism. Oxfam has already carried out a similar assessment in a previous project resulting in learning and applying corrective measures during the implementation of the project. Regarding PSEA, Oxfam has a designated PSEA team, including integrity and safeguarding focal points, and confidential counselors trained on PSEA, intimidation and bullying responsible for providing capacity building to partners on PSEA and ensuring compliance of all field staff with Oxfam’s PSEA policy. Oxfam’s has also a PSEA trained staff trained by the UN PSEA Network, which is expected to have a greater role in further enhancing awareness on the PSEA and improving current mechanisms and systems at partner organisations.
