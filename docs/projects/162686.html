Project Summary

The International Organization for Migration (IOM) has been working in close collaboration with the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC), as well as all United Nations agencies (OCHA, WFP and UNHCR in particular), to establish a system to collect individual data on internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees from internal displacement in order to provide information and a better understanding of the movements and evolving needs of displaced populations. Until 2018, IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) program in Sudan had primarily focused on registration activities, including biometric registration, conducted exclusively on the request from humanitarian partners and government counterparts – or based on need depending on the context of the emergency – to identify beneﬁciaries to better inform the delivery of humanitarian assistance, such as Non-Food Items (NFIs) and other distributions, cash transfer, relocation, etc.
Registration activities collect individual data according to selected characteristics of displaced individuals and their family members. The activities aim to build a list of all individuals present in one location at a point in time (updated beneﬁciary lists) and provides details of their characteristics (educational, livelihoods, needs) and additional vulnerabilities. Population groups covered in the past were IDPs and returning IDPs but the exercise can be expanded and the methodology adapted to include other target groups such as Sudanese returnees from abroad and foreign nationals.
Registration data is captured by IOM ﬁeld teams through direct individual interviews with people in the location where they reside (i.e. private homes, communal building, camps), and are usually with the heads of households. In the case of (biometric) registration, IOM staff obtain ﬁngerprints and pictures of all beneﬁciaries for future veriﬁcation. Registration is a lengthy and costly operation; thus, the data is not updated frequently. Quicker veriﬁcation exercises can be carried out to conﬁrm existing ﬁgures.
DTM activity reports for registration will be published with maps on the county-level and DTM global website, as well as distributing externally to its mailing list. Updated beneﬁciary lists for targets responses are provided to partners based on data sharing agreements. Aggregate data is additionally shared with partners through regular the Information Management Working Group (IMWG) meetings with IMWG.

1 - Needs

Currently, DTM in Sudan provides rapid emergency registrations, biometric registrations and data veriﬁcations in order to meet the immediate information needs for beneﬁciary selection, vulnerability targeting and programming. Registration has been conducted in over 1,500 locations across Sudan starting in 2004, and it is activated upon request by the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC), one of IOM’s humanitarian partners, or in response to IOM’s internal programmatic needs. Data sharing agreements need to be signed prior to registration with all entities requesting individual and non-anonymized records for assistance purposes. DTM will continue to register affected populations based on data requests and its use for direct assistance, although registration will be done on behalf of the emergency program.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Main Output: individual / nominal list of affected populations is disseminated to speciﬁc humanitarian agencies and sectors to inform direct response. Activities 1. Registration and verification of IDPs and returnees; data sharing; 2. Update the existing database to include all the collected information and train staff in data entry, database management and how to generate information. 3. Trainings and refreshers on standard DTM tools and protection practices for IOM enumerators. 4. Undertake information campaigns and sensitization meetings on the aim and modalities of DTM prior to data collection exercises. 5. Data and information sharing: IOM staff will also compile quarter-yearly reports to be shared with the Government of Sudan, humanitarian actors and relevant stakeholders/partners considering sensitivity of information and sharing as appropriate in the public domain or bilaterally, in addition to sharing datasets with selected partners.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Displacement Tracking Matrix registration services principally focus on the registration and verification of IDPs and returnees. Beyond normal transport arrangements, the process of collecting, analyzing and storing information has a neutral impact on the environment and therefore no mitigation is necessary.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

DTM activities already exist in Sudan and humanitarian partners from different sectors others rely heavily on the information on vulnerability. Data is only available upon request, which needs to be considered, and in line with the data protection principles, based on vetted data-sharing agreements. IOM as been conducting DTM activities in Sudan since 2004 and has a well-established methodology that ensures the protection of IDPs and returnees during registration activities. Registration activities are conducted to collect data to improve the effectiveness of future activities to the benefit of IDPs and returnees. Activities are conducted with police protection where necessary, in open areas and in daylight.
