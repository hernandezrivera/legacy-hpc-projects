Project Summary

The main goal for this project is to mainstream and implement the adoption of coordinated Community Communication and Engagement (CCE) and AAP best practice plans for the entire cluster system in South Sudan. This will be achieved through information sharing and the use of standardized community engagement activities via the Communication and Community Engagement Working Group. The overall outcome will be a mainstreamed CwC and the IASC AAP operational framework, as developed in the previous 2018 HRP plan.
Internews as the CCE WG chair and IOM and Acted as the co-chairs will all contribute to this project by different means. Internews through the CCE WG and CCE Technical Expert positions as well as supporting the WG meetings, strategic workshops and trainings whereas IOM and Acted through the Data Manager position and data management and other training expenses.
The proposed project aims at increasing the ability of humanitarian organizations to respond to the actual needs of the population, and to change the relationship in between humanitarian agencies and beneficiaries. This will enhance program quality, through strengthened AAP, by:
1) Enhancing the coordination and collaboration among humanitarian actors of Communication with Communities projects and activities, to support the targeting of PiN and to maximize resources;
2) Supporting the development and implementation of a national CCE framework based on principles of AAP by lying the groundwork for the creation of a Common Feedback Platform directly linked to the HTC, the Durable Solutions Working Group and the PSEA Task Force.
With the support provide by this project, a dedicated CCE Coordinator will be managing the day to day activities of the CCE Working Group, a Technical Adviser will support Working Group Members and the harmonization of the CFM road map.
This project is built on the need and desires among humanitarian actors and PiN for deeper cooperation between implementing partners and clusters to share best practice and pool CCE and AAP experiences both nationwide and at the local context. The results of the assessment will be used to design a strategy plan for the creation of a CFP that will be able to give a snapshot of the access to services and satisfaction of beneficiaries of humanitarian aid in the country.
The use of practical CCE methodologies will support a humanitarian response based on respect for the dignity of communities affected by civil conflict in South Sudan.
CCE activities will be implemented in a more coordinated and consistent way, to respond to the need for both better access to information and for communities, and better accountability in humanitarian response through mainstreaming community communications and engagement. These activities, coordinated and improved thanks to the work of the CCE Working Group, will ensure that the most vulnerable communities (particularly women, children, persons with disabilities, elderly and socially excluded) have better access to reliable, accurate, timely and effective information so that they can:
- Make informed decisions about their own lives;
- Have accountability to services delivered by humanitarian actors;
- Be fully engaged in the decision-making process about the humanitarian response and service delivery.
- Be engaged by the humanitarian agencies so that their concerns and issues are understood and, if needed, plans can be adapted by the implementing organisations.
The role of the CCE Working Group remains essential especially because, while some mechanisms exist at an agency level, coordinated messaging, complaints and feedback systems, two-way information sharing, and enhancing local governance structures at an area/regional level remains essential and yet under-performed, to ensuring access to services for persons in need.

1 - Needs

Internews is drawing from the experience of the past 13 years working on Community Engagement to and from IDP’s and refugees in South Sudan using both AAP and CwC/CCE techniques. The following are the needs identified by Internews: 1. Limited CCE Awareness and ExpertiseThe 2016 IAHE report of the response to the crisis in South Sudan highlighted how there is limited awareness of AAP as an IASC policy priority or in terms of IASC documents guiding practice in this area. Despite strong interest in CCE and AAP by the HCT and ICWG, presentations to cluster groups by the CCE WG found that many partners still felt that they needed a lot more support in how to include a CCE element to their response and that it was not a priority during an emergency, but rather a ‘nice’ activity to carry out at a later stage of the response. 2. Local leadership and representation of vulnerable groupsThe events in 2018 in Maban, as well as protests in the Bentiu, Juba and Malakal PoC where, as a result, activities have been suspended or withdrawn completely, demonstrates how proper engagement with respect for all can help mitigate against or minimize such events occurring. Our experience of working in PoCs across South Sudan has shown that traditional engagement with established camp leadership needs to be supplemented with a wider CwC based approach encompassing more of the community. Traditional leaderships within the PoCs are largely male dominated groups which may not represent the views of the wider community and which often do not effectively relay information back to their communities. Ongoing leadership struggles add to the rumour, conjecture and often unrest in all of the sites, with the various groups then becoming increasingly political. While this need to develop better tools and practices has been recognized, several staff from different agencies said they lacked any practical guidance on how to go about this, including training or direct support and advice from their organizations. 3. Lack of resources to maintain CCE in a formal, cross-cutting and consistent wayWithin the response, CwC/AAP is still seen as largely about passing on information, often one way, from agencies to communities, although there are also some strong examples of two-way communication and face to face interactions these are rare occurrences, and often only conducted by Internews on individual projects. What is lacking is a team to maintain CCE in a formal, cross-cutting and consistent way at the inter-agency or cluster level locally and nationally, which would affect the way clusters are fulfilling their own duties to Community Engagement and Accountability to AffectedPopulations. 4. Lack of a system for the participation of affected people in all phases of the program cycleGreater and more consistent attention to CCE can strongly improve the quality of the response in the future. Involvement of affected people in all stages of theresponse project cycle is still minimal and their expressed priorities (e.g. for education) often overlooked. The 2018 and 2019 HRP notes the need to scale up and embed good practice in CCE, which is exemplified by the work of Internews. Agencies and aid workers at all levels need assistance to move beyond information collection and sporadic cooperation in CCE activities towards embracing the full necessity and benefits of a commitment to community engagement. This is possible by focusing on the participation of affected people during the program cycle, with the creation of dedicated support to build capacity and integrate AAP into all clusters through training and mentoring.

2 - Activities

Activity I: Recruit a CCE Working Group Coordinator The role of the CCE Working Group Coordinator will be to act as the lead and primary support for the working group to fulfil its mandate to development CWC and AAPmethodology. This person will capture both the technical knowledge developed within the working group and steer the members. The coordinator will also play a pivotal role in linking the advancement of CCE techniques with the process of mainstreaming practices to the clusters in collaboration with the CCE focal points. E.g. assisting the development of CCE tools to inform all phases of the project cycle across all sectors. Activity II: Recruit a CCE Technical Expert The role of the CCE Technical Expert will be to act as the primary support for the working group members to help fulfil their mandate to implement CWC and AAPmethodologies within their work. This person will help the CCE WG Coordinator to capture both the technical knowledge developed within the working group and steer the members, to find CCE solutions to the challenges identified in the clusters. The Technical Expert will also be the go-to person to provide expertise for the implementation of new and innovative CCE techniques. Activity III: Recruit a CCE Data Management officer- (IOM)IOM will hire a Data Manager that will work to support in the design of a CFM framework and test it. The Data Manager will be responsible to help with the data processing and analysis for the creation of the CFP (see activity V and VIII). The Data Manager will also help members of the CCE to process and harmonize their data and it will work with the CCE Technical Expert to help designing the data flows (including data standards). Activity IV: CCE WG meetingsThe CCE Working Group Coordinator will be responsible for the gathering of the CCE working Group on a monthly basis, including drafting the agenda and sharing notes and minutes. The Coordinator will also make sure that ad-hoc meetings can be organized to discuss specific issues, being these urgent issues or issues that require more dedicated attention. The CCE WG will also meet specifically to design strategies to support National Campaigns Days. Activity V: CFP Feasibility Assessment and Resource MappingThe Technical Expert will start his work by conducting, on behalf of the CCE WG, a feasibility assessment complete with a mapping of the CCE resources available and used in the country. The assessment will look at the possibility of creating a Common Feedback Platform (CFP) for South Sudan. Through face to face interviews, surveys and focus group discussions, the Technical Expert will try to assess:- Who and how feedback are collected- How affected communities are involved or not in the delivery of the response- If a CFM could help mainstreaming and inclusion of persons with disabilities;- If and how responders are ensuring men, women, boys and girls and people with disabilities are fully informed of their rights and entitlements;- How to enhance complaints, accountability, and feedback mechanisms;- Who and how is conducting communications campaigns, operating mass communication systems, and ensuring widespread awareness raising sensitization.The final report will help identify gaps and strengths and will be used by the HTC and the WG as a road map for 2020. Activity VI: Strategy Workshop for National CEE Minimal Standards and CCE data managementThe Coordinator and the Technical Experts will also support the WG in the organization of a 3-day National Workshop in support of the HCT and the drafting of the National Humanitarian CCE Strategy. The workshop will see the presence of NGOs, CSOs, national organizations and civil society organizations, UN agencies, vulnerable groups and IDPs representatives, to discuss what should be the minimal standards required for CCE in the South Sudan response. 9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The Communities Communications and Engagement (CCE) approach thatInternews uses is essential for humanitarian intervention to be effective;organizations have a wealth of information to share in an emergency - theyspend millions of dollars in activities designed to change knowledge, attitudesand practice, and these CCE programs are essential to build communityengagement, mobilization and ensure they take place with the entirecommunity, rather than just with leadership groups. Over the course of the pastyears Internews has delivered more than 400 hours of humanitarian contentinforming communities about the humanitarian response provided. Equally,agencies need information from local communities in order to effectively targettheir work, improve the impact of their interventions, deliver transparency andaccountability to communities, and respond to and meet the real needs ofpeople. Strengthened communications with communities means betteraccountability to communities. Strengthening humanitarian agencies’ CCEstrategy and tools ensures actionable and useful information is delivered in atimely, reliable and useful way. Following the implementation of Internews’Humanitarian Information Service in South Sudan, the organisation developedit’s own approach for CCE and AAP anhas set up an independent communications feedbackloop using more than 100 listening groups across 4 PoCs, thus providing avoice for communities and a platform for discussion between all actors. . The services actively support agencies to deliver with broader impact; reaching those without access to information and allowing them to know what services are available, who is providing them, and, in the absence of these services or a shortfall in standards, who to talk to.
