Project Summary

The project aims to support the IDPs in North West Syria through distribution of basic and seasonal NFI kits in addition to help people cope with the situation in the area and survive the harshest of conditions both in and off-camps. Also the project includes light rehabilitation (doors and windows replacement) and medium rehabilitation for damaged houses, to cover the raising needs for shelter in North Rural Aleppo and Western Rural Idlib areas after the huge displacement that took place last year and continue to happen until this day. The project will be targeting both IDPs and host communities to alleviate the pressure on community resources and to prevent or reduce the tensions inherent in sharing them. The intervention will be done through in-kind modalities preferably bought from local market ensuring support to local economy. Allocation of items in areas will be done though needs assessment which will be multi-sectoral in nature to capture all aspects of IDPs’ and their hosts’ conditions, to explore full range of intervention and to ensure an appropriate intervention.
The project concept is in line with both sector objective:
1. Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support
2. Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.

4 - Needs

As 2020 has started, the needs in Syria has been probably the highest in the country due to the mass displacements that are happening since May 2019. With the start of a new decade new military campaigns parallelly started in south of Idlib and wester rural Aleppo which resulted in more than 600 thousand people on the move towards western Idlib and Northern Aleppo countryside, having no end in sight these numbers are expected to keep raising this year and the needs are getting higher and higher by the hour. According to HNO 2019 assessments there are 4.4 million people in need for NFIs and 4.7 Million in need for shelter in Syria. As the displacement in north Syria has reached 600 K in Idlib, which is also now facing huge influx of IDPs from east and south mostly towards closer to the Turkish border, it is expected that the number will rise to 900 K in 2020. As per the latest report from the CCCM cluster, NFI and shelter are the top needs respectively, with 100 per cent of people reported NFI needs and 89 per cent of them reported shelter needs. While return movements have increased to some areas and an estimated 64 per cent of communities are underserved, this is exacerbated by the deterioration of purchasing power resulting in families not able to meet their needs.As per HNO 2020 Up to one million IDPs are residing in last-resort sites in the north-west and north-east, a significant proportion of whom are in improvised shelters which will need replacement/ repair in the next year. In addition, some communities in sub-districts in Aleppo, Idleb are overburdened as they host more IDPs than resident populations with limited shelter and hosting capacity, where repair or rehabilitation are needed alongside a multisectoral response to ensure access and availability to basic services.Different gender and age needs including women children elderly and people with special needswill be taking in consideration while designing the project.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Outbreak of conflict prevents access to project locations.Mitigation: Experience from previous operations found that while conflict occurred, a cautious approach that plans for these types of delays is expressed in the project work plan. Further, the project design flexibly targets at a unit level of “sub district.” By using this target instead of specific Communities, the project can respond district-wide, wherever non-conflict areas with access allow.Risk 2: Current controlling entity or local council no longer supports QC activities or directly interferes with activities threatening humanitarian imperativeMitigation: staff/partners keep in close contact with the various local councils in order to be able to address any issues at an early stage. QC is transparent throughout all actions and communications with all stakeholders to ensure our status as impartial, neutral, and independent is unquestionable. QC will suspend operations in one location while continuing in another, until such time as delivery of aid is assured to be unimpeded. Risk 3: bad weather conditions (Rehab and Infrastructure). Mitigation: By starting all Shelter rehabilitation activities during spring until the end of summer to avoid bad weather conditions which will result in delay on rehabilitation works, and cut access to Targeted locations.Risks: 4 Overcrowding and/or integrating groups that do not have social histories together has the potential to increase risks of exposure to different forms of GBV.Mitigation: Use the GBV specific assessment questions to understand the different GBV risks present in the collective centers and/ or camps as well as different households' specific needs to feel safe in/around their shelter structures. Drawing on this, implement GBV risk reduction activities through specific shelter and settlement design-focused interventions.
