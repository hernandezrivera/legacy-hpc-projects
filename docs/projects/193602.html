Project Summary

The proposed project aims to support 500 vulnerable Gazan households (2,800 individuals, F:1372 and M:1428) through Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance to improve access to food and support their multiple immediate essential needs and access to lifesaving services.
The project will support the vulnerable households in the Middle area, Khan Younis, and Rafah with high levels of food insecurity and those on the Ministry of Social Development's (MoSD) waiting list, Female Head households, households with cancer patients needing urgent assistance, and households with people with disabilities (PWDs) will be prioritized by providing MPCA for three months to cover basic needs (Food, non-food, health, hygiene needs, and other basic needs).
This project will be implemented in partnership with two local NGOs. The first one is Palestinian Al Nakheel Association for Progress and Development (Al Nakheel) and the second one is Palestinian Environmental Friends (PEF).
Al Nakheel and its partners will validate these needs through phone calls, and field visits, and will coordinate with the Gaza Cash Working Group (CWG), Food Security Sector (FSS), and other actors implementing cash to maximize assistance impact and avoid duplication. The project will be based on the Do No Harm framework with a gender transformative approach and all targeted households will be selected according to protection-related and socio-economic vulnerability criteria.

The project aligns with the HRP's second Country Strategic Objectives: "The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under International Humanitarian Law"
and FSS Objective# 2: "Low-income households who are facing limited access to food are able to meet their basic food needs", and to the MPCA's Specific Objective: “Vulnerable HHs in oPt affected by shocks and stresses due to conflict and natural disaster supported with MPCA are able to meet their immediate essential needs, access to lifesaving services and decreases the incidence of negative coping strategies.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip faces a chronic humanitarian crisis that made most of the persons in need of some forms of urgent humanitarian assistance. The last large-scale escalation of hostilities in Gaza and the blockade imposed on Gaza 15 years ago, the internal Palestinian conflict and COVID-19 had extremely increased poverty and unemployment rates, The 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview identifies over half of the population in Gaza as food insecure and in need of assistance. The unemployment rate for the second quarter of 2022 was 44.1 percent in the Gaza Strip, with unemployment among males in Gaza reaching 39 per cent and up to 64 percent among females. The poverty rate in Gaza has increased to 59 percent in 2022 in comparison to 53 percent in 2017. High unemployment and poverty rates have limited families’ abilities to purchase food and basic living supplies, eroded savings, and forced families to take out debt, according to (the WFP Palestine Country August 2022) “one-third of the Palestinian population, 1.79 million people, suffer from food insecurity. Of those 1.1 million Palestinians are severely food insecure. Most of them (90 percent) live in the Gaza Strip”. Furthermore, according to the OCHA June 2022 Fact sheet 1.3 million out of 2.1 million Palestinians in Gaza (62%) require food assistance, and according to (FSS-HNO2023) An estimated 1.8 million individuals (33.6% of the population) suffer from moderate to severe food insecurity. In 2022, approximately 1 out of 3 people experienced some form of food insecurity, 84% (1.54 million people) of which are expected to need humanitarian assistance. Al Nakheel has investigated humanitarian needs in KhanYouis, the Middle area, and Rafah five focus groups discussions for farmers and agricultural workers were conducted (14 female, and 61 male ) with 2 PwD in the three targeted areas their ages between 20 to 65 years with different marital statuses, The main finding (1)- 100% of the attendees stated that they are affected directly by Gaza humanitarian crisis. (2)-82 % of the attendees stated that they are in need of cash assistance to pay some of their debts and meet their most basic needs.(3)-90 %of attendees female illustrated their need for cash assistance to maintain their livelihoods to reactivate their production.(4). The people with disabilities indicate that they are actually in a bad economic situation and need cash assistance to secure food for their families while most of the cash is spent on healthcare.(5). Females focused on their rights to benefit from cash assistance - equal with the males. Furthermore, according to the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2022, the key findings indicate that 81 % of households reported financial difficulties in meeting any of their household’s basic needs, 70 % of households reported difficulties meeting essential needs because they could not afford them, 55.7% of household expenditure (in cash or credit) was reportedly spent on food, 79 % of households that reported their household having recently taken on debt for any reason in the 3 months prior, 91.8% of the respondent households expressed their need for any form of humanitarian aid or assistance in the future, Moreover, The (MSNA) indicates the total number of people in need (PIN), in the Gaza strip in 2023 is 1,188,989 (53.4% of the total population), and The SEFSec report shows that the food security status in Gaza worsened in 2020, with the share of severely food insecure HHs reaching 40.7%, increasing by 4.9% since 2018. This brings the total number of HHs experiencing severe or moderate food insecurity to 64.4%. In early 2021, poverty rates were above 50%, and approximately 80% of Palestinian HHs received some form of social assistance. This enabled scarce cash resources to be spent on other essentials, preventing further deterioration of food security and livelihood status and reducing the impact of negative coping mechanisms.The intervention will be built on the understanding of gender-specific needs and expectations as well as socio-economic factors and cultural sensitivities and raised PSEA awareness as well as.

2 - Activities

The project activities will be jointly implemented by two local NGOs to provide multi- Purpose Cash Assistance to support the Vulnerable Households in southern governorates in the Gaza strip in closely coordinating with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), Gaza Cash Working Group (CWG) and Food Security cluster (FSS). Output: 500 Vulnerable households received MPCA to meet their immediate essential needs and access to lifesaving services. Activity 1. Selection of beneficiary Households Beneficiaries will be selected based on the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) waiting list and will be prioritized depending on gaps identified by (MoSD) at the time of the intervention. Al Nakheel and its partners will carry out a verification and vulnerability assessment survey through home visits using the Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT). Based on the survey, scoring will be done for all nominated HHs, The main selection criteria will depend on selecting vulnerable households that are affected by humanitarian crises and natural disasters, Such as(1) households whose houses were damaged due to the escalation in Gaza.(2)Households whose livelihoods were affected by humanitarian crises.(3) Households have lack to access to food and basic needs due to disabilities and protection issues.and will give priority to households(1) families with female-headed households,(2)families headed by a person living with a disability.(3) families with cancer patients.(4) families with elderly, and people with disabilities.(5) large family size and High dependency rate.Al Nakheel and its partners will collect disaggregated data by sex, age, and also by disability for all project beneficiaries. Al Nakheel partner will be working in Rafah and will be responsible for selecting the vulnerable households following up and collecting data after disbursement from the beneficiaries in Rafah, Al Nakheel partners is a well-known local NGO in Rafah it will be useful to select the vulnerable households and delivered the cash assist without bias and with a view to alleviating existing inequalities and Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Al Nakheel and its partners will continue working with local committees in each locality to identify and refer extremely vulnerable households who are not registered and not receiving assistance with MoSD, this action combined approach aims to minimize risks of inclusion and exclusion errors and ensure that those most vulnerable households can be identified and assisted with unrestricted cash to meet their most immediate basic needs, without bias and with a view to alleviating existing inequalities and Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), the list of HHs will be cross-checked with FSS partners to avoid any duplication of assistance. Activity 2.: Provide three rounds of MPCA transfers to 500 Households (2,800 individuals, 858 Males, 824 Females, 570 boys, 548 girls)Three cash transfers will be delivered through local Financial Service Providers (PalPay), Each beneficiary will receive the transferred amount of 1,080 ILS (roughly 310 USD) per household per month according to the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) this transfer value is in line with the cash working group’s (CWG) recommendation reflecting the monthly income of targeted HHs. MPC will support HHs to cover and access other urgent basic needs (Food, non-food, health, hygiene needs and other basic needs) to reverse their negative coping strategies such as selling assets or incurring debt. Activity 3 . Monitoring Cash disbursement (Payment Verification) will be monitored by Al Nakheel directly at the Bank (Internet banking services), local Financial Service Providers (PalPay), through field visits for targeted households and through phone calls for all beneficiaries to ensure that they received the entitled amount in a timely, safe and dignified manner, The Personal Identification Number (PIN) code will send an SMS to each HHs that is used for cash withdrawal transactions the network of agents and outlets participating with (PalPay), delivered the cash through local Financial Service Providers (PalPay) making it suitable for both gender and the elderly or less well-educated to receive cash securely, easily, and dignifiedly manner from available the network of agents and outlets in each locality in Gaza. The Post distribution monitoring (PDM) survey will be conducted after the second payment. Al Nakheel and its partners aim to use the vulnerability assessment data as baseline information and monitor them through its PDM of targeted beneficiaries, utilization of cash and, anywhere possible, changes to the income status at the Household level. Al Nakheel will provide training in partnership with PNGO for all staff working on its humanitarian project, particularly in the field and those responsible for cash and voucher assistance for Gender-Based Violence and Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is a key driver to maximizing positive impact. Al Nakheel and its partners, follow the Code of Ethics and Conduct ensuring that the beneficiaries are treated with respect and dignity, and preventing abuse. The Partners through the nature of work understand the concept of "do no harm" and consecrate their effort to provide the received theMulti-Purpose Cash Assistance to the local community to strengthen peace among them therefore, the staff is highly recommended not to use their power during the project implementing stages and not to be an element of the conflict, and not to use this Cash to support their sides in the conflict to prevent harm to the community, Partners follow the Rights-Based Approach Manual as it deals with beneficiaries as rights holders rather than service recipients and empowering them to protect and claim their rights. Partners use an effective complaint system so that beneficiaries can give feedback on their treatment and quality of services Partners follow the humanitarian principle of DO Not Harm through preventing and activities that may cause negative outcomes for the target beneficiaries. The project will be adequately announced in the targeted communities using various outreach tools to give equal opportunities to benefit from the project, The targeted groups will be selected based on transparent selection criteria that ensure humanitarian assistance is delivered without bias, and with a view to alleviating existing inequalities and vulnerabilities. The selection of beneficiaries will fulfill the transparent and clear criteria for the project to be reducing suffering, maintain human dignity, and reach the most valuable people. Gender has been strongly incorporated into the design of the project. the partners adopted “Zero Tolerance” regarding any action causing GBV, and all the staff members signed and trained on GBV and harassment prevention policies PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse). Al Nakheel PSEA focal point received training through PNGO with the partnership PSEA Network which is expected to have a greater role in further enhancing awareness of the PSEA and improving mechanisms and feedback. The gender focal point in Al Nakheel will be responsible for monitoring and following up on any Gender-related complaints, The gender risks facing project implementation are protection risks associated with cash programming in Gaza including an increase in domestic violence and/or intra-household power imbalances (i.e. who controls and spends the cash) and the exclusion of women or individuals with special needs in the process of awarding the cash (e.g. due to secondary social status and restrictions on movement) Mitigation measures include: - use of female staff members to make female household members more comfortable. - in-person visits if households are not able to collect cash. - a beneficiary orientation that informs male and female household members about the potential risks, especially for female-headed households as well as an introduction to Community Accountability and Reporting. - on a case-by-case basis, staff will conduct household visits for orientation. - training staff on the detection of potential protection risks.
