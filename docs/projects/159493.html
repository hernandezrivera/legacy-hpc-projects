Project Summary

This food security proposal aims at addressing food insecurity in South Central Somalia regions caused by floods, drought, conflict and forced internal displacement. The project supports victims of conflict and disaster to protect their livelihoods, enhance local food production and adoption of resilient strategies to mitigate against the recurrent shocks. Beneficiaries will be targeted based on their needs, which shall majorly be determined by IPC levels, and mostly IDPs and host communities. The interventions will focus on emergency response for acutely affected households, and these will benefit from unconditional multi-purpose cash assistance, cash for work/assets (cfw/cfa), and cash vouchers whereby the cash amount will be based on the MEB per region of target.
Activities will also be implemented through targeting communities that need to restore lost livelihoods through building of assets while participating in conditional cash-for-assets activities thus providing cash for restocking, and in many cases conserved surface water for animals and drip irrigation. Usually the participation of women in cfw activities is low and the project intents to raise female participation rates. Households (hh) in protracted IDP camps that lost all their livelihoods will be supported with alternative income generating activities (IGA) while most vulnerable IDP hh with school going boys and girls and people living with disabilities shall be supported with food vouchers. Rural populations will be supported with activities such as fodder production by agro-pastoralist hh, for which especially women shall be targeted, and agricultural inputs to riverine farmers. Additionally, governance on natural resource would not only be enhanced through this project plan, but also gain transformative capacity thereby reducing conflicts in the use and managing of resources.
Amongst the riverine communities along the Shabelle River DKH’s partner shall promote the use of crops that mature faster, support small scale irrigation, provide farming tools and seeds, train farmer on good agronomic practices and conduct market assessments to inform interventions.
DKH and the implementing partners will mainstream protection in ensuring that only deserving cases are selected to gain in the project.
In addition, gender will be a major consideration through determination of intervention participants. The Target number of HHs are 5,300/ 31,800 persons.

Project Activities

Objective 1: To improve immediate access to food by the most vulnerable households among the targeted populations in South-Central Somalia through provision of conditional and unconditional assistance. Activity 1.1: Conduct Pre-Crisis Market Analyses (PCMA) and complementing existing studies according to the EMMA Toolkit in the targeted locations in order to define the details of the cash transfers. Activity 1.2: Provide Unconditional Multi-Purpose Cash (UMPC) through mobile money transfer to IPC 3 & 4 people in targeted areas for a period during acute needs (target 3000p/500hh) Activity 1.3: Provide conditional cash for assets to drought affected communities that live in vulnerability criteria that allow them to do physical work (IPC 2&3). (Target 3000p) over 3 months Activity 2.4: Provide food vouchers to most vulnerable IDP households with school going children that have people living with disabilities, single mothers, low coping capacity in Banadir IDP camps. (Target 3000hhs). Activity 1.5: Post Distribution Monitoring (Target: 5% of all beneficiaries)OBJECTIVE 2: Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings Activity 2.1: Provide agriculture inputs to riverine agro-pastoralists near Beletweyn/ Hiraan. This will be in the form of water pumps for irrigation, tools and certified seeds for seedlings. (target 500 hhs). Activity 2.2: Support fodder production in irrigated gardens/fields around sand dams and other water collection or conservation points in Hiiraan, Midle Shabelle, Galgadud and Mudug through provision of seeds, tools, irrigation materials and agricultural consultation for fodder production (target 500 HHs). Activity 2.3: Support IDPs in Banadir with training, materials, devises and marketing for IGAs that manufacture products or provide services that are requested as relief items or (humanitarian) services. Target: 300 persons OBJECTIVE 3: Support household and community resilience to shocks, through rehabilitation and/or restoration of productive assets, enhance livelihood strategies and disaster preparedness. Activity 3.1: Form multiple communities’ asset management committees and sensitize them for peaceful and transparent allocation of existing resources amongst the member communities Activity 3.2: Sensitize pastoralists on more intensive animal keeping approaches that are relatively independent from climatic shocks. Activity 3.3: Conduct community-based Disaster Risk Reduction and Sensitization sessions

Mitigation Measures

2b) Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes: I: DKH's partners will ask the communities to have women, boys and girls participating in meetings, II: In meetings, women, boys and girls will actively be involved in discussions, III: DKH's partners will pay attention that committees are gender and age group balanced composed, IV: where appropriate, women, boys and girls will be particularly asked about their consent with decisions, V: some activities are designed in a way that they require the selection of women, boys or girls as beneficiaries 3b. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. i) DKH follows a code of conduct that prohibits any sexual or other exploitation of beneficiaries, this will be adhered to by all partner and implementation staff and any deviations will be dealt with accordingly.ii) Based on DKHs code of conduct, all national partners of DKH must also have and apply a code of conduct with such content, iii) A reporting system ensures that PSEA cases get known and disciplinarily treated, if necessary prosecuted,iv) Gender parity of staff at DKH and amongst implementing partners ensures that is in direct contact with beneficiaries helps to prevent PSEA cases or to report such cases, V) partner organizations are liable for the correct behaviour of their staff. 3d. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources: i) Where multiple communities use the same resource, DKH and the partner have guidelines to form multi-communities resource management committees that allocate the resource in a transparent way to the user communities, ii) In the creation of the asset that contains the resource, members of all user communities will be involved to ensure that there is buyin through structured consultations and equal participation, iii) Target communities will be consulted before any assets development is done to ensure that they are in acceptance with the creation of identified asset(s) before works can start, iv) All targeted user communities of resources must build a covenant through participatory methods about the agreed use of the resource,v) Where possible, such agreements shall be recognized by local authorities through engagement with DKH and implementing partners

Please provide further explanation

DKH and partners also contribute with this project to the SDGs. The project will also integrate activities that lead to durable solutions in economic recovery of pastoralists and IDPs. Especially activities using the modality of cash for assets follow always a double objective: to deliver immediate live-saving assistance and to provide assets that the targeted communities need to perform their livelihoods and to become independent from climatic hazards and humanitarian aid. Therefore, a complementary effect of the project is intended to support the economic transition of pastoralists, the biggest economic group in Somalia in terms of population, from a traditional nomadic mode of production to a more intensive approach of animal husbandry, as well as to provide opportunities for those that lost their livelihoods to integrate into urban economies. In this context, DKH and its partners pay attention to the creation of awareness among targeted populations and their ability for self-management. This meets the endeavours of the Somali government to re-build an administrative structure and services that meet the needs of the people. While being focused on the delivery of humanitarian services, this project also addresses objectives of the National Development Plan.
