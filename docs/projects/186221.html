Project Summary

The high level of violence, insecurity, frequent lockdowns and ghost towns continue to contribute to deterioration of the food security situation in the North-West and South-West regions. Many areas in the North-West remain challenging to reach for humanitarian partners due to insecurity and several NSAG checkpoints. Meanwhile, transportation also remains challenging due to bad road conditions, having a negative impact on the operational humanitarian costs and on the prices of locally produced food items. Mobile communication networks remain limited, especially in rural areas, making it challenging to reach the affected population with cash assistance. The lockdowns and ghost towns make access to markets and services challenging, for the population. About 80% of farms in remote locations are inaccessible and sometimes crops are abandoned. Few markets are functioning in some divisions with partial access to regional markets. Humanitarian assistance can significantly improve the food security situation.
The number of households forced to flee their villages in search of safer areas has rapidly and steadily increased since November 2017. Almost 573.9.people have been internally displaced in the two affected regions , 383,600 returned to their communities and an additional 68600 people have sought refuge in neighboring Nigeria and are all in need humanitarian and protection assistance ( OCHA siterep September 2021) Bui and Mezam are in phase 3 (crisis situation) of the cadre harmonisee assessment (CH Oct 2021) and highest in terms of population in need. 61,500 people for Bui and 78,300 people.
80% of population in the two affected regions rely on agriculture and small trades for their livelihood. Threats and attacks continue to have negative impact on trade, markets, and access to land. Farmers, small-scale fishermen and their families find themselves in an extremely vulnerable position due to the disruption of their activities. IDPs have had to resort to negative food-related coping strategies, reducing the number of meals (to one meal a day) and adult consumption in order to feed the children. Additionally, restrictions on movements, poor infrastructures, and checkpoints continue to hinder access to land and markets.
This project aims to provide Livelihood of 500 vulnerable IDP families living in Bamenda in Mezam Divsion and in Jakiri, and Kumbo Sub Divisions in Bui Division through training and support on Market gardening, crop production and Poultry Farming
SHUMAS has been quiet involved in providing livelihood assistance to IDPs in the NWR. 80% of the IDP households who benefited from SHUMAS integrated poultry and market gardening project has had a tremendous positive impact in their livelihood. Given the continues pressure from other vulnerable persons for assistance, we intend to scale up this activity to benefit 500 additional vulnerable IDPs in Mezam (Bamenda) and Bui (Kumbo and Jakiri Sub Divisions) The 500 IDPS will be trained and supported on integrated poultry farming and market gardening in 20 sessions .
After training, each participant will receive promotional materials such as
25 three weeks chicks, 25 layers - 3 bags of feed, - 3 sachets of vegetables seeds, 2 feeder,
2 drinkers, 2 bag of manure, a hoe, a wheelbarrow, maize and potatoes seeds ,
Vulnerability targeting will be done to select the most vulnerable (IDPs living with disability, Female headed households, GBV victims , pregnant and lactating women and households headed by the elderly).
Each participant will participate by providing space for brooding, gardening and farming.
We will put in place strong outcome tracking system and backfilling. A whatsapp forum will be created to enable beneficiaries share their experiences, give their reports and market their products.
The project will be implemented for 1 year starting from February to December 2022

1 - Besoins / Needs

The NW crisis has led to a great drop in agricultural and animal productivity, availability and access to products and food stuffs. Humanitarian actors' access to such common areas has been affected, which also limits the possibility of implementing productive asset creation projects. All this, combined with the frequent and massive displacement of populations in the affected areas, leads to the abandonment of assets and huge losses of assets (productive and non-productive) and livelihoods - fields and cultivated agricultural areas, animals, pastures, housing, granaries, cereal stocks, etc. - and the loss of livelihoods. The Cadre Harmonisé, analysis in October 2022, 775696 people are currently in the crisis phase and 641,249 projected in the crisis phase as well. Analysis in the cadre harmonize also indicates 40% of IDP families adopt potentially irreversible emergency food-based coping strategies, compromising their productivity and future ability to cope with shocks. Methods of coping include relying on less preferred, less expensive food, borrowing food, reducing portion size or number of meals. 28% of IDP households in the NW resort to livelihood based coping strategies such as selling their land, begging or selling the last female animal. The project intends to improve the livelihood of 500 vulnerable IDPs and host families in Bamenda, Kumbo and Jakiri in the Northwest regions through training and support on poultry farming, crop farming and market gardening. Based on the market analysis conducted and experience, poultry farming will generate a monthly income of about 63000frs and vegetable gardening on 200 m2 land can provide a minimum income 35000 a month. . Specific objectives a) Enhance the productive capacity of 500 vulnerable IDPs and host families (350 women and 150 men) on poultry farming, (egg production and table birds) backyard gardening, crop farming, basic management and marketing skills. b) Improve the mental, emotional, physical and behavioral needs of the 500 vulnerable IDPs and family especially GBV survivors through group Provide psychosocial support from one on one and group counselling) Support them with promotional materials consisting of 25 three weeks chicks, - 3 bags of feed, - 3 sachets of vegetables seeds, crop seeds (maize and potatoes) 2 feeder, 2 drinkers, 1 bag of manure, a hoe and a wheelbarrow. d) put in place a strong outcome tracking system and backfilling when and where ever necessary. Activities will include;1 Organize a preplanning meeting with all project stakeholders to ensure everyone is on the same page 2 Carry out vulnerability registration of IDPs 3 Purchasing of training materials 4 Organize and run two days training session in a safe an easy to access location especially for those with disabilities 5 Purchase promotional materials - Distribute 25 three weeks chicks or 25 layers - 3 bags of feed, - 3 sachets of vegetables seeds, 2 feeder,2 drinkers, 1 bag of manure, a hoe, a wheelbarrow, maize seeds , and potatoes 6 Distribute the materials 7 Provide psychosocial support to 500 affected IDPs especially GBV survivors for a period of 12 months in 4 phases 8 Put in place strong outcome tracking system and backfilling when and where necessary - Carryout monthly follow-up trips to ensure all the beneficiaries are successfully implementing their activities 9 Write progress reports monthly and final reports We expect that this project will provide the following results; Outcome The beneficiaries improve their level of economic sufficiency reducing their vulnerability and enabling them to meet their daily needs The 500 beneficiaries become more confident and feel protected and safe to carry on with daily activities and integrate themselves in the society Improvement in health and nutrition.
