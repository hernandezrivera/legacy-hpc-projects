Project Summary

SDI efforts to strengthen the capacity of the education system, increase safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children aged 5-17 years and improve the quality of formal and non- formal education of children ages 5- 17 years within the protective education environment.
The project aims to help children who dropped out from schools through repairing to return to their schools where that corresponds to the priority objective of the education cluster in HRP 2019
In this program, SDI will rehabilitate 80 damaged schools in affected locations in Northeast Syria.
SDI will target the schools Idleb (Ariha – Idleb – Saraqab ) and Aleppo (Jebel Saman – Atareb - Daret Azza – Afrin) where its severity scale 5 (based on HNO2019).
The rehabilitation activities will include repairing damaged classrooms and WASH facilities; sanitation systems, bathrooms, solid waste disposals facilities, drainage facilities and adequate water for personal hygiene and to clean toilets rely on INEE standards.
After rehabilitation the schools, the project will provide furniture; such as desks, chairs, blackboards and laboratory equipment.
During implementation the project we will take into consideration gender sensitive and disability friendly that would ensure the protection needs of boys and girls as well as the needs of children with disabilities allowing adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities.

Needs

As per the HRP 2019, 2.1 million children are reported to be out-of-school and 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Most of out of school children are at primary school age and/or in hard-to-reach and besieged locations. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor. The escalation of violence has led to increasing war-related psychological trauma for children, their families and education personnel. Exposure to violence decreases attendance leads to drop out and affects learning outcomes. In some areas, over 50% of children with disability have education-related unmet needs. The high proportion of crisis-affected children and adolescents out of the public education systems is of high concern since they are at bigger protection risks situations and/or in the long term miss out important opportunities to build their future. During the eight years of crises over one in three schools were damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Consequently, many children were forced to stay out of school or if managed to access schools, they were often exposed to unsafe and overcrowded classrooms. The destruction and damages of schools have led to an acute need for additional learning spaces for both host community children and internally displaced children residing in these areas. Teaching capacity remains overstretched with more than 150,000 teachers and education staff who have left their positions since the start of the crisis, the capacity and capability of available teachers are being severely over-stretched. These shortages have limited the quality and provision of education. The changes in control in central and south Syria in early and mid-2018 as well as increasing hostilities in northwest Syria in September 2018 have caused new waves of civilian displacement into Rural Damascus, Idleb and Aleppo. Existing education capacity in these areas was already limited prior to recent displacement influx. Schools in IDP/returnee receiving communities have limited absorption capacity for newly arriving students and services are usually insufficient or non-existent. However, despite that, the shift of control have opened some accredited education opportunities to school-age children in the newly accessible areas, in the Central and South West Syria. Parallel to this, the year 2018 experienced a flow of over 987, 216 self-organized returns of IDPs and refugees from neighboring countries to areas where some stability has been restored. However education systems in those areas of return are fragmented, school facilities are already overcrowded and poorly equipped and overburdened with multiple needs and limited resources to absorb the increasing numbers of returnee students. Therefore such shortfalls require redressal through education systems strengthening, school and classrooms rehabilitation, capacity building of teachers and facilitation for the reintegration of refugee returnee children back to school through advocacy, coordination with the Ministry of Education to scale up access to education for both returnee and crisis-affected children.ٍAccording to the results of the assessment which SDI has conducted it in Idlib and Aleppo, we found as follow: 100% of schools surveyed need restoration work. half of the schools damaged more than 25% and need to rehabilitate theirs. 52 % of children in schools with special needs and 33% of children have chronic diseases. In addition, most of the schools need to have windows, blackboard, lighting, rehabilitation of classrooms, roofs, floors and sanitation systems. To design the program, we consulted local authorities and other actors in the areas including women, men and older person by holding the meetings and focal groups discussion in order to involve them in the designing process of project activities.

Implementation of activities

SDI will rehabilitate 80 damaged schools in the most affected locations in northeast Syria. SDI will target the schools in (Aleppo / Afrin district - Idlib Al-Maa'ra and Ariha) where its severity scale 5 (based on HNO2019).Schools will be repaired to the targeted areas that promote equity and the physical safety of learners, teachers and other education personnel. Schools will be designed giving careful thought to who uses the learning space. Spaces will be appropriate to the sex, age, physical ability and cultural considerations of all users. Entrances and exits will be repaired to allow students, teachers and other education personnel to exit safely in an emergency. The rehabilitation activities will include repairing damaged classrooms, roofs, floors and WASH facilities; such as sanitation systems, bathrooms (Girls, boys and disabilities), solid waste disposals facilities, drainage facilities and providing adequate water for personal hygiene and to clean toilets rely on INEE standards. Sanitation facilities will be accessible for persons with disabilities and will maintain privacy, dignity and safety. Toilet doors will lock from the inside. To prevent sexual harassment and abuse, separate toilets for boys/men and girls/women will be located in safe, convenient and easily accessible places.After rehabilitation the schools, the project will provide furniture; such as desks, chairs, blackboards and laboratory equipment. The rehabilitation activities will be carried out by SDI staff. Rehabilitation needs and related costs will be assessed and on the basis of which a targeted rehabilitation plan is designed and then implemented. During implementation the project we will take into consideration gender sensitive and disability friendly that would ensure the protection needs of boys and girls as well as the needs of children with disabilities allowing adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities. M&E activities:Monitoring and evaluation will be a dynamic part of this project. The M&E team will implement the monitoring and evaluation plan of the project to measure the project progress and to evaluate the impact of the project so that the project manager can obtain evidence-based information of the project’s outcome, outputs and overall performance and specify whether activities are carried out successfully and whether the project is going according to the work plan and reporting any changes. To ensure project compliance with the adopted plan and producing the expected outputs.Satisfaction surveys will be conducted to evaluate the quality of rehabilitation and to learn how beneficiaries benefited from rehabilitated schools.M&E field assistants will conduct Focus Group Discussions (FGD) in order to find up how much the overall project is successful as a whole (the type of services intervention, project duration, project start date, etc.) and for learn lessons to improve similar projects in future.Complementary: SDI will coordinate with WASH cluster for sanitation messages and Health cluster for health issues. SDI has a project in community health where will target the children in the rehabilitated schools to achieve effectiveness and complementarity in the SDI programs.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Security accidents or fires happening inside the learning spaces Mitigating measure: All staff, guards, cleaners and facilitators must be trained on safety and security standards and fire fighting. Risk: Community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighbouring community not served/cannot access or if information offered is not consistent between communities. Mitigating Measures: Continuously ensure service mapping, consultation with community on program interventions, regular communication with different communities on availability of services including host communities and returnee populations. Expansion of services to non-camp locations where needs are present, to avoid concentration of services in camps. Monitoring Mechanism: Regular consultations with communities, FGDs, feedback boxes Risk: Resistance from local authorities to accessing and implementing programming. Mitigating measures: SDI has established relationships with relevant local authorities and stakeholders, such as local council, education directorate, ministries, community gatekeepers and religious leaders. Monitoring Mechanism: Regular meetings with relevant authorities. Risk: Affected populations and SDI staff, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment. Mitigating measures: Training will be provided to SDI staff anti-Harassment Policy and Code of Conduct.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: Access to targeted areas remains possible and safe. Risk1: Access to one (or more) of targeted areas becomes hindered or insecure. Likelihood: medium. Effect: medium. Mitigation: SDI will advise staff on alternative routes to mitigate the risk. If the security situation changes in a certain area, we can wait or postpone activities for a few days or change targeted areas to another accessible area in need of support. Assumption 2: Skilled and unskilled workers can be hired from the local market to complete rehabilitation works. Risk: It might be difficult to find enough qualified technicians and engineers to implement the rehabilitation works, Likelihood: low. Effect: low. Mitigation: contract and recruit high-quality workers from SDI roster or hire from a wider geographic range if needed. Assumption3: Rehabilitation items and materials are available to be procured locally. Risk 3: It might become difficult to locally secure one (or more) of the needed rehabilitation items/services of good quality or qualified workshops to implement the rehabilitation works. Likelihood: medium. Effect: high. Mitigation3: Social Development International (SDI) through previous experience was able to directly purchase the materials from larger markets within Syria and transport them to the project sites. Moreover, SDI will seek to contract high-capacity trusted vendors who can deliver the required rehabilitation works in Syria effectively.
