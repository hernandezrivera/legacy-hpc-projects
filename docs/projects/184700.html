Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains dire with humanitarian disasters alerts being sounded in Jubaland state among other locations. A number of shocks experienced in 2021, among them flooding, drought, Locust infestation, COVID 19 pandemic, persistent WASH challenges, conflict between the state government and Federal government, have worsened the humanitarian situation in Jubaland state, specifically ,Gedo region.
Conflict and insecurity in Gedo region have forced hundreds of thousands of people to flee their homes in 2021 and are expected to remain key drivers of displacement in 2022. Conflict-induced shocks exacerbate the humanitarian situation of both IDP and host communities. According to JMNCA, in 2022 approximately 1,619,415 IDPs and 4,818,944 vulnerable host communities will require lifesaving WASH assistance due to shocks experienced in 2021. Currently About 6.1 million people in Somalia are in the Severe category” who lack access to safe water, proper sanitation services and are highly vulnerable to Acute Water Diarrheal (AWD) and cholera while around 379,757 people (2 percent) are in “Extreme condition as they access water directly from rivers and surface bonds, practice open defecation and lack access to soap and other essential hygiene materials and are susceptible to communicable diseases.
The proposed implementation strategy is to deliver lifesaving WASH assistance to the most vulnerable populations in Jubaland state, Gedo, who have been affected by the deteriorating humanitarian condition impacted by drought, conflict, Locust infestation and Covid 19 pandemic. The strategy aims to ease the suffering of 5500 affected households by recurring humanitarian disasters in Jubaland state, through improving and enhancing access to emergency basic WASH services.
This will be done through provision of emergency water access using water trucking to the 1500 families; Construction of 2 new water infrastructures equipped with appropriate pumping and power systems, tanks and distribution networks;Construct rehabilitate 2 solar powered boreholes and construct 10 berkerds in strategic locations based on the WASH guidelines; Construct 70 ( gender and disability sensitive) pit latrines in Gedo to promote safe defecation and promote good hygiene practices; Establish and train 12 Water Resource Management committee who will be responsible for the management and maintenance of the constructed berkerds and water infrastructure . Women will be allocated 50% representation in all committees; Distribute 1000 ceramic water filters at household level to improve water treatment; Engage men, women, boys and girls in 20 hygiene promotion strategic locations such as the market place, health centers and schools

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash programming has contributed to greater level of accountability through electronic reporting, local ownership engagement, building local coping mechanism and support cohesion and transition to development. The project will target vulnerable households from both IDP and host communities who have an able-bodied member that can engage in CFW activities. In WASH, Cashfor work will be adopted for construction and rehabilitation of water facilities, rehabilitation of latrines , construction of garbage pits and community clean up campaigns. E vouchers on the other hand will be used for distribution of water , Do No Harm approach will be applied to ensure that the interventions do not cause or fuel conflict. The aim of Cash programming approach is to strengthen livelihoods at household level, giving families an income through which they can be able to meet other basic needs including water access

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The proposed strategy was premeditated in a way that will strengthen the demand and supply chain in the target locations and boost the local economy. All construction materials and project equipment and supplies will be sourced locally in the target location to boost the local economy , Cash based modality for work in construction of WASH facilities will strengthen livelihoods at household level, giving families an income through which they can be able to meet other basic needs including food

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

DF has a good standing with communities where we work. On the basis of this, DF has a fair understanding of the current sensitivities surrounding ‘conflict prevention’ in Somalia. Recognizing these complexities and sensitivities, DF has developed risk mitigation strategies that have been utilized to enable project implementation. First, DF employs national staff who understand the local context and are well known and respected by local authorities. DF national staff can access locations that non-Somali nationals cannot access, hence ensuring continuous project implementation and monitoring even in challenging contexts. Further, DF also employs remote monitoring systems to follow up ad monitor projects where physical access is a challenge. Second is a deliberate approach to project branding: selecting language that will explain the project fairly and accurately, in a way that will be acceptable to stakeholders. Third, is an approach of open, ongoing stakeholder engagement involving DF, local communities and government. Regular security updates will be sought from International NGO safety organization security teams to ensure that staff are well informed on security situation for necessary precautions and arrangements.The emergency response will (as far as local conditions allow) meet minimum SPHERE standards, work within the framework of Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) and Do No Harm (DNH) to ensure beneficiaries wholesomely participate in project planning and implementation. The program will promote empowerment of women by creating greater opportunity for their involvement in key institutions and decision-making processes for instance in the community water project management committees and through the cash-based interventions. It will identify gender roles and responsibilities; access to and control over household productive assets and resources; household decision-making and risk management strategies (savings and contingency funds); access to and control of program benefits; effective participation in decision-making of both genders in community-level structures; women taking up leadership and securing their positions in the labor force through cash for work, which is currently skewed towards men; enabling boys and girls (youth) to participate in program activities and in matters that affect their lives and livelihoods.
