Project Summary

Approximately 2.7 million people require access to WASH support in Somalia. In drought affected regions, water scarcity and inadequate quality are leading causes of displacement and conflict. Of the people in need and highly vulnerable, a large percentage are children, women and the elderly, who critically lack access to clean water and are acutely at risk of gender-related violence and high morbidity and mortality due to water-borne disease outbreaks. Many households lack access to adequate sanitation leading to frequent open defecation while safety of people, particularly women and children to access latrines and water points remains a serious concern and a serious risk for exposure to violence. IDPs in particular are the most impact by an inability to access WASH services. Most of the primary IDP settlements are in Crisis or Stressed in Laas Cananood and Baidoa.
In this context, ACTED will implement a WASH cluster-specific intervention for IDPs and rural communities in areas affected by drought aiming to 1) Provide reliable and sustainable access to sufficient safe water, based on identified strategic water points and construction of new boreholes; 2) Establish an appropriate system for the management and maintenance of facilities, with different groups contributing equitably; and 3) Enhance community sanitation.

Project Activities

Water point construction In order to provide reliable and sustained access to sufficient safe water to vulnerable communities affected by water scarcity, strategic locations will be identified in consultation with the local water authorities and in coordination with the WASH cluster. Locations will be targeted as a priority intervention area based upon the communities’ severe WASH vulnerability and food insecurity; lack of available WASH infrastructure in the area, coupled with high population density; and the absence of a permanent water source. As first step of the construction process, ACTED will undertake hydrogeological surveys in the two selected locations. Subject to the results of the surveys, drillings operations will be launched through a shortlisted/prequalified drilling contractor, followed by equipment of the boreholes with a pump and generator set. Infrastructure will be provided at site level, including elevated water tank, water kiosk, operators room and livestock troughs. Water point rehabilitation ACTED will rehabilitate a critical water infrastructure facility ensuring that the rehabilitated source is accessible to the beneficiaries and benefits the wider community. The intervention location will be identified in consultation with the local authorities, othering intervening agencies and the WASH cluster, based on the following criteria: the targeted groundwater source is strategic (serve a large number of people); it is damaged by the drought (especially due to overuse) and in need of repair. ACTED will aim to rehabilitate a four boreholes serving approximately 20000 people. A technical assessment will be conducted with local authorities, to assess key features of the borehole. The rehabilitation works may entail construction of a water kiosk, supply and installation of solar system to have hybrid system, submersible pump and construction of water pipeline and other rehabilitation works as necessary. Capacity building (water committees and WASH training) ACTED will provide capacity building and trainings to community members in order to ensure the sustainability of community water management systems. ACTED will select/revive and train five water management committees (WMC) in the targeted communities – one in each location. WMCs trainings will aim to reinforce committee’s skills so they are able to ensure good leadership and management; that they are aware of good hygiene practices; and that the water facilities are protected from contamination. Latrine construction or rehabilitation: ACTED will construct latrines to benefit IDP and rural households located in the proximity of the new/rehabilitated water points, in order to ensure safe excreta disposal. Informal IDP settlements will be prioritized for this intervention. Latrines will be built in blocks of 3. As per WASH cluster standards, each latrine will serve up to 8 households. ACTED will construct long term locations type of latrines with a pit 3 m deep, 1 m wide and 1 m long for each toilet. At each latrine block, a handwashing station will be installed. ACTED will undertake the construction of the latrines by contracting a private operator. ACTED WASH technical team will supervise the construction works. Due to dense population in IDPs settlements, pit latrine will be expected to fill up and thus, communities will be supported to desludge at least once, as part of the project cost. Based on gender, a privacy screen will be provided and a shelf for soap. These aspects will be agreed during participatory design sessions with communities. Finally, to ensure that the latrines are protected and are gender sensitive, solar lights will be provided, in and outside of the facilities to ensure 24-hour usage. ACTED will engage the community in deciding where the lighting of the latrines will be installed. Both women and men will be consulted before the lights are installed. 10% latrines will be suitable for use by people with a disability.

Mitigation Measures

ACTED will target water assets which will serve both displaced communities and rural communities. Strategic targeting of water assets in underserved areas ensures an inclusive approach. If WASH management governance structures in place are not inclusive, they will be integrated so that marginalized groups of the population will also be represented. WASH Management Committees and communities will be empowered through capacity building activities and dissemination on information on services available as well as rights of the affected population. A CFM mechanism for the targeted locations will provide individuals to present complaints, including reporting discriminatory delivery of humanitarian assistance. Trainings conducted by ACTED in for WMCs have specific modules on Protection and GBV mainstreaming and through pre and post training test ACTED will be able to measure if improvement of knowledge occurred. If WASH management governance structures in place are not inclusive, they will be integrated so in order to equally represent different ages and gender of the affected population. A CFM mechanism for the targeted locations will provide individuals to present complaints, including reporting discriminatory delivery of humanitarian assistance. Trainings conducted by ACTED in for WMCs have specific modules on Protection and GBV mainstreaming and through pre and post training test ACTED will be able to measure if improvement of knowledge occurred. The latrines and water points will be constructed and rehabilitated in consultation with communities to ensure that they account for the needs of men, women, boys and girls, and address their specific needs. ACTED will work target rural and IDP communities to ensure that the resources do not exacerbate resources competition. Ensuring equitable access between groups aims to ensure the sustainable use and management of the water assets. ACTED will regularly report significant issues to the National and Sub National Cluster. Whenever possible, ACTED will try to expand on-going activities in order to target newly formed sites not receiving assistance. When not possible, ACTED will advocate for new funds and ensure to report to other humanitarian actors the most urgent needs of the sites.

Please provide further explanation

ACTED aims to target WASH assets and establish WMCs in an effort to ensure that the assets established are durable and that the committees have the requisite skills to manage, operate and maintain the assets. Water Management Committee (WMC) will be established or revived each comprising of 5 members and will be trained on operation and maintenance to ensure sustainability of WASH infrastructure assets after the life of the project. They will also be trained on water treatment, safe water handling and storage practices. The WMCs will participate in water chlorination and maintenance of hand washing facilities and latrines in sites through replenishing soap and water. ACTED will sensitize communities maintenance and rehabilitation of water points, under the supervision of WMCs. As part of ACTED’s exit strategy, the rehabilitated assets will be handed over to community representatives in both areas and will be managed by the WMCs, thereby ensuring the sustainability of the action. On water treatment, ACTED will treat the water at the source following WHO drinking water standards. Chlorine will be procured through the WASH cluster and used for boreholes and shallow wells directly through a bulk chlorination process. Moreover, WMCs will be trained in both locations and will be able to replicate the chlorination methodology, thereby ensuring sustainability of the action. ACTED will establish MoUs for the WMCs to ensure that the interests of the community and the responsibilities of the WMC members are formalized.
