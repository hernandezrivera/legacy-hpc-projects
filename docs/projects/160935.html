Project Summary

In 2020, Mercy Corps planned to implement a comprehensive water, sanitation and hygiene promotion interventions to reduce and eliminate diarrheal disease transmission and reduce the burden of morbidity and mortality associated with faecal-oral infections. Mercy Corps know that rapid response of effective WASH infrastructure and services can prevent or lessen the impact of diarrheal outbreaks that can exacerbate the human suffering accompanying humanitarian crises. Mercy Corps Nigeria short term summary of intervention are Providing Safe clean water, provision of improved Sanitation facilities, Promotion of sustainable Hygiene best practices and behavior change, Environmental management, vector control, capacity building and good governance.
The Primary purpose of Mercy Corps WASH intervention is to reduce morbidity, mortality and malnutrition from Water- borne diseases through safe and appropriate access to clean drinking water, hygiene promotion, Waste management, distribution WASH NFI’s and appropriate access to sanitation infrastructure and facilities.
Mercy Corps WASH intervention primarily targets the affected population of Internally Displaces Persons (IDP’s) returnee and Host communities

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis has dramatically affected access to key water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services in North East Nigeria especially Eastern, Southern and central Borno state. As the result of poor access of WASH services by the IDP’s, returnees and Host communities, there is high morbidity, mortality and malnutrition rates among the affected communities. Across Northeast Nigeria, almost 2.5 million people are in need of safe water , access to improved sanitation facilities and sustainable adoption to key hygiene best practices. In IDP camps as well as among the host population in Dikwa, Ngala, Damboa and Gwoza, access to safe drinking water is limited and sanitation including hygiene best practices is poor. As the result of high influx of new arrivals from insecure LGA’s, inaccessible areas and settlements, this further straining the limited existing WASH facilities within the IDP camps and Host communities. The average allocation of water currently ranges between 10 to 13 liters per person per day, below the minimum standard of 15 liters per person per day, while IDP’s are sharing a drop-hole latrine with an average of 72 people, below the minimum standard of 50 people per drop-hole latrine. Women and girls still spend a long-time queuing at the water points within IDP camps and Host communities. Moreover, water scarcity still remains a key driver of conflict among IDP’s Returnees and host communities.The 2018 cholera outbreak across the Borno states has been particularly deadly. At least 4,250 cases reported, including 112 associated deaths, with women and children disproportionally affected . This calls for an intensive hygiene promotion and education to the IDP’s and Host communities for sustainable adoption of key hygiene best practices by the IDP’s and Host community members. The emergency nutrition situation faced by newly-arrived children from inaccessible areas is of critical concern, with an average GAM prevalence of 11 percent and above in Borno State. Those proxy indicators reveal a serious nutrition status and an urgent need to improve WASH conditions which are key to cutting malnutrition rates, as exemplified in malnutrition-diarrheal diseases cycle. Furthermore, Lack of access to safe water and other WASH facilities poses specific protection risks including sexual violence and harassment to girls and women. Mercy Corps, therefore, propose to continue with comprehensive water, sanitation and hygiene promotion interventions to address the alarming, prevailing, and emerging acute humanitarian needs of IDPs, returnees, Host communities and other vulnerable populations in Ngala, Damboa, Gwoza, and Dikwa LGAs.

2 - Activities

Mercy Corps WASH activities shall be addressing three key thematic areas of: 1) Supply of Safe clean water, 2) Provision of improved sanitation facilities and 3) Hygiene promotion.1) Supply of safe clean water:i) Rehabilitation and construction of water points.To reduce morbidity, mortality and malnutrition from water-borne diseases amongst IDPs and host communities and to ensure conflict affected communities have access to adequate supplies of safe water, Mercy Corps will rehabilitate or construct water points to address the alarming, prevailing, and emerging acute humanitarian needs of IDPs and other vulnerable populations in Ngala, Damboa, Gwoza, and Dikwa LGAs. These activities will take place in both IDP camps and host communities through contract driven approach. ii) Formations and training of WASHCOMs for sustainability: Sustainable water sources access will not be possible unless communities themselves take the responsibility for the operation and maintenance of the rehabilitated/constructed water points. Mercy Corps, will ensure the sustainability of the intervention by reviving and/or selecting and training members of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene Committees (WASHCOMs), pump mechanics and pump attendants from within the affected communities.iii) Water Quality Measures: Mercy Corps will ensure that the water provided for communities meets acceptable WHO drinking water standards of zero E.Coli contamination per 100 ml of water sample. Mercy Corps’ WASH technical team will take water samples from various water sources, household water containers, water vendors (donkey carts and water trucks) for water quality testing. The key parameters to be monitored are ( E-coli form, PH, Turbidity, Free residual chlorine, Total chlorine ,temperature and other dissolved chemical parameters.iv) Safe clean Water trucking for new arrivals and IDP’sMercy Corps will conduct water trucking for the new arrivals to support the already stressed existing water points to try meet the high demand of safe water for the new arrivals. Water trucking will be conducted through contract driven approach . Mercy Corps WASH technical team will ensure that the water supply through trucking is of good quality and will monitor the quality of water( Water treatment and monitoring the free chlorine residual) 2) Sanitation i) Provision of improved sanitation facilities.To address identified sanitation gaps in IDP Camps in Dikwa, Ngala Damboa and Gwoza LGAs, Mercy Corps will ensure that the affected communities have full access to improved sanitation facilities that are secure, sanitary, user friendly, and gender appropriate in all the targeted IDP camps and host communities by supporting the construction &rehabilitation of latrines. For host community settings, Mercy Corps will construct single stance latrines that are to be shared by three households each. In camp and camp-like settings, Mercy Corps will construct blocks of emergency communal latrines (VIP latrines), and shower shelters through contract driven approach. 3 ) Hygiene promotion To deliver the hygiene messaging, MC will make use of hygiene promoters conducting hygiene campaigns in both IDP camps and host communities in the targeted LGAs of Gwoza, Dikwa, Ngala, and Damboa. The CHP's will be selected in the target locations and receive a thorough five-day training at the beginning of the program. The trained CHP will conduct door-to-door hygiene sensitization in both IDP camps and host communities.ii) Cholera preparedness response plan, prevention intervention and awareness:Mercy Corps intervention will be cholera preparedness, respond plan; community awareness and prevention intervention that will contribute to Health and WASH cluster objectives by including activities related to surveillance, case management, capacity building, infection prevention and control, and public awareness in all the targeted areas/ LGA’s.iii) Distribution of Hygiene kits to the new arrivals.
