1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The gap of assisted families with immediate shelter NFIs and Emergency Shelters is still big and the number of IDPs continues to increase especially in Cabo Delgado, a further expansion in humanitarian capacity and presence on the ground is critical to prevent a further deterioration of the humanitarian situation. In this HRP for 2022, IOM aims to assist 37.389HHs with essential household items and support 6,500 HHs with construction, retrofitting and/or upgrading of shelters that withstand harsh weather conditions which will contribute to secure safe, dignified, and healthy living conditions of the population affected by the insecurity crisis, but also that are extremely vulnerable to natural hazards. In addition to these efforts, IOM will ensure that local artisans and population, increases their knowledge and applies the Build Back Better principles. In addition, to the regular programming a common shelter and Non Food Item (NFI) pipeline is managed by the Shelter Cluster Lead Agency (IOM in this case), assuring harmonization of items and sustained availability of core items, intending to reach more than 69,444 individuals (13.889HHs) aiming to provide the gap in shelter-NFIs that partners require to meet their target with items being prepositioned ahead of time increasing readiness to respond.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM is implementing the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) which enables the humanitarian community to identify priorities and activities through multi-sectoral assessments that take into consideration gaps in humanitarian service coverage, including health, WASH, protection and shelter services. The DTM displacement monitoring and emergency tracking tools will further inform humanitarian programming of protection risks and needs for better targeted and evidence based interventions ultimately contributing to more secure settlements for affected populations.Regarding project design, IOM ensures that the shelter Kit content is tailor made to mitigate protection risks and takes into consideration the needs of vulnerable groups.Greater protection concerns are mainstreamed into the multi-sectoral teams (in addition to having protection representation within IOM structure), who conduct referrals and services specifically targeted for those vulnerable persons. IOM is an active member of the Protection cluster and shelter-protection assessment visits inclusive of HH surveys that focus on vulnerability criteria and protection aspects are performed to ensure integration of these aspects into the design of the program activities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

IOM with support from other clusters, have conducted multisectoral community engagement exercises which include focal group discussions, visits from the affected communities, community and household assessments, joint multisectoral immediate response assistance (Food, hygiene and shelter) etc. to understand their most immediate pressing shelter and NFI needs and for those displaced, the intentions of the communities to resettle into sites either set up by the Government (to be verified that have meet the minimum standards), or plots provided by the Government within the host community. Communities will be engaged in on the job training exercises with the intention to give guidance on the use of the shelter materials and improved construction techniques. A rights- and community-based participatory approach is essential to create meaningful involvement by displaced residents in protection and assistance activities. Land tenure verification will be conducted in consultation with community leadership and validated with community members and with local authorities. The community’s leadership, but also other representatives of men, women and youth, elderly and persons with disabilities will be included in the design of programming and assistance activities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities will be targeted and have a higher score while selecting the most vulnerable houses. These people, if with no labour capacity, will receive technical and labour support for the construction of their shelters. And estimated 20% of the population with disabilities will have been assisted under our programing.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

IOM will seek strict adherence to the Sphere Standards for Humanitarian Assistance which identify the environment as a cross-cutting issue to be incorporated to all humanitarian assistance. In that sense, IOM will encourage the use of assessment tools to assess the environmental impact of the construction related program activities. This tools focus from the selection of the shelter sites, the construction of structures reviewing the possible negative environmental impacts, the management considerations while occupied and also considers the possible decommissioning or dismantling of the structures to reutilize the resources. The use of this tools will engage local actors and communities to understand the different conditions particular to the working areas.
