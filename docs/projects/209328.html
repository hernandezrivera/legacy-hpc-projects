Project Summary

20.3 million People in Sudan are now experiencing high levels on food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+), this accounts for 42% of the total population. The highest levels of severity and prevalence of food insecurity in Sudan are reported in the Greater Darfur. The implications of the ongoing war including displacement and disruption to agricultural production are likely to exacerbate the situation. The target localities in this project demonstrate high intercluster severity of need and number of people in need (West Darfur: Forobaranga 5 and 65,099 PiN, Habila 4 and 114,439 PiN, and Kerenik 4 and 202,784 PiN; and Central Darfur: Zalinge 5 and 452,985 PiN, Azum 4 and 149,402 PiN, Gharb Jabel Marra 4 and 172,045 PiN, and Wadi-salih 3 and 175,788 PiN). All the targeted localities are at IPC 3 (crisis) except for Zalinge which is at IPC 4 (emergency). This project aims to respond to this crisis through provision with emergency FSL support focused on lifesaving and life-sustenance of people in need. Lifesaving agricultural support will be through provision of farming inputs and protection of livestock assets of affected populations. In addition, livelihood restoration interventions e.g. goat restocking and income generating activities especially targeting women and youth. Further, unconditional cash transfer will target the most vulnerable food insecure population as essential life-sustaining support.
The project objective is to improve the food and livelihood security and wellbeing of 182,400 conflict-affected person, including IDPs (76,920), host communities and vulnerable residents (60,000 and 45,480) in West Darfur’s Forobaranga, Habila and Kerenik localities (West Darfur total: 76,200) and Central Darfur’s Azum, Gharb Jabel Marra, Wadi-Salih and Zalinge localities (Central Darfur total: 106200). The project will achieve its objective through implementing emergency and lifesaving interventions to improve agricultural production, protect livelihood assets, diversify income sources, and provide immediate support in the form of cash to vulnerable people. The project interventions include the following:
1) Provision with farming inputs such as seed (food and cash crops), tools, irrigation equipment and extension packages to 12,065 HHs (72,390 individuals)
2) Support pastoral and agro-pastoral HHs (625) with livestock restocking and feeding programs (3750 individuals), focusing on women and youth
3) Livestock voucher treatment program and vaccination campaigns to reach 16400 pastoral and agro pastoral HHs (98400 individuals), targeting 820,000 heads of animals
4) Training and provision of start-up kits to 60 HHs (360 individuals) focusing on female-headed families to provide alternative income generating activities such as milk and leather processing, soap making, sewing and perfume making
5) Unconditional cash transfer to 1250 HHs (7500 persons) to meet immediate needs of vulnerable individuals included persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women and malnourished children

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF G will ensure to integrate the protection mainstreaming in this project focused on improving food and livelihood security in Central and West Darfur states through incorporating measures to identify, prevent, and respond to protection risks and concerns. The project will ensure that vulnerable populations are safeguarded, and that the interventions promote the dignity, safety, and rights of all individuals involved. VSF G will conduct comprehensive conflict and vulnerability assessments to understand the context and potential sources of tension in the project areas, and to identify groups or individuals at higher risk, such as women, children, elderly, or marginalized communities, especially among IDPs. VSF G will also analyze potential protection risks, including gender-based violence, discrimination, or any other threats that may arise during the project implementation and develop mitigation and management strategies for each challenge that may occur. Along with that VSF G will encourage community engagement and participation throughout the project life cycle and involve them in decision-making to ensure their needs and concerns are well considered. VSF G monitoring, evaluation and feedback and response mechanisms are in place to allow community members to voice their opinions and report protection concerns. Conflict and gender-sensitive approaches are well integrated in VSF G programming, recognizing and addressing the specific needs and vulnerabilities of different community groups as well as women and men in the target communities. VSF G in coordination with partners provide capacity building training for project team on protection principles, including the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. VSF G will continue to coordinate with the protection sector to ensure complementarity to create a safe and supportive environment for vulnerable populations in Central and West Darfur.

VSF G has clear guidelines for receiving, processing and responding to community’s feedback and complaints. The CRM involves phone calls, SMS, Focus Group Discussions, as well as from suggestion boxes and through monitoring missions. Focal person(s) collect and process different types of feedback from individuals or community groups and channels them as appropriate to the project team or senior management to address the issue or make a response. Feedback related to implementation approaches or quality are considered to contextualize and further adjust or improve the program.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Selection and prioritization of all project beneficiaries will be through a participatory strategy that will engage beneficiaries at every stage of the project to make them of the active stakeholders in the project. With the skills development for beneficres , the project beneficiaries will be facilitated with equipment so during the 3rd month of the project will conduct focus Group Discussing with the beneficiaries especially with trained beneficiaries will be meet regularly to discuss how they are progressing and what kind of support they need from other stakeholders, Feedback and complaints will be regularly collected from beneficiaries directly through community and 1-to-1 meetings in addition that accountability number will be assigned and maintain by M&E staff the number will be shared with beneficiaries in all project sites to provide feedback and raise complaints. All feedback and complaints received will be recorded on the form categorized and accordingly shared with respective VSF staff for action. Details about the feedback and complaint cases will be logged on the VSF CRM for follow up and implementation.Prior to the conduction of all scheduled trainings to the target community, FGDs and one-on-one discussions will be conducted with each category to understand their special needs and concerns that may need the other stakeholders to be aware of. This document will be kept as a confidential document so that their personal concerns will not be exposed to others. Considering the sensitivity involved in dealing with the beneficiaries, beneficiaries will be formed into self-help groups (5 households in one group) based on the type of activities and groups formulated they are engaged in. Any field visits by FSL Officer or community mobilizer from other stakeholders will be accompanies by another group members. When a formulated farmer group or IGAs of beneficiaries start working after the training, there will be monthly reviews in which the project beneficiaries will discuss about their challenges and how they could be supported through groups.
