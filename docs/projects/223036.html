Project Summary

UNHCR’s strategic vision is to provide services that follow a public health approach by integrating health and nutrition services to foster access and ensure a continuum of care for beneficiaries, in line with UNHCR’s public health strategy and nutrition sector strategy. In collaboration with UNICEF and WFP, UNHCR and its partners will implement comprehensive nutrition programs that focus on treatment and prevention, with the overall objective of reducing the impact of acute malnutrition among Rohingya refugees and improving the nutritional well-being of children and women.
These services will be delivered through 11 integrated nutrition centers across 8 camps, as well as two stabilization centers, which will be integrated with primary healthcare facilities. Treatment programs will follow the Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) approach, including community outreach to ensure timely identification and referral to appropriate nutrition programs, such as the Stabilization Center (SC), Outpatient Therapeutic Feeding Program (OTP), and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP). Preventive programs will be strengthened through Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programs (BSFP), Growth Monitoring and Promotion (GMP), and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) programs, using both group sessions and individual counseling. Additionally, UNHCR will expand support for children under five months and their mothers through Community Management of At-risk Mothers and Infants (CMAMI).
Based on monitoring and assessments of staff capacity, ongoing capacity building and mentoring of partner staff on key nutrition programs—as well as on cross-cutting issues—will be conducted to enhance the integration of nutrition programs with other sectors. Community engagement efforts will continue, with partners working to strengthen participation through community dialogues with block leaders, majhis, and fathers of children receiving nutrition support.
To achieve the overall objective of improving nutrition among children under five, collaboration with other sectors (protection, health, WASH, food security, livelihoods, and education) will be essential to enhance humanitarian efforts and ensure a multi-sectoral approach to addressing high malnutrition levels. UNHCR will lead the implementation of annual nutrition surveys following the Standard Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) methodology and will support other nutrition sector assessments.
In 2025, UNHCR and partners will reinforce program monitoring and make necessary adjustments to ensure the delivery of high-quality programs and accountability to the affected population. There will be an increased emphasis on nutrition-sensitive programs, disability inclusion, and the Age, Gender, and Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) approach across all nutrition centers, in collaboration with other sectors. UNHCR and its partners will adhere to the standard of national, global, and Nutrition Sector-recommended guidelines for the implementation of the program activities. UNHCR will ensure ongoing collaboration with the Nutrition Sector by actively participating in its initiatives and program interventions. As a key member of the SAG and the AIM, IYCF, and CMAM Technical Working Groups, UNHCR will play an active role in contributing to sector priorities and technical discussions.
UNHCR will continue supporting the Government’s localization strategy by engaging a national NGO and building its capacity to deliver comprehensive nutrition programs, with the goal of expanding coverage in the future. Given that the Cox’s Bazar camps are frequently affected by monsoon rains, cyclones, and fire outbreaks, UNHCR will work with the Nutrition Sector and partners to coordinate emergency response plans. This includes establishing mobile nutrition teams (MNTs) and ensuring adequate supplies and funding to rehabilitate nutrition centers, thereby safeguarding the uninterrupted delivery of life-saving nutrition services.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The malnutrition situation in the Rohingya refugee camps remains critically high based on the UNICEF/WHO classification of acute malnutrition. According to the 2023 Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS), the overall prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) among children aged 6-59 months is 15.1%, while Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence was at 2.0%, and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) was at 13.1%. Chronic malnutrition (stunting) is at 41.3%, exceeding the 30% critical threshold. Anemia among children under five also remains a public health concern and the prevalence was 38.1% in unregistered camps and 40.1% in registered camps. The anemia prevalence is notably high among children aged 6-23 months, with a prevalence of 56% in unregistered camps and 60% in registered camps of Cox’s Bazar. Contributing factors to high malnutrition levels include poor sanitation, vulnerability to infections, and inadequate maternal and caregiver support.In response, UNHCR and its partners are committed to strengthening programs targeting malnutrition among children under five years, integrating gender-responsive approaches to meet the unique needs of vulnerable groups. Programmatic efforts will include enhanced support for adolescent girls, medical cases, older adults, and persons with specific needs. Given the high nutritional vulnerability of adolescent girls—who face unique risks related to reproductive health—programs will emphasize age-appropriate interventions, including tailored nutrition support and protection considerations to address their specific needs.To ensure quality nutrition services, UNHCR will provide funding and technical capacity to train nutrition staff and volunteers according to established protocols. Monitoring systems will be strengthened to allow timely adjustments, ensuring accountability and the delivery of high-quality programs. All interventions will incorporate the age, gender, and diversity mainstreaming (AGDM) approach, with a focus on nutrition-sensitive programming, disability inclusion, and gender-responsive service delivery across all nutrition centers.UNHCR will also support the Government’s localization strategy by engaging a national NGO partner to expand service coverage, prioritizing community-led and culturally sensitive interventions. UNHCR-funded nutrition programs aim to serve an estimated 103,865 beneficiaries, including 40,774 children under five years, 26,695 adolescent girls, and 24,396 pregnant and lactating women, aligning with sectoral needs assessments and gender-specific priorities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection partners implementing UNHCR-funded programs will ensure protection mainstreaming in all components to ensure the "Do No Harm" principle is achieved in service delivery to all beneficiaries receiving support through the integrated nutrition centers. Assessments of the partner’s understanding of key protection principles and mainstreaming will be jointly implemented by UNHCR's protection and nutrition units. Training on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), gender-based violence (GBV), child protection, and referral pathways between nutrition and protection in the camps will be carried out to ensure the necessary staff capacity to support the refugees to access services in a dignified manner. In addition, other components identified as potential protection risks will be monitored and mitigation strategies identified. Regular monitoring of protection-related issues will be ensured throughout project implementation, as well as necessary actions to prevent GBV experienced by women, girls, boys, or men receiving assistance through nutrition programs.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNHCR is committed to ensuring that its nutrition programs are inclusive, accessible, and protective for beneficiaries of all ages and abilities. While the primary focus remains on children aged 0–59 months, adolescent girls, and pregnant and lactating women, the program will actively engage and address the needs of all age groups. Community awareness sessions and group discussions will incorporate tailored messages on maternal and child health, fostering a greater understanding of nutrition, health, and protection across diverse age groups. Special attention will be given to elderly caregivers, recognizing their vital role in caregiving and addressing their specific needs to ensure they can fully benefit from the services provided. Nutrition centers will be designed and adapted to be disability-friendly and child-friendly, ensuring physical accessibility and inclusive service provision to create safe and accessible spaces that accommodate diverse requirements. Persons with disabilities will be prioritized for service provision, and measures will be in place to address their barriers, and unique needs, including tailored support and guidance to ensure their full participation in program activities. Inclusive reporting mechanisms will be established within the nutrition centers to empower individuals of all ages and abilities to safely report concerns or incidents. These mechanisms will be designed to accommodate the specific needs of children, elderly individuals, and persons with disabilities, ensuring accessibility, confidentiality, and effectiveness.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to the affected population (AAP) will be ensured throughout the project cycle by actively engaging refugees at different stages of programming through consultations and participatory approaches. UNHCR will ensure that partners establish robust feedback and complaints mechanisms in all nutrition centers, supported by a clear and integrated plan to address issues that arise. These mechanisms will offer multiple channels of communication to accommodate diverse needs, ensuring accessibility for all groups, including persons with disabilities and those with limited literacy.Refugees will also be actively involved in awareness-raising initiatives regarding existing programs, new interventions, or any changes that may impact their access to services or the overall nutrition status of beneficiaries. To strengthen transparency and trust, UNHCR and its partners will implement a process for learning dissemination based on feedback received. This will include sharing key insights and program adjustments with the community through community meetings, information sessions, or other accessible formats. The dissemination process will ensure that refugees are informed about how their feedback has been considered and integrated into program improvements, fostering a sense of ownership and collaboration.UNHCR will monitor the feedback and complaints mechanisms established by partners to ensure they meet UNHCR’s standards. This will include enhancing the capacity of all INF focal points to mainstream AAP as a continuous monitoring priority. The learning gained from feedback will also be used to refine the overall approach and will be incorporated into ongoing program planning and implementation. UNHCR’s AAP focal point will provide technical support to ensure continuous improvement and alignment with best practices.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

In 2025, UNHCR remains dedicated to mainstreaming the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within its nutrition programs in the camps, ensuring that all staff, volunteers, and partners adhere to the highest standards of protection for beneficiaries. UNHCR will focus on embedding these protective measures through training, community engagement, and effective referral mechanisms. Nutrition centers, which serve as critical access points for support, will be strengthened to provide a safe, respectful, and dignified environment for all beneficiaries. By improving site safety, expanding staff training, and enhancing the availability of reporting channels, UNHCR aims to mitigate the risk of abuse and exploitation.Key to these efforts will be the increased focus on inter-sectoral collaboration, referral and linkages, and responsive community feedback mechanisms. Community engagement will be emphasized to enhance awareness regarding available reporting channels, with targeted sessions to inform vulnerable groups on how to report SEA incidents. Regular monitoring of protection-related issues will be ensured throughout the project implementation, as well as necessary actions to prevent GBV experienced by women, girls, boys, or men receiving assistance through nutrition programs. Through these sustained efforts, UNHCR aims to uphold a safe, abuse-free environment within its nutrition programs, strengthening the resilience and dignity of the refugee community.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. UNHCR is committed to reducing the negative impact of some elements of its nutrition programs on the environment through sensitization and training of nutrition staff and volunteers on climate change principles. UNHCR will emphasize the need for proper disposal of nutrition packaging for the purpose of reducing pollution and improper disposal in the camps. In addition, monitoring of the non-use of polythene bags in line with the directives of the Office of The Refugee Relief and Repatriation (RRRC) will be ensured, while all partners will be supported to procure jute bags or alternatives to carry or store nutrition supplies. UNHCR will continue to support the partners to improve the existing solar system and to install new solar systems in the nutrition facilities as per requirement.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. UNHCR’s project includes an embedded emergency preparedness and response component to ensure a swift and effective response during unforeseen crises such as heavy rainfall, flash floods, fires, and cyclones. A comprehensive contingency plan enables UNHCR to fund and support partners in responding to emergencies, reinforcing the availability of essential services and supplies. Upon forecasts of extreme weather events like cyclones or heavy rains, UNHCR will ensure that emergency nutrition supplies are readily available in all nutrition centers, allowing continuous provision of life-saving support to beneficiaries even if road access is disrupted. Mobile Nutrition Teams (MNTs) will be established within each nutrition center and will be activated immediately following any emergency, reaching vulnerable families in affected areas. These teams will prioritize the delivery of critical nutrition supplies for children and mothers, addressing urgent needs and minimizing disruptions to nutrition care. This approach not only strengthens the resilience of nutrition programs but also ensures that beneficiaries continue to receive vital assistance regardless of the challenging circumstances.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project actively engages youth aged 15-24 by creating opportunities for them to work as community nutrition volunteers, with a focus on training them to serve their own community effectively. Young women are prioritized for these roles, empowering them to play a central part in health outreach and nutrition support. The program includes structured, door-to-door nutrition awareness sessions aimed at raising knowledge on essential health practices. Additionally, the project addresses adolescent girls' nutritional needs by providing Iron and Folic Acid (IFA) supplements, helping improve their overall well-being and health outcomes.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project promotes self-resilience for refugees in a limited capacity by providing opportunities for individuals to serve as Community Nutrition Volunteers. Through this role, refugees receive essential training in nutrition and health practices, enabling them to actively support their own community’s well-being. This approach fosters a sense of empowerment, allowing volunteers to build valuable skills and contribute to community nutrition and health, which are key elements of self-resilience. Although partial, this initiative enhances refugees' ability to participate meaningfully in their community’s development and prepares them to take on more independent roles in the future.
