Project Summary

The project is designed for 25000 children from 6 to 17 years. APADE will include critical stakeholders to identify communities with high concentrations of IDPs and host community children that were out of school and conducted rolling Community Education and Conflict Assessments, engaging both communities and government officials to ascertain the level of safety, number of beneficiaries in the community and the needs for education. We will focus to enroll out-of-school children ages 6-17 into formal schools and non-formal learning centers, as well as provide psychosocial support required to implement the emotional healing process.
The project will establish learning centers within communities and provide vocational skills to adolescent boys and girls to improve their economic status and make them self-reliant. The project will also provide WASH facilities to prevent the Covid19 pandemic, dignity kits to the learners to improve their health condition, as well as daily snacks to encourage enrollment, especially girls, attendance, retention and completion. The project will provide teaching and learning support to the educators and the learners alike to support the acquisition of basic education skills. To help learners cope with trauma, social emotional learning will be introduced as a stand-alone subject and infused into the core subjects of literacy and numeracy.
To ensure gender inclusiveness in the enrollment of learners, the project implement a girl enrollment campaign and will establish four types of learning centers: Non-Formal Learning Centers for boys and girls ages 6-12, Adolescent Girls and boys Learning Centers for young girls only ages 13-17 and Centers for the Physically Challenged for learners living with disabilities ages 6-17.
In their survey, Education and Conflict Assessments findings indicated that girls were still underserved, and more boys were enrolled than girls. The project then will implement one more category of center, Girls Learning Centers for girls only, ages 6-12, to test girls enrollment and close the gaps identified in gender parity in enrollment and provide more opportunity for girls to access basic education. Wherever possible, learning facilitators will employ multi-grade teaching techniques to meet the individual needs of their diverse groups of learners.
The success of implementation will stand on a good relationships with a broad network of key stakeholders in the communities. The project will adapt its programming to align to the volatile communities in Adamawa. The project’s Collaborating, Learning and Adapting (CLA) approach became a powerful tool that not only made the project more responsive to its beneficiaries, but also helped to embed it in communities and build local ownership.

1 - Needs

Education is an essential aspect of building a person’s capabilities and capacity to lead and live a good life and also foster the development of one’s society. However child education is undermining by the persistent undergoing conflict and religious crisis in Northern Nigerian. Since 2009, violent attacks by extremists have forced more than 2.2million people to flee their homes, including more than 1 million school-aged children. As the violence erupted, people moved to secure and safer zone, sometimes far from their homes. This resulted into chaos, panic, trauma and loss of property and lives. Local governments faced challenges providing quality education services for those affected by the conflict, including the unequal distribution, poor quality and unfriendly learning environment of formal government schools. These educational challenges combined with cultural and religious constraints, the marginalization of vulnerable groups, specifically females and children with physical disabilities - resulted in a high percentage of out-of-school children and youth. Many internally displaced children lacked access to safe and friendly learning environments. In 2016, Human Rights Watch reported that at least 611 teachers had been deliberately killed and an additional 19,000 had been forced to flee since 2009. The report also states that, since the beginning of the conflict, more than 2,000 people, most of them female, have been abducted by the group, many directly from their schools. Thousands more students and teachers have been injured by the attacks, some in deadly suicide bombs. Between 2009 and 2015, attacks in northeast Nigeria destroyed more than 910 schools and forced at least 1,500 to close. By early 2016, an estimated 952,029 school-age children had fled the violence. With little or no access to education, these children face a difficult future for years to come. Note that the impact of covid 19 in this circumstance worsen the volatile and fragile communities of IDP’s and returnees and undermine significantly their subsistence means and their capacities to enroll their child to schools especially girls

2 - Activities

This project is designed to deliver both basic formal education and vocational skill to children and adolescents in the safe and protective learning environment. The will enroll out-of-school children ages 6-17 into formal schools and non-formal learning centers, as well as provide psychosocial support required to implement the emotional healing process. The project will implement training program and support for teachers in achieving the highest professional standards as a central strategy for improving school quality. The supply, distribution and use of textbooks will be implemented in this project. They should be available to teachers and pupils and used meaningfully in the classroom. For pupils in financial hardship writing materials and exercise books will be provided on the selection basis. The project will establish learning centers within communities and provide vocational skills to adolescent boys and girls to improve their economic status and make them self-reliant. The project will also provide WASH facilities to prevent the Covid19 pandemic, dignity kits to the learners to improve their health condition, as well as daily snacks to encourage enrollment, especially girls, attendance, retention and completion. The project will provide teaching and learning support to the educators and the learners alike to support the acquisition of basic education skills. To help learners cope with trauma, social emotional learning will be introduced as a stand-alone subject and infused into the core subjects of literacy and numeracy.To ensure gender inclusiveness in the enrolment of learners, the project implement a girl enrolment campaign and will establish four types of learning centers: Non-Formal Learning Centers for boys and girls ages 6-12, Adolescent Girls and boys Learning Centers for young girls only ages 13-17 and Centers for the Physically Challenged for learners living with disabilities ages 6-17.In their survey, Education and Conflict Assessments findings indicated that girls were still underserved, and more boys were enrolled than girls. The project then will implement one more category of center, Girls Learning Centers for girls only, ages 6-12, to test girl’s enrolment and close the gaps identified in gender parity in enrolment and provide more opportunity for girls to access basic education. Covid19 prevention will be the transversal thematic.
