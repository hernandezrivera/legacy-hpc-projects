Project Summary

This project aims at emergency main season food security and livelihood support intervention with the promotion of appropriate small scale resilience activities amongst 11000 households who are food insecure living in Gogrial West County. The immediate objective is to support vulnerable households with direct crops kits, vegetable kits, fishing kits and tools so as to enable them to have access to food through their own production. The long term objective is to protect vulnerable households from socks that will affect their ability to access food, the objectives are in line with HRP strategic objective 2(SO2) and in support of FSL cluster objective 2. To deliver the required results, the key program strategy is to implement the direct distributions of crop kits, vegetable kits, fishing kits and tools to boost household food production and sustain food security. The distribution of FSL emergency livelihoods kits will be complemented by Strengthen coping mechanisms and skills in agricultural production through training of farmers groups and fishermen groups on improved agronomic, vegetable utilization and preparation, post-harvest handling while fishermen received training on sustainable fishing, hygienic handling and preservation of fish in order to reduce postharvest loses. Post distribution, post planting and post harvesting assessment will be conducted. To ensure accountability to the affected population (AAP) a committee will be formed to address issues that may affect the beneficiaries negatively during the project implementation. The project will target mainly vulnerable returns, flood affected persons, IDPs, and the host population for the main cropping season, second season and dry season responses; a total of 8000 HHS will be supported from 30 bomas of Gogrial West County. The main drivers to disaster are covid-19, flooding, conflict, poor harvesting, the economic crisis that resulted in issues such as rape, killing obstruction of the livelihood activities. On the issues of rape, GBV women and girls are the most affected. The intervention will therefore to the larger extend address the needs of women and girls as they are the main victim of disasters. The project will promote an inter-cluster strategy with WFP food for asset beneficiaries, lactating mothers at the nutrition centres, farmers affected by fall armyworm, flooding and locust. RWDSS is a very strong and active partner of the food security and livelihood cluster at national and state levels and has participated in several joint assessment missions in its areas of operation. RWDSS has record experience working with WFP in implementing purchase for progress project, GFD, FFA, School feeding programs and Cash vouchers. This solid experience of RWDSS both in terms of context, high impact previous implemented projects and active presences will give the opportunity to implement this project with quality results among the affected population

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to receive an influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan through different points of entry in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Upper Nile States.), Aweil (Northen Bahr el Ghazal State) and Raja (Western Bahr el Ghazal State) and Warrap . The high levels of acute food insecurity in South Sudan remain worrying, with the most severely acute food insecure populations significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance.The latest data shows that 5.83 million people (46 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). An estimated 35,000 people are classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in the Duk (3,000) and Nyirol (3,000) counties of Jonglei State; and the Rubkona County (15,000) of Unity State. An estimated 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).The most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) are Jonglei State (61 percent), Unity State (58 percent), Upper Nile State (56 percent) and Lakes State (53 percent).Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.16 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). 870,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to suffer acute malnutrition in this period. An estimated 72 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap.Within this context, RWDSS, in partnership with SSHF will provide livelihood support to ensure assistance reaches the most at risk populations in Gogrial West, Gogrial East, Raja and Awiel East Counties of Warrap, Northern Bah el Gazal and Western Bah el Ghazal States according to the IPC acute or emergency phase classification. With livelihood and asset creation and enhancement assistance (livelihood kits: crop, vegetable, and fish) and training will be provided to vulnerable food insecure households

2. Project activities

• Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP): Conduct Safety Audit, Formation and Training of AAP Committee(s) RWDSS will conduct a safety audit before the selection of distribution points with the women, girls, men and boys to assess the possible risk factors regarding the protection of women and girls. The safety audit will be guided by the Gender Assessment Safety Audit Tool.• Mobilization and Sensitization of Communities at the County/Payam/Boma Levels, RWDSS will mobilize and sensitize the communities on direct distribution interventions.• Establishment of a clear complaint mechanism and feed back to communities’ scheme. RWDSS will provide details of the meetings (certified meeting minutes and participants attendance lists with their contact details.• Beneficiary Identification, Selection, and Registration .RWDSS will target the food insecure for beneficiaries based on selection criteria. Assistance will be targeted in particular towards those displaced who have not been absorbed by host communities, and who are congregating in rural areas 60-70 percent female and child beneficiaries (a large percentage of the IDPs are women and children).• Provision of Inputs through Direct Distribution of Emergency Livelihood Kits (Crop, Vegetable and Fishing RWDSS will collect inputs from identified FAO storage hubs and transport them to the distribution areas upon notification within three working days. During the distribution, RWDSS will provide technical information and guidance to the beneficiaries on best use of agriculture and fishery kits that are being distributed• Provision of Basic Training and Activities on Good Agriculture and Aquaculture Practices In handing out agricultural inputs, the RWDSS will create awareness and provide trainings on improved techniques (e.g. climate smart agriculture (CSA), soil and water management (SWM), integrated production and pest management (IPPM), post-harvest management) in the use of agricultural inputs and aquaculture inputs distributed.. • Formation and Strengthening of Farmer Groups (Crop Production, Vegetable Production, Post-harvest Handling and Management) RWDSS will identify community facilitators/trainers to receive trainings and support for delivery of community trainings to farmers. Training topics will draw from a menu of good agriculture and Aquaculture practices, including precision and timing of field operations (e.g., planting, weeding, and harvesting); IPPM; promotion of drought or flood tolerant crop varieties; soil and moisture management techniques (e.g., micro-irrigation, conservation agriculture techniques and soil fertility management); seed saving multiplication, post-production management, and strengthening seed systems. The training will also include topics related to climate change (e.g. conservation agriculture, CSA), • Establishment of Crop and Vegetable Production Demonstration Plots RWDSS will establish one demonstration plot in identified payams. The demonstration farm will be established by selected groups of interested farmers and will be located in a public and well frequented location (e.g. a primary road) to act as a showcase. The plot will be fenced and have a gate in order to protect it from animals, and this will be done by demonstration farm members at their own cost. • Post-harvest and Preservation Training.RWDSS will focus on post-harvest and preservation techniques such as sun drying techniques, using mainly plastic sheets. This will enable better preservation of produce. This will also be a mechanism to build long term capacity and sustainability within the communities on crop and vegetable farming. • Utilization and Nutrition Training RWDSS will organize at least one vegetable utilization and nutrition training in the selected communities. The training will be organized in collaboration with health centres so that mothers with malnourished children will benefit from this program. • Formation and Strengthening of Fishery Groups
