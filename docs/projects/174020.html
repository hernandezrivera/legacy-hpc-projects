Project Summary

Boko Haram insurgency in the Northeast Nigeria has created a protection crisis affecting the Lake Chad region, exacerbated by high handed and non-human rights complaint counter-terrorism operations characterized by violations and abuses of human rights and International Humanitarian Law in the most affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY). These violations includes killings, rape, widespread displacement, deliberate targeting of civilians and various forms of sexual and gender-based violence and abuse as well as forced recruitment and use of children to detonate PBIEDs. The regionalized armed conflict concentrated in the Lake Chad basin area has triggered a humanitarian crisis with about 7.1 million people in the most affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe in need of humanitarian assistance. At the same time, the military has adopted Rules of Engagement and Code of Conduct for counter-terrorism operations, which continues to be characterized by excesses by security agencies including indiscriminate rounding up of civilians, including women and children; prolonged detention; extra-judicial killings, torture, a pattern of disproportionate use of force and other human rights violations. In the context of Covid-19 response, there have been reports of misconduct, violence and harassment by security forces in enforcing restrictions on movement consistent with previous reports of abuses and violations in the context of counter-terrorism operations. Current protection responses have focused both on addressing abuses and violations by Boko Haram and human rights impact of the counter-terrorism operations by national authorities. This project builds on on-going OHCHR support to the humanitarian intervention, and aims to strengthen protection monitoring and response in BAY states. This will be supported by strengthening coordination to address protection challenges/gaps in relation to displaced persons, sustaining mechanisms for monitoring and reporting early warning risks factors, training and dissemination of information on human rights to rights-holders (IDPs and host-communities), capacity building for national authorities (government, security agencies, MDAs, INGOs, CSOs) to strengthen the context for realization of the primary duty of Government to protect civilians while mainstreaming human rights in the operating procedures of national bodies. It also aims to enhance access to justice through strengthening engagement with the national authorities in re-establishing rule of law institutions decimated by a decade of conflict and insecurity.

1 - Needs

Boko Haram insurgency and responses thereto has been characterized by violations and abuses of human rights. The capacity of the UN to provide a coherent response to the situation will be enhanced by strong and effective human rights capacity on ground. Humanitarian responders face the challenging tasks of protecting and assisting persons affected by Boko Haram insurgency as well as counter-insurgency operations by the Nigerian Army. There is a need, therefore, to continue and strengthen existing OHCHR engagement to support sensitization on human rights, access to justice, and prevention and response to violations of international human rights and humanitarian law faced by the displaced population. The envisaged support will continue to strengthen coordinated information management and analysis on violations and abuses, advisory support to the humanitarian leadership as well as timely human rights advocacy and response. The intervention will seek to support the operations of the National Human Rights Commission to strengthen the integration of human rights in the humanitarian response, strengthening the centrality of protection. It will also support the operations of the National Committee against Torture in monitoring places of detention related to counter-insurgency operations, and identify and recruit expert-consultant support to undertake complex studies on extant accountability framework for terrorist offenders including applicability of the Administration of Criminal Justice Act (2015) and the Terrorism Prevention (Amendment) Act (2013) as well as analyses of independent monitoring of terrorism trials.

2 - Activities

The project aims to strengthen existing UN human rights engagement as a component of the overall UN response to the humanitarian crisis in Northeast Nigeria. In this regard, it will strengthen protection monitoring and support coordination to address and mitigate protection challenges/gaps especially for the most vulnerable population and ensure effective access to justice and assistance to victims. It will also provide expertise for human rights advice, education, report drafting, developing advocacy and communication tools, supporting community sensitization programmes and engagement at strategic levels as well as trainings on human rights for right holders and capacity building for national authorities to enhance community resilience and accountability. The intervention would strengthen protection of humanitarian civil society actors and awareness-raising among government counterparts, in particular the military and the police, to ensure that measures taken to combat terrorism are grounded in respect for human rights and the rule of law. The intervention will also aim to strengthen the administration of justice and national accountability mechanisms including the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) to respond to violations. This will be achieved through joint work with monitors deployed by CSOs and the National Human Rights Commission.
