Project Summary

Given the ongoing protection crisis in South Sudan, Nile Hope will continue to work with populations affected by conflict and displacement and provide timely access, availability and utilization of multi-sectoral services to vulnerable and conflict affected population in Nasir, Leer, Akobo, Fangak, Maiwut and Nyirol Counties of Upper Nile, Unity and Jonglei States, and other stakeholders to enhance the protective environment of individuals and communities especially women and girls, and identify, prevent and address protection risks. The project objective is aligned with the Protection Cluster and Humanitarian Response Plan objective by providing immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority GBV needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach areas. The project will target a total of 23214(Men 5538, Women 8177, Boys 3562 and Girls 5937) IDPs, host communities, and returnees. This project is designed to improve responses to cases of GBV, enhance the ability of survivors to recover from GBV, and make progress towards changing the social norms that perpetuate GBV, with the intent of fully mitigating the effects of GBV and preventing future cases. It will provide GBV response services and address critical gaps in information and knowledge among women and girls on GBV response, which limit access to services and heighten GBV risk. Nile Hope will encourage health-seeking behaviors among GBV survivors, facilitating referrals, and creating supportive structures to prevent GBV. Nile Hope will work in collaboration with local partners and will establish and operationalize women and girls’ friendly spaces and provide periodic individual and group psycho social support for women and girls. This will include dialogue sessions, psychosocial support through group counseling sessions, skills building, production of reusable sanitary pads using locally available materials, information sessions on pertinent issues to women and girls on access to available services, sexual and reproductive Health issues on safe deliveries, family planning and post abortion care, personal hygiene, menstrual hygiene management, decision making and peer to peer support. This project will support the healing process of GBV survivors by providing community based case management and psychosocial support services and engage men and boys in community dialogues and awareness raising sessions on GBV, its effect and consequences and their roles in prevention. Protection interventions will be integrated within Nile Hope’s Health, Nutrition, WASH, Education, Peace building and FSL programmes to compliment holistic assistance to communities through static and mobile services. Nile Hope will ensure commitment to diversity and gender equity as Nile Hope’s Protection sector’s core business is premised on women and girls’ empowerment and therefore will strive to periodically carry out gender and protection analysis including assessments of vulnerabilities related to age, sex and diversity, and will give priority to vulnerable population. Nile Hope will ensure protection from sexual exploitation and abuse for beneficiaries through awareness and training to Nile Hope’s staff, partners, volunteers, vendors, contractors and awareness to ensure beneficiaries are aware of PSEA and reporting mechanisms. The project will ensure that women are engaged and supported to become first responders within their communities. Nile Hope will work to advance women’s rights, voices and leadership through increasing their knowledge and their participation in humanitarian coordination. This will contribute to women’s protection and empowerment and will transform women’s position in emergency response and resilience, while helping to address the core issues that affect communities in emergencies.

1 - Needs

People’s protection needs remain severe, while violence and violations of international humanitarian law (IHL) persist as a reality of daily life for many South Sudanese – in particular for women and children. Close to 2.5 million people have fled the country and sought refuge in the neighboring Countries, while nearly 2 million people are estimated to remain internally displaced. The conflict has caused deaths. Conflict-related sexual violence is a defining feature of the crisis. The situation is exacerbated by the lack of shelters and basic needs for both the IDPs and the Host communities as floods destroys shelters and interrupted crop production in Akobo, Maiwut, Nyirol, Fangak, Nasir and Leer Counties. various assessment reports have indicated a large number of vulnerable population which have no access to GBV services in the counties, this has been necessitated by lack of women and girls friendly spaces, lack of dignity kits supplies, and lack of trained case managers and series providers (Nile Hope Uror rapid need assessment report, 2019). According to the August IPC analysis, Akobo and Nasir are projected to be in Phase 4 come April 2020. Flood related crop destruction, poor food accessibility due to degradation in the road infrastructure brought about by heavy rains and inflation in the price of basic commodities in the market are part of the factors that are bound to contribute to the worsening of the situation come 2020. Food prices in the market are high as most products come across the border from Ethiopia through Gambella. As it is currently, the infrastructural weaknesses in Akobo contribute to higher transportation costs of nutrition commodities, with porters periodically hiking prices. With the extensive geographical coverage we have, this leads to an ever-burgeoning cost of transport that proves to be unsustainable in the long run. Clan fights, revenge killings and fear of attacks from the neighbouring Murle community are a constant threat to normal programming activities in Akobo.In Nasir and Maiwut, markets is also considered to be a source of food, nevertheless, compared to Akobo and other Counties, the transportation from Gambella make it hard to get goods on time by the communities. The security situation remains fragile in Greater Maiwut following the incident involving forces of Sudan People’s Liberation Army in Opposition and a section of mobile force command loyal to General James Ochan Puot on 30th and 31st of July and subsequent fighting and displacement in Maiwut, Meth, Merdiet and Turu locations, this fighting displaced the population of Maiwut, exposing women, girls and children into protection risks. There are armed actors in the areas leading to the market, as it is said it is for civilian protection. These armed actors create fear among the communities. As result of conflict, women when accessing far villages in search for food end up being exploited and abused.The deepening economic crisis have forced women and girls to risk their lives to fend for their families including resorting to negative coping mechanisms such as sex for food “transactional sex” The continued practice of dowry payments perpetuates the belief that girls are both a source of wealth for their families and property of their prospective. There is a complex relationship between gender roles, social norms, food insecurity and GBV and a persisting phenomenon of ‘hyper-masculinity’ amongst men often exacerbated by the conflict of risk and insecurity. This reinforces harmful power differentials between men and women (and children), often leading to GBV and other forms of violence. Patriarchal societal norms that promote inequality (including men minimizing the occurrence of violence, and both men and women accepting the use of violence) are common. While the crisis has dramatically increased the challenges that households face, the gender division of labour has not shifted, but rather has strengthened with wome

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Provide psychosocial support to GBV survivors using the appropriate case management tools, make appropriate referrals and document using Gender Based Violence Information Management Systems (GBVIMS). Activity 2: Trained and coach Protection officers, case workers/social workers on PSS and case management Activity 3: Identify, train and support community focal points in providing Psychological First Aid and refer survivors to available services Activity 4: Train frontline service providers (Case managers, police, community leaders, community support teams) on GBV, referral pathways, survivor centred approaches and psycho social first aid.Activity 5: Conduct outreach, door-to-door sessions, and awareness raising campaigns on GBV risks and the referral pathway, and key GBV messages and generate feedback on quality and relevance of GBV servicesActivity 6: Conduct regular safety audits and develop community based risk mitigation strategiesActivity 7: Establish community based mechanisms to provide protection assistance and psycho social support to women, girls and vulnerable people affected by the conflictActivity 8: Constructed and equipped 7 women and girls friendly spaces in the conflict affected locations.Activity 9: Provision of dignity kits for women and girls in all the project locations. Given the high needs of these kits, Nile Hope through UNFPA and GBVSC will provide the dignity kits to the vulnerable women and girlsActivity 10: Nile Hope will conduct service mapping; this activity will take place in all the project sites to know the number of the service providers and the different services they offered, this will also helps in the issue of duplication of humanitarian service.Activity 11:Conduct GBV assessment and quarterly coordinated safety Audits to identify the protection risks and concerns for women and girls and share the findings with concerned service providers and sub clusters to mitigate the identified GBV risks.Activity 12:Engage men through the EMAP (Engaging Men in Accountable Practice) program to foster social behavior and act as agents of change by being accountable to negative social behaviors in the community

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To mitigate such risks several sector specific measures are recommended that Nile Hope will ensure that Protection risk assessments are conducted and protection mainstreaming is included throughout the program cycle, such as aligning to the protection sector recommended response packages for a harmonized response, ensuring the understanding and monitoring of the contextual environment is well known, up to date and integrated into the distribution processes, and applying needs based targeting / selection criteria. Furthermore, Nile Hope as a SAG member of the National GBV sub cluster will continue to support other humanitarian partners to train their staff on the ground while equipping them with adequate tools to limit and monitor the liability and effect of these risks

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Nile Hope will involve affected communities directly in planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation of this project. Nile Hope will deliberately facilitate formation of project management committees composed men and women from the targeted communities. The committees will support Nile Hope in planning, implementation and monitoring of all the project activities meant to serve affected communities. Nile Hope is highly gender sensitive when it comes to formation of these committees. The committee will collect all the feedback from the individuals living with disabilities and enure that their concerns are documented and addressed during the project implementation.
