Project Summary

In response to the demonstrated nutrition needs on the ground, Human Appeal Turkey (HAT) is planning to operate 6 nutrition rapid response teams (RRTs) across North West Syria (NWS) targeting the children under 5 (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) among others of the most vulnerable groups within the areas of priority (As per the nutrition cluster and this allocation paper) with essential life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services as follows:
The project will be implemented in 9 communities across 2 sub- districts (Dana, and Maaret Tamisrin) provide nutrition services for 41,774 beneficiaries including (12,529 Pregnant and lactating women 15319 children under five years and 12,960 other caregivers (3696 men and 9264 women)) (41,281 IDPs and 493 HCs including 322 PWDs). The total project beneficiaries will be 14360 male, 27413 female, including 27371 children under 5.
HAT will promote the ultimate utilization of the pre-existing resources and expertise relevant to the intervention including the use of the trained staff at in the organization and the usage of the assembly points, warehouses, OTPs as well as the relevant technical expertise and measures of access and coordination at the field level. The proposed RRTs are planned to function according to the TORs and SOPs adopted by the nutrition cluster, and they will be providing the following nutrition services:
• Screening 22400 children 6-59 months, and 9600 PLWs, referral and follow up who are malnourished.
• Provide emergency IYCF counseling for 3875 caregivers including PLWs.
• Disseminating messages on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices, for 15.000 caregivers including PLWs.
• Screen, referral CU5 and PLWs with dangers signs and Medical complications.
• Support hygiene promotion and rising WASH awareness by providing Key messages to prevent the risk of COVID 19 transmission and other infections.
• Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against childU5/PLWs during Nutrition screening.
• Capacity building of 32 frontline nutrition services providers on IYCF-E and CMAM programs in the context of COVID-19 and provide continuous technical support for human appeal teams to build their capacity and strengthen their experience in nutrition field work.
As a part to COVID 19 response, HAT is going to apply adjustments of the nutrition activities to mitigate the risk of infection while ensuring continuity of life-saving nutrition services, where they are going to integrate key messages on hygiene and COVID-19 symptoms, and (IPC) measures, implement a simplified protocol for the treatment of acute malnutrition, use a separate MUAC tape for each child and PLW, distribute micronutrients for two months in advance if available, screen and refer suspected COVID-19 cases, applying recommended IPC measures, provide IYCF-E counseling, either at the appropriate distance of 1 meters apart or remotely by phone or video and disseminate IYCF-E messages use platforms such as text messages and WhatsApp videos, posters in public places.
HAT team will train the caregivers of MAM and SAM cases on MUAC and edema screening, referral, recognize signs of deterioration, and give them a mobile or WhatsApp number of the CHW to contact in case the child’s condition deteriorates.
• Project team will ensure effective coordination with the nutrition cluster and the partners through the submission of Monthly 4Ws, Supplies assessment, surveys, and services mapping. in addition to sharing HAT RRTs work locations, and updates with the Cluster partners to avoid any duplication in the services or overlapping.
• Effective Monitoring for the projects activities, outputs and outcomes, where HAT MEAL team will conduct regularly a beneficiaries interviews, satisfaction surveys, monitoring visits, FGDs, reflection sessions, sensitization on the complaints mechanism and PSEA hotline.

4 - Needs

As per nutrition sector severity report in HNO 2022, Maaret Tamsrin (109579 PIN) and Dana sub-districts (373,333 PIN) are classified among the areas of most people in need with a severity score for both areas of 5, where 23,074 PLWs in Dana and 6,773 in Maaret Tamsrin suffering from pregnancy anemia in addition to 119,913 children in Dana and 35,196 C6-59 are suffering from anemia as well. Furthermore, 5891 in Dana plus 1,729 SAM cases in Maaret Tamsrin requires SAM management support within the 2 sub-districts which are full of IDPs camps and very populated areas.Also, and according to the RRT Coverage and Gap Map by the nutrition cluster, it is clear that many parts of NWS are still suffering divergence between the identified GAM rates and RRT coverage, what potentially could result in further deterioration of the nutritional status of the affected population unless addressed otherwise. This applies to areas like Maaret Tamsrin, Dana sub districts as well as the vast majority of the Euphrates Shield areas. The aforementioned situation is also confirmed within the community surveillance report by the nutrition cluster in March 2021, as areas like are among the top 10 sub-districts in terms of the highest GAM rates while others like Dana is among the top 10 sub-districts in terms of the highest MAM among PLWs across the region.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection priorities are integrated within all programming. HAT staff will be trained on child safeguard codes of conduct and other emergency programming standards such as SPHERE. Conflict sensitivity tools such as Do No Harm will be used to assess proposed project activities, identify potentially harmful impacts on beneficiaries and adapt accordingly. In doing so, a cross section of project beneficiaries, hosts, the field team and other relevant actors will be engaged to gain more understanding of the operational contexts, concerns, interests and social dynamics as part of such analysis. 1. Planning Phase Map and analyze the stakeholders, their interrelationships, roles, influence and their potential impact on the intervention. Mapping of the potential external and internal sources of violence, danger, coercion and take preventative and responsive measures to address, mitigate or reduce these threats. Ensured that the plan addresses the specific needs of the most vulnerable groups and individuals, gender issues are critical needs that must be given a special consideration at the planning stage. 2. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

This project reflects the different needs of women, men and children throughout the project cycle i.e. design, implementation and monitoring. The distinct health care needs of women, men, girls, boys, elderly and disabled are well analyzed. Project team will make sure that the RRTs have female staff to address the needs of women and promote gender equality. Project team will also identify the facilities which has specialized services to provide medical assistance to GBV survivors and psychosocial support. In case if the team faced any cases to be referred to the specialized facilities and professionals. PSEA: For protection from PSEA the following mitigation measures will be used: Emplacement of effective complaint and feedback mechanism. Effective recruitment and performance management of staff. Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about HA , the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the project it is implementing and what it intends to deliver. Do no harm will be further mainstreamed as follows: The identification of the targeted locations was dependent upon the needs and gap analysis and no particular political or social affiliations. Also, all the patients including men, women, boys and girls and both from host and IDPs will be able to access quality services free of cost.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Inadequate acceptance of the intervention within the targeted camps, communities and houses. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: Special consideration will be paid to contract staff members from within the same communities targeted with the intervention, and ensuring gender balance within the teams to increase their acceptance among the served populations. High level of coordination with the local authorities was and will be taking place to ensure proper facilitation of the teams’ mobility within and among the planned locations. Risk 2: Lack of qualified nutrition staff Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: HAT have pre-existing staff members that are familiar with nutrition related topics and interventions. Both organizations will conduct comprehensive capacity building for the newly recruited staff members CMAM and IYCF- E in the context of COVID 19 so they achieve the desired level of technical capacity needed to provide quality services. Risk 3: Increase influxes of IDPs due to security-related incidence, what may result in increased rates of COVID-19 transmission due to overcrowding within the targeted areas. Likelihood: Medium. Impact high. Mitigation Measures: HAT will apply a triage system that enables categorization of the beneficiaries according to their symptoms to limit the risk of transmission of coronavirus especially for the most vulnerable groups including children and women with malnutrition.
