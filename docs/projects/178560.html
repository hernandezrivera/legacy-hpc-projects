Project Summary

This project aims to support the food security of HHs through empowering the women at 15 communities (3 in Dana subdistrict, 2 in Qourqeena subdistrict, 1 in Jandairis subdistrict, 1 in Afrin subdistrict, 6 in Sharan subdistrict, 2 in Atreb subdistrict, 2 in Ariha subdistrict ).
Where this project aims to support vulnerable female-headed households through vocational training on food processing to empower women, distributing food processing raw materials, and food processing tools vouchers to 500 HHs.
the activities are:
1- training 500 female-headed households on food processing through the following sessions
- Four Theoretical sessions include (food processing, HACCP, business management, marketing) for one month.
- 12 practical training sessions that planned to cover the most women needed training topics (for 12 weeks).
2- Distributing 500 food processing tools-vouchers with a value of 120 $ per one.
which enable getting on main required kitchen equipment. Those assets can be used sustainably in future small projects by women.
3- Distributing 500 food processing raw materials-vouchers with a value of 200 $ per one for 4 rounds (50 $ per round/per month).
So, enabling women to get vegetables, milk, and other main raw food materials for processing.
Also, follow up and mentoring the women to help them in putting a plan for next season. That small grant aims to encourage women of starting their small business projects.
4- Establishing women groups include the active members of those participated women and building their capacity to be a nucleus in society and a basic pillar of participatory work among women. Also, handing them over the training items for future usage.
As well, during the training sessions, the IHR will try the enhance awareness of women about COVID-19 through awareness sessions about that epidemic, health measures that must be considered for protection. where the iHR will use several methods to spread awareness to all people.
So, that support can enhance the women's access to livelihoods,t, therefore, giving an opportunity to establish a special business.
as the HACCP training to improve the knowledge of women about food safety and the best practices regarding taking care of their children's food. that may be reflected on the health of family members.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, 14.2 million people in need. 12.4 million people are food insecure. The evidence highlights an alarming deterioration, caused by a combination of factors including currency depreciation and associated loss of purchasing power, considerable hikes in food prices, large population displacements, lack of livelihood and income-generating options, losses in agricultural production and assets, all compounded by ongoing conflicts and the COVID-19 pandemic. IHR assessed needs in targeted communities. Analyzed data showed the food security in those areas ranges between high and medium risk. where Sharan sub-district stated a high risk due to overcrowding of population in parallel of low access to services. Due to the instability of working conditions affecting by COVID-19, 60% of HHs heads forced to sell one of their assets to meet the living requirements. 80% of M and F have equal access to labor market. For women's needs, 45% are looking for job opportunities, 21% need vocational training and 34% need cash for food or shelter. As the work options are limited to some temporary work. Regarding children's needs, they were represented by supporting education and protection sectors. Based on assessment, one of the available works for women is food processing. it had been negatively affected over the past years due to the war, while some women still practicing it. The respondents confirmed the need to empower women by enabling them of producing food processed since it will increase the produced food items in the markets, therefore reflecting on prices. the main difficulties or shocks in the past 6 months faced by the HHs were high food prices and the expenditures for sickness and health

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection risk 1: Child labor. Mitigating measures: IHR will be sure that the staff working in this project are trained and fully aware of child safeguarding, Child protection, and GBV principles. Protection risk 2: Lack/difficulty of accessibility for PWDs in the proposed activities. Mitigating measures: 1- Availability of wheelchair for PWD at the reception point, trained staff to deal with PWDs, Priority for the PWDs, included in the criteria, House to house distributions that will ensure that those with special needs that can’t reach the distribution points can reach the assistance. Protection Risk 3: Sometimes, crowding during the distribution of bread, may not provide the services at the desired quality, which might affect the BNFs dignity, especially women. Mitigating measures: Set up separate waiting and entry lines at registrations Raise the awareness of community leaders on GBV and child protection principles and make sure that all BNFs can voice their concerns through the designated channels. Protection Risk 4: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation for women during the implementation. Mitigating measures: Choosing appropriate time for distributions of bread and receiving of the wheat crop, voucher exchanging in Hawala agent will taking place during daylight hours to allow women to return home safely, ensuring regular security and incidence reporting, conducting awareness sessions for women about the issues surrounding with GBV to minimize such damage
