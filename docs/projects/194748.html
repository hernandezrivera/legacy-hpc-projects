Project Summary

Improving and sustaining essential, equitable, quality health services at primary and community levels are pivotal for effective response to the needs of the affected population. The protracted crisis in Syria has left only 54% of primary health care centers (PHCs) fully functional. The Cholera outbreak that was declared in September 2022, is further draining health resources. Overall, there is a lack of available, qualified, and trained staff, with limited financial means to support service provision, supervision, and referral services. Findings from AAH assessments reveal that communities face a range of challenges, including rampant poverty, poor employment, limited access to healthcare resources, stigma, transportation challenges and proliferation of illicit substances, all impacting communities’ ability to engage in healthcare, as well as women’s health seeking behavior and decision-making. Access difficulties constitute a distinctive barrier to health care in areas with high severity scores. AAH proposes an integrated approach to meet the priority needs in the 2023 HRP, focusing on activating community structures for the sustainable use and provision of public services and prevention of undernutrition and epidemic diseases, and restoring essential health systems and capacity within targeted communities to ensure local capacity meets the different needs of women, men, girls, and boys. Targeting criteria are based on needs severity and assessment, vulnerability (including prioritization of PLW, WRA, CU5, IDPs, returnees, populations hosting displaced persons, and people with socioeconomic hardships), contextual volatility and strategic operational presence of AAH. The project will intervene at multiple levels within the targeted health districts, including primary static and mobile healthcare facilities. Readiness of health facilities to foster resilience and effective management of outbreaks will be promoted through the provision of medical supplies and support for health information systems. Medicine shortages in the supported facilities will also be addressed. AAH will increase availability of MHPSS services by supporting points for MH counselling in NES. Competency-based capacity building and leadership/governance empowerment for community and health/nutrition workforce will be included. PHC staff will be trained on integrating primary health care services (including MHPSS and nutrition) with activated supervisory visits to ensure adequacy of implementation. Midwives will be targeted by tailored capacity building programs aiming at empowering them with the skills, knowledge and tools needed for quality provision of SRH services integrated with nutrition and MHPSS. Community Health Workers will also be trained to integrate and deliver MHPSS, nutrition and RMNCAH prevention at the PHC, community, and household levels within the selected health districts. Optimal menstrual hygiene practices will be promoted at community level with linkages to PHCs, targeting female adolescents. A comprehensive MEAL plan is designated to support collection and tracking of sex, age and disability disaggregated data in line with proposed health interventions and is undertaken directly by MEAL in collaboration with AAH’s Health and Nutrition department. Monitoring methodologies include needs assessments, baseline and endline surveys, and activity monitoring. Systematic collection of information on risk environment, gender roles and responsibilities, gender relations and decision-making dynamics, access to and control of resources, and social norms, will be done with a conflict sensitivity approach. Qualitative interviews and satisfaction surveys will complement monitoring activities to examine the project’s effect on social dynamics in communities and pathways of change. Focus group discussions and key informant interviews with stakeholders, such as health facility workers and beneficiaries, at key points and midway through program cycle.

3 - Needs

With 12.43 million people in need estimated for HNO 2023, health needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient and inequitable access to healthcare due to a combination of fuel and transportation shortages, destruction of health facilities, insufficient human resources, and (imposed) access constraints. The Cholera outbreak revealed high vulnerability to epidemics, especially in northern Syria, in addition to inconsistency in reporting systems. The fragile health system continues to face compounding emergencies and chronic challenges, which affect the availability and quality of health services, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. According to AAH’s Maternal Health and Nutrition study conducted in 2021-22 in Hama and Aleppo, the top three reasons for not accessing services at public PHCs are a lack of availability of services, unavailability of medicines, and a lack of gynecologists to provide services. The study revealed gaps in community knowledge and practices related to reproductive, maternal, newborn and child health. The most common issues were related to low education rates, high prevalence of early marriages, and low inter-pregnancy intervals. There was low uptake of antenatal and postanal care services, poor family planning, inadequate maternal nutritional habits, and deficient maternal mental health. Moreover, GBV was common in all areas. These are all factors contributing to poor RMNCH and increased morbidity and mortality.

4 - Implementation of activities

AAH will continue its approach of activating community structures for sustainable access and provision of public services, preventing undernutrition and epidemic diseases, and restoring essential health systems and capacity within targeted communities. AAH’s Cholera response was integrated within its existing health programs, and is in line with the national response plan. Targeting and selection criteria are based on vulnerability, highest severity and needs assessments. In 2023, AAH will identify emergency rehabilitations to reinstate safe and dignified conditions of health services. This will be complemented with the provision of furniture, medical supplies, medications, and equipment. Competency-based capacity building and leadership/governance empowerment for community and health workforce will continue. Gender-sensitive MHM activities, including awareness sessions and distribution of MHM kits, will be provided through the community health networks. The project will intervene at multiple levels, including primary static and mobile healthcare facilities. A MEAL plan supports collection and tracking of data in line with proposed health interventions. Monitoring and beneficiary data will be disaggregated by sex, age, and marginalized groups, including people living with disabilities. Monitoring methodologies include needs assessments, baseline and endline surveys, and activity monitoring as well as qualitative interviews, satisfaction surveys, and focus group discussions.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: women, girls, men and boys receiving health services are over-exposed to the risk of harm. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: Child Safeguarding polices and systems in place, regularly monitored and corrective action taken. Sufficient safe, clean and sex segregated bathroom facilities provided. Risk: health facilities are attacked. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: apply the designated de-confliction mechanism. Continue reporting attacks against health facilities. Continue advocacy efforts with all parties. Risk: gender inequality and GBV. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: train all health staff on how to engage with GBV survivors and provide information in an ethical, safe and confidential manner. Ensure referral mechanisms to specialized GBV support, health care, MHPSS, and child protection. Risk: improper management of sensitive data. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: electronic data and information properly protected. Clear exit strategy about how to withdraw resources while ensuring that patients and healthcare workers are not exposed to further risk; including ways to protect and/or destroy data and information. Risk: not equal provision of and access to health services for vulnerable groups. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: analyze and disaggregate all data. Implementation of health services in line with global and health sector guidance. Ensure that complaints and feedback mechanisms are accessible.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH’s policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to SEA. Implementation of humanitarian aid activities can sometimes have the unforeseen impact of creating unequal power balances within the AAH team and for recipients of aid; the organization is committed to mitigating against such risks. AAH prohibits SEA by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of PSEA polices is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in summary dismissal. AAH reserves the right to refer such cases to the relevant legal authorities in the abuser’s country of origin and in the host country. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect; support awareness raising of the PSEA policy and report any instances of a breach of this policy. All employees and representatives of AAH are trained on Safeguarding, Protection and PSEA and are signatories to AAH’s ethical policies and Code of Conduct, and are therefore obligated not to engage in sexually exploitative behaviour linking sexual favors with the receipt of aid, engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age or be involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH staff and communities AAH works with have access to safe and effective reporting systems. AAH will investigate allegations of SEA in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: security situation allows safe response; access to target areas and beneficiaries is granted; partners are willing to collaborate on timely manner; availability of suppliers and items in the market, prices remain stable; no major displacements occur. Risk: security situation is unstable and/or worsens, AAH has no access; there are additional administrative procedures imposed on D-INGOs, and restrictions on access to beneficiaries; lengthy processes to obtain approvals. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: AAH coordinates with local authorities, Ministries, and stakeholders to monitor the situation, to maintain access to target areas and beneficiaries, to develop relationships and mitigate restrictions. Security rules and plans are in place and regularly reviewed. Re-planning of activities to recover eventual delays. Advocacy and coordination with the humanitarian community. Risk: significant devaluation of EURO/ USD against SYP; International bank transfers are not fluid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be monitored and, if materialized, communicated to donors and options discussed. Risk: aid diversion, financial fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: strict policies and procedures in place for procurement, financial management and risk management; internal controls set up. Monthly monitoring of financial spending.
