Project Summary

The project will seek to use sports to address the psychosocial needs of children by incorporating a peacebuilding component into the project at school and community level, targeting both in-school and out-of-school children. Coaches, teachers and youth leaders will be trained to provide MHPSS and life skills sessions. Peace clubs will be formed and linked through sports and will serve as an entry point to peace building. This project has been designed to be community-based and community owned, so it is expected that the peace clubs and the sports sessions will continue after the project ends, thereby further contributing to the social cohesion process. The project primarily contributes to SDG3 Good Health and Wellbeing through the delivery of sport and play activities. Sport and play will be used as a tool to promote peacebuilding, but through delivery the activities will have direct impact on the physical and mental health and wellbeing of the participating children. In addition, the delivery of weekly peace clubs, life skills, MHM support, and PSS sessions will all positively impact the participants mental and physical health as they learn vital skills to lead a healthy lifestyle. The project will work to tackle gender inequality through the increased active involvement of girls in sport throughout the project lifecycle. Activities will be designed to cater for the needs of girls, specifically, 4,995 girls will receive menstrual hygiene management support and services, with consideration for sustainable and environmentally conscious products. Girls will be given the freedom to choose activity participation and ensure their active and enjoyed involvement. The project will also aim to remove negative attitudes towards girls and women through wide awareness raising.
Protecting the environment will be mainstreamed in the project by:
• Using existing grounds and facilities already in place at schools and communities, helping eliminate damage caused by creating new spaces.'
• Teaching key climate-driven life skills such as taking pride in the environment, the importance of protecting the natural space around you, litter picking and reducing waste, and community cooperation to protect their environment.
• Sharing MHM knowledge to adolescent girls that promotes the use of sustainable hygiene products.
Peace, justice, and strong institutions will be promoted through peace clubs. Children and Young people bring to the forefront the major issues in their communities and countries, and their engagement often serves as the connecting tissue between diverse peace efforts, by fostering social cohesion in their community, bridging divides between communities, and linking the peacebuilding at different levels. Children and young people have been known to forge pathways and spaces through arts, culture, sports and especially through social media interactions and the development of new technologies for peace. The primary goal of the peace clubs in this project, is to empower children and young people participating in the project to become agents of peace and promote peace-building initiatives in their own schools and communities.

The on-going crisis in Cameroons NWSW has affected over 900,000 children, many of whom have been exposed to violence, family separation, exploitation & abuse: with children being the first victims of the crisis. Many children have been separated from parents and their communities after fleeing fighting. The crisis has affected relationships, created mistrust & weakened social cohesion. Through play, children, adolescents, and young people can make unique contribution to peace and social cohesion.
