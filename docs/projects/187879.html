Project Summary

IOM will help to address the critical gaps in healthcare services in NWS by supporting one primary healthcare facility in Barisha, Idlib, through an IP. IOM will cover procurement of the facility’s medical supplies, running costs, salaries of essential staff, and urgent rehabilitation, allowing the health facility to be fully functional and retain its staff. Consultations in the PHC will include a comprehensive range of preventive and curative health services according to the WHO Essential PHC Service Package: basic trauma and injury care, communicable diseases (including compliance with early warning systems for disease outbreaks (EWARN reporting)), sexual and reproductive health, child health, basic nutrition services, basic mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, and non-communicable disease management.
In addition, IOM, through an IP, will operate one mobile health clinic to provide health care services in two IOM camps that are in remote locations without any current access to services, Kafr Nabi and Mashhad Ruhin. The mobile health team will service each camp 2-3 days per week and will include services such as communicable disease, child health, basic nutrition, MHPSS services, non-communicable disease management, and sexual and reproductive health and hold awareness sessions on various health, hygiene, and nutrition topics. The mobile health clinic will be able to provide basic medications onsite as well as orientate people towards vaccination sites for both routine childhood and COVID-19 vaccinations for adults.
IOM will support hospitals designated to receive COVID-19 patients and those who need hospitalization in the intensive care unit (ICU) with eight lifesaving ventilators with Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) devices. Under WHO’s technical guidance and supervision, these ventilators will be distributed to recently established COVID-19 hospitals in Azaz district, Aleppo. As there is currently only one hospital with an ICU in Azaz district (Mare'a hospital), and the catchment population of Azaz district is 620,671, these hospitals required conversion to COVID-19 case management facilities given the high COVID caseload in Azaz combined with a shortage of health facilities and personnel elsewhere in the district.
IOM will support the Syrian Immunization Group (SIG), which is the only entity authorized by the WHO to implement vaccine activities in NWS Syria, with the procurement of necessary equipment to ensure continuity of services, expand the reach of 200 mobile vaccination teams while maintaining quality data entry and access to the DHIS-2, a health information system. The mobile vaccination teams are vital in the effort to vaccinate the most vulnerable and remote populations in NWS deprived of accessible health care, including those in camps and other hard to reach areas, and to mitigate the effects of infection with the COVID-19 virus.
IOM, through an IP, will aim to support dialysis center in Jarablus to ensure lifesaving and continuity of care to the most vulnerable affected people under severe limitations to access specialized care by supporting the running cost of the dialysis center in Jarablus city (HeRAMS code: B0208014) and collaboration with WHO plans to support dialysis centers with dialysis kits. There are 7 functional dialysis machines in Jarablus dialysis center, and 2 of them had been allocated for patients with Hepatitis B (HBV) and Hepatitis C (HCV).
The IOM, through an IP, will aim to support the Haritan hospital for Women and Children located in Idlib - Harem - Al-Dana - Deir Hassan, which provides free health services to all those in need without discrimination, within the specializations of women's surgery, gynecology, obstetrics, pediatrics, and incubators, by providing incentives to the staff, monthly running cost, and purchasing medical and non-medical equipment, the hospital covers an area that includes 600,000 population, most of them live in camps.

4 - Needs

Only half of the health care facilities in NWS are currently functioning. Attacks on health care facilities have also forced some health responders to suspend their services. The COVID-19 pandemic further stressed the already weak health care system in NWS. Meanwhile, affected populations in NWS often live in very crowded conditions in and out of camp settings with limited access to clean water, food, and health care services.The third wave of COVID-19 hit NWS during the period from August to December 2021. At the same time, the COVID-19 vaccination rate in NWS is very low (almost 5%). An urgent response to the severe, life-threatening health needs in NWS requires a parallel approach, comprising a swift response to the expected wave (Omicron variant) to alleviate the over-stretched health system whilst simultaneously supporting the vaccination effort. This also requires the maintenance of ongoing, regular, and essential health services to meet the urgent health needs of children and adults alike and protect the health system.The Dialysis centers in Syria are at a breaking point, as they have been struggling since the beginning of the conflict to keep delivering services to the most vulnerable categories including Patients with End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD). IOM is in constant coordination with the health cluster to identify priority needs and with organizations working in the field to coordinate efforts and avoid overlapping activities.

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM will strengthen the response and preparedness against COVID-19 epidemic, by supporting hospitals designated to receive Covid-19 patients who need an intensive care unit with ventilators that will be able to provide oxygen to two patients at the same time, and it will also provide equipment and supplies to the Syrian Immunization Group to help it perform its tasks and work to increase proportion of the population who received the vaccine, which helps to increase the community's immunity against COVID-19.IOM will cover the gap in the health system regarding primary, secondary, and specialized health care by covering staff salaries and operating costs for:1. A mobile health clinic providing primary health services, nutrition services, and health awareness-raising services for the affected population in two IOM camps.2. A primary health care center in Barisha sub-district that provides general consultation in addition to pediatric services, reproductive health services, nutrition services, and health awareness-raising services.3. A dialysis center in Jarablus city, which provides hemodialysis services in Jarablus district and supports the operation costs for the hemodialysis center, including fuel, internet, stationery, cleaning materials, catering, meals, transportation, medicines, and maintenances as well.4. A women and children's hospital in the Al-Dana sub-district, which provides specialized services in gynecological surgery, obstetrics, pediatrics, and incubators.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Attacks on health care have been a prevailing feature of the Syria crisis. Despite extensive advocacy on the part of health actors, media and the UN, the attacks continue, and violence is still a critical issue facing healthcare providers and affecting their ability to deliver health services. The IOM is keen to take advantage of mechanisms for visibility, advocacy, and accountability as follows:• Utilizing the Syria Humanitarian Notification System.• Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM), as well as Health Cluster Flash Updates.• Continuing advocacy efforts by the health sector and UN agencies with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL).• Support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG) and humanitarian leadership is critical in highlighting protection challenges facing health workers across Syria.• Providing preparedness trainings for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents.• The COVID-19 epidemic is one of the most important factors affecting the work continuity of health facilities, and IOM is keen to follow all mitigation measures and use PPEs to reduce infection of health personnel.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IOM ensures that risk mitigation and prevention of SEA is mainstreamed into all activities and that PSEA and other related concerns are mainstreamed throughout the project. IOM maintains minimum PSEA standards ensuring it’s a) code of conduct includes PSEA language, b) all staff are trained on PSEA, c) internal SEA reporting mechanism and investigative capacity exist and are known within the organization, and d) PSEA awareness-raising activities with beneficiaries and local community are conducted during the activities. Specific mitigation measures also include:• Organizational policies and field activities have been assessed for gaps and risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse using PSEA Field Checklists.• The organization has a code of conduct that includes a comprehensive section on PSEA. The organization adopts the Humanitarian Code of Conduct (CoC) for the Syria Cross-Border Response (2020) and/or ensures that its own CoC is consistent with it. All staff, including contractors, sub-contractors, daily wage workers, and volunteers have understood and signed the CoC.• All staff are trained regularly on PSEA.• IOM has internal reporting mechanisms for both staff and beneficiaries to safely and confidently report complaints related to sexual and other misconduct.• IOM will work to take into consideration the GBV cases and will consider the survivor to be a priority to benefit from the services provided in this project to reduce violence and mitigate its effects.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

IOM will coordinate with the Health Cluster, WHO, COVID-19 taskforce and any other relevant partners to ensure that activities are not duplicated with other actors. Whenever possible, IOM will offer multi-sectoral support (such as FS support in TB centres) and referral pathways to specialized services for vulnerable populations. Additionally, IOM will involve all stakeholders throughout the project cycle including project design, implementation, and monitoring. IOM will communicate selection criteria to beneficiaries, provide services according to verified needs, and establish and communicate complaint and feedback mechanisms for all project activities. The safety and security play a vital role in the project design and implementation in terms of the project locations identification, implementation approach as well as contributing to resolving the security issues that might encounter the project team. Before any project start, IOM safety and security team in collaboration with access dept will also train the teams, including casual workers, on the best practices and mitigation measures to avoid the security risks and war remnants. With the start of the new pandemic infections in Syria, IOM also focus on the preventive measures and train/ increase the awareness of the staff and beneficiaries on the importance of applying the COVID-19 preventive measures in accordance with the WHO guidance to ensure the protection of staff and beneficiaries against COVID-19 virus.
