Project Summary

In 2023, Shafak Organization proposes to mitigate and respond to the protection risks faced by the conflict-affected people in Jabal Samaan, Jarablus, Al-Bab, and Azaz districts in Aleppo governorate, as well as in Harim, Ariha, and Idleb districts in Idleb governorate.
The project aims to empower local communities and conflict-affected individuals in Northwest Syria and support their role in building society and in determining their future by the provision of protection services and emergency protection services to 130,750 IDPs and host community members, through five community centers, and 28 mobile outreach teams. The provided protection services will comprise both mitigation and response services as well as specialized and non-specialized protection services.
The proposed project will offer gender and age-appropriate general protection services including, structured and recreational PSS, recreational and MHPSS activities, case management involving life-saving assistance to vulnerable people in ensuring their access to existing services, referral to specialized and non-specialized services, awareness raising on protection-related topics, life skills and vocational training to enhance the community resilience and livelihoods opportunities, legal assistance and legal awareness raising session of HLP and CSD through qualified and well-trained legal teams, protection monitoring, as well as supporting community committees. Moreover, Shafak will establish community committees from the same communities and train them on protection topics such as; CPMS, Safe identification, and referral and communication skills to enable them to identify and refer people in need to the case management team.
Shafak will maintain a gender-balanced team in all its projects in order to facilitate the access of all gender groups to services in line with humanitarian principles. Moreover, training on HLP and CSD will be delivered to partners’ legal staff and community actors in Syria and Turkey by specialized legal staff in accordance with the protection cluster and HLP TWG guidance and SOPs.
These services will be offered to vulnerable IDPs and host community members with particular emphasis on quality services to the following groups:
• Women-headed households
• Children-headed households
• PWDs
• Elderly women and men
• Family has children at risk of child labor
• UASC and orphans who are being cared for by another family.
• New IDPs who fled their home and have no financial means
In case of emergencies, the project activities will be shifted to respond to the emergency protection needs of newly displaced people delivering the protection emergency package like PFA, referrals, and emergency case fund support to respond to the urgent needs of IDPs.
The activities will emphasize equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical, and social specificities of targeted populations. All static centers will be rehabilitated and well-equipped to be disabled-friendly spaces.
COVID-19 precautionary measures will be taken in line with WHO, Global Protection Cluster and the Protection Cluster’s Guidance, including strict enforcement of physical distancing, minimizing of participants per activity to a maximum 12 people and maintaining the social distancing between BNFs and staff, the use of personal protective equipment (PPE) by staff/beneficiaries.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection emergency characterized by over 11 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world.Lack/loss of civil documentation impacts all population groups with specific vulnerabilities for women and children. It limits the ability of widowed or divorced women to inherit property, get custody of children, legally remarry, or register children born through subsequent relationships. Negative coping mechanisms are on the rise such as increased GBV, or child labor including its worst forms, which further strain the capacities of families and communities to protect the most vulnerable, especially children, adolescent girls, and boys. Older persons and persons with disabilities and chronic illnesses face a range of challenges unique to their individual circumstances, compounded by their displacement where familial/community support networks have broken down.Children and adolescents face violence, abuse, and exploitation in many life situations including exposure to new forms of violence due to harmful coping mechanisms within the family. Adolescent boys are more likely to be killed and injured, separated from family, or to be involved in child labour, while adolescent girls are particularly at risk of child marriage, online harassment, and other forms of GBV including sexual violence. “HNO 2022”

Monitoring visits will be carried out via male and female MEAL staff using session evaluation forms, checklists, pre-post evaluation, and satisfaction surveys. CM will be monitored and evaluated by the trained CM team ensuring confidentiality principles through the satisfaction survey tool and checking the CM files

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk Identified1: the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by the project personnel on project BNFs and women staff. Mitigation: 1- provide awareness raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA 2- train the new staff on PSEA, CSG and to have refresher trainings to the old staff. 3- Distribute banners, PSEA reporting mechanism including the suggestion box, WhatsApp number...etc. Risk 2: No reporting from SEA survivors: Mitigation: 1- Raise awareness between project BNFs about PSEA and reporting mechanisms. 2- Apply various reporting channels that are accessible to all BNFs. 3- Emphasize on the principle of confidentiality in dealing with SEA cases. 4- Assign gender-balanced PSEA focal points to encourage women and girls to report.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1:Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due to increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures:1.1 Close monitoring and participation in info-sharing mechanisms and meetings.1.2 Security-related SOPs in place. 1.3 Remote monitoring mechanisms in place.1.4 Maintain strong lines of communication with Shafak HQ security in order to preempt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover, or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly. Risk 2: Disputes between host communities and IDPs; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 2.1:Targeting host community, 2.2: ensure Complaint mechanism is accessible by all people. 2.3 integrity and clear verification process conducted according to announced criteria. 2.4 Prior coordination with local actors and stakeholders in the community to ensure the process.Risk: Outbreak of COVID 19; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 3.1:Daily check of staff to ensure their safety is a major symptom of the Coronavirus.3.2:Emphasis during work on the need to leave a distance of at least 1 between workers.3.3:The necessity not to exchange equipment and tools before sterilization. 3.4:Ensure the presence of awareness brochures within the work area and in a clear place.3.4:In case of imposed curfew. Suspend the activities and change the modality to be through service providers and distribute the dues of the staff
