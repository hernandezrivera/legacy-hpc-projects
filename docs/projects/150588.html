Project Summary

This project aims to provide protection to vulnerable houses by upgrading the state of the houses to reach the minimum standards of shelter cluster Repair and upgrade. AICBG will target around 300 household (1740 beneficiaries) in three marginalized areas in Gaza strip, which are (Rafah, Khanyounis, and central province); each area will have approximately 100 household.
This project will enhance Community Engagement through focus groups, workshops and meetings. Families targeted will be the focus in determining the intervention required. Neighborhood committees will take part,not to forget volunteers.
The project will benefit all the layers of the society including women, children, and elderly, unemployed people, low-income families and people who need urgent intervention.
Projects objectives align with country strategic and cluster objectives.
Country objectives:
The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, which solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
Cluster objectives:
• Displaced populations are provided support that mitigates immediate harm and ensures adequate and safe temporary shelter solutions.
• Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity.
• Capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner is increased.

Needs

Gaza strip is a conflict area where nearly two million inhabitant lives, most of them lives in harsh environment. Starting from exposure to harsh natural weather with no shelter protection, moving to the continuous rounds of conflicts including three major escalations and the ten years siege. All of these factors led to a huge destruction to economy, housing, infrastructure.AICBG implemented a number of projects targeting vulnerable families in Gaza strip at 2017 and 2018. AICBG staff conducted field visits 950 vulnerable family. AICBG engaged people in the process, through field survey, questionnaire, workshops and focus groups with beneficiaries and neighborhood committees to discuss the best possible interventions. According to the field survey, a lot of vulnerable cases visited needed urgent protection which will be provided in this project. Beneficiaries and neighborhood committees will take part into choosing the protection required.AICBG created a database according to the assessment made and the results were as following: The total number of vulnerable houses visited 950 house. 42% of the families live in houses whose ceiling is made of asbestos or metal sheets. Asbestos fibers inhalation is a major cause of lung cancer according to WHO, which is a direct cause of death. Metal sheets leakage problem at winter also causes displacement adding more suffering.  47% of the houses lacking appropriate doors (Main door, bathroom and kitchen) and windows, which leads to privacy, issues in the same family especially for women and girls. 13% of the houses have one or more disabilities, which need adjustment to fit theses disabilities especially in bathrooms, doors and ramps. 10% female headed household lives in a poor condition, which needs to be upgraded to the minimum humanitarian standards. 37% of the households live in houses with poor electrical connections or dangerous electrical installation that may pose a danger to their residents. 25% of the households live in poorly ventilated houses; These households suffer from severe indoor heat in the summer and extreme cold in the winter. 22% of the households suffer from a very poor sanitation and plumbing works. These leads to unfit health houses and act as a conduit for diseases outbreak. 40% of the families lack adequate health facilities that is suitable for human use and lack the minimum standards of public health, personal hygiene, privacy and human dignity. According to AICBG, database urgent intervention is a need to provide protection, which aligns with the standards of Shelter Repair and Upgrade. 300 families will benefit from the project from the three provinces targeted (Rafah, Khanyounis and Central Province).A previous study conducted by the Islamic Relief-Palestine (Poor families in focus – 2017) showing similar results to our work. This study provided data on (3342) households, a list with the results below:  37% of the families live in houses whose ceiling is made of metal sheets or asbestos.  17% of the households are not equipped with safe electric fittings; they have very dangerous electrical installations that can cause disasters for family members, especially children. 22% of the households live in poorly ventilated houses; These households suffer from severe indoor heat in the summer and extreme cold in the winter 15% of households having a very poor sanitation network that can often flood into the house while 8% of the households do not have a sewage network and depend on cesspits due to the absence of a sewage network in the area. According to the results of the field survey conducted, the vulnerable family’s needs urgent intervention in order to reach the minimum shelter cluster standards.

Activities

The project will be implemented according to shelter cluster repair and upgrade standards and “Arab and International Commission to Build Gaza” (AICBG) experience in this field. This project will target a (300) Eligible household from previous database AICBG collected from similar projects, in Cooperation with the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH).AICBG will divide the budget of the project into two parts. The first is remedial activity, which takes about 90%; the other is responsive activity, which have the rest 10% for emergency works.Mechanism of action:1. Making interviews with the beneficiaries (all members of the families including father, mother, elderly, sons and daughters) to determine the intervention required.2. Organizing workshops to the beneficiaries clarifying the project stages and the intervention required.3. Creating Focus groups with the beneficiaries and neighborhood committees to asses and determine the best possible intervention in this project. 4. Distribution of introductory leaflets and objectives of the project.5. choosing targeted beneficiaries:AICBG will apply the Assessment social data form and the shelter vulnerability score system, which aligns with the shelter cluster standards to assess the beneficiaries and make priorities.6. Sorting data acquired and creating a profile for each case.7. Creating sheets containing contact numbers for AICBG staff in order to give assistance to help the beneficiaries according to the project objectives. 8. A discussion with beneficiaries about implementing the project, self-help method will be used, in some cases neighborhood committees and other volunteers will take part in the process.9. A registered shelter cluster partner will participate with AICBG. The role of this partner is:1- Participation at Helping with the evaluation process.2- Technical assistance to the beneficiaries.3- Coordinating with neighborhood committees and volunteers to contribute in finishing the requirements of the project.4- Contributing in work evaluation and measuring the effect of the project on beneficiaries.10. A final report will be made showing all stages and works to each beneficiary.Monitoring and evaluation:1. AICBG will hire a staff for monitoring and evaluation.2. Periodical reports will be done (monthly, quarterly).3. Field visits to assure thing going according to plans.Community Accountability:1- Accountability: set up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints such as making evaluation model, questionnaire to estimate the service provided by the project.2- Establishing a complaint monitoring system for the targeted communities by providing numbers to contact and direct the issues to involved department.3- Local neighborhood committees will be established at each province. Protection mainstream: Responsive protection: AICBG will reserve 10% of the total budget for the responsive protection which is reducing risk of displacement by providing NFI, temporary shelter, Rent allowances, supply kits includes blankets, house ware and other necessary tools.This process will be fully coordinated with the shelter cluster and other NGOs working in this field. Findings and lessons learned will be shared with project stakeholders including the shelter cluster, local partners, and other NGOs to contribute to the organizational learning and promoting the effectiveness of future projects. AICBG will use standardized beneficiary selection criteria, which aligns with the Shelter Cluster’s vulnerability parameters.
