Project Summary

The objective of the project is to address the basic needs of most vulnerable communities through the rehabilitation of schools, and enhance resilience and wellbeing of school attending children in Raqqa and Aleppo Governorate. The 5 identified sub-districts present a medium-high education severity level (from 3 to 4).
DanChurch Aid (DCA), member of ACT Alliance, will rehabilitate 7 schools infrastructures in Raqqa governorate, and provide PSS curriculum in schools of Raqqa and Aleppo governorates. The school rehabilitation will focus on accessibility and gender sensitive WASH services. DCA will communicate with People with Disabilities attending the school to receive information about barriers and possible improvements school improvements to facilitate access.
Children in schools will be able to enroll in DCA 10-session PSS curriculum, adjusted for the age groups 6-9, 10-12, and 13-17. Providing sessions in schools complements DCA’s Protection and Child Protection intervention modalities through a mobile approach. This integration further allows referrals to Child Protection Case Management (CPCM), as well as identification of topics for information dissemination sessions proposed under Protection/CP. Schools where PSS sessions are implemented will receive a recreational kit, whose content will be decided based on need and adjusted based on school specific gaps or requests. These kits aim to enhance the longevity of psychosocial support sessions, providing heightened opportunities for recreational activities within the school after DCA’s activity ends.

3 - Needs

Political insecurity, cross-border conflict, health crises, and the impact of the economic instability and inflation, continue to deplete affected populations of resilience and enhances vulnerability. The unprecedented devastation of schools affects child wellbeing, where there is a lack of a safe learning environment and WASH facilities. Devastation may have adverse impact on children’s ability to overcome trauma or other psychosocial/psychological consequences of the conflict, as it may serve as a constant reminder. The combination of stressors hinders child development, which can be negatively impacted by prolonged/protracted exposure to stress. These are some of the factors which result in a non-attendance rate in Raqqa and Aleppo of 22% and 16% respectively, which are 2 of the highest 4 in the whole of Syria (WOS HNO, 2023). This situation exacerbates access and safety concerns for those with mobility challenges or disability (in 2022 it was assessed that 24% of people in Syria report having a disability of some kind). For example, Ar-Raqqa has one of the highest level of dissatisfaction regarding WASH facilities in schools, with a 86% of respondents dissatisfied (HNAP, WOS -WASH household survey 2022).

4 - Implementation of activities

To address the significant lack of safe learning environments, DCA will liaise with the local authorities to identify the school infrastructures most in need. DCA team will then proceed with the technical assessment of the schools, identifying the rehabilitation needs while consulting with the school administration and users through FGDs. Later we will select the schools based on DCA selection criteria and budget availability. Once the rehabilitation plans are approved by local authorities, the rehabilitation will start and be regularly monitored for quality assurance by the shelter team. The rehabilitated school will be later handed over to local authorities for daily management. Once the rehabilitation is completed, in collaboration with the M&E team an endline survey in conducted with KIIs to evaluate the project’s impact. A curriculum of 10 structured and sustained psychosocial support sessions, will be implemented for children aged 6-17, adjusted based on school administration identified priority needs. Identification of school children in need of individual support will occur to enroll said children in CPCM processes. Through independent monitoring and feedback, led by M&E teams – overall quality of PSS sessions will be assessed throughout the implementation phase. This, in combination with coordination with school administration and the community (through DCA Protection teams), as well as noting of ad hoc feedback will further feed into project adjustment and design.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Risk of intra- and inter (neighbouring) community tensions resulting from service delivery reaching a limited number of individuals/communities. Likelihood: Medium Impact: Medium Mitigation measures: Ensure community outreach explaining services and criteria as relevant prior to activity start, ensure active engagement with communities for early warning purposes, ensure engagement of caregivers and school administration in decisions relating to modalities.Protection Risk: Lack of acceptance of topics addressed during PSS service delivery, resulting in backlash against participants, school administration, or DCA staff.Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: Ensuring two-way communication with affected populations (caregivers and school administration), with clear requests for and noting of perceptions and potential reactions and adjusting messaging in a consultative manner.Protection Risk: Extremely vulnerable or marginalized groups are unable to access services, either physical access or through lack of knowledge of services.Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation Measures: Ensure coordination to ensure access to community-level mappings, with other agencies and community representatives to have up to date information on present population groups with AGD disaggregated figures.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA Staff members, or contracted staff for rehabilitation works. Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation Measures: All DCA Project staff will be regularly trained on DCA Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding, and PSEA, including reporting mechanisms. All DCA contracted staff, including suppliers and service providers, are held to the same commitments and sign the DCA Code of Conduct. All DCA PSS facilitating staff will conduct introductory sessions with clear messaging on PSEA and CFM mechanisms. These messages will include specific notes on the non-reciprocal nature of assistance. DCA ensures all its group activities are conducted with a minimum of two facilitating staff members, where culturally appropriate one of each sex. DCA is a member of the PSEA Network, and through the network will be continually updated on new/revised messaging, best practices, and reporting mechanisms. DCA PSEA FP will ensure dissemination of messaging to all project staff and management. Ensuring presence of a complaint box in schools targeted.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in locations impact project implementation causing delays and changes in operational areas and threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation measures: DCA has robust safety protocols and SOPs, including designated staff in each area of operation who actively monitors the security situation. The management team remains flexible, and changes project locations and modalities as and when required from a safety and overall needs perspective. DCA’s Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are regularly reviewed, and last comprehensive update was conducted in September 2022. If the security and access situation deteriorate, staff and beneficiary will prevail. Any pull out from programme areas will be determined on the basis of a simultaneous protection risk analysis, including assessment of risks in conducting the best practice of informing populations of intervention pause/end. Risk: Inability to conduct group activities/closure of schools due to COVID-19 or other air- or vector-borne disease infection prevention control measures. Impact would be delays and decreased access. Mitigation measures: DCA has a previously developed online module for children and caregivers. This will be reviewed to ensure continued relevance but is ready to be implemented as is. Access for beneficiaries would be reliant on internet connectivity, and availability to platforms such as Zoom and WhatsApp.
