Project Summary

The Overall goal for this project is to improve the health and well being of approximately 110,000 internally displaced persons.
The purpose of this project is protect people from water, sanitation and hygiene related diseases through provision of access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene promotion in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states.
The IRC will work to improving access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene; through the construction and rehabilitation of water points, construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, supply of WASH NFI's to vulnerable households and through hygiene messaging at IDP camps, host and returnee communities as well as in health centers, nutrition centers and schools. Working in health and nutrition centers will help improve the service delivery as well as provide a platform for sharing key hygiene messages to the patients and their care givers. Also households with severely malnourished children will be targeted for hygiene and water storage kits, which will be followed up with hygiene messaging in their homesteads.
The IRC through the WASH sector, will also work closely with other partners to continue promoting menstrual hygiene management (MHM) in IDP camps as well as continue coordinating during emergency response such as cholera outbreaks.

In Yobe and Adamawa state, the IRC will focus it's WASH activities in schools, health and nutrition centers and also adopt long term recovery and development strategy in host communities. The IRC will build on current experience of establishing, training and working closely with community structures which include WASH Committees, School WASH clubs and Community health clubs to promote community ownership of installed water and sanitation projects.
The IRC will continue to coordinate its activities with the WASH sector and work closely with the local and state level Ministry of water, Ministry of health and Ministry of education during the assessments, implementation and monitoring of its WASH projects.

Needs

Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 2018 denotes that the ongoing crisis in north-east Nigeria resulted to approximately 10.2 million people (52% women and girls and 48% men and boys) to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2018. This conflict, also led to the destruction of basic infrastructures and disruption of water and sanitation in schools and health centers. The HNO 2018 also estimates that approximately 6 out 10 displaced families live in host communities, while the others in formal or informal camps. With the security situation improving in some LGAs in north-east Nigeria, the IOM-led Displacement Tracking matrix (DTM) from 2014 recorded 1.3milion returnees of which 56% are women although majority of them are going back to locations where health and WASH infrastructure and services have been destroyed. For returnees and host communities whose WASH services are still functional, the continued influx of IDPs continues to increase pressure to the already limited water and sanitation infrastructure facilities. (UNICEF WASH Gap analysis, Sep 2018) confirmed that the average water allocation in 73 IDP camps across 11 LGAs in Borno state is 14 liters per person per day which is below the sphere standard of 15litres, while people sharing latrine drop hole is at an average of 72 people instead of the standard of 50 people per drop hole. From the multi-sectoral needs assessment carried out by OCHA INGOs in Adamawa in September 2017, 62% of respondents cited problems with access to water with 63% mentioning distance as the main constraint with 16% citing insecurity to the existing water points as their main barrier. The HRP 2018 denotes poor access to water, sanitation and acute nutrition conditions as the key issues that requires immediate focus. WASH assessments have confirmed that lack unsafe water, hygiene and poor sanitation exacerbates malnutrition among vulnerable groups including children under five, pregnant or breastfeeding women. The IRC noted overcrowding in most camps, increasing the risk of spread of waterborne diseases. Most IDPs have been displaced multiple times and often, without warning, leaving many belongings behind including essential NFIs such as water storage and hygiene kits. Women and girls are the hardest hit as critical NFIs for their menstrual hygiene needs are peripheral and largely ignored as other needs are prioritized. From a recent FGD carried out by the IRC in Bakassi and Konduga IDP camps, several responses remained consistent. Women and girls going through menses in the camps often face challenges of lack of sanitary supplies, such as lack of soap and detergent, access to water, or a space to dry and clean their menstrual cloths. Formerly, when women and girls were given disposable pads, they disposed of it in the pit latrines, which has been since discouraged by the WASH partners because it clogs latrines. Thus, the women and girls were feel that there were limited resources and options when it came to managing menstrual hygiene. Currently, post displacement, many family members tend to share a single shelter made of tarpaulin, and in some cases, two families will share a shelter and therefore the issue of lack of privacy post-displacement, and inadequate latrines and bathing shelters also resounded as another challenge in MHM in the camps. Out of the women interviewed 88% of women and girls preferred the re-usable sanitary pads.

Activities

The IRC will drill and equip new motorized boreholes and rehabilitate existing motorized boreholes in IDP camps, Schools, health centers and nutrition centers. In host communities and returnee areas in Yobe and Adamawa state, the IRC will prioritize hand pump boreholes for ease of maintenance by community members. The IRC will continue with operation and maintenance of all motorized hybrid borehole system in IDP camps through the provision of diesel, engine oil with continued chlorination of the water systems. For each water point constructed and rehabilitated, the IRC will establish and train a WASH committee that will be linked to the LGA level Ministry of water and natural resources. The IRC will construct gender segregated communal latrines and bathing shelters in IDP camps and informal settlement while following the WASH sectors recommended standard of one latrine drop hole to serve a maximum of 50 individuals and 1 bathing cubicle to serve 100 individuals. The IRC will ensure all latrines are gender marked and lockable from the inside. The IRC will also install a privacy wall to all female latrine and bathing facilities. One door in each communal facility will be constructed with an access ramp and doorway to allow for the disabled and elderly to use with ease. The IRC will work with WASH com members to maintain the latrines and bathing facilities. In host communities in Adamawa and Yobe states, the IRC will build on current experience and construct household latrines through community participation where community members were mobilized to contribute labor and locally available materials with the IRC only providing hardware materials for the concrete slabs. Each latrine facility will be provided with a hand washing station with users through community volunteers encouraged to refill the hand washing stations with water as needed. In host communities, the IRC will encourage community members to construct tippy taps in their homestead. The IRC will work closely with the education sector partners to provide basic WASH services in schools across Adamawa, Yobe and Borno states. The IRC will provide new motorized and hand pump boreholes, rehabilitate existing motorized and hand pump water points, construct latrine facilities that promote Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) for women teaching staff and girls as well as provide MHM and hygiene messaging through the establishment and training of school WASH clubs. The IRC will improve service delivery in Nutrition and health centers through the construction of new and rehabilitation water points, construction of sanitation facilities and medical waste management facilities. The IRC will also use this platform to share key hygiene messaging through brief hygiene sessions to the sick and there care givers that will be conducted by trained Community health workers (CHEWs). Also the IRC will target households with children discharged from the Nutrition centers with WASH NFIs. The IRC through coordination with the WASH sector will continue to coordinate its hygiene messaging and IEC materials to pass key hygiene messages to the IDP populations. The IRC will develop and supply IEC materials, identify and train Community hygiene volunteers (CHVs) in IDP camps. In host communities the IRC will train community health clubs (CHCs) and work closely with mother to mother support groups. The IRC will target vulnerable households in camps for the supply of water storage and hygiene kits. This will target new arrivals as well as top-ups for the existing populations. The IRC will also target women and girls of menstruating age in IDP camps and schools with MHM kits containing re-usable pads, plain cloth/wrapper, underwear, pegs, clothes line, bathing and detergent soap. The IRC will ensure at least 60% of members are women in the CHCs, School WASH clubs and CHVs to incorporate views from women and girls.
