Project Summary

UNFPA will continue to promote, coordinate and provide accessible, confidential, survivor-centered services to address GBV in line with the Inter-Agency Standards for GBV in Emergency Programming. In all UNFPA 23 supported Women Friendly Spaces (WFS) and selected 15 health facilities, case management, mental health and psychosocial support services, clinical management of rape, family planning services and counseling, treatment of sexually transmitted infections and emergency referrals will be provided to GBV survivor and vulnerable women and girls. UNFPA fosters social cohesion by working in both refugee (80%) and surrounding host communities (20%).
Prevention and mitigation strategies, mechanisms and initiatives to support community-based interventions in line with IASC principles and guidelines and AAP principles will be strengthened. The project will ensure community engagement and awareness aiming at mobilizing communities, including youth and men utilising SASA methodology to address negative social norms and to prevent and respond to GBV in a safe and timely manner. As women and girls continue to face protection risks, advocacy and communication for change will be implemented to guarantee their dignity and safety. To ensure the safeguarding of beneficiaries, UNFPA will continue to collaborate with the PSEA network to reinforce Sexual Exploitation and Abuse reporting and response mechanisms and conduct training on SEA.
The GBV sub sector will enhance engagement with other sectors and government for integration of GBV prevention, mitigation and response programming for quality services of GBV survivors using Inter-Agency Minimum Standards. The sub sector will expand comprehensive GBV prevention and response programs for women, and girls, focusing on case management and multi-sectoral referral systems. UNFPA will continue to mainstream GBV interventions for DRR in its Disaster Response emergency preparedness and response plans, through specialized capacity building PERU team and ensure a contingency stock of dignity kits.
Capacity will be enhanced on the IASC GBV Guidelines to sector leads, government stakeholders, MoWCA, Bangladesh Police, implementing partners and fostering effective coordination in planning, implementing and monitoring essential GBV risk mitigation actions in all humanitarian response and services. This will be supported with regular community based GBV risk monitoring and joint safety audits. The JRP 2021, will deliberately target disability inclusion in GBViE response.

1 - Identified Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) is a global phenomenon that exists beyond cultural, geographical, religious, social and economic contexts. It is a human rights violation which undermines the health, safety and dignity of its victims. In Cox’s Bazar, (GBV) continues to be a threat to women and girls in the Rohingya refugee camps and surrounding host communities. With many refugees having experienced serious violence during flight, they are also adjusting to factors that contribute to daily risk of GBV: increased overcrowding, stress due to the uncertainty of the protracted situation, limited privacy and lack of lighting across the sites, all of which exacerbate existing safety and security risks. Furthermore, negative practices have arisen such as increased child marriage, trafficking, and isolation of women and girls inside households. The reported types of GBV incidents include rape, physical assault, sexual violence, forced marriage, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. According to the August GBVIMS incidence report, 84.4% of all violence incidents were related to Intimate Partner Violence. Considering that GBV is generally under-reported, the recorded cases represent only the tip of the iceberg. This highlights the continued urgent need of ensuring GBV survivors have access to quality comprehensive GBV services and ensuring protection mechanisms are in place. The most recent joint multi sector needs assessment reports that 74% of refugee households in the camps mentioned ‘Majihis’ as their preferred point of referral for cases of sexual violence. These findings raise concerns given that the Mahji system is highly male-dominated and often criticized for being exploitative. The Protection Sector Working Group reported that the Majhi system hinders gender equality in the camps, with serious allegations of corruption, extortion and charging fees for access to humanitarian aid, including with instances of violence and GBV. As such, upscaling awareness-raising on GBV services and safe referral points and intensifying behavioral change programs to address socio-cultural barriers remains a priority. Findings of a Rapid Gender Analysis 2020 conducted among the Rohingya Refugees and Bangladeshi host communities, indicate that COVID-19 pandemic has increased gendered safety and security risks for the women, girls, and boys. The findings show increases in intimate partner violence (IPV), polygamy, transphobic violence, and violence against sex workers, as well as a rise in both girls and boy's child marriages. The addendum to the Joint Response Plan reported that specific and restrictive gender norms have also disproportionately impacted women and girls during the Covid19 pandemic and their risk of exposure to GBV has increased due to the need to stay indoors, as well as the reduced presence of case workers to receive and address GBV incidents. The home of refugee women which should be the safest environment for them is no longer a sanctuary; the August 2020 GBVIMS incidence report highlighted that 91.1% of all violence incidents being reported occurred in their own residence. Women and girls from surrounding host communities have unaddressed basic needs and are also exposed to serious protection risks due to a combination of factors, including poverty, limited access to livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services. The Rohingya influx has inevitably had a significant impact on competition for limited existing resources, access to land, and the environment in host communities as well as inter-community relations. Respondents from the Host community in the Joint MSNA mentioned women are particularly vulnerable due to their financial dependence on their husbands and less likely to receive justice as a result of corruption.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Provision of GBV lifesaving services in 23 UNFPA supported Women Friendly Spaces in camps (19) and surrounding host communities (04) including survivor centered case management services based on the ''do no harm'' principles, mental health psychosocial support interventions and emergency referrals. 2. Provision of GBV-SRH integration in UNFPA supported Women Friendly Spaces. 3. Provision of comprehensive case management services for GBV survivors in UNFPA supported health facilities. 4. Provision of community engagement and awareness aiming at mobilizing communities, including youth and men, to prevent and respond to GBV in a safe and timely manner, to seek sustainable solutions, and to address issues of stigma. 5. Support life skills development programmes in 10 UNFPA supported Women Led Community Centers in camps (8) and surrounding host communities (2). 6. Strengthen community-based Protection systems to prevent and mitigate protection risks by supporting different community groups. 7. Ensure capacity building of partner organization on GBV related topics including GBV basic principles, case management, Mental Health Psycho Social Support, psychological first aid, suicide prevention, GBVIMS. 8. Promote the inclusion and participation of communities of concern, particularly women, persons with disabilities, sex workers, transgender and minorities in all aspects of programming. UNFPA will also participate in the newly formed Working group on Gender Diverse Population. 9. Promote equal opportunity for Rohingya refugees with disabilities to access and benefit from UNFPA’s GBV services through increased collaboration with organizations specialising on disability inclusion. 10. Provide high quality technical assistance and supervision to ensure GBV services adhere to the do no harm principle and IASC guidelines; and Strengthen advocacy and communication for GBV prevention and response. 11. UNFPA will ensure a robust Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) response strategy is implemented across different activities and will include strengthening of reporting mechanisms, SEA training to staff and volunteers. Selected PSEA focal points in WFS will receive training on SEA investigation. 12. Continue to strengthen GBV SS coordination to ensure adequate multi sector response and survivors have access to quality GBV services. This will include strengthening of the quality of service provision and monitoring through capacity building of providers. “Do no harm “principle will be prioritized through updated safe and dignified referral systems, mapping of available services and the promotion of the GBV standard operating procedures. The SS will also ensure community awareness raising for women, girls, men and boys are streamlined with intersectoral partners, PERU team and include messages on GBV services, COVID 19, DRR and PSEA. The strategy will be implemented in collaboration with national, and international humanitarian actors, and increasingly with the MoWCA, Bangladesh Police and other government of Bangladesh service providers. 13. Ensure provision of essential preventive and mitigating measures are included in emergency preparedness response plans and initiatives by GBV SS partners and ensure the procurement, pre-positioning and distribution of dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls at risk of GBV. 14. Ensure capacity building for all GBV focal points in the Protection Emergency Response Unit and other sectors on GBV Mainstreaming and strengthen referral mechanisms to ensure survivors access available services. 15. Strengthen advocacy and communication for GBV prevention and response.
