Project Summary

In rural Mayo-Danay, the dual challenges of widespread home childbirth and the devastating effects of recent floods have left many children without legal identity. Traditional and cultural practices often lead mothers to deliver at home under the supervision of traditional birth attendants, bypassing formal health facilities where birth certificates can be automatically issued. Additionally, many mothers lack awareness of the importance of birth registration, further perpetuating the issue.
Persistent floods on the other hand, have exacerbated these challenges, displacing families, limiting access to registry offices, and making it even harder for mothers to access registration services. Without birth certificates, children face barriers to education, healthcare, and legal protection, limiting their access to future opportunities and exposing them to risks such as child marriage and exploitation.
This project addresses these issues by leveraging community-based solutions and integrating birth registration into local practices. It aligns with Cameroon’s commitment to achieving universal birth registration and UNICEF’s child protection goals.
Summary
The intervention titled "Securing Rights, Building Resilience : Ensuring Birth Registration for Vulnerable Children in the Far North Region of Cameroon" aims to tackle the dual challenges of unregistered births and the aftermath of flooding in rural Mayo-Danay.
Purpose: The primary goal is to ensure that all children have legal identity through birth registration, which is a critical step towards safeguarding their rights and access to essential services such as education, healthcare, and legal protection. The project also focuses on raising awareness among mothers about the importance of birth registration and incorporating these practices into local customs.
Target:
Geographic Focus: The project targets the rural areas of Mayo-Danay in the Far North Region of Cameroon, which are particularly affected by home childbirths and floods.
Beneficiaries: The primary beneficiaries are the children born in these regions who are currently without legal identity. Additionally, the project supports mothers, traditional birth attendants, and community leaders who play a crucial role in the birth registration process.
By leveraging community-based solutions and aligning with national and international child protection goals, this intervention strives to build resilience and secure the rights of vulnerable children in the Far North Region of Cameroon.

Community Engagement and Awareness: Through the involvement of TBAs, community leaders, and religious authorities, the project raises awareness about the importance of birth registration. The use of culturally adapted communication methods, including local languages, storytelling, and visual aids, ensures messages resonate with diverse audiences. Localized Communication Strategies: Targeted communication efforts aim to change community norms and attitudes towards birth registration, fostering a sense of collective responsibility. 4. Logistical and Financial Barriers Families in remote areas face logistical and financial barriers to accessing registration services, contributing to low birth registration rates. Activities Addressing This Need: Mobile Registration Units: By bringing registration services directly to remote villages, the project reduces logistical and financial barriers for families. This approach simplifies the registration process and ensures more children receive birth certificates. Integration of TBAs: Empowering TBAs to facilitate the registration process ensures that families can register births without incurring additional costs or traveling long distances. 5. Inadequate Civil Registration Infrastructure The local civil registration system lacks the capacity to manage mobile and community-based registration processes, limiting its effectiveness in increasing birth registration rates. Activities Addressing This Need: Strengthening Civil Registration Capacity: The project trains local registration officers to manage mobile and community-based systems, building a resilient administrative infrastructure. The introduction of disaster-resilient tools ensures the continuity of services despite environmental challenges. 6. Policy Gaps and Lack of Supportive Legal Framework There is a need for policy advocacy to integrate home-birth registration and simplified processes into national policies, ensuring long-term improvements in birth registration rates. Activities Addressing This Need: Policy Advocacy for Sustainable Change: The project advocates for integrating home-birth registration and simplified processes into national policies. This creates a legal and procedural framework that supports continuous improvement in birth registration rates, ensuring the program’s long-term impact. Innovative Approaches The project employs several innovative approaches to address these needs effectively: Community-Led Registration Networks: Leveraging TBAs and local leaders as key actors in the registration process creates a decentralized network that integrates formal and informal systems, building trust and reducing resistance. Localized Communication Strategies: Using culturally adapted communication methods ensures messages about the importance of birth registration resonate with diverse audiences. Mobile Units with Immediate Issuance Capability: Equipping mobile units to issue birth certificates on-site eliminates delays and enhances efficiency. Resilient Infrastructure Design: Introducing disaster-resilient tools ensures long-term functionality and continuity of services despite environmental challenges. Monitoring Through Participatory Methods: Involving community members and TBAs in monitoring progress creates a feedback loop that enhances program effectiveness and fosters a sense of ownership. Anticipated Impact The project aims to achieve the following outcomes: Immediate Outcomes: Increased birth registration rates, particularly among children born at home or in flood-affected areas. Behavioral Change: Improved awareness and acceptance of birth registration within communities, fostering a sense of collective responsibility. Systemic Resilience: A strengthened, disaster-ready registration system that integrates traditional practices with formal processes, ensuring continuity of services. Long-Term Change: Enhanced access to education, healthcare, and legal protection for registered children...
