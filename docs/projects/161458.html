Project Summary

There can be no health services without medicines and medical supplies. This project will target all the ten operational hubs states, focusing more in areas which bear the highest burden of the current floods, IDPs, areas with disease outbreaks and other vulnerable segments; (children and women of childbearing age, who account for 30% of the population). Preparedness and prevention activities on EVD will also be given high priority in EVD high risk counties. WHO will cover all the Health Cluster priority locations with emphasis on the earmarked for integrated approach with three other Clusters.
WHO will be engaged in frontline health services by using the emergency medical mobile teams. The team will be deployed in areas where there is outbreak of diseases, large population displacement, areas which have been prioritized in the Needs assessment working groups. The teams will conduct outbreak investigations, collection of samples, training of frontline health care workers and multi-sectoral needs assessments.
WHO will also be involved in procurement and prepositioning of core-pipeline. The core pipeline will enable the procurement and prepositioning of inter-agency emergency health kits to support health cluster partners providing life-saving humanitarian response in deep field areas. These will include VHF kits, IEHK, DDK, Nutrition Kits, Basic Unit IEHK, Major and Minor Surgical Kits, Malaria Kits including antimalarial drugs and malaria RDTs, and Trauma & Surgical Kits), basic medical equipment & supplies for life saving surgery & comprehensive EmNOC in selected health facilities, state & county hospitals
The existing disease surveillance system [IDSR and EWARN] will be strengthened through verification of health alerts and events, outbreak preparedness and response. Supportive supervision will also be provided to enhance surveillance capacities at state, county, and health facility level. Rapid health assessments in acute & protracted emergencies, and operationalize a national Public Health Emergency Operations Center at national level. Medical mobile teams to will deployed to provide rapid response in areas with limited or no humanitarian partners in a timely manner, and provide lifesaving health services to displaced populations, hence reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality
WHO will target to reach a minimum 450000 beneficiaries and a maximum of 500000 beneficiaries. It is expected that these supplies will complement the regular streams of the MoH and bilateral funding to partners.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian operations in South Sudan has entered its fifth year in 2018, and remained one of the most expensive and challenging. According to UNOCHA, the number of people affected since 2013 reached more than 4m including 1.9m internally displaced with up to 85% estimated to be women and children. Access to health services has been eroded. Disease outbreaks have lasted longer than ever and reached previously unaffected areas, weakening the already vulnerable people’s resilience and ability to cope with multiple shocks. In 2017, South Sudan registered the longest-running cholera outbreak in its history, which began in June 2016. Vaccine preventable diseases like measles spread remained unchecked, and cases of kala-azar and meningitis are on the rise. With only less than optimum number of health facilities fully operational in most part of the country, the absence of basic health care services means that cases of emergency obstetric and other preventable disease continued to pose significant public concern. Floods, recurrent outbreak, conflict and displacements are key hazards that continue to affect a large population of South Sudan. Over 50% of the country is inaccessible because of either flooding or the continuing armed conflict, which hampers humanitarian access and increases the cost of implementing emergency operations. The health system is very fragile and this has become a public health crisis. The ongoing Ebola virus disease outbreak in Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) has posed a greater health risk in South Sudan. As of 31st October 2018, some 285 cases were reported with 180 (63%) deaths. Security remained the biggest challenge faced by the response teams in Beni and Butembo of DRC, undermining the response activities. Thus, putting both civilians and frontline health workers to greater risk of spreading the Ebola virus to South Sudan and other neighbouring countries. Sexual and gender-based violence is rampant recording high figures of 4,310 in 2016 and 5,732 in 2017 respectively with survivors lacking access to adequate services. Cross cutting issues like HIV/AIDS, TB, Mental health, disabilities and non-communicable diseases including lifesaving trauma care are being cut-off from life-saving services. This project will target all the ten operational hubs states, focusing more in areas which bear the highest burden of IDPs, and other vulnerable segments; children and women of childbearing age, who account for 25% of the population). Preparedness and prevention activities on EVD will also be given high priority in EVD high risk counties. It is essential to have targeted emergency preparedness in some states depending on their vulnerability to emergencies. Health cluster partners heavily rely on WHO supported supplies due to the poor supply systems. Efforts to strengthen the pipeline in 2020 will continue to avoid breaks and ensure that health partners are able to promptly response to public health threats. There are glaring gaps in life saving surgical interventions. All state hospitals rely on WHO to supply them with vital equipment and supplies for case management. This minimized the need for referrals and prevented unnecessary deaths from delayed treatment; WHO remains the only agency involved in such interventions and identifies needs in hospitals (such as training for male and female health workers) and supports training courses designed to ensure they have the knowledge and skills to perform emergency surgical care. This project will strengthen capacity for emergency and epidemic preparedness and response in states with populations of humanitarian concern and further expand the delivery of essential health care package to the most vulnerable populations in South Sudan.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Health is a human right, not a mere service. WHO ensures that the provision of emergency health kits to complement front-line health services contributes to the well-being of the communities, and makes the beneficiaries aware through the accountability mechanisms in place that services are available. Kits for the management of trauma, rape and mental health disorders are included in this project. The emergency medical mobile teams will bring service close to the deep front communities to ensure that they do not travel long distances in search of service.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

WHO will engage the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own it. Stakeholder workshops will be organized in the location identified for implementation of the response. The community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CHD, community leaders, women, youth groups, religious leaders and representatives of beneficiaries. This will be the medium through which communities will be encouraged to express their concerns, views and provide regular feedback to the implementing partner in a regular structured modality. Other reasonable modalities for feedback that is useful to the communities/beneficiaries will also be considered. This feedback will form part of the project performance reporting to the Health Cluster and will help guide the fine tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experience
