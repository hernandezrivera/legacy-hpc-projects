Analysis of the general situation in the country
The 2023 Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview report shows that the context in Syria remains one of the most complex humanitarian emergencies in the world. As the thirteenth year of the protracted crisis passes, Syria's humanitarian and economic indicators continue to deteriorate and humanitarian services continue to decline as the country has witnessed a cholera epidemic and several climate disasters. This exacerbated the already deteriorating situation, as economic activities continued to contract, the value of the currency began to decline, and the prices of basic goods rose sharply, in addition to rising transportation costs for obtaining health services due to rising prices for medical services and drug prices. In light of these factors, Syria witnessed the earthquake disaster last year, which increased the demand for rehabilitation services as a result of the resulting disabilities.
Despite all national efforts to support the public health system, Syria still suffers from signs of weakness in the health system. Coinciding with public health emergencies and the many challenges that affect not only the accessibility and availability of health services, but also the physical, mental and psychological health of the population, this is reflected in the regions of northeastern Syria, especially the population living in camps, which are areas in urgent need of strengthening Health services and strengthening humanitarian protection services. As a result of the impact of climate disasters, such as lack of rain and the lack of clean and sterile drinking water, which causes further deterioration in the epidemiological context and exposes the population to an increased risk of contracting infectious diseases.
According to data from the System for Monitoring the Availability of Health Resources and Services throughout Syria for the second quarter of 2023, 35% of hospitals are classified as out of service or partially functioning, while this percentage for primary health care centers reaches 38%. .
The general situation in the targeted area
Al-Hawl camp, the largest refugee camp in northeastern Syria. It hosts 64,683 refugees, including Iraqis (45%), Syrians (40%), and people of foreign origin (15%), who were displaced from the lands occupied by ISIS after its recent battles against the SDF forces. 2019. More than 90% of the camp’s population are women and children, two-thirds of whom are under the age of 18. The camp opened a long time ago. It is closed and surrounded by a barbed wire fence, with the movement of people restricted through tightly controlled entry and exit gates. The camp is now very crowded, with an average of seven people living in each tent, and sometimes several families sharing the same space. The camp consists of 8 sectors. 1, 2, 3, 4 and 7 accommodate Iraqi citizens, 5, 6 and 8 accommodate displaced Syrians, and Appendix A contains third-country citizens.
