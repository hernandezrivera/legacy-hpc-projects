Project Summary

The ongoing conflict in Sudan, particularly in the Blue Nile region, has led to a significant number of civilians taking refuge and facing challenges such as insecurity, limited access to basic services, and limited access to healthcare. The Sudanese Armed Forces and Rapid Support Forces have been involved in the conflict, resulting in over 6.1 million individuals (242,666 households) taking refuge both inside and outside the country. The ongoing conflict has also led to increased sexual and gender-based violence, family separation, and child recruitment. Health facilities in the region are struggling to provide adequate staff and supplies, and the situation remains unstable. All the clusters face the following challenges: insecurity, access, communications challenges, limited operating capacities, supply chains, bureaucratic impediments, looting, operational costs, and lack of funding. In the Blue Nile there are 118,093 displaced people arriving in six localities since the conflict began. The conflict has spread to central Darfur and West Kordofan affecting all the basic services. This project will be implemented in Zalingei town with WASH activities, addition to WASH in West Kordofan in El Nuhud and El Khoway localities. Fighting in Sudan has resulted in devastating consequences for civilians. Needs in the country across all clusters were already at an all-time high, when fighting broke out. The protection of civilians remains a key concern, with reports of increased sexual and gender-based violence as well as reports of family separation and child recruitment. Access to health continues to be hampered by the ongoing conflict. Health facilities lack sufficient staff and supplies and are barely functional in some areas. All the clusters face the following challenges: insecurity, access, communications challenges, limited operating capacities, supply chains, bureaucratic impediments, looting, operational costs, and lack of funding. In Blue Nile, the security situation remains stable but unpredictable, in terms of displacement in BN, IOM/DTM estimates 118,093 displaced people (23,643 households), added to that Blue Nile is one of the regions with protracted IDPs and returnees who didn’t adequately re-establish after the displacement because of the long-lasting conflict in the area. WASH activities will be implement in Gedarif, Blue Nile, West Kordofan and Central Darfur and it will complement the other sectors and will provide lifesaving activities and gender and culturally sensitive WASH and protection services while ensuring sustainable environmental practices for newly vulnerable displaced, areas prone to cholera and acute watery diarrhoea (AWD) outbreaks and flooding, for vulnerable residents and vulnerable protracted displaced in Blue Nile state for 65,970 (36,284 women, 29,687 men, 263,88 of them are children) from Damazine, Kurmuk, Rosaries, Wad Almahi, Bau and Geissan localities in Blue Nile state, 70,000 ( 23,100 female, 18600 male, 15,400 girl & 12,600 boy) from Zalingei locality Central Darfur. In West Kordofan will target 18,370 persons (7,348 women, 6,429 men, 2,755 girls and 1,837 boys) in El Nuhud and El Khoway. In Gedarif 13,555 (4,473 women, 3,660 men, 2,982 girls and 2,440 boys) from two localities West Galabat and Wasat Gedarif.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

across all sectors the project is going to implement, IRW will mainstream protection and GBV. The project will ensure that services access points are no cause of protection concerns or give way for GBV occurrence. In all trainings that are rendered by the project, a session will be dedicated to cover issues related with protection and GBV to increase alertness and vigilance among the community structures. In addition to the project member’s structures, the project will also have a Protection and GBV concern focal points who will be equipped with the required support so that they are able to observe and identify any concerns and immediately notify the project member’s structures for the required immediate action. IR Sudan staff will ensure all IR representatives involved in the project including staff, volunteers, implementing partners, contractors, donors or guests when visiting are aware of and adhere to IR Sudan’s Code of Conduct. The IRS Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect children, vulnerable women and men that are beneficiaries of the project from violence, exploitation, neglect and abuse perpetrated by employees, partner organizations, contractors, official visitors and their communities. Communities and children will be sensitized on the rights of children and made aware of the complaints mechanism in place to ensure they feel comfortable to report and concerns of abuse and confident that appropriate action will be taken and will ensure that they are protected from any repercussions. Regular monitoring and auditing of the IR field offices will be carried out by CO technical staff to ensure that policies and procedures are observed, and participation and feedback from the communities is availed.
