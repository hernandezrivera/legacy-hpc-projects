Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to save lives by providing integrated and sustainable WASH services to 166,506 crises-affected communities in Greater Fangak (Fangak, Pigi/Canal, and Ayod counties of Jonglei State) This will be accomplished through the provision of safe and clean water, hygiene, and sanitation to reduce water-born disease and thereby decrease preventable morbidity and mortality.
Fangak, Pigi/Canal and Ayod counties in Jonglei State continue to experience high levels of food insecurity, driving risk of famine rates above the FAO Critical Level. As such this project will address water bonne-related illness by building resilience through integrating WASH in nutrition cholera prevention and sustainable agriculture and also provide timely, equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene for Conflict-affected returnees, and flood-affected IDPs. Host communities and returnees, thus contributing to the WASH clusters strategy for 2024.
WASH Program:
With the vision of "building hope and health for life, "Alaska Health Project South Sudan (AHPSS) has static presence in Greater Fangak Counties since 2008. The mission of the organization is to improve health outcomes by providing clean water and supporting agricultural initiatives for vulnerable populations within Fangak, Canal/Pigi Counties, and Ayod Counties.
To date, AHPSS has successfully drilled 60 boreholes in 7 Payams of Fangak and 3 in Canal/Pigi, with a scale-up plan to 2 Payams in Ayod county. According to Fangak County RRC, there are an estimated 80 total boreholes in Fangak County, meaning AHPSS is responsible for creating 75% of the county’s total borehole infrastructure. In addition, there are very few boreholes in Canal/Pigi and Ayod counties. AHPSS is estimated to have drilled around 50% of these wells
AHPSS creates new water infrastructure and understands the geohydrology of the state. AHPSS specializes in the construction of elevated motorized mini solar water yards using gravity flow, flood resilient platforms in flooded areas, reconstructions, and rehabilitation and repair of boreholes
To enhance the sustainability of these boreholes, AHPSS conducts routine water quality screening and testing for E-coli and Total Coliform, by ensuring that all contaminated boreholes are disinfected. AHPSS conducts Safety Audits of WASH facilities to enhance community acceptance and commitment of water safety net.
AHPSS conducts disinfection of water points. An estimated 150 waterpoint disinfections will take place this program year. AHPSS recognizes that Ayod Counties in Greater Fangak is a priority 1 location for the WASH cluster with almost 99% of the population taking untreated water directly from the river or swamps.
AHPSS provides skill training for pump mechanics and maintains a network of woman-led waterpoint management committees (WMC).
AHPSS supports linkages of the local private sector for WASH spare parts in the Fangak, Pigi, and Ayod Counties. This effort will guarantee the availability of WASH supplies (spare parts, plumbing materials). One of the key learnings from the operation and maintenance of water supply infrastructure (Motorized Mini Solar Water Yards and IM2 hand pump) has been the need for local support in the provision of skills to repair pumps and pipelines, and vendors to readily supply spares.
AHPSS will strictly enforce the Safeguarding policy which advocates for zero-tolerance to sexual exploitation. AHPSS will ensure that all project staff have a police clearance or background check, an orientation on safeguarding including GBV and PSEA
AHPSS program is scaling up to drill new boreholes in Greater Fangak (Fangak, Pigi/Canal, and Ayod Counties). In addition, AHPSS will conduct borehole rehabilitation that were submerged by flooding, and flood-resilient platforms, upgrade existing boreholes to water yards, repair an estimated 60 boreholes, and train new waterpoint management committees.

1. Needs

South Sudan is currently facing a complex humanitarian crisis characterized by extensive internal displacements. The underlying causes of these displacements include communal clashes, flooding, insecurity, violence, natural disasters, and cross-border movements. Between 1st and 15th October 2024, enumerators from the Displacement Tracking Matrix documented significant disaster (flood) related displacements affecting 10,719 individuals. As of 25 February 2024 according to the IOM/UNHCR/RRC monitoring dashboard, 577,129 people have crossed the border from Sudan to South Sudan through various points of entry from 15 April. So far in February, 36,202 people were recorded arriving, of which 58 percent were South Sudanese and 41 percent were Sudanese. This makes the average daily arrival 1,448 people, with daily recorded arrivals ranging from 793 (25 February) to 2,094 (2 February). The 27th situation report on the response to the Sudan crisis, covering the reporting period of 3 to 16 February, covers response, gaps, and challenges. Returnees from Sudan Arrive in Fangak County: On 26 February, the RRC in Fangak County reported the arrival of 642 individuals (107 households) into Fangak from Sudan, hosted by relatives and friends The current flood situation: Findings from the recent IRNA joint partners assessment conducted in Fangak in September 2024 show that about a total of 139,382 individuals from Returnee, IDP and Host communities have been affected by flooding. The flood has destroyed and impacted villages from all 7 payams in Fangak County. Over 80% of the villages in Fangak County have been damaged or destroyed by flooding in some capacity. The affected population is facing limited basic humanitarian services and coping with the shocking effect of the flood. The hungry children, mothers, and the elderly need life-serving food and WASH services. It terms of WASH, Flood waters have contaminated most of the water sources, including wells and surface water, making them unsafe for consumption and thereby threatening life. Many households reported difficulty accessing clean water due to damaged infrastructure and increased distances to safe water points as many HH have had to flee to higher ground. The IRNA emphasizes the urgent need to disinfect water points, protect water points with flood-resilient infrastructure, and create additional water points in newly settled high ground to protect life. According to the IRNA, A significant number of latrines have been destroyed or rendered unusable due to flooding. There has been an increase in open defecation practices especially around the constructed dykes and in the waters as a result of the lack of functional sanitation facilities, posing a high risk of waterborne diseases in the IDP camped locations. Health partners have reported a suspected case of cholera this week which is an immediate threat to life exacerbated by the lack of proper sanitation facilities. The IRNA stresses the need to protect existing latrines with flood-resilient infrastructure GBV has also been an issue facing vulnerable communities affected by flooding. Women experiencing physical and emotional violence and sexual harassment due to inadequate latrines and cultural exclusion from decision-making have been on the rise. AHPSS's safety audits suggest that increased accessibility to water and sanitation facilities and other simple interventions such as additional security lights at water and sanitation facilities could have a significant impact on reducing GBV. Integration of WASH into health and nutrition facilities in Fangak, C/P, and Ayod has also been an issue. Many health and nutrition facilities have shifted to new locations due to flooding and are without boreholes or latrines. For Example, the IMA facility in New Fangak which identified the suspected case of cholera has no functioning latrine. The facility does have access to a new borehole which was drilled by AHPSS most recently

2. Project activities

The WASH Cluster’s 2024 strategy focuses on integrated responses, particularly in high-priority counties experiencing acute vulnerabilities in both WASH and nutrition, often compounded by high WASH relater GBV (2024 HRP). These counties include Fangak, Canal/Pigi, and Ayod which are AHPSS areas of operation. This WASH cluster strategy directly corresponds with AHPSS program goals for the 2024/25 financial year. AHPSS is planning to expand to meet the growing needs in the northern Jonglei region. This year HRP, and AHPSS targets to drill new boreholes that will be installed with IM2 hand pumps, wells in Fangak, Canal/Pigi, and Ayod, conduct rehabilitate and pair boreholes that were previously submerged by flooding, construct flood resilient platforms, convert existing boreholes to water yards, and repair In addition to creating this new infrastructure, AHPSS is seeking to improve the safety of water points by disinfecting them, conducting water quality tests, and supervising safety audits of existing water supply and sanitation facilities. Recent disease outbreaks in Fangak and ongoing concerns of Gender-based violence have highlighted the need to strengthen safety protocols for water points. This year, another area of WASH expansion will include a renewed effort to recruit and train water point management committees.
