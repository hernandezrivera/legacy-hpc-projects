Project Summary

The project aims to support ER efforts and community revitalization; It will contribute to rehabilitation and restoration of disrupted livelihoods, providing skills, income opportunities through two main interventions. During this project, Sadad will work to serve 3 three sub-district and 14 communities (2 in Ariha, 11 in Ehsem, and 1 in Sarmin)as following: (Orm Eljoz, and Ariha in Ariha sub-district) and (Balshun, Balyun, Ehsem, Arnaba, Bara, Rami, Joseph, Kansafra,Marayan, Marata, and Bsames in Ehsem sub-district ) and (Sarmin in Sarmin sub-district )for one year.
1). Sadad, in close coordination with the local councils and local humanitarian groups, will step up the gap by establishing a waste removal system.
The process will start with preparation and identifying the garbage points. Then selecting 360 CFW beneficiaries and distribute the equipment and tools for the workers. The workers will collect the waste from the streets and neighborhoods to transfer it to the landfills covering 22 working days per month.
In addition to conducting awareness-raising sessions in the targeted communities to promote hygiene and effects of the spread of diseases, Covid-19, and wastes danger against children and the elderly.
2). Creating job opportunities, Sadad will implement integrated livelihood & empowerment activities through selecting 400 (Men 200, Women 200) entrepreneurs and MSMEs owners in Ariha, Ehsem, and Sarmin sub-districts, through launching 8 training cycles, each cycle lasts for three months, 80 participants per course.
The project aims on improving their capacity in entrepreneurial skills and concepts, like marketing, Feasibility study, Project design, and business management in addition to viewing videos and films about entrepreneurship subjects, hosting successful entrepreneurs, networking with business institutions, and carrying out awareness sessions for female entrepreneurs.
In each cycle, the project will work on developing their business plans and organizing Demo/Pitch Days in Ariha, Ehsem and Sarmin sub-districts to present the projects then the project will support 80 winning entrepreneurs of the startups and MSMEs, (8 in each cycle) to start or recover their business focusing on women-led businesses.
- Establishing small to medium food processing workshops targeting 80 female-headed households.
- Regarding the job creation, the project will target 1200 cash for work workers related to conducting agricultural services in targeting project locations during 12000 working days in average 10 working-days per worker.
The project aims to support 80 of PWDs (Male:60, Female:20) with lower limb amputation/disability through:
- Referral (internal and external) to organizations and available physical and psychosocial rehabilitation services;
- Financial support by small grants to support continued and expanded services;
- Training, consultations, and mentorship in business.
Sadad engage with target communities throughout the project design, implementation, and monitoring, to ensure that all interventions are meaningful, relevant, and community-driven. Beneficiaries (both IDPs and host communities) are engaged at each step in the intervention and information is shared freely and openly through direct consultations with a variety of actors, including men, women, youth, local producers and traders through both qualitative interviews.
Both IDPs and host communities members in particular HHs headed by a child, a female member and/or PWD, will be prioritized as beneficiaries for CFW.To ensure all potential beneficiaries have equal and non-discrimination access to the interventions of the project.Sadad will follow Do No Harm approach through coordination meetings with related stakeholders such local councils, Local market owners, enterprises before submitting the proposal, identifying the locations with access issues that are being targeted under this proposal. In addition to, cash working group to follows the Cash for Work (CfW)

4 - Needs

In northwest Syria, 97% of people live in extreme poverty (they are part of a household where each individual lives below $1.90 per person per day). Coupled with the low purchasing power of the Syrian Pound (SYP), poor families are struggling to meet their most basic needs (Situation Report No. 31 – September 2021 OCHA)Facing deteriorated living standards, families are increasingly adopting harmful coping mechanisms. Female-headed households have taken on more debt. 28% of families now adopt crises or emergency food-related coping strategies, including withdrawing children from school to have them work instead, selling property, migrating due to lack of food.• High numbers of female-headed households increase the burden on communities, services, and local authorities to secure their need in addition to high numbers of families without breadwinners; in Ariha SD 2174 female-headed households and nearly 700 households in Sarmin SD without a breadwinner.• The increased prices and weak income considered as a threat to those vulnerable households, wherein Ariha SD household average income in no more than 75 $per month and the average spending in the same SD equal to 155$ .• The lack of local services also increased the burden on local councils/authorities to respond to the civic needs of the population, in Sarmin a local council representative has informed that the town has no access to solid waste and debris removal because of a lack of support to the civil authorities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection: Risk: The risks of SEA or GBV can be compounded during project implementation Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation: -Ensure effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse by integrating PSEA in Sadad policies and making sure that all personnel read and signed code of conduct, and that all contracts with partners/suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA or GBV. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties.Risks: 1. Sadad response activities unintentionally lead to intra-community tensions arising from the beneﬁciary selection or limited resources and response capacities. 2. The principle of “Do no harm” is not guaranteed due to unclear objectives based on the community needs, validated through a transparent community consultation and participation process. Mitigation measures: 1. Perform conﬂict analysis before planning the response, ensuring that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the community or communities concerned and keep them informed of the project implementation. 2. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community board.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: - Abuse or exploitation of beneficiaries by staff during beneficiary selection and distribution processes. - Lack of staff awareness and code of conduct on PSEA. - Lack of availability of information on reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries. - Lack of female staff in the field. Mitigation/prevention measures: - Code of conduct includes PSEA language. - All staff are trained on PSEA. - Internal SEA reporting mechanism for staff and investigative capacity exist and are known within the organization. - PSEA awareness-raising activities and materials for beneficiaries and local communities are conducted and shared during the activities and reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries are available. - All field activities are supervised by humanitarian staff, including female staff members to mitigate PSEA and other protection risks. - Beneficiaries regularly receive information regarding the free and unconditional nature of humanitarian aid and know about and have access to complaint mechanisms and hotlines.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS:-All activities will be implemented in safe and secure environment with clear and applicable contingency plan.-Do-No harm approach is adopted while supporting MSMEs so the local vendors will not negatively affected by the intervention.Sadad will ensure CfW daily wage rates do not distort local labour market and draw workers away from local employment opportunities, therefore harming local producers.Risk: Operating in a warzone exposes staff to high safety and security risks.Mitigation: continues evaluation of roads, development of a communication network, capacity building for staff on how to deal with checkpoints, good and effective communication with parties and forces.Risk:Possible market distortion risks / risk of markets volatilityMitigation :a regular channel of communication with ERL, SCHF will be established for information sharing on developments and possible emerging issues to market stability or market distortion / markets volatility.Risk :CFW participants do not always work the full dayMitigation : 1.Set up a system for payment for partial days.Risk : CFW may cause disruption to local labour markets.Mitigation : 1. Do not set wage rates above the average level. 2. Limit the number of days that people can work with the project.Risk : Weather conditions and annual events delay CFW timelinesMitigation : Consult weather forecasts and annual calendar events when planning the timetable for CFW activities.
