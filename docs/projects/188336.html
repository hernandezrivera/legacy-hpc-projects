Project Summary

According to the HNO 2022 draft issued by the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), 13.4 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance - a 21% increase compared to 2021 - with needs increasingly exacerbated by the deteriorating economy. Increased exposure to ongoing hostilities, particularly in northwestern and northeastern Syria. limited access to the Services; Displacement and gender-based violence: cases of torture, detention, kidnapping, sexual violence, denial of humanitarian assistance, lack of access to it due to lack of civil documentation, among other factors, incidents of early marriage of girls and boys
The project aims to reduce gender-based violence in Northwest Syria by integrating protection services with early recovery services provided at the AHF Center in Azaz, Syria which provides degrading training activities ending with funding for female-headed families
The proposed project will provide implementation of services to prevent and respond to gender-based violence, including the provision of one-time Individual Protection Assistance (IPA)
Implementation of mental health and psychosocial support activities
Implement empowerment activities through providing small grants to women with the aim of achieving economic independence, AMAL initiative, life skills, GBV awareness sessions and campaigns targeting women, men, boys and girls both in IPDs and the host community as well as capacity building training for humanitarian actors (works in other sectors) about
Publication of IASC Guidance for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions into Humanitarian Action.
The project team will be trained in GBV, Case Management, PFA, PSS, YMC and Amal Initiative.
The project team will be composed of 1 project manager, 1 female psychotherapist, 5 outreach workers male - 5 female, 6 female case workers, 4 female psychosocial supporter, 4 trainers contracting,
Contracting a means of transportation to transport the mobile awareness team
AHF will form community committees from the same communities and train them on GBV topics such as; Basic concepts of GBV, communication skills to enable them to identify people in need and refer them to organizations involved in case management. Moreover, the community committees will play an important role in raising the issues of gender-based violence faced by the community and publicizing the activities of the project.
. Strategies for empowering women and girls will be strengthened, with a special focus on groups most at risk (ex-adolescents, divorced and widowed girls, women and girls with disabilities, and older women) to prevent GBV and GBV
You will work in accordance with inter-agency guidelines and tools to benefit from capacity building. The project will target host communities, IDPs, returnees, community centers and camps. All activities are aligned with the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan as well as the Strategic Objectives of the Protection Cluster. Services will be provided for one year. The project will coordinate with the affected population and stakeholders and is designed to promote gender balance. The project aims to involve people with disabilities through classifying and identifying types of disabilities, community counseling for them during project design and implementation, addressing the risks they face, and activating their participation in the project through employment and activities. Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation is an active member of the GBV sub-cluster, and this strengthens the coordination mechanism and contributes to effective response interventions. It will also coordinate with focal points within the project area to ensure further interventions.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance- a 21% increase compared to 2021 - with needs increasingly exacerbated by the economic downturn. Of the 13.4 million people in need, nearly ten million (75 percent) are women and children; Including approximately 2.3 million females and males with disabilities aged 12 years and over. The above needs are underlined in the Aswat 2022 report which notes that according to a group of women and girls who participated in community focus groups in 2021, a range of different forms of violence affected them similarly to previous years. Sexual, emotional, psychological, and physical violence was mentioned as occurring commonly within the community, as well as early marriage, economic violence, and denial of opportunities and resources. The same report, Aswat 2021,stated that divorced and widowed women and girls continue to face various forms of gender-based violence, which have been more evident in the emerging coronavirus crisis. Also, social conditions greatly affected women and girls and helped expose them to risks of discrimination and societal violenceA group of women also mentioned various forms of discrimination directed towards them, such as work or access to services, in addition to issues related to divorce and inheritance.The project will help respond to current needs through awareness-raising sessions, capacity building and implementation of referrals to specialized service providers

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Rosk1:Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services. Likelihood: Hight. Impact: High. Mitigation1: 1.GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs. 2.ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Risk2: Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging and lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to WGSS and access services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation2: 1.Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. 2.sure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities. 3.Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture. Risk3: Livelihood opportunities may increase risks of GBV and expectations of income may be unrealistic and might create dependency. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation3: consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the programme. 2. Ensure beneficiaries know services where to disclose if violence occurs 3. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report. Risk4: Lack of inclusive criteria for GBV survivors, especially in distributions. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation4: inclusion of GBV survivors in distribution criteria


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Low level of obligations related to sexual exploitation and abuse, women disproportionately exposure to misconduct by humanitarian workers at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, and low level of safe reporting of complaints of SEA.Mitigation:1 - Impose mandatory training on protection from PSEAon all employees, including management, field staff, day laborers, volunteers, contractors and vendors, in each area where there are active processes to ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and to equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to both. 2 - Create mechanisms for reporting protection from SEA at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting.3 - Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations.Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards, and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation.4 - Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys, and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities, and societyAll project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA
