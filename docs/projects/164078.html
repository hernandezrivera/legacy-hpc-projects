Project Summary

This project aims to improve education service provision in NW Syria; a geographic location that has absorbed a lot of IDPs as a result of the recent displacement wave movements started in Dec 2019. The overall objective of the project is to increase access and quality of education to conflict-affected children. The specific objective is to scale up safe and equitable access to formal & non-formal education for 19 schools with 10000 school-aged girls and boys between ages 5 -17 “boys: 5472, & girls: 4528”. Plus, 434 schools’ personnel “238 men, & 196 women”. In addition to what was mentioned. The project will be divided into two parts the first one will be the provision of full support to 9 learning facilities (7 formal education & 2 non-formal education) in Atareb, Daret Azza, and Dana areas, and the second part is the provision of rehabilitation for 10 school buildings in Euphrate Shield area.
The project proposed activities will be as follows:
- Provision of school personnel incentives for the 9 learning facilities.
- Covering the running costs for the 9 learning facilities.
- Establishing/updating the students' enrolment records the 9 learning facilities.
- Distribute teaching and learning materials for 9 learning facilities.
- Provide means of transportation for CwD for the 9 learning facilities.
- Procure and distribute stationery school bags for students the 9 learning facilities.
- Conducting awareness sessions.
- Updating the schools’ safety plans, and install/renew first aid equipment for all learning facilities.
- PTCs the 9 learning facilities.
- Conducting ASER tests the 9 learning facilities.
- Fuel distribution during the winter season the 9 learning facilities.
- Providing/printing textbooks for students the 9 learning facilities.
- Teachers and education personnel pieces of training EiE/TiCC/ SLP…
- Provision of formal education for 7 schools and non-formal education for 2 TECs.
- Establish sensitive WASH facilities with all the schools.
- Conduct full rehabilitation for 10 school buildings.
The locations on the sub-district level are Atareb, Daret Azza, Dana, & Azaz. Taking into account that locations have been identified based on accessibility; however, this might change due to the situation on the ground.

4 - Needs

The situation in Syria was exacerbated especially as the conflict enters its ninth year, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. Some 13.1 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 5.6 million people are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and services. Over half of the country’s population has fled; 4.8 million people have sought refuge in countries neighboring Syria and beyond, and 6.3 million people are internally displaced. In many cases, households have been displaced multiple times in response to endless new waves of conflict. Lack of safety and security constitutes the greatest barrier to accessing education, with schools and education facilities seemingly under attack and occupied by armed actors. Humanitarian Needs Overview, Syria, 2019 The current population in the proposed location of intervention “which is Atareb, Daret Azza, Dana & Azaz sub-districts” is around 112,460 according to HNO 2019. The estimated number of IDP arrivals in assessed communities ranged from 13,085 - 15,305 individuals, a 59% increase compared to the number of arrivals reported in December. The demographic map in Syria has faced drastic changes; constantly fluid and subject to change on a daily basis, and the educational condition of their children in the elementary school ages that’s in northern suburbs of Idleb and western suburbs of Aleppo, more than half of the displaced people are children. According to recent assessment conducted by The Assistance Coordination Unit (ACU) ‘Schools in Syria 2019 Edition 05’ said that the percentage of partially destroyed schools yet still suitable for education reached only 11%; in northern Aleppo countryside, namely Azaz, Al Bab. In addition to the IDPs influx in Azaz due to the recent displacement waves. Thus, there is a huge need for school buildings rehabilitation.

5 - Implementation of activities

The first part of the project will be for the 7 formal education schools, 4651 (2528 boys, 2123 girls) school-aged children including 195 CwD and 182 (88 men, 94 women) educational personnel are in need to the continuity of the educational process through covering the incentives for the education personnel, covering monthly running expenses, equipping the schools with what is needed to ease the access the CwD, conduct minor rehabilitation for school building, provision of students' and teachers' kits, provision of textbooks & recreational materials, and improving the capacity of teachers by delivering technical trainings. Noting that these schools had 60% IDPs. On the other hand, the 'one' school in Dana sub-district had 100% IDPs and could absorb the non-formal students during their transition into formal education in that area. For the non-formal education TECs, 368 (204 boys, 164 girls) school-aged children including 26 CwD and 17 (9 men, 8 women) educational personnel are in need to rehabilitation and expanding the TEC to absorb more children, equip the schools with what is needed to ease the access the CwD, covering the incentives for education personnel, covering monthly running expenses, provision of students' and teachers' kits, improving the capacity of teachers by delivering technical trainings, provision of textbooks & recreational materials, transition the non-formal education students into formal education, and establish appropriate WASH facilities. The second part of the project will be the rehabilitation of 10 school buildings in Euphrate Shield area with total BNFs 4981 children (B:2740, G:2241) and 217 school personnel (M:120, F:97) The activities will be monitored by MEAL department through Complaints Mechanism; which includes: 'WhatsApp number, Complaints & Suggestion Boxes, Face-to-face complainants, Focus Group Discussions with students on a monthly bases, & FGDs with Teachers and students’ parents in the final evaluation, & Complaints email.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The number one risk is security, as schools have been one of the main targets in the conflict. Although the situation has slightly improved due to the cessation of hostilities, it is unknown how long it will last. The security situation is not one that IhsanRD can play a major role in mitigating, besides perhaps providing additional safety and security training for teachers to use with their students in the case that security concerns arise. Other mitigation will be the pausing of activities in the case of major conflict escalation including rehabilitation and training (as was previously done in past Ihsan education programs). In the case that schooling is stopped, IhsanRD, in cooperation with the parent-teacher committee will look into alternative mechanisms for education delivery, such as smaller sessions, home-based learning etc. Finally, IhsanRD has chosen a location that isrelatively safer and has historically been a location where IDPs flee to, not where people are fleeing from. One risk includes the disinterest of parents to take part in the parent-teacher committees. As the context in Syria is one of war, there are often priorities that families list, and most often they focus on food and health. The secondary is usually education and PSS support. In order to mitigate this risk, the IhsanRD team will have to conduct strong advocacy and outreach during the project set up phase of the project when meeting with stakeholders. Furthermore, the rules and mandate of the committee will be formed by the community itself (with Ihsan field team guidance), so as to encourage ownership and interest in the topic at hand. Risks about procurement under this project, IhsanRD may face many risks such as lack of materials availability, transportation risks, worrying about suppliers’ commitment. IhsanRD will deal with trusted suppliers from IhsanRD vendor list and cooperate with the Local Council to reduce the transportation authorities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: changes regarding the security situation in the target areas, including the deterioration of safety and mobility conditions, as well as the displacement of staff and/or beneficiaries, in ways that delay or exclude program access and provision (MEDIUM-HIGH): Mitigation measure: IhsanRD monitors the security situation in Northern Syria, and establish emergency response and contingency plans to deal with changes in stability and access. These plans include relocating program sites if necessary, as well as using alternative modalities of service delivery, such as homeschooling. Risk 2: Increased internal displacement, both into and out of the target areas (MEDIUM-HIGH):Mitigation measure: Our monitoring of conflict dynamics includes close attention to migration flows in Northern Syria. In instances where we are unable to meet the educational needs of IDPs arriving at the catchment areas, head teachers and school Protection Facilitators will attempt to link children and their families with other schools in the community. Conversely, when conflict factors cause students and teachers at our schools to flee, we will coordinate with local authorities to fill personnel gaps and continue service delivery, if the security situation is safe to do so. Risk 3: Border closures and delays in transporting project goods and supplies (MEDIUM): Mitigation measure: If supply lines are disrupted due to border closures, we rely on alternative channels for procurement in local markets, where possible. As a contingency measure, we also divide the program supply between warehouses in and outside of Syria. Risk 4: During closure periods, we also undertake advocacy with relevant parties to appeal for resumed humanitarian access and entry instability of local markets (MEDIUM): Mitigation measure: monitoring exchange rates and market dynamics to anticipate and plan for such changes and adapt budgeting and planning accordingly. Setting fixed prices with contractors and suppliers.
