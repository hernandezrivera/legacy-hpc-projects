Project Summary

The protracted crisis in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, especially erstwhile FATA has a long-lasting effects on the lives of millions of people. The area has been affected by militancy and consequently large scale displacement of over 5 million people in-between 2004 to 2016 (when Pakistan faced huge notified displacement), thus depriving the people of basic social needs and income generation opportunities. Each tribal district has witnessed large-scale extremist violence followed by a number of both small and large scale governmental military operations, culminating in the displacement of 5 million people. Politically erstwhile FATA (currently called Newly Merged Tribal Districts (NMD)) was disenfranchised because there was lack of proper governance structure maintaining and safeguarding health, education, livelihoods, roads services and other safety nets. The NMDs are now formally merged with KP, comprising of seven tribal districts including North Waziristan, South Waziristan, Kurram, Orakzai, Khyber, Mohmand and Bajaur. These districts are adjacent to the 2,300 kilometres border with Afghanistan and remain a deprived and dangerous region in Pakistan, with the legacy of 2 decades of militancy still present..
The huge challenge of displacement combined with lack of resources and perpetuated by the impacts of the COVID pandemic, has limited the ability of the government to cater to the basic needs of displaced communities in terms of shelter, food, WASH, health and protection, hence people have been left to their own resources. The devastation in the district is far beyond the economic response capacity of the government. In these circumstances, humanitarian actors have played pivotal roles in supporting affected communities, however there remains a significant gap between the needs and the response. Specifically, women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities have suffered the brunt of crisis being exposed to an inhospitable environment where their specific needs were ignored. On return from displacement, tribal communities have faced the unfolding misery of damaged houses, schools, lands, markets, irrigation structures and road networks. This situation prevails all across Orakzai, especially upper and central Orakzai due to the high prevalence of militancy and mass displacement combined with low human development indicators and literacy rates. Considering the high level of vulnerabilities, huge need, limited number of humanitarian actors working in the area and meagre resources within government, multi sector humanitarian initiatives for the returnee communities are imperative to rehabilitate and recover the livelihood assets and basic services of the affected population. Although IRP’s proposed initiative is exclusively humanitarian, it’s important to recognise that the intervention focusing on the poorest and most vulnerable groups will facilitate the government and contribute towards overarching rehabilitation efforts.
According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (January – August 2020) 25% population of District Orakzai tribal district has been classified in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) and 10% in Phase 4 (Emergency). The KP Tribal Districts Transition Framework (2018-20, extendable) is a top priority of the incumbent government, humanitarian and development partners, aimed to address the remnant humanitarian and much needed recovery/reconstruction needs of the people in the KP Tribal Districts. The Framework is aimed at sustainable return process, laying a strong foundation for access to life sustaining public services and work out durable solutions within the economic environment of the region that the impoverished communities are effectively mainstreamed in the national economy.
