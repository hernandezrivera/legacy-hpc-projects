Project Summary

Considering the high numbers of newly arriving people to Dana and Maaret Tamsrin sub
district, there is a significant need for the provision of Formal and non- formal education
support to minimize the gap in education provision for vulnerable IDPs populations and
the Displacement is likely to have caused significant disruption, both to IDPs and HCs, for
IDP children and youth put a significant strain on the capacity of host communities to
accommodate increased education needs, Whilst there is willingness from HCs to
support education needs of IDP children, school capacities are in many cases far
overstretched.
Overwhelming schools’ capacity to meet education needs through expecting them to
accommodate new influxes of IDP children poses the risk of both undermining quality of
teaching and learning for all school going children, and potentially ‘doing harm’ through
raising tensions between vulnerable IDP and HCs.
Considering the prolonged and violent nature of the conflict in northern Syria, moreover,
there is a high likelihood that populations, both HCs and IDPs, have experienced
significant war related stress/duress.
So, BRD will go ahead to cover the education needs in 4 schools in Dana and Maaret
Tamsrin sub district through implementing this project in following locations:
Idleb, (Shekh Bahr) - Shekh Bahr school–1st stage
Idleb,Haranbush school–1st stage.
Idleb, Alhuria school–1st and 2nd stage
Idleb, Burdaqly school–1st stage
BRD aims to support the Education opportunities through delivering Educational and
Protection services in north of Syria for the most vulnerable Syrian children (girls, boys,
men, women and Disabilities persons).
BRD will provide a complementary Education services to improve the educational
infrastructure that increase physical access to schools for all children.
Project designed to enhance the quality of Education through rehabilitate WASH
facilities in all school taking into account the difference of sex and gender.
Building capacity of educational personnel, covering monthly stipends, running costs,
teaching and learning materials, PSS sessions, stationery, school bags to children and
teachers (girls, boys, Men and Women), and in regard to System strengthening, the
project will respond to the gap of school textbooks by printing and distributing
educational curriculum in place, considering the sensitivities of the Syrian crisis.
Intervention is planned to cover all the Education objectives after 9 years of the Syrian
crisis that imposes education as urgent priority to prevent a lost generation.

4 - Needs

In 2019, The impact of hostilities is particularly severe on women and children whoconstitute 76% of the population in north west Syria, of which over 53% are children.Further, people with disabilities are disproportionately impacted by the ongoing conflict.Over 180,000 school-aged children are in need of education services. 2.1 million Childrenin Syria are out of school, and a further 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out. Thevulnerability of school-age children with no access to learning opportunities hasincreased over the almost nine years of the crisis, putting children at higher risk of beingexposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violations. The intensification of hostilities insome areas has negatively impacted thousands of children who lost access to educationopportunities and the access to education is lost when children are forced to suddenlyleave their area of residence. More than 1 in 3 schools are damaged or destroyed, whichcausing overcrowding in operational schools, 65 to 75 child per class. As the conflictcontinues, effective teaching becomes increasingly challenging due to over-crowdedclassrooms and lack of resources. Children have been expose to the psychologicaltrauma because of hostilities, displacement and family upheaval generated by the crisis,many children and teachers are affected by psychological trauma. According to needassessment conducted by BRD for those schools which shows the children are in needfor education services which more than 1900 child out of 2300 are not going to schoolsas well as the rest of them walking for long period to go next community to attend inschool. latrines need rehabilitation/construction for segregated latrines, those blocksspread the disease to children. BRD going to target those children in mentioned locationswith EDU and PSS services to reach those children who no one supporting them.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Learning facilities could be attacked, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school.Mitigating MeasuresLearning facilities can be de-conflicted using the de-confliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA.All attacks will be reported to the Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism.Learning facilities will have safety protocols in place. These protocols should be adequately resourced and regularly practiced. Resources may include walkies-talkies, basic firefighting equipment, basic first aid supplies, flash lights, exit signs, etc.Caregivers should know the learning facility’s safety protocols and what will happen in case of a security event.Preparedness training such as self-care, first aid, fire drills, evacuation drills and stay-and-shelter drills should be regularly practiced. These processes and procedures will be appropriate for the age of the involved students. Where relevant, students and education personnel will be trained on unexplored ordinance risk awareness.Students, their caregivers, teachers and other education personnel will be provided PSS. Those services will have the capacity, ability and resources to address basic needs. For needs that are beyond what will be reasonably expected from learning services there will be a referral system to specialized protection services.Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities.Mitigating MeasureChild Safeguarding polices and systems in place (organization staff, teachers, contactors,etc)will ensure a survivor centered approach that ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected person.The survivor should have a say in the processesSufficient safe and clean bathroom facilities will be provided. will be segregatedOpportunities for children to be alone with adults will be avoided.
