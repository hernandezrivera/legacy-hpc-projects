Project Summary

The proposed shelter and non food items project will be implemented in Tonj East, Tonj North and Twic Counties in Warrap County. The project will seek to target 19,500 persons as follows: Internally Displaced Persons: 9,500 persons; Returnees: 4,500 persons and vulnerable host community: 5,500 persons . Twic and Tonj East counties are classified as severity 1 while Tonj North is ranked 2. This implies targeted population are facing severe needs and are expected to depend on humanitarian assistance to meet their S-NFIs needs in 2025. In locations with functional markets, Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) will be adopted for 3,000 persons ( Tonj East: 1,000 persons; Tonj North: 1,000 persons and Twic : 1,000 persons) to stimulate local markets. Where markets are absent, in-kind modality will be adopted. To integrate the response, Trust Guarantee Community Development Aid will work in close collaboration with Nutrition, Health, WASH, Education, Mine Action and Protection by ensuring cross referrals for assistance. For example-to mainstream protection, TGCDA will work with protection partners for joint advocacy and communication on Housing, Land and Property issues.TGCDA protection and GBV officers will work closely with SGBV and protection partners to facilitate referrals of gender-based violence and other protection cases it may come across during implementation of the project. TGCDA will participate will participate in joint messaging with the Health and Nutrition partners on Malaria prevention, distribution of mosquito nets and proper utilization of mosquito nets targeting pregnant and lactating mothers and those seeking nutrition services. Training will be conducted in target locations to strengthen the capacity of affected persons on durable shelter and non food items. Community leaders, local authorities, relief and rehabilitation commission will be involved in all stages of implementation to cultivate and entrench accountability. Project data will be segregated by age, ability and sex in order to ensure increased participation of women, girls, youth and persons with disability in the implementation. To entrench accountability to the affected persons, a robust Accountability to Affected persons will be formed on contact with the community and will be responsible for project oversight on behalf of the community.

1. Needs

According to Shelter and non food items (SNFIs) cluster, the total number of people in need (PIN) of SNFIs assistance in 2025 in three counties of Warrap State is as follows: Twic: 21,000 ; Tonj East: 9,000 and Tonj North: 9,400 people. In these counties, the SNFIs needs are driven by flood and conflict induced displacements, influx of returnees, worsening food insecurity and time poverty. According to IRNA assessment conducted by partners in Warrap State in September 2024, Twic County registered 24,876 Internally Displaced persons, while Tonj East recorded 15,000 and 30,000 internally displaced persons respectively. As of 29th November 2024, the South Sudan’s Floods snapshot report indicated 69,000 people in the three counties were affected by floods, majority of whom being women and children. Due to floods and conflict, food insecurity has worsened, with Twic and Tonj East projected to remain in emergency food insecurity situation or worse in September 2024- March 2025 according to 2024-2025 Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) data. In April to July 2025, the populations in the three counties are expected to face emergency food insecurity or worse. In the current context, deterioration of the food security is attributed to the seasonal depletion of food stocks, high food prices, reduced market functionality, access challenges caused by insecurity and disruption of livelihoods and displacement caused by floods and insecurity. Due to inflation, the few items that are available in the market are prohibitively expensive and out of reach for the majority of the IDPs and vulnerable host communities. The worsening humanitarian situations has led to collapse of living standards of the population, with survival based on extreme coping strategies such as liquidation of household assets, which will lead to large needs in the short and long term. During the assessments, some of the returnees and IDPs were observed staying in schools, abandoned buildings and church compounds. Others were sleeping under trees and in open spaces with no mosquito nets, blankets and sleeping mats. Displaced women, girls and children who are sleeping under trees, open spaces, and overcrowded rooms are exposed to Gender Based Violence and other protection risks. Sick, elderly people, pregnant and lactating women are facing elevated risks of malaria infection. Shelter and non food items verification exercises conducted by Trust Guarantee Community Development Aid, IOM, the World Vision and other partners noted widespread lack of cooking and serving items among returnees and internally displaced persons. Those interviewed indicated that they were not able to carry the items with them since they had to free in hurry to seek safety. As a result, households are forced to borrow the few items available. Due to fighting, markets in the three counties are hugely disrupted with areas outside county headquarters having no functional markets. As such, affected persons are forced to walk long distances in search of SNFIs that are prohibitively expensive and out of reach of the IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities. Considering these needs, TGCDA seeks to provide basic SNFIs to 19,500 IDPs, returnees and host communities households in Tonj East, Tonj North and Twic Counties. In-kind assistance will be provided in locations with no markets while Cash Voucher Assistance will be conducted in areas with functional markets. The proposed assistance is integrated and will be provided in close coordination with humanitarian partners working in Warrap State, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission, Chiefs, IDPs and Host Community Representatives as well as SNFIs cluster at national and state levels. 

2. Project activities

1 : Community meetings: Inception meeting will be conducted with community leaders, RRC and local authorities to introduce the project, provide information on assistance modality and role of the affected persons in the implementation. During this phase, an AAP committee comprising of community representatives will be established and tasked with mobilization and project oversight. Midterm meetings will review progress made on set objectives and targets. Challenges and lesson learnt will be interrogated and necessary adjustments made in the second phase of implementation. 2: Rapid SNFIs needs verification: With the support of RRC, SNFIs cluster and the affected persons, TGCDA will conduct a rapid needs assessment and verification exercise to accurately determine the level of SNFIs needs in target Counties. The findings of the exercise will inform the response and act as a reference document during implementation. 3: Conduct detailed market assessment in close collaboration with Cash Working Group, SNFIs cluster,AAP and traders to obtain information on capacity of markets to support CVA. A CVA Committee comprising of affected persons, traders and local authorities will be formed to negotiate prices and ensuring compliance with the set SNFIs cluster Transfer Value of USD34 per household for Twic, Tonj North and Tonj East Counties. 4: Selection of beneficiaries: Through the AAP and locally hired enumerators, TGCDA will conduct selection of 19,500 persons, out of which 3,000 for CVA. Beneficiaries for each county dis-aggregated as follows: IDPs: 9,500 persons; Returnees: 4,500 persons and vulnerable host community: 5,500 persons). For in-kind, allocation per county being as follows: Tonj East: 5,000 persons, Tonj North: 5,000 persons and Twic: 6,500 persons. For CVA, beneficiaries are dis-aggregated as follows: Tonj East: 1,000 persons, Tonj North: 1,000 persons and Twic: 1,000 persons). The selection criteria will include: severely sick persons, separated and unaccompanied children, orphans, elderly persons, individuals without community links, persons with disability, mentally ill persons, care givers of separated and unaccompanied children, lactating and pregnant women. During the selection exercise, information will be shared on Complaint and Feedback avenues participants can easily and safely utilize to submit complaints and receive feedback. Suggestion boxes, complaint desks and toll free numbers will be shared with participants to facilitate submission of complaints. Selected beneficiaries will be issued with tamper proof tokens which they will redeem during distribution of SNFIs. 5: Collection, Prepositioning and distribution of SNFIsFollowing the cluster’s approval of Rapid Needs Assessment and Verification Report. TGCDA will raise SNFIs request from Core Pipeline, collect and preposition the items in its field warehouse awaiting distribution. TGCDA project staff, AAP and enumerators will supervise setting up of distribution venues that are accessible by elderly persons, people with disability and considered safe for women and girls. All distributions will be conducted during light hours (between nine in the morning and three o’clock in the afternoon) to allow beneficiaries sufficient time to walk to distribution centres and return home during daytime. 5: Post Distribution monitoring: Cash and In-kind PDM will be conducted using templates developed and approved by the SNFIs cluster and will focus on how accountability and information sharing was ensured, how distribution process was carried out, how protection and safety risks were mitigated, impact and environment protection. Random sampling of 20% of in-kind and CVA participants will be employed during the PDM. FGDs, KIIs, Observation and transect walk will be adopted during the PDM. PDM reports will be shared with the SNFIs cluster and stakeholders for review and consideration.
