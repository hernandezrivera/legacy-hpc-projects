Project Summary

In line with the WASH cluster objective #2 and country strategic objective #2, the action aims to ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians. through ensuring basic WASH services for conflict-affected, un- or under-served and vulnerable populations and institutions in the Gaza Strip. The proposed intervention responds to the SDG #6. SDG6 aims to ‘ensure available and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all’ and includes targets for universal access to drinking water, sanitation and hygiene for all by 2030.
The project aims at reducing the public health and environmental pollution risks that may occur as a result of wastewater percolation from existing cesspits in the target locations in Gaza Governorate. It will benefit nearly 14850 people of which 49% are women in four locations namely in El-Zaytoun South of Gaza, Aldaraj w Alsabra middle of Gaza, Shejaiya in East of Gaza area and Juhor ad Dik south of Gaza City. Total number of beneficiaries in each location can be listed as follows: El-Zaytoun 11000, Aldaraj w Alsabra 550, Shejaiya 2750 and Juhor ad Dik 550. Moreover, the project will indirectly benefiting all people in the target four locations through direct pollution prevention of their areas and also prevent groundwater pollution.
According to the information obtained from CMWU and PWA during the coordination meetings organized with them on 27 September 2021, these information were coordinated again on 29 September 2021 WASH cluster coordination team, area focal points, technical focal points and governmental counterpart focal points and they identified the four locations listed above which are in need of sewer system to connect more HHs in order to avoid flooding of wastewater to neighborhoods and to coastal aquifer. Accordingly, the project will contribute by supplying and installing 4100 m of UPVC sewer lines, 140 concrete manholes and 300 house connection. The project will contribute also in closing down all the cesspits in the target locations to stop further pollution of aquifer and prevent public health risks, hence, reducing ensuring no harm principle.
Also, the project aims at improving the WASH facilities at 500 war affected households in 11 locations in Gaza and Deir Al-Balah governorates to make sure that the access of nearly 3000 inhabitants of which 1481 are women to essential wash services is improved. The number of beneficiaries in each location can be summarized as follows: Shejaiya- 300, El-Zaytoun 300, تل الهوا - 300, الرمال الشمالي-300, الرمال الجنوبي-300, الشيخ عجلين- 300, block 1 - 300. block 3 block 4 -240, block 1 block 2 block 5 -240, block C السوق وكلبوش -300 and block L block f -120.
Following to the termination of Israeli military assault on Gaza on 25th May 2021 PHG has mobilized its entire team in Gaza and conducted rapid assessment to the damage in line with WASH damage assessment and according to the information obtained from the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MOPWH), these information were coordinated on 29 September 2021 WASH cluster coordination team, area focal points, technical focal points and governmental counterpart focal points and they identified the 11 locations listed above which are in need of urgent rehabilitation of damaged WASH facilities. Accordingly, the project will contribute by rehabilitating 500 WASH facilities for the benefit of 500 households including internal WASH connections, sinks, water mixers, toilets, laundries and PE roof tanks.
In general, this intervention aims to improve WASH conditions for more than 17,850 people (3,200 families) in 13 locations in Gaza and Middle area Governorates, through the installation of 4.1Km of sewage networks and the rehabilitation of WASH facilities for 500 homes that were damaged during the last war in 2021.

1 - Needs

Over the past decade, sewage systems are a life hazard that affects the lives of Gazans, and the problem was made worse by the damaged infrastructure due to recurrent hostilities, especially after the war in 2021. The continued political and physical isolation of Gaza has exacerbated the severity of these issues, as the restrictions in place limit control over and access to its natural resources, thereby inhibiting its ability to cope with and adapt to these challenges. however, other issues such as insufficient and unreliable electricity, import restrictions on equipment, and funding shortages to infrastructural reform have all exacerbated the presence of functional sewage systems in many areas. According the (PCBS,2017), about 85.1% of households in the whole Gaza are connected to wastewater networks, while about 3.7 % were using porous cesspits and about 0.7% were using tight cesspits. The wastewater water crisis is reflected on the public health and on the surrounding environment causing serious illness and pollution due the overflow of the wastewater. Accordingly, an urgent intervention is required to increase the coverage rate, particularly in areas with no sewerage infrastructure. Targeted locations have been facing a growing environmental crisis for many years now. Based on Gaza WASH Vulnerability Map 2021, PHG will target (El-Zaytoun, Aldaraj w Alsabra, Shejaiya) neighborhoods in Gaza area and Juhor ad Dik south of Gaza City which are considered one of the densely populated and marginalized areas in Gaza. Currently 2,700 households in these 4 locations have no access to wastewater networks and rely upon cesspits and open areas like in Juhor ad Dik to dispose of household waste. These cesspits contaminate the water aquifer and cause regular sewage flooding along the roads. The project aims to improve the environmental conditions through the installation of the sewage networks in the internal streets of targeted neighborhoods and back filling the sewage cesspits which are the main pollutant of the aquifer which negatively affect the environment and health. In order to prioritize the areas with high humanitarian needs, PHG has carried out several meetings with relevant stakeholders, including CMWU, PWA, Municipality of Gaza, Municipality of Wadi Gaza. Both CMWU and PWA has recommended Gaza governorate as priority area. Regarding Gaza, the daily volume of wastewater generated is about 70 m3, where 86 % of household are served by sewage networks and the remaining 14% uses cesspits as a mean of disposal. The area of high priority in Gaza is densely populated and Lacking sanitation service in the project area. According to PWA and relevant Municipalities, there are frequent complaints of citizens in that region due to the daily overflow of sewage from cesspits. Due to proximity of the aquifer to the target area, the wastewater seeps through the soil layers to the aquifer causing deterioration in the groundwater quality which is reflected by high levels of Nitrates (NO3). Consequently, the target community is suffering from serious health problems, especially the children. Furthermore, the population in that region suffers from the financial burden of emptying cesspits from time to time and inability to pay these costs. Therefore, sewage water floods streets leading to critical environmental and health conditions on the population and the surrounding areas.Following to the termination of Israeli military assault on Gaza on 25th May 2021, thousands of Palestinian houses were demolished either completely or partially and people were obliged to flee to shelter schools or hosted by relatives’ houses. In addition, there are many people whom their houses were partially damaged they prefer to live in their houses under very poor service conditions. It is estimated that almost 16115 houses and 74 public building were demolished. The most critical part was the Gaza City, Northern Gaza (Biet Lahia and Beit Hanoun), Eastern Gaza (Alshjaiya and Al Zaytoun) in addition to AL Wusta Eastern Khan Younis and Rafah. Following to ceasefire, PHG has conducted rapid assessment to the damage and arranged meetings with stakeholders in order to assess the needs and to respond to the newly emerging situation to enable the families whose houses were partially damaged to live in it in dignity. PHG has targeted the houses that needs slight rehabilitation, more precisely will target 1000 house for rehabilitating the damaged WASH facilities in the houses to ensure safe drinking water supply and sanitation and hygiene conditions for those people which is highly urgent especially under the current pandemic of COVID-19. Accordingly, an urgent intervention is required to enable the war affected families whose houses were partially demolished to live in their houses in dignity and safety in Gaza, PHG will target 50% of the assessed houses (500 houses) in (Shejaiya, El-Zaytoun,تل الهوا ,الرمال الشمالي, الرمال الجنوبي، الشيخ عجلين) neighborhoods in Gaza area and (block 1, block 3 block 4, block 1 block 2 block 5, block C السوق وكلبوش, block L block f) in Deir Al-Balah. In order to prioritize the areas with high humanitarian needs, PHG has carried out several meetings with relevant stakeholders, including MOWPH and relevant municipalities and they have recommended Gaza and Deir AL-Balah governorates as priority area.Current situation has reduced household access to adequate water and sanitation services for these families and for some of them who are not able to mitigate the interruption of services, they are adopting negative coping mechanisms such as decreasing their water consumption and discharging wastewater. This condition has been worsened by the last hostilities as many families lost essential items such as water tanks, or have their sanitation facilities and networks damaged, exacerbating the stresses among the families’ members and the needs for specialized WASH interventions to alleviate their suffering.

2 - Activities

All project activities and interventions have been reviewed, evaluated and coordinated with the relevant stakeholders, including cluster coordination team, area focal points, technical focal points and governmental counterpart focal points to avoid and overcome work duplication, as well this cooperation and coordination will be maintained and improved at implementation stages at all levels with all stakeholders and organizations. The approach of implementation for this project will starts by formulating a project committee that consists of 5-7 person , which will include representatives from the community , women , municipality , local committees , the role of the committee is to assist in collecting contribution if any (local members) , also will facilitate work of the project staff in the field particularly to solve any conflict if raised through implementation period , the women whom representing CBOs and community will be active and expected to contribute equally in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project side by side with the committee members, they will be given a significant role in implementation from the beginning . the design of the systems will be done under the supervision of PHG , and expected to be supported by CMWU ,PWA , MOPWH, Funder representative, and member form the community whom will be as members in a steering committee of 3-5 persons , they will be updated/reporting back in periodically meeting by PHG engineer / head of office /representative for the progress and obstacles faced through implementation , and recommendations form next periods , the SC will support and help to find solutions to improve work and progress . the tendering process will go through the PHG procurement system, procurement plan will be designed for this purpose. The M& E officer at PHG, will follow implementation stage by stage through the M&E module and will insure achieving outputs and will measure indicator on regular bases. The main activities that will be implemented under the project which will target the neighborhoods of (El-Zaytoun, Aldaraj w Alsabra, Shejaiya, تل الهوا ,الرمال الشمالي, الرمال الجنوبي، الشيخ عجلين) neighborhoods in Gaza area, Juhor ad Dik south of Gaza City and (block 1, block 3 block 4, block 1 block 2 block 5, block C السوق وكلبوش, block L block f) in Deir Al-Balah can be summarized as follows: 1- Supply and installing of about 4.1Km of UPVC sewer pipelines with different diameters, 2-Supply and installing of about 140 concrete manholes with different sizes in the targeted areas 3-Supply and installing of about 300 house connections the targeted areas. 4- Rehabilitation of 500 WASH facilities in war damaged houses. Beneficiaries of WASH facilities will be identified and coordinated with the MOWPH in cooperation with WASH cluster. The WASH facilities rehabilitation will include kitchens, toilets with all relevant tools, fixtures and extensions as well as roof top water storage tanks. The proposed modalities are will improve the sanitation services in the target areas, protect the aquifer from pollution due to cesspits and raise the economic standard of living for the region’s residents, to enable the war affected families whose houses were partially demolished to live in their houses in dignity and safety in Gaza. The aim of these activities is to address the problems identified under the needs assessment which is related to poor water quality and water borne disease infections especially for children as well as those obtained from the women and men interviews during the assessment.

3 - Indicators

The indicators are: - 2,700 households in the targeted areas are connected to a sewage network. - Waterborne diseases affecting children under 5 years old and women in the targeted areas is reduced by 15%. – 4.1Km of sewer pipelines, 140 manholes and 300 house connections are installed in the targeted areas. - 50% of cesspits (150 cesspits) are closed in the targeted areas. - The financial burden required to emptying the cesspits will be reduced by 300$ a yr. – 500 households WASH facilities are rehabilitated in the targeted area. PHG will conduct risk and community assessments prior to project implementation to verify each indicator of the project to ensure cost-effective and timely project implementation. Moreover, PHG will conduct interviews with relevant stakeholders, municipalities, CMWU, MOPWH and PWA staff for verifying baseline data and baseline indicators. End line surveys will be also conducted following project implementation in order to measure the impact of the project on targeted communities especially the impact on women and children. The results will be reflected in the final project report. These tools together with baseline and end line data analysis and KAP assessment performed by PHG project team will be used to derive activity monitoring and reporting to management, donors and stakeholders on monthly, midterm and final project reporting periods or as established in the project agreement. PHG team will liaison with all stakeholders including WASH cluster, PWA, MOPWH, CMWU for any arising issue during implementation and will take proper actions to address these problems. Finally, PHG will ensure timely reporting both narrative and financial to donors and relevant stakeholders as per the project agreement and will ensure that reporting quality is met through its quality management system. This will be ensured through the Monitoring and Evaluation officer who will follow up with project manager to ensure compliance with the quality and timely reporting.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PHG aim with all of its projects is to ‘Do-No-Harm’ and to maximize positive impacts. In conjunction with this policy, PHG has ensured these practices by focusing on female heads of household, empowering the most vulnerable of families. By doing community outreach, PHG is able to help normalize female participation in public spaces. By providing home visits involving female evaluators, women can feel more safe in their own homes. PHG’s two-fold aim with all of its programs is to ‘Do-No-Harm’ and to maximize positive impacts. For this project, PHG will ensure that beneficiaries, staff, and partners will be protected as much as possible from contracting or spreading COVID-19 by taking all safety precautions and measures throughout the entire project phases, including interviews, needs assessment, field visits, distribution of material, and technical implementation. Throughout the whole implementation phases and project activities PHG will be followed a clear protection approach to ensure a solid engagement at the community level by local community committees. Their role throughout the whole project will be that of guaranteeing compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female-headed households, households with people with disabilities), moreover the name and personal data of beneficiaries will be kept confidential and safe from unintended use by applying effective data protection measures, prevention and response measures will be put in place in case of physical/sexual violence arising from lack of WASH facilities, in coordination with protection actors, Exposure to safety threats will be considered through the process of hygiene and dignity kits distribution, ensuring that beneficiaries will not be under any risk of theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst receiving their hygiene kits home through door-to-door distribution of hygiene and dignity kits will be taken in account. Furthermore, the hygiene kit size will be taken in consideration, ensuring that beneficiaries are able to carry home with no additional burdens (physical or financial) and without the assistance for others to carry them and in case they need help, an assistance should be provided for free and the hygiene promotion sessions will be conducted based on the culture respect and religious inclusion and that there is a proportionate number of female hygiene promoters. To avoid discrimination the proposed actives were tailored to target the most marginalized and vulnerable groups in the community in terms of sanitations. It will be ensured that the process of beneficiary registration and documentation is inclusive through applying a systematic outreach, gender balance will be ensured in the distribution teams and hygiene promoters through assigning enough women available as a contact point for women in the community, specific needs of the vulnerable group’s women, girls, older persons will be checked and provided e.g. sanitary pads and adult diapers in hygiene kits and provide additional personal hygiene items for persons with incontinence or severe diarrhea, an inclusive orientation session will be conducted on WASH services and how to use hygiene kits to ensure equitable access to all types of activities, the information on WASH will be distributed through various communication means to reach the broader community and to account for different literacy levels eg. door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms and the hygiene Promotion messages will be simple to make it understandable for the children.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposed activities were developed through regular contact with beneficiaries and recent needs assessments, in coordination with the Palestinian Water Authority, CMWU, MOPWH and Area Focal points. In line with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on the accountability to affected population, PHG is engaging households in the intervention design. As women and girls in sanitation bear most of the burden of carrying, installing sewer systems and connections to the household level, their involvement is crucial to effective WASH and sanitation projects. Women also have the most responsibility for sanitation, hygiene, health of the home and the children. Moreover, inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. In this way, men and boys will be encouraged to be actually engaged in their promotion, thus contributing to the improvement of the health and hygiene in their households and communities. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level. PHG staff and project committee role throughout the whole project will that that of guaranteeing compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female farmers, female-headed households, households with people with disabilities). Beneficiaries will be consulted from the project onset regarding their preferred type of assistance and will be invited to participate in fully empowered committees. Awareness raising: in order to maximize the project’s impact, beneficiaries will be informed and sensitized by PHG staff and project committee on the prevention of major health related risks and best hygiene practice. In line with human rights PHG will be followed a clear and accessible grievance mechanism will be set up for receiving suggestions and complaints and assisting in detecting any misconduct, Feedback mechanisms will be confidential that the beneficiaries are not required to reveal their identity to the project staff, all the staff including hygiene promoter, workers and hygiene kit distributers will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, all staff, hygiene promoter, workers and hygiene kit distributes will receive a training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and the full rights of beneficiaries for receiving WASH assistance will be preserved through strictly confirming to all implementing stuff as the beneficiary don’t have to pay or provide services to anyone in exchange for Wash services.
