Project Summary

According to the Government of Kenya (GoK) and the UN, the country is facing its worst drought in 40 years. More than four million people are "food insecure," and 3.3 million cannot get enough water to drink. Prior to that 2016/2017 the Horn of Africa was affected by severe drought and by famine in 2011. It is estimated that over 4.3 million people are in need across 13 counties in Northeastern Kenya.
While there is a regular monitoring of a number of indicators linked to drought across the Kenyan Arid and Semi-Arid Lands (ASAL) region through the Long Rains Food Security Assessments and the Short Rains Assessments, such assessments are not mandated to understand the mobility dynamics and vulnerabilities associated with the movement of people and needs, specific to those dynamics.
The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM)-Mobility Tracking and Multi-Sectoral Assessment activities identified 4 types of mobile populations that moved as a result of the drought: (i) absentees are people that left the location and have not yet returned, (ii) arrivals are people who came to the location of study from other places in Kenya, (iii) returnees are people that left the location but came back, and, (iv) foreign-nationals are non-Kenyans who live at the assessment location.
Key activities shall include:
1. Pre-DTM roll-out workshop with government stakeholders in Turkana
2. Identify settlements within the ward with drought induced mobility
3. Train and deploy local team leaders and enumerators on data collectionConduct validation workshops with government officials (CCs, DCCs, NDMA and county government representatives)
4. Publication of DTM reports.
