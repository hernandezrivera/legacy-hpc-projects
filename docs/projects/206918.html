Project Summary

UNICEF proposes to support life-saving and life-sustaining actions targeting vulnerable young children, pregnant and breastfeeding women—both displaced and in host communities, addressing immediate nutrition-related deprivations. UNICEF's action will support the treatment of severe acute malnutrition, first-line response to displacement, and improving maternal and child feeding practices in conflict-affected locations.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Compounding effects of multiple displacements, poor access to livelihood services in areas of return coupled with disease outbreaks (cholera), negatively impacted the food insecurity and nutrition status of children and women. Very high stunting levels persist in the North (33 to 73 per cent) with very low minimum acceptable diet for children 6-23 months (SMART 2023). 2023 Interphase Classification for Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN) estimates that 211,938 children under five are in need of lifesaving treatment for wasting, of which, 75,604 with high mortality risk, due to severe acute malnutrition (SAM) in conflict and climate shock prone districts. This will be further exacerbated by the El Niño, which is expected to cause overall higher temperatures and limited precipitations increasing the risk of drought.UNICEF’s response to the nutritional needs of people affected by conflict includes health and nutrition screening and referral services, treatment of severe wasting in children under five years of age, in-service training and supervision of frontline health staff and community health workers for quality nutrition care, including micronutrients supplementation for malnutrition prevention. Multisectoral collaboration with health, food security, protection and social and behaviour change sectors will be enforced through geo-convergence of activities to reduce vulnerabilities and improve maternal and infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices for optimum nutrition.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection issues for nutrition mainly concern the security while accessing and utilizing nutrition services delivered at health facilities and mobile brigades which are normally located and built in such a way that limits protection risks. Nutrition humanitarian partners will be trained on specific protection considerations and referrals pathways, in close collaboration with child protection services to support protection mechanisms for vulnerable children.UNICEF will also build the capacity of frontline humanitarian actors on inclusive humanitarian action and will empower children and adolescents with disabilities to participate in humanitarian processes and decision-making. Emphasis will be put on identifying and addressing barriers to accessing services, information and humanitarian assistance by children and adolescents with disabilities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Structural and deeply embedded gender inequalities are widespread across the country, which translates into systematic exclusion of women and girls from decision-making (at household and community level), limited access and control to resources, exposure to gender-based violence and harmful practices, limited access to services and overall lacking agency on strategic choices for their lives and well-being. Conflict in the northern regions and natural disasters have been exacerbating such inequalities, significantly exacerbating the exposure to multiple forms of violence. Women and girls’ resilience results are compromised by these pre-existing inequalities, and their vulnerability to GBV and negative coping strategies is enhanced by the disruption of basic services and GBV prevention and referral mechanisms. UNICEF will adopt a crosscutting approach to GBV risk mitigation, strengthening humanitarian workers' and key stakeholders’ understanding and engagement to protect women and children with and without disabilities. Adolescent girls and boys including those with disabilities will be empowered with a view at promoting a more substantial engagement in preparedness planning and enhancing their resilience in humanitarian crises, including after natural disasters.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Malnutrition and disability are closely interrelated and have a number of points of convergence. Commonly this relationship is framed as a lack of appropriate nutrition in both pregnant and breastfeeding mothers and young children leading to impairment. Therefore, UNICEF efforts focus on improving maternal and child nutrition to prevent or mitigate disability, while strengthening the early identification of developmental delays among children with SAM.The inclusion of children with disabilities will be achieved through a) capacity building of UNICEF’s implementing partner; b) training of health workers on early identification of developmental delays; c) data collection/disaggregation for medical mobile brigades and d) mainstreaming disability and identification of development delays among SAM children.Case management of acute malnutrition services will be adapted to include children with disabilities or caregivers of children with disabilities as 5 per cent of the total target.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Climate change exacerbates undernutrition through three main causal pathways: 1) impacts on household access to sufficient, safe and adequate food; 2) impacts on care and feeding practices; and 3) impacts on environmental health and access to health services.The Global Climate Risk Index scores Mozambique fifth among the countries most affected by natural disasters over the period 2000-2019, and first in 2019, having experienced cyclones of unprecedented severity that affected nearly 2.5 million people, many of them from Northern provinces. Aligned with the UNICEF Nutrition Strategy 2020-2030, UNICEF will: a) mainstream assessments on the impact of climate change-related effects on maternal and child diets in the planned nutrition assessments, b) increase knowledge and awareness raising for action through SBCC programs for use of locally adapted foods who are more resilient to climate change and c) support implementation of climate resilient emergency programming and adaptations.
