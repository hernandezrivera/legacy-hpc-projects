Project Summary

Lebanon is experiencing one of the worst economic crises in the world since the mid-ninetieth. Many factors exacerbated the already existing issues in relation to lack of services, weak infrastructure, and the high employment rate; the effects of the Syrian refugee crises, Covid-19 pandemic, Beirut Port blast, and recently, Cholera outbreak. The multi-facet crisis affected the unemployment rate among the labour force of communities residing in Lebanon; raised by 29.6% (between 2018-2022) and put more constraints on households to secure their basic needs (Labour Force Survey 2022, by ILO and CAS). The inflation rate significantly increased to record higher than 170% where the Lebanese Lira lost 95% of its value. The purchasing power of households significantly decreased and urged them to adopt crisis coping mechanisms reflected in cutting cost on education, WaSH, food and health.
Although Lebanon is considered as an upper-middle income country, public services and infrastructures are poor and have been furtherly affected by the economic crisis, lack of reforms, and the implications of the Syrian refugees protracted crisis. Lebanon’s infrastructure is reaching a collapsing point affecting the access of water for up to 4 million people. Similarly, the wastewater infrastructure is in need of major support and reform and is threatened to collapse at any time posing serious risk on the environmental and the health sectors. Bekaa valley has the second highest vulnerability for water and sanitation despite the presence of their existing water and wastewater related infrastructure (UNICEF,2021). Based on preliminary discussions with different stakeholders in Bekaa, alongside the challenges related to fuel increasing costs and electricity cuts, the municipalities reported they lack the water resources necessary to sufficiently support all the water-related needs of residents and the villages themselves. As for Beirut and Mount Lebanon, over 2 million people reside in them, among them177,782 registered Syrian refugees and 10,000 refugees and asylum-seekers from other countries such as Iraq and Sudan (UNHCR, December 2022). The water-related tensions and incidents reported are both inter-communal and intra-communal. Within this context, the project aims at providing essential humanitarian support to the most vulnerable people affected by the economic crisis; through supporting WASH infrastructure and systems to ensure adequate, equitable and safe access to water, sanitation and hygiene, and to strengthen the resilience and self-reliance of the most vulnerable crisis-affected population including migrants.
Since its establishment in 2013, Nabad uses a right-based approach to address the issues faced by the vulnerable groups and to increase their access to clean water and proper sanitation. This action adheres to the strategic objectives of the ERP focusing on supporting the most vulnerable among the Lebanese, migrants and Palestinian in Lebanon at individual and household levels and is in line with the Wash sector priority. The project will focus on: Apply a system approach at area-level to ensure the maximum accessibility by all residents, use clean energy and solar pumping to increase access to water, engage community members and increase their participation, and address public health risk through putting efforts into the reduction of water-related diseases. The proposed geographical areas are all categorized as areas hosting significant numbers of vulnerable populations and and with severe, uncovered needs. The beneficiaries will be selected on the basis of vulnerability assessments. The project is designed to benefit the most vulnerable which is also reflected in the proposed action’s strong focus on gender and other vulnerability criteria like size of households, assets, disabilities, and displacement status. Support to infrastructure is equally based on underserved and poorest populations but will also benefit the wider community.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

For the below 3 outputs, Nabad will conduct,- Safe and sufficient access to water: Rehabilitation of water supply at HH level: Replacement/maintenance of water, Installation/repairing water pumps, Replacement/maintenance of existing water tanks, Distribution of water filters and disinfection material as required, Rehabilitation of boreholes and installing water pumps with solar panels. The boreholes will be connected to reservoirs and water point distribution channels to be distributed and reachable to the whole community, Solarization of water supply system, Water Quality Monitoring. - Access to adequate sanitation services: Replacement/maintenance of water networks as necessary, Installation/repairing water pumps, Water treatment and safe storage, Installation of water filters and disinfection material. Replacement/maintenance of existing water tanks, awareness sessions on the importance of clean and safely stored water and water tank cleaning campaigns with the students in schools, Solarization of water supply system, Water Quality Monitoring.Rehabilitation of school and primary health centres toilets:Rehabilitation of latrines in schools and the Primary health centers that will have a hygiene promotion committee each, will be trained to deliver awareness sessions on all Wash-related topics and ensure proper hygiene and sanitation practices.- Access to critical hygiene items:Water management awareness, Water-related diseases and vector control awareness

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Nabad’s Risk Management Matrix will be continuously updated taking into account the mitigation of anticipated risks such as: increased social tensions, by targeting the most marginalized groups across nationalities, perceived aid bias, by clarity and transparency over targeting and eligibility and safety related risks including but not limited to GBV, by adopting a Community-based protection approach and ensuring communities are engaged in humanitarian decision-making processes. “TAKALAM” the community complaint and feedback mechanism at Nabad is functional and will be further developed to increase PWDs inclusion. Access to beneficiary information will be restricted to maintain their privacy and confidentiality aligned with NABAD’s data protection policy. All staff and associated personnel will be trained on PSEA, child safeguarding and the reporting mechanism. Gender and age disaggregated data will be collected and analyzed to monitor and understand the changing gendered realities.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Nabad adopts a two-way communication approach that serves the emphasis on transparency and accountability towards its beneficiaries and other stakeholders where it will ensure timely sharing of information along the project cycle, to be disseminated through information sessions and meetings. Nabad will keep its stakeholders informed of the progress challenges encountered. Whereas with beneficiaries, the existing mechanisms of sharing feedback and information will be utilized to its best to enable beneficiaries share their concerns and have access to relevant information through different channels and tools such as: information sessions, awareness sessions, community WaSH committees, posters, leaflets, SMS...Nabad will establish WaSH committees that will be selected from the community members of different ages (youth and adults), gender and diversity groups and will be trained as relevant. These committees will play the role of key agents in passing knowledge to their communities.
