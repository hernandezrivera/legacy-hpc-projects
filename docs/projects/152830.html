Project Summary

The action will be an integrated WASH and Nutrition program where WASH integrated interventions starting from SAM/MAM admission at OTP/SC will be targeted “mother/caretaker – malnourished children” until the household level aiming to break the vicious circle of diarrhea diseases/nematodes with malnutrition, addressing also small stock open defecation affecting the young children. Emergency hygiene and sanitation programming will be done alongside nutrition treatment and prevention targeting vulnerable populations affected by conflicts at health/nutrition sites and in the community. Community volunteers such as hygiene promoters, hand pump mechanics and MIYCN support groups will be engaged in coordination with government structures while local leaders will be engaged in consultative meetings and encouraged to actively support programming.

Needs

South Sudan is still facing a deepening economic crisis, insecurity and insufficient agricultural production at household level that has caused unprecedented levels of food insecurity and alarming levels of acute malnutrition. In August 2018, FEWNET reported that 5.3 million people were facing Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 and 4) acute food insecurity, of which 1 million people were facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Most nutrition surveys showed GAM rates above the 15% WHO emergency threshold but in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, GAM rate was found to be at critical levels (GAM 15.0%-29.9%). Given the current food security situation, immediate action to maintain and save livelihoods will have lasting impact. In 2019 CEDS will focus on the integrated response in Northern Bahr El Ghazal and Warrap because some pockets of the IDPs and returnees have integrated into the community. This has created a burden to the population leading to high manifestations of malnutrition, illnesses and deaths. Malnutrition has become chronic and cyclic, usually reaching peak rates in the pre- harvest periods, lower in the post-harvest period but always exceeding the threshold for emergence intervention. One of contributing factors has been an increasing pressure on the already limited food production couple with poor water and sanitation infrastructure as well as poor hygiene practices. Communities have low access to safe and protected water source and sanitation because of poor operation and maintenance of WASH facilities which expose them to the risk of diseases such as diarrheal, malaria, Acute Respiratory Infection (ARI) all of which are among the main causes of child mortality. The other contributing factors have been; poor feeding due to unavailability of dietaries food, devaluation of the South Sudanese Pounds and inadequate food commodities supply in the market.To address these couple of issues around malnutrition, illnesses and deaths related to WASH; the hygiene promoters will be trained on Integrated Packages (Nutrition and Health, Food security and WASH Services delivery). Other aspect will be the distribution of WASH supplies, institutional Latrine improvement through construction, hygiene awareness to pregnant and lactating women, awareness of safe storage of drinking water, safe disposal of feces.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

CEDS takes proactive steps to ensure the accuracy, accessibility and timeliness of information provided to the beneficiaries. To ensure that even the hard to reach members of the community receive information about the organization in general and the different project, CEDS employs a multichannel approach to provide information to the beneficiaries about projects as well as assistance to be provided. During projects initiation community meetings, the project staff take time to explain to the beneficiaries about the project, assistance to be provided and all other relevant information about the project. Since the community leaders have a constant access to the affected communities including the project beneficiaries, CEDS passes information to the community leaders about the proposed projects and all the relevant information about the assistance to be provided which is in turn provided to the community member including the potential beneficiaries. CEDS as well uses project committee members to pass information to the beneficiaries since they project committee members have access to the beneficiaries and are able to pass timely information to the beneficiaries. Since the literacy level among the beneficiaries is low, CEDS uses pictorial IEC materials to provide information to the beneficiaries. This IEC materials including posters and flyers are made available at strategic public locations reachable to most of the community members as well as the beneficiaries. CEDS works very closely with a local Intra news supported non-profit radio station (Akol yam) to provide information to the beneficiaries. In these information provision sessions, the beneficiaries have an opportunity to call to seek for clarification on all relevant information about the assistance to be provided.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CEDS has zero tolerance to sexual abuse and exploitation and take all the necessary stringent steps to protect all stakeholders including beneficiaries and staff against sexual exploitation and abuse. CEDS has in place a protection against sexual exploitation and abuse policy (PSEA) policy that is in force and must be read and signed by all staff and any person doing business with CEDS. CEDS has put in place as well safe mechanisms including a toll free phone number, an email address as well as a point person through which any cases or even suspicion of sexual exploitation and abuse can be reported. CEDS takes a proactive initiative to inform the beneficiaries about their right to be protected from sexual exploitation and abuse and how they can raise concern about the same. Moreover, new staff who joins the organization must be inducted on PSEA but also refresher trainings and workshops are organized semiannually for the staff to reaffirm the importance and the organizations commitment to PSEA. To better adopt to the changing environments, CEDS carries out a general analysis of any reported sexual and abuse cases reported and uses these learning to strengthen the PSEA policies and initiatives.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CEDS consults with a wide range of stakeholders to ensure that the views of all the stakeholders are catered for including the hard to reach members if the community as the elderly and the sick. As part of the wider consultation initiative, CEDS has carried out a needs assessment that has reached a proper representative of the affected population and widened the consultation coverage. The needs assessment included household surveys that reached the neglected member of community, focused group discussions with interest groups including children, the youth, women, men and special interest groups as well as key informant interviews with key members of the communities including youth leaders, religious leaders, leaders of special groups as the disabled, educational leaders as teachers among other. Again CEDS has organized consultative workshops with the interest group leaders and also held community consultation meetings where the community members are consulted in different aspects of the project.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Accountability to beneficiaries including collecting and acting on complaints and feedback will be core to this project. Multiple channels will be put in place to ensure that complaints and feedback from beneficiaries and all other stakeholders are collected and acted on. With members selected by the project beneficiaries, the project will have in place complaints and response committee who will be responsible for routine collection of complaints and feedback from the beneficiaries during the day to day implementation of the project. During project monitoring, the project staff will intentionally take initiative of collection the feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries for action. On a monthly basis, the project staff will hold community feedback meetings where the community members including the beneficiaries again have an opportunity to share their feedback and complaints with the project staff either privately or in public as they wish. The project will have in place a toll free hotline in which the beneficiaries as well will be able to call and share their feedback and complaints as need be. The project will partner with Intra news supported local radio (Akol Yam) to hold talk shows on the right of the beneficiaries and the community members to give feedback. During these radio session, the beneficiaries will be encouraged to call the radio call the radio station and give feedback and as well complain about the project.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CEDS will ensure that disabled persons have equal opportunity in this proposed project as other community members. During beneficiary’s identification, priority will be to household headed by the disabled and as well the beneficiary’s selection exercise will be carried out at village level so that the disabled are can easily access selection site. This will be conducted purely on basis of needs targeting the most vulnerable, regardless of tribe, ethnicity, gender or political or religious beliefs. Needs are based selection on pre-defined vulnerability criteria, in accordance with international humanitarian standards. However, extra notice will be placed on the disabled people. Disabled people are given priority and encouraged to participate in CEDS programs depending on their vulnerability category.
