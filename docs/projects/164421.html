Project Summary

UNHCR as a lead agency coordinating the NFI responses in Syria and from Gaziantep (Turkey), will provide through this project the lifesaving and life sustaining NFI support, through distribution of NFIs and seasonal supplementary items to IDPs, returnees and affected host communities.
This project aims to assist 434,940 families/2,174,700 individuals with emergency NFIs kits including cash/voucher for NFI and 251,200 household/1,256,000 individuals with the provision of Seasonal NFI kits (winter/summer items) in addition to emergency NFI kits including cash/voucher for NFI. The response will be implemented from Turkey and from inside Syria.
Based on the draft HNO for 2020, the NFI Sector estimates that 3.42 million people require NFI assistance in Syria in 2020. UNHCR’s NFI programme recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group through a range of NFI interventions. Beneficiaries are selected based on the degree of eligibility and vulnerability, with priority given to newly displaced, IDPs living in hard to reach locations and returnees. Within these population groups most vulnerable members like female headed households, persons with disabilities, and elderly persons at risk require core assistance, supplementary and seasonal assistance (winter/summer items) including cash/voucher for NFIs.
Emergency NFI response will be provided through inter-agency convoy deliveries to hard-to-reach locations and through distribution of seasonal and complementary items.
The overall project target reflects targets for two types of assistance package, however a portion of beneficiaries will receive both types of kits, depending on their needs and the season in which assistance is provided. NFI kits will be provided to IDPs, returnee families and host communities, in line with vulnerability criteria. A standard winter kit will be provided as one-time distribution in addition to the NFIs. Additional supplementary items consisting of sleeping bags and adult winter jackets will be provided to most vulnerable IDPs residing in sub-standard shelters. If family is assisted for the 1st time during winter season, it will receive both standard NFI kit and additional supplementary winter items. The families who have received standard NFI kit before winter will receive only additional supplementary items during winterization program.
UNHCR’s NFI Programme will also prioritize those with longer term needs and support the resilience of local communities and households within the humanitarian response by protecting and restoring livelihoods, access to essential services and rehabilitation of socio-economic infrastructure through the local production support and introduction of pilot voucher scheme. Pilot voucher scheme in exchange of local manufactured items produced through partner’s livelihood support project will help reduce dependence on harmful coping strategies. In this regard, this pilot project aims to meet lifesaving NFI needs, particularly of newly displaced people and returnees through area-based approach. The pilot project is currently in the phase of the market assessment; however, the implementation is conditional to receiving approval by the Government.

4 - Needs

As per draft 2020 HNO an overall estimated number of People in Need has decreased from 4.4 million in 2019 to 3.42 million in 2020, as a result of extensive distribution of NFI aid by partners and improved access to markets in some parts of the country. Identification of the target beneficiaries is conducted by UNHCR Field Offices in close coordination with the partners and the NFIs distribution is conducted in coordinated manner with all the agencies working in the targeted areas to minimize gap and duplication. Local partners and CBOs play a critical role for needs identification and distribution, in view of their close and direct contact with the targeted population and familiarity with the areas where they operate. Based on the needs identification conducted through implementing partners and local coordination with other NFIs resourcing agencies, the annual operation NFIs plan for each Governorate is prepared and adjusted in line with developments of situation in the field. Requirements for sourcing of specific complementary items are identified through Post Distribution Monitoring reports in consultation with beneficiaries.

5 - Implementation of activities

Following activities will be implemented through Partner organizations: 1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs including cash/voucher for NFIs:The emergency response from Turkey and from inside Syria, will respond to the NFI needs of 2,174,700 newly displaced people, returnees and affected populations. UNHCR will keep around 5,000 emergency kits available in 6 Hub warehouses in Syria to be able to respond immediately in case of an emergency situation. UNHCR emergency NFI kits consist of high thermal blankets, plastic tarpaulins, jerry cans, foam mattresses, kitchen sets, sleeping mats and solar lamps, including cash/voucher for these items. UNHCR aims to support 19,805 families with voucher for NFI (11,076 IDPs families and 8,728 Returnees families). 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance including cash/voucher for NFI:The live-saving winterized response from Turkey and from inside Syria, will provide NFI support to 1,256,000 IDPs, Returnees and host community members to protect people from harsh cold winter conditions. Winterized NFI kits consist of NFI kits in addition to extra plastic tarpaulins, adult winter jackets, winter clothing kits and sleeping bags, including cash/voucher for these items. PoCs who haven’t received any NFI kits will be targeted.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks related to inclusion and social cohesion, physical safety, SGBV, will be taken into account when providing NFIs and vouchers to beneficiaries. 1. Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across membersMitigation: agreeing on minimum assistance package across sector; assessing and validating any changes before they occur; identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions; ensuring well organized distribution plans to avoid overcrowding; 2. Risk of tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees. Mitigation: Ensuring that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status; selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed; distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population; ensuring that distribution is conducted in a proportional manner and that selection criteria is transparent and groups are fully informed; ensuring that the NFI distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population; enhancing the monitoring mechanisms in place by linking post distribution monitoring with the protection risk analysis; and increasing the presence of UNHCR staff during distribution. 3. Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Mitigation: Sector partners ensure that their staffs have read/are aware of the Code of Conduct; Sector partners develop a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly; enforcing adherence to the Code of Conduct by all partners; prioritizing female headed households, people with specific needs, older persons and communicating it to the community; holding pre consultations with communities through mobile teams; giving special consideration to people with specific needs; training the distribution staff about the rights of beneficiaries to receive assistance.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Operational risk: Lack of access is hampering needs assessment and effective monitoring of delivery of assistance that may lead to aid diversion. Mitigation: Establish harmonized monitoring systems and tools (for example, conduct monthly reconciliation of monthly distribution, post distribution monitoring). Capitalize on information collected through ORVs and in the community centres and systematize methodologies used for focus group discussions in the community centres. Establish mechanisms for communicating with communities. Contingency stocks are being prepositioned into six hubs in Syria to be able to respond to humanitarian needs in timely manner. 2. Risk of fraud: The risk of potential for aid diversion, corruption and fraud, leads to reduced assistance to the persons of concern to UNHCR and a decline in donor funding. Mitigation: Strengthen internal controls that will mitigate and prevent potential procurement and financial fraud. Clear SOPs for beneficiary selection and distribution process used by UNHCR and partner organizations in intervention design, implementation and monitoring. Staff trained on dealing with aid diversion. Enforce reporting mechanisms embedded in anti-fraud policies. The findings and recommendations in partners’ monthly and quarterly reports, post-distribution monitoring reports, are carefully reviewed and implemented. 3. Reputational Risk: Emanating from the above identified risks resulting in lessening of donor confidence, damage to Partner and UNHCR reputation and reduction in funding thereby limiting operational capacities. Mitigation: Implement proactive actions on operational and financial risk to correspondingly reduce the likelihood of materialization of any reputational risk.
