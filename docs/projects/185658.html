Project Summary

HI will continue its 12+ years of broad support for refugee/FDMNs and host communities by addressing the immediate acute and chronic health needs of refugees and host communities through multi-sectoral mobile units, center-based services and a strong referral pathways. The proposed intervention will be delivered through twin modality:
1. Multi-sectorial mobile units composed of qualified Nurses and Midwives, Physiotherapists, PSS officers, Protection Officers and social workers will be deployed to identify and provide tailored and individual assistance to the most vulnerable, especially the persons with disabilities and their caregivers who might be excluded from services due to mobility or social barriers. This mobile teams deliver home-based services focused on education for beneficiaries and their caregivers on safe, effective therapeutic exercises and interventions.
2. Static points: Multidisciplinary teams support beneficiaries with rehabilitation, PSS, basic healthcare through nurses and protection. Training is provided to persons and caregivers on basic home-based rehabilitation and proper use, maintenance and repair of mobility devices. Group sessions on MHPSS and assessment for protection beneficiaries take place.
-In home-based and center-based services, Nurses will support beneficiaries with: wound care, bedsores care, ante and post-natal care. A key focus will be to provide maternal care for women who cannot access health facilities (i.e. women and girls with disabilities), including the prevention and detection of pregnancy-related risks and complications. Nurses refer to the nearest Health Facility and follow up to ensure they receive needed specialized services.
- Support to government hospitals and health facilities both in the host and refugee communities to provide rehabilitation services. HI coordinates at Age & Disability Working Group (ADWG) as co-founder to provide services to unserved camps if disability specific services are required. HI also have modality of outreach rehabilitation team to serve the areas where HI doesnot serve on regular basis.
- PSEA focals are assigned at each site offices who ensure PSEA principles are mainstreamed through all activities through extensive training of all staff and volunteers using PSEA Network materials and recommendations. Feedback is regularly elicited through suggestions boxes, a dedicated hotline for host and refugee communities, community discussions and 1:1 interview. A dedicated Focal Point to receive complaints is assigned in the Cox’s Bazar base to coordinate with camp level team leaders to ensure referral for needed services and ensure survivor advocacy as needed.
HI is currently implementing four projects that addresses above mentioned health services funded by different donors. To provide the service for the host community people, HI support 5 Health Facilities. It includes Cox's Bazar Sadar hospital, Ukhiya Upazila health complex, Teknaf Upazila health complex, KTP MSF-OCA-331 and IOM Leda-200 with rehabilitation staff (physiotherapist) provide services in those facilities. Both host community people and beneficiaries from the camps can receive services from these facilities.
HI welcomes complaints and feedback from the beneficiaries to integrate beneficiaries’ views in the project activities. HI MEAL team leads the complaints, feedback and response mechanism. Currently different complaints and feedback collection modalities exists in both host communities and camps. Modalities are face to face interviews, group discussions, hotline and feedback box. Based on the sensitivities of the complaints HI provide solution as soon as possible.
Provision of basic nursing & SRHR services (ANC, PNC, FP awareness) through individual, family and group sessions to women, men, boys and girls with significant focus on vulnerable people (persons with disabilities, pregnant women, older persons, chronically ill and who could not access the facilities etc) in the camps.

1 - Identified Needs

According to REACH age & disability assessment study (May 2021), the prevalence of persons with disabilities was 12% among refugees and ranged from 6% to 19% based on the camp and the presence of mobility barriers. In shelters and around camps, 52% and 76% of persons with disabilities reported struggling with mobility issues. As of UNHCR Factsheet October 2021, a total of 907,766 individuals was recorded, among which 52% are children, 44% are adults and 4% are older persons while women and girls are 52% of the refugee population. The lack of recognized legal status of Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh placed them on a precarious legal footing, denying rights attached to the status of refugees and fulfilment of their basic needs through income generating activities. Due to this, Rohingya refugees remain the most vulnerable group in Bangladesh, relying heavily on humanitarian aid. Particularly exposed to risks of human rights violations and recourse to negative coping mechanisms such as early marriage, survival sex, and child labor, it is well documented that Rohingya women face particular protection concerns and barriers accessing services that can partly be attributed to social cultural norms that prevent women from engaging more fully in the public sphere. 65% of the refugee camps and 52% of host community households not having been able to access protection services and faced barrier (J-MSNA, October 2021). Limited or no access to quality sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services during emergency and crisis conditions puts women and adolescent girls at higher risk of morbidity and mortality that requires utmost importance in terms of service design, delivery and implementation. Furthermore, it is well documented that Rohingya refugees are suffering from severe mental health problems, such as depression and psychosocial impairment caused by persecutions, violence, and forced displacements in Myanmar, aggravated by their impoverished and inadequate living conditions and unemployment. Further, older persons and persons with disabilities are often at increased risk of violence, abuse and neglect because of attitudinal (stigma and discrimination), communication and environmental barriers. As a result, negative perceptions of persons with disabilities and their perceived limited ability to report violence and defend themselves put them at risk for further protection risks and violence. Additionally, persons with disabilities and older people face significant barriers (e.g. lack of accessibility) to access mainstream services, including specialized services. A lack of available specialized services (i.e. Rehabilitation services to prevent and reduce functional limitations, limited access to assistive devices (REACH study, 2021 says 56% reported wanting to receive access to assistive devices, but have not been supported), medication, chronic disease treatment, etc.) thus puts them at greater risk of exclusion. In addition, the influx of humanitarian support has concentrated essential services like health and protection services in camp areas and the ongoing fencing of the camp creates additional barriers for services to reach the host community having to walk an hour or more to receive health services. In an HI internal assessment 56% of host community members surveyed said that assistance they received was not frequent enough, only 8% were visited by a community health worker in the last 14 days and 43% said not enough relief was provided. Resentment and frustration at the lack of support for the extremely poor area coupled with property disputes and fears of COVID 19 spread have created a conflicted environment. In regards to the above-mentioned needs, HI has begun to expand services into host communities (Nhila union of Teknaf) and in the camp 24 and 26 to provide dynamic and multi-sector services at a household and center basis to the most vulnerable households there.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Provision of physical and functional rehabilitation sessions including provision of assistive devices and specific items at health facilities/hospital to persons (including women, men boys and girls) with injuries and/or with disabilities.Activity 2: Provision of technical support to the relevant health facilities (3 government hospitals and 2 partners' clinics) through training for health workers on basic early physical rehabilitation care to improve referrals, understanding of rehabilitation care within the health facilities and ensure comprehensive and optimum care for the patients/beneficiaries. Activity 3: Provision of physical and functional rehabilitation sessions including assistive devices and orthosis through centre and home-based care to persons (including women, men, boys and girls) with injuries and/or with disabilities.Activity 4: Capacity building of the caregivers of persons with disabilities and/or injuries on basic home based-care to ensure the understanding of the importance of rehabilitation and optimum care. Activity 5: Provision of individual level psychosocial support to overcome stress related symptoms that effect individuals with long-term chronic disabling conditions. Activity 6: Provision of group-based psychosocial sessions (emotional processing groups etc.) to persons of concern and their caregivers. And Peer Support Group (community connectedness) to address barriers faced by persons of concern with disabilities, older people, and other vulnerable groups.Activity 7: Internal and external referrals to basic and specific specialized services. Activity 8: Provision of basic nursing & SRHR services (ANC, PNC, FP awareness) through individual, family and group sessions to women, men, boys and girls with significant focus on vulnerable people (persons with disabilities, pregnant women, older persons, chronically ill and who could not access the facilities etc.) in the camps. Activity 9: Provision of inclusive sports events to promote inclusion of people with disabilities in community life as well as recreational support for children at risk.Activity 10: Awareness session with Health service providers in the camp and surrounding host communities regarding access to persons with disabilities and service provision
