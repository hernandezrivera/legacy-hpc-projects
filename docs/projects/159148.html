Project Summary

Conflicts and climate change in Somalia have negatively impacted on the education needs of the affected girls, boys and adolescents. Lack of educational opportunities has left children in psychosocial distress, vulnerable and exposed to a range of socio-political risks and exploitation. This has increased the propensity for negative coping strategies, including engagement in child labour, forced and early marriage and recruitment into radicalism or criminal groups.
More than half of the primary school-aged children (65% for girls and 59% of boys) have no access to education and gender gaps in enrolment and retention are also significant with girls facing specific barriers to education (Somalia Joint Multi Cluster Needs Assessments 2019). In addition, those accessing learning opportunities are not assured of continuation due to school drop-outs and closure of schools exacerbating protection risks.
Displacement continues to affect access to education for thousands of children. An estimated 108,000 school aged children of the 270,000 people have been newly displaced in Somalia between January to August 2019 (UNHCR Protection Return Monitoring Network). There are also cases of the displaced population suffering multiple and secondary displacements leading to disruptions in education cycle affecting both enrolment and retention apart from quality.
Somalia remained the country with the highest number of cases of the recruitment and use of children (Children and Armed Conflict Report of the Secretary-General 2018). Attacks on schools have had effects on access to education with a total of 2,228 boys and 72 girls, some as young as 8, were recruited and used by parties to conflict. In addition, there were 77 attacks on schools including killing, abduction and threats against teachers, destruction and looting.
UNICEF emergency education will work closely with Child Protection and WASH to promote an integrated approach. Attention will be focused on enhancing safe and protective learning environment that is gender and disabilities inclusive to meet the needs of vulnerable girls, women, boys and men for both displaced and host communities. In line with Education Cluster strategy, the project aims to deliver quality education and improve access to education services and learning opportunities in a safe and protective learning environment for the most vulnerable and marginalized girls and boys and at risk of exclusion. The project aims to target 130,000 (65,000 girls) across the country, among these 68,114 will be displaced children.
UNICEF will deliver a comprehensive EiE interventions prioritizing education supplies, training of teachers and Community Education Committees (CECs), emergency incentives, provision of safe drinking water and supplementary school feeding, sanitary kits for adolescent girls among others.
To ensure the protection of children, education response will be aligned to Education – Child Protection Integrated Response Framework. It will strive towards improved access to protection services to reduce vulnerabilities and strengthen safety of learners. This will be attained through strengthening child protection systems in schools, psychosocial (PSS) care and referral cases, PSS First Aid, create awareness on protecting children in and out of schools through child to child clubs, CECs and community leadership. This is aimed to ensure schools and the community support children’s physical, cognitive, social and emotional wellbeing. In addition, support for adequate WASH facilities and hygiene promotion activities with emphasis on health and hygiene and Acute Water Diarrhea (AWD) prevention at school level, will be undertaken for learners and teachers.
Systems strengthening will be key in supporting MoE in strengthening management and coordination of education in emergencies. This is aimed to enhance long term sustainability, improved governance and enhancing education capacity to reinforce quality delivery of services.

Mitigation Measures

Protection of displaced civilians: Centrality of protection will be key area that this response plan focuses on to ensure children have access to inclusive learning opportunities in a safe and protective environment. The activities will be designed in such a way that will address protection risks related to effects of conflict and natural disasters such as displacement. To ensure the protection of children, this education response will be aligned to Education and child protection integrated response and in line with Education – Child Protection Response Framework. It will strive towards improved safe and protective learning environment with access to protection services to reduce vulnerabilities and strengthen safety of learners. In line with the Education Cluster response plan, this education response strategy will have a two-pronged approach; a) prioritize education support to children where they live and b) education opportunity to children in place where they move to ensure vulnerable and marginalized girls and boys continue their education and have access to protection services.Mitigating risks of exclusion from humanitarian services: UNICEF and implementing partners will ensure community consultations and discussions during assessment, planning, implementation and monitoring phases of the project. This will ensure the views and needs of the community are the at the centerstage of the project as they are best placed to find the most suitable solutions to address their own problems. Key stakeholders, such as Community Education Committee (CECs), community leaders, parents, teachers and girls and boys will be engaged to raise awareness of child rights and influence behaviours and practices that will enhance overall enabling learning environment for children.At the school level child clubs will support peer to peer support, rights awareness and will take part in schools' decision making to ensure children's voices are heard and they are adequately protected. The project will be designed to address specific need for girls in recognition they face specific challenges. This will be aimed to enhancing girls access to learning opportunities. Adolescent girls will be supported with sanitary kits to ensure regular school attendance.Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be put in place to provide a forum for the beneficiaries to address concerns to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. The community engagement will be ensured at every step of the project, from the beginning to the end of the project. Protection mainstreaming: The community will be engaged to ensure improved safety at and on the way to and from schools, including establishing a safe and violence-free environment and improved infrastructure, such as gender responsive learning and sanitation facilities. Teachers will be trained in psychosocial support and provide referral pathways for children in need of specialized child protection services such as counselling and case management. School will identify a male and female teacher to be child protection focal points to support learners in need of protection support.

Please provide further explanation

This intervention will be anchored and aligned to key priorities of the government 2017-2021 Education Sector strategic Plan (ESSP). This provides opportunities for linkages of the humanitarian-development. The response will continue to support strengthening of the government systems to ensure children benefit from increased access to services. Engagement of Strategic partnerships between civil society, UN agencies, donors and government, including the regional authorities will be strengthened to benefiting from each one’s comparative advantage and ensure complementarity and synergies under humanitarian and development actors. With the ongoing effort to improve policy environment, strengthening systems for planning by the development actors provide an opportunity that will complement education in emergencies initiatives strengthen the capacity of Ministry of Education. UNICEF will work closely with the Ministry of Education (MoE) at all level to strengthen their capacity to enhance effective governance, management, coordination and monitoring thus contributing to a more effective and efficient education system that is responsive to the learning needs of children whether in emergency or as part of the longer-term development context. This is with a vision to eventually help the government to fully take up responsibilities embedding education in emergencies in the mainstream education within the Ministry of education. This will further enhance long term sustainability, improved governance and enhancing education capacity to reinforce quality delivery of services. Extensive engagement of community and local authorities in taking the lead in school governance will foster local ownership and greater parental and community buy-in
