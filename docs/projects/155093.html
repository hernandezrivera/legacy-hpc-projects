Project Summary

The main objective of this Project is to strengthen the protective mechanisms of vulnerable children and Adolescent in refugee camps and in the host community. The project targets 299,770 persons through strengthened community-based mechanisms, provision of psycho-social support, specialized case management and through integrating and mainstreaming child protection and gender in other sectors. The project will strengthen referral mechanisms and will empower children, adolescents and communities by promoting their active participation. Peaceful co-existence with host community will also be strengthened. The partnership with the Department of Social Services (DSS) will help to promote social coexistence through sports, peace-building, meetings, and coordination.
In the camps, UNICEF will target 60,646 girls, 56,700 boys, 47,905 adult males and 51,223 females. In the host community, UNICEF will target 3,296 refugees (824 girls, 824 boys, 824 men and 824 women). UNICEF will also target 80,000 vulnerable Bangladeshi in the host community. The project will be delivered through partnership with 4 local organizations, 8 international organizations and in partnership with the Department of Social Services.

Needs

According to the Inter-Sector Coordination Group-ISCG (November 2018), an estimated 1.2 million Rohingya refugees and vulnerable Bangladeshi host community in Cox Bazar are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2019. The Child Protection Sub-Sector (CPSS) estimates that 391,281 boys and girls (including adolescents) are in need of specific protection and psychosocial services. The Multi Sector Needs Assessment report (July 2018) highlighted several protection concerns requiring specific responses. Girls are in need of protection from sexual violence. During the assessment, 41% of the girls expressed concerns over sexual violence. Adolescent girls are also in need of specific protection from early marriage. During the study. 20% of adolescent girls were assessed to be at risk of child marriage in total disregard to their rights as enshrined in the UNCRC. Boys are in need of protection from radicalization and recruitment by armed groups. During the study, 26% of boys expressed fear of recruitment by armed groups. There is established needs for protection of boys and girls from trafficking and kidnapping. During the study, 49% of the children were assessed as at risks of trafficking and kidnapping. Both boys and girls also need protection from violence. The result of the needs assessment found that 27% of children were concerned over violence in the families and in the communities. Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) needs specific care and protection including suitable alternative care arrangements and family tracing and reunification services. The UNHCR Population Data and Key Demographic Indicators (Oct. 2018) indicates that more than 5,812 UASCs are in need of care and protection. There is also need for protection of persons with disabilities. The UNHCR Population Data and Key Demographic Indicators (Oct. 2018) mentioned above highlighted that more than 8,459 families have already been registered as having Persons living with disabilities (PWD) whose dignity and wellbeing are at more heightened risk due to the displacement and difficult living conditions. The psychosocial distress arising from living in a refugee camp, coupled with the forced displacement of families presents women and children with difficulties that requires psychosocial support to promote positive coping and functioning. The need for psychosocial support is crucial recognizing the difficulties that women and children are predisposed to including inadequate livelihood systems, insufficient adult support, limited access to education, health care and risky physical environment. Family support to children is insufficient and the legal status of children and their families remains unclear due to lack of formal recognition as refugees. Children live in poor conditions in the refugee camps and are at risk of exposure to violence, drugs and substance abuse. UNICEF will directly support a combined total of 299,770 persons. In refugee camps, UNICEF will target 56,700 boys, 60,646 girls, 47,905 adult males and 51,223 adult females. In the host community, UNICEF will target 3,296 refugees living out of camp (824 girls, 824 boys, 824 men and 824 women). Lastly, UNICEF will target 80,000 vulnerable Bangladeshi in the host community (20,000 girls, 20,000 boys, 20,000 women and 20,000 males).

Activities or outputs

The key planned activities in line with the Child Protection Sub-Sector plans include:1. Case Management: Specialized Case Management using standardized inter-agency tools will be provided to Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) and to children who experience violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. UNICEF will support alternative care, family tracing and reunifications and monitoring of the situation children including children with disabilities, support the roll-out of the inter-agency Supervision and Coaching package and the roll out of CPIMS+. 2. Psychosocial Support (PSS): Structured psychosocial support will be provided to children, adolescent and parents through existing child friendly spaces (CFSs) and Adolescent Friendly Spaces (AFS). In 2018, UNICEF established 63 static and 10 mobile CFSs and 73 AFSs. In 2019, the quality of PSS services will be enhanced and the centers supported to function as hubs of coordinated services. 3. Community Base Child Protection Systems: UNICEF will strengthen Community-Based Child Protection mechanisms for both refugees and host community through social mobilization, active participation of local structures such as Child Protection Committees (CPCs), Children Clubs (CC), Parents Groups, Adolescent Clubs, Youth Clubs and local leader through trainings and Capacity building trainings. 4. Capacity Building: Promotion of local capacities is central to UNICEF’s work. UNICEF plans to maintain partnership with 5 local CBOs/organizations, 8 International organizations and with the Department of Social Services (DSS) who through project cooperative agreements will benefit from capacity buildings trainings. 5. Life Skills for adolescent: UNICEF will support adolescent and vulnerable young people through life skills, resilience and ‘hands-on’ skills building activities targeting boys and girls especially the most vulnerable. The project will strengthen existing adolescent safe spaces and access to information, participation and community mobilization in protection activities. 6. Coordination, accountability and feedback mechanisms: UNICEF actively participates at the bi-weekly Camp in Charge meetings. UNICEF hosts and actively participates at the Child Protection Sub-Sector meetings and the Case Management Tasks Force meetings. UNICEF also closely coordinates with DSS, UNHCR and all other CP actors. UNICEF will ensure all IPs are trained on humanitarian code of conduct/Do no Harm principles. 7. Mainstreaming: Child Protection and Gender will be mainstreamed and inter-sectoral coordination will be strengthened. Referral pathways will be developed and strengthened. UNICEF will ensure safe programming and work to combat and prevent sexual abuse and exploitation (PSEA). All staff are mandated to take the UN PSEA within 2 months of employment. 8. DRR: As Cox Bazar is regularly impacted negatively by weather vagaries including winter coldness, UNICEF will support winterization targeting the most vulnerable children, encourage children to support afforestation by planting trees around schools, CFSs and AFSs and Children will be sensitized about climate change. 9. Peaceful co-existence with host community: UNICEF’s program promotes social cohesion between the refugees and the Host Community. The partnership with the Department of Social Services (DSS) will help to promote social coexistence through sports, peace-building, meetings, and coordination. 10. Exit and sustainability Plans: The partnership with 4 local organizations and with DSS ensures local ownership. The involvement of local refugee structures, CPCs, Adolescent Clubs, Youth Clubs also ensures local ownership and promotes sustainability.
