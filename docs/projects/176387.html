Project Summary

The project will provide the humanitarian community with timely, accurate and actionable data and analysis to enable a fair and evidence-based humanitarian response and inform resilience, transition and recovery programming. Key deliverables will include a regularly updated location- and area-level baseline on the presence, number and needs of IDPs and returnees across the country; biometric data management for fair, effective and targeted delivery of humanitarian assistance; representative multi-sector data on household needs and vulnerabilities in urban areas that will feed into South Sudan’s inter-agency multi-sector needs assessment (MSNA); and regular updates on population mobility at key internal transit hubs and border points.

1 - Needs

A solid evidence base on the scale and dynamics of population displacement and return is key to informing humanitarian and transition planning, as well as the more immediate response. While over 1.6 million people have already returned to their homes, many face situations of vulnerability and insecurity that hinder the sustainability of durable solutions, while the same number remains internally displaced. More than half a million were newly displaced in 2020 as a result of flooding but also ongoing subnational and localised conflict. Furthermore, more than seven million South Sudanese citizens (60% of the population) are projected to be facing severe acute food insecurity before the end of July 2021 (IPC South Sudan). Multiple shocks across the country in the second half of 2020, had a significant impact on the availability of data crucial for informed response. Despite the halt in country-wide armed conflict, insecurities remain a high concern in certain areas, compounded by logistics challenges stemming from poor transportation infrastructure that prevented movements to certain areas or interrupted planned missions (e.g. DTM missions to Tonj South, Kapoeta East, Kajo-Keji, etc.). In this context, it is even more so relevant to establish a reliable and sustainable information management system to facilitate timely exchange of actionable data and information and to improve and expand analysis spectrum to inform long term preventive and development efforts (ACAPS, March 2020). Comparative needs analysis across the country allows for prioritization in a context of limited resources and widespread needs, to enable principled humanitarian interventions and create environments conducive to the progressive resolution of displacement. Infrastructural and service mapping in areas of return informs the government and other development actors about local gaps, ensuring targeted and equitable investment in early recovery that is sensitive to conflict dynamics and addresses rather than exacerbating existing inequalities.

2 - Project activities

DTM proposes a continuation of the following activities: Mobility Tracking, Flow Monitoring (including Displacement Site Flow Monitoring), biometric registration, and surveys (including multi-sector needs and vulnerabilities surveys and intention / perception surveys). Mobility Tracking refers to payam and location level (village / site / neighborhood) data collection of stock figures on the presence and number of IDPs (humanitarian baseline), returnees and other relevant population categories, combined with multi-sector location assessments to assess the prevailing humanitarian conditions. As of end of 2020, DTM covered 2,854 locations in 509 payams of all 78 counties across South Sudan (Round 9). Data collection is conducted through the existing network of focal points, including 88 county and state RRC focal points, more than 400 enumerators and 6,600 key informants across the country. DTM will leverage this network to monitor ongoing displacement events (Event Tracking), providing a more dynamic picture of displacement that better accounts for repeated and/or short-term displacement of communities and their impact on humanitarian conditions as a compounding shock. Flow Monitoring: DTM will continue to collect and analyze data at points of entry / exit to / from the country, internal mobility hubs, and major displacement sites. The data will be used to generate an indicative picture of key internal and international human mobility corridors and their evolution over time, enabling partners to understand mobility dynamics and their relationship with humanitarian conditions, as well as supporting border health actors in the response to the COVID-19 pandemic. As part of IOM’s global Mobility Restrictions Monitoring initiative, DTM will also continue to carry out remote assessments of COVID-19 restrictions and prevention measures at points of entry and mobility hubs. Multi-sector needs and vulnerabilities surveys will continue to be carried out at the household level to provide representative profiles of humanitarian conditions among urban population and displacement sites, monitor household-level reintegration dynamics and assess the impact of internal and external shocks including the COVID-19 pandemic. This will continue to be carried out in collaboration with WFP and humanitarian clusters, within the inter-agency MSNA/FSNMS+ for South Sudan, complementing rural FSNMS data in the IPC process and enabling partners to design an effective and targeted response in urban and camp settings. In major IDP camps, intention and perception surveys will also be carried out. To enable representative sampling while reducing operational challenges and costs, DTM will develop updated enumeration areas based on analysis of high-resolution satellite imagery and field mapping of non-residential areas. IOM’s biometric registration system, active since 2014, with more than 960,000 beneficiaries active in its database at the end of 2020. DTM assists partners in targeting affected populations and ensuring fair and accountable distributions through beneficiary identification, authentication at food distribution and biometric maintenance activities. DTM will focus on continuing ongoing biometric verification and maintenance activities in current and former PoC sites and maintain basic capacity to conduct emergency new registrations of IDPs, returnees and other affected populations. The frequency and breadth of activities has been prioritized to focus on core humanitarian programming, but data on return dynamics, return intentions of IDPs, re-integration in urban areas, economic mobility and early warning in areas affected by localized violence through Event Tracking of displacement incidents will also inform transition, resilience and recovery programming, contributing to a nexus approach. Modularity of DTM methodologies provides flexibility to adjust activities in light of external shocks including the COVID-19 pandemic and increases in insecurity.

3 - Project indicators

DTM will monitor success of the project through annual satisfaction survey. More frequently, DTM will share the data with partners through Needs Analysis Working Group to inform direct prioritization of humanitarian response. Based on the discussion and updates shared by partners, the DTM team will be able to monitor if flagged needs were responded to by partners. Moreover, DTM will regularly disseminate information products in a timely manner with partners to ensure data and information reach targeted audience and informs response planning.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DTM staff and enumerators receive training on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), IOM’s GBV in Crisis Framework and Accountability to Affected Population principles to inform beneficiaries / respondents and share information about complaints mechanisms.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DTM engages community representatives of vulnerable groups when conducting assessments at the group level and disaggregates household / individual level data collection by sex, age and vulnerability criteria. Furthermore, DTM pro-actively engages in tailored data collection on selected vulnerable populations in collaboration with IOM and external subject-matter experts, for example on persons with disabilities in displacement sites in collaboration with Humanity and Inclusion. These data collection exercises involve specific training for enumerators to collect information on vulnerable individuals in a safe and dignified way. Interviews with key informants and direct observation are two main methods for data collection in DTM. Field teams composed of PSEA and protection trained enumerators engage with local communities to gather best estimates on the populations living on site at the time of the assessment, including their demographic breakdown, vulnerabilities (pregnant, lactating women, etc.) and needs. Information pertinent to the needs of the most vulnerable members is also captured through facilities, infrastructure mapping, which gathers information on the presence and functionality of infrastructure and quality, availability and accessibility of services at boma level (distances from boreholes, access to education, medical facilities, availability of medical services, etc.). The broad and granular reach of these assessments ensures that vulnerable displaced, returnee and other affected populations – including those living in the more remote areas of the country – are given a voice in the humanitarian response. Multi-sector household survey data includes gender and age information about the head of household, indicators about the presence of individuals with vulnerabilities within the household and a demographic breakdown of household members, as well as specific indicators of need targeted to vulnerable members of the household.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

By and large, the beneficiaries of the project are other humanitarian organizations, including UN agencies, international and national NGOs and government actors such as the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management (MHADM). With the aim of facilitating access to the data by all humanitarian partners regardless of their size and location within South Sudan, DTM publishes all non-sensitive data and analysis on its website as reports, maps and datasets, which are also circulated through a public mailing list. Tailored analysis is presented and disseminated in bilateral engagements with partners and humanitarian clusters and through active participation in coordination bodies such as the Needs Analysis, Information Management, FSNMS+/MSNA and Population Movement Working Groups and the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group. DTM is also an active participant in local humanitarian coordination bodies in Malakal, Bentiu and Wau, where it has static presence, and liaises with local partners across the country during regular staff deployments. Regular engagements also take place with national authorities through the RRC, MHADM and the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), and with local authorities at state and county level. In particular, data collection operations are always coordinated with the local RRC/ROSS secretaries to ensure appropriate communication with local authorities and communities. DTM is also involved in the public health response to COVID-19, actively presenting relevant analysis to the Ministry of Health and other response partners through the National Steering Committee for COVID-19, and with county health authorities near key points of entry. At the local level, DTM enumerators are trained to provide information on the project to key informants and community members during data collection. They are also responsible for escalating feedback and complaints from local communities to DTM field staff and the project management team in Juba. Beneficiaries provide feedback and complaints through the above interactions, as well as through a yearly user satisfaction survey disseminated through DTM’s mailing list. Biometric registration activities have embedded complain filing mechanisms, designed and maintained in collaboration with protection partners and CCCM (for activities that are taking place within displacement and protection of civilian (PoC) sites). IOM has an independent PSEA complaint mechanism, which permits reporting of SEA cases directly to IOM’s senior management in the mission or to a dedicated unit at headquarters.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

DTM regularly adjusts its data collection tools and information products to account for feedback from data users, including international, national and local humanitarian, development and government actors. It also promotes, and actively participates in, joint data collection and analysis initiatives that bring together multiple humanitarian actors, actively seeking thematic advice from humanitarian clusters, technical agencies and other data users. BMR modalities are similarly reviewed on a regular basis, in consultation with assistance providers, CCCM and protection partners.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

DTM does not directly provide humanitarian services to South Sudanese populations in need, with the exception of biometric data management. While the risk of safety incidents affecting beneficiaries during biometric registration, authentication or verification cannot be excluded, exercises are preceded by a safety assessment to mitigate the risk. The activities are undertaken in coordination with other humanitarian partners, community structures and where relevant local authorities / UNMISS to ensure appropriate risk mitigation and protection measures are in place according to the context of each activity. Vulnerable individuals enjoy facilitated access to biometric services in collaboration with protection partners, minimizing their exposure to risk. Lack of access / usage of complaints and feedback mechanisms by beneficiaries of biometric identity services, respondents and key informants participating in DTM assessments is rated as a medium risk due to the remote locations in which some DTM activities take place, which limit the ability of respondents and communities to access feedback and complaints mechanisms. Moreover, these beneficiaries may lack knowledge of the complaints and feedback mechanisms in place. DTM attempts to mitigate this risk by mainstreaming basic protection training to its staff and enumerators, stressing the importance of informing communities about the existence of feedback and complaints mechanisms. By embedding its data collection network within local communities and closely cooperating with local authorities (RRC / ROSS) and humanitarian partners, DTM ensures that feedback and complaints can be voiced both internally and through independent actors. DTM pro-actively involves members of marginalized communities and vulnerable individuals when conducting registration activities and other assessments. To achieve this, DTM provides data collectors with appropriate protection training and strives to improve the diversity of its staff and enumerators. The risk of vulnerable groups or individuals being left out is low and the impact would mean either a lack of registration or not being represented in the evidence base for various programming, rather than actual denial of assistance. In both cases, the impact would be moderated by the fact that data users triangulate DTM data with independent primary and secondary sources. Personal data gathered from biometric registration and household surveys is stored and handled according to IOM’s data protection principles. Mitigation measures include secure data storage infrastructure and software, limitations in data access rights within the team to sensitive and personal data, and risk analysis before the inclusion of sensitive indicators in datasets that qualify as containing personally-identifiable information. Risk of interference from external stakeholders is possible as the ongoing conflict provides incentives for local and national political actors and power brokers to manipulate humanitarian aid for personal and group gain, including by influencing the informational basis for the response. DTM mitigates this risk through regular remote and field monitoring of data collection exercises by IOM national and international staff, rotating national staff to different areas of the country to ensure cross-monitoring and avoid the establishment of excessive links with local authorities. By operating across conflict lines and upholding humanitarian principles of impartiality and independence, IOM minimizes interference by conflict actors in its humanitarian activities, including data collection. Data is validated against secondary sources and contextual information to flag and address possible anomalies before publication, and DTM works closely with IOM’s protection and conflict analysis teams to ensure that published data and analysis are appropriately contextualized.
