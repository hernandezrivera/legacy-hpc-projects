Project Summary

The high influx of South Sudanese refugees in States bordering South Sudan including
West Kordofan, South Kordofana and White Nile States has overwhelmed available basic services and livelihood opportunities in host communities on top of the already huge number of IDPs and returnees who exist in these communities. The situation has caused competition among different groups over access to basic services and livelihood opportunities with triggers for conflicts if unabated. The situation is worsen by the ongoing economic crisis which has skyrocketed prices of basic food stuffs and other essentials beyond the affordability of vulnerable households especially refugees. In an effort to make ends meet, the affected groups have resorted to ravaging forest and environmental resources such as the collection and sale of firewood, wild fruits, use of burnt bricks for shelter construction, etc. as part of their copping mechanism with detrimental effect on the environment. The situation has triggered humanitarian need especially with fading absorption capacity of host communities. Unless something was done to abate the situation, it will reach a point where refugees and communities would no longer cope with the situation. The proposed intervention will focus on saving life of refugees and other at risk people affected by shocks in the aftermath of the situation, while seeking to support a smooth and gradual transition from humanitarian aid to self-reliance. Specific objectives are:
• To improve refugees’ access to basic needs (food, clothes, medicine, shelter, etc.) in non-camp setting through lifesaving emergency agriculture, temporal jobs and other alternative livelihoods.
• To promote community resilience and enhance refugees’ access to lifesaving services through the provision of basic services and socioeconomic assets depleted as a result of conflict or overuse.
• To provide support for refugees alongside host communities to address urgent issues affecting refugee welfare while promoting co-existence between refugees and different host community groups.
Intervention will include support to lifesaving through emergency agriculture and temporary jobs creation for refugees and vulnerable host community members. Secondly, the project will seek to restore and or deliver social services, socioeconomic assets depleted as a result of overuse due to large influx of refugees into communities. Furthermore, in an effort to mitigate the impact of the humanitarian and economic crises on the social dynamics of the target communities, UNDP will intervene through social cohesion activities that will reduce competition and restore trust and confidence among refugees and other groups.
Lessons from previous interventions informed that targeting individual refugees without the communities has limited impact and has potential to create conflict between refugees and host communities. Proposed intervention will be inclusive and community-based, ensuring direct support to refuges while enhancing the capacities of communities to sustain recovery and stability. Hence 75% of assistant will go to refugees and 25% to host communities. This is a multi-year intervention that seeks for $21,889,000 to support 72,960 individuals (12,160 households) in all 3 states.
Interventions will strengthen and add value to ongoing support by UNHCR and UNDP in targeted locations, thus creating overall impact of assistance while paving way for humanitarian-development-peace nexus. Interventions will be area-based and multi-sectoral in approach.
Environmental safeguards will be incorporated in all aspects of the proposed interventions to reduce vulnerability to climate change-induced risks. Interventions will seek to promote improved natural resources management, efficient energy utilization and environmental protection through training on NRM and community environmental action plan (CEAP) development and implementation. The use of improved fuel efficient stove will be promoted.

Needs

West Kordofan, South Kordofan and White Nile States are among those Border States heavily hit by the huge influx of South Sudanese refugees due to violent conflict in South Sudan which started in December 2013. According to UNHCR, a total of 846,204 South Sudanese have so far entered Sudan of 15 January 2019. Out of this number, 351,135 are hosted in the above-mentioned three states and more are expected arrive in addition to the already high number of IDPs and returnees who exist in the said states. IDPs also exist in the said states due to conflict between Sudan People’s Liberation Movement-North (SPLM-N) while returnees exist following the cessation of Sudan and South Sudan. The large presence of these displaced populations in the affected states has outmatched the available basic services and economic opportunities thereby triggering a clear need for humanitarian assistance. The situation has also led to growing competition and tension between displaced populations and host communities over the available basic services, land resources, livelihoods and economic opportunities, thereby creating increasing pressure in host communities with the propensity to precipitate social instability except something is done to remedy the situation. In a joint analysis by UNDP and UNHCR in 2018, it was estimated that over 50,000 people are in need of short terms assistance including food, medicine, emergency jobs, seeds and tools, etc. The needs assessment also shows the clear lack of economic opportunities, high youth unemployment, diminished absorption capacity of host communities and high dependence on humanitarian aid. Despite no alarm over poor harvests in the area in the past one year, food insecurity remains an issues among displaced population due to rise in food price. This situation is compounded by the ongoing economic crisis including high rate of inflation, increase in prices of food and essential items, shortage of fuel – all of which have negatively affected refugees thus presenting a huge risk and severe consequences to the livelihoods of the already-vulnerable populations who are net buyers of food and essential items. The situation requires urgent attention. The risk of no action will exacerbate the humanitarian situation which will force refugees and vulnerable households to resort to negative coping strategies. The proposed intervention will critically analyse the vulnerability of refugees and other vulnerable groups and design interventions tailored to the specific needs of each group with focus on lifesaving. Protection, gender, women empowerment and environmental safeguards will be incorporated in all aspect of project activity.

Activities

The project seeks to support South Sudanese refugees alongside vulnerable host communities with the following interventions: Support to lifesaving through emergency agriculture and temporary jobs creation – This includes provision of seeds, tools, fertilizers, agricultural trainings, extension services, etc. to enable staple food production for the short term needs of the affected population. Farming activities (such as vegetables production, backyard gardening, small ruminant production) with nutritional outcomes will also be considered. Any surplus food will be sold by refugees to meet other needs like medicines, clothes, shelter, school fees of children. Support will also include emergency job creation in agricultural fields. The intervention will build the capacity of local communities for improved preparedness in handling new emergencies and to cope with future shocks and stresses through tailored trainings. Support to the restoration of basic services and socioeconomic assets – This includes to restore/deliver social services and socioeconomic assets depleted/destroyed as a result of overuse due to the large influx of displaced populations. Project will expand existing water points into a more reliable water distribution yards to cater for additional consumption attributed to the presence of refugees. Rehabilitation of community schools and health centres will also be given due consideration. Improved co-existence between refugees and other groups in the communities – Activities will include the training of refuges alongside community members on how to identify and address issues affecting refugee welfare. It will also include organizing sporting and other joint social activities between refugees and host communities aimed at increasing community acceptance of refugees. Furthermore, sensitization on gender, SGBV and women reproductive health will also be carried out.

Impact on environment/natural resources

During needs assessment carried out by UNDP in the proposed areas in 2018, it was discovered that ravaging forest and other natural resources was among the coping mechanisms of displaced populations and vulnerable members of host communities. Actions such as firewood collection, use of burnt bricks for housing, collection of wild fruits, uncontrolled burning of farm sites, improper disposal of refuse, to name but a few ware among the environmental hazards practiced in the targeted communities. Hence the proposed intervention will incorporate environmental safeguards in all aspects of the proposed interventions to reduce vulnerability to climate change-induced risks. Interventions will seek to promote improved natural resources management, efficient energy utilization and environmental protection through training on NRM and community environmental action plan (CEAP) development and implementation. Proper refuse disposal will be promoted among beneficiaries. The use of improved fuel efficient stove will be promoted especially among women who are frequent users of natural and forest resources. Soil and water conservation techniques such as minimum tillage, controlled burning of farm sites, mulching, the use of organic manure for backyard gardening, etc. will be encouraged.
