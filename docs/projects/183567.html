Project Summary

UNMAS aligns with its Country Programme Strategy in contributing to two outcomes 1.The United Nations, and its humanitarian partners in Gaza, maintain critical functions, uninhibited by explosive hazards. UNMAS is the only provider of specialized explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) capacity that is permitted to operate in Gaza by the Gazan and Israeli authorities. UNMAS continued presence has been requested by the Palestinian and Israeli authorities, the UN RC/HC, OCHA, UNRWA and UNDP. UNMAS continues to provide ERW mitigation measures to UN and humanitarian partner sites to ensure that vulnerable communities are protected and prepared against the threat of explosive hazards from past, present and future escalations in conflict. UNMAS maintains an emergency EOD presence in Gaza to remove and destroy ERW posing a threat to Gaza communities. This allows UNMAS to be ready for a rapid response during potential conflict and to maintain an Emergency Response Preparedness Plan (ERPP) to ensure access for the Humanitarian response in any future Gaza conflict.
Outcome 2: Conflict affected people know how to mitigate the risk of explosive ordnance. An individual’s risk of exposure varies on factors that include where they live and work and the information they have at hand. ERW risk awareness is critical to mitigating the threat posed to civilians. UNMAS has identified two types of educational activities that are required in the specific context of Gaza. After several years of community engagement with beneficiary stakeholders and local partners they have developed targeted curricula which consider the needs of different gender and age groups as well as persons with disabilities: (i) risk education provides information for civilians to protect themselves from existing ERW threats, (ii) Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) is a novel form of risk education providing resilience training to prepare for future escalations, and is critical to avoid civilian casualties. To address COVID-19 restrictions, remote delivery methodologies have been developed as well as dual messaging addressing ERW and pandemic mitigation. Informal street sessions with social distancing measures have also been utilised.In the West Bank, UNMAS works with the National Mine Action Authority, Palestinian Mine Action Centre (PMAC), to enhance their capacity to provide risk education to vulnerable communities in the West Bank and to address the needs of victims of ERW accidents. There are 16 minefields and over 46 identified areas with recorded ERW contamination that pose a risk to shepherds, farmers, women and children. Lack of resources has impacted PMAC ability to coordinate EORE in the West Bank and provide age, gender and disability appropriate risk education where needed. Victim Assistance (VA), is the pillar of mine action that meets the short, medium and long-term needs of women, girls, boys and men who have been injured by explosive ordnance and addresses affected families and communities. UNMAS works to support PMAC in developing their capacity in addressing this requirement. UNMAS has consulted, via a number of workshops, from 2019 to 2021, to develop a project that will assist PMAC to develop and implement an EORE strategy in the West Bank, that is inclusive of the varied needs of women, men and persons with disabilities; and training of trainers to women volunteers for EORE, with a special focus on children in the West Bank.
In this project, UNMAS will ensure for the partner to utilize a complaint channel, and announce the confidential contact lines established by UNMAS and SAWA, a free toll number, social media platforms and WhatsApp contact. The channels will be adapted to the main target of this project that are women, men and boys and girls. In the selection process of an implementing partner for delivery of risk education, the partner that has a complaint channel and meets PSEA requirements was selected.

1 - Needs

Existing ERW contamination poses a threat to communities in both Gaza and the West Bank. The current level of ERW contamination is impossible to determine; however, on average since 2017, there are still approximately 100 items of ERW cleared every month in Gaza and ERW accidents continue to occur. During the recent escalation of hostilities in Gaza in May 2021, the Israeli Forces (IF) launched an estimated 4,223 strikes via air, sea and land. Damage to basic infrastructure in Gaza is severely affecting the provision of electricity, piped water and essential services. A sizable proportion of the items dropped did not explode on impact, the estimated failure rate is 10%, meaning the conflict has left a dangerous explosive legacy with a large number of ERW remaining on the ground across Gaza. By mid 2021, the recorded number of ERW accidents in Gaza was 78, with over 230 casualties. A trend of decrease and then increase in accidents following military escalations can be seen, indicating an ongoing ERW threat to the local population. Men are more vulnerable to life loss and injuries by ERW due to their larger engagement in the public sphere, including participation in the provision of first response services, construction work and scrap metal collection, farming and fishing. More than 160 known and accessible Deep Buried Bombs (DBB) were cleared by UNMAS, the latest being in 2021 which posed a threat to the local population as one DBB was located under the populated neighborhood and another in the middle of populated market area. There remains at least 10 DBB cases that still pose a threat to the safety of communities in Gaza and will need to be cleared. While the exact number of cases will only be known when bombs are discovered, it is estimated that there are many items of uncleared unexploded ordnance, clearance of which would support vulnerable communities to become more resilient to the impact of ERWs. An individual’s risk of exposure varies on factors like where they live, work and the existing knowledge on how to protect and prepare themselves against ERW threat. UN/humanitarian staff, construction workers, and other at-risk populations including children, persons with disabilities, boys and men are particularly vulnerable. All populations at ERW risks require targeted knowledge to safely respond to ERW contamination. There is concern amongst mine action partners of an increase in desensitization to the risks of ERW amongst the Gaza population leading to accidents. There exists a gap in the capacity of the Gazan authorities to respond appropriately to ERW contamination, highlighting the need for intervention. According to the Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) that are conducted in 2021, only 19.3 per cent of households in Gaza reported the presence of members who have received information or training on the risks of ERW which indicates an urgent need to ensure that humanitarian actors and the general population receive risk education to reduce risk from ERW. Community engagement with female groups and local CSOs have allowed women to be particularly targeted in CPP sessions as they have been identified as, requiring skills to prepare and protect themselves and their families against future ERW contamination and conflict. UNMAS provides different types of EORE to mitigate the risk of explosive ordnance, including: coordinated community sessions, training for professionals including UN and humanitarian staff, street sessions for beneficiaries not otherwise reached, especially children and reconstruction sessions for construction workers on sites and are provided on request. UNMAS also delivers Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) resilience training to empower females to understand ERW risks, how to prepare homes for bombardment or evacuate persons with disabilities safely. Since 2019, UNMAS has reached 14,459 people (2,613 women, 1,687 men, 5,179 girls, 4,980 boys) via 623 EORE sessions and a further 317 CPP sessions have been delivered to 7,284 beneficiaries (5,678 women, 1,606 men). Since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, UNMAS has temporarily suspended regular classroom EORE activities and started specialised street sessions covering both ERW and COVID-19 safety messaging. To date, 38,163 beneficiaries (6,224 women, 19,206 men, 4,547 girls, 8,186 boys) have been reached via 10,778 street sessions. Remote methods of delivery are being developed to allow continuation of delivery in case of future movement restrictions. In the West Bank, the PMAC has mapped 46 areas that have recorded ERW contamination. These areas are either military training sites, which occupy 20% of the West Bank, or hotspot areas that see demonstrations on a regular basis. Community engagement through EORE sessions have identified that ERW contamination poses risk to farmers and shepherds while performing their livelihood activities and impedes their access to the land. It also impacts the lives of the Bedouin communities who often live in these areas, including women who are harmed while handling ERW to safeguard their children and community. To assess the extent of the impact of ERW in the West Bank, UNMAS is conducting a household survey of West Bank communities to enable PMAC to prioritize their EORE activities. Due to ERW contamination, the PMAC are providing vital EORE to the communities in villages and residential areas around the minefields and areas highly contaminated by ERW. Children are especially at risk in the communities, as ERW contamination exists around the schools in the areas.

2 - Activities

To implement its project and contribute to the two outcomes, UNMAS will undertake the following activities taking into account the COVID-19 restrictions in Gaza and the West Bank. Activity 1: Provide risk assessments at UN locations, Activity 2: Provide EOD response to UN locations, Activity 3: Excavation and destruction of DBB discovered at UN and partner locations is carried out by UNMAS, Activity 4: Conduct EORE training session for UN and partner personnel, Activity 5: Deliver EORE sessions to construction workers implementing development and reconstruction projects in Gaza, Activity 6: Deliver CPP training sessions in Gaza, Activity 7: Deliver workshops with PMAC and stakeholders to develop and adopt EORE Strategy, Activity 8: Provide capacity enhancement support to National Authority on provision of gender responsive Activity 9: EORE to vulnerable communities in the West Bank, Activity 10: Provide training to the EOD Police, Activity 11: Initiate a KAP survey in the West Bank to map ERW contamination and impact UNMAS has tailored much of its risk education messaging to include integrated EORE/COVID awareness in coordination with UNICEF and WHO to ensure the messaging is appropriate, relevant and up to date. All messaging is being converted into remote alternatives to ensure that remote learning, when needed, is available, accessible to all and social distancing guidelines can be followed by UNMAS staff and by community-based organization partners. After 2 years of feedback and surveys from the risk messaging and CPP sessions, the modules are now customised to the requirements of the participants, for example the information can be adapted to suit the varying needs of women, men and persons with disabilities. UNICEF is a member of the Mine Action Working Group for Gaza which enables UNMAS to consult with other mine action partners to incorporate child protection principles. UNMAS has specific risk education messaging tailored to children that uses comic book characters, risk education games, role playing, music and dance as well and positive reinforcement in a simpler and child friendly manner that have been developed by risk education experts globally. The implementing partner for this project, the Save Youth Future Society (SYFS), is a non-governmental organization that invests in development of Palestinian youth’s potential and strengthens their role in determining their life goals. Since March 2001, SYFS has implemented 156 projects under these thematic programs such as education and capacity development with a total value of more than USD 30 Million. In 2021, the SYFS is implementing 15 projects through strategic partnership with UNFPA, UNDP, UNICEF, EU, GIZ, NPA and Catholic Relief Services. SYFS has accumulated valuable experience also in risk education and other awareness raising activities at the community level, particularly that the organization has successfully implemented similar projects with partnership with Norwegian People's Aid (NPA) aiming to enhance and empower the crises-resilience to mitigate the risks associated with explosive weapons among the individuals with more focus on households.In order to ensure participation of the community, this project will conduct focus group discussion with men, women, children, people living in rural areas, persons with disabilities, during the planning, implementation and evaluation phases of the project, to identify the needs and the best methodologies and communication channels to effectively deliver EORE and CPP messages. Based on the results of the surveys, UNMAS will utilize context-appropriate communication channels to enhance community participation, particularly among women, persons with disabilities and other marginalized populations, and develop user-friendly communication materials and platforms grounded in the context of communities, that could include leaflets, social media and radio programs that are simplified with diagrams and translated into local languages. For the selection of the participants with disabilities for focus group discussions, UNMAS will consult with the local organizations that support persons with disabilities. Gender analysis will be conducted in this project, separately or as part of existing needs analyses of target populations. Systematic use of gender analysis, sex-disaggregation of data, and commissioning of surveys is planned to be conducted in this project. Complaints received through planning and implementation of this project will be handled based on the complaints handling workflow prescribed in the UNMAS guideline on complaint mechanism. The mechanism will be executed through development of a misconduct briefing for beneficiaries, distribution of awareness cards in Arabic, and providing PSEA training for staff and contractors. One Arabic speaking contact person will be allocated each for the project in the West Bank and Gaza. The complaint can be reported through a confidential contact line, and the reporting person will be protected from any repercussions for reporting and be able to report misconduct without giving her/his name. Throughout the implementation of this project, the project will ensure all the UNMAS and implementing partner staff to complete a disability awareness course. The materials developed through this project will use images/drawings that show persons with disabilities in empowering positions in all materials, in order to mainstream their participation and equality in all aspects of life and society. Data collection will be conducted disaggregates by gender, age and types of disability. This includes training of internal and external staff in data collection using the chosen tool. During the implementation of the project, UNMAS will consult with the Organisations of Persons with Disabilities that operates at the target locations to ensure that the developed materials are disability inclusive in the local context.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

One of the aims of the project is to deliver the service of ERW clearance in Gaza which is essential in restoring normality to Gaza, a place that is under constant threat of war. UNMAS ensures through pre- and post survey on clearance and risk education is carried out to effectively take the principle of DNH into consideration in relation to beneficiaries by improving accountability towards beneficiaries, building relations with beneficiaries, and understanding the contexts and community dynamics in which the projects are taking take place both in Gaza and in the West Bank. UNMAS consults and involves communities from the identification phase of a project through surveys, community consultations and workshops with staff, partners and other relevant actors. The project design has considered the safety and the dignity of beneficiaries as a first priority, given that the target group is very broad and covers men, women, boys and girls directly affected by ERW contamination. All staff involved in the project, including partner organizations’ staff, are or will be trained on protection and gender mainstreaming as well as on PSEAH to promote best practices and to ensure that no harm results from UNMAS intervention. UNMAS interventions have also all been planned in a gender sensitive manner, and collection of disaggregated data collection according to the protection principles is maintained. Child protection principles have been integrated in project planning, and UNMAS ensures that the different needs of children are being met in targeted risk education sessions. In terms of COVID-related prevention and no harm: UNMAS ensures that its offices are safe for the staff members to return to, and adhere to all minimum precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and good hygiene measures. All UNMAS activities have been adapted and implemented during the COVID-19 restrictions. Remote messaging to beneficiaries will be activated as soon as face to face is no longer advisable, shorter and more informal street sessions have been devised and regular need assessments will be conducted to identify any new emerging need.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through its activities UNMAS works to ensure that equal access is available to all those in need. In its focus on the technical side of ERW clearance, and the provision of risk education and CPP, UNMAS creates an atmosphere of safety that enables people to move around and rebuild the lives of people in need. UNMAS risk education is tailored to different groups, taking into consideration the needs of the groups. For example, CPP sessions are provided at times that stay at home mothers are available, and delivered in locations where childcare can be made available. Sessions tailored to children ensure that children who have other child care duties can attend with their siblings, and are directly after school or a time that is convenient. UNMAS also ensures that access for persons with disabilities is made available through its CBO at women and family centres. Throughout the project cycle, persons with disabilities will be mainstreamed in line with the IASC guidelines. UNMAS and its partners will ensure that persons with disabilities are able to access the UNMAS interventions on the same terms as other members of the population. During the preparation phase of the activities, the accessibility of persons with disabilities to the activities will be assessed and adjusted to promote the participation of persons with disabilities. UNMAS intervention indirectly touches on human rights in Gaza by enabling people to go about their normal lives, go to work and schools without the threat of explosions. In addition, age and gender disaggregation is systematically included in all reporting and data capture. UNMAS will continue monitoring data related to victims of ERW and other explosive hazards, disaggregated by gender/age/location, to ensure early identification of potential/actual new trends in terms of at-risk groups and victim profiles, and timely gender sensitive recommendations for preventive action. UNMAS will also continue to work with partners to engage men and boys particularly, who in Gaza are most at risk of being injured/killed by ERW. At the same time, UNMAS will ensure that women continue to be identified and encouraged to actively attend CPP sessions and ERW briefings in order to further reduce the low number of female casualties caused by ERW in Gaza and to better relay awareness messages amongst families. As mentioned above, PSEAH complaints received from beneficiaries will be handled based on the complaints handling workflow prescribed in the UNMAS guideline on complaint mechanism. UNMAS will ensure the complaint mechanism to be accessible for all beneficiaries through provision of a misconduct briefing for beneficiaries, distribution of awareness cards in Arabic, and providing PSEA training for staff and contractors. Beneficiaries are able to report misconduct anonymously without giving her/his name through a confidential contact line.
