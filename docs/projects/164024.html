Project Summary

The Health in Emergencies Assisting Lives (HEAL) Project will benefit 25,000 highly vulnerable individuals, including 7,000 men, 7,000 women, 5,500 boys and 5,500 girls through the restoration of access health services through the rehabilitation of five health centres in the governorates of Deir-ez-Zor, Aleppo, Homs and Rural Damascus.
The HEAL Project will target underserved, overburdened host communities with a high IDP population, urban and rural communities with no current access to local health facilities and areas with high numbers of vulnerable individuals including women, boys and girls, older adults, pregnant and lactating women in addition to areas with high morbidity rates and health risks. Where possible, ADRA will implement health centre rehabilitation as part of an integrated approach including WASH, livelihood, food security or education activities. The project emphasizes an integrated approach to consider MHPSS, GBV, BCD , referral etc.

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has had a devastating impact upon health care services across Syria due to largescale infrastructural damage, health staff shortages, and lack of availability of medical supplies leaving approximately 12 million people remain in urgent need of health services including 1.43 million children under 5 years old, 3.07 million individuals living with a disability and 3.22 million women between 15 – 49 years old (Draft HNO 2020). Conflict-related damage to health services has compromised equal access for men, women, boys and girls, particularly in rural areas or host communities with a high IDP population. It is estimated that only 64% of hospitals and 52% of primary health care centres are fully functional (Draft HNO 2020), causing many already vulnerable individuals to travel long distances in search of adequate health care facilities.In eastern rural Deir-ez-Zor, the lack of functional health services has caused increased pressure on local health services, particularly in Al Mayadin, which hosts many IDPs and returnees as well as providing basic health care for many surrounding villages with damaged facilities. Due to the presence of leishmania in the area and frequent reporting of UXO-related injuries, adequate health care services for highly vulnerable groups such as women, boys, girls, people with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women and the elderly, are critical to ensuring a protective and sanitary environment for the local population. Overburdened health services has also been identified in Aleppo city, Douma (Rural Damascus) and Rastan (Homs) which are all conflict-affected hosting communities with a high number of IDPs and returnees, and additional patients visiting from rural / surrounding communities seeking specialized services, or basic health care where essential services are not available in their home community. Many of these services are provided in health facilities already in need of rehabilitation.

5 - Implementation of activities

Following coordination with the Health Sector, SARC and local governorates, ADRA will identify high severity areas where there is a shortage of health centres and select centres which, upon rehabilitation, will become functional for the local community. ADRA will conduct community-based needs assessments, where possible, through household-level surveys and key informant interviews including feedback from men, women, boys, girls, older adults, people with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, and young adults. Based on beneficiary feedback, ADRA will ensure the local health needs are taken into consideration regarding meaningful access to health services and protection concerns. ADRA will aensure stakeholders are aware of the anonymous complaints mechanism to report any feedback .During the technical assessment , ADRA will coordinate with the Centre Manager ensuring that all community members have access to the centres, including people with disabilities, through installation of a disability ramp and ensure gender-segregated WASH facilities suitable for people with disabilities. Based on community consultation, ADRA will prepare the BoQs contract the necessary work. It is expected that the centers will require light rehabilitation, however, in areas where there is a critical need for medical services, ADRA will consider closed and more heavily damaged centers, provided they fit within the budget and there is sufficient capacity to open the centre post-rehabilitation. ADRA will also provide furniture and equipment essential to the functioning of the centres.Upon completion, ADRA will hand over the works to either SARC or the Ministry of Health who will be responsible for future maintenance works and operating costs. ADRA will monitor each phase of the project through its MEAL staff officers( in the needs assessment , the implementation process and post assessment survey), The technical officers monitor phase of the rehabilitation process with contractors.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

During the implementation of the project, the following protection risks may occur. The risk of inter-community tensions due to perception of discrimination, if a health center is chosen in one area over another. Community based / local inequality may arise contributing to feeling of exclusion. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. As a mitigating measure, ADRA will ensure comprehensive service mapping and communities, municipalities and other relevant authorities involvement in the selection process.The risk of discrimination of people with disabilities may occur. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. As a mitigation measure, ADRA will do the following. During the technical assessment stage and design of the intervention, ADRA will consult with the management of the health centers to ensure protection mainstreaming component is included. This will include, but not limited to, ensuing that the facilities are easily accessible and disability friendly. Attention to protection will be paid during rehabilitation of the centers to ensure proper marking of the building site to prevent access, especially for children, young adults, teenagers and elderly. The rehabilitated health facilities can be the first point of contact for Gender Based Violence (GBV), sexual exploitation and abuse medium. In addition, the risk can increase if the abuser finds out about the reported cases. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. As a mitigation measure, ADRA will ensure that the ADRA staff present at the center are aware of the risk and know the safe referral pathways in place in the area. The staff will also be trained on communication with persons (male, female, boys and girls) with diverse disabilities. Also, to provide privacy and eliminate the risk of sexual harassment, assaults and exploitation of the staff and patients, the rehabilitated health centers will be equipped with WASH facilities and rooms with lockable doors.

Qualified staff unable to be retained/recruited in the project area 5. Planned activities pose protection risks to staff, stakeholders or beneficiaries 6. Undue pressure placed on ADRA staff by stakeholders to prioritize specific locations oractivities outside of the selection criteria 7. Potential for corruption or fraud, especially with project procurement Mitigation Measures:1. Regular monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, stakeholders and staff. Temporarily pause activities when necessary and utilize staff from the target area for monitoring. If the situation does not improve, select a new target are for implementation.2. Closely monitor exchange rates, use open and competitive tender processes, adjust targets or search for additional funding to complete the works3. Apply for permissions in advance and conduct regular follow-up with the relevant ministries, including high-level meetings where necessary4. Recruit early in project life cycle5. Conduct protection risk analyses in target areas and plan activities in close coordination with community members to ensure support, or pause activities until the area is considered safe.6. Work closely with stakeholders and explain criteria and red lines in the early stages of target area selection. Where necessary, ADRA will coordinate with the sector leads and UN representatives or cease activities until support is granted7. Adhere strictly to the ADRA Syria Procurement and Finance Policies
