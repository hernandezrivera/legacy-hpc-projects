Project Summary

TCDI 2023 HPR project is designed to addressed the most pressing lifesaving WASH needs identified in the targeted locations. This project will contribute to the overall WASH objective and ensure populations affected by Northeast Humanitarian crisis have reduced risk of exposure to WASH related diseases through provision of access to lifesaving water services, sanitation and hygiene. TCDI will response to the severe cholera outbreak as a result of the recent floods.
The project has a contingency plan for the projected outbreak of flooding during the rainy season of 2023. These will be addressed through drainage clearing, construction of local drainages as well as rehabilitation of broken drainages. Cholera crisis will be addressed through the new cholera response approach, implementation of Case Area Targeted Intervention and as well ensure distribution of WASH supplies.

1 - Needs

According to the humanitarian situation report released in November 2022; Nigeria is experiencing the worst flooding the country has seen in a decade. More than 3.2 million people have been affected. At least 612 people have lost their lives and over 2,700 have been injured, as of 3 November according to the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA). The flooding has impacted 34 of Nigeria’s 36 states. Flooding has forced 1.4 million people to flee their homes, adding to the already high levels of displacement in the country. Over 300,000 homes have been destroyed or damaged, creating a shelter crisis for hundreds of thousands of people. A severe cholera outbreak has killed more than 465 people and affected over 18,000 others in 31 states in Nigeria since January. The north-east has been particularly hit hard, with 443 fatalities and 14,000 people affected, as of 3 November. Borno has 81 per cent of reported cases (11,000), followed by Yobe (2,400) and Adamawa (200). The recent flooding threatens to exacerbate the spread of cholera and other diseases including malaria and typhoid fever. Also, according to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview, shortages of clean water and poor sanitation and hygiene practices will have a negative impact on the wellbeing and living standards of an estimated 3.03 million people in 2022. This statement was proved by the recent WASH assessment conducted by REACH initiative published in April 2022, titled "ASSESSMENT OF HARD-TO-REACH AREAS: WATER, SANITATION AND HYGIENE (WASH) & HEALTH". According to the findings of this assessment, the proportion of assessed communities where the major source of drinking water was reported to be an unimproved water source (e.g., surface water, unprotected well): By percentage of assessed settlements, the following are the most often reported primary drinking water sources: 51% access water from an unprotected well, 18% from surface water, 8% from a borehole, 7% from a protected well, and just 1% from a tab. Borehole functionality was reported to be 38% in the access locations.

2 - Activities

1. Emergency trucking of chlorinated water before sustainable water supply sources are developed. 2. Provision of safe drinking water at community and/or household level, through new construction and rehabilitation of water supply systems including handpumps, boreholes rehabilitation or construction, water systems and networks rehabilitation. 3. Ensuring access to safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene through maintenance/repair and replacement of water system parts (including Handpumps, Solar Systems, Generator Systems, and Electric pumps), operation and maintenance of existing water systems, batch chlorination, bucket chlorination at the points of use, water treatment tablets and provision of fuel for water supply. 4. Provision of gender-segregated sanitation facilities and services (latrines, showers, handwashing stations) to protect the health and dignity of the affected population also supporting vector control campaigns through the development of solid waste and faecal sludge management systems. 5. Desludging of emergency and institutional latrines as well as related decommissioning of sanitation facilities. 6. Affected people are reached with community-informed hygiene messages promoting hygienic attitudes, behaviours and practices, including, household water treatment, proper handwashing with soap at critical times and Menstrual Hygiene Management. 7. Provision and prepositioning of WASH NFIs to the people in need (hygiene kits, replenishment hygiene kits, WASH cholera kits, WASH Nutrition kits, sanitation kits and MHM kits).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Beneficiaries are involved in all stages of the project from needs assessment to project evaluation. Before the start of the implementation of the project, TCDI will organize an inception meeting to inform all beneficiaries and key stakeholders about the purpose of the project, TCDI’s identity and mission, and the importance of raising feedback and complaints about TCDI’s work. To ensure the project complies with Do No Harm principles, TCDI continuously and proactively engages with local communities during community meetings and project activities. This is particularly important before the start of the project. All staff is trained in best accountability practices in the field. Beneficiaries are directly involved in hardware activities (e.g. construction & rehabilitation of water points, latrines, and bathing shelters) through decision-making regarding site selection. Hygiene promoters are recruited from within the local community. Those hygiene promoters will act as stepping stones for the community to provide additional insight, understanding, and feedback from the beneficiary community. A specific budget line is included for accountability costs, which includes materials and training for staff. Materials will be tailor-made to beneficiaries, meaning information will be translated into local languages and visual materials are used.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

TCDI actively commits to Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), and pro-actively informs and guides all TCDI staff on why and how to be accountable to beneficiaries. Beneficiary accountability is one of TCDI's quality standards and priorities across all humanitarian interventions. TCDI handles all complaints and feedback through its Complaints & Response Mechanism (CRM) system. Each project has a complaint/feedback focal point to coordinate complaints and feedback received. Beneficiaries have access to this mechanism verbally (phone, face-to-face) or non-verbally (email, SMS). Different channels are being used, but experience shows community committees are the most effective way to collect feedback and complaints from the community since most beneficiaries are illiterate or do not have a phone. All feedback is registered by the MEAL officer and reviewed by the Programme Manager and Country Director. He/she deals with all complaints and feedback according to TCDI Complaint Handling Procedures.
