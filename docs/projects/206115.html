Project Summary

The project aims to provide cost-efficient, safe, dignified and flood-resilient access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services to IDPs, host communities, and returnees living in extreme and catastrophic conditions in South Sudan.
For that, IOM’s interventions will focus on locations identified by the WASH Cluster as key priorities: intersectoral priority 1 counties (Rubkona), priority 2 counties (Malakal) and priority 4 counties (Wau, Abyei Region). Through its Static and Rapid Response Teams, IOM will extend its support to acute and protracted needs to Unity State and Upper Nile. IOM will remain the main water service provider in Wau’s Naivasha IDP Camp during 2024.
IOM’s HRP project contributes to the 5 WASH Cluster Strategic Objectives, focusing on integrating WASH programming with Nutrition, providing access to basic WASH services in PoC/Camps, whilst mitigating WASH GBV and reducing community’s exposure to environmental hazards. In total, IOM plans to reach 300,000 individuals (67,158 women, 64,305 men, 81,094 girls, 87,443 boys) by deploying a range of activity packages that combine hardware and software interventions, adapted to the context at play and to the needs of the most affected population. To support nutrition gains, IOM will rehabilitate/install WASH infrastructure within nutrition sites and/or at community level, while in locations where WASH services need to be rapidly restored due to sudden emergencies, IOM will rehabilitate/drill new boreholes, rehabilitate/set up/operate surface water treatment systems (SWATs) and affordable filters at household level. IOM will also ensure the sustainability of WASH infrastructure through the formation and training of community-based operation and maintenance groups. These hardware interventions will be complemented with hygiene promotion, as well as distribution and demonstration on usage of WASH NFIs and/or Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) to help communities sustain the learnt practices. All activities will contribute towards mitigating GBV risks and to improve accessibility to people with special needs, as demonstrated by IOM’s commitment to allocate 2.9 % of the project budget to GBV risk mitigation mainstreaming Where needed, activities will be adjusted to be climate resilient and/or to build the capacity of communities to respond quicker to natural disasters. Where possible, IOM will also prioritise market-based programming approaches, using local markets to support WASH responses. As the largest direct WASH service provider in camp settings, IOM will also continue servicing conflict-affected populations in Malakal Protection of Civilians (PoCs), Bentiu and Wau’s Naivasha Internal Displacement Camp (IDP), and Bentiu’s IDP sites (A, B and C). IOM will provide WASH services through the operation and maintenance of safe and accessible water supply systems, care and maintenance of accessible and secure sanitation and handwashing facilities, as well as management of solid and liquid waste. IOM will also continue investing in cost-effective and climate-resilient means of ensuring adequate service provision within these settings by setting up, maintaining, and scaling up water supply solar systems. IOM will support the transition process at these sites working to transfer WASH service provision towards community-based ownership where possible, through a participatory approach, with a focus on women’s empowerment. Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), and COVID-19 prevention measures will also be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle.

1. Needs

South Sudan's remains a protracted crisis context, affected by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and public health challenges, including impact of climate change resulting in extraordinary flooding. Big part of South Sudan's population (8.5 million people) will need humanitarian assistance in 2024 (an only 1.6 million are IDPs) – 5.5 million of which will have inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) conditions are predicted to be the most severe across Upper Nile, Unity, Abyei Region and Ghazal states – all areas where IOM plans to intervene through HRP. WASH conditions will continue to be dire due to results in 2023. Lack of latrines and/or overfull latrines lead to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases. Approximately 40 per cent of the total population have access to safe water while 10 per cent have access to improved sanitation. Some 60 per cent of the population practice open defecation. The impact of floods, linked to the effects of climate change, and high levels of food insecurity has increased water and sanitation needs. The WASH Cluster further predicts that the combination between the poor access to WASH services describe above, combined with elevated levels of food insecurity, will continue to have a detrimental impact on children's health, leading to malnutrition and stunting. – all states where Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold. PoCs camps and IDP sites will continue to be at risk of disease outbreaks, climate change side effects and conflict. For example, HEV is likely to remain in crisis in Wau, with an ever-increased risk of the virus spreading to host communities/other sites, considering population movement. Several exogenous drivers, such as conflict, climate change, outbreaks, funding gaps, as well as chronic lack of investment in WASH infrastructure and service provision, will also continue to hamper access to WASH services; which, combined with the high number of people in need, will only add more pressure to already scarce WASH infrastructure. Due to ongoing flood levels in 2023 it is also likely that there will be an increase in waterborne diseases and added WASH needs in the most affected/at-risk states. The country is still prone to ethnic and politically motivated conflict coupled with localized violence and disputes over land and cattle, limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH infrastructures.

2. Project activities

IOM’s activity package per each WASH Cluster SO: SO 1:• Rehabilitation/Drilling of water sources at nutrition sites and/or at the community level. In flood-affected areas, borehole chlorination and/or bucket chlorination at water collection points, and/or household water treatment and storage supplies to cover at least for two months.· Construction of sanitation facilities in nutrition sites.· Capacity building of WASH CHPs and Nutrition workers, and WASH NFI kit distribution to SAM caregivers and PWLMs, including demonstration on usage and hygiene promotion. SO 2.1: • Provision of water supply, hygiene promotion, sanitation as well as solid and liquid waste management services across PoC/Ex-PoC sites. • Continue rolling-out the Transition Plan across PoC/Ex-PoC sites, with the WASH Cluster and WASH partners, transferring WASH service delivery to IDP communities/mandated authorities, including the development of O&M plans based on cost recovery principles and pro-poor market-based programming elements, the professionalization of existing cadre of WASH volunteers and integration into new community-based WASH groups. • At new IDP sites/affected population at host communities, promote community engagement in sanitation, by constructing of emergencys and durables latrines at institutional level and household level.• Rehabilitation and/or drilling of boreholes, installation and operations of SWATs, hygiene promotion, distribution of WASH NFI and/or MHM kits combined with training and demonstrations on usage of distributed items.SO 2.2:• Triangulate conflict sensitivity and protection considerations with IOM-led WASH safety audits, gender and barrier analysis, to: i) investigate community norms and practices related to WASH that may increase the risk of GBV affecting women, girls, boys, men, people with special needs and the elderly, when accessing WASH activity venues, WASH infrastructure and distribution sites; ii) analyze physical safety of and access to WASH facilities/services to identify associated risks of GBV; and iii) assess the level of participation and leadership of women, adolescent girls and other at-risk groups in WASH. • Develop safety action plans with communities to ensure safety, privacy and dignity when accessing WASH facilities.• Promoting women's participation and leadership through skills-building opportunities in WASH.• Ensure WASH community outreach materials include basic information about GBV risk reduction and that GBV messages are incorporated into hygiene promotion and other WASH related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility.• Training of WASH staff and community volunteers on PSEA and GBV risk mitigation, including GBV referral pathways, so they can provide survivors with information on where they can obtain support.• Training and distribution of Menstrual Hygiene Management kits to conflict and flood affected women and girls under menstrual age. SO 3:· Construction/Rehabilitation of WASH flood-resilient infrastructures while maintaining access to PSNs (water points: elevated platform, protection of the sanitary seal; Latrine: pit lining, increase the lining above ground level to avoid rainwater filtration; PSN: ramps).· Capacity building of local authorities, community-based organizations, pump mechanics, and IDPs Camp management Committees on WASH infrastructures' operation and management, WASH-related Disaster Risk Reduction activities, including WASH preparedness and response plans for potential shocks (diseases outbreak, floods, displacement).
