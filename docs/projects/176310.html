Project Summary

Under the Humanitarian Response Plan 2021, IOM will sustain its strong support to the WASH Cluster's capacity to respond to South Sudan's humanitarian needs by managing a portion of the WASH core pipeline. To provide life-saving assistance to the most vulnerable people affected by multiple humanitarian crises, IOM will continue its effective WASH Core Pipeline management by providing emergency supplies that will improve access to clean water and adequate sanitation and hygiene materials, targeting 1,215,000 people in need. The project activities are in line with the WASH Cluster objectives and will serve to support timely, safe, dignified, and equitable access to WASH services to contribute to the prevention of famine, malnutrition, and disease outbreak, such as COVID-19, cholera, and dysentery, and will prioritize the most vulnerable population affected and/or displaced by conflict; recurrent sub-national violence; heavy season rains and massive flooding, food insecurity and malnutrition prevalence related and GBV risks

1 - Needs

South Sudan's operating context remains complex, with a protracted humanitarian crisis, including intermittent sub-national, localized violence, inadequate infrastructure, and recurrent long and heavy seasonal rains. An estimated 5.6 million people in South Sudan are impacted by insufficient access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. The current COVID-19 pandemic further exacerbates this. According to the South Sudan HNO 2021, increased returnees from the protection of civilian (PoC) sites to their areas of origin and movement to urban areas will increase the demand on already limited water and sanitation infrastructure. Due to the communal nature of access to water sources, the inadequate WASH services impact all population groups, including IDPs, host communities, returnees, and other vulnerable groups. WASH was the fourth-most commonly named priority need for women in October 2020, as reported by 14 percent of assessed settlements across South Sudan. In contrast, WASH was the fifth most frequently identified form of aid needed by men (as reported by 8 percent of assessed settlements) and children (as reported in 5 percent of assessed settlements). Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continues to have a detrimental impact on children's health, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. The COVID-19 pandemic also continues to exert pressure on already scarce WASH resources in a country already facing multiple humanitarian needs. For the past two years, South Sudan has also experienced recurring long and heavy rainy seasons resulting in widespread flooding that affected most states. All these factors and unprecedented shocks have resulted in an increased demand for WASH emergency supplies and highlighted the criticality of preventing interruptions in the supply chain through a robust WASH pipeline. As one of the leading pipeline agencies in South Sudan, IOM manages a portion of the country's WASH core pipeline and supports the WASH Cluster in providing partner organizations with timely access to life-saving materials to meet their water, sanitation, and hygiene needs. Depending on the local context and severity of needs, the WASH Cluster and pipeline teams will maintain a context-specific approach to responses. The WASH supplies provided to partners' responses are determined by assessing the needs of target communities and prioritizing key life-saving items. The most vulnerable and underserved populations will be targeted, specifically, those displaced (including protracted and new displacements, inside and outside of PoCs), at risk of GBV, malnutrition, famine/food insecurity, and those affected by environmental disasters. IOM's extensive pipeline management experience has built its capacity and expertise to meet partner needs in a complex environment characterized by major logistical and accessibility challenges, including surges of insecurity and a lack of basic infrastructure. South Sudan has a poor road network with less than 100 kilometers of paved road. Most markets have collapsed due to the protracted conflict, and the affected population cannot access the most basic needs. About 70 percent of the country's road network becomes inaccessible during the rainy season, which has increasingly lengthened from July to December for the past two years. Airstrips within South Sudan are of poor quality, with less than 10 airstrips used continuously in the wet and dry seasons. During the dry season, insecurity along the main supply routes is a significant challenge restricting movement and increasing transportation costs. Transportation by road where/when possible remains the most cost-effective means of delivery. The preposition of supplies in key-hub locations across the country is important as part of WASH Cluster's crucial effort to ensure swift access to emergency relief items in a coordinated approach.

2 - Project activities

IOM core pipeline will provide critical WASH supplies to implementing partners so that the vulnerable and underserved have improved access to life-saving WASH relief items in the timeliest manner. The targeting will prioritize the most vulnerable groups specifically, those displaced (including protracted and new displacements) by conflict and natural disasters, at risk of GBV, disease outbreak, and/or those facing a nutritional crisis, famine, and food insecurity. Women and adolescent girls of menstrual age in school and/or community will receive crucial support, such as menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits, to cater to their specific sanitary needs with dignity. Vulnerable children affected by moderate and severe acute malnutrition (MAM/SAM) will be prioritized to receive the WASH NFI and hygiene kits designed for household usage. Critical WASH supplies, such as soap and household water treatment that will help to curb COVID-19 transmission, will be included as part of WASH NFI supplies to be distributed to beneficiaries. The following activities are proposed: - Procurement of emergency WASH supplies for the humanitarian response based on assessed needs, targeting 1,215,000 individuals- Continuous support to WASH Cluster coordination team to administer and process partners' approved request for frontline response and distribution- Coordination with the WASH cluster, partners, sub-national coordinators, Logistics cluster, and all other stakeholders relevant for pipeline operations- Setting up additional preposition location in Kajo-Keji, Central Equatoria- Transportation of procured WASH pipeline supplies to six IOM logistic hubs outside Juba (Kajo-Keji, Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek) for prepositioning of stocks- Appropriate storage and management of the WASH emergency supplies at IOM warehouses in Juba, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek, and Bentiu- Regular monitoring of WASH core pipeline supply delivery, supplies quality control, storage management, and supplies utilization- Management of the WASH Cluster pipeline request system and participation in WASH Cluster Core Pipeline technical working group to provide additional assistance and support to the Cluster- Conduct post-distribution monitoring of select WASH partner response to gather beneficiary feedback on the quality of the items distributed, assess the appropriateness and effectiveness of the response, as well as understand how cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed throughout the response- Undertake pipeline survey with partners for the efficiency, appropriateness, and coverage of the pipeline service

3 - Project indicators

IOM utilizes institutionalized internal structures to monitor activities at the output and activity level and allow for effective data collection, analysis, and reporting. Progress against deliverables will be monitored, challenges identified, and potential amendments recommended. 8.11. Mitigation Measures

- All WASH pipeline-approved requests from partners go through a rigorous review process by the WASH cluster and its respective subnational coordinators, through which assessment reports and response/project documents are required for approval. The review considers Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and gauges how partner organizations target beneficiaries with priority given to the population's most vulnerable segments. The WASH cluster also requires partners to undertake rapid need assessment (RNA) before a response and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) after a WASH response. There are recommended tools and templates to conduct these activities that categorically incorporate questions on inclusion, participation, and protection to measure the effectiveness of the response in addressing the needs of the affected population. Findings and recommendations are given to ensure that issues are raised and addressed for future response.- IOM has a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IOM staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IOM Contractors. Extensive training and workshop on protection against SEA has been rolled out, and all IOM Core Pipeline staff has undergone at least one training in the past year. IOM Core Pipeline also requires its WASH partners to sign a memorandum of understanding to promote accountability. This agreement includes clauses on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse that partners are also obligated to enforce strictly since they directly interact and deal with beneficiaries.- In any humanitarian emergency, the partner agencies conduct an inclusive consultation process with the community leaders and the representatives of all groups within the community to assess the affected population's needs. Planning of the response starts here wherein distribution centres and areas of assistance and services are discussed and agreed upon so that not only community leaders decide on important matters. Access and security of beneficiaries are the most important factors that are considered. Community members also take ownership of the process, and tasks such as off-loading, guarding during the distribution, enumeration, and such are given to qualified community members. During the actual response, security is also monitored by the response team. PDM surveys have questions directly related to the safety of beneficiaries during and after distribution, and results are taken into consideration so that the principle of "Do No Harm" is continuously ensured across all contexts of humanitarian response.
