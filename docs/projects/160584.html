Project Summary

Through this project, ADRA aims to improve access of population affected by the conflict in Eastern Ukraine to dignified shelter and basic needs.
This will be achieved through providing light , medium and heavy shelter repairs to private houses , multistory buildings and social institutions .Additionally , it will be achieved by providing winterization insulation to private houses and delivering solid fuel and NFI kits .
The project will be implemented in both GCA & NGCA Donetsk Oblast with main focus on settlements near the contact line (0-5 km), and will be targeting vulnerable groups with focus on elderly people, persons with disabilities or injury, persons with chronic illness, and women & girls.
ADRA will sign MoUs with all local authorities in the targeted locations. This will help to increase partnership responsibility and involve local authorities in identification of the most vulnerable households, create awareness among the local population, and cooperate in repair activities.
The locations will be identified in Donetsk Oblast in close coordination with the Shelter Cluster, NRC and other Shelter actors, focusing mainly on suburbs of urban settlements and remote areas which aren’t covered by other humanitarian actors.
For light & Medium repairs , ADRA will be focusing mainly on multistorey buildings in addition to private houses . Also , The Shelter team will conduct visits to the nominated houses and buildings to verify beneficiary eligibility, identify damage and execute relevant BoQs. Based on the executed BoQs, and through a public and transparent competitive process, ADRA will hire local workers and identify local SMEs to conduct the needed repairs. Community leaders will be involved to strengthen communication with the local population, whom will be informed and briefed by the Shelter about project goals and planned activities
The project proposal is prepared based on Humanitarian Response Plan 2019 (HRP). The project will contribute to the implementation of HRP Collective Outcome 2 & 3: (CO2) Provide emergency assistance and ensure non-discriminatory access to quality essential services for populations in need, (CO3) Affected people have consistent access to critical civilian structures. The project will also contribute to the Education Cluster Objective 1: Improve access to safe schools (education facilities) that provide quality education services to conﬂict-affected learners in a protective learning environment , and to the Shelter/NFI Cluster Objectives 1 &2 : Objective 1:Address essential shelter and NFI needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conﬂict affected population through monetized/in-kind assistance and contingency . Shelter/NFI Objective 2: Contribute to adequate transitional solutions (monetized or in-kind) related to shelter and NFI needs meeting minimal international and national shelter standards .

1 - Needs

Nearly five years of continuous conflict in eastern Ukraine has led to over 50,000 residential buildings incurring damage on both sides of the 'contact line'. Based on the Shelter Cluster’s damage database , some 5,300 vulnerable HHs in GCA alone continued to require humanitarian shelter interventions (HNO 2019). Significant gaps remain, especially in ‘hard to reach’ areas with high insecurity & limited physical access. Since the beginning of the conflict, only 12,453 buildings (22,968 HHs) have been repaired in NGCA, leaving still thousands of HHs in an extremely vulnerable situation. Winterization still remains the most acute concern & the main life-saving activity, especially when it comes to solid fuel distribution in the areas along the contact line. Rural families spend over 20 % of their limited income on heating, & their food consumption scores are nearly halved during winter. Bad road conditions make basic services even more difficult to access than any other time of the year. Higher utility costs force families to make cuts in other much-needed areas, such as medication, schooling or even food. Those HHs who are unable to afford the higher utility costs are forced to collect firewood, often in areas contaminated with landmines(HNO 2019).Around 30,000 vulnerable HHs in both GCA & NGCA are struggling to adequately prepare for the coming winter & were identified as a priority for receiving winterization assistance by Shelter/NFI Cluster partners. Including 5,531 of vulnerable HHs (or 13,385 people) in GCA 23,182 HHs (or 56,100 people) in NGCA. The overall number of people in need for winterization assistance in GCA for 2020 is 50,200 HHs ( 121,485 people), while on NGCA this figure is 105,903 HHs ( 256,285 people) (Shelter & NFI analysis rev. Shelter Cluster) The Shelter Cluster identifies heating & warm clothing as priorities in Donetsk NGCA. A total of 600,000 conflict-affected people need support in the procurement of non-food items. The REACH multisector analysis for NGCA confirms: Electricity/gas/water supply & heating account for a significant proportion of HH expenditure & make supply more difficult, while expenditure on coal for heating is equivalent to a salary of 3 months for many HHs. The FGD also showed that these remote communities have no physical or financial access to the market & therefore, even if they receive cash, will not be able to purchase the non-food items needed to meet their basic needs. Until proper market access is restored, the only solution is direct distribution to meet the immediate needs of families in some areas. The HNO 2019 shows that 180,000 vulnerable HHs in the NGCA, if not supported, will not be able to meet their winter heating needs (heating, gas/coal/electricity, warm clothing, etc.). The Shelter Cluster identifies Donetsk NGCA as "the region with the greatest need for fuel oil support".Around 700,000 students & teachers in more than 3500 educational institutions continue to suffer from the sustained personal, social & economic impact of the conflict . More than 400,000 children experience the direct impact of the conflict as they live,play and go to school within the20-km on both sides of the“contactline”,where shelling and extreme levels of mine-contamination threaten their lives and wellbeing.According to the Education Cluster, 750 educational institutions reported physical damage from the conflict since 2014 & 94 schools experienced attacks or disruptions from the conflict in 2017: The ongoing fighting has damaged homes, hospitals & schools. Since the start of 2019,the Education Cluster has received reports regarding 32 conflict-related incidents resulting in physical damages to school infrastructure, 2 education facilities that have been temporarily closed, 8 cases of military presence in close vicinity to the education facility and 9 incidents resulting in threat of death or injuries to students,teachers and parents .

2 - Activities & outcomes

• Result 1 : Improve access to safe schools (education facilities) that provide quality education services to conﬂict-affected Activity 1.1 : 10 social institutions(Schools/Kindergartens) in NGCA are supported with repairs and renovation works (around 2,000 beneficiaries) . Small scale rehabilitation of damaged education facilities and kindergartens, as well as procurement and distribution of required equipment, individual education and early childhood development kits will be done . The locations will be fully coordinated with other education in emergencies actors, especially UNICEF and the Education Cluster based in Donetsk NGCA . The set criteria will include: institutions already attended by children , and not located in the areas which are under continuous shelling or military operations in the last 1-2 years .The lists of schools will be provided by local de-facto authorities to local partners based on the needs and the vulnerability criteria set by ADRA . Detailed BoQs of the works and repairs needed will be prepared , according to which , a public and transparent procurement tender will be launched in GCA to identify a qualified Ukrainian contractor to carry on the works . • Result 2 : Providing adequate permanent shelter and wintering assistance for the most vulnerable conflict affected population  Activity 2.1 : Conducting light repairs to 1000 HHs (GCA) Activity 2.2 : Conducting medium scale repairs to 600 HHs (GCA) Activity 2.3 : Conducting heavy repairs to 50 HHs (25 GCA, 25 NGCA) Activity 2.4 : Conducting building insulation repairs to 100 HHs in GCA Donetsk Oblast • Result 3 : improve access of affected population to essential shelter and NFI needs . Activity 3.1 : Delivering acute emergency shelter kits ADRA will procure 100 emergency shelter kits(NGCA) as per the cluster’s recommendations (Tarpaulins, Transparent plastic sheet, Battens, Nails for battens, OSB, Lath, Nails for lath, Foam). Kits will be stored in ADRA’S and NGCA partner’s warehouses. According to the emerging situations, beneficiaries will be identified as per established criteria in consultation with local authorities, communities, and other Shelter actors.  Activity 3.2 : Distribution of solid fuel for winterization supportADRA and its implementing partner will distribute 2 tons of high quality coal per household as per the Shelter Cluster recommendations to 2000 HHS (1000 HHs in NGCA & 1000 HHs in GCA) will receive coal for winterization support , 2400 beneficiaries Activitiy 3.3 : Distribution of winterization NFI kits ADRA and its implementing partner will distribute NFI winterization kits to 1000 vulnerable households in Donetsk NGCA. The procurement will be conducted directly by ADRA through a public and transparent tender procedure , to identify a qualified supplier in GCA who will procure requested materials and deliver them to Donetsk NGCA , and the distribution will be made by the supplier and the local implementing partner. The implementing partner will support ADRA with lists of beneficiaries, receiving and processing registration documents and monitoring of activities. Risks to beneficiaries are minimized by ensuring distributions are taking place at beneficiaries' place of residence during daylight. The winterization kit will include a double-layered coat, thermal underwear, a scarf, a winter hat, a boot ,socks and blanket for each beneficiary
