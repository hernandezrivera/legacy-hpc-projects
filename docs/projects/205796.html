Project Summary

The project will increase the availability and accessibility of essential health, maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health (MNCAH) and GBV services to the host and displaced population affected by insecurity and floods in Bor South and Twic East counties in Jonglei. This will be achieved through the provision of holistic and gender-sensitive primary healthcare activities. The expected results are the provision of quality and holistic primary health care services, including sexual and reproductive health (SRH), MNCAH, GBV care, including mental health; Increased community awareness, especially among the most vulnerable population, on the right to mental health and SRH through coordination with local authorities (SmoH, CHD, RRC) and communities; provision of essential drugs and pharmaceutical supplies; improved quality of care through deployment of better trained essential personnel; rehabilitation of infrastructure; and improved accessibility through the deployment of mobile medical units in remote areas.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in SS remains dire, as a result of prolonged conflict, social and political instability, and climate-related shocks-such as recurrent severe flooding, which continue to be the main drivers of humanitarian needs(1). Civilians continue to bear the brunt of the violence with inter-communal violence reported to be highest in 5 states across the country, including Jonglei(2). Endemic conflict and recurring climatic shocks have resulted in massive displacements, limitation of people's access to critical (humanitarian) services and livelihoods, and disruption of humanitarian operations(3). In 2022, MdM, Tearfund and SMC conducted a Needs Assessment(4) covering 4 counties in Jonglei state namely, Bor, Twic East, Duk, and Uror. These 4 counties have been severely affected by floods, and experience endemic insecurity due to intercommunal clashes which have resulted in massive population displacements, the majority being women and children to hard-to-reach areas where access to essential health services is either limited or entirely unavailable. The findings of the assessment align with those presented in the 2023 ISNA study where the main barriers to access and utilization of care services were among others, long distance to HFs; low coverage of health services in general(non-functioning health units, lack of access to essential drugs or services for pregnant women and children or immunization services) especially nutrition, MHPSS/GBV; lack of referral systems; insecurity; Limited access to mental health services and inadequate qualified health personnel. The environmental diagnosis(5) conducted in 12/2022 in Bor also revealed a high risk of flooding in the area, which requires constant monitoring and the establishment of measures to ensure the protection and access of the population to HFs. Floods are the main hazard affecting Bor, as they often limit fishing activities, which is the major source of food for the population(6). The islands of Bor particularly, which are considered relatively safe from cattle-raiding, one of the main drivers of conflict in the area, continue to receive civilians despite there being major gaps in terms of access to essential health and MHPSS/GBV support services due to the lack of proximity to health services on the mainland. In Twic East, the centralization of HFs in the main town has made it difficult for those situated in more rural areas to access essential health services(7).Furthermore, since fighting erupted in Sudan in April 2023, SS has received over 380,000 arrivals(8) who continue to put pressure on already constrained resources, with humanitarian actors reporting huge gaps in basic service provision. Bor county is among the counties reported to have the highest number of people in a severe state of need in 2023 (pin: 116,391). With an estimated 11,000 returnees intending to settle in the host communities of Jonglei state, the health sector in Bor and Twic East urgently require reinforcement to meet the increasing health needs of the populations(10). Despite a significant drop in the number of people projected to need humanitarian assistance in 2024 compared to 2023, flooding and insecurity challenges remain unabated and, coupled with decreased funding resources for health services, these factors continue to pose a significant challenge of access to humanitarian assistance and available health services. 2. Project activities

In line with the National Health Sector Strategic Plan (2023-2027), National Health Policy for South Sudan (2016-2026), IASC MHPSS Guidelines in Emergency Settings, WHO Pyramid Framework and BPHNS for South Sudan, MdM will ensure implementation to address priority health needs and enhance provision of basic essential health services while ensuring protection mainstreaming and gender integration in Bor South and Twic East counties. The project will focus on strengthening the capacity of the CHD/MOH and local implementing partners to deliver quality comprehensive PHC services with curative consultations, SRMNCAH, MHPSS and GBV interventions. To achieve this, MDM will undertake the following key activities: i. Support deployment of essential health staff in 4 supported facilities i.e., Thon athiei PHCU, Ex POC MMU, two MMUs to the islands in Bor South and Twic East ii. Build the capacity of the health workers: provide technical guidance, support supervision and on-the-job training for health facility staff and community-facilitators, iii. Support strengthening of the referral system: the project will facilitate emergency referrals for critical cases to the next level of care through providing fuel, maintenance for the ambulance vehicle and deploying a fully equipped speed boat to facilitate transfer of critically ill patients to the next level of care iv. Provide integrated PHC consultations with MHPSS/GBV/SRH services: MdM will provide essential medicines, consumables, medical equipment and other supplies, for at least four target health facilities including one PHCU, and three MMUs and two women/youth centres and community mobilizers in the two counties v. Support health promotion and disease surveillance: By supporting the WHO/MOH EWARS and in conjunction with BHI, the project will improve real time detection, reporting and response to communicable and notifiable diseases through strengthening of community-based surveillance and early- warning system. COVID-19, Measles and other notifiable disease surveillance, risk communication and community engagement will be integrated with enhanced IPC protocols and screening, for early detection, monitoring and referral through the RRT for case investigation, management and contact tracing. Community facilitators at PHCUs will disseminate comprehensive and simplified package of SGBV/MHPSS and WASH, health prevention and promotion messages at household and community level with referrals for health and counselling services. The facilitators will also target vulnerable groups plus men and boys to be advocates and allies in the prevention of SGBV. Referral mechanisms from the community and within the health facilities will be strengthened with particular focus on reporting and referral of SGBV cases and acute severe life-threatening illnesses. At PHCU level, the intervention will focus on quality primary level assessment, diagnosis and care for common mild acute childhood illnesses using IMNCI approach and adult conditions, including trauma, treatment for non-communicable and communicable conditions, SRH, health education, routine immunization, screening for malnutrition and referral; at MMU level, outpatient care, diagnosis and treatment for common communicable and on communicable diseases, childhood and maternal services including EPI,IMNCI, ANC, nutrition screening and referral services integrated with MHPSS/SGBV care will be provided. Sexual Reproductive Health care will be enhanced through screening and referral support for CEMoNC and CMR.
