Project Summary


.The intervention will include the provision of essential WASH services by delivering IRS that aims to reduce disease transmission by targeting the sandfly vector, and protecting humans from bites, our NGO plans to implement IRS in the highest incidence sub districts in both Ar Raqqa, Dair Ez Zour and Al Hasakeh Governorate. The prevention activities will be supplemented by routine awareness campaigns before, during and after the IRS. IRS is the application of insecticide to the inside of dwellings, on walls and other surfaces that serve as a resting place for sandflies carrying the Leishmania protozoa. IRS kills sandflies when they come in contact with treated surfaces, preventing disease transmission. IRS is an equal opportunity activity, i.e. the benefits of the program are available to all regardless of age, gender, or disability status. For IRS to be effective, the following conditions must apply: · Majority of vectors (i.e., the sandfly) must feed and rest indoors · Vectors are susceptible to the insecticide in use · Houses have “sprayable” surfaces · A high proportion of the houses in target areas are sprayed (more than 85%) The IRS campaigns will be conducted door-to-door with blanket coverage of the targeted population. The IRS campaign will be conducted prior to or just inside the disease transmission season, which starts in March and lasts until October, with peak sand fly number soccurring in September of each year. As IRS lasts about six to seven months, depending on the wall timing ensures maximum benefit and protects beneficiaries throughout the entire high-transmission season. The residents also perceive the benefits of IRS through are duction of nuisance pests such as cockroaches, bed bugs, flies and mosquitos, which also transmit diseases, such as flies acting as mechanical vectors for cholera, which also presents an added health benefit. Our NGO proposes to use the WHO-approved insecticides ICON & FICAM for IRS. The campaign will also include animal houses and outbuildings, where sand flies and other vectors also thrive. In line with WHO’s latest IRS guidelines, a minimum coverage of 85% of all households in targeted areas will be achieved to ensure maximum protection. Effective awareness campaigns, the employment of local workers, the recruitment of female workers to facilitate access to homes, and printed IEC/BCC materials accompanied by verbal explanation during the house-to-house visits increase IRS acceptance.
The selection of target areas will be guided by the latest leishmaniasis incidence numbers. Success is measured through IRS evaluations surveys during and after the campaign, as well as data analysis and recommendations after the campaign has been completed.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In 2023, MENTOR diagnosed and treated a significant number of Leishmaniasis cases in Northwest Syria. Among these cases, 10,977 were CL. To address this public health challenge and not lose the progress in controlling the disease, our NGO plans to implement Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS) campaigns in high-incidence sub-districts within Ar Raqqa, Dier Ez Zour and Al Hasakeh Governorate. IRS involves the application of insecticides approved by WHO to indoor surfaces where sand flies rest. By targeting these resting sites, IRS aims to reduce the sand fly population and interrupt the transmission cycle of Leishmania parasites, ultimately reducing disease transmission. The effectiveness of IRS hinges on several factors. First, a majority of sand flies must feed and rest indoors, making them susceptible to the insecticide. Additionally, dwellings in target areas must have sprayable surfaces, and a high proportion (more than 85%) of houses should be sprayed to achieve maximum protection. Our NGO plans to conduct IRS campaigns door-to-door, ensuring blanket coverage of the targeted population. These campaigns will precede or coincide with the disease transmission season, which typically runs from March to October, with peak sand fly activity occurring in September. Importantly, IRS not only targets sand flies but also provides ancillary benefits. Residents perceive a reduction in nuisance pests such as cockroaches, bed bugs, flies, and mosquitoes, which can transmit other diseases like cholera. By addressing multiple health threats, the IRS contributes to overall community well-being. Local workers, including female recruits, facilitate access to homes and increase trust among community members. Ensuring the safety of beneficiaries and staff is paramount throughout Our NGO’s interventions. Prior to IRS campaigns, beneficiaries receive thorough briefings on safety measures, potential side effects of insecticides, and where to seek help if needed. Our NGO employs local staff irrespective of gender, ethnicity, or religious beliefs, fostering community trust and inclusivity. Furthermore, a robust Feedback Response Mechanism (FRM) is in place to address any concerns or grievances. Despite the benefits of IRS, several risks must be mitigated to ensure successful implementation. During the IRS and IEC campaign, Our NGO ensures staff are never alone with beneficiaries, prioritizing safety and trust. The FRM and Safeguarding Policy with a Code of Personal Conduct is applied for all personnel. Community engagement to address access restrictions, logistical challenges, and misconceptions about activities is key. Our NGO regularly updates security procedures, conducts daily assessments, and avoids placing staff or assets at risk. Security staff continuously analyze the situation in Syria in collaboration with the Country Director.
