Project Summary

Lack of sustainable access to clean Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) facilities remains a prominent issue within the humanitarian landscape in Somalia. Recent displacements, repeated disasters and a chronic lack of investment in durable WASH solutions have resulted in exposing the Somali communities to all categories of WASH-related public health threats. According to OCHA, 1.15 million people in Somalia are in need of emergency WASH assistance. Among these, the most vulnerable groups include the IDP communities (0.8M), with people exposed to high risks of disease outbreaks. Furthermore, lack of adequate sanitation facilities at the camps is a major leading factor for open defecation; exposing IDPs to contracting diseases and contributing to the increase in reported incidents of sexual harassment towards the IDPs - particularly women, children and PWDs. Poor hygiene and sanitation practices combined with the use of unsafe drinking water has resulted in Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD) outbreaks to the IDPs camps.
The project is a life-saving intervention that aims at improving access to clean drinking water and safe sanitation facilities for the drought and conflict affected populations in Banadir, Bay, Bakool and Gedo. Total of 10,800 individuals including women, girls, boys and men and people living with disabilities will have access to water and safe sanitation facilities.
The project includes that 10 shallow wells in Bay, Bakool and Gedo will be constructed for 5,000 people (Each well to cover 500 individuals) who are affected by drought and conflict. Similarly, further 20 shallow wells in the target locations will be rehabilitated to ensure access to safe and clean water for the affected population.
Additionally, the project will ensure the construction of 200 ventilated improved pit (VIP) latrines targeting 800 drought and conflict affected households (Around 4,800 individuals) within the targeted locations. Simultaneously, 125 VIP latrines will be rehabilitated so further 1,000 households (Around 6,000 individuals) of the affected population will have improved access to sanitation services.
The project will also ensure the construction of 10 toilets in selected 10 schools in the target areas where 450 students (300 boys and 150 girls) will have access to gender segregated toilets.
Also, the project will enhance the hygiene promotion of the target populations where 400 handwashing facilities (in the target IDP camps, selected schools and the target health facilities) will be established in Banadir, Bay, Bakool and Gedo. The establishment of these handwashing facilities will enhance the personal hygiene of the target population and the behavioral change of the people will be assured. Additionally, routine campaigns for the promotion of hygiene and sanitation will be conducted by HA using WASH cluster guilders and 150 community hygiene promoters will be trained.
Furthermore, 5 strategic waste management sites will be established to ensure the wellbeing of the environment and help to prevent the outbreak of the communicable disease such as cholera and other waterborne diseases.
Also, 1,800 sanitation tool kits which will be in line with WASH cluster guidelines will be provided to 10,800 people from 1,800 households to enable them use for the waste management and environmental sanitation. Each household will acquire 1 Wheelbarrow, 1 pick axe, 2 shovel/spade, 4 brooms, 4 rakes and rope & buckets. The community will also be mobilized on the recommended waste management procedures for the health their community and the environment at large.
In line with WASH cluster guidelines, the project will provide 1,800 hygiene kits for the displaced households. The kits will contain 2 jerry cans, 100 pieces of water purification tablet, 2,400 pieces of soap and 3 pieces of sanitary clothes for women and girls. The items will support the affected populations use the items for domestic use.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Human Appeal will work closely with other stakeholders and will ensure the vulnerable, minorities needy women; girls, men and boys are given priorities. Consultations will be held with community leaders, women groups in the focus group discussions (FGD) with women, girls, boys and men to explain the project to them and hear their views about it in relation to issues such as selection criteria and activities appropriate for all groups. Such consultations are expected to give traditionally marginalized and displaced groups, women and girls a sense of ownership and empowerment. The Do No Harm policy will be applied to target affected population. All interventions will be implemented so as not to worsen existing gender imbalances. Human Appeal will also mobilize the communities on their entitlements, right to receive all the money and why they should not pay tax. Human Appeal will also apply AAP to ensure accountability. Human Appeal is working in the area for long time and has skilled local staff with good knowledge of the area of implementation, Human Appeal enjoys good relation and reputation with the community which increases acceptance. Dialogue with local authorities and information sharing on project activities will be done. HA safety department will ensure that security and context assessments before the start of the project and advises the management accordingly. Human Appeal will also engage with conflict-affected communities and parties (national and international) in order to minimize disproportionate and indiscriminate targeting of civilians and civilian assets vital for survival, including through enhanced delivery of assistance and support to community based self-protection mechanisms.
