Project Summary

Save the Children proposes food security/livelihoods response in South Kordofan and Central Darfur’s most food insecure and conflict-affected localities. Vulnerable populations in these localities, particularly children and women are experiencing extreme difficulties in accessing sufficient nutritious food in order to ensure survival. To ease the food insecurity situation among the most vulnerable, cash transfers will be provided to women headed households, and cash grants will be provided to farmers and skilled women to restock seeds, tools and animals and start small businesses.

Needs

According to Sudan price bulletin for Oct 2018, prices are expected to decline slightly during the November to January 2019 peak of harvest but will remain well above last year and the five-year average. After January, staple food prices are then expected to increase through mid-2019. Overall, millet and sorghum prices are projected to be around 40% above last year and 90-150% above the 5-year average. Market update indicated that the sorghum prices significantly increased in all states compared to the same period last year (May 2017) with a sharp increase in SKS (91%). This significant increase in price of sorghum can be attributed to shortage in fuel supply which contribute to increased transportation cost. In addition to the depletion of stocks and approaching the mid of the lean season. The deteriorating macroeconomic conditions, including devaluation of the local currency, high inflation and cash unavialability continue to erode HHs purchasing power.WFP May 2018 food security monitoring report shows that most clusters in SKS demonstrated a level of food insecurity that was comparable to the level observed one year earlier. The most significant increase in the proportion of food insecure HHs was observed in Elliri, where the proportion of food insecure HHs increased from 13% to 62% for IDPs and from 21% to 54% for refugees between May 2017 and May 2018. Following a similar trend, it was observed that the prevalence of poor food consumption also increased in Elliri, from 2% to 33% for IDPs, and from 1% to 29% for refugees.SCI baseline survey conducted in Elliri in SKS indicated that only 7.5% of interviewed HHs have an acceptable food consumption score (FCS), 41.8% borderline while 50.7% poor. The report also revealed that the average coping strategy index (CSI) is 34.95 which is relatively high. This result of CSI alludes to the use of negative coping strategies by HHs on daily basis having long-term impact on their resilience.The macroeconomic crisis is hindering food access for many poor households, and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected between February and May in parts of Red Sea, Kassala, Darfur, North Kordofan, South Kordofan, Blue Nile, and among most IDPs in SPLM-N controlled areas of South Kordofan and Jebel Marra. Nearing the peak of the lean season, most poor households will rely heavily on markets to access food. However, many poor households will face difficulty meeting their food needs during this time given the likelihood of extremely high food prices. Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are likely in many areas. Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is expected among IDPs and poor households in SPLMN areas of South Kordofan and Blue Nile and among some IDPs in inaccessible areas of Jabal Mara.

Activities

1. Provide agricultural inputs and training to boost crop production under traditional rainfed cultivation. This includes provision of improved seeds of major food and cash crops grown in the area; it also includes provision of a set of basic hand tools as well as conduction of trainings and extension services for the farmers on the appropriate cultural practices in planting.2. Support women and women groups to establish Kitchen-garden. This introduction of rain-fed vegetable production, usually grown in the house yard, includes provision of vegetable seeds as well as training and extension package. This activity is targeting women who will be selected by the committees. SC will also procure the vegetable seeds from the specialized companies. Extension agents from the horticulture department of the state Ministry of agriculture will provide the training3. Provision of small ruminants to most vulnerable households. The project will also focus on income generation projects such as availing small ruminants for the vulnerable households who lost the stocks during the conflict. The most vulnerable households will be targeted who will be identified by the committees. Each household will be provided with 3 heads either goats or sheep depending on the circumstances. Animals vaccination will be provided4. Provide one-time conditional cash grants and capacity building trainings to (300 women x 7 = 2100) to (re)start income generating activities. The project is also proposing to address the low income among beneficiaries by introducing businesses relevant to each location. Intensive training session on business management, micro-finance and marketing will be delivered through experts from the targeted states and outside. The committee will also select and organize the beneficiaries (preference given to women). For each group start-up capital will be provided to run the business

Impact on environment/natural resources

A key tenet of Save the Children’s work is mainstreaming environmental considerations into all aspects of project design and implementation, as it is well recognized that environmental degradation can have major impacts on a country’s future development. The environmental impact of the project will be fully analyzed prior to the provision of cash grant for IGAs. SC is also committed in every sector of the project focused on construction or rehabilitation to use locally procured renewable resources. This not only lessens the impact on the environment, but also serves to make structures as contextually appropriate as possible. Tree planting will also be promoted as well as use of Fuel Efficient Stoves among participating women. FSL activities take the environment into consideration. To avoid clearing of vegetation cover when machines are used under mechanized crop production, this project will promote farmers using small hand tools. The irrigated vegetable production will be implemented in already open areas to avoid the need to clear more bushes and shrubs. The small ruminant restocking activity will not result in big increase in number of animals that adversely affects the environment, as generally small ruminants are less hazardous to the environment compared to cattle and camels.
