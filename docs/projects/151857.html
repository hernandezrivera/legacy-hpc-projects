Project Summary

Implemented through the e-vouchers modality, used by TGH in Ukraine since 2016, the project will provide fast and easy access to cover the essential hygiene needs of 5000 vulnerable households and 50 schools situated within a 5-kilometers area along the contact line for a period of three months.
The project will be implemented in partnership with small shops located all along the contact line and with whom TGH has partnership within similar projects. 65% of these shops are run by women. TGH intends to mainstream gender approach and keep this ratio.
By applying the e-vouchers approach TGH provides cost-effective innovative solutions allowing reaching out the most hard to reach areas and people with low mobility.
Indirect objectives of the project are enhancing capacity of shops, supporting local economy and strengthening solidarity mechanisms within and between communities.

Needs

Households identification conducted by TGH within similar ongoing projects showed that hygiene represented only a share of 11% of households’ expenses far behind food (42%), health (25%) and shelter (22%). With most expenses spent on food and other essential products, there is few disposable cash left for hygiene. Households along the contact line are thus deprived of access to essential hygiene. Low income gains along the contact line very often prevent household from accessing basic hygiene. According to TGH data, the average monthly income of households in the 0-15 km zone from contact line, is as low as 1,207 UAH per person, far below the official minimum subsistence level in Ukraine (3,300 UAH). Average monthly income of families with children under 5 years old and of women-headed households are even lower – respectively 957 UAH per person for the first group and 921 UAH per person for the second one. The low incomes levels explain the narrow share allocated to hygiene products. Such distribution of costs represents a sanitation risk especially for families with young children, with older persons or persons with special needs who cannot afford buying certain dedicated products which are extremely expensive (diapers for babies or for adults are one of the most expensive hygiene products and thus one of the less accessible for vulnerable households). Between August 2017 and August 2018 prices for hygiene items increased by 7% according to cash working group market analysis which makes it more difficult for vulnerable households to access expensive items such as diapers.The reduced share of expenses allocated to hygiene products also brings a negative dynamic at the local market as TGH was able to witness from its monitoring activities. Outside urban centers due to low demand of expensive items despite the high needs, shopkeepers tend to reduce their assortments of hygiene products thus creating deficit for certain essential products . As an example the market survey led by ACCESS, SCI, NRC 2018 shows that the least available items are adult and infant diapers, at 53% and 29% of locations. Of the 152 institutions and schools surveyed by TGH, 1/3 is not able to cover even half of their hygiene needs. Often parents are asked to contribute for basic hygiene products and items at schools and kindergartens. This poses a real threat to schools’ hygiene conditions and their capacity to welcome children in a safe and secured environment.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Access to hygiene products for vulnerable population in the buffer zone is ensured through distributions of e-vouchers.Activity 1. Identification of beneficiaries and schoolsTo ensure a transparent process and accountability towards vulnerable populations, identification of beneficiaries will be done in close contact with local authorities and actors with criteria and selection process clearly defined.TGH will select the 5,000 most vulnerable beneficiaries through a comprehensive questionnaire focusing on individual characteristics of persons (men/women, children/adults/elderly) and their vulnerabilities. This identification process will enable TGH to address hygiene needs on each household (HH) based on its composition and to cover needs created by each particular situation.Schools and kindergartens will be identified in close link with competent authorities. TGH has a database of 190 schools situated all along the contact line. The schools and kindergartens will be surveyed and their needs in hygiene will be assessed. The 50 most vulnerable of them will be targeted with this project. Synergy with rehabilitation works provided by other NGOs will be sought in order to provide a comprehensive and integrated WASH response. Activity 2. Activation of partner shops network and deployment of e-vouchers system.A network of partner shops will be activated across 11 rayons alongside the contact line. Refresher training sessions will be conducted for the shops on the use of the interface, roles as well as fixing of prices for the priority hygiene items.The-voucher modality employed by TGH will ensure adherence to the ‘do no harm’ approach and support local economy. Activity 3. Programing and distribution of e-vouchers.E-vouchers of different values will be provided within the project depending on HH vulnerability:- Small family kit – 502 UAH- Family kit –801 UAH- A kit for persons with special needs – 1390 UAH- Baby kits for children under 5 – 800 UAHThe e-vouchers will be provided with the aim to cover hygiene needs of vulnerable HH for three months. Beneficiaries and schools will be assigned to nearest shops. The innovative visual monitoring system TGH uses allows monitoring of the redeeming of vouchers in real time, allows to analyse consumption trends by beneficiaries and to inform decision-making. Each selected school will receive three kits of 2805 UAH each. Values of these kits are based on the WASH cluster recommendations. Activity 4. Diffusion of key hygiene promotion messages in targeted schools.In order to promote healthy hygiene behaviours among children near contact line TGH will be in close link with schools targeted by distributions. If some unhealthy behaviors are noticed and reported by teachers TGH will provide hygiene promotion literature to implement better practices among pupils. Outcome 2. Monitoring and accountability of the project are ensuredActivity 1.Implementation of a mechanism for feedbacks and complains.TGH will open a hotline for the affected population to closely monitor the project from the start and enhance TGH accountability towards beneficiaries and donors. Feedback and complaints mechanism will also be put in place and will allows fast and efficient response to feedback and complains and decision-making. Activity 2. Monitoring of project implementation, coordination with key actors.Baseline data collected during identification phase, will be used as a reference all along the project to measure progress. PDM will be ensured after the end of each distribution. TGH will closely monitor the use of vouchers in order to identify consumption patterns. PowerBi, an interactive data visualization tool, will be employed to monitor purchases in real time and ensure fast and informed decision-making.Coordination with relevant key actors at each stage of the project will prevent overlaps and ensure the most efficient coverage of beneficiaries needs along the contact line.
