Project Summary

Mozambique, a climate hotspot in sub-Saharan Africa, faces severe impacts from climate change, affecting large populations, particularly vulnerable and low-income communities, with over half being children. Mozambique’s high susceptibility to climate shocks is compounded by gaps in basic social services, poverty, and other deprivations. According to the Global Climate Risk Index, Mozambique ranked among the countries most affected by extreme weather events from 2000 to 2019 and was one of the top three most impacted in 2019 alone.
Cholera is endemic in Mozambique, with cases reported every year at varying scales and severity over the past five years. As of 14 August 2024, a cumulative total of 16,544 cases and 38 deaths (case-fatality rate (CFR) of 0.2 percent) have been reported in 8 out of 11 provinces in the latest outbreak, which started on 1 October 2023. The previous year’s outbreak recorded over 34,000 cases and 132 deaths, with a case fatality rate of 0.4 percent, affecting all 11 provinces. Most provinces and districts affected by cholera are flood-prone areas and have recently experienced heavy rainfall and cyclone activity, worsening the situation in already fragile and vulnerable communities.
With a high likelihood of cyclones, storms, and flooding in the upcoming rainy season, the risks to WASH infrastructure, population displacement, and service interruptions are significant, increasing the likelihood of WASH-related disease outbreaks such as cholera, acute watery diarrhea (AWD), mpox, and other infectious diseases.
UNICEF’s preparedness activities for 2025 will cover the entire country of Mozambique, specifically targeting areas identified by the Instituto Nacional de Gestão e Redução do Risco de Desastres (INGD) as high-risk for cyclones, floods, and public health emergencies. These preparedness measures aim to ensure the availability of critical WASH supplies, including hygiene kits with menstrual hygiene management items, for the most vulnerable families in these areas. To enable rapid deployment of supplies, UNICEF will pre-position emergency WASH items in its own warehouses (in Maputo, Beira, and Pemba) and collaborate with the Direcção Nacional de Abastecimento de Água e Saneamento (DNAAS) to ensure the WASH pipeline is adequately stocked in INGD warehouses across 11 provinces.
UNICEF has established contingency standby agreements with partners across the country, which can be activated quickly for rapid response. Additionally, UNICEF has long-term agreements (LTAs) with WASH goods and services suppliers, both within Mozambique and offshore, to facilitate rapid procurement and positioning of essential items. UNICEF’s regional warehouses in Pemba, Beira, and Maputo, along with INGD storage facilities in 10 provinces across the country, will enable efficient, countrywide support.
To further strengthen response capacity ahead of the 2025 rainy season, UNICEF will support capacity-building initiatives for WASH Cluster partners, especially local NGOs and government counterparts, focused on emergency preparedness, response, and coordination.
This project will reach an estimated 350,000 people across Mozambique, enhancing their resilience and access to critical WASH services in the face of severe natural disasters and public health emergencies.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This countrywide preparedness and response initiative aims to provide critical WASH-related supplies across Mozambique, targeting over 350,000 people, including men, women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Emphasis is placed on integrating protection measures and giving special consideration to the needs of women and girls. Preparedness activities will prioritize districts at high risk of cyclones, floods, and public health emergencies, such as cholera, ensuring timely access to essential WASH supplies, including hygiene kits, to support the most vulnerable populations. This immediate, life-saving response will help strengthen resilience and safeguard health and well-being during emergencies.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The priority activities for this intervention include: 1. Procurement and Prepositioning of essential WASH supplies, including hygiene kits, in areas prone to cyclones, floods, and cholera. 2. Distribution of Hygiene Kits and Dignity Items to vulnerable individuals, communities, and institutions (such as schools and healthcare facilities) affected by natural disasters or public health emergencies throughout Mozambique. 3. Post-Distribution Monitoring to assess the impact and effectiveness of distributed supplies. 4. Strengthening Coordination and Surveillance Mechanisms at both national and sub-national levels, ensuring alignment with other Cluster coordination structures to address critical inter-sectoral needs and manage supplies efficiently. 5. Mainstreaming Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) by integrating feedback mechanisms and ensuring transparency with affected communities. 6. Training and Capacity Building for WASH preparedness and response among partners and government stakeholders to enhance their effectiveness in emergencies.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNICEF places the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and the principle of "do no harm" at the core of its mandate. For all UNICEF personnel, SEA constitutes serious misconduct, subject to disciplinary action, including immediate dismissal. Organizations partnering with UNICEF are expected to implement robust SEA prevention, investigation, and corrective measures; failure to do so may result in termination of their partnership with UNICEF. To ensure adequate safeguards, UNICEF’s procedures include screening, informing, monitoring, and training of partners on SEA-related protocols. Aligned with the 2010 UN Human Rights Council recognition of WASH as a human right, UNICEF and its partners are committed to ensuring equitable access to safe WASH services for all. This includes providing gender-segregated, culturally appropriate communal latrines designed to meet menstrual hygiene, health, and accessibility needs. WASH facilities will also be well-lit and accessible to enhance safety for all users.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will set up help desks and suggestion boxes at distribution points to gather feedback from beneficiaries. Additionally, post-distribution monitoring will be conducted to assess the effectiveness and utilization of the supplies provided.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities encounter numerous challenges within community settings, including:• Unclear or unstructured distribution processes for WASH hygiene items• Physical inaccessibility of distribution points• Difficulty using the aid items provided• Limited accessibility of WASH facilities• Inappropriate communication methods or channelsThis project will ensure that distribution points are accessible to all, including people with disabilities, and will address these barriers to support inclusive access to WASH services and supplies.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNICEF’s commitment to children encompasses every aspect of their environment, including all factors that affect their survival and development. The organization is dedicated to preventing and minimizing negative environmental impacts in its supported programs and aims to enhance the immediate surroundings of children—such as their homes, schools, and communities—whenever possible and within its technical expertise. As a member of the UN System, UNICEF is committed to supporting the implementation of various action plans adopted during the major UN conferences of the 1990s. These action plans, particularly Agenda 21, highlight the importance of integrating social, economic, and environmental considerations at the policy, planning, and management levels.
