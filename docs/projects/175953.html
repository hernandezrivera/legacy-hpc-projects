Project Summary

This project contributes to attainment of cluster Objective 3: by facilitating conflict and gender sensitive access to safe housing, land and property for women, men, girls and boys, with sufficient security of tenure, access to basic services and livelihood.
These will be achieved through; (1) Raising awareness on HLP and the law,
(2) Provision of free legal aid including; legal information, counselling, referrals representation in the courts, observe and advice traditional courts on principle of natural justice, fair trials, women and child rights, and (3) strengthening and establishing innovative and sustainable community based alternative dispute resolution mechanism.
Due to the complex nature of HLP issues and limited information on HLP issues in the project locations, SSLS intends to implement these interventions in a period of at least twelve (12) months in order to empower communities to take proactive role in addressing HLP issues in a sustainable manner.
SSLS will closely coordinate with the protection partners and regularly share information about plans for implementation of the activities and progress report to avoid duplication of activities.

1 - Needs

It is over two years since the signing of the revitalized peace agreement however, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. According to the latest South Sudan Humanitarian needs assessment conducted by OCHA in 2020, South Sudan remained a protection crisis. About 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. Insecurity including prevalence of sexual and gender-based violence, lack of basic services, and unresolved housing, land and property issues continue to affect millions of people and undermine efforts for voluntary return in large numbers. Over 8 years of protracted conflict in South Sudan has weaken the justice mechanisms and rule of law institutions, despite capacity building efforts by the United Nations and other partners. There is a culture of impunity for crimes, including conflict related sexual and gender-based violence. Little to no progress has been made toward establishing transitional justice mechanisms. A National Framework for Return, Reintegration and Relocation of Displaced Persons: Achieving Durable Solutions in South Sudan was launched in 2019 and supporting Action Plan and Task Force in 2020. Despite this progress, housing, land and property claims often go unaddressed and disputes serve a potential recipe for conflict, new displacements and protection risks. According to South Sudan Humanitarians Needs Overview released by OCHA in January 2021, 1.5 M people are in need of Housing, Land and Property rights protection. Lainya, Juba and Wau are ranked at severity 4 and Yei ranked at severity 3 by the protection cluster means these locations are of high HLP needs. While Yei is ranked at 3 meaning a medium severity, Yei has been selected because SSLS has already established office in Yei and this will be used to reach Morobu which falls under severity 4. Displaced and returnee populations including women, girls, men and women in Yei, Lainya, Juba and Wau have limited knowledge of the legal framework on housing, land and property rights and the available dispute resolutions procedures and mechanisms and do not have access to legal aid. These undermine their ability to access justice for redress of HLP disputes. There is also lack alternative dispute resolution mechanisms to for addressing HLP claims outside court. Therefore, this project will raise awareness amongst host communities, IDPs and returnees including women, men, boys and girls, provide legal counselling and aid for host communities, IDPs and returnees including women, men, boys and girls for HLP cases and establish community based mechanisms for addressing HLP disputes in the selected locations.

2 - Project activities

The project activities will include; (1) provision of free legal aid targeting 2000 spontaneous returnees, IDPS host communities including women and girls in Wau, Juba Yei and Lainya through (1 ) Raising awareness on HLP rights, legal framework and available mechanisms for HLP disputes Resolution targeting 8000 spontaneous returnees, IDPS host communities including women and girls, men and boys in Wau, Juba Yei and Lainya, (2) establishing and strengthening 4 innovative and sustainable community based alternative dispute resolution mechanism and (3) Enhancing coordination amongst local HLP stakeholders.1.0 Free legal services provided to about 2000 spontaneous returnees, IDPS host communities including women and girls in Wau, Juba Yei and Lainya 1.1Establish 1 new Legal Aid clinic (LAC) in Lainya and sustain operation of the three already established clinics in Wau, Yei, and Juba: Each LAC will provide free legal aid to about 300 beneficiaries including men, women , girls and boys during the project period and about 1,200 beneficiaries will be met by the 4 LACs and the CMGs. Each of the Legal Aid Clinics will be staffed with one Legal Aid Attorney, One Legal Aid Officer and 15 CMGs. The Legal Aid Clinics will provide legal information, counselling, referral and litigation support on HLP cases. The SSLS will rent one office in each of the project location. The establishment of the new LAC in Lainya will require all basic furniture, generator, computers and Printer to aid smooth running of the static legal aid clinic. The Legal aid officers will be responsible for the day to day running of the legal aid offices providing legal advice, counselling and referral. The Legal aid officers will also be responsible for managing and monitoring and monitoring the work of the CMGs. The Legal Aid Attorney will be responsible for handling complex HLP cases that may require litigation or mediation. The Legal Attorney will provide technical advice to the CMGs in handling HLP cases. 1.2Conduct Mobile Legal Aid outreach: SSLS will conduct quarterly mobile legal aid to provide on spot legal advice, information, counselling and referrals to target beneficiaries in the project areas targeting about 200 beneficiaries per project location.Due to the scattered population in the project locations as a result of the conflict, the monthly mobile legal aid outreach will benefit specifically identified remote areas. Each Mobile Legal Aid Outreach will target about 200 persons including men, women, boys and girls. The selected category of beneficiaries will include IDPs, refugees’ returnees and host communities. 2.0: Raising awareness on HLP rights, legal framework and available mechanisms for HLP disputes Resolution targeting 2000 spontaneous returnees, IDPS host communities including women and girls, men and boys in Wau, Juba Yei and Lainya. 2.1 IEC materials SSLS will produce 3,200 T-shirt and caps with key precise messages on HLP rights, 2.2 Conduct radio talk shows SSLS will conduct quarterly radio talk shows to raise awareness on HLP rights, the legal framework and available mechanism for HLP disputes resolution targeting about 6000 spontaneous returnees, IDPS host communities including women and girls, men and boys in each project location. 2.3 Conduct community awareness events SSLS conduct quarterly community awareness outreach session in each of the project location to raise community awareness on HLP rights, the legal framework and available mechanism for HLP disputes resolution targeting spontaneous returnees, IDPS host communities including women and girls, men and boys in each project location. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SSLS has PSEA policy and mechanisms for handling PSEA cases. the inception meetings and radio talk shows will be used to inform beneficiaries about SSLS mechanism for handling PSEA cases.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will make use of local languages, interpretation and translation to ensure that vulnerable persons including persons living with disabilities and illiterate persons can be heard and actively participate in the project implementation and benefit from the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SSLS has in place feedback and complaints mechanisms for beneficiaries. The project will be launched through inception meetings with beneficiaries and stakeholders. these meetings will provide opportunities to inform the beneficiaries about the SSLS feedback and complain mechanisms. This will help ensure their active participation and contributions to shape the project implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback from beneficiaries will be categorized into three themes: complains, complements and recommendations. SSLS Senior management Team and the Board of Directors will take the complains seriously, investigate and address them in manner that avoid repeat. The complements will be noted to identify activities and approaches of the project that reflect best practices to inform implementation of the project. The recommendations will be analyzed based on the theory of change of the project to shape the project implementation.
