1 - Needs

CASS is implementing Health and Nutrition activities with funding from World Bank through UNICEF and it is they only humanitarian agency with a permanent present in Manyo County (Wadakona), the rest of the actors: implementing mobile Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) and Education support are based in Renk and they reach Wadakona via speedboat. As a result, civilians have limited access to basic services, especially water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services. The prevalence of acute malnutrition in Renk County, a neighboring county of Manyo is very high. According to the IPC for Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN) scale, Renk was classified as IPC AMN Phase 5. This county recorded the highest GAM prevalence of 32.1% in a SMART survey conducted in June 2019. The county, including Manyo continued to show persistently extreme critical levels of acute malnutrition for the 4th year in a row. According to the IPC for Acute Malnutrition classification, the GAM rate of above 30% is considered Extreme Critical (IPC AMN Phase 5). The persistent acute food insecurity is attributed to unusually high food prices, limited access to fish and the high number of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia that have put additional pressure on the available resources as they settle in their places of origin and re-build their livelihoods. Additionally, poor road conditions and continued insecurity related to inter-communal conflicts and cattle raiding particularly in Maiwut and Manyo counties have significantly disrupted access to markets and fishing. Children Aid South Sudan (CASS) has conducted rapid nutrition assessment in Wadokona Payam and in the surrounding Bomas using MUAC and Odema assessment parameter to children aged (6 59) months for acute malnutrition, the proxy global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate were 27% which is above the emergency threshold with SAM prevalence of 10% which extremely high above the SAM emergency threshold of <2%. The high prevalence of acute malnutrition among children aged (6 59) months could be due to IPC 3 classification and inaccessibility to Manyo County with basic nutrition response activities. CASS currently running inpatient therapeutic treatment center (SC) with funding from Common Humanitarian fund (CHF) from health cluster for management of SAM with medical complication. This is one of the important component of CMAM with average weekly admission of 4 6 children aged (6 59) months with severe acute malnutrition couples with common childhood illness (Medical complication). With support from SSHF, CASS proposed to continue supporting the SC in Wadokona PHCC and three OTP sites within the surrounding Bomas. These sites were selected as a result of the Rapid assessment conducted. CASS hopes by implementing this project, will build resilience of the population to cope during emergency. Being a national organization with better understanding of the local context, CASS hopes to be able to maintain access to basic nutrition services even within the mix of reduced humanitarian corridor. The project will target children and PLW as well as the elderly and all the vulnerable community. It will have little or no negative impact on the environment.

2 - Activities

1. Preposition nutrition supplies for rapid effective and efficient emergency responses in Manyo County.2. Establishing OTPs, stabilization centers & TSFP for management of acute malnutrition to children, PLW and the vulnerable community3. Engage CNVS to strengthen community case management, trace defaulters and enhance referral systems from community level to health facilities. 4. Conduct effective M&E with regular supervision using QSCL and standardized nutrition monitoring checklist at each OTP, SC, TSFP C108& outreach sites 5. Provide IYCF, biannual micro nutrients supplementation, de worming for boys and girls of U5 age and PLW, mother support group and including people living with HIV/AIDS.6. Sensitization and education of Care takers and PLW on good hygiene and sanitation practices for prevention of malnutrition.7. Conduct regular screening and growth monitoring at health facilities and outreach sites8. Build capacity in the three states establishment and smooth running of state level cluster, nutrition survey TOT, training on nutrition treatment, joint monitoring and development of state level nutrition response plan

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. The protection environment is sufficiently safe. Risk assessments will regularly be conducted by CASS Field Coordinator and all movement to project site will be coordinated with the relevant Cluster leads, UNDSS, UNMISS and local authorities/RRC to ensure clearance for safe passage, flexible response and effective team work. The CASS Juba security advisor support will be available at all times to respond to queries and provide advice. CASS field teams will also be sensitive towards staffing and the underlying conflict tensions. Deployment of national staff will be done within their home areas, where the risk of ethnic violence is minimal. In addition, all staff working in the field are already provided with hostile environments awareness training (HEAT) and regular security briefings. Surge capacity will be used where appropriate in order to reduce risks in areas with a high potential for ethnic conflict.

This project will provide beneficiaries with information in many ways:- 1. Project Launch:- this will inform the beneficiaries that the project has been secured and the implementation is starting. It will enable them to follow the implementation of the project and tract the progress. 2. Make feedback reciprocal:- all the report on the conducted surveys and discussions with communities shall be shared with the participating communities. Beneficiary accountability is an attitude and an approach that promotes the dignity of those affected by enabling them to influence decision-making and play a more active role in their own recovery. To be truly accountable, project staff must accept responsibility for their actions and agree that they can be called upon to give an account of how and why they have acted / failed to act. For the purposes of this project, beneficiary accountability is understood to be implemented across 5 standard practices: Participation, Transparency, Feedback and Complaints, Monitoring and Evaluation, and Staff Competencies and Attitudes.
