Project Summary

Through comprehensive CCCM response and services, IOM will improve living standards, strengthen accountable life-saving service delivery and provide a path for durable solutions for conflict affected IDPs in sites, site-like settings and in host communities in the Northern provinces of Cabo Delgado. In response to the ongoing, complex response in the region, CCCM services need to be maintained through on-site and mobile approaches, supporting IDPs and partners through service monitoring, community engagement and sustainable site-planning and development to ensure minimum standards are met and displaced populations are living in dignified conditions as the context evolves. CCCM will play a great role in maintenance of site infrastructure through more intense community engagement to counter vandalism and continued site upgrades. Rapid response to newly displaced populations will be facilitated by coordinated reception centre management. IDP sites which require a change in assistance modality and that are now ripe for lesser site management presence will be progressively handed over to the oversight of local authorities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

IOM CCCM plans to target 200, 000 people in need through a full CCCM response including site planning and improvement, community engagement, information management, coordination of services, and capacity building. With the ongoing displacement in the northern regions of Mozambique and the evolving context, displaced populations remain in dire need due to heavy congestion in some temporary sites exposing Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) to further risks. Displaced persons living outside of sites are equally vulnerable as host communities are overwhelmed and traditional social protection safety nets are not able to meet the needs of these many displaced people. Although IOM will maintain its site planning and development support , the organisation will focus more on site maintenance and site upgrades due to a change in the needs necessitated by the evolving displacement context. IOM will continue improving the living conditions in some of the sites so that they meet the set site management standards. Up to 2024 through community engagement and community participation, governance structures in sites and site-like settings have increased in their inclusivity through women and disability inclusion committees engaging with leadership, equipping these structures to support the community and service providers while ensuring protection and social cohesion. Complaint and Feedback mechanisms have continued to be in operation up to 2024 in line with protection principles and regular capacity building of governance structures, partners, and government counterparts will continue to be prioritised. In hard-to reach areas or in areas of new displacement, mobile teams will provide coordination support and advocacy for improved services as well as support reception management of new arrivals. IOM CCCM will activate the Urban Displacement Out of Camps (UDOC) approach in specific districts and areas where there are concentrations of IDPs living in the host communities. As CCCM Cluster lead agency, IOM will also take on the responsibility of CCCM provider of last resort. IOM will engage in regular CCCM partner and service provider meetings to ensure response consistency across sites and between actors, bringing these meetings to district and site level as appropriate. IDP sites that now require lesser site management presence will experience an adjustment in site management approaches. These sites will be equipped with self management skills and handed over to Local authorities for continued oversight. Mechanisms will be put in place to ensure that site management can be reactivated in the event of new emergencies arising.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Output 1: Strengthen the role of women, youth and People With Disability in community governance structures in relocation and temporary sites through community engagement and capacity building. Ensure AGD mainstreaming across camp governance structures. All IDP representatives actively participating in site governance structures will undergo Protection / GBV / PSEA training. Output 2: Continue to expand the implementation of Complaint and Feedback Mechanism, Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM). Enhance access to CFM for populations who have mobility challenges. Ensure all site residents have the information they need to safely access services and make informed decisions regarding their displacement. Provide updated demographic and site information to partners on a regular basis. Provide site level coordination to ensure predictable and effective service provision in sites. Ensure inclusion of IDPs living outside sites in the response through Urban Displacement and Out of Camp approach. Facilitate multi sector first line assistance for newly displaced populations through enhanced reception centre management.Output 3: Coordinate and carry out site planning and site upgrades with government and humanitarian partners to identify viable land for new sites and agricultural land. Implement capacity building trainings with government technicians on site planning and demarcation. Implement site maintenance and repairs for community infrastructure. Ensure that damaged shelters are fixed, drainage is in place and functional, implementation of accessibility works for PWDs. These will improve safety and will be implemented in a physically, socially and culturally appropriate manner for the site residents. Provide training and ongoing technical support including on-the-job supervision to CCCM partners for camp management, site planning, information management, community engagement and registration.Output 4. Handover of site management responsibilities for IDP sites that require lesser site management support. IOM will train local authorities on CCCM, coordination and site management responsibilities. Communities will receive training on self-management skills, PSEA and will be connected with service providers for easier follow up on service gaps. SDPI will be equipped with tools for reporting. Infrastructure mapping will be completed and the information will be shared with the cluster and relevant partners.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

In order to ensure none of the CCCM project activities and modalities contribute tofurther discrimination of marginalized groups, an assessment will be conducted todetermine the dynamics that exist in the community including factors exacerbatingmarginalization. CCCM team will promote representation of marginalized communitiesin decision-making and community governance structures (i.e. Community DisabledCommittee; Proportional Representation among Block Leaders; bilateral consultationwith leaders of newly arrived communities). Through CwC, Camp Management willensure marginalized groups are reached with all public awareness messages,information on project activities and concerns regarding access to services. With theaccessible complaints and feedback mechanism most marginalized groups will beable to address issues of exclusion/lack of information, and lack of representation.Prior to any CCCM intervention in new sites, Camp Management teams will regularlyconsult with different actors including community leaders, state and local authorities,OCHA and partners regarding camp administration and land usage to ensure that thedisplaced population will not be forcibly evicted from the site. Where returns areaffected by housing, land and property (HLP) issues, Camp Management will link themwith HLP actors for assistance.CCCM will participate in inter-agency safety audits toidentify gender-based violence (GBV) risks and address related issues. In addition,Camp Management will collaborate with protection and security actors for thedevelopment of community based protection measures that enhance the safety ofwomen and girls reducing their exposure to sexual violence including proper siteplanning, infrastructure, and site care and maintenance interventions to reduce GBVrisk (i.e. Solar lighting, elimination of dead-end corridors, better designed publicspaces) and engaging with women’s governance structures, representatives, and IDPsdirectly to identify and address safety concerns. Through the GBV referral pathwaysCamp Management team will be able to refer GBV cases to GBV actors for assistance.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

IOM will use CCCM Cluster tools to conduct rapid assessments engaging with the target population to ensure that the project is in line with their needs. Through the project implementation, Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and CBCMs in sites and site-like areas, and the reinforcement of the national Linha Verde contact number, will be used to ensure that feedback is considered as the project progresses. Inaddition to this, the creation of community committees and strengthening of preexisting leadership structures will provide an additional means for the affected population to hold IOM accountable. Camp Management will promote the representation and participation of vulnerable members including persons with disabilities, women and girls, older persons and persons with chronic illnesses in community structures and CCCM forums to raise issues of exclusion, stigmatization and access constraints to services. Messages and meetings minutes will be context specific and tailored for the targeted audience respecting age, gender, and diversity translated and shared via notice boards keeping in mind cultural context and target beneficiaries. The project contains a PSEA training element to ensure that IOM and committees receive PSEA training and are aware of reporting structures, including Linha Verde. SEA information will be provided through posters in public spaces and engagement through the community committees and townhall meetings as required.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Baseline data for the project is disaggregated to show disability. IOM will work within the project to ensure that people living with disabilities are represented in community structures, such as through a Disability Inclusion Committee. The project will use assessments to understand the main barriers to inclusion of PWDs and understand how or support can help to overcome these. IOM will work with the Disability Inclusion working group to in development of IEC materials that are related to PWDs. In sitedesign, IOM CCCM team account for structural elements to support accessibility such as bridges, ramps and advocate for adaptations in water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities. IOM will provide mobile teams that will conduct household visits to capture the concerns PWDs through CFM. IOM teams will provide response to PWDs through household visits on issues raised through CFM. Information sharing by CCCM teams will take into consideration access to information by PWDs who have mobilitychallenges.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Site planning and development needs to be done in a more sustainable and coordinated manner as it is crucial in reducing the risk on a negative impact on the environment. Through thorough assessment of the area prior to implementation, the site planner will be able to adjust intervention modalities to prevent environmental degradation of resources such as water sources and trees. In addition, the interventions will use locally sourced materials wherever possible to reduce the transportation requirements and to ensure that materials are appropriate for the context. Labour based interventions will be prioritized to reduce use of fuel and increasing community participation in the sites. Community Engagement throughout the project will ensure community a buy-in to environmentally friendly practices through community committees.
