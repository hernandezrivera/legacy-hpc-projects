Project Summary

Considering the ongoing crisis (15th April 2023) which increased the humanitarian needs of majority of people in Sudan, and increased burden on humanitarian context by influx of newly forced displacements CRS is revising the project to contribute to the new escalating humanitarian needs. Bearing in mind the dynamic and developing situation of Sudan in which the country is prone to additional multiple crises, people often have multiple vulnerabilities. This project is revised to specially respond to the urgent needs of people affected by current war crisis, food insecurity, conflicts by provision of inclusive timely gender sensitive lifesaving assistance through general food distribution, Cash and Vouchers, nutrition while looking at durable and sustainable livelihoods and food security actions applying nexus programming in FSL. Under FSL the project will increase the target to 266,699 (213,359 females and 53, 340 male) 80% females) vulnerable people including children, people with disabilities, women headed households, IDPs, Returnees, adolescents, youth girls and elderly affected by crisis/ conflicts in Central and west Darfur and Red Sea states. While in nutrition this project targets 165,408 people in need mainly children and PLWs with eBSF and TSF. The project designed to mainstream gender and connect with durable solutions in humanitarian work.
This project provides food distribution to highly vulnerable people/ beneficiaries including vulnerable households with able bodied members, women heads, PLW/G, elderly and PWD, and nutrition interventions for moderately to severely malnourished CU5 and PLW/G, CRS will build on emergency response to targeted communities in West & Central Darfur and Red Sea to sustainably transition them from a prolonged emergency to recovery and development. The cross-cutting issues of gender, protection, nutrition-sensitive programming, and natural resource management (NRM) will ensure that the path forward is socially equitable and valuable resources are sustainably used and preserved for social justice and future generations.
CRS will monitor implementation of the project, including targeting the vulnerable CU5 and PLW/G in West & Central Darfur state and Red Sea. The project will engage community mobilizers, nutrition screenings (supervision of CHPs alongside MoH), monitoring, and reporting.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian needs have been dramatically increased due to outbreak of war In April 15th 2023 resulting in a huge influx of IDPs, as reported by UN that about 2 million people internally displaced. This has increased the humanitarian needs of people as well as the humanitarian target to 19.9M, as per FSL sector. IPC report for June and September highlighted the deterioration of the food security which is worsen between June and September where 11.7 million people are likely to be in Crisis or worse (PC Phase 3 or above) which is an alarming situation. Food insecurity In Sudan is increasing as about 34 % of the population are food insecure (WFP, June 2022), economic and political crisis, conflict and displacement, climate shocks, including consider as a combination of contributing factors. Western and Central Darfur consider as states that host the highest caseload of IPC Phase 3 and Phase 4 during which requires urgent and timely interventions to avoid escalating of the situation to more worse phases. In addition to Red Sea state which also host a large number of IDPs adding to the existing food insecurity and nutrition burden. Further to this, droughts and seasonal floods, and poor harvests are significantly affecting peoples’ access to food in Sudan. Further to this, Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Assessment (June,2022) revealed that, central Darfur and West Darfur and Red Sea have the highest food insecurity prevalence with 59, 56 and 18 percentages. Furthermore, based on 2022 Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Assessment (June 2022) 42 % female-headed households are food insecure while 9 % of them are severely food insecure. West Darfur and Central Darfur have prevalence of food insecure with 72 % and 63 %. More specifically, the Bendasi in Central Darfur and Kereneik, Ag Geneina and Beida in West Darfur are the areas where half of the population is food insecure. Additionally, between 40 and 45 per cent of residents in these localities experience food insecurity (HNO,2023). In that Vein, food or crop loss is becoming a major concern, as stated by WFP, that 'one third of all food produced for human consumption is wasted which equivalent to 1.3 billion tons'. It was proven that post-harvest food loss is one of the leading causes for food insecurity of millions of families across the world In both states, GAM prevalence is above the emergency level of 15% : Central Darfur at 17.8% and West Darfur at 19.1% prevalence. The underlying causes include food insecurity, poor infant feeding practices, poor dietary diversity, and inadequate access to WASH and health services. Approximately 81% people in Sudan do not have access to a functional health facility within 2 hours walk from their home, and 25% of households do not have functioning water point in their location nor access to sufficient water for basic needs. The Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2023, revealed that, Women’s and girls’ financial status push them to work in unfavorable and insecure environments that expose them to risks. Limited access to livelihood opportunities in the face of the economic conditions has resulted in heightened risk of exploitation where Incidents of GBV against men and boys, including sexual violence have also been reported. There is increased risk of GBV among IDPs aggravated by inadequate camps structure/ settlements, and access to food and water supply gaps that require women and girls to go long distances to collect water and firewood, to work and that put them in a risk of harassment and violence. The fear of GBV has a ripple effect in the livelihoods and survival opportunities of women and girls. It thus limits women and girls movements, curtailing their access to livelihood options and exposing those most vulnerable to continued violence (HNO, 2023). The report has also highlighted the increased needs of people with disabilities to access services as they are likely to have limited access to nutrition.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CRS work is guided by Its strong Safeguarding policy that puts the protection of people in need as a top priority. The project focuses on enabling factors to mainstream protection, conduction of safety audit will be carried-out across the interventions to ensure do no harm. Female participation will be ensured to empower women through financial and learning inclusion by engaging women in Savings and Internal Lending Communities SILC to enhance their social and economic meaningful participation. This project will also increase participation of women, youth, and people with disabilities in awareness interventions as well as inclusion in community committees such as food distribution committees, nutrition committees and community development committees. CRS will mainstream GBV and closely coordinate with GBV Sub-cluster (GBVSC) and GBV working groups at the states and national level to strengthen the quality of the project delivery
