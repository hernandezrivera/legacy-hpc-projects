Project Summary

IRC Bangladesh is implementing its integrated child protection and youth development program since the beginning of the Rohinga Influx. The program covers both camp and host community (1E, 1W, 5, 8E, 15, 22, 23 and 25 and all 11 unions in Tecknaf and Ukhiya). Our interventions are designed to prevent and respond to sexual abuse and exploitation of children and adolescents with case management services, psychosocial support, economic empowerment, community-based protection mechanism and CSO capacity development. For case management IRC has trained case worker (60% female) who are professionally skilled in addressing diversity and inclusion in service delivery. Structured Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) provided for psychosocial support to children age 6-10 year and adolescent age 11-19 years. Caregivers are also supported with structured positive parenting curriculum to support children recovering distress situation. Adolescent empowerment program includes both social and economic skills development opportunity for adolescent achieving positive coping mechanism and building social cohesion. The environment, DRR and COVID risk and awareness is at the center of program content development and delivery. IRC has its’ adapted module ready and tasted to implement in a situation of pandemic. Local partners and CSOs are supported with capacity development initiative to better work with children and youth. IRC’s integrated program approach (protection, GBV and health) ensure comprehensive services to the clients, especially to the SGBV survivors. External coordination and representation in sub-sector and task forces is our key priority for sustainable changes. Client centered complaint mechanism is established to do no harm (complaint box, hotline, child friendly reporting) and clients are part of project design and evaluation. Direct beneficiaries are children and young people who are at most risk of abuse and exploitation age 10-24 years with equal representation of boys and girls including 7% children and young people with disability.

1 - Identified Needs

Protection needs of children and adolescents are high and often complicated. With children and adolescents constantly exposed to violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation, discrimination and exclusion. Often the level of discrimination is high based on their age, sex, disability and diversity. Female adolescents are at highter risk of being victims of sexual abuse and kidnapping than make adolscents (69% versus 45% and not going outside after dark-59% versus 38%) . Children with disability are constantly at risk of neglect, mental, physical and sexual abuse, they face high discrimination and served last . Based on the CP monitoring assessments conducted in 2020, the key child protection concerns included child labor, child marriage, neglect, emotional and physical abuse. Cox’s Bazar remain one of the poorest and vulnerable districts in Bangladesh, there is high level of poverty and low level of literacy and employment, the situation of Tecknaf (literacy-26.7%) and Ukhiya (literacy-36.3%) sub-districts termed as worst. Children both in refugee and host community are forced into the labor activities to meet family needs. Child labor rate in camp and host community is at 16% and 49% respectively. Children moving for income opportunities affecting a disproportionate number of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), many of these children taken up parenting responsibility of their younger siblings. Further, it is affecting their social and emotional development- poor anger and anxiety management, not able to build relationships with their peers, inadequate ability to maintain meaningful relationships last adapt to negative coping skills.Additionally, adolescent girls have become victims of child marriage due to inadequate learning and safety spaces in the community, 17% of adolescents and 25% of caregivers believe that it is acceptable for parents to arrange a marriage . Child marriage rate in host community is even higher (20%) due to conservative social norms and limited or no scope for girls to engage in economic activities that later identify them as economic burden for the family. With the rise of child marriages in the community, it has had a negative effect as the number of child mothers in the community keep rising. Programing supporting adolescent boys has been ignored over the years forcing many adolescent boys not to be engaged in any meaningful skill development programming. This is leading the boys to easily engage with negative coping mechanism. With no opportunity to understand gender concept, they are representing negative masculinity that is reinforcing gender stereotypes. There is limited access to national protection systems including response services (case management) formal, non-formal and tertiary education in both Rohingya and host community children. Lack of material support and high level of poverty are also cited among causes of children protection risks. The current level of interventions for adolescents that are out of school is inadequate to fully empower them with skills and mitigate the tendency among them, to engage in high risk behavior. The outbreak of COVID-19 has proven to have a significant impact on parental stress, with caregivers and parents expressing their level of anxiety and psychological distress, economic hardship, and competing priorities, make it challenging for caregivers to fulfil their responsibilities. Data collected through pre and post-tests from our parenting programs indicates that caregivers and foster parents are under mental, behavioral and relationship stress, which affects provision of child care.
