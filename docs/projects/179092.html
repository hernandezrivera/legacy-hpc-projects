Project Summary

Implemented in cooperation with the Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC), INTERSOS Health program follows an integrated approach which is aimed at building resilience and capacities of communities affected by the crisis in Syria. INTERSOS will implement multi-sectoral actions where health will be integrated with components of Protection (child protection and GBV) and Education. Health projects aim to achieve the following main results: strengthening skills and capacities of health personnel on PHC and COVID-19 related topics (IPC, Triage), enhancing access to essential health services, raising awareness on good health behavior and practices in the Governorates of Hama and Rural Damascus. The action applies a combined mobile and static approach whereby static health facilities are enhanced and equipped alongside with the deployment of mobile health teams in hard-to-reach areas with no health clinic available. As a final result, access to quality PHC services for vulnerable communities in hard-to-reach areas will be increased and expanded. A special focus will be also given to prevention and response mechanisms related to the COVID-19 pandemic to minimize health risks and provide necessary information and equipment. A comprehensive capacity building program will contribute to strengthen Health Workers and first-line responders’ skills to decrease health risks, protect and assist vulnerable individuals with high-severity needs. Specific, tailored, technical trainings and capacity building sessions will be provided to SARC Health staff and volunteers as well INTERSOS community mobilizers on relevant COVID 19 health topics, PHC, common childhood illnesses, basic emergency obstetric and new-born care and health education and promotion. A static Health Facility (HF) an unserved area (Morek, Hama Governorate) will be supported through minor rehabilitation (guaranteeing the necessary physical and sanitary conditions for the provision of health services) and provision of essential medication and equipment. As a result, the HF will fully function to serve the related catchment population. INTERSOS will facilitate and monitor the delivery of health services to beneficiaries through SARC health workers and INTERSOS medical staff to ensure continuity and availability of PHC services through diagnosis and treatment of common illnesses among children, adolescent, adult, elderly and PLW according to the national protocols. 3 Mobile Health Units will be deployed in Hama Governorate in Murek, Suran and Mohardeh where vulnerable people will have access to consultations and basic medical supplies. In addition, RCCE and behavioral change campaigns will be launched and rolled-out on COVID-19 prevention and response as well as on other prominent heath-focused issues. This will be conducted through a network of community mobilizers and local influencers on the ground. In addition, vulnerable students will be supported in some areas where target schools have been identified. Awareness sessions for students, parents and other community members will be provided in Hama on nutrition practices, hygienic practices (including IPC related to Covid-19), Infant and young child feeding and other health seeking behaviours.

4 - Needs

The Syrian health system remains fragile and years of crisis have heavily disrupted the access to health services for millions of people living below emergency standards for health care (HRP, Dec 2020). 12.4 million people are in need of health care services according to OCHA– 10% of which have disabilities. 70% of the sub-districts (188 out of 268) have the most severe health needs and severity score of 3 and above. 0.5M children are malnourished, and thousands suffer from severe malnutrition. COVID-19 has further weakened the health system. Epidemiological analysis indicates that the month of March 2021 witnessed the second highest monthly caseload (3,321). Thousands of more people suffer from cancer, diabetes, and other chronic conditions for which treatment is limited. COVID-19 has further exacerbated and weakened the health system, which has limited testing capacity to detect community transmission and inform public health response. In 2020, INTERSOS conducted needs assessment in Hama (Muhardeh, Kernaz, Hamra, Suran) and in Rural Damascus (Zamalka, Beit Sawa, Modira, Misraba), jointly with SARC, holding KIIs with community leaders and SARC community mobilizers. In addition, secondary data was processed (HNO 2021, HRP 2021, Health Sector reports, 4Ws), and consultations were held with other stakeholders (WHO, INGOs). INTERSOS Syria team and the Medical Advisor conducted additional consultations, field visits and FGDs in Hama and Rural Damascus to further investigate needs, gaps, and opportunities. Findings from the data analysis help to improve the quality of provided services and health assistance, and to design an evidence-based response to effectively address identified needs within the Syrian healthcare system.

5 - Implementation of activities

INTERSOS applies a combined mobile and static approach to provide support to hard-to-reach areas. The program has been designed in coordination with Health Sector partners, SARC HQ and field branches to ensure complementarity and optimization of resources. Implementation: Capacity building for Health Workers (HWs), SARC volunteers and community mobilizers on COVID-19 (hygiene, IPC measures, early case identification and PFA) and common childhood illnesses, basic emergency obstetric and newborn care; Support a static health centre through minor rehabilitation and provision of medical equipment and supplies; Provision of PHC services through the deployment of MHUs for diagnosis and treatment of common illnesses among children, adolescent, adult, elderly and PLW; Development of IEC material and dissemination of communication and behavioral change campaigns through RCCE. M&E plan (indicators and means of verification): # of PHC consultations (Facility registers, Monthly reports, Field visits reports) # of live births attended by skilled health personnel (delivery records, activities reports, monitoring reports); # of health facilities rehabilitated (intervention reports, activities reports; distribution lists; data analysis, survey reports, list of products, material, supplies and equipment distributed; photos); # of health staff, SARC volunteers and community mobilizers trained (Pre-post-test results; training reports; attendance lists.); # of people reached through awareness raising sessions, communication/behavioural change campaigns (activities reports; attendance sheets ; trainings reports; pre post test; distribution lists; data analysis; survey reports; list of IEC products; surveillance reports; training attendance).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Risk of discrimination, perceptions of discrimination/exclusion of vulnerable groups, lack of communication and engagement of target groups. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigating: Ensure that consultations are undertaken with communities, in a way to tailor and adapt the intervention according to needs; Continuous involvement of community throughout the project cycle, in line with the principles of accountability to affected populations (AAP); Adopt and enforce a lean and effective Complaint Response and Feedback Mechanisms, Production and dissemination of key messages, which are inclusive and accessible to wide audiences (e.g. RCCE campaigns). Risk: GBV victims and survivors lack safe, dignified and stigma-free access to health services and support. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigating: Ensure Health Workers are trained on GBV principles and response strategies and are enabled to identify GBV cases and provide safe referrals; include PSS and GBV prevention and response activities in health interventions;Secure protection mainstreaming in all health programs. Risk: PSEA Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigating: strict PSEA policy and SOPs are in place; Provide inception and refresher training to INTERSOS and SARC staff; Ensure a proper reporting system, data protection and referral pathways are in place. Risk: Increase of tensions between IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities due to unequal provision of assistance by the project. Likelihood: Low-medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: Secure that a thorough mapping of affected communities and conflict-sensitivity analysis are performed at the onset of the project; consultations and transparent communications with both groups to foster social cohesion.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risks: Staff recruited has prior conviction for SEA or related offence; Implementing partner, staff member, suppliers, (etc.) do not comply/accept PSEA policy; SEA perpetrated by staff members/service providers; reinforced stereotype of beneficiaries through wrong communication and images. Mitigation: job opening state that INTERSOS has PSEA and CP Policies that all staff is expected to comply with; tests include the Attitude Scale; assessment about the partner’s commitment and capacity to prevent and respond to SEA is conducted; where possible, training are offered to partners to identify SEA risks and define related SEA mitigation measures throughout programming; SEA risk analyses and mitigation strategies are elaborated at the country level: categorization of interventions, implementation modalities by risk significance level, structure to report, investigation structure; “Do No Harm” principle guide the collection and use of images and information about beneficiaries; images and stories only with the full understanding and permission of the subject. The informed consent needs to be signed and filed in a secure location; all staff and affiliated workforce must read, understand and sign the INTERSOS PSEA/CP Policy Declaration prior to contract start date; all staff must receive the PSEA/CP Policy induction training; all staff must receive one PSEA/CP Refresher training per year; awareness raising sessions are to be conducted with communities and other actors by PSEA FPs.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

INTERSOS Syria has developed a Contingency Planning Process (CPP) with risk analysis and related mitigation measures, whose key components can be summarized as follows: Reputational Risk: unable to deliver on-time, breach of sanctions and/or do not harm principle. A detailed SOPs on procurement of medical supplies and drugs is in place and into force, thorough market research and due diligence exercises are conducted for verification of quality standards and legal requirements, strong communication, reporting mechanisms and robust monitoring strategy are followed; Security and Access: unable to reach and implement in targeted areas. INTERSOS relies on security assessment and protocols, combined with SARC guidance, supervision and networks in the field; Revisions of target communities, workplan and scheduled activities might be envisioned if necessary. GBV and PSEA: risk of reinforcing gender stereotypes by excluding women from participation; risk of PSEA. PSEA strategy enforced; Financial Risk and Accountability: exchange rates fluctuations affect achievement of targets; lack of transparency; diversion or misuse of project financial resources. INTERSOS has an internal system and SOPs to secure compliance and accountability: careful, systematic and rigorous monitoring both of project activities and project spending is carried out; financial controlling rules and processes are in place; regular communication with the donor in case of economic shock and re-alignment of targets. Health Risks linked to the COVID-19 pandemic: staff and beneficiaries might be exposed to health hazards and risks of contagion while participating in project activities. INTERSOS takes into account precautionary measures related to COVID 19.
