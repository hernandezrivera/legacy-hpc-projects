Project Summary

The objective of the project is to address the basic needs of most vulnerable communities through the provision of shelter rehabilitation, distribution of tents in informal settlements and seasonal cash distributions. The shelter rehabilitation (targeting 2,400 individuals) will be implemented either through a cash modality (conditional shelter grants) or by contractors. The combination of these 2 modalities ensures the coverage of both owners, renters and free-hosted households, with a focus on reinforcing the HLP rights to avoid forced evictions which is a factor to consider, especially in Ar-Raqqa city. The tents will be delivered to households (targeting 3,600 individuals) in informal settlements, selecting manufacturers in the country to stimulate the local economy. Finally, the seasonal cash distributions (targeting 5,000 households) will ensure a quick and flexible emergency assistance response for IDP households (primarily in informal Settlements) and vulnerable host community households. DCA will prioritize beneficiary/ households who are recently displaced, households with disability family members, single-parent-headed households, and households without livelihoods.

3 - Needs

The humanitarian situation is incredibly complex and constantly evolving due to 10 years of civil war. This has caused unprecedented devastation of shelters, 40% in Ar-Raqqa (HNO 2021). In accordance with MSNA 2022 findings, 34% of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelter, of which 19 per cent reside in unfinished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying ‘adequacy issues’. In conjunction with this, 38 per cent of the overall population are renting or hold weak forms of occupancy agreement, which hampers eligibility for shelter assistance and/ or heightened risks of eviction (HNO 2023). In addition, compared to 2022, the number of IDPs living in last resort sites increased, lacking crucial infrastructures and emergency shelter solutions (HNO 2023 draft). These issues, combined with a constant depreciation of the Syrian pound and increase of construction materials in the local markets, resulted in a higher number of people not able to meet their basic needs, 85% according to HNO 2023 draft. In this scenario, Ar-Raqqa remains in dire need of reconstruction and rehabilitation with a focus on HLP documentation, according to the NES Shelter working group (WG). Shelter conditions also have serious protection implications with risks of increases in mental stress, domestic violence and general feeling of living an undignified life.

4 - Implementation of activities

To address the significant lack of safe and sustainable shelter options in NES, DCA will establish safe and accessible shelters for HHs impacted by conflict. Regarding shelter rehabilitation, DCA will conduct the following activities: Community Liaison, identification of most impacted communities; Survey and Clearance of Community Homes (coupled with EORE); HH Assessments and Repair Plan development; Cash / Contractor support to execution of repair plans; technical Follow up. For tent distributions, Informal settlements which didn’t receive any shelter support in the last 3 years will be prioritized based on the dashboard of Informal Settlements WG. Based on pre-assessments, a shortlist of settlements will be selected for assessments and verification. The team will also be present during the distribution and installation of the tents and will do a follow-up a week after to ensure they were installed correctly. For seasonal cash distributions, the selection of locations will be based on analysis of gaps and needs in coordination with local authorities (HAO and Kumin) and WGs. After selecting, assessing the availability and capacity of local markets, the assessment, verification and later support will be carried out. All projects will be concluded with a Post-distribution Monitoring survey after a month, to assess the efficacy and impact of the activities. HHs will be selected based on vulnerability, livelihood and access to essential facilities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Increase risk of intercommunity tension due to diverse assistance packages across members Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measure: • Agree to minimum assistance package across sector • Assess and validate any changes before they occur • Identify, mitigate or exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions • DCA will establish and facilitate complaint response mechanism to mitigate grievances in the community Protection Risk: Housing land and property issues (Eviction of tenants after completion of works, confusion between owner and occupant) Likelihood: Medium Impact: Medium Mitigation Measures: DCA will coordinate with local authorities and local leaders for shelter rehabilitation interventions who will oversee verifying the ownership of the shelters where rehabilitation works is carried out. Through the signature of an MoU (between the project, -local authority, occupant, and shelter owner- if applicable, the responsibilities of all parties will be agreed upon before any rehabilitation work commences. The MoU notes that should the occupant of the shelter be a tenant, the owner commits to allowing the tenant to remain rent free for at least 3 months and in the shelter for at least 12 months with no increase in the rent.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA staff members or partner staff members.Likelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: DCA Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about the provision of available services and who services are for. DCA PSEA focal point in coordination with HR will conduct training and refreshers to project staff and partner staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DCA will establish a complaint box that will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentiality. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by the member of complaints handling team or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA follows principles of Do No Harm across its project activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project location impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas and threated the safety and security of DCA project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation Measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), and conducts regular security- related training for staffs. Project Management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation, and evacuation plans remain in place. Risk: Local authority- imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent DCA project staff from moving and/ or working, and/ or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/ KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries. Assumptions: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff) and Hawalas will be able to continue transferring money.Mitigation measures: DCA staff will follow local authority- imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should selected hawala agent is not able to transfer funds.
