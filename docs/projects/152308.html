Project Summary

This project aims to improve access to free comprehensive Primary Health Care (PHC) services including Nutrition, Mental Health and GBV services for conflict affected populations in Borno State, Nigeria in Maiduguri / Jere LGA (local government areas), and in Damboa LGA.
Médecins du Monde (MdM) has been active in Nigeria since August 2016 in the fields of primary healthcare (PHC), nutrition, gender-based violence (GBV) and mental health and psychosocial services (MHPSS). MdM aims to respond to the emergency situation caused by the violent armed conflict in the North-eastern part of Nigeria.
Three different modalities of intervention will be implemented to follow the dynamic of IDPs movements in Borno State. Those 3 modalities of intervention will be closely monitored in order to timely adapt to changes:
• “MdM run” fixes clinics in IDP settlements and informal camps;
• Revitalization & strengthening of Primary Health Care services in MoH facilities;
• An Emergency Preparedness & Response Plan (EPREP).
This project proposal will enable a scale up of MdM’s capacity in the field to ultimately reinforce its emergency healthcare intervention as a way to respond to the immediate needs of the target population while strengthening the capacity of local organizations and MOH in the locations which are of safe access for humanitarian teams. This support is aims to improve the access to health services through different modalities (MDM clinics, support to MOH HF when possible, EPREP) and reinforcing MOH capacity to deliver free PHC services to host and IDPs communities. In addition, Primary Health Care structures will be continually assessed, and support will be adapted according to identified gaps in terms of rehabilitation, provision of medical equipment and drugs, and staff capacity building. Primary Health Care structures will be targeted according to the influx of IDPs in its catchment area and take into account the dynamic movement of IDPs population.
MdM aims to provide a rapid response to the immediate needs of IDPs population in new spontaneous settlements accessible or during outbreaks in Borno State. Activities of the emergency response team will be coordinated with other stakeholders’ active in the state, through continued coordination mechanisms at local level and throughout MdM’s involvement within the relevant Clusters. In terms of operational approach, MdM will consider first direct support to MOH and SEMA, wherever HF is still available or functional, in reinforcing care management capacities and quality of care. Should this be the case, MdM will set-up a temporary clinic to cover the essential and most urgent healthcare needs in the targeted area.

Needs

Problem 1: Lack of access to health care services and low availability of medical suppliesWhere: Northeastern Nigeria, particularly Borno StateTargeted / affected population: All IDP and host population living in Borno State, particularly women and childrenOccurrence/Frequency: Constant problemSeverity of the problem: High. Background: Deterioration of health status of the population; high morbidity and mortality rates; low life expectancy (53 men/56 women); probability of dying between 15 and 60 years m/f (per 1 000 population, 2015) 368 men/318 women; total expenditure on health per capita (Intl $, 2014): 217; Government expenditure on health was 3.9% in 2010, increased to 5.7% in 2015 and decreased to 4.2% in 2016 (FMOH 2018).Consequences: Lack of access to health services combined with prolonged state of crisis will exacerbate existing high levels of morbidity and mortality. This will also result result in long term negative socio-economic impacts. Problem 2: Nutrition and food insecurityWhere: Northeastern Nigeria, particularly Borno StateTargeted / affected population: All IDP and host population living in Borno State, especially in northern and east Borno State, inaccessible due to the conflictOccurrence/Frequency: Acute problem during the lean season, lessened at times of harvest. Recent food and nutrition assessments from OCHA estimate that 2.6 million people are food insecure – and require assistance - from October to December 2017, and this figure was predicted to increase to 3.7 million by the June to August 2018 lean season, should adequate food and livelihoods assistance not be provided in time.Severity of the problem: High.Consequences: Lack of access to nutrition services will increase morbidity, mortality and stunting among children under 5. Malnourishment hampers immune systems and increases the likelihood of serious morbidity or mortality from endemic diseases such as malaria and cholera. Problem 3: Mental Health Where: Northeastern Nigeria, particularly Borno StateTargeted / affected population: All IDP and host population living in Borno State, particularly displaced women and children in and outside of campsOccurrence/Frequency: It is not possible to get data of the epidemiological burden of mental disorders in the North East, however, there is evidence of an increase, using trends from Federal Neuropsychiatric Hospital Data from before and after the insurgency (see tables 1 &2).Severity of the problem: Many persons suffering from mental Health problems in addition to GBV survivors do not have proper access to medical and mental health support services, so the gap between the patients reached and the existing needs is huge. Consequences: Untreated mental health and psychosocial problems, can lead to further decline in the general health, wellbeing and quality of life in general of the population., This further exacerbates disruption in relationships and communities, and leads to diminished resilience to cope with everyday difficulties. Problem 4: Gender-Based Violence Where: Northeastern Nigeria, particularly Borno StateTargeted / affected population: All IDP and host population living in Borno State, particularly displaced women and girls, children in and outside of campsOccurrence/Frequency: However largely under-reported, due to fears of stigma or retaliation, limited availability or accessibility of trusted service providers, impunity for perpetrators, and lack of awareness of the benefits of seeking care, GBV is acknowledge as a primary protection concerns in North-East Nigeria. All conflict related settings seeing a large increase of GBV is occurring and threatening affected populations, affecting most particularly women and girls.
