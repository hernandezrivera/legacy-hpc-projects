Project Summary

UNHCR and its implementing partners propose to target 8,100 vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community individuals with the provision of unrestricted, unconditional multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) during the year 2021. The eligible vulnerable households will be identified using a socioeconomic vulnerability scorecard, endorsed by the Cash and Markets Working Group (CMWG). Cash assistance will be provided as multi-month (three installments) using electronic pre-paid cards however, in the case of emergencies and sudden displacement, the assistance strategy shall be changed from multi-month to one-off transfers. The amount of cash transfer value (CTV) is calculated based on the family size considering Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) which shall be monitored on a monthly basis by the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JIMMI). Finally, PDM will be conducted to analyze the effectiveness and impact of the MPCA on selected participants of the project. Through the proposed project UNHCR and its implementing partners will, therefore, mitigate the need of the most vulnerable conflict-affected IDPs and returnees households to resort to negative coping mechanisms, by helping them to address their basic needs through facilitated access to basic goods and services available in the market, empowering them and promoting the dignity of choice of its recipients and stimulating local markets.

1 - Needs

Libya continues to suffer from interlinked political, conflict and insecurity, damaging its economy, weakening state institutions, and facilitating criminal gangs and non-state armed actors. Insecurity and conflict remain the central driver of displacement. According to IOM DTM Round 32 published in August 2020, there are about 392,241 IDPs and 493,716 returnees in Libya. HNO 2021 further confirms, of those who remain displaced, about 173,000 people (44 percent) need assistance due to a combination of factors relating to an inability to afford rent and/or basic needs, increased protection and/or health risks due to living in sub-standard shelters or are in fear or at risk of eviction. Among displaced, women, children, people with disability, elderly and those with chronic illness are at higher protection risks and face challenges in accessing basic services and commodities given other factors that influence their vulnerability. The political stalemate has resulted in a governance vacuum and coupled with widespread violence and insecurity has disrupted the economy and public service delivery across the country. The living conditions, including access to clean drinking water, medical services, and safe housing have all degraded due to the protracted situation, particularly for women and children. The spread of the COVID-19 pandemic has further debilitated the already weak economic situation in the country. To prevent the spread of COVID-19, national and local authorities introduced a series of preventive measures and curfews and restricted movements within the country. Additionally, the shutdown of oil production by the LNA forces, which reduced considerably the country’s revenue that depends 95% on oil exportation affecting badly the state’s budget. All this has disrupted the formal and informal labor market. The majority of persons in need, whether formally employed on an hourly or seasonal basis, or in the informal economy have seen the elimination of their livelihood, some populations potentially more than others, including the ones without access to any of the social safety net or solidarity mechanisms in the country. Women also faced additional challenges accessing medical assistance due to pre-existing gender-related mobility constraints coupled with strict movement restrictions due to COVID-19. The inability to access livelihood and the resulting loss of income continues to be a major issue for communities in Libya. According to MSNA 2020 initial findings, about 20% of IDPs/Libyan HHs with members working reported disruption of work as a result of Covid-19 mostly in Muzruq, Al-Jabal Al-Akhdar, Sehba. The loss of income continues to impact people’s ability to cover their basic needs and pay rent. Similarly, MSNA 2020 reported 50% of the interviewed sample reported their inability to cover their basic needs. IDPs are reported to struggle meeting their shelter needs while for returnees, health, shelter and communication are the main three elements they are unable to cover. MSNA 2020 further reported IDP communities tend to resort to negative coping mechanisms including spending savings, borrow money from friends or relatives and/or reduce expenditures on essential non-food items and this is found mainly in Tripoli and Zwara mantikas. When these strategies are exhausted and the financial status deteriorates further, communities start to sell productive household items, reduce expenses on health to prioritize food, rent. A combination of high inflation, a decrease of purchasing power, a systemic liquidity crisis that triggers limited withdrawal rates and delays salary payments, the lack of necessary documentation limiting access to income-generating activities and raising barriers that exclude the most vulnerable from using financial significantly affects a conflict-affected household’s capacity to pay for basic items and services such as medicines, and healthcare, food, education supplies, fuel and rent.

2 - Activities

1) Cash assistance eligibility determination for 8,100 IDPs, returnees, and host community individuals using socio-economic vulnerability scorecard endorsed by CMWG of Libya. All eligible households will be correctly and accurately recorded in the respective beneficiary database/IMS used by UNHCR’s partners and be updated on a regular basis to reflect changes. 2) Following eligibility determination, a final distribution list will be prepared, and cash entitlements will be calculated based on the family size. 3) Payment instruction to financial Service Provider (FSP) will be initiated for the selected households. 4) The selected households will be provided with the electronic pre-paid cards and will be instructed on how to use the pre-paid cards, the payment schedule of the programme, the entitlement amounts, the coverage period of each payment and how to access a helpdesk and use complaint and feedback mechanism 5) Recipients queries relating to cash entitlements will be addressed and resolved in a timely manner. Referrals to appropriate feedback and complaints response mechanisms and tools, where appropriate, will be conducted. 6) continuous process monitoring and Post Distribution Monitoring Surveys.

6 - AAP/ Accountability mechanisms
6.1 - How is your project accountable to affected populations?

Common feedback mechanism

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNHCR implementing partners will connect to the Common Feedback Mechanism (CFM) that the Emergency Telecommunications Sector (ETS) is implementing. Parallel to ETS-CFM, Partners will use dedicated compliant and feedback mechanism/phone lines, email and other communication means to receive feedback and dedicated database to register the complaints and feedback, to manage the feedback workflow based on the level of sensitivity and prioritization in compliance with organizational CFM SOPs. To respond to the dynamic context and complex needs of the targeted vulnerable populations, the implementing partners will conduct regular assessments and interviews including key informant interviews and Focus group discussions (KII and FGDs) to obtain a detailed understanding of the impact of the interventions. The overall feedback will be collected from the communities requiring informed consent from beneficiaries. The partners M&E teams will receive and record all incoming feedback, to be analyzed, and guide appropriate corrective measures if needed.
