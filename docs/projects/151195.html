Project Summary

This project targets 2415 children (1280 being girls) from IDP settlements, host communities and returnees to be supported by supplementary school feeding, emergency provision of water to save lives, Construction of 4 TLS in Mareerey, Warsheikh, Cadale and Hawadhley primary schools which are among the four targeted schools with very high population of learners due to migration where pupil population have greatly shot up due to close proximity to town centers. The 3 temporary learning spaces constructed will each have 5 classrooms (total of 15 classrooms) to serve 375 boys and 450 girls, hand washing facilities, separate latrines for girls and female teachers and learning materials including incentives for 45 teachers (18 female). The newly accessible areas pose many cultural/traditional challenges in reaching the gender parity in enrollment of learners. As such this project estimates to reach at most 40% of girls and up to 60% boys. It is hoped that recruitment of female teaches, provision of sanitary pads to school girls and construction of gender sensitive latrines for will boost girl child enrollment by about 30%.

Needs

Statistics reveals that children of schooling age in need stands at 2.4 (Education cluster HNO) with Middle Shabelle accounting for almost 150,000 by SHARDO estimation. These are affected by drought and now due to April/May 2018 floods in Jowhar and most villages along River Shabelle. The effects being increased rates of dropout among learners while teachers are also deserting classrooms for better jobs. The situation has worsened especially in schools charging fees with most dropout cases noted to be of girls between ages of 8 and 17. Attacks on education has increased with Education Baseline survey showing 664 incidences/attacks while SHARDO's recent assessment indicate 12 incidences/attacks in Balcad schools alone.Middle Shabelle needs includes food and water to mitigate on the effects of drought and floods. Most families pull out their children from schools due to displacement by floods and to help in the homes by collecting firewood, fetching water thus contributing to high school dropout rate and increased child labor. Almost all schools in Middle Shabelle and especially Balcad and Warsheikh districts remain closed since April 2013 due to lack of teacher incentives affecting approximately 5000 learners (1500 girls) SHARDO assessment 2017. Main gaps includes dilapidated learning structures; lack of supplementary feeding program to help retain children in schools, lack of water and WASH facilities in schools, lack of separate latrines for girls to encourage retention; total lack of teacher incentives; lack of female trained teachers; lack of TLM; inadequate classrooms and TLS for learners to protect them from the myriad of protection issues including rape, child labor, early marriages of girls, radicalization of boys into militia groups, FGM and lack of sanitary pad for adolescent girls among other GBV issues that mostly hinder girls from accessing education; lack of adequate women in decision making school structures like CECs and Education ministry staff.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The UN Secretary General’s policy committee decision 2011/20 identified coordination as oneof the key gaps in durable solutions, arguing that it was ineffective between key actors ondevelopment, human rights, humanitarian and peace-building, thereby affecting any effortsgeared towards IDPs and refugees. This project thus will strive to enhance coordination between all stakeholders in building resilience of most vulnerable communities including learners and teachers in education settings. Following IASC guidelines, this project hopes to achieve durable solution through voluntary return to original settlements especially for 2017 drought and 2018 flood affected children of school going ages, local integration of children and families in places where host communities are receptive to IDP and resettle IDPs in accordance with the government development plans and UNHCR in cases where the other two scenarios can't be achieved. Despite the challenges that Middle Shabelle has faced, it is worth noting that there is relatively sufficient protection environment for long term planning and implementation of EiE programs. The ministry of Education has produced its ESSP that guides the sector in long term goals while linking the same to the MDGs thus supporting durable solutions/resilience. The once perceived hard to reach areas are opening up since the elimination of armed militias in most parts of Middle Shabelle thus enabling voluntary returns for flood affected IDP children and families. Even though this proposal isn't focused on systems building for ministry staff, efforts will be made to promote peaceful coexistence between host communities and IDPs through social integration and inclusion in project cycle. The long term gains will greatly contribute to the achievement of the set goals of building resilience through strengthened livelihoods, skills trainings and psychosocial support to learners affected by crisis. The chosen activities also widely contribute to building the resilience of learners and teachers to cope with the crisis with girls supported with sanitary pads; teachers and CECs trained on protection aspects such as DRR, PSS and encouraging child to child clubs in schools and TLS.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The severe drought and the ongoing conflict continue to cause displacement. Displacement dynamics have consistently been directed mainly towards urban centers and merging with urbanization dynamics of increasing pressure on services in larger towns and cities. Humanitarian conditions in many of the informal settlements and public buildings occupied by the displaced throughout Somalia continue to be dire; and may be further aggravated for communities who are forcibly evicted from their location of settlement or continue reside with insecure tenure. In IDP settlements, many reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision. Where the basic needs of IDP are not met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing services, or from the sheer absence of services due to poor targeting of settlements. The lack of core services contributes to serious public health risks, preventable deaths, and increased risks of GBV for women and girls, often with inadequate access to reporting and referral mechanism or accountability of perpetrators. Of specific concern is the large number of drought-affected IDP communities from conflict areas, who are often made up of separated families with men and youth remaining behind, increasing the exposure of women and girls to protection risks related to hazardous livelihood activities and reduced protection to GBV. Further, these IDPs with their perceived links with non-state armed actors face increased exposure to security threats and discrimination from the host communities.To mitigate on these, all stakeholders should ensure that efforts are made to return IDPs to their original homes to continue being productive rather than dependent of aid assistance. These will need concerted efforts in building capacities of both communities and governments to provide security, revive economies and ensure education is not interrupted at all times.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Most reports have noted the exclusion of women and girls on issues of social economic development. This has made women and girls more vulnerable to crisis especially those around conflicts, drought and floods as witnessed frequently in Middle Shabelle. The old are rarely consulted in the decision making processes and the young as well. Displacement affects individuals of all ages and sexes, as well as from diverse social backgrounds, in different ways and needs may evolve over time. Many IDPs, whether recently displaced or in a protracted displacement situation, are compelled by a lack of access to adequate assistance and livelihoods, to resort to negative coping mechanisms with serious protection repercussions. For instance, children are at a heightened risk for recruitment into fighting forces, child labor, early marriage and dropping out of school. Schools serves as a safe space for children and is critical to ensuring that children displaced by or affected by drought and conflict do not only have access to education but are better able to cope, survive and recover from the crisis impacts and contribute to longer term peaceful development of Somalia. Despite substantial progress on mainstreaming durable solutions in national development frameworks, the context of ongoing insecurity and drought means that safe and dignified durable solutions (return, local integration or settlement elsewhere) to displacement limited access to services and humanitarian assistance, and diversion of assistance.14 As a result, many are forced to move from their communities and some also move preemptively for fear of violence being deliberately imposed on them.SHARDO having been trained on AAP will ensure that people of all ages and gender are involved in all project processes. Age and gender will be greatly looked at in all project cycle processes.All staff will be required to sign a code of conduct that ensures non discrimination based of either gender or age.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Middle Shabelle is prone to conflict having been previously largely occupied by militia forces. Conflict may lead to loss of lives, maiming, disruption of learning and normalcy, deprivation of basic human rights including right to education, freedom of association and movement among many other rights and trauma among the community. As a result of general insecurity, inter-clan conflict escalations, indiscriminate attacks including through improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and aerial bombardments, as well as military offensives and withdrawals in the context of the conflict between the non-state armed actors, the Federal Government of Somalia (FGS) and supporting actors. Accountability for violations is limited, with traditional or formal mediation and justice mechanisms having been disrupted, or having become disrespected or inaccessible. This is further aggravated regarding violations for marginalized groups where societal inequalities result in reduced protection.SHARDO's approach will be to promote peaceful coexistence by inclusion of all clans and groups (minority and majority) in finding answers to common problems affecting them.Where conflicts and violence is experienced, SHARDO will do its best to take no sides but will record and report all incidents in confidentiality to necessary agencies.
