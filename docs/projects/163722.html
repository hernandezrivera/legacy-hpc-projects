Project Summary

NRC will provide four main types of services (information, counselling and legal assistance (ICLA) as well as referral services) to Libyans and non-Libyans in Libya, focusing on three main thematic areas: civil documentation, HLP rights, and access to services. The overall objective of NRC’s ICLA programming is that conflict affected Libyans are able to claim and exercise their rights vital for achieving durable solutions. To achieve this, under this project NRC will contribute to three primary objectives: that target groups to 1) claim and exercise HLP rights; and 2) possess legal identity, including civil documentation, and 3) better access basic services (including government). These objectives are interrelated.

1 - Needs

In Libya the Family Booklet and Passport are the most comprehensive identity documents accepted. Other important documents that affect the accuracy of the family booklet range from birth certificates (registration), death certificate, and marriage certificate (or ratification). Impact of the conflict and related displacement resulted in the loss or destruction of civil and important documentation by affected civilian. According to the 2019 MSNA, 12% of families reported not having key documentation for each member of the family. In 2018, 8% percent of households in Derna were reported missing documentation. This situation particularly affected those displaced. In the South, Mercy Corps report on documentation in the south estimate some 30% of persons are without basic, necessary, documentation. Some IDPs and other vulnerable people have reported to be missing civil documentation such as national ID cards, family books and land and property documents. The barriers to access range from administrative red tape, dysfunction of various authorizing bodies, lack of sufficient materials, complicated processes, decentralized systems, and cost. This presents particular concern for those originating from ethnic minorities, single women and children. This results in a large numbers of persons in Libya at risk of Statelessness. Furthermore, the registration of important life events would be more difficult for most categories of the above persons. Housing, land and property (HLP) matters in Libya are often complicated. Conflict since 2011 has exacerbated such HLP issues with former owners often claiming their original rights in previously expropriated real estate and consequent disputes over rights in destroyed property. Evidence of ownership and other rights in real estate were made more difficult because of the state-ordered burning of real property registers in the mid-1980s and the suspension of all formal registration since 2011. As a result of conflict, many people have been forcibly displaced from their homes, with destruction to property widespread in certain areas. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) are mostly renting housing (61% of IDPs according to REACH’s MSNA) . Security of tenure (primarily protection from forced evictions) for IDPs who rent is low – 6% of conflict-affected households reported facing the threat of eviction in the past 6 months and only 5% of rental agreements are written. Pressures of not being able to pay rent regularly; rising rental prices and disputes between landlords and tenants are leading to the risk of forced eviction. The shortage of affordable and adequate housing in areas of displacement, insecure tenancies and forced evictions are all reported in the Libyan context. These challenges are compounded by economic hardship, limited livelihood opportunities and an increase in rental prices. The layers of law – both in theory and practice – have played a role in the complex nature of efforts to identify real estate rights; resolve real estate disputes and enforce relevant resolutions or judgments at individual and community levels.

2 - Activities

The overall objective of this project is that conflict affected Libyans are able to claim and exercise their rights vital for achieving durable solutions. This will be achieved through the following activities:General Information Sessions (Legal Information): NRC will provide 1,200 individuals with information, counselling and legal assistance on access to civil documentation and HLP as well as other basic services. This will primarily be done through mobile group information sessions (GIS), NRC will raise awareness on the rights and procedural requirements and how to exercise them. ICLA Officers will deliver GIS in camps and informal settlements. GIS aims to ensure that IDPs are provided with general information on their LCD and/or HLP rights, and how to access those rights, including any related procedural requirements. Counseling and Legal Assistance: Following GIS, NRC will provide 100 beneficiaries who require legal advice tailored to their particular circumstances are invited to one-on-one counselling sessions. The ICLA Officers will also be mobile within camps and non-camp areas to provide information on processes and provide tailored advice to beneficiaries as well as referrals to other service providers. Mobile counselling will cover LCD, HLP rights, procedural requirements and how to exercise them, and accessing other government services. Counselling will aim to provide persons in need with summary legal advice and/or referrals to partners and service providers who are positioned to assist in the given context. Facilitating Access to Essential Services through referrals: NRC will map the greater Tripoli, and Derna areas for all humanitarian actors (international, national, governmental, etc) to make sure persons without access to basic services are able to be connected with them. ICLA will also begin this mapping exercise in new areas that NRC Libya expands to as well as more remote and hard to reach areas through desk research and coordination.
