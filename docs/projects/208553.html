Project Summary

The project will provide access to quality, survivor-centered GBV prevention and response service to women and girls in host communities, IPDs settlements and hard to reach and underserved conflict affected communities focusing on provision of life saving GBV specialize service to address the negative impact of conflict and economic crisis on women and adolescent girls by promoting their access to humanitarian assistance (Provision of Case management, PSS, Dignity kits, Referrals & Safe spaces), and reducing their vulnerability and building their resilience (Skills development, Economic empowerment & Livelihood).
The project aims to promote access to integrated GBV prevention and response services to conflict affected communities in Bade, Geidam and Fune LGAs of Yobe state. The project will achieve this using integrated community-based and survivor centred approach through direct and indirect interventions such as Prepositioning of 1 Dignity Kits production center in (Fune and Bade) and Establiment/Renovation safe spaces in 2 underserves areas, production and prepositioning of Mentruak Hygiene Management supplies to women and girls of reproductive age in flood affected areas, Skils acquisation on production and packaging of dignity kits, economic empowerment and livelihood support to GBV survivors, GBV case management, PSS and referrals, capacity strengthening for service providers and government agencies, Community system strengthening for PSEA including provision of tool free line for access to information on GBV service, and complain and feedback mechanism. GBV prevention through reporting, behavior and communication change (BCC) through Peer2Peer community sensitization, and support for LGA level GBV partners coordination meeting.
Strategically, through collaborative efforts of relevant government/sectoral partners, women and girls from informal settlement and host communities will be trained safety and GBV risk mitigation through access to space spaces and adaptive business skills that are relevant for income generation, local markets and Voluntary Savings and Loans, including self-employment during crisis situations.
Additionally, The project will intervein in Bade Geidam and Fune in Yobe State focusing on women and girls from informal settlement and host communities aiming to reach GBV survivors, marginalized and vulnerable women female-headed households, single women, widows, female adolescent without a family and women with physical disabilities, etc. Other beneficiaries include; community members including local leaders, young people, men and boys and duty holders will also be part of the intervention through sensitization activities among others.g
Furthermore, the project will support institutional strengthening for enhanced GBV service provision through capacity building for partners on GVBiE, GBV mainstreaming, WGSS, OSC, and MPSS. AFRYDEV will further support operationalization of MPSS centre at the State Specialist Hopsiptal by engaging service of MPSS providers to address gap in shortage of manpower. The project will also support community system strengthening for GBV prevention through reporting, Peer2Peer community sensitization to promote behavior and communication change (BCC), and local government level GBV coordination. AFRYDEV will put in place flood contingency plan for prevention and response of GBV in areas prompt to flooding in Geidam and Bade that are badly affected by flood to mitigate worsening of humanitarian crisis in this areas.
The project will ensure context specific approach to the production and distribution of dignity kits through standardization of the dignity kits and at the same time ensure localization of the contents by patronizing the local markets. The project will ensure mainstreaming and integration by providing linkages to other protection sector interventions such as NFIs, WASH and ER&L for a more holistic response.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in NE Nigeria is now in its twelfth year, and an increase in violent attacks over the past year has resulted in further deterioration of the security situation causing mass displacement and worsening of humanitarian needs. The humanitarian context in north-east Nigeria remains challenging with over 7.8 million people in humanitarian need expected for 2024. Children, particularly girls, remain the most affected, with them, along with women, making up 83% of those in need. Vulnerabilities differ based on location, age, and displacement status and interplay of the main drivers (conflict and insecurity, the impact of climate change, disease outbreaks, food insecurity and malnutrition, population movement and camp closures, economic factors, and historical underdevelopment). The protection of civilians remains a major concern. The crisis has devastated civilian infrastructure and livelihoods, leaving many dependent on humanitarian assistance and in urgent need of access to basic services. Protection concerns are pressing, particularly for women and girls. They are threatened by violence, abduction, rape, genderbased violence, forced and child marriage, and other violations of their rights. Women and girls still lack adequate protection, access to justice, and access to services. With significantly decreased humanitarian funding expected in 2024, these vulnerabilities are expected to compound. An estimated 1,829,002 million individuals (32 percent IDPs, 25 per cent returnees, 43 per cent host communities) need GBV interventions across the BAY states. Borno State has the bulk (48 per cent) of thosein need, followed by Adamawa at 34 per cent and 18 per cent in Yobehey bear the brunt of limited humanitarian interventions unless adequate steps are taken. A similar number of host community LGAs were also identified to be in dire need of GBV response services including MHPSS, livelihoods support, access to justice and safe-houseshelter, which are lacking or minimal. These include Gombi, Lamurde, Maiha, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Askira/Uba, Gubio and Gujba. Five LGAs have significant numbers of returnees: Askira/Uba, Chibok, Gubio, Konduga and Mafa. It is worth notingthat incidences of GBV, especially intimate-partner violence (IPV) including sexual violence, continue to be reported across the BAY states, with women and girls disproportionately affected. Trend analysis from the 2022 Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS) report (January-June) highlighted that 99 per cent of the reported incidents of GBV affected women and girls. IPV was reported by 65 percent of survivors who sought help at various service points. In terms of prevalence across the states, Borno had the highest percentage of reported incidents (83 per cent) followed by Adamawa (13 per cent), and Yobe (4 per cent). As previously mentioned, vulnerable people at risk including women and adolescent boys and girls have been forced to start or increase their wage-earning works at an earlier age to cope with economic instability. Adolescent boys and girls, and vulnerable women are also reported increasing pressures to adopt negative coping strategies, with 13.4% of adolescents reporting that they had engaged in at least one negative coping strategy at some point since the start of 2023. Common harmful coping mechanisms among adolescent boys include begging, stealing, and drug abuse, and among adolescent girls harmful coping mechanisms include begging, stealing, engaging in sex for survival, and dropping out of school. Adopting harmful coping strategies has increased safety and protection risks (Nigeria Rapid Gender Analysis 2022) Women and Adolescent girls focus groups reported facing risks such as exploitation, abuse, and sexual violence because of the overlapping crises. To manage rising food insecurity, women and girls reported adopting harmful coping strategies such as reducing their number of meals (70.2%).

2 - Activities

To achieve the objectives of this project, AFRYDEV will support provision of GBV daily case management PSS and referral for specialized service to survivors of GBV in Bade, Geidam and Fune LGAs through engagement of case worker at the women girls safe spaces as well as working collaboratively with general hospitals and Primary health care for PSS service for survivors. AFRYDEV will establish 1 Dignity Kits production centers in (Bade) LGAs and equip the center with supplies such as menstrual hygiene management kits in flood affected areas and other mateial assistance to GBV survivors to support empowerment and women and girls with skills acquisition on tailoring/production reusable menstrual pad and other content of dignity kits, and knowledge of GBV including risk mitigation, safety and security. AFRYDEV will further support renovation 2 women and girls safe space in hard to reach and underserved communities of Fune and Geidam LGAs. AFRYDEV will also conduct capacity building strengthening for Health care workers on provision of MPSS to women and adolescent girls survivors. AFRYDEV will collaborated with Ministry of Health and State Primary Health care Board to train 20 health workers from SARC center and Trauma Unit in the two LGAs on MPSS and GBV Case Management. AFRYDEV will conduct Training of government officials and humanitarian partners/women led organizations on GBV Sector Coordination, including GBV sector core functions, SOPs and referral pathways, while will further support update, validation and dissemination GBV referral pathways. AFRYDEV will support training of 20 women and girls survivors on toiloring and production of dignity kits, and facilitate production and distribution of 500 dignity kits to women and adolescent girls. AFRYDEV will conduct safety and audit vulnerability assessment to inform the development of flood contingency plan for prevention and response of GBV in Geidam and Bade LGAs. AFRYDEV will support economic empowerment of 20 vulnerable women and girls including GBV survivors and provide them with training and start up materials and mentorship on Voluntary Savings and Loan (VSL) including access to market. AFRYDEV will provide 40 stakeholders with training on Community system strengthening for PSEA, and GBV prevention through reporting. AFRYDEV will conduct Community outreach/sensitization on the principles of GBV prevention that address negative social norms to promote behavior and communication change (BCC) through Peer2Peer community sensitization, and Awaereness on PSEA. AFRYDEV will further support for LGA level GBV partners coordination meeting to strengthen GBV prevention and response efforts. AFRYDEV will collaborate with the Yobe State Justice Reform team to conduct 3 days familiarization workshop to government and GBV partners in Damaturu on VAAP Act and Child Protection law to strengthen access to justice for women and girls survivors. AFRYDEV will work collaboratively with FIDA and the Criminal Investigation Department and Gender unit of the Nigerian Police force to provide victims support fund to GBV Survivors for transport from remote areas to health facility, police facility for investigation as well as court for legal proceedings. AFRYDEV will also conduct 1 days women summit to commemorate World menstrual hygiene day and 3 days event to commemorate the 2024 16days of activism.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Fundamental to the operation of African Youths for Peace Development and Empowerment Foundation is respect for the dignity and basic human rights of people within Nigeria and throughout the world. AFRYDEV has a PSEA policy it ensure strict adherence. A robust communication strategy has been in place ensuring every person who represents AFRYDEV reflect the organization principles and values in their professional conduct, regardless of whom they are dealing with, or where they are working. AFRYDEV PSEA policy aims to provide a safe and trusted environment that safeguards everyone from SEA including beneficiary communities, workers, program participants and partner organizations. AFRYDEV is committed to safeguarding the people it helps and who it works alongside. We recognize that the nature of AFRYDEV’s work places our workers and program participants in positions of authority and trust in relation to the communities we work with, especially vulnerable adults and children. AFRYDEV workers and program participants have an obligation to uphold high standards of personal and professional conduct at all times and must not abuse this position in order to exploit or abuse another person. AFRYDEV will actively prevent and respond to SEA and maintain an organizational culture that prioritizes safeguarding against SEA so that it is safe for those affected to come forward and report incidents and concerns with the assurance they will be handled sensitively and appropriately.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

AFRYDEV has a strong Feedback & Complaint mechanism that ensures Capitalization of information coming from the beneficiaries is taken into account during project implementation in order to adapt the action based on the new knowledge acquired. Project activities are tailored to meet the specific needs of all groups (gender/age/people with disabilities) and M&E system is designed to include the perspectives of these groups, to examine the specific issues in the local context and to determine how interventions have a different impact on them. In collecting data, participants should be treated fairly and with dignity, and their interests should be protected. Three basic principles must be adopted: respect, do no harm and non-discrimination. AFRYDEV shall ensure that the beneficiaries are sensitized and informed of their rights to complaints about the implementation from the onset through APP system of reporting established by AFRYDEV.
