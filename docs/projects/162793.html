Project Summary

The proposed 12-month project is designed to meet the needs of nutrition services for internally displaced people and vulnerable host community in Belil locality (Kalma camp sector 6 and 8), Alsalam locality (Bulbul Abujazo, Bulbul Dalal angra, Abusalalah, Barakatoli and Elsafia), Gereida locality (Babanousa, Forica and Old camp and Ditto village) and Dimso locality (Dimso and Eltomat) in South and Assalaya locality (Assalaya), Adilla locality (Adilla, Sharif, Wadjodah and Habib Suleiman), Abukarinka locality (Abukarinka and Hilal), Elferdous locality (Elferdous) and Yassin locality (Yassin and Seleah) in East Darfur states. ARC will provide nutrition services integrated to the primary health care facilities supported by ARC in South and East Darfur states. The nutrition activities will be implemented in an integrated approach with health and WASH. The goal of this project is to contribute to the reduction in nutrition-related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high-quality multi-sectoral life-saving nutrition interventions for children under five and pregnant and lactating women who are below the emergency threshold. The project will support 23 Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programs, 23 Outpatient Therapeutic Programs and 3 Stabilization Centers (SC) including referrals to secondary health care and provision of essential drugs. ARC will follow CMAM approach to implement the intended interventions.
ARC will use the mother support group strategy to promote appropriate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices and Social Behavior Change and Communication (SBCC) among the target communities. ARC will also enhance the technical capacity of ministry of health staff through conducting training on CMAM package, IYCF and training on monitoring and reporting of the nutrition activities.
All activities will be implemented in close collaboration with local institutions/ line ministries and communities such Community Relief and Development Committees (CRDC) to ensure sustainability and active community participation.

1 - Needs

As per the draft of Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020, an estimated 2.7 million children under 5 suffer from acute malnutrition and 5.7 million are food insecure. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate is 14.1 percent, including a severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate of 4.39% and a moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) rate of 9.7%. The percentage of underweight children is 28.9% while those suffering with chronic malnutrition (stunting) is 36.5%. Where pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are concerned, approximately 805,000 women suffer with acute malnutrition (S3M II, 2018). Per the findings of 2018 S3M survey (draft), both South and East Darfur have state level GAM (Global Acute Malnutrition) rates above to the WHO 15% emergency threshold for acute malnutrition at 16.43% and 16.88% respectively. The SAM rates are 2.82% and 3.76% respectively. As per HNO prioritization of the localities based on the severity of key lifesaving sectors (health, wash, nutrition, and FSL), the localities where ARC operational have been classified as following; three localities (Beliel, El salam and Gereida in South Darfur) classified as priority 1 (highest severity 5), five localities (Abukarinka, Adilla, Elferdous and Yassin in East Darfur and Dimso in South Darfur) classified as priority 2 (second highest severity 4) where Assalaya is classified as priority 3 (not under category Priority 1 or Priority 2).. These localities have a GAM and SAM rate ranking of either very high or severe as well as nutrition specific and sensitive indicators in access to food, hygiene, sanitation, safe water and social protection coverage. Stunting (low height-for-age) among children under 5 is also high; 23.85% in South Darfur and 39.3% in East Darfur. The percentage of children aged 0-6 months who are exclusively breastfed is only 69.44% and 66.5% in South and East Darfur States respectively.By August 2019, 5.8 million people were food insecure and in need of food and livelihood assistance. The 5.8 million people included 1 million in IPC phase 4 (humanitarian emergency) and 4.8 million in IPC phase 3 (acute livelihood and food insecurity crises). A further 11.8 million however, were in IPC phase 2 (stress phase). The cost of food and essentials increased leaving vulnerable families unable to afford the most basic items (Sudan IPC Key Messages report, August 2019). Many families were forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms including reduced food intake and smaller food portions thereby decreasing their energy and nutrient intake. Other practices that families resorted to included, liquidation of livelihood assets, early marriage or school drop-out to engage in work. Where children under 2 were concerned, many states failed to achieve age-appropriate dietary diversity for example, the rate of children in East Darfur who failed to achieve age-appropriate dietary diversity was 89.5 percent while in Sennar this figure was 74 percent (S3M II, 2018).

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ARC is focused on implementing systems and methods that reveals customer experience of organizational services so that it can align a response and re-establish trust between ARC, refugee’s the host communities. Trust works in both directions and must be built on the foundation of honesty and mutual accountability. ARC commitment to accountability to the affected population will focus on the following; - governance, transparency, feedback, participation, design, monitoring and evaluation. ARC will work with community structures in increasing the role of the community participation in maintaining the services. In addition, through the established community feedback and response mechanism, ARC will continue to facilitate community consultation meetings to ensure active community participation, transparency and handling community feedback. The community members and beneficiaries provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities provided by ARC and other development partners in their communities in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible to improve accountability across ARC operational areas. ARC’s Feedback/Complaint Response Mechanism (F/CRM) provides a formal channel through which ARC beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can register voices and complaints. The F/CRM is centrally operated through feedback forms and a toll-free hotline number from all mobile phone networks companies (Sudani, Zain and MTN). ARC has developed awareness materials including banners and flyers placed at ARC project service delivery points such as health centers, water points etc. with the hotline number and explanations on how the FCRM works as well as information about our accountability standards and practices to which customers should hold ARC accountable. In addition, through the MEAL department, ARC conducts regular customer interviews and post distribution monitoring using standard survey feedback forms. As per ARC procedures, the analysis of feedback information from communities is fed directly to senior management for action. These mechanisms have proven to be an effective tool in providing beneficiaries and communities with a voice and ultimately improving ARC operations and identifying misconduct within the organization. A well-structured FCRM also enhances lessons learned and good practices across our network. To facilitate use of FCRM, regular orientation sessions are conducted by MEAL and program staff in targeted project sites. In these sessions, beneficiaries and community members learn how they can approach FCRM through interactive discussion.
