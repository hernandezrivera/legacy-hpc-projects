Project Summary

Shafak is set to implement a comprehensive Mine Action project encompassing Risk Education (RE) and Victim Assistance (VA), building upon the success of an existing Explosive Remnants of War Risk Education (ERW-RE) program in Idleb and Aleppo.
Emphasizing continuity, Shafak proposes the seamless continuation of RE activities through existing teams. The organization recognizes the enduring nature of ERW challenges and believes in sustaining long-term impact by instilling safe behaviors in children. Shafak aims to provide ERW-RE to children in communities across Idleb and Aleppo, reinforcing the importance of safe practices in their daily lives.
Under the ERW-RE component, eight teams, each comprising five members including two groups of two and a team leader, already trained by Shafak, will target locations in Idleb and Aleppo. These teams will engage school-age children in RE activities, distributing informative materials about ERW dangers. Additionally, they will extend RE outreach to the older generation by displaying posters in public streets and communities to enhance awareness. Priority will be given to schools and locations previously unreached.
Beyond schools, Risk Education will be extended to IDP camps and host communities. While sessions are open to all community members, special efforts will be made to reach those most vulnerable to accidents, including children, young men, and IDPs unfamiliar with local threats. The teams will conduct sessions in both schools and community spaces, distributing flyers and posters to expand the reach of Risk Education messaging.
Moreover, Two "Training of Trainer" (ToT) specialist will impart RE training to Community Focal Points (CFPs) in affected communities, as well as RE teams and workers from various NGOs. This initiative aims to improve the relevance, integration, and reach of RE messaging across Northwest Syria. The gathered insights will contribute to understanding protection gaps and enhancing future programming, serving as valuable input for advocacy initiatives.
Furthermore, the project will enhance the resilience of People Living with Injuries and Disabilities (PLWDs), particularly ERW survivors, by facilitating referrals to specialized services.
In total, the project aims to reach 131200 individuals through Mine Action services."
In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs. (HNO, Summary, 2024). Within the protection sector, numbers indicate an increase in the number of PiN in 2024 to 2,375,850 in Idleb, and 3,566,142 in Aleppo who are in dire need for protection services.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Shafak's project implementation in Syria, in collaboration with local councils and the Directorate of Education in Idleb and Aleppo, encompasses various activities. This includes the Victim Assistance (VA) component, aiming to establish a case management system through the hiring of four case workers for referrals to specialized services. Additionally, the Risk Education (RE) training will be expanded, reaching RE Community Focal Point volunteers, existing and new RE teams from various NGOs, and internal-to-NGO RE focal points. Shafak will directly train six teams focusing on community liaison, safe behavior messaging, and the use of KoBo, deployed in both Idleb and Aleppo. The teams will conduct RE Awareness Raising Sessions for beneficiaries at the highest risk of ERW accidents. Shafak's expertise will tailor awareness materials for student's families. Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) activities will ensure a comprehensive understanding of the lecture content. The establishment of a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) reflects Shafak's commitment to addressing concerns and receiving community feedback. Protection Risk Analysis identifies risks such as sexual exploitation, tension between IDPs, and trauma effects, with mitigating measures including recruiting respected volunteers, clear communication of selection criteria, and sustained advocacy on explosive hazards. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating measures cover security risks, such as shelling, with solutions like choosing response centers away from military locations and maintaining threat contingency plans. The document emphasizes continuous communication, field supervision adjustments, and competitive hiring practices to address potential challenges. The PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy addresses two identified risks. Firstly, the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by project personnel on project BNFs and women staff is mitigated by providing awareness-raising sessions in the targeted community, training new staff on PSEA, CSG, and conducting refresher trainings for existing staff. Distribution of banners, PSEA reporting mechanisms, including suggestion boxes and a WhatsApp number, further contributes to mitigation. Secondly, the risk of no reporting from SEA survivors is addressed by raising awareness among project BNFs about PSEA and reporting mechanisms, implementing various accessible reporting channels, emphasizing the principle of confidentiality in handling SEA cases, and assigning gender-balanced PSEA focal points to encourage reporting, particularly among women and girls.
