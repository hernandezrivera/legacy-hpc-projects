Project Summary

The project- Strengthening Surveillance, Case Investigation and Rapid Response for COVID-19 in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States of North-East Nigeria is aimed at slowing down the community spread of COVID-19 through stronger surveillance approaches, prompt case investigations and rapid response to alerts. In order to address the increased risk of COVID-19 outbreak within the BAY states of North-east Nigeria, through this project, WHO will support the state governments to enhance epidemic preparedness and capacity building to effectively respond to emergencies in the targeted areas through:
The deployment of mobile technologies at wards and LGAs level to improve disease surveillance and reporting by the deployment of mobile phones. Based on the lessons learned in previous initiatives and leveraging the deployment of health service delivery platforms in the affected communities, WHO will increase capacity of the MHT and CORPs to quickly notify potential outbreaks and alerts through the procurement and distribution of mobile phone equipped with specific alert notification application. The clinician in the MHT who is usually a nurse or midwife will be responsible for reporting and carrying out surveillance. While for the CORPs, which is one-man team, each member will be assigned with responsibility of reporting diseases among children under 5 years of age in their community. The identified personnel will be trained on case definition, detection and timely reporting of diseases of priority through the use of mobile technology disease surveillance.
Surveillance actions will focus on rapid detection of imported cases, comprehensive and rapid contact tracing, and case identification. In a scenario in which sustained community transmission has been detected, the surveillance coverage will be expanded to include monitoring the geographical spread of the virus, transmission intensity, disease trends, characterization of virologic features, and the assessment of impacts on healthcare services. Robust COVID-19 surveillance data are essential to calibrate appropriate and proportionate public health measures. The existing EWARS/IDSR mechanisms with more than 200 reporting sites will be strengthened for timely collection of alerts and disease data from the health facilities.
Actions will target 2,989,473 persons across the BAY states.

1 - Needs

The likelihood of an explosion of COVID-19 in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe is very high, since it is bordering three countries (Cameroon, Chad and Niger) and there is a constant movement of travelers from these neighboring countries for trade as well as conflict around the lake Chad Basin. The impact caused by imported Covid-19 cases in these three States is estimated to be high due to lack of previous experience in managing the disease and the large numbers of internationals working in the aid industry in the region. Although the country has the capacity to confirm COVID-19, event-based surveillance and community surveillance are not fully established in these States and Laboratory confirmation must be undertaken in Abuja. There has been no previous experience of such disease in Nigeria and in the North-eastern part of the country. Despite the presence of capacity for laboratory confirmation of EVD within the country, challenges in community-based surveillance and lack of ideal isolation facilities may contribute to increased risk of spread. In general, the risk for COVID-19 increases with more international staff from affected COVID-19 countries working and supporting in the humanitarian work in the BAY states, possibility of community spread, combination of lack of adequate health care, overcrowding due to new conflict related displacement, including the influx of IDPs, refugees and returnees raise from Cameroon,(affected country) the risks of outbreaks of epidemic prone diseases such as cholera, Yellow fever hepatitis, cerebrospinal meningitis and measles.

2 - Activities

The existing community-based surveillance (CBS) will be strengthened and expanded in all accessible areas. The presence or rapid deployment of an outbreak rapid response team needs to be ensured in all camps and hosting communities for investigation of alerts and referrals of suspected cases for diagnostics, potential isolation and case management. Community mobilization teams and health volunteers are important allies for the early detection of COVID-19 cases in camps and host communities. CBS is an important intervention for contact monitoring, even in the absence of quarantine and isolation. Community health volunteers and other community elements of the camps and host communities will be trained on a simplified case definition and alert notification procedures. Case investigation needs to be ensured following alert notification. Surveillance actions will focus on rapid detection of imported cases, comprehensive and rapid contact tracing, and case identification. In a scenario in which sustained community transmission has been detected, the surveillance coverage will be expanded to include monitoring the geographical spread of the virus, transmission intensity, disease trends, characterization of virologic features, and the assessment of impacts on healthcare services. Robust COVID-19 surveillance data are essential to calibrate appropriate and proportionate public health measures. The existing EWARS/IDSR mechanisms with more than 200 reporting sites will be strengthened for timely collection of alerts and disease data from the health facilities. Efforts and resources at points of entry (POEs) including Maiduguri international airport, Yola airport and all entry points on international border will focus on supporting surveillance and risk communication activities. The key actions are development of action plan, training of port health authority staff, establishment of isolation facility at the airport and transportation of suspected passengers to treatment centers, dissemination of IEC materials to passengers and monitoring of the POE screening process for effective and necessary adjustments.
