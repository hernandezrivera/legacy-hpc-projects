Project Summary

POINT organization aims to enhance the resilience of affected communities in Idleb – Dana area in NWS and northern Aleppo – Mare and Aghtrin areas through Increasing livelihood opportunities and to have community resilience strengthened, and socio-economic vulnerabilities of communities affected by the COVID-19 pandemic and economic deterioration reduced.
To achieve that, Point aims to target 4 communities to support the livelihoods of vulnerable crises- affected communities, by starting with Market mapping which aims to identify market gaps and needs, and then announce and selection of participants according to eligibility and vulnerability criteria and giving priorities to women-headed HHs and later to provide vocational training for 320 persons on topics that will be identified according to the market mapping with giving priorities to vocations related to PPEs production and other locally produced general covid19 protection related items such as hygiene items.
After finishing the VT, Point will deliver SME training for the successful applicants considering their attendance percentage, test results and trainers’ evaluations according to which each participant will submit a business plan which will be evaluated by a formed committee.
Each successful business plan will be supported with cash grants of 1200$ for the first 200 best plans to be paid on three tranches. Each tranche will be 400$ which will be delivered either by directly by POINT finance team as the value of the tranche is not too big and the documentation will be handled by POINT, or when applicable, POINT can contract with money transfer service provider to deliver the payments like (PTT).
After delivering the grants for the 200 participants, Point team will be mentoring and coaching for the established businesses and the MEAL team will be monitoring the activities and later will conduct an end-line evaluation.
The remaining 120 participants will be supported with job-related kits of 400$ each to help them engage in the market and work. These participants attended all the vocational training and the SME training but their business plans couldn't get high scores to be funded will be supported with job-related kits. The kits will be procured by POINT according to its policies and will be delivered to the beneficiaries by its logistic team according to POINT templates and procedures.
Besides the above mentioned activities, Point will be focusing on the rehabilitation of key access roads to the most-in-need IDPs sites and on the reparation of damaged drainage infrastructures outside camps which were affected by floods and that are essential to prevent calamities from threatening IDPs lives, and this goes in line with the ERL cluster approach that aims to respond to the Covid-19 for which is the rehabilitation/restoration of access to basic utilities, local economic infrastructure, and other social infrastructures through access ways/roads to and from camps (light rehabilitation of critical access ways to overburden camps and communities to facilitate in/out movements and humanitarian assistance)
These roads will be identified according to the list that was prepared shared by CCCM cluster and its partners that were badly affected by the last floods. The project aims to improve access of humanitarian assistance to camps and prevent new floods in the coming winters.
To achieve this aim, Point plans to rehabilitate and maintain roads in Idleb-Dana area (in Atma and Karma) for (7 KM) in strict coordination with CCCM and ERL clusters and partners to enhance the economic situation of the camp residents, and improve access to the camps by providing easy transit roads that support the transport of goods to the camps and pushes the economy in the region, which leads to improving the conditions of the population in an economic level. Moreover, POINT will work on reparation and rehabilitation of drainage systems outside the camps to prevent floods.

4 - Needs

As per 2021 humanitarian needs overview (HNO), More than 90 percent of the Syrian population is now estimated to live below the poverty line. The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated this economic downturn by further reducing already sparse income-generating opportunities in a context where 50 percent of the work age population is now estimated unemployed, 300,000 jobs lost since the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic,Moreover, A distinct category has emerged, the working poor, households unable to meet the cost of basic needs despite one or more members of the family are working.As per the UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment, before the pandemic, 41.9 percent of households could cover their expenses, while currently, only 9.7 percent can do so. This is exacerbated by a severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital.Due to COVID-19, there has been a decrease of 19.8 percent in average employees in the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) sector. In particular, there was a decrease in employment of 18.2 percent for males and 19.7 percent for females. Most enterprises lost significant revenue ranging from 40 percent to 100 percent, and about 13 percent had to close their operations.According to an assessment conducted by Point for the roads in NWS in Dana subdistrict, and as per the CCCM and ERL clusters reports and several reports from the field, many roads have been badly affected by the floods last winter and they became completely or partially damaged and caused some access to camps to be blocked or difficult, and most of the drainage systems need rehabilitation and maintenance to avoid future floods.

5 - Implementation of activities

market assessment will be conducted which aims to identify the key gaps in skills, and disruptions in the local market.Announcement for the training in collaboration with the local actors and through the local active social media channels.selection of participants according to eligibility and vulnerability criteria and giving priorities to women-headed HHs and most vulnerable persons.Conduct vocational training for 320 participants in the selected topics based on the labor market assessment (two months duration each) and includes theoretical and practical in site training. Conduct SME training for 5 days to enable female and male beneficiaries to prepare business plans that include all aspects of managing the expansion or establishment of small businesses. evaluate and select of best business plans by an evaluation committee and put scores for each submitted business proposal according to a set of criteria.signing MOUs with accepted business ideas applicants which contains all elated information and commitments of each party.delivering the grants’ payments: each grant will be divided into 3 payments to guarantee the commitment of the beneficiary to establish the proposed business.mentoring and coaching, end line evaluation.participants who attended all the vocational training and the SME training but their business plans couldn't get high scores to be funded will be supported with job-related kits of 400$ each to help them engage in the market and work.rehabilitation of the access routes to IDPs camps, by rehabilitating 7 km of key access roads to the most-in-need IDPs sites and repairing the damaged drainage infrastructures outside camps by contracting with a contractor through a tendering process.

Sharing the selection criteria with the local community before starting with the implementation.Protection risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance grants among beneficiaries. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Point will share the developed selection criteria for participant and the grants mechanism including the proposed projects’ assessment criteria.Risk : Due to social norms, girls’ parents may not allow them to attend as planned; the training activities and the mini-grants may lead to increased risk. Mitigation: announcing and community sensitization for the project in the name of Point under the umbrella of Killis education directorate will increase the level of trust on the project and will allow women to participate safely.
