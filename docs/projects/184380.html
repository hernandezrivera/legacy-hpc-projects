Project Summary

Through this intervention, TSS intends to support the IDPs, host subsistence farmers and fishermen in Bosaso and Qandala districts to increase their livelihood self-sufficiency. An assessment done by TSS team established that more than 650 HHs (3,900 individuals) in Bosaso depend on fishing for their livelihood. Fishermen however receive little or no support from the government or International Organizations. They indicated many challenges including that had not received any training on fishing or related fields. TSS intends through this project to support 200 fishermen HH with training and equipments to enhance their livelihoods.
Covid'19 pandemic remain a great threat to the community especially to the elder persons that compose a substantial number of the population. It is critical to support the most affected households to begin to restore lost crop agriculture or fishing livelihoods, thus enabling them to have increased food availability through personal production, as well as a small income for increased food access. The project targets 5,000 HHs of Bosaso and Kandara (2000 M, 2000 F, 500 Boys, 500 Girls). Of these, 500HHs (400 M, 1000 F) from closer to the sea will be supported with fishing kits which avails immediate benefits by providing white meat for the HHs(fish) as well as providing an alternative source of income through the sale of surplus fish. This income will be used to supplement the HHs food requirements as well as address their urgent needs. To ensure maximum realization of benefits as well as ensure sustainability, HHs will be trained on improved fish production practices. The selected households will be further disintegrated into vulnerability cadres to include people with disability, lactating and expectant mothers and elderly people. The selected fisher folks shall be strengthened on better fish harvesting and handling techniques. They shall be trained on environment friendly fish catching methods (correct net sizes and hooks), fish hygiene, preservation, packaging and marketing. The households will be supported to increase their immediate food stock and have their knowledge of basic food production enhanced to boost their food preserve and stores to sustain them through the lean period. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to reserve seeds for the following planting season using both known traditional and conventional methods. A further 2500 HHs (1500 F, 1000 M) will be supported with vegetable kits to address their nutritional requirement in the short-term (early 3-weeks). Income from the sale of surplus produce will be used to purchase alternative food requirements such as rice as well as supplement the HH dietary needs. Farmers will receive basic training on improved agronomic practices with regards to micro-irrigation of small-scale vegetable production and Covid'19 awareness lessons. All 5000 HHs will be supported with crop kits and farming tools for the main cropping season thereby increasing the farmers’ access to quality seeds and tools.
Sound contextual analysis was carried out to ensure that the risks and needs of people in the most vulnerable situations are identified in a holistic, focused and accurate manner and that the drivers of vulnerability are comprehensively analysed and understood. Special attention was paid to the needs of the people most at risk, taking into account that there may be many layers of vulnerability such as the probability of violation, abuse, harm, suffering, disadvantage, inclusion and discrimination. Geography socioeconomic status, coping mechanisms and power structures including gender, age, disability and conflict dynamics.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The elderly and the disabled are very much in need of safety nets, TSS will use cash transfers in order to efficiently and effectively reach these vulnerable groups. Also some identified women and child headed HH will require immediate support to redeem them from hunger and malnutrition, hence the cash transfer programming will serve as safety net to them too. Items like seeds, tools and fishing gears will be distributed through voucher redemption. Cash-based interventions, where feasible, are often a more dignified way of assisting affected populations, as they empower people to determine their own needs and the best way of meeting them. They can also promote peaceful co-existence with host communities, as well-designed and run cash-based interventions have a multiplier effect, directly benefiting the local economy

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

TSS project design probed and considered a variety of market functionality by interrogating a wide rage of issues, -Consultations with local stakeholders during the selection process to verify staple and preferred foods, particularly in cash or voucher programs where beneficiaries will make purchasing decisions themselves. Some things to consider: • What are the main commodities in the staple food basket? • What commodities are prioritized by the intervention, through direct distribution, vouchers, procurement or education/messaging? • What prioritized or preferred commodities have precarious availability in the market? • What commodities may be substituted or complemented by the intervention? • If distributed commodities are not found in local market, what are the closest substitutes? The level of risk that TSS Cash intervention poses to local prices was also analyzed and found that it could be determined by a number of factors: • Size of intervention and presence of other programs • Potential external risk • Modality: in-kind distribution, cash or voucher, local or regional procurement • Timing of intervention in seasonal calendar • Availability of market baseline • Market integration • Market competition • Households reliance on markets for food purchases. TSS categorized the Risks into high risk and low risk and possible mitigation measures, formed inclusive and gender representative feedback small focus groups discussions making effort to obtain information specific to men, women, girls and boys to minimize the possibility of overlooking specific needs related to age and sex to capture and document the mitigation measures for the above identified risks
