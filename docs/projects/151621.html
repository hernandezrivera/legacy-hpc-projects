Project Summary

This project will target IDPs and other highly vulnerable populations with an integrated package of health and nutrition interventions to prevent mortality and reduce morbidity and malnutrition, especially focusing on integrated IMAM, maternal/child focused primary healthcare, and hygiene/sanitation interventions. The project will integrate limited protection interventions to address the needs of vulnerable girls, boys, and women. The project will focus on IDP or urban populations in Baidoa town, Afgooye Corridor (Mogadishu and Afgooye District of Lower Shabelle) and accessible areas of Bay and Bakool including Dinsoor, Xudur, Wajiid, and Ceel Barde.

Needs

Based on FSNAU post-Gu Survey 2018, IDP Populations in Mogadishu, Afgooye Corridor, and Baidoa remain food insecure (IPC 3), indicating their high level of vulnerability, and continue to exhibit critical rates of malnutrition and high morbidity, driven by inadequate access to basic WASH services, primary healthcare and nutrition services, leaving them at high risk for disease outbreaks. Women and girls face highly elevated risks of GBV due to conditions in IDP camps; children face risks of child labor or being left at home while parents seek work or aid; adolescents face risks of forced recruitment by armed groups. Women and children make up the majority of IDPs, and female headed households and unaccompanied or separated children/adolescents, older persons and persons with disabilities have multiplied vulnerability in displaced settings. In SW State, IDPs and other populations living in the districts of Dinsoor, Xudur, Wajiid, and Ceel Barde face a similar situation. A recent rapid assessment by SOS (September 2018) found that nutrition and health services were inadequate to meet needs, with government and NGOs struggling to maintain consistent quality services. The assessment indicated that the most vulnerable within these districts, including IDPs, are suffering from high rates of morbidity, and are in urgent need of WASH, health and nutrition interventions to address morbidity and prevent outbreaks of disease like cholera and measles. Isolation due to AS presence between towns makes these conflict-affected populations particularly vulnerable, especially to outbreaks. Furthermore, CRS anticipates possible return of IDPs from other areas to these districts, exacerbating the need for scale up of basic services. The target populations/locations described here all represent potential Cholera hotspots. Scale-up of integrated assistance is critical to save lives, reduce malnutrition, and enhance the protection of the most vulnerable in these locations.

Activities

In Nutrition: 1) OTP and TSFP integrated with each MCH, providing IMAM services with referral to SC as needed. 2) CHWs to provide screening and referral in the community as well as case follow up. 3) Micronutrient Supplementation for children and PLW. In Protection: 1) Provision of PSS to GBV survivors by a counselor attached to the MCH. 2) Distribution of dignity kits to survivors of GBV. 3) Creation of Child Friendly Spaces at MCH or service provision points in community locations – this will facilitate access to health/nutrition services for women who cannot leave children at home, and will provide PSS for children. In addition, this will provide a entry point in the community for sensitization, screening/referral, etc.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk of conflict with host communities: - The project will mitigate this by providing services to vulnerable persons without respect to IDP/host community status. Both IDP and Host community leaders will be consulted and involved in the project cycle. Risk of GBV, especially sexual violence, for women and girls who are displaced: - The project will mitigate this risk by integrating into health/nutrition/hygiene sensitization efforts messages around GBV prevention and where survivors can seek services. The project will train staff including CHWs to report/refer survivors of GBV, and will sensitize communities that they can come to any health facility or service provision point to receive assistance, and can use project accountability mechanisms to report GBV. This will make communities safer and service provision safer, so that women and girls at risk of GBV are less likely to be excluded from accessing service due to this fear. Risks to women, girls, boys and men who must walk long distances to access services (This presents a number of risks to every group – for women and girls GBV is one of the risks raised – but there are many others. For children this raises the risk of children being left alone while caregivers access services). - The project will locate health/nutrition services in areas close to the most vulnerable populations, using mobile service provision if necessary, to prevent individuals walking long distances, and facilitating access to services. - The project will construct adequate infrastructure in locations that are close enough to facilitate safe access by displaced populations and other populations in greatest need.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risks that some groups will not be able to access infrastructure safely and with dignity: - The project will construct infrastructure that is gender-separated and marked as such, lockable from the inside, safely located within settlements or communities so women/girls/children/vulnerable groups are not forced to walk unsafe distances to access; An adequate percentage of latrines and handwashing stations at facilities can be safely and easily accessed by children, older persons, and persons with disabilities. CRS and implementing partner will ensure gender balance in staffing and that staff are representative of target populations. All staff will undergo protection mainstreaming training and sign a code of conduct meeting UN requirements, including mandatory reporting for PSEA. Risks that caregivers, especially women and caregivers who are children/adolescents will not be able to access services without leaving children unattended. - The project will construct child-friendly spaces at or near service provision points, facilitating and encouraging parents to have children accompany them to access services. The project will implement a robust feedback loop in line with the AAP framework, to ensure all groups (men, women, older and younger adults and adolescents -- including marginalized groups and various social groups) are able to provide and receive feedback from the project. Indicators in the monitoring plan will track data disaggregate by sex and age where applicable . The monitoring plan will include indicators directly tracking effectiveness of the feedback mechanism, and feedback received from multiple mechanisms will be formally incorporated into the monitoring plan for review by project management to inform adaptive implementation and included in reporting. Targeting of service provision points will involve participatory processes to ensure all groups can access, especially the most vulnerable and in greatest need.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project will provide a critical PSS intervention through child-friendly spaces that will help mitigate a number of negative effects of armed conflict / violence on children. This will provide a community entry point for the project to engage children and caregivers with health, nutrition, and hygiene sensitization and interventions (like screening/referral) but will also allow the project to identify and refer vulnerable children and adults who may have experienced or are at risk for rights violations.
