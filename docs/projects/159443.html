Project Summary

Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH), together with its national partner organisations, will focus on the assistance of people in need with live saving and inclusive interventions, targeting mostly IDPs and host communities in acute water shortage, and preparedness for and mitigation of risks that have deep impact on the lives of people and their livelihoods. DKH interventions include preparedness, response and recovery activities. In all activities, feedback and complaint mechanisms will be installed to fulfil DKH’s accountability. People with disabilities are included in the targeting on hh and community level to ensure their access to humanitarian WASH services.
DKH will make use of Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) whenever feasible. In all CVA modalities, DKH implements market assessments following the MISMA and EMMA tools. Retailers are identified and interviewed for an intake to a network of retail-suppliers that accept payment through vouchers. Retailers are contracted for the redemption of restricted vouchers if they offer relevant relief items that they either have in adequate quantity in store or available within 24 hours from wholesalers, and if they have the financial capacity to advance a significant number of vouchers before getting reimbursed. Beneficiaries are identified for vouchers on hh-level with an electronic questionnaire based on vulnerability criteria and needs and run through the KOBO tool, for unconditional multi-purpose cash through IPC, and for cash-for-work/assets through a community approach. Vouchers are handed out to the beneficiaries personally; cash is transferred through mobile money. After each distribution, post distribution monitoring is done and recorded.
Following these approaches, DKH will provide unconditional and restricted water vouchers to households that are affected by acute water shortage during draughts, and thus maintaining existing water logistic and economic structures of the private sector, but maintaining the purchase power of the end-user of water supply systems to ensure their survival. Rehabilitation of community operated boreholes cum training in Management & Organisation (M&O) and Operation and Maintenance (O&M) for the WASH committees is planned to address acute water shortages. Hygiene education campaigns shall accompany all water and sanitation activities. Hygiene kits will be distributed to menstruating girls in education. In congested informal settlements camp cleaning campaigns shall be done through ffw and latrines shall be de-sludged by making use of small-scale enterprises that use equipment which can access the latrines. Disposal of liquid waste shall be only at allowed sites. Solid waste in IDP settlements shall be collected and disposed or recycled at designated places. DKH’s partner SYPD is prepared to intervene in case of floods of the Shabelle River with distribution of treated and tested water from the river to displaced people in Beletweyn and Jowhar where two EmWatKits are prepositioned. Emergency latrines shall also be provided to the same people. Construction and rehabilitation of water collection and conservation structures, also funded under livelihood projects, for the access to (sub-) surface water such as water pans, sand dams, shallow wells, berkads, and the creation of multiple community water resource management committees are activities to assist communities to prepare and to mitigate climate induced shocks. Beneficiaries shall become sensitized for the treatment of water from unsafe sources for human consumption on hh level with water filters, cleaning of containers at the inside. Water samples from the hhs will be tested at random and tested with DKH's own water testing kit. In informal settlements, the project addresses the protection issue of women and girls through installation of solar lights on the way to toilets and inside the toilets. The project targets 30000 beneficiaries, sometimes with multiple activities.

Mitigation Measures

3A. ~mitigation1)The project will ensure the location &access routes to WASH facilities &distribution points are safe. WASH committees and stakeholders involved will identify communal water sources for rehabilitation & prioritize on those sources in visible/central locations and not more than 500meters from settlements as recommended by WASH cluster. The project will coordinate with DKH’s Shelter/NFI project to deliver provision of lighting near communal facilities and household solar lamps.2)The project will ensure hygiene kit size is appropriate for beneficiaries to carry home with no additional physical/financial burden without dependency on others to carry for them 3)The project will ensure all WASH staff, IPs, volunteers &people working with affected populations understand, sign &adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. 4) In cooperation with protection actors, the project will enhance the capacity of community WASH committees to monitor, report and refer protection concerns in accordance with standard operating procedures 5)The project will ensure the design and construction of the WASH facilities will maintain the safety and dignity of its users by building gender segregated latrines and bathing facilities for males and females making sure they are clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users. The facilities will be provided with locks for privacy3C. ~mitigation 1)The project will ensure the mode and frequency of distributions in particular menstrual hygiene kits to women and girls minimize exposure to safety threats like intimidation, extortion and sexual assault on their way home from distribution points. The distributions will consider the frequency,location and timing of the distributions by ensuring the distributions occur much earlier in the day and in safe locations where the access routes are safe to use. 2)In cases of overcrowding at water points, the WASH committees will schedule time shifts for water collection in consultation with beneficiaries, recognizing the different gender roles coming to play. Women &girls will be given priority &scheduled for earlier shifts to allow them have ample time to carry their water home without risking their safety. 3)The project will ensure the hygiene promotion services are respectful and inclusive of cultural and religious practice and a proportionate number of female hygiene promoters to enable women and girls to access them. 4)The project will work with protection actors to enhance the capacity of community WASH committees to monitor, report and refer protection concerns like sexual violence against women and girls. 5)Clear information to the distribution teams, WASH committees and the affected communities explaining that beneficiaries do not pay for or provide services/favors to anyone in exchange for receiving WASH assistance will be shared ensuring this is communicated to women and girls3D. ~mitigation 1)The project aims to work with WASH committees and avoid community tensions between camp &host communities. The project will include host communities in decisions on WASH needs 2)The project will avoid community tensions by providing the host community with access to WASH camp assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic WASH services &facilities. 3)The project will enhance the existing complaints &feedback mechanism to be accessible, confidential &well understood to gather suggestions, complaints, community perceptions &promoting empowerment. The community can submit feedback on existing relations 4)The identified information on power dynamics will be used to inform of any existing barriers to peaceful coexistence within the communities and support in identifying ‘who’ is able to influence decisions on WASH interventions. The rehabilitated WASH assets can be used as platforms for fostering a spirit of unity.

Please provide further explanation

The project focuses on providing immediate support to displaced communities including host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) affected by drought, floods and insecurity. The project notes that the impact of displacement on host communities is particularly important in the target communities because displaced people are often hosted in under-served and marginalized areas where absorption capacity can be limited. There the project will achieve resilience as IDPs in the long run will no longer have specific assistance and protection needs linked to their displacement and can exercise their rights without discrimination resulting from their displacement.The program puts displacement affected communities at the center of the response through approaches aimed at ensuring that the immediate human needs are dealt with. Through the implementing partners the programme aligns itself to the Somali National Development Plan so that government engagement and agreed policies are at the heart of response through engaging the administration in each area.The program is further developed through participatory approaches to avoid bias or discrimination between IDPs and hosts. This approach has therefore enabled the program to develop area-based approaches for planning and coordination which recognize the importance of the specific context and which build upon preexisting coordination networks as well as lessons on what is not currently working and why. DKH will be supporting capacity development of the local implementing partners SYPD, DBG, CPD and NAPAD to enhance and facilitate local leadership.
