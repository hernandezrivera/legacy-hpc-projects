Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to assist 578,602 people in vulnerable communities with humanitarian food assistance, Emergency livelihood support and resilience sustenance in Upper Nile, and Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Project interventions to be implemented will prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and improve coping strategies for the target beneficiaries in IPC 3+ locations. Moreover, the project seeks to enhance emergency food production and contribute to the reduction in dependency on food and agricultural inputs. World Vision will adopt a combination of intervention approaches including unconditional food Assistance (in-kind and cash) provided to IPC4+, emergency livelihood support to IPC3+ target communities, and resilience building at the household and community level-this intervention prioritizes livelihood productivity, creating market access and value addition, support on non-farm and anticipatory action interventions. For greater impact WV will implement complementary interventions (health, nutrition, WASH, and protection) from different donors and in the targeted locations, while maintaining coordination and complementarity with the efforts of other humanitarian actors and in alignment with line ministries. In order to reach the target beneficiaries with the prioritized cluster objectives, World Vision seeks to raise a total of $2,500,000 for the 2025 project budget.

1. Needs

According to recently released IPC report, November 2024, South Sudan is experiencing deteriorating food insecurity and malnutrition as a result of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks – primarily widespread flooding – and conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is contributing its part for the deteriorating situation. The same report shows that the food insecurity from November 2024 to March 2024 remain high and severe with a projected 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). In the peak season, however, the projected number shoot up to 7.69 million (April to July 2025). The key drivers for food insecurity in the target locations include economic crisis- the economy is experiencing a macroeconomic crisis that is characterized by worsening currency depreciation, high food prices and declining household purchasing power; conflict and insecurity- forced displacement, interruptions in humanitarian aid delivery, and localized conflict and insecurity especially in the Greater Tonj area of Warrap, Luakpiny/Nassir County of Upper Nile, and parts of Jonglei, Lakes, Central and Western Equatoria states; climatic shocks- floods and dry spells contribute to low agricultural production. Flooding was 10 percent higher in2024 compared to 2023 and the floods not only displaced people but also disrupted delivery of humanitarian support, and contributed to low access to health services in the face of increased disease burden driven poor sanitation practices thus, increasing the risk of acute malnutrition; and low agricultural production- unpredictable and extreme weather patterns, poor access to quality seeds and use of traditional farming methods negatively impacted agricultural production, resulting in increased humanitarian food assistance needs.WV aims to reach the most vulnerable populations in Warrap, Upper Nile and Northen Bahr el Ghazal states with food assistance, emergency food production through support of agricultural and fisheries inputs, and to support and strengthen households’ ability to bounce back from disaster in partnership with different donors including WFP, FAO, BHA, BMZ, GFFO, ANCP/DFAT, GAC, UNDP, and WB. These donors will continue implementing humanitarian cash and food, livelihood support, and resilience interventions in the target locations.

2. Project activities

In addressing the priority humanitarian needs in South Sudan, WV will align implementation to FSL cluster objectives: - Cluster Objective (CO1): Emergency Food Assistance: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5). WV will implement: General food distribution; Cash and voucher distribution (unconditional); and GFD+; Humanitarian livelihood support; Focus on seasonal food assistance with other multi sector integrated actions (Protection, WASH, Nutrition & Health actors); Post distribution monitoring (PDM).Cluster Objective (CO2): Emergency livelihood support: This includes emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support. WV will implement the following activities: Emergency food production; main and dry season agriculture seeds and tool distribution; fish, vegetable and crop kits; Emergency food & livelihood support; Fishing kits/ vegetable kits; Livestock vaccination; disease prevention &treatment; Vaccination campaign support; Small ruminants restocking; capacity building efforts, household benefiting from VSLA to boost up HHs income.Cluster Objective (CO3): This is a resilience building effort where the intervention attempts to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs by supporting and strengthening households’ ability to absorb stressors. Under this objective, WV will implement the following activities: Capacity building effort (CAHWs and ESP), provision of irrigation pumps and ox-ploughs to enhance vegetable and crop production all season round, support market functionality and support value chain addition; support saving groups and income generation (business skills), and vocational training; support post-harvest losses management including introduction of appropriate technologies; firm up the use of climate smart agriculture; empower community in disaster risk management. The project results will be monitored through a MEAL plan which consist of Indicator Tracking Tables (ITT), Detailed Implementation Plan (DIP), M&E plan and project tracker. This tracking is done on a regular basis (monthly, quarterly, and annually) with results measured and quantified. World Vision South Sudan uses a results-based management to deliver project results. The project results will be closely monitored through a dedicated monitoring and evaluation system, consisting of tested tools such as Indicator Tracking Tables, Detailed Implementation Plan, and project tracker. The following key indicators, among others, will be closely monitored: - # of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers - # of people receiving seeds and tools - # of people receiving fishing kits - # of people trained in improved agronomic practices - # of livestock vaccinated and treated - Proportion of people with improved dietary diversity - % increase in food consumption/income - Proportion of people with low Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES). During reporting, the project outputs and outcomes [PDM and Annual Outcome Monitoring (AOM)] will be measured based on the above indicators whose values will be tracked in a rolling basis.
