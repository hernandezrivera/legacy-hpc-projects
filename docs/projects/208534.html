Project Summary

DRC will provide MPCT to new arrivals, people affected by disasters and people referred with different protection needs in MMC and Jere LGAs. This modality will allow beneficiaries to focus on addressing their immediate needs and will prevent negative coping mechanisms. MPCT beneficiaries will subsequently be integrated into other longer-term assistance. By doing so, DRC will ensure that new arrivals can start recovering and rebuilding their lives.

1 - Needs

In more than 13 years of conflict, affected people in north-east Nigeria have experienced violence, repeated displacement, loss of and/or separation from family members, destruction of property and livelihood assets and a deterioration in living conditions. Their resilience has been tested over and over, with children, women, female-headed households, people with disabilities and older persons enduring the most suffering and becoming even more vulnerable.Over the years, floods have become more severe across Nigeria, including in the BAY states, displacing people, destroying or damaging infrastructure, and heightening the risk of waterborne diseases such as cholera and increasing the incidence of malaria. According to the State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA), flooding in 2022 impacted more than 37,000 households in Borno state, 32,000 in Yobe and nearly 30,000 in Adamawa. The National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) reported that a total of 279,000 people were affected by flooding in the BAY states, with 133 people reported to have died, 73,000 displaced, and 49,000 houses damaged. Some 38,000 hectares of farmland were destroyed. As many as 73 per cent of IDPs in camps, 53 per cent of IDPs out of camps, 38 per cent of returnees, and 42 per cent of communities hosting IDPs do not own any productive assets.16 The Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) for 2023 highlighted higher debt levels among conflict-affected households in 2022. Some 62 per cent of respondents incurred debt to meet basic needs, compared to 53 per cent the previous year. For many IDPs, returnees and host communities, spikes in food and commodity prices (including agricultural inputs) and the scarcity of livelihood opportunities have limited their ability to either procure or grow food, and access health care, shelter and/or education services.

2 - Activities

The action will target vulnerable people living in conflict-affected communities, regardless of their status – Internally Displaced People (IDPs), host communities, and returnees. However, while PiN will be identified through different channels based on their vulnerability and on their protection concerns, the partners anticipate that most of them will be extremely vulnerable internally displaced people affected by the government-mandated camp closures in Maiduguri and, most recently Monguno, living in host communities, with specific focus on households headed by women, children or PwD. Needs assessment/protection monitoring reports and evidence from feedback mechanisms will be used to determine the prioritization of communities in the targeted LGAs, while the collaboration with community structures and parasocial workers will constitute a first entry point to pre-identify potential PiN. The assessments will integrate strong protection mainstreaming to ensure the beneficiaries are selected based on their protection situation, and that the response provided contributes to improve it; especially, the joint assessments will evaluate the protection-sensitive negative coping strategies adopted by the beneficiaries to cope with the lack of access to basic needs. Activity 1: Households eligible for all sectoral assistance will be provided with a multipurpose cash transfer, when the market assessment confirms cash is the most adapted way to respond to humanitarian needs in the areas of intervention. In MMC and Jere LGAs, where cash is particularly adapted, MPCT will allow DRC to cover the gaps observed for protracted displaced people, as they are often deprioritized by other external partners, and to complete the in-kind assistance they already received through other grants. Activity 2: DRC will verify that minimum requirements are met as a precondition to deliver cash assistance; emergency needs in targeted communities, markets must be functional and have the capacity to provide the goods and services beneficiaries need to meet the identified needs, cash transfers are safely accessible and accepted modality for beneficiaries to receive assistance. DRC will align the cash transfer value of the MPCT with the latest Nigeria CWG guidance which is currently 69,312 NGN (approx. 81 EUR) for a household of 6 with an amount of 11,552 NGN per capita. DRC will disburse the cash transfers based on household size with household size 1 to 3 receiving a fixed rate of household of 3 while household size 8 or more will receive a fixed rate of HH size 8. Households size from 4 to 7 will receive cash transfer value per capita. The purpose of the financial support is to enable the MPCT recipients to cover their basic needs and reduce their use of negative coping strategies and therefore DRC will be providing the beneficiaries with a monthly MPCA for three months. Activity 3: DRC will conduct a Rapid Market Assessment before and after the MPCT distribution to measure the impact on the market due to the cash inflow in the communities. The monthly market monitoring of the main commodities in the Minimum Expenditure Basket will demonstrate that the market are accessible and able to absorb the increase in demand and goods are available. The monitoring of prices and its fluctuation trends will be shared with the Cash Working Group (CWG) and will be analyzed against the MEB can the cash transfer value. Activity 4: DRC MEAL team will conduct a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) 30 days after each cash distribution.
