Project Summary

"The previous phase of the intervention has contributed to an increase in the appropriate usage of handwashing. The respondents from the end of project assessment reported that handwashing points installed under the project were utilized for hygiene maintenance. In terms of safe and inclusive access to water, the water point accessibility for people with disabilities, pregnant women, adolescent girls and aged persons has been enhanced. The increase in ease of accessibility for pregnant women adolescent girls, and people with disabilities, it improved from 11% to 55%. So, maintenance of these critical, and essential WaSH facilities and services are vital to reduce the risk of disease outbreak.
Keeping in mind the gravity of the issue, Christian Aid along with it’s implementing partner will continue to ensure lifesaving water and sanitation, Hygiene services, Solid Waste Management, and FSM facilities to the Rohingya and host communities in 2023. Focus will be provided to address the key challenges facing WaSH service such as congestion at water points, unequal distribution, inclusive access, and protection concerns in bathing cubicles and latrines."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Gaps/Needs are Dirty and dysfunctional latrines as well as insufficient sanitation facilities were a common concern across all camps and host communities. The affected community during the monsoon seasons caused widespread flooding to the camps and host community areas, resulting in a request for support from DPHE to assist in the assessment and the response. The camps and host community population was affected by the floods, affecting both water and sanitation facilities. The large majority of households in the host communities require access water from tubewells/handpumps, but only 79% of households are reporting having enough water for their domestic need (only 65 percent in Ukhiya where groundwater is scarce). In camps Current Hygiene knowledge and access to soap remains high although behavior change remains an issue that requires further research to assess the impact of knowledge on hygiene habits. Access to MHM remains a barrier for many females through stigma, taboo and restrictive practices but also from a lack of MHM kit individuality due to common practices of one size fits all or variations of inter sector kit and insufficient coordination. It also requires wide scale of Hygienic Practice monitoring, staff capacity building and meaningful community engagement.For sanitation functionality has an average of 93% and is a continual process for partners. Despite additional latrine instillation has been improved in existing facilities with inclusion and privacy features and regular facility monitoring by the Area Focal Agencies and partners show number of latrines has stayed constant with 2022 figures, highlighting the continued lack of operational space for WASH infrastructure. Although the gap in bathing spaces has reduced, there has been a significant increase in private spaces, with 78% of households reporting private bathing space inside or attached to their shelter for but those bathing places sometimes associated with laundry spaces (73%) and dishwashing places (79%), very often those spaces are unhygienic and poorly maintained or used as alternatives for urination and defecation, with 9% not connected to any form of drainage. Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence with 82% preferring private bathing spaces. Pilot programmes have been instigated to install women friendly spaces and WASH blocks but are challenged by space and the lack of direct water supplies to each facility. A continual concern regarding sanitation is the quality of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades, improvements or scale-up. Solid Waste produce daily in the targeted area, SWM remains one of the main challenges as only 56 percent of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated area and 13% reported disposing of waste direct to open space. On the other hand, other households are satisfied with the household level segregation and waste collection practices."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The Rohingya crisis remains the largest and most complex humanitarian crisis in Bangladesh, with significant numbers of people needing assistance. The post covid-19 pandemic has exacerbated the suffering and has increased the vulnerability of refugees and host communities and triggered additional challenges and restrictions in the delivery of the humanitarian response. CAID along with its collaborative partners draws on the experience and operational capacity of camps and the host communities and existing facilities and networks to “contribute to international efforts to meet humanitarian needs and increase resilience and self-reliance of Rohingya and host populations” through the delivery of a well-coordinated and inclusive program in strong partnership with national and local partners. Working in this viable and complexity situation is designed to enable broader geographic reach, better coordination of and with the key stakeholders, and improved collective response to the needs of Rohingya and host communities affected by the crisis. CAID is committed for the collective actions to restore the protection for refugees. As a part of the project exit strategy plan CAID has strong possibilities for extensions of the project aligned with donor's agreement, long-experiences working in the humanitarian background especially in the Rohingya camps. CAID has a great experience of smooth operations in inclusive WASH interventions since the beginning of the influx, CAID with the partnership with DSK and World Vision, funded by UKAID and UNOPS, implemented a great deal of WASH interventions at Camps. Moreover, CAID and CARE Bangladesh have been jointly implementing the same AHP Consortium project funded by DFAT. In addition, we have also experienced about the hand over procedures, as previously we handed over the UKAID and UNOPS WASH infrastructures to CARE and BRAC. The exit strategy will be properly coordinated with the WASH focal points and the WASH sectors in future.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection mainstreaming was tracking progress of all diversified groups in the aspects of protection within the camps and host community using evidence of success and lessons to develop knowledge and advocacy at all levels of life and living. Safe and secure volunteery inclusive WASH services will be provided to the targetted camps 14 and 15 keeping in the mind about the protection concerns for all level of stakeholders included volunteers, staffs, partner's, vendors, refugges communities male, female, boys, girls, transgender, persons with disabilities, LGBTQ and others section of merginalized people. For every construction of WASH infrastructure repairing, maintenance and operations the protection issues like preventing GBV, PSHEA and SGBV, are given higly consideration.

3.2 - Gender

The project focused on gender equality and equity aligned with every aspects of life. Moreover, addressing barriers and implementing targeted and mainstreaming strategies to ensure gender inclusion in particular for the transformative softskills and lifeskills components on WASH mainstreaming with the repair and maintenance interventions in the camps. The project highlighted special consideration of gender equity and decrasing the gender gaps among the project participants keeping them follow-up and regular gender training.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Age inlcuding (0-under 18 years) incorporated with the interventions in the process of Child safeguarding issues. Preventing sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse (PSHEA); child protection; and social safeguarding issues are merged as a cross cutting issues with the every interventions. The MEAL Plan, Monitoring and Evaluation systems and COMPASS (CAID developed own mechanism for feedback and response collection and tracking) integreted with the interventions for ensuring the Child Safeguarding to amplify the community voices at the field level. Moreover, the relevent changes in the project documents are appreciated by the community voices where every age groups (0 to 2, 3 to 5, 6 to 12, 13 to 18, 19 to 34, 35 to 60 and 60 to 80 and 80+ population) treated with respect and dignity to provide the community feedback and possible solutions to implement the interventions in an alternative way suggested by them.

3.4 - Diversity

The interventions of CAID included diversity - inclusion of other marginalized groups who are left behind people in the community like the Hijra, Transgender, Lactating mothers and pregnant women, elderly people, and vulnerable groups. The persons with disabilities are considered as high priority in the lens of inclusion and diversified types of special abilities in critical, essential WASH interventions. Particularly the project promoted the ramp, handril, grill, grab-ber, commod chair, staircase and inclusive WASH facilitieis aligned with BOQ during the designing and drawing following the sector guidance where age, disability inclusion commonly contributed for the effective and quality service delivery.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The key strategies of CAID across the interventions are designed to respond to a complex and protracted crisis, and will be explored through the accountabilities for expected and unexpected outcomes of the response, to promote program adaptation, ensure accountability to affected communities, and require good coordination across the interventions in Camps and the Host Communities, and high standards of transparency towards the community people. CAID along with partners mobilized the communities and people affected by crisis to know their rights and entitlements by which they have access to information and participate in decisions that affect them, having safe and secure communication mechanism by the COMPASS (CAID developed the own mechanism for the CFRM process). The organizational opportunities and learning environment from the humanitarian assisstance created scope for the refugees to be more practical receiving quality services from the partners and donors.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

yes, we have the resilience and self-reliance outcome interventions in the camps which is undirectly inter-linked with the project.
