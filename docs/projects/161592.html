Project Summary

This 12 Months project is designed to have an inclusive response to needs of children with disabilities in other to address protection risks for children living with disabilities among IDPS, returnees and host Communities. We shall Provide Psychosocial support to children and caregivers through conducting different educational and recreational activities in the child friend space and provide specific MHPSS needs for children and caregivers affected by the conflict. The Project will seek to address the need of children who are affected by protection risks including abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect and violence; through integrated case management process. This will require that we develop and maintain an integrated inclusive database of child protection problems that shall be supported by the Inter Agency Case Management forms with anticipated ground for the roll out of the CPIMS+.

1 - Needs

Due to activities of Non State Arm group (NSAG) also called Boko Haram, and with intensified military offensive against the group in northeast Nigeria, children continue to suffer the most part of the insurgency. An estimated 1.8 million children and adolescents (0.96million girls and 0.84million boys) in north- east Nigeria are in need of child protection and specialized support services. At least 7,778 girls and 7,473 boys have been affected by protection risks including abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect and violence; analysis of the case management cases recorded indicates that children living with vulnerable caregivers has carried the higher proportion of children affected by protection risks. Whilst there are no statistics available on the needs of children with disabilities, the narrative from the response has highlighted that there is a need for deliberate interventions to address protection risks for children with disabilities. More than 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent girls) are estimated to have been associated with armed groups, abducted, killed or victims of violent attacks by armed groups since the beginning of the conflict. Psychosocial distress has mostly affected IDP children and there is a clear need to assess the specific MHPSS needs for children and caregivers affected by the conflict.The ability of an estimated 110,700 parents and caregivers to care appropriately for children has been negatively impacted, putting children and families at risk of negative coping mechanisms including child marriage and dropping out of school. There is immediate need to strengthen local capacities in the communities to contribute to prevention of some of the violations and abuses face by Children in emergency need to prevent any form of violation. Prevention is key to stopping child rights violations and local interlocutors are in well placed to do this. It will be crucial to provide critical minimum response assistance to some of the victims such as children living with disability, children formerly associated with armed forces or groups; child survivor of gender-based violence, unaccompanied and separated children and other children at risks including their caregivers. These response will complement the efforts of other partners already in that community.

2 - Activities

Our overriding strategy is to work through existing traditional support structures with community, the intervention will mostly entail capacity building and empowerment activities that aim to pass on basic child protection and psychosocial support skills to these community structures to identify, document, monitor and report incidents of child rights violations. GSF will employ a localised community-based social reintegration and case managemnt approach in implementation of the project together with the ministry of women affairs and social development. This will involve mobilisation of resources in other to understand the child’s personal skills, family support and engagement of community leaders through child-led activities.Economic reintegration of CAAC approach will entail assistance to children who want to return to school and vocational skills training institutions. GSF will use the Child Friendly Space approach in bolstering the community-based reintegration of vulnerable children, Using Journey of life, tree of life and narrative theatre sessions to children in child friendly spaces. They shall have play and recreational materials based on children’s ages. We also propose to recruit CSF animators from each location. We shall continue to structure and provide age specific discussion groups for both boys and girls especially adolescents, capacity building for community base animators as well as continue interactive activities that impart children with survival and coping skills, enhance their self-esteem and strengthen their relational abilities. This will reduce risks of targeting children who were previously enrolled with NSAG. The case workers will conduct family tracing with CPC within their LGAs and Inter- state, Foster care family arrangements will be utilized for temporary care and support of separated children. This arrangement will be closely monitored by the members of CPC in the communities with the agreement of the ministry of women affairs and child development.GSF will put in place child club to eradicate child right concerns and these children shall be trained both on child rights and journey of life, tree of life and narrative theatre sessions as peer to peer support which is a new intervention as an added value to the community. However, peace building themes will be crosscutting
