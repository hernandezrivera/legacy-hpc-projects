Project Summary

Chronic malnutrition was a problem in Syria even before the crisis and increased rates have been observed recently (SMART surveys 2019) with one out of every 8 children stunted (low height for age). The national prevalence of chronic malnutrition of children is 12.6%, with Deir-ez-Zor, Idleb, Aleppo, Al-Hassakeh and Quinetra governorates either already have reached or almost reaching the WHO high level of stunting classification .This increase reflects long-term inadequate dietary intake, in addition to poor infant feeding practices. latest data form the SMART survey 2019 showed a low average exclusive breastfeeding rate of 28 per cent and a minimum acceptable diet of 26.5 per cent.
A significant increase of acute malnutrition has also been observed among PLW, maternal malnutrition rates has increased five-fold compared to last year highlighting the vulnerabilities of PLWs, particularly to poverty, inadequate diet, heavy workload, and harmful coping mechanisms such as prioritization of food for children .
SC will aim to improve the nutrition outcomes of conflict-affected pregnant women and young children 0-24 months through comprehensive, integrated nutrition interventions. the project will directly address both acute and chronic malnutrition in northwest and northeast Syria by focusing on the first 1,000 days when the child is most vulnerable to the impacts of poor nutrition. We will continue and scale up the current response involving skilled maternal, infant and young child nutrition services that include group education and specialized one-to-one counselling; along with integrated components of positive physical and cognitive stimulation for the child, and MHPSS support for PLW, young girls, and adolescent mothers
SCI nutrition interventions will target pregnant women and caregivers of under 2 years old with specialized nutrition counselling in order to improve maternal nutrition and resolve particular breastfeeding, complementary feeding, and artificial feeding difficulties. a total of 20 MBAs (fixed and mobile) will be established in camps and non camps settings in Idlib , AlHassake and Deir ELzor to provide education group sessions for mothers, fathers, and key stakeholders on the WHO/UNICEF recommended IYCF practices, maternal nutrition, and hygiene, trained outreach teams will be attached to each MBA to conduct rapid IYCF assessmnets ,and Improve equitable access to curative nutrition services through timely identification and referral of acutely malnourished cases for pregnant and lactating women, and boys and girls under 59 months of age in each catchment area.
The project will ensure a strategic, integrated approach with Child Protection (Parent-Toddler, CFS, alternative care), Education (ECCD), community health (ANC and PNC outreach, hygiene activities), mainstreamed with MHPSS (relaxation, grounding techniques, referrals) and SGBV (MBAs/Women safe spaces, referrals), to address some of the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition.

4 - Needs

Continued hostilities in the NWS and NES with large scale displacement have resulted in poor nutrition outputs through 2019 (HNO 2020). The primary nutrition needs identified for 2020 are based on nation-wide SMART surveys and MUAC assessments in 33 IDP camps, informal settlements and collective centres. The nutrition status of populations has become more severe in some sub-districts in Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Idleb, and Deir-Ez-Zor governorates (HNO 2020).NWS and NES are considered the most deprived areas. 2019 data suggests increasing trends of acute malnutrition among internally displaced children and mothers, poor IYCF practices across Syria, worsened maternal nutrition status, and a rise in chronic malnutrition rates.Hassakeh and Dier Ez Zor reflect stunting rates of 19.2 and 22% respectively, the highest ever for both. In particular in NES, life-saving infant feeding practices are very poor, with low exclusive breastfeeding rates during the first 6 months, and low dietary diversity. In both camps and especially Annexes, where mostly women and children reside, there is a clear plan in 2020 to scale up IYCF services by the nutrition sector.The consequences of chronic malnutrition are transgenerational, impacting a child’s life in the short- and long-term. Stunting and lowered immune function cause higher risk of infectious diseases and impact cognitive development, education, and productivity as they grow older. Addressing this requires scaling up interventions focusing on improving IYCF practices and maternal nutrition in the first 1,000 days of life (pregnancy-24 months).The 2019 Idlib SMART survey shows that the proportion of stunted children increased from 14.2 % (10.2 - 19.5, 95% C.I.) in 2017 to 17.4 % (13.6 - 22.1 95% C.I.) in 2019. While the prevalence of acute malnutrition is still low in survey area, the highest prevalence was among younger children, indicating poor IYCF-E practices, especially exclusive breastfeeding and complementary feeding.

5 - Implementation of activities

SC promotes and supports WHO/UNICEF recommended IYCF feeding practices to address malnutrition. In 2019, SC had 12 MBAs in NE and NW, plus 5 mobile ones in NW. This will be scaled up to 20 MBAs in 2020. The MBAs are spaces for mothers to meet, bond and discuss infant feeding challenges. Outreach teams will conduct mass MUAC screenings for PLWs and children 6-59 months, refer, and follow-up CMAM cases. The Nutrition team consists of counselors and educators who receive training on the WHO/UNICEF 40-hour IYCF-E course and how to provide basic support to caregivers during counselling including PFA and supporting/referring survivors of GBV. SC will conduct simple rapid assessments for all PLW and mothers with children 0-24 months to decide the type of support needed, including individual counselling and follow ups for mothers facing breastfeeding challenges. Considering the high rates of anemia among pregnant women, a specific focus on maternal nutrition counselling and utilization of iron rich foods will use material supported by the nutrition and FSL clusters. Closer support and regular follow up will be conducted for malnourished children 6-24 months, and mothers who need relactation support. Daily education sessions address common feeding challenges, misconceptions, and promote IYCF-E lifesaving practices, and include demonstrations on how to improve complementary feeding practices and dietary diversity in emergencies.ECCD activities for children ages 0-3 will integrate with IYCF services. CP teams will receive training on key IYCF messages and supported to ensure IYCF support needs are identified. SC will integrate with FSL programs to improve dietary diversity for PLWs and children under 5.Orientations with health workers and stakeholders at key forums such as the NES INGO forum will advocate adherence to the BMS SOP, highlight the dangers of uncontrolled infant formula, and underscore the life-saving practices of breastfeeding and complementary feeding.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Community tension due if services are offered in one community but not a neighboring community. Mitigating Measures: Continuously ensure service mapping, consultation with community on program interventions, regular communication with different communities on availability of services. Expansion of services to non-camp locations where needs are present, to avoid concentration of services in camps. Monitoring Mechanism: Regular consultations with communities, FGDs, feedback boxes. Risk: Barriers to access for marginalized groups Mitigating measures: Feedback informs planning, implementation, and MEAL activities to ensure marginalized groups: women and girls, children with disabilities, and the elderly can participate. Activities are age and gender sensitive. Monitoring Mechanism: FGDs, feedback mechanism Risk: Affected populations and SC staff and volunteers, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment, or violating SCI CSG rules. Mitigating measures: Training for SC staff and volunteers on SC’s Anti-Harassment Policy and Code of Conduct, and all representatives sign the SCI CSG policy. SC will provide trainings on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through SC’s PSEA focal points. CSG trainings link to SC and partner focal points. Risk: GBV Mitigating Measures: The MBAs operate through developing strong interaction between mothers, PLWs, caregivers and the nutrition teams. This ensures a continuous feedback channel on gender-related issues to strengthen the inclusion of gender-specific service delivery. As the primary target group includes mothers, PLW's, caregivers and children under 2 years, the scope of the Nutrition program contributes to gender equality and increases awareness across target age groups. Overall Monitoring: SC upholds the importance of setting up various channels of communication and complaint to mitigate the risks or solve the issues in a rapid and most culturally sensitive way.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(S&S) Staff movements must be pre-approved and refresher security trainings regularly conducted. The Hostile Environment Awareness Training is mandatory for NES deployments; local staff are trained on Advanced Personal S&S. SC’s partners engage with relevant stakeholders and receive timely updates. SC works on a common response to unacceptable conditions. -Airstrikes SC supports partners to immediately report security incidents to be analysed and develop and implement comprehensive safety plans. -Loss of access SC and partners conduct thorough security assessments and maintain updated contingency plans. If necessary, SC will suspend programs and work to resume activities. If this is not viable, SC would assess possibilities for relocation. Work permits: Measures are in place to mitigate the Gov. of Turkey (GoT) legislative environment hindering SC’s ability to operate, I.e. using a third-party HR company to overcome work permit challenges. -Diversion of resources to terrorist/ proscribed groups In 2018, interviews were conducted to identify areas for increased risk mitigation; an action plan was developed. SC launched an initiative with OCHA to provide external training on international humanitarian law and negotiation with armed actors. Partners have their own systems of anti-terrorism vetting checks for all contracted suppliers and SC’s Syria office vets against the third-party database for procurements $750+. -Escalation of military activity at Turkey/Syria border SC program locations and contingency plans are under constant review and the Hibernation, Relocation and Evacuation plan is shared with staff. Offices and guesthouses have the necessary S&S infrastructure. -Disruption to cash transfer operations In NES, SC is conducting a review of Hawala delivery to identify mitigation measures/additional suppliers. In NWS, NGOs have seen temporary suspension of Bab Al Hawa and have faced challenges in money transfer due to GoT restrictions.
