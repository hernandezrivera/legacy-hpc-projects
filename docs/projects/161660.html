Project Summary

The continued crisis in the North East of Nigeria has lead to massive displacements of men, women and children with unprecedented consequences on livelihoods, basic infrastructure and services. (IOM) reports that over 1.9 million people have been displaced within January and May 2019, of which 92 percent are women and PLW in the Northeastern (NE) States of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY). The insecurity and the large-scale displacement of people has also had a devastating effect on agricultural production and markets, With food prices being relatively higher due to the negative impact of the armed conflict on markets and livelihoods. In 2019, the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) estimated 7.1 million people in need of humanitarian assistance out of which 4.2 million are estimated to be children while 80 percent are women. The inaccessibility to farmland and livelihoods opportunities attributed to insurgent’s attacks and military operations is one of the causes of malnutrition in the North East. The conflict continues to have a direct impact on people’s nutrition status. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months remain consistently concerning, with over one million children suffering from SAM and MAM across the BAY states. This includes 367,000 children under five (179,830 boys and 187,170 girls) suffering from SAM and 727,000 (356,230 boys and 370,770 girls) with MAM (HRP, 2019). The crisis has also exacerbated pre-existing conditions of malnutrition, driven by a number of inter-related factors, including: weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; prevalence of water-borne diseases; poor hygiene conditions; and seasonal food insecurity, culturally tailored believes of caregivers, and community leaders on child nutrition are other sources of malnutrition of children under 5 (0-59), (6-59). In Michika the total number of MAM is 4473 against SAM 1191 and PLW 6068 while in Hong, MAM 5168 against SAM 137 and PLW 7011; Mubi North , MAM 3097 against SAM 825 and PLW 4202 ; Mubi South MAM 3476 against SAM 925 and PLW 4715.
It can be observed that the rate of malnutrition among PLW is higher than that of children MAM and SAM, meaning, a good prevention of malnutrition should target PLW (pregnant and lactating women) and children under 5. In addition, the security issues in the North east should be addressed in order to give access to agricultural activities to the vulnerable returnees; and emphasis should be put on hygiene promotion and IYCF good practices.
To strengthen prevention of malnutrition in Adamawa, AHA shall provide quality preventive nutrition services to PLW (Pregnant and lactating women), groups of vulnerable children through supplementary feeding programs.

1 - Needs

The inaccessibility to agricultural activities to build the self-resilience of the crisis affected population in the North East has led them to malnutrition and other form of undignified coping mechanism. The lack of sanitation as per standard, poor hygiene attitude have also exacerbated their nutrition situation with several cases of children under 5 MAM, and SAM. In Michika the total number of MAM is 4473 against SAM 1191 and PLW 6068 while in Hong, MAM 5168 against SAM 137 and PLW 7011; Mubi North , MAM 3097 against SAM 825 and PLW 4202 ; Mubi South MAM 3476 against SAM 925 and PLW 4715.It can be observed that the rate of malnutrition among PLW is higher than that of children MAM and SAM, meaning, a good prevention of malnutrition should target PLW and children under 5. in addition, the security issues in the North east should addressed in order to give access to agricultural activities to the vulnerable returnees; and emphasis should be put on hygiene promotion and IYCF good practices.To prevent the children under 5 MAM, there is need to strengthen and scale up quality preventive nutrition in host communities of the above mentioned LGAs. There is need to reach vulnerable groups ( 0-59);(6-59 months);(12-59 months);(6-24 months), PLW ( pregnant and lactating women) with multiple micro-nutrient supplementation. Most of the cases of malnutrition are related to culturally-tailored believes of community leaders, mothers, fathers and caregivers on children feeding. There is therefore the need to form groups of mothers to mothers, fathers to fathers and build their capacities on IYCF good practices (cooking demonstration). There is a need to reach new mothers and caregivers with hygiene promotion key messages. Furthermore, nutrition staff should be trained on PSEA while vulnerable groups of under 5 children need to be given Vitamin A to booster their immunity against childhood sicknesses including Micro nutrient tablets to pregnant and lactating women (PLW).

2 - Activities

Act1: Promoting optimal Infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices through community-based support groups.Act 2 Formation of mothers to mothers groups, fathers to fathers groups.Act3 promotion of hygiene key messages and counseling on IYCF good practices targeting new mothers and caregivers.Act 4 Training of mothers to mothers groups and fathers to fathers groups on screening of malnourished children using MUAC.Act5 training of caregivers, mothers and fathers on IYCF keys messages (cooking demonstration) using locally sourced food.Act 6Training of hygiene promoters to reach new mothers and caregivers with hygiene promotion including exclusive breastfeeding.Act 7 Cash-transfer (voucher) to support mothers and caregivers Act8 Awareness rising on GBV prevention in nutrition services.Act 9 Training of nutrition staff on U-report.Act10 Awareness rising on exclusive breastfeeding and complementary/ supplementary feeding targeting PLW and caregivers.
