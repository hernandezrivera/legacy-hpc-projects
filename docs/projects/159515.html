Project Summary

The project will ensure equal access and needs based assistance to improve the quality of integrated services for populations affected by displacement, well-coordinated and integrated humanitarian intervention to 30,000 IDPs (5000 men, 15 000 women, 3000 boys and 7000 girls) to improve their well-being, their resilience and participation in decision-making processes. It will respond to emergency needs by empowering community and strengthening the capacities of local actors to have access to both essentials camp management service. Several strategies will be used including joint interventions with other clusters such as FSL, WASH, ES/NFI, and ensuring that our activities and the whole intervention complements other sectors and partners activities and not duplication. HRSS using CCCM cluster strategy and initiative of cash for work will provide a cash based intervention to enhance resilience building and copying mechanisms. HRSS through a multi-sectoral approach will mainstream and integrate protection, health, WASH, Nutrition, Education, FSL in its intervention through intentional massaging, joint missions among others and will integrate HLP, MHPSS, PSEA, and GBV issues and ensure gender balanced teams. Hope Restoration has experience working in Panyijiar and Nyirol implementing CCCM and protection. If funded, the project will contribute significantly to service delivery in hard-to-reach location through organizing, carry out multi-sectoral needs assessments, create site level service map and protection of people with special needs in coordination with actors; and strengthening community structure by training community members on CCCM and humanitarian response.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is currently facing humanitarian crisis influenced by unprecedented levels of food insecurity, resulting from climate effects but aggravated by the conflict. The IPC (June 2019) highlighted that 6.9 million (61%) of population in South Sudan is facing crisis and 1,815,000 individuals are in need of emergency humanitarian services. It has also classified 25 counties in South Sudan to be under emergency phase. These include; Kapoeta, Akobo, Bor south, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol, Twic East, Uror, Cueibet, Yirol East,Yirol West, Nasir, Kajo-keji and Panyijiar. In the listed counties, due to ongoing violence whole communities are forced to flee, living behind all their life belongings and basic household non-food items, settling in different areas and lacking the financial means to support their families, construction shelter, and access safe and dignified sanitary facilities. 1 465 542 are reported as displaced persons that crossed back to the country, where by 80% of this people live outside in camp like setting and self settling camp across the country.A good number of IDP's had been reported in Unity and Jonglei state where there are 247,278 IDP's in Unity state and 184,753 IDP's in Jonglei state according to latest DTM round 6 report published on 1st of October 2019. In such context, the needs remain dire as IDPs continue to face serious risks across the country such as lack of privacy, inadequate humanitarian support in the different sites of displacement more so in the hard to reach areas and inadequate livelihood activities particularly among the youth which has made them to resort to criminal activities. About 80% of IDP population continue to live in host communities and spontaneous settlements, many in hard-to-reach areas with limited infrastructure and accessibility. Major efforts will be required to ensure potential for integration is fully explored, and peaceful co-existence initiatives; enhancing thereby community participation. To this effect, further engagement in integrated needs assessment by ensuring equal access and needs based assistance to improve the quality of integrated services for populations affected by displacement,strengthen inclusive community participation to ensure local ownership, self-governance and self-reliance and improve engagement with vulnerable population with priority on addressing protection gaps and building resilience in Panyijiar and Nyirol of Unity and Jonglei State respectively.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project does not to any degree detract from duty that the Government has as the primary responsible party to protect IDPs and enable them to find solutions for displacement. All activities undertaken by this project will be coupled with robust advocacy to the Government to uphold their responsibilities under international law and Chapter 3 of the Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (ARCS does not supersede existing guidelines and established procedures for protection-mandated organizations to facilitate returns and relocations to address specific protection risks at the individual or household level.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The length of displacement in South Sudan continue to increase. The protection environment in areas where IDPs have fled remains fluid but has generally declined and there are no locations that provide absolute security. Throughout the conflict, some IDP have spontaneously returned or relocated to find better and safer solutions to their displacement. The capacity of IDPs to act on their intentions is varied with some requiring more assistance than others. IDPs have been able to pursue better solutions when they have the resources and conditions necessary to do so. Many IDPs find themselves in areas where freedom of movement is severely compromised and their resources and coping mechanisms depleted, both of which limit their ability to decide where they will reside and to act on their intentions.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Protection assessments will be done for areas of concentrated movement, with active participation from the host community and including analysis of housing, land and property concerns, service mapping, and identification of conditions that would compromise progress toward achieving more sustainable solutions. Assessment will also include locations that are currently not conducive for solutions to monitor the situation, identify potential risk, and to collect information to share with IDPs. Protection risks along the potential routes of travel will also be taken into account.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Transitional Durable solutions that enable IDPs to improve their protection environment and take steps that will lead to more sustainable solutions need to be taken into consideration and supported. Protection driven support to individuals, households, and communities could enable IDPs exercise their right to choose the best of available solutions for their displacement /. A area based approach to displacement seeks to restore the rights of affected populations and place them at the center of the process whereby, IDPs are in a position to make voluntary and informed decisions, IDPs are engaged and involved in processes that directly impact their well-being and participate in the planning and management of such processes, IDPs have access to humanitarian and development actors, IDPs have effective monitoring mechanisms.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. HRSS is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. HRSS is a lead national NGO on GBV in Unity State and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. HRSS with other partners have developed a referral pathway in Panyijiar, Mayendit and Rubkona, and will develop another in Nyirol and will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools HRSS will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in-charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. HRSS staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. HRSS is also a very active member of Protection Cluster.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

HRSS will participatory implement this project in a conflict sensitive manner, ensuring that inclusive participation of all stakeholders and in particular the beneficiaries at all stages of this project cycle. Participation will be enhanced through conducting participatory needs assessment in an inclusive and participatory manner and frequent consultations of key beneficiary groups in decision making particularly women and girls. HRSS will spend at least two weeks in the field to engage and work closely with beneficiaries to set up a project implementation committee that will last throughout the project, nominated by the target beneficiaries and are composed of the beneficiaries. This committee will share with all the beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, the elderly, those who are illiterate, women and girls issues agreed upon during their meetings such as types of assistance needed, safety audits, meeting and distribution locations and sites. HRSS will also set up a distribution committee composed of protection specialists, beneficiaries and local leadership to monitor any distribution process, and to provide feedback to HRSS on issues concerning protection such as SEA, HLP issues, livelihoods, the distribution process and the whole intervention process from the beginning of the project. HRSS will also use the presence of beneficiaries to pass messages such as health, livelihoods, SGBV, HLP among others through messaging and awareness creation. The feedback team will be composed of the project implementation committee representatives, representatives of various constituencies of beneficiaries and HRSS’s protection advisor. Conducting both pre and post distribution awareness and monitoring respectively will provide feedback from the various groups of beneficiaries. Reports developed by HRSS will be available for community, partners and even government authorities to access for references. This action will address key lifesaving threats identified.
