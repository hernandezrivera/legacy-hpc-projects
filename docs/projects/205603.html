Project Summary

The project will focus on Increasing the equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services on prevention of acute malnutrition of children 6 -59 months of age and pregnant and lactating women of child bearing age (PLWs) and build resilience among young children, adolescents through behavior change communication and micro-nutrient supplementation to boys & girls 6-23 months and Prevention of malnutrition through provision of BSFP to boys & girls 6 - 23 Months including PLWs in Fangak Canal/Pigi, Leer, Rubkona, and Women, Men will benefit from MIYCN Counseling focusing on social behavior change communication, improving households dietary intake children 6-59 months will be provided with Vitamin A, (674 children 12-59month will benefit from deworming by the end of 2024
HFO project will contribute to the achievement of Nutrition Cluster strategic objectives for humanitarian response in 2024 by conducting community nutrition screening and referrals of SAM/MAM cases children 6-59 months, support children 6-23 months & PLWs during BSFP distribution, The project will coordinate meetings with IPs,CHD SMOH, and focuses on strengthening MIYCN Counseling, vitamin A supplementation and deworming services and community outreach integrating with health and hygiene promotion, Distribution of WASH Kits, LLINT during extensive community mobilization
1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2024
4: To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2024
The recruitment of community Nutrition workers, community nutrition volunteers and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) counselors will be conducted in consultations with community members and local authority for their participation in the selection criteria by making sure local stakeholders are involved and gender balance is taken into account of getting workers from both males and females where necessary and including the people with disability. The nutrition response will be designed to ensure that Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) is a part at all stages of the response. HFO staff will continue to use the Nutrition Cluster and Global Nutrition Cluster operational framework on AAP that includes minimum and/or major key activities and indicators focused on participation, establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms, transparency and communications, planning and implementation of nutrition responses as well as monitoring and reporting. The project response will promote a consultative approach by consulting a wider range of affected communities to ensure communities have simplicity and have their inputs on the objectives of the nutrition response, the activities, the locations, and their titles where the most affected groups are, and will consist of a package of treatment and prevention services delivered through the health facility/nutrition sites and through community outreaches, as per national BHNS with support from MtMSGs, CNVs integrated to EPI. This will be supported through an ongoing capacity building effort at facility and community levels, including community mobilization and engagement for participation. Hard-to-reach areas will be covered through outreaches services and Integrated packages of services with Health, Food Security, WASH, Protection, HFO will continue to report to NIS/5Ws activity-info.

1. Needs

South Sudan ten states’ health system depends mainly on humanitarian assistance (88%) and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies, & three years of consecutive flooding, disease outbreaks and increasing sub national violence have resulted in 15.8 million in humanitarian need & a growing number of threats against humanitarian workers. More than 1.97 millions of those people are in Emergency IPC Phase 4, and more than 43,000 face catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) levels of hunger At least 0.7 million returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. The country’s humanitarian needs are Outpacing the resources available to respond, and the situation has worsened as the country is battling the floods that has submerged entire villages. The malnutrition situation in South Sudan The malnutrition situation in Sudan remains dire. Atthe start of 2023, child stunting and wasting rates are among the world's highest. The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) stands at 13.6%. In around 40% of localities GAM rates exceed the 15% emergency threshold (WHO). 4 million children in need out of which over 3M children under five years of age are suffering from wasting; 611,000 having SAM, 2.4M having MAM and 936,000 PLWs requiring treatment. The health and nutrition needs are high based on current conflict in Sudan IDSR indicates that more cases of communicable diseases that are due to lack of clean drinking water & limited access to the static nutrition services caused by Panyikang and Rubkona,Leer, Fangak, & Canal Pigi Counties crisis, Severe flooding that have resulted into severe food insecurity across affected Counties of Unity and Jonglei States of South SudanThe World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations’ Children’s Fund (UNICEF) estimate a national immunization coverage of 51% and only 24% verified by card for most antigens. Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile States share a range of complex implementation challenges: insecurity due to armed conflict, flooding, poor road network, political and cattle raiding as barriers to humanitarian access, weak health systems persistently under resourced, ongoing natural disaster risks, Absorbent & crowded populated borders and sizeable mobile populations, including nomadic and pastoralist communities and internally displaced person (IDPs), and returnees/refugees 1.
