Project Summary

The aims of IOMs’ health interventions aim to respond to the lifesaving needs and improve the resilience of affected populations, with special attention to gender and age considerations, and vulnerable groups. In the camps,34% Rohingya refugee populations, have reported going into debt to pay for health expenses, and 27% seeking lower-quality care or medication, due to a lack of money (Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment J-MSNA 2020). This demonstrates some challenges in the already strained health service system in Cox’s Bazar. To address this, this intervention aims to contribute to i) increased safe, dignified and meaningful access and utilization of quality comprehensive healthcare services, focusing on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases (NCD); ii) improved health seeking behavior, encouragement of healthy living and promotion of the utilization of essential service package and community engagement, with special attention to vulnerable groups iii) measures to prepare and (timely) respond to health emergencies, during disaster (including monsoon and cyclone) and outbreaks of communicable disease (e.g. COVID-19 and AWD). IOM also aims to contribute to the improvement of quality of services through performance monitoring, evaluation, coordination and capacity building.

1 - Identified Needs

Since 2017, nearly 1m Rohingya have been displaced to Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The Government of Bangladesh & humanitarian partners have continued to coordinate & provide lifesaving support & assistance. These populations have increased health related vulnerabilities due to factors such as risk of communicable diseases, increased susceptibility from underlying conditions, limited access to comprehensive health services (referrals & management of non-communicable diseases), poor health-seeking behaviors, & unhygienic living conditions. In addition, the Rohingya refugees remain vulnerable to the threats posed by natural disasters. According to the J-MSNA 2020, 34% reported going into debt to pay for health expenses and 27% reported seeking lower-quality care or medication, due to a lack of money, demonstrating some challenges in the already strained health service system in Cox’s Bazar. In addition, since declared as Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC), in March 2020, COVID-19 has tested health systems globally, with varying modalities & effectiveness of preparedness & response measures adopted. As of 30th Nov 2020, there were 462,407 confirmed cases of COVID-19 and 6,609 deaths in Bangladesh, & in Cox’s Bazar district, 5,508 confirmed cases so far among the refugees & host communities. These latest situation reports show trends of fewer cases than neighboring countries (e.g. India with 9,431,691 cases and 137,139 deaths), & possible explanations include lower rates of testing (by 30th November, 18,775 in camps vs 42,288 host), refusal of facility management & unreported COVID-19 cases and deaths. Thus, the need to remain vigilant, & continue to maintain the current measures, in order to appropriately & timely respond. IOM’s effort to addresses the needs of these vulnerable populations includes maintaining the Emergency Health Program, with 35 health facilities in both the refugee camps & host communities. The adequate number & cadre of trained staff (450 skilled health workers & 350 Community Health Workers/volunteers -CHW/Vs) will continue to be deployed across these health facilities, providing Sexual & Reproductive Health (SRH) services, child health services, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS), integrated case management & psychosocial support for survivors of GBV, facilitated referrals, & management of non-communicable Diseases (NCD) & Communicable Diseases. The deployment of CHWs/ Vs will strengthen community based promotive & preventive services to improve health seeking behavior and promote utilization of essential health services. These community level activities will also serve as channels for communication for community inputs, & the provision of information & feedback.IOM aims to continue strengthening overall preparedness and response for health emergencies, disasters and communicable disease with outbreak potential, by integrating COVID-19 activities into EPR (emergency preparedness and response). Monitoring & reporting will continue through Early Warning, Alert & Response Systems (EWARS), in order to investigate diseases with outbreak potential, initiate laboratory tests & trigger response actions. Isolation & treatment facilities, (including 3 SARI ITCs and one quarantine centre) will be maintained for comprehensive management of communicable diseases. IOM will recruit, & train health and support staff on relevant topics for outbreak response, (e.g. IPC, and case management ) & continue to lead the health sector mobile medical teams (MMTs) working group supporting & coordinating emergency lifesaving health services in the aftermath of disaster. IOM will support operations & logistics activities (such as provision of kits, medicines, PPEs, coordination of the Dispatch & referral Unit) as well as maintenance and repair costs, & also aims to contribute to the improvement of quality of services through performance monitoring, evaluation, coordination & capacity building
