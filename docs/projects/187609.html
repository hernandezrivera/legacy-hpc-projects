Project Summary

The protection concerns and vulnerabilities UNRWA identified in 2021 amongst Palestine refugees are anticipated to continue, if not increase, through 2022-23. These include: (i) psychosocial trauma and distress, particularly amongst children; (ii) increased rates of GBV, with women and girls most affected; (iii) more economic destitution leading to increased reliance on negative coping mechanisms; (iv) child protection concerns, especially violence against children; and (v) exclusion and discrimination, particularly affecting persons with disabilities and older persons. Female heads of households, unaccompanied and separated children, older persons and persons with disabilities are at particular risk of falling into further poverty and exploitation. Protracted displacement and the high level of destruction in several Palestine refugee camps and gatherings, coupled with the ongoing economic crisis, may result in an increase in shelter, housing, land and property rights (HLP) and civil documentation issues.
UNRWA will continue to provide increasingly needed protection services to Palestine refugees in Syria, especially in light of the current deteriorating economic situation and the additional protection concerns resulting from the COVID-19 pandemic, and in line with its commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity and impartiality. The Agency will continue to focus on mainstreaming protection in service provision, including through area-based structures and staff capacity building in the identification of intersecting factors affecting individual vulnerability. The Agency will also work towards building a stronger data-driven protection approach to strengthen evidence-based prioritization and programming.
PSS activities for 11,360 refugees, including children, older persons and persons with disabilities, will remain a priority in 2022, to strengthen the response to the increasing psychosocial needs, to foster social cohesion and prevent the adoption of negative coping mechanisms. In light of the risk of COVID-19 exposure, safe practices will be used for in-person PSS activities for particularly vulnerable groups like older persons.
Legal support will continue to be offered to refugees (mainly women) experiencing GBV, through assistance in obtaining documentation and referrals to external partners. The threat caused by ERW remains a major protection concern in Syria, particularly for children, who are most at risk of being harmed. UNRWA will continue to address this primarily through awareness raising sessions for all UNRWA students (i.e., 50,000 children) and people from refugee communities (1,500), as well as training for Agency staff (200). Particular focus will be given to areas seeing spontaneous returns of refugee families, especially in Yarmouk, Ein el Tal and Dera’a camps which are potentially heavily contaminated with ERW.
Protection monitoring, reporting and advocacy will remain a priority. UNRWA will continue to participate in global protection mechanisms; engage, as appropriate, with the International Human Rights System (IHRS) in line with the Agency’s IHRS framework; and interact with relevant stakeholders, including the protection coordination structures of the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) at country and area levels. UNRWA will continue to monitor and advocate for, and respond to, particularly vulnerable groups within the Palestine refugee community, including individuals who remain displaced within Syria and Palestine refugees willing to voluntarily return to their place of habitual residence from within Syria and from abroad, noting that those returning to newly accessible areas with limited services are often amongst the most economically vulnerable.
UNRWA will also strengthen adherence to humanitarian principles, including by conducting installation checks to ensure they are compliant with UN values (including neutrality) and that they are safe, accessible and inclusive spaces.

4 - Needs

Protection risks and vulnerabilities identified in 2021 are anticipated to continue in 2022/23. As mentioned in the Syria HNO 2021, there are 13.13m PIN, and the deteriorating economy and widespread poverty, lack and loss of livelihoods, destruction and loss of housing & property, protracted and multiple cycles of displacement, substandard living conditions (even for people in areas of relative stability), family separation and the breakdown of community support structures and the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic have depleted the coping abilities. Palestine refugees (PRs) are among the most vulnerable. Findings of a socio-economic assessment that UNRWA conducted in June 2020 reflect that PRs in Syria, are among the most heavily impacted. Levels of psychological distress, reliance on negative coping mechanisms, child protection concerns, exclusion and discrimination, mainly of persons with disabilities and older persons, have increased and are expected to continue increasing. The assessment also highlights that protection risks for women and girls have increased due to disruption of pre-existing protection mechanisms and crucial services (family planning, child and maternal health & sexual and reproductive health care services; legal assistance; counselling services). Among the results, the increasing level of GBV has been identified by nearly 50 percent of Palestine refugee households, thus remaining of particular concerns. The threat caused by also ERW remains a major concern.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNRWA will continue to provide increasingly needed protection services to Palestine refugees (PRs) in Syria, especially in light of the current deteriorating situation. UNRWA in 2022/23 will further strengthen community-based approaches and targeted support for PRs with specific protection needs. This will include regular in-person PSS sessions, particularly for children, older persons and persons with disabilities, to strengthen the response to the increasing psychosocial needs, foster social cohesion and prevent the adoption of negative coping mechanisms. Also awareness raising sessions will be a priority in 2022/23, including on explosive ordnance and on legal issues. Support will continue to be offered, including to individuals experiencing GBV, through assistance in obtaining documentation and referrals to external partners. Another priority will be capacity building of humanitarian actors in relevant protection related fields, aiming to improve service provision. UNRWA will continue to participate in global protection mechanisms; engage, as appropriate, with the International Human Rights System (IHRS) in line with the Agency’s IHRS framework; and interact with relevant stakeholders, including the protection coordination structures of the Humanitarian Country Team at country and area levels. UNRWA will continue to monitor and advocate for and respond to particularly vulnerable groups within the Palestine refugee community, the displaced and returnees.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of SEA in the context of provision of protection services Likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigation Measures: - 1. UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. - 2. UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. - 3. UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. - 4. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. - 5. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. - 6. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure confidentiality and set up accessible and efficient complaint mechanisms. - 7. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 55% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. The RBM approach to programming ensures evidence-based decision-making and accountability. Risk UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1.Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1.UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2.PPEs will be provided to frontline staff.
