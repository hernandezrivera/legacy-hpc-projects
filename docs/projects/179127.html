Project Summary

ore than 2.7 million are Internally Displaced People (IDPs), many of whom are living in over-crowded locations with limited access to essential services. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385 camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021 alone). Women and children represent 80 per cent of this caseload while more than 22,000 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs.
The type and scale of needs faced by people and families in northwest Syria is pervasive and multifaceted due to a convergence of factors arising from prolonged and multiple displacement and lingering conflict, a sharp reduction in purchasing power, the loss of essential livelihoods and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.

4 - Needs

The sector analysis concluded that 59% of the Syria population requires WASH assistance, namely 12.2 million. Over 7 million people is highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including 1.9 million people living in IDPs last resort sites that require comprehensive and continued WASH assistance to survive. The response will be delivered through service delivery for the trucking water and in-kind for the Hygiene and Covide-19 sessions.This Project targeted the IDPs in Camps in Al Dana Distract and Ariha about 1650 families about 12000 IDPs to supply them by clean water and Hygiene kits and Covide-19 Kits and Hygiene Promotion sessions to educate IDPs about the correct use of water and Body hygiene.SoP has been addressing the crucial needs for (water and Hygiene ) of IDPs in Idleb since 2016 As the number of Syrian IDPs in area continues to grow and further needs are being identify by Sop Teams through periodic field assessments and post-distribution monitoring. To Assess the situation in Northwest of Syria under three different sectors and propose optimal response modalities and develop operationalplan there, Space of Peace Organization conducted a multi-sectorial needs assessment.Allocated teams, resources and staff time; IT supportTrain staff and partners

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Movements of water trucks in congested, camp like settings 2-Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards3-Not enough water distribution points available so people have to wait for extended periods to get water, fights and harassment could occur; inequality in distribution .4-Water distribution not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution.5-Time of water delivery not appropriate causing safety risk for beneficiaries or creating pulling factor for school-age children.6-Long distance to water collection points and route to be travelled not safe (exposure to GBV risk, UXO/ERW hazard).7-Available means of transporting water not supporting equality and accessibility of water distribution for vulnerable groups.8-SoP will continue to monitor protection risks, working with protection colleagues if specific issues arise – and will ensure that any activities including targeting, distribution and monitoring do not increase these risks .most relief assistance is being delivered in a low-profile manner , through the door- to- door Thisdistribution modality helps to limit the exposure of beneficiaries and the local association members.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: The security situation remains stable and the center remains accessible.The acceptance of SOP and its center by the local community and the local authorities issustained. The need for GBV programmed in the area persists. Risks 1:military escalationerupts in the targeted location mitigation: we will train our staff on emergencypreparedness and GBV closure SOPs. the situation will be monitored closely with the field staff and the local authorities. Risk 2:the congeniality of the GBV is breached.mitigation: we will put in place strict disciplinary measures and train the staff on the code of conduct. Our staff have been working in this center for several years and they are aware of the sensitivity of GBV cases.- Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school;-Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities .-Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards (sufficient number of water points with adequate quality etc.) and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design and implementation phases;- Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding water delivery schedule.
