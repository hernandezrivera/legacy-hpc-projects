Project Summary

The food and economic security situation in Syria continues to deteriorate. An estimated 15.5 million people (65% of the population) are in need of food or livelihood support, Or assistance from national safety nets (HNO 2024). These estimates are based on a country-wide assessment of over 42,000 households which shows an increase in food insecurity compared to last year. At least 12.9 million people are in need of food assistance, if 2.1 million who live in camps are all food insecure, and a further 2.6 million are at risk of food insecurity. The driving factors of food insecurity are likely to remain in place in 2024 and if the required support is not provided, there is a high probability that vulnerable people will fall into extreme poverty and that the number of people in need will increase further during the year. In addition, a scale up of food assistance and Agriculture assistance remains a priority.
Syria is in economic decline with sharp deteroriration of economic situation for majority of the population making households have become less resilient. Households are increasingly relying on negative coping mechanisms such as, child labor and child marriage (resulting in more children out of education), as well as the sale of productive assets (resulting in decreased livelihood potential), among many other negative coping strategies. The lack of basic services negatively impacts the living conditions of households, as well as the functionality of schools, businesses, and markets. In multiple reports and assessments, households report that their top needs are for food and access to livelihoods. Over one-third of households have no employed household member, and the Syrian currency dropped drastically between 2020 and 2023.
A significant portion of the Syrian labor market operates in informality, and the dire economic situation increased the average power disparity between the worker and the employer. Employers and employees must learn their work rights and responsibilities. Due to conflict and economic decline, purchasing power in many areas of Syria continues to decrease, which significantly affects growth prospects for businesses that already suffer from sanctions, inflation, broken supply chains and other poor market conditions. In June 2021, the Syrian Pound became more stable due to factors such as the devaluation of the SYP by the Syrian Central Bank and the arrival of the annual harvest. As a result, food security stabilized and improved somewhat. However, post-July 2021 onward, the situation of food insecurity worsened due to an increase in the price of subsidized bread, a rise in the price of subsidized diesel, import restrictions, and a fall in wheat production. The Central Bank of Syria has devalued the Syrian pound four times in 2023. The latest devaluation occurred at the end of October, with the exchange rate reaching SYP 11,557 to a dollar from SYP 8,542. As a result, the local currency has depreciated by 56% in the parallel market since the beginning of the year, 75% over two years, and 83% over three years.
In October 2023, the food basket for a family of five cost SYP 928,587, which is a 100% increase compared to the beginning of the year. The cost has also quadrupled in two years. Although the monthly minimum wage doubled to SYP 187,940 in August 2023, it only covers 20% of the total basket cost. This is a significant reduction in purchasing power compared to three years ago when the minimum wage covered 50% of the total basket cost.
Target: NRC's Interventions will target Socio-economic vulnerable households with unable to meet their basic survival needs (Female headed households, persons with disabilities, households with no abled bodied bread-winner, household who lost their complete source of income and relying on negative coping strategies to fulfil their basic needs).
Transfer value and frequency: 6 months and as per the recommended CWG HCT transfer value.
