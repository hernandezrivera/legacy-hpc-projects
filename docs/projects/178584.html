Project Summary

This project aims to continue and increase the accessibility to essential life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services for all age and sex groups; boys, girls, pregnant and lactating women and other caregivers (other women, and men) with attention to people with special needs without any sexual discrimination, among IDPs and host communities, in NW Syria with ,where the project will provide comprehensive quality CMAM services, disseminate and support optimal IYCF-E practices and promote health and nutrition and basic hygienic behaviors within the population in need in northwest Syria, Besides, the project is going to provide harmonized emergency community-based services for all children and women of the population in need by applying integrated health, nutrition, child protection, and WaSH approaches.
This project will target 6 subdistricts :Afrin, Atareb, Al-Dana, Salqin, Harim and Maaret Tamsrin, targeting 160000 (IDPs 115487, Host communities 44513) including PLWs ,children under 5 ,caregivers and men , the number of targeted children under 5 years is 77000 and 39000 PLWs. the scope of services include:
4 fixed CMAM center within the health facilities:
1. Al-Hakeem PHC center level 3 in Afrin includes (OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion and micronutrient interventions)
2. Kafr Naseh PHC center in Atareb subdistrict includes (OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion and micronutrient interventions
3. SDI salqin PHC in Salqin includes ( OTP,TSFP, IYCF-E promotion, and micronutrient interventions) ,
4-Harim CMAM center in Harim subdistrict (OTP,TSFP, IYCF-E promotion and micronutrient interventions)
And through 11 Rapid Response teams that will be dispatched to the areas of high IDPs in Atareb, Salqin, Afrin, Al-Dana,Harim, and Maaret Tamsrin subdistricts to provide nutrition, child protection, health, and hygiene promoting services to children under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women with including the most vulnerable populations such as those with disabilities and other caregivers including men.11 RRTs will screen of children 6-59 months and PLWs for malnutrition, referral and follow up who are malnourished,provide emergency IYCF counseling for caregivers including PLWs,disseminating messages on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices,and distributing micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and PLWs.
in addition to referral for CU5 and PLWs with dangers signs and Medical problems to get required health services
Moreover ,they will support hygiene promotion and rising WASH awareness by providing Key messages to prevent the risk of COVID 19 transmission and other infections
This project will provide the appropriate nutrition services to all age and sex groups; boys and girls, women, men, with attention to people with special needs, without any sexual
discrimination, among IDPs and host communities, Whilst ensuring that inclusion is mainstreamed throughout these activities to avoid exclusion of the most vulnerable populations such as those with disabilities
As a part to COVID 19 response, SDI is going to apply adjustments of the nutrition activities to mitigate the risk of infection while ensuring continuity of life-saving nutrition services
As well as, SDI will conduct training to build capacity of frontline nutrition services providers on IYCF-E and CMAM programs in the context of COVID-19.
SDI will receive the needed nutrition supplies for this project from UNICEF through PAC organization and from WFP.

4 - Needs

Despite the relatively stable safety and security conditions across NWS since March 2020, the nutrition needs for the conflict- affected population particularly the pregnant and lactating women as well as children under 5 remain extremely high.Furthermore,and since the confirmation of the COVID19 first positive case across NWS in July 2020, the pandemic has been largely affecting the humanitarian conditions of the affected population.The pandemic has been resulting in increased rates of morbidity and mortality among the population particularly within the most vulnerable groups of children, women and people with disabilities. Further,Many of the people displaced have been displaced several times,80% of displaced are women and children intensifying existing vulnerabilities .According to (HNAP)for Syria, NWS as of March 2021 hosts more than 4.3 million populations, 2.7 million of whom are IDPs. Also, and within the region, sub-districts like Dana, Salqin, Harim, Maaret Tamisrin, and Afrin are among the highest in terms of overall population and IDPs content(Dana sub-district hosts around quarter of the total population across the whole NWS with an IDP VS HC ratio of more than 81%).The nutrition surveillance report by the nutrition cluster as of March 2021covering 35 Sub-districts across NWS, and involving 8470 PLW (43.3% of whom were married at an age younger than18)and 31727 CU5: That report informed an overallGAM rate across NWS of 2% with average severe acute malnutrition(SAM)rate of 0.1%, average moderate acute malnutrition(MAM)rate of 1.9% and a MAM rate of as high as 11.9% among the PLWs.All of the above pieces of information highly inform the need to sustain and scale up the nutrition response to people in need.

5 - Implementation of activities

SDI will implement the project as following SDI will operate 4 fixed CMAM center within the health facilities: 1. Al-Hakeem PHC center level 3 in Afrin includes (OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion and micronutrient interventions) 2. Kafr Naseh PHC center level 2 in Abin Saman in Atareb subdistrict includes (OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion and micronutrient interventions) 3. SDI Salqin PHC center in Salqin includes ( OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion, and micronutrient interventions),4-and Harim CMAM center At the fixed HFs,SDI will screen all children under 5 years and PLW who visit the clinics for nutrition services or other services (RH, or IMCI clinics, etc), select beneficiaries through MUAC screening and/ or Weight for Height/ Weight for Length (Z-score), provide SAM treatment for children with uncomplicated SAM using Plumpy nut and MAM treatment for children and PLW with MAM using Plumpy sup, Provide essential WASH services in CMAM sites, Provide micronutrient supplementation to pregnant women and children, Provide one-on-one counseling on WHO-recommended (IYCF-E) and maternal nutrition practices, disseminate IYCF- E messages. In the fixed center, the nutrition staff is one doctor, one nutritionist, and 1 nurse, also 4 CHWs for outreach activities. Also, 11 RRTs that will be dispatched to Atareb, Salqin, Afrin, Al Dana ,Harim , and Maaret Tamsrin subdistricts to provide field nutrition, child protection, health, and hygiene promoting services,The RRTs will screen,provide emergency treatment, if needed,registers and refers all identified cases(CMAM and IYCFE,IMNCI-E and BEmONC)to the respective fixed healthcare and PSS facilities with the special referral slip for tracking and immediate service in the final place of IDP destination

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Some women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated, such as divorced women and widows, may not be able to access basic services. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigation measure: more female CHWs will be allocated to provide services at the community level and RRTs will access all bene ciaries especially new IDPs to services and refer cases to receive appropriate and specialized care, in this project female staff is about 85%, that increases access of women and girls to health services. Risk related to GBV on CHWs, and RRTs Staff when visiting the homes, and IDPs sites, also risks of GBV on beneficiaries and health and nutrition staff in health facilities Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High, Mitigation measure: each two CHWs will work together, RRTs have female and male staff, training of health staff on providing first-line support, identification of GBV cases, safe referrals, and ensuring access of the affected population to the Minimum Initial Service Package for reproductive health,Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers of women and girls, Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High, Mitigation measure: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, including victim assistance,train staff on the code of conduct, and address any SEA complaints,SDI is a member of PSEA network.Risk of discrimination or if a service is provided to specific groups. Likelihood: Low, Impact:High Mitigation measure:the project design encompasses accessibility of services by vulnerable groups (physical accessibility,etc.)

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: Regular and timely provision of specialized nutritious foods and available to targeted bene ciaries. Risk1: Access to targeted areas becomes hindered or insecure. Mitigation measure1: Stock prepositioning in multiple, safe, and close locations to delivery sites and secure enough supplies to avoid stop the treatment of patients Assumption2: Technical skills are available. Risk2: shortage in the specialized technical staff in the targeted areas. Mitigation measure2: More non-health and health workers will be trained to provide appropriate services. Assumption3: No further deterioration of the security situation. Risk3: Attacks on health facilities and health workers during moving between camps, communities, and houses. Mitigation measure3: SDI will set up CMAM centers in safe and accessible locations that can be easily reached by bene ciaries. Moving the center to another safe area to avoid the risk of bombing. Assumption 4: All children and women especially those who may have physical limitations are able to reach services. Risk: Traditional habits prevent women from leave homes, physical disabilities prevent some people to access services Mitigation: SDI will bring the services close to communities and camps, to including all people in need with focusing on children, women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated, such as women who have experienced divorce, early/forced married and widowhood, who may not be able to access basic services including nutrition
