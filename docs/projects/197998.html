Project Summary

This project proposes to reach 7.150 people in Ancuabe with GBV programming, in a safe and inclusive manner.
CARE will prevent and respond to protection risks by establishing WGFSs in sites and within them, provide individual case management, MHPSS, in-kind support and economic empowerment initiatives to survivors.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will target IDP and host communities in Ancuabe district with GBV programming, delivering both prevention actions and response services to GBV survivors. Ancuabe is an underserved district with low presence of humanitarian actors. GBV prevention and risk mitigation activities will focus on raising community awareness and providing information on available services and referral pathways. GBV service provision will focus on identification and training of community mobilisers as first line responders, MHPSS, case management, construction of WGSS, in-kind support through distribution of dignity kits and livelihoods recovery activities. The project will identify and support particularly vulnerable women and adolescent girls, including pregnant and lactating women, women with disability, elderly women and widows.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

As an underserved district with a significant IDP population, this project will strengthen the availability of much needed GBV response services. In line with CARE's Cabo Delgado RGA findings and recommendations, the project will link GBV services with livelihoods recovery work.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project will adhere to the Do No Harm principle. This begins at the beginning of the project cycle, with project design being informed by various analyses conducted by CARE including a recent Conflict-Sensitive Gender Analysis and the needs assessment to be conducted at the beginning of the project to ensure a relevant and appropriate response and identify potential risks. CARE will regularly collect data through our Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs), as well as complement them with secondary data, to best examine how our intervention may interact with the conflict and social context. CAREs 4 FAM entry points include Linha Verde, a toll-free hotline that serves as a feedback mechanism for humanitarian assistance recipients, Complaint Boxes, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly with project focal point during activities. The results will inform any adaptation of activities to prevent negative impacts. Finally, CARE has robust safeguarding policy in place, with accountability holders throughout the organizations, ensuring safety and dignity are prioritized. In the proposed project, CARE plans to conduct a training on PSEA to all staff and key community stakeholders so they can identify, respond to, and prevent protection risks.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

All CARE projects are held to standard by the CARE Gender Marker, an accountability and learning tool that monitors how well gender has been integrated into the approach of humanitarian projects. This ensures all projects are, at the very least, gender sensitive. Through the needs assessment, routine project monitoring, CARE considers gender and different age groups by monitoring and analyzing sex and age disaggregated data and changing protection risks and needs. Based off these findings, the project design and activity implementation can be informed to adapt to the distinct needs of women, men, boys, and girls of different ages. The assessments to be conducted is a crucial tool to identify the different needs, roles, relationships, and dynamics between different population groups. CARE will adapt activities accordingly to findings and routinely follow-up on any feedback given by beneficiaries through Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAM). In WGSS, CARE will adapt planned activities to ensure their appropriateness and benefit to different age groups. In community dialogues, CARE will engage both men and boys to analyze, discuss, and transform their attitudes. Through these activities, CARE will explore and challenge harmful gender norms that perpetuate GBV, as well as the role men and boys’ play.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

During the needs assessment planned at the beginning of the project, CARE will be able to identify and assess the needs of People with Disabilities (PwDs). This will allow CARE to consult PwDs to determine how assistance can assistance can be delivered in an appropriate and accessible manner. During WGFS activities, CARE will accommodate PwDs to remove barriers to participation. This includes setting up a ‘buddy’ system for PWDs to access and have support in the WGSS and adapting the WGSS to be accessible and safe (e.g., removing tripping hazards, etc.). In the proposed awareness raising, CARE will highlight the specific protection risks PwDs face and how they can be responded to and prevented. Messaging dissemination will come in a variety of formats, to account for different disabilities (radio, use of images, speakerphones, etc.).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Overall, the project will have a minimal impact on the environment. As this project has a material support element through dignity kits, CARE will make smart procurement decision to reduce the environmental impact of humanitarian operations and keep packaging at a minimum (or have it be recyclable/reusable). In CAREs programming, aid packaging waste has minimal environmental impact as nearly all materials/packaging will be re-used by beneficiaries in some way. For example, at the CARE warehouse, pallets will be reused (all pallets made of wood which upon end of lifespan can be utilized by community members); items such as sacs distributed alongside family kits are used by community members to sell charcoal and other goods. For aid packaging, plastics straps can be used to secure tarpaulin in shelter and plastic bags have been used to transport and plant seedlings and wrap food in, such as cereals and beans. Resourcefulness of the recipients of aid and CAREs own reuse of material/packaging in its own warehouse, ensures resource efficiency. During project activities, CARE will monitor the use of material and packaging, to ensure no environmental damage occurs.
