Project Summary

In 2024, IOM's Child Protection (CP) programming is dedicated to proactively addressing and preventing child protection issues while empowering vulnerable children, parents, and caregivers in Camps 9 and 20. This aligns with resolutions from the 2022 and 2023 CP Sub-Sector rationalization processes. Objectives include enhancing Community-based CP (CBCP), cultivating inter-generational connections, and promoting positive behavioral changes. To achieve this, IOM aims to fortify CBCP at the camp block level, empowering the community to safeguard children. IOM supports adolescent groups, fostering skills and awareness for effective child protection.
Importantly, IOM provides specialized child protection services, including case management, structured activities in child-friendly spaces, and outreach at the block level, with a focus on the needs of children with disabilities, as well as those caring for family members, and young mothers. IOM’s case management prioritizes the needs of each child and their family, ensuring that issues are systematically tackled with a focus on the child's best interest, to mitigate and respond to interconnected risks and vulnerabilities leading to protection violations. IOM’s Psycho-Social Support (PSS) emphasizes education that aims to preserve Rohingya heritage and encourage inter-generational dialogue. Parents and caregivers receive PSS and positive parenting support through facilitated group discussions based on evidence-based curricula involving both adults and children/adolescents. IOM maintains its commitment to supporting child survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) and trafficking in the camps through both direct implementation and through partner organizations that work with other IOM units specializing in legal interventions.
IOM works with the government, including the Office of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) and Camp in Charge (CiC), and offers training to law enforcement agencies like the Armed Police Battalion (APBN) to mainstream child protection. IOM’s programming also adopts a comprehensive multi-sectoral approach and leverages community-based initiatives that prioritize inclusivity across age, gender, disability, and other dimensions of diversity. IOM’s CP team works closely with involving other IOM units, such as Health/Mental Health, Shelter, WASH, Site Management/Communication with Communities (CwC), and Social Cohesion to ensure the incorporation of child protection, child safeguarding, and considerations of age, gender, and disability throughout the programming, along with disaster preparedness and emergency response coordination. By intertwining strategic objectives with community engagement, IOM strives to create a robust and sustainable framework for child protection in the specified camps.
Discussed more below, to ensure accountability to the affected population, IOM establishes feedback mechanisms, regularly consults with beneficiaries, and has dedicated officers and champions focused on preventing and addressing Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) issues among beneficiaries.
IOM coordinates all activities with the CPSS, of which it is an active member. IOM is also an active member of the Protection Sector. We co-chair the Anti-Trafficking Working Group and have actively worked to ensure that trafficking issues affecting children are properly being understood and addressed. We are a technical member of the Age and Disability TWG, where again we will be working to ensure that CP issues are addressed and supported for the full response. We are also an active member of the Gender Diverse Population Working Group and are committed to supporting children of diverse SOGIGESC.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The decrease in humanitarian assistance and the concurrent developments in the security situation has worsened existing child protection concerns in the camp as evidenced by recent information collected in the JMSNA, CPIMS+, by internal IOM Protection Monitoring. For example, the J-MSNA and CPIMS+ data collected in 2023 indicate a rise in child labor cases within Rohingya refugee camps. This increase is attributed to the increasingly challenging living conditions, restricted movement, limited livelihood, and educational opportunities, and increasing violence, environmental hazards, and the multiple reductions in rations in 2023. According to internal IOM reports for Q3 2023, economic hardship and limited access to education have also resulted in a growing number of adolescent boys being recruited by criminal groups. Opportunistic criminal groups manipulate these young boys with the promise of financial support. Some adolescents are coerced into becoming drug peddlers or informants, exploiting their age to sell in the community without suspicion. This rising involvement of children in criminal activities adds another layer of complexity to an already precarious situation. Inter-Agency protection monitoring efforts coordinated by the Protection Sector note a marked increase in abduction for ransom and forced disappearance in the camps rose alarmingly during the first half of 2023 in comparison to the same reporting period for 2022 with a total of 367 registered cases representing a 490% overall increase. Some of the abductions impact children; on June 2, five Rohingya young males and one host community shopkeeper were abducted from the Rohingya, and a 16-year-old boy had his hand cut before being released as a message for families to pay the ransom. The modality in which refugees are kept hostage upon ransom illustrates the vulnerability of the population and has long term effects on children and the feeling of safety they have in their communities. During Q3 2023, internal reports by IOM highlight a noteworthy development: a concerning rise in missing children’s cases in camps 9 and 20. The reported instances have escalated from one per month to four or five, encompassing various reasons such as children leaving for work, traveling abroad, or experiencing disappearance linked to alleged physical abuse by madrasa teachers. Some parents have reported awareness of their child's departure but lose contact post-camp exit, prompting distress and eventual missing child reports. The potential existence of unreported cases raises significant apprehensions. Cuts in reduction in WFP rations, with adverse effects on nutrition, disease incidence, mental health, and an increased risk of GBV, potentially leading to child marriage, or survival sex, among other negative coping strategies. To prevent and respond to child protection concerns, it is imperative to strengthen community-based child protection efforts to ensure inclusion, participation, and meaningful access to services in a dignified manner, particularly for children with disabilities, as well as boys, girls, and individuals with diverse sexual orientations, gender identities, and expressions, and sex characteristics (SOGIESC). Empowering the refugee community, religious leaders, volunteers, children, and adolescents through capacity-building and awareness is essential to enhance their participation in decisions concerning their lives. Community-led activities and key messages delivered through community members can effectively reach high-risk adolescents and their families. Increasing individual case management, including legal services, and MHPSS activities are necessary to address the current high volume of protection concerns.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IOM maintains consistent coordination and collaboration with other protection actors, referring cases, as necessary. The organization's support for children and communities aligns with the rationalization principles, focusing on adopting an integrated approach, ensuring appropriate coverage of the camps, and promoting continuity. IOM is a co-chair of the Anti-Trafficking Working Group (ATWG) and actively participates in various protection forums. This active engagement aims to ensure that activities are continuous and aligned with the objectives of the Protection Sector, avoiding redundancy or overlap. Within the camps, IOM has programs in General Protection (GP), Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and Counter-Trafficking (CT) programming, which complements the Child Protection (CP) efforts. This comprehensive approach enables IOM to provide a wide range of services, offering a holistic package of support to the children they assist. Furthermore, IOM has established effective coordination with the Camp in Charge (CiCs), which facilitates the smooth execution of their work without encountering significant issues or obstacles.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IOM prioritizes protection in its humanitarian response in Cox's Bazar, implementing diverse, integrated activities to uphold the fundamental rights of crisis-affected individuals. The focus is on empowering them to build resilience, ensuring safety, and preserving dignity. In IOM, protection is viewed as a shared responsibility, with efforts aimed at reducing risks and enhancing the safety, resilience, and participation of all affected individuals. Specialized services, including general protection, counter-trafficking, child protection, and GBV support, are provided through integrated protection specialists. IOM conducts ongoing protection monitoring, community consultations, safety audits, and risk mapping sessions to address specific needs and safety concerns for diverse demographics, including people with disabilities and those in the LGBTQI+ community. As part of protection mainstreaming, IOM actively participates in technical working groups related to age, disability, and gender diverse populations. A 'Do No Harm' approach is integral to community engagement and activities. Capacity sessions are conducted for protection and non-protection community volunteers, advocates, and support groups to improve community access to information and decision-making support. Collaboration with non-protection components ensures mainstreaming initiatives, such as targeted Cash for Work programs for vulnerable families, accessible shelters, and safe spaces for gender-diverse individuals in livelihood programming. IOM remains committed to safeguarding the well-being and rights of those affected by the crisis.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Age considerations, particularly child safeguarding mechanisms, are integral to every facet of IOM programming, ingrained from development through implementation and evaluation. A continuous emphasis is placed on upholding the best interests of the child. For instance, IOM's MBR (Mother’s and Sister’s Ways) programming exemplifies age-appropriate, culturally acceptable two-way information exchange developed in consultation with women and girls. The Girl Shine Curriculum, implemented as part of this program, supports and empowers adolescent girls and caregivers. All IOM CP programming caters to diverse age groups, needs, and abilities, tailoring inclusive activities for both young children and adolescents. The involvement of parents and caregivers ensures a protective home environment, making them aware of CP risks and involving them in identifying solutions. IOM advocates for Age and Disability inclusion, evident in its role as a technical partner in the recently re-established Age and Disability Technical Working Group. While the CXB response lacks accurate disability prevalence information, IOM's CP programming, using the REACH 2021 estimation of 12% disability prevalence, aims to support at least 12% of children with disabilities. Collaboration with community-level Family Support Groups of Persons with Disabilities is integral to this support. IOM facilities, including CP spaces, are disability-friendly, with ongoing efforts to enhance inclusivity. Collaboration with the Gender Diverse Population Working Group ensures that CP services are inclusive to the gender-diverse and LGBTQI+ population. IOM remains committed to inclusive, age-sensitive programming and advocacy for the rights and well-being of all individuals involved in the response.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM's CP program prioritizes its community-based and participatory approaches, which aim to be responsive to a diversity of needs and ensure inclusive participation. IOM is actively engaged in seeking feedback from the affected population to gain insights into emerging risks and threats and to shape how activities are carried out. IOM maintains vigilant monitoring of the protection environment and activities within the camps to prevent any harm resulting from our initiatives and to ensure the safe implementation of multi-sectoral programs Further, IOM will continue to conduct gender and age responsive Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) surveys and other types of response monitoring through structured questionnaires, Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) desks, case management feedback surveys, and regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and consultations within CP spaces and at the community level, including with individuals with disabilities and those with diverse gender identities, including those with diverse sexual orientations and sex characteristics, are utilized to maintain a community-driven and ""do-no-harm"" approach.IOM has a well-established PSEA hotline, online platform, feedback boxes within the CP spaces and other facilities for the community for providing feedback. IOM also appointed PSEA champions from the staff in all IOM AoR camps who regularly provide capacity sessions to the volunteers, community-based support groups to ensure that are well informed about the reporting mechanism.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM is committed to ongoing efforts related to the PSEA. IOM will continue to roll out PSEA training packages and offer Training of Trainers (ToT) sessions for PSEA Champions in the coming year. This is intended to ensure that IOM staff, implementing partner staff, and volunteers adhere to the PSEA guiding principles and mandatory reporting systems. IOM has a designated and dedicated PSEA Officer to support and address the concerns of the beneficiaries. IOM has a robust reporting mechanism (hotline, online platform) in place and will continue to actively participate in the PSEA network while providing support to implementing partners (IPs) and the Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) in terms of capacity building and advocacy. IOM provides PSEA training to partners and ensure that partners have a PSEA policy and established reporting mechanism in place. IOM regularly delivers adapted PSEA training packages for the community volunteers, women support groups , and other community-based groups. IOM also disseminates IEC materials on PSEA to the community to ensure their understanding and accessibility on reporting is in place. Finally, IOM and WFP – in partnership with Translators without Borders had launched PSEA at the Front-line, a campaign to further empower frontline humanitarian workers and partners on PSEA through a comprehensive package of communication and outreach materials.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

IOM, along with its implementing partners, is an integral part of the Rapid Protection Assessment team, Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU), and Mobile Medical Team (MMT). This ensures an immediate response to the needs of people affected by crises and disasters. They maintain close coordination with Site Management and the Camp-in-Charge (CiC) offices and provide support to Disaster Management Unit (DMU) volunteers, offering training on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and safe referrals for gender-based violence (GBV) as well as child protection, including response to child/family separation during disasters. IOM also remains committed to raising awareness about Protection Key Messages, with the aim of helping the community mitigate the impact of disasters before, during, and after they occur. This effort includes house-to-house visits conducted by trained volunteers to ensure that children, including children with disabilities and other vulnerable populations receive necessary support. The organization maintains an environmentally friendly approach to providing assistance and tries to reduce paper and plastic waste, as well as limit the usage in the camps. IOM’s facilities, including our WGSS child-friendly corner in in camp 9 is constructed with treated bamboo and other environment friendly materials. IOM replicates this environmentally friendly approach wherever appropriate to ensure sustainability.
