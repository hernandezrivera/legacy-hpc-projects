Project Summary

The project aims to reach 70,000 children (boys and girls) ,teachers, PTAs and education
officials with essential education services. The influx of newly displaced children has
exerted pressure on already inadequate education services in camps and gatherings and
return areas which seriously suffered lack of education services. According to the
interagency report in March 2020 it was estimated that 81% of the existing schools had to
try and accommodate an additional newly displaced thousands of student’s boys and
girls which in turn affected the quality of education and effective classroom management,
leading to an increased risk of drop out in schools due to lack of conducive learning
environment for children especially for girls and children with special needs.
inflation and economic challenges impacted Sudanese people’s lives and their purchasing
power decreases, many families cannot afford schooling costs. As a result, children are in
risk of dropping out of school, becoming exposed to additional risks and violence and
child labor.
The overall objective of the project is to provide protective and qualitative education to the
school age children boys and girls in most deprived locations, camps, gatherings and
return areas.
1) Provision of protective and safe temporary learning spaces by using semi-permanent
material linked with basic separated sanitation facilities to children, youth and teachers
affected by acute emergencies 2) Capacity building and enhancement of Teachers, PTAs
and Gos officials on EiE, child centred disaster risk reduction, early childhood development
and on psycho social support and lifesaving message

1 - Needs

Hostilities continue to be reported in the Jebel Marra and prospects for the newlydisplaced remain uncertain. more than an estimated 280,000 displaced between 2020 and 2021 withmajority are in Darfur and South Kordofan the vast majority of whom are women andchildren. The flood affected over 950,000 peoples in 2020 and 2021 accross the country. the Education basic infrastructure was significantly ruin. The influx of newly displaced children has exerted pressure on alreadyinadequate education services in camps and gatherings and return areas which seriouslysuffered lack of education services. According to the interagency reports of 2020 , it wasestimated that 81% of the existing schools had to try and accommodate an additionalnewly displaced thousands student’s boys and girls ( South,West and Central Darfur ), whichin turn affected the quality of education and effective classroom management, leading toan increased risk of drop out in schools due to lack of conducive learning environment forchildren especially for girls and children with special needs. Based on the feedback fromthe departments of education( state ministries ) in North, South, Central Darfur and SouthKordofan that there is urgent needs to support establishment of TLs and provision ofteachers in undeserved localities and camps . An assessment conducted by UPO acrossschools in June, Jan, August 2020 in Darfur shows that the education service coverage inthese areas is below the minimum standard for EiE . One classroom is used by 180 pupils(boys and girls on average in IDPs camp in South Darfur 130 pupils and 96 in SouthKordofan. In all the locations, over 100 pupils, both boys and girls use a single latrine andthe existing structures are not gender segregated . Although community volunteers havestepped in to assist, the teacher to pupil ratio is also far from the INEE standard of oneteacher to 60 learners and many of these volunteers are urgently lack of capacity building,further affecting the quality of education. Overall, more IDPs make school absorptioncapacity even less and necessitates provision of semi-per classrooms and WASH facilitiesthat meet immediate children boys' and girls' needs. The education sector in East Darfuralso faces tremendous challenges due to a huge IDP caseloads and ongoing tribalconflicts, Based on the assessment conducted by UPO in Dec 2020 in close coordinationwith MoE Yassin, Sheria localities, gap has been reported, about 9,600 children both (5,000 boys and4,600 girls) are identified to be in need lifesaving education. WASH in schools is a keycomponent of safe learning environment and has been identified as an urgent need.
