Project Summary

To respond to the critical WASH needs in the targeted tribal District, SHED will rehabilitate Government/ Communal existing water supply systems/, Water quality monitoring and surveillance, water treatment, and provision of temporary water storage facilities. All relevant stakeholders including line departments, Public Health Engineering Department (PHED), District Education Office (DEO), District Administration, Wash Cluster, Community Organizations, Taleemi Islahi Jirga (TIJs), will be involved in the implementation of the project activities. The proposed project will ensure provision of clean drinking water to the targeted population through improvement/rehabilitation of DWSS endorsed by PHED through various scope of work including rehabilitation, improvement, construction, repair, installation of pipeline and solar system and installation of water tanks (500 gallon) for water storage at various communal locations.
SHED Hygiene promoters will mobilize the targeted communities to take collective actions for ensuring hygienic environment in the targeted area. Hygiene Promoters will conduct awareness raising sessions with 6,151 individuals (1,599 men1,661 women, 1,417 boys and 1,474 girls). SHED will provide sanitation kits to the most vulnerable for the construction of latrine to minimize the health risks associated with open defecation. Among these kits 10% will be provided to PWD and elderly persons with disable friendly commodes. Each sanitation kit will include shovel, pickaxe, p-trap, commode and PVC. For taking ownership and to ensure community participation, the beneficiaries will construct the latrines with their own resources. SHED will also provide 1000 WASH kits to assist the returnees to continue Health and hygiene practices. SHED will conduct Pre KAP (Knowledge, Attitude and Practices) Survey to measure the knowledge, attitude and practices of the people about the importance of healthy practices.
SHED will make functional WASH facilities in one school in the targeted areas. This will including 2/3 separate single latrines, washing pads and water supply.
The Project will contribute and compliment to other sectors like Health, Education and Nutrition as well as protection through provision of WASH facilities. The project is designed in a way to contribute significantly to gender equality and will equally address the issue of TDPs returnee men, women, boys and girls access to clean, safe drinking water and better sanitation facilities. SHED will ensure accountability and transparency towards the target group. SHED has Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) in place which will be implemented throughout the project. Complaint boxes will be installed at visible locations and the targeted population will be informed and educated on how to make complaints and how these complaints will be addressed. Monitoring and reporting will be integral part of the project. The M&E team will regularly visit the project area and will monitor the project activities. Gaps identified during the monitoring will be addressed for improvement and achieving the set targets.

Needs

As many as 441,700 families have returned to their homes in various areas of FATA /Tribal districts till 30 June 2018 according to UNOCHA report (UNOCHA – IDPs Factsheet as of 30-6-2018) out of which there are 40,757-Kurram, 74593-South Waziristan, 28,797-Orakzai and 96,960 families to Khyber tribal districts - who are facing numerous issues in rehabilitation. The major issue is their houses, health facilities, schools and other community infrastructure is fully or partially damaged. SHED designed the project activities on the following need assessment:-Preliminary Findings – FATA Vulnerability Assessment – 2017Issues and Gaps: Despite efforts that have been invested by both government and humanitarian partners to provide safe drinking water, half of the water sources remain unprotected (i.e. canals, rivers, open wells, ponds, springs, etc.). Poor sanitation, lack of water, lower water quality and inappropriate hygiene practices are catastrophic to the health of school-aged children. The physical environment and cleanliness of school facilities significantly affect the health, attendance rates and well-being of children. Disease spreads rapidly where hand-washing facilities and soap is not available, and where toilets are in poor condition. Lack or limited supply of electricity limits the operation of existing water supply schemes, while the rocky terrain makes hand pumps generally ineffective. The capacity of FATA line departments is also insufficient as they have limited technical staff and presence at the agency level making it harder to ensure sustainability of current initiatives. According to the Detail Needs Assessment (DNA) data analysis conducted by WASH cluster, the sources of drinking water available in Kurram Agency are mainly open springs 65% and 33% use piped water which is not confirmed to be potable at the point of collection. Only 1% use unprotected hand pump. Similarly, the analysis further shows that 69% women, 7% adult men and 19% girls collect water from the sources. Water source distance from house to collection points are within 500 meters 15%, 500 meters to 1KM 35%, 1KM to 3KM 42%, and more than 3 KM 6%. In addition, time consumed in collection of water, less than 15 minutes 4%, 15-30 minutes 44%, 30 minutes- 1hours 37%, more than 1 hour 15%. Only 21% respondents have access to latrine whereas 79% do not have access to latrine and they practice open defecation. 73% respondents were of the view that they construct latrine by themselves. These latrines are 13% Flush/Pour flash latrine, composting latrine 7%, pit latrine with slab 35%, latrine draining to canal/river 5% and latrine without slab is at 40%. 89% respondents were of the view that the reason behind not constructing the latrine is that it is very expensive while 7% expressed it was not a priority. The analysis regarding hand washing shows that 69% wash hand with water, however, 85% of the HHs observed did not have any dedicated hand washing facility available. Diarrhea was reported at 15% whereas Malaria at 11%. Proposed Actions:  Improve, install, repair and rehabilitate existing water systems with integrated water safety components to create more durable water supply options at Community and Institutional level. Improve and increase water storage at community, household and institutional level. Construct, rehabilitate and improve safe and adequate sanitation facilities through community led models with focus on component sharing modalities for the most vulnerable, supported by strong community mobilization. Rehabilitate and repair sanitation facilities in schools and health centers. Carry out hygiene promotion activities at household, community and institutional level using specified approaches. Reinforce and systematize community feedback mechanisms.
