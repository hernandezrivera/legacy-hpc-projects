Project Summary

The project will reach the most vulnerable 207,000 people (74,520 girls, 49,680 women, 37,260boys and 45,540 men) which corresponds to 34,500 HH, (Nassir 9000HH, Maiwut 4500HH, Akobo 10,000HH, Leer 5000HH and Fangak 6000HH) 60 % female headed and 40% male headed and 15% of the overall percentage of persons with disability. We shall contribute to FSL cluster objective 2 ‘’Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19’’ by providing emergency complementary production inputs (crop kits, fast growing vegetable kits and fishing kits) and also FSL cluster objective 3 “Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks “ through Capacity building on, natural resource management, value addition, post-harvest handling and marketing. This will improve food production and provision of fishing kits will contribute to better nutrition at the HH level and in other cases improve HH income due to the sale of excess fish, this will take place from January 2022 to December 2022. We shall also provide unconditional cash assistance in line with FSL cluster objective 1 Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19. This response plan is aimed at an effort to prevent an even more severe food shortage as well as strengthening the coping capacities of the vulnerable host community and IDPs. Nile Hope shall liaise with FAO to get in kind support for the crop fishing and vegetable kits for the emergency responses but will procure for the other ongoing developmental projects
The production inputs will be given to 25,000 HH (9,500 Female HH and 6,600 Male HH) 7500 in Akobo,5000 HH in Leer,4000HH in Fangak,2500 HH in Maiwut and 6000 HH in Nassir ,which corresponds to 150,000 beneficiaries vulnerable Host Communities and IDPs and unconditional cash assistance to 9500HH (2000 HH in Maiwut,3000HH in Nassir,2500 in Akobo and 2000HH in Fangak) which corresponds to 57,000 beneficiaries. Special focus will be given to people with disability to make sure they are not left out, we shall also ensure that we main stream protection in all our activities to ensure that we are not exposing the beneficiaries to harm with our intervention. Capacity building training will be conducted on improved crop, vegetable and fishing practices ,value addition ,marketing and continuous practical sessions in the farms to selected recipient beneficiaries at the Payam level as TOTs to the rest of the Community for sustainability purposes targeting 10,000 people (6000 women and 4000 men). The overall project objective is improved food security and livelihoods at house hold level for the vulnerable population at risk of hunger and malnutrition

1 - Needs

Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in South Sudan with increased & protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impact of market poor market functionality due the protracted conflict, COVID-19 pandemic &the political uncertainty in most of the supply routes. The annual flooding also has really affected the already vulnerable community especially in greater Upper Nile .8.3 million people face acute food insecurity in the country, 2 million acutely malnourished women and children & 2 million IDPs (WFP Situation report #299). Akobo is severely food insecure (FSNMS round 27, Feb 2022) and at risk of famine if not support is provided this is due to the protracted conflict and the massive flooding which has destroyed people’s livelihoods and caused large displacements. Most of the cultivated lands have been destroyed. This is expected to have consequences on food security for vulnerable IDPs, returnee &host community. Immediate scale-up of humanitarian assistance is needed to save lives and prevent total collapse of livelihoods. Leer County has been indiscriminately affected by the protracted conflict, recurrences of flood .It is classified as a county of extreme concern according to FSNMS round 27, Feb 2022. The County is predominately agro-pastoral community whose main livelihoods depend on rearing of livestock, subsistence farming, fishing and petty trade. Fishing activity remains to be the most well fitted livelihood activity. They also engage in rain-fed agriculture (maize and sorghum) once a year during the main season. The swamps create opportunities for irrigation & fishing activities to take place both in dry & main seasons. There is need to continue supporting the community to prevent further escalation of food insecurity. Fangak is placed as a county of extreme concern according to FSNMS round 27, Feb 2022. The community there is vulnerable &It is hosting IDPs fleeing fighting In Unity state &returnees from Sudan. We also have the recent flood in 2021 that have displaced more than 10,827 people (IRNA August 6th 2021 .This has exposed the community to further vulnerability. There is also decreased access to food &disruptions in the supply chain due to the conflict between government & IO forces over control of the Tonga market which supplies Fangak. Maiwut is placed as a county of very high concern according to FSNMS round 27, Feb 2022. The community has experienced recent shock caused by flooding that caused mass displacement which left them extremely vulnerable. They are also trying to recover from the effect of the ongoing conflict between the warring parties over control of the area & is also hosting high number of returnees from Ethiopia. The population is in dire need of immediate humanitarian support. Nasir County remains a high priority location (FSNMS round 27, Feb 2022) as a result of protracted conflict which has led to multiple displacement which prevents the people from fully engaging in food production coupled with poor harvest due to flooding in 2021. The already vulnerable host community is extremely overwhelmed by the many returnees coming from Ethiopia & IDPs who previously ran from the conflict hot zones. The compounding effects of widespread violence & sustained economic decline that have further diminished the capacity of people to face threats to their safety &livelihoods. Nile Hope aims at preventing food shortage by providing production inputs (fishing, crop &vegetable kits), & also scale up the ongoing unconditional cash assistance to ensure the community access food & other basic needs. We shall also continue building their resilience by supporting producer groups through capacity building training on improved production, post harvest handling, value addition & marketing .We have ongoing projects in partnership with DCA, FAO, UNICEF &WFP in the proposed counties to continue supporting the vulnerable communities but the gap is enormous.

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash is convenient and does not require huge logistic support like the in kind support .It also allows the beneficiary to choose the lifesaving need that they need in the House holds. Support to local market is required for South Sudan to grow, cash assistance gives a chance to the local markets to grow, due to the reason that in cash projects, there is a small amount of resources allocated for improving or establishing market infrastructure as well as the capacity building of the traders through skills enhancement

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Nile Hope is part of the cash working group and part of the reach led joint market monitoring initiative (JMMI) where market data is collected and analyzed monthly to inform cash programming. We also have ongoing cash and market projects in the target locations which allows NH to conduct rapid market assessments that provides quality information regarding the markets in the target locations

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

We promote meaningful access, safety and dignity of our beneficiaries We shall ensure all the distribution sites are selected in consultations with all the stake holders and the local authority to ensure they are safe and accessible to all the groups targeted. Appropriate distribution time will be agreed upon with all the stakeholders and the beneficiaries’ representative to ensure it is convenient for the target beneficiaries and is not too early in the morning or late in the evening, which can expose them to insecurity and other protection issues We shall also work in collaboration with our GBV sector and other actors on the ground to ensure that we work with the existing structures to combat any protection or GBV related issues ensuring we link our project to the established referral pathways . In the project committees we shall have a well detailed selection criteria to ensure it is inclusive and representative .We shall also deploy the vulnerability matrix during selection of the beneficiaries to guide the process and our team will use the set criteria to verify the people to be targeted. The Feedback and complain mechanism will be localized and will also encourage the use of pictorial illustrations to ensure the community understand and utilize the established mechanisms. In the feedback and complain committees, we shall ensure all the groups and gender are represented to ensure the feedback is not biased. Nile Hope objectively works and cooperates with the Community through their representatives and the local authority to ensure they are no conflicts arising from selection of the beneficiaries ,targeting of the payams and bomas . This is aimed at ensuring that the decisions made includes all the stake holders and are not left to the local authority to decide, this approach has build trust in the areas we are serving .We remain neutral and only focuses on the needs identified in the community
