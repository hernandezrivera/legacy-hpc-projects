Project Summary

The project seeks to contribute to the reduction of mortality and morbidity related to drought and disease outbreaks in drought affected areas of Zimbabwe through the provision of life-saving and emergency basic health services and interventions. The project will be done in collaboration with UNICEF, UNFPA, World Vision and other Health partners with the thrust of creating synergies among the agencies will be used to leverage health impacts.
WHO interventions will seeks to strengthen epidemic preparedness and response for 3 million people in 52 drought affected districts in Zimbabwe. This will be achieved through supporting capacity enhancement of coordination structures at national, provincial and district levels. These structures include the Rapid response teams as well as the Civil protection committees at various levels. The project will also strengthen diseases surveillance for priority diseases conditions as well as malnutrition. To compliment surveillance efforts, the project will also support improving laboratory capacity through capacity building and procurement of reagents for the laboratory. Cases management for diarrhoeal conditions, acute malnutrition and other drought related conditions and this will be carried out in collaboration with UNICEF. The project will also provide technical support for preventative vaccination campaigns which will be carried out in the 52 districts target for interventions. For improved programme quality, MOHCC and health partners shall regularly carry out supportive visits to the facilities to ensure adherence to treatment protocols as well as reporting

1 - Activities

The project shall focus on surveillance including nutrition and laboratory surveillance, case management, Coordination and Water, sanitation and Hygiene ( WASH). The proposed intervention will be implemented in the 52 drought affected priority districts in the country.Under surveillance component the main focus will be rolling out the third generation of Integrated Diseases surveillance and response which is a strategy to build district and provincial structures capacity to detect and response to diseases and conditions that cause higher levels of morbidity , mortality and even disability. Trainings shall be rolled out from the national, provincial and district levels and it is expected that this will contribute to improved flow of health information and effective response. The project will also seeks to improve laboratory capacity through capacity enhancement of laboratory staff on detection of priority disease conditions . Further to that the laboratories shall also receive reagents to support detection. Nutrition surveillance shall also be supported in the 52 priority districts and monitoring of SAM, MAM as well as other conditions such as wasting, low birth weight, stunting and pellagra shall be carried out under the proposed interventions. It is worth noting that standard tools from MOHCC shall be used. Printing and distribution of Surveillance tools (Case definition charts, Case based investigation forms, Active case search tools, and Early warning and alert forms and nutrition surveillance tools.Training of Health Workers on Case management of diarrhoea and acute malnutrition shall be carried out. To ensure that the gains realized through training are consolidated, MOHCC and other health partners shall also carry out regular supportive supervision to support trained health workers with on the job mentoring on case management . Diarrhoea and cholera kits shall also be procured and distributed to the target districts.For effective detection and response, the project will also train Rapid Response teams at National, Provincial and district levels to ensure that they deliver their mandate of developing and overseeing the implementation of emergency preparedness strategies, action plans and procedures. The project will also strengthen coordination platforms such as Civil Protection structures. On WASH interventions, the project seeks to improve access to potable water supplies through procurement of water quality monitoring kits as well as training of environmental health cadres who are responsible for water quality monitoring at all levels.To ensure that the impact of the project is realized WHO will also support regular monitoring of progress in the field together with teams from the Ministry and other health partner. WHO will also support the documentation of best practices that are realized from the field for peer learning.

2 - Indicators

Success of the project shall be measured by the prevalence of diarrhoeal diseases as well as case fatality rates from conditions such as cholera. For surveillance, completeness and timelines rates will be used to measure the success of the project. The number of samples received and processed will be used used to measure the effectiveness of laboratory support. For nutrition interventions GAM, SAM and prevalence of conditions such as marasmus and pellagra will also be used
