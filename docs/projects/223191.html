Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to quality child protection services in targeted LGA’s of Borno State, particularly in the communities of Bama and Pulka In Gwaza LGA’s. These areas were selected due to their proximity to the epicenter of Boko haram and the high influx of returnees, escapees and CAAG. The goal of the intervention is to target the most vulnerable and marginalized boys and girls ensuring they return to a safe and protected environment, thrive and meet their psychosocial support needs, through safe family sessions, life skills sessions and case management, and socio-economic reintegration (skills acquisition training) for former CAAG and other vulnerable children. The project will target 6300 direct beneficiaries including PWD (2000 girls, 1800 boys, 1,400 women, and 1,100 men. Beyond those directly involved, other community members will benefit from this intervention through awareness creation, sensitization and knowledge sharing. 1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states remains one of the most severe in the world today. A total of 7.9 million people is estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024). The ongoing insurgency has led to the breakdown of community protective structures, women and children constitute 78% of the overall population affected by the crisis with 12% of the affected population living with disability. thousands of women and girls and boys have been abducted with ongoing violence and its socioeconomic impact translated into increased protection risks. Key protection concerns related to increased exposure of girls and boys to abuse, exploitation, neglect, abduction and violence, including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), mostly affecting girls. In more than 14 years of conflict, affected people in north-east Nigeria have experienced violence, repeated displacement, loss of and/or separation from family members, destruction of property and livelihood assets and deteriorating living conditions. Their resilience has been tested over and over, with children, women, female-headed households, people with disabilities and older persons enduring the most suffering and becoming even more vulnerable.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

LABDI will establish a safe and accountable complaint and feedback mechanism in the targeted communities and provision of AAP personnel, which will address any form of SEA that may arise. LABDI will involve the community at every step of the program, especially focusing on the vulnerable and marginalized, including women, girls, boys, men, people with disabilities, and the elderly. All staff, volunteers and associates, will undergo gender-based violence (GBV) and protection from sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training before they begin working on the project. This is to both help prevent GBV and SEA and also to spot potential warning signs of gender exploitation and abuse in targeted communities. LABDI will collaborate with other agencies, communities, and stakeholders to develop a more holistic environment of protection for vulnerable groups, and children at risk. LABDI will deploy the principle of “Do no harm” and employ appropriate measures to avoid exposing people to further harm as a result of project actions and ensure that the environment and way in which assistance is provided do not expose people to further hazards, violence or human rights abuses or violations. LABDI will sensitively manage information so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized, LABDI will ensure that the affected population has access to humanitarian assistance against any form of direct or indirect discrimination.
