Project Summary

South Sudan continues to suffer from unacceptably high levels of malnutrition. According to IPC-AMN 2024 findings, the malnutrition crisis in South Sudan continues to deteriorate in 2025, driven by multiple, converging humanitarian crises. Approximately 4.3 million children under five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, will need life-saving treatment and preventive care. This includes 0.65 million children under five at risk of severe acute malnutrition, 1.4 million at risk of moderate acute malnutrition, and 1.1 million mothers requiring urgent treatment. In 49 counties, acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%, with one county in Upper Nile projected to reach IPC-AMN phase 5. By mid2025, eight out of ten children under five are anticipated to be at risk of acute malnutrition, with 7.69 million people, including 1.5 million young children, facing severe food insecurity. Repeated outbreaks of fever, diarrhea, and respiratory infections, coupled with poor nutrition, water, and sanitation access, add to the crisis. The situation is further aggravated by economic challenges, climate impacts, ongoing conflicts, heightened GBV risks, and spillover effects from the Sudan crisis. In 2025, eight children out of every ten children under five are projected to be at risk of acute malnutrition, IPC-AFI results suggest that 7.69 million people including 1.5 million children under five to be severely food insecure by July 2025. The response strategy aims to provide timely, quality treatment for 0.53 million children with acute malnutrition,1 million of whom have severe or moderate cases and 0.8 million pregnant and breastfeeding women. Preventative interventions will reach 2 million children aged 6-59 months, 2 million pregnant and breastfeeding women, and adolescent girls. Nutrition interventions will follow a multisectoral approach, coordinating with WASH, Health, FSL, Protection, and Education clusters. The Nutrition Cluster will enhance coordination, implementation, and localization efforts, especially at subnational levels, and strengthen real-time nutrition data systems for faster response and resilience programming. Humanitarian assistance will transition gradually to government support, leveraging platforms like the HSTP. Nutrition Cluster will continue strengthening robust nutrition information system in the country.
Overall, the response will target 383,834 SAM, MAM and PBW cases across various Counties (Kapoeta East 191,917, Wau County 191,917 and Tonj North County, including the non-displaced, returnees, and internally displaced persons. More so, the IRSS will make deliberate efforts to intensify coordination with County Health Departments, Stakeholders, Partners and integrate programming with the Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Health, and Protection clusters to ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical multi-sectoral basic services that will contribute to the reduction of acute malnutrition and ultimately reduce morbidity and mortality.
IRSS will closely monitor the nutrition status of the population, especially screening of children under five years and pregnant and Breastfeeding women (PBW) and strengthen the nutrition response through the Food Security Nutrition Surveillance and Monitoring System (FSNMS) and use of routine program data. The children under 5, adolescent boys and girls and PLWs are also vulnerable to consume minimum food intake due to natural and manmade disasters like flood, drought and conflict. As a result, an estimated 4.3 million children under five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, will need life-saving treatment and preventive care.

1. Needs

To address the suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable children and women, Islamic Relief South Sudan response project will aim to increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative and curative nutrition services for children, adolescents and women at 9 static nutrition facilities and 7 outreach sites in Wau, Tonj North, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North counties experiencing multiple deprivations, including high acute malnutrition rates, food insecurity, morbidities, high GBV concerns and poor WASH conditions.In 2025, the IDPs, Returnees and Host communities of the proposed project area are vulnerable due to flood, drought and conflict as a result, an estimated 116,271 people, including 69,763 children under the age of 5, and 46,508 pregnant and breastfeeding women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourishment in the Kapoeta East and North of Eastern Equatorial States, Tonj North of Warrap State, and Wau of Western Bahr-Ghazal State out of the cluster identified People in Need.Their situation is worsened by localized drought/dry spell in Kapoeta, intracommunal conflict, access constraints and operational interference by arm groups, public health challenges in Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, and Eastern Equatoria states. The leading cause of morbidity is malaria, followed by acute respiratory infections and acute watery. diarrhea. In 2025, morbidity rates are expected to increase proportionally for each of these causes as they did in 2024, Therefore, out of the cluster PIN, IRSS response project will reach to 383,834 SAM, MAM and PBW cases across various Counties (Kapoeta East 191,917, and Tonj North County 191,917), including the non-displaced, returnees, and internally displaced persons.To address the acute malnutrition of the people in need the Islamic Relief South Sudan will be providing support to the food insecure malnourished children and PLWs through introducing nutrition sensitive kitchen garden and sensitizing the community people for hygiene practices and optimum maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN). The project will also focus on early childhood development through providing playing materials for the children in line with Nutrition Cluster objectives. The project will help the community people for early child protection and gender-based violence and risk mitigation at nutrition sites. The objectives of the proposed project: 1. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025 2. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PBW in prioritized locations by the end of 20253. To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 20254. To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025

2. Project activities

To provide emergency nutrition support to crises- affected under-five children and women, Islamic Relief South Sudan will focus on implementing the following key activities at the IRSS supported health and nutrition facilities in the project targeted counties. 1) Conduct facility and community-based screening, admission, and treatment of 15,276 (6,110 boys and 9,166 girls) 6-59 months children suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and referral of under-five years children, 37,776 (15,110 boys and 22,666 girls), pregnant and breastfeeding women 13,500 identified suffering from moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) to the nearby facility with the TSFP services. 2) Conduct 70 sessions of integrated health and nutrition mobile outreach clinics to provide community mass screening, curative consultation, and EPI vaccination to under-five children, PLW and vulnerable communities living in hard-to-reach areas. 3) Conduct quarterly community mass awareness and advocacy campaigns on causes of malnutrition, its prevention, MIYCN counseling, and other behavioral change reaching 72,353 caregivers of 0-23 months children. 4) Conduct 20 quarterly cooking demonstration sessions, Kitchen gardening and optimal MIYCN trainings to 14 groups of mother-to-mother support groups comprising of 200 members. 5) Conduct Vitamin A supplementation to 37,500 boys and girls aged 6-59 months and deworming campaigns with Albendazole tablets to 40,500 children aged 12-59 months in semester 1 & 2 respectively. 6) Recruit, train and provide incentives to community nutrition volunteers and social mobilizers to support active case finding, screening and community referral of SAM and MAM cases. 7) Procure and provide preventive packages such as soaps and LLITN to caregivers of 5-59 months children and PLW admitted and treated for SAM and MAM for prevention of hygiene related diseases and malaria infection. Procure and preposition IEC and ECD materials to nutrition facilities for stimulation play of under-five children. 8) Link under-five children and PLW identified suffering from SAM and MAM with their caregivers to FSL for cash voucher assistance, health for medical, EPI, & antenatal care services, WASH for WASH kits, education and Protection services. 9) provide capacity building trainings to the staffs. 10) Conduct joint supportive supervision and monitoring visits to health and nutrition OTP facilities together with CHD and other implementing partners. Conduct Quarterly mass MUAC screening for acute malnutrition and improve active case finding through Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and caregivers’engagement and treatment of SAM (15,276) cases and referral of MAM (37,776) and PLWs (13,500) for management, provide relevant nutrition supplies and drugs for management of acute malnutrition, Conduct CMAM training to health & Nutrition workers & CNVs o Manage CMAM beneficiaries using recommended guidelines andprotocols In the Nutrition centers and communities.
