Project Summary

The project’s objective is to facilitate voluntary returns for communities of IDP households remaining in IDPs camps in the metropolitan area, through the provision of assistance package of services, including basic medical care and access to livelihood support for empowering vulnerable women. Through this project IOM aims to assist the 12 000 most vulnerable IDPs

Needs

An estimated 38,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs), remain in 27 camps across earthquake affected areas in Haiti West Department. The vast majority of the IDP population continues to live in extremely precarious conditions as at least 40% of IDP households continue to live under makeshift tents which have inevitably deteriorated with time. Some other camps have gradually –and naturally- integrated within the urban fabric of the metropolitan area of Port-au-Prince through efforts of the IDP community itself to settle through improvements to the transitional shelters donated by the international community as part of the post-earthquake response to temporary housing, or by building new houses on the land where they were displaced to. Ongoing displacement has been one of the most visible and important consequences of the 2010 earthquake. Despite a joint effort by the Government of Haiti (GoH) and the international community to prioritize sites for return and relocation programs based on the different degrees of risk the population is exposed to (taking into account factors such as sanitation, access to water, exposure to environmental risks, security and potential threats of eviction), IDPs continue to face risks of various natures. Individuals living in sites composed in their majority of makeshift tents are particularly vulnerable to the adverse effects of heavy rain and high winds. As WASH facilities may be damaged under extreme weather conditions, IDPs are also exposed to an increased risk of contraction of water-borne diseases. Furthermore, the risk of forced eviction increases for the entire IDP community as we enter into the tenth anniversary of the earthquake and owners of the land where the camps are located become ever more impatient to claim their properties back.

Activities or outputs

Relocation assistance and Health Care for the Camp Residents.• Registration/Needs Assessment: • Identification of Appropriate Housing Solution:• Health Care• Camp Decommissioning• Follow-Up VisitsEmpowering Women and Improving Livelihood In this project, IOM will provide trainings and workshops to approximately 80 vulnerable mothers and single mothers with social awareness and sensibilisation program to enhance capacity for leadership and alliance among rural women to build confidence, strengthen mutual support, develop confidence, advocacy and public speaking skills for influencing decision that affect their lives and facilitate meaningful participation of women in decision-making process. One of the objective is to promote diversification of women’s opportunities for livelihoods and improve household incomes by introducing market-linked enterprises.
