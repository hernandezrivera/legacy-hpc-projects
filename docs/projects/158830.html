Project Summary

The project addresses the country strategic objectives SO2 and SO3 in terms of providing the essential services, and responding to protracted stress. The project will focus on ensuring access and building the resilience of education staff, children and their families, which is directly linked to education cluster specific objective 1. Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children and objective 2. Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to potential emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school based psychosocial services. The schools are included among the lists identified by MoE, cluster and WV’s assessment. Data was collected and needs were aggregated from the recent assessment conducted by WV field team in the targeted locations. The objectives will provide 2950vulnerable students, teachers and PTA with the support needed to maintain their resilience and to promote safe access in particular locations. It will ensure vulnerable students (boys and girls), school staff and community ( parents and community members) affected by the conflict and insecurity have equitable access to safe education through activities that will provide them with: 1. physical resources which aim at improving their access to safe and inclusive education services through providing temporary learning spaces ( TLS) and tools. 2. It will provide students, staff and community members (PTA) with means to cope with protracted stress through PSS/PFA capacity building. 3. WV will conduct PSS/PFA related activities such as psychodrama at schools to help children cope with stress. The project will establish strong monitoring and learning mechanisms to assess and track progress against project objectives. The monitoring will be conducted in accordance with existing WV monitoring systems under the local responsibility of the field team in close liaison with the country office (Jerusalem).

1 - Needs

World Vision’s 2019 updated assessment for 84 Area C schools in its areas, filtered schools on the basis of high potential risk of violations due to location near segregation wall, bypass roads, proximity to settlements and checkpoints. Additional information was obtained through general observations by WV field staff; FGD with community members, and children. Secondary data was collected from targeted schools, sources including EiE cluster the DoE’s and OCHA’s reports. Findings show that 45% of WV’s schools are subjected to education related violations from IF and settlers’ attacks. More than 35 schools reported experiencing one or more violation within the past year. 20 of the assessed schools need to rehabilitate their spaces to ensure safety and inclusiveness, as children need to be in a safe and inclusive environment. Through education means, children can receive the PSS support to assist them in fostering positive coping and adaptation mechanism when confronted by adversity. As per the HNO, Education process continues to be severely compromised in Area C and children are in need of PSS support. Data has revealed that 48% of assessed schools don’t have counselors to support children in need. Teachers likewise, lack capacity to provide children with PSS services revealing a high need to capacitate staff. WV’s previous work through Ocha’s 2nd standard allocation 2018 grant implemented a need based Psychosocial support (PSS) to promote the wellbeing of children, staff and family members in 36 schools. Field findings revealed the need to continue with similar interventions due to recurrent events and to sustain the resilience of children. Findings from FGD’s show the provision of PSS support to the targeted beneficiaries has reduce negative behaviors. Needs persist in almost all of Area C schools, WV is committed to support schools through its technical program, however it cannot secure the needed funds for all schools in its areas. Accordingly, a focused assessment was carried out for 11 targeted schools due to their high need and with YWCA a need based response was identified to provide further support. Different needs of girls and boys are considered, as girls are more susceptible to GBV in marginalized areas. Provision of extra classrooms for two schools in Hebron, will contribute to children’s safety and reduction of school drop-out. The 11 schools; 7 co-ed, two girls’ and two boy’s schools, are assessed as generally worse off, in terms of violations, infrastructure and safety. Seven , are selected from areas identified by MoE as most vulnerable in 2018/2019 .Two schools are selected due to dropouts and the risks faced from the long commute to nearby villages, particularly for girls. All selected schools are subjected to human right violations facing more than 60 violations within the past year. 6 out of 11 schools do not have counselors to support children. In reference to MoE JPAS, there is an urgent need to provide children in Area C with PSS, as also reflected in 2019 HNO. Installing extra temporary learning spaces (TLS) in two schools in Hebron will address the issue of safety and provide adequate and inclusive learning environment to help decrease dropout rate. The need to enhance the security at Oreef School in Nablus persists. WV has enhanced the safety measurement at the school, it installed a steel shade to protect students from stones thrown by settlers from the overlooking settlement. PSS training to staff, students and parents was provided. DRR tools and evacuation training were conducted through WV’s technical program. The needs at the school are pertinent, WV has committed itself to finish phase two of the shade, as it needs to cover all area from the main school gate, reaching to the main entrance of the building. The school’s fence needs to be rehabilitated and a new emergency exit is essential, as the existing one leads the settlement which jeopardizes students’ safety.

2 - Activities

The 11 schools are included among the list identified by the MoE and EiE cluster as the most vulnerable to attacks. The needs are aggregated from WV recent assessment. All Activities will be gender-sensitive, allowing male and female learners – together or separately - to do activities that are familiar, reassuring and fun. Activities will also attend to the needs of CWD’s. Objective 1: Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for children. Indicator Activity 1.1- Provision of 2 caravans to 2 targeted schools. It will be implemented while ensuring high community participation. The schools were also selected based on needs as identified school principals, DoE education department and community. The additional temporary classrooms (TLS) will help the schools add two grade levels to help accommodate students who have to walk distances to other areas to continue their education. It will also help provide safe and adequate learning spaces to replace old dire ones. 1.2 Provision of material support and teaching/learning supplies to 5 schools. The 5 schools will be provided with material support and teaching and learning supplies to enable students and teachers execute activities. WV will assess the needs of tools and supplies teachers and students need to implement school based activities. It will also use the tools made available through the previous grant to capitalize on the activities and ensure building the capacity of all staff, including new ones. All relevant tools and rehabilitation will be carried out through WV procurement department following donor’s requirements. 1.3: Provide essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools to ensure that schools environments are safe for students. As per the assessment conducted one of the targeted schools have reported the need to rehabilitate its school fence and open an emergency exit to protect students, ensure safety while within school perimeters, and promote school attendance. Objective 2: Most Vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school based psychosocial services. Activity 2.1: Provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff and students affected by the education related violations. This aims to improve teachers and students coping mechanisms with emergency situations, and release stress. WV assessment revealed that the schools are in high need due to location, number of violations and lack of counselors, as only 4 schools reported to have school counselors. There is still a need to build capacity of school staff in new added schools and build on the capacity of previous staff (teachers, counselors and principals). WV has produced evidence of the effectiveness of its PSS /PFA support, of which curriculum was developed by WHO. The manual developed by the ministry is based on WHO of which is the same as WV’s, and due to the results published in Disaster Health Journal, October 2015, show positive benefits to beneficiaries confirming that PSS/PFA contributed to safety and reduced distress. The modules developed through Ocha’s 2nd allocation strategy has helped counselors and teachers use the manual in a more effective way. 2). 2.1.2 A teacher training will be conducted by WV in collaboration with MoE to include all school staff. 2.1.3 To ensure the activities related PFA/PSS training is well implemented and enriched to reach out to all targeted children and help reinforce their academic school work, in collaboration with YWCA a training of teachers and counselors on establishing relevant PSS activities to help children release stress 2.1.3 Awareness sessions to parents on PSS/PFA techniques will be provided by WV in collaboration with MoE supervisors and school trained counselors.

World Vision uses the "Do No Harm" Principle (also known as "Local Capacities for Peace" or LCP analysis tool) to integrate conflict-sensitivity wherever it is relevant.
