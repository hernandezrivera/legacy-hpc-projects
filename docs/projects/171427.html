Project Summary

This project aims at providing emergency food security and livelihood support to
vulnerable households engaged in farming, animal breeding or shery with limited
financial capacity to acquire the needed production inputs, as well as acutely vulnerable
households with limited capacity to cover basic food needs in Gaza Strip with a specific
focus on ARA. Vulnerable households affected by the IHL/IHRL violations, deteriorating
economic situation, C19 pandemic, and/or other types of shocks and engaged in farming,
animal breeding or shery will receive livelihood support (i.e. cash assistance, vouchers,
in-kind rehabilitation). It will help them to maintain their production capacity and protect
their livelihoods, while the most acutely vulnerable households suffering from limited
access to food and to income opportunities will be supported through the provision of
unconditional cash-based assistance (MPCA) and the creation of temporary job
opportunities (CFW) to cover their immediate basic needs. PUI also focuses on the
infrastructural challenges for protecting agricultural livelihoods in the ARA, such as
shortages of electricity and water in agriculture, through the provision of solar PV systems
for agricultural wells and water ponds. The Action will enable vulnerable households
engaged in farming, animal breeding, or shery, including vulnerable women, to maintain
their agricultural activities and enhance their coping capacity to future shocks.
The project will contribute to the following overarching country strategic objectives: SO2:
The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the
provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under
IHL. SO3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis,
including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root
causes of threats and shocks are pursued. Specifically, it will contribute to the following
food security sector objectives: Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and
their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced (CO1) and
Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to

meet their basic food needs (CO2).
The planned action was closely coordinated with the Food Security Sector, Ministry of
Agriculture (MoA), ministry of social development (MoSD), INGOs, NGOs, local partner
CBOs, as well as with other relevant local actors. Also, coordination with key actors in the
area of intervention took place with ACTED, Care, Action against Hunger, ACAD, PARC, and
UAWC.
It is worth mentioning that extensive consultations were held with all the main
stakeholders to discuss the proposed action, avoid duplications, and ensure prioritization of the most urgent needs.
PUI will implement the proposed action with its two strategic partners, namely PARC and
UAWC, to benefit from and build on the added value and specific experience of each
organization. The partnerships with PARC and UAWC have proven to be successful and
effective in the joint projects implemented during the last year. PUI will rely on the strong
and extensive experience of both organizations to ensure the quality of its intervention,
and the distribution of roles and responsibilities among the partners will be done based on
the specialization and the experience in the intervention area. The intervention areas for
the different activities were determined according to the priority/focus matrix prepared by
FSS in consultation with the ministry of agriculture (MoA). Moreover, similar consultations
were held with the ministry of social development (MoSD) regarding the target group and
intervention areas for MPCA.

1 - Needs

The ongoing deterioration of humanitarian situation has markedly affected the households’ ability to generate a decent income to meet their basic needs, with the weak purchasing power and limited cash ow. People whose livelihoods are dependent on the ARA at land and sea are exposed to another layer of vulnerabilities due to the enforcement of these areas. In April and late August 2020, PUI’s phone survey on the impact of COVID on farmers, fishers, sheep breeders and daily workers showed the following: 78% face challenges to reach the local/public markets, 90% reported an increase of the family expenditure on food, non-food items and hygiene materials, 88% reported that monthly income has been heavily affected with a third totally lost income sources, access to land and irrigation of crops were negatively affected by the increased prices of agricultural inputs. PUI phone assessment with 50 women sheep breeders showed that low demand and the fluctuation of the local product prices left a negative impact on the generated income in the light of the deteriorated economic crisis and the spread of COVID-19. Rehabilitation of livestock barns will enhance the sustainability of the business and contribute to mitigating the probability of infection by diseases and the mortality rates through ensuring a proper ventilation to the livestock. In October 2020, PUI and its partner (UAWC) held a meeting with MoA regarding the urgent needs in the fisheries sector: 200 boats are in need of urgent rehabilitation, 150 boat engines need to be repaired, fishing nets to be distributed and there is a need to distribute marine navigation devices. Also, the ministry confirmed that the suspension of fishing activities, following the detection of first C19 cases and during latest escalation, led to losses in the fishery sector estimated at 594,020 USD. According to PARC’s assessment conducted in 2017 and validated in 2020 to identify the green houses (GH) and water ponds in all Gaza strip, the total number of GH is 8,779 and the water ponds is 2,805. Accordingly, PARC established ponds for 300 farmers but there is still a significant gap as the water and electricity issues are still there. Regarding the green houses, the findings of the survey as well as the information from MoA confirm the need for rehabilitating greenhouses owned by small-scale farmers in light of the bad economic situation and the recurrent shocks they are exposed to. In addition, an assessment conducted in January 2020 with 349 vulnerable fishers from different GS governorates revealed negative effect of the economic crisis on their livelihoods. Besides, the high prices of the fishing equipment/tools, low purchasing power, decreased demand on fish, as well as the Israeli restrictions add another layer of pressure on the fishers. 22% were exposed to property damage, 4% confiscation and 1% injury incidents. The average amount of losses is estimated at 9,334 ILS (2,714 USD). The results confirmed the need to provide livelihood support to fishers so that they can cope with all the above-reported challenges. PUI assessment, conducted with ARA farmers in September 2020 over the phone, revealed that restrictions, recurrent IHL/IHRL violations, COVID-19, and blockade affected their ability to generate income. Moreover, the salary cut by PA as well as the delay of MoSD subsidies, led to a drastic decrease in purchasing power. Also, generated income was affected by the sporadic irrigation resulted from recurrent power cuts. Farmers struggle to secure the basic needs for themselves and their families, accumulating high amounts of debts. Due to COVID-19 pandemic, farmers stated that the man power availability has been affected by the concerns of risk of infection as well as the inability to cover wages due to the lack of income. Farmers called for urgent livelihood support to enable them to maintain the food production and protect the at-risk livelihoods.

2 - Activities

PUI will partner with PARC and UAWC in this project. They will be effectively involved in the design of the intervention, have a direct role in implementing the different planned activities, and will participate in the different stages of the project including monitoring and reporting process. Activity 1: Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs for farmers, fishers and breeders. a. Provision of conditional cash assistance (CCT), vouchers, and/or inputs to 240 (20 F, 220 M) vulnerable households engaged in farming or shery and affected by protection threats and/or other types of shock both economic and environmental in the ARA. Farmers/ fishers will receive cash and/or voucher assistance amounting to 800 USD (farmers) and 900 USD (fishers). It represents the average cost of agricultural inputs to cultivate two dunums with vegetable crops or rehabilitate small-scale greenhouses (1 dunum maximum) or fishing inputs/equipment needed to relaunch the fisher’s livelihoods (essential boats rehabilitation, boat engines maintenance, etc.). b. Rehabilitation of poultry and livestock farms for 80 breeders (25 F, 55 M) Breeders will benefit from the rehabilitation works implemented and supervised by the partners (PARC for poultry, UAWC for livestock). The assistance amount (1,000 USD/BNF) will cover the essential rehabilitation works needed. c. Rehabilitation works and provision of solar systems for two large-scale agricultural wells and 10 small-scale water ponds which will serve 170 (20 F, 150 M) farmers in average (30 kw/well, 3kw/pond). The cost of rehabilitation works and providing solar energy for water wells (40,000 USD/well ) and ponds (4,000 USD) was estimated based on the previous experience of partners in this field where they provided PV systems for a number of water wells and ponds during the last few years knowing that the proposed activity will be coordinated with MoA and FAO. Activity 2: Cash-based basic needs support to households with limited access to income and/or food due to recurrent and emerging crises. a. Provision of multi-purpose cash assistance or e-vouchers to 200 HH (100 FHH, 100 MHH) acutely vulnerable HH affected by economic shocks and/or C19 pandemic to cover their immediate basic needs. The households will receive tailored multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to cover their immediate basic needs for three months. PUI will rely on the monthly transfer value (1,196 ILS/345 USD) determined by MEB TF. The transfer value is equivalent to 60% of the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) value for a HH of six (6) members. PUI. b. Creation of temporary job opportunities (CFW) for 150 agricultural laborers (50 F, 100 M) and 20 male fishers. The agricultural labors will benefit from the creation of temporary job opportunities through CFW (40 working days) to support small-scale farmers. The daily wage will be (60 ILS) according to the standard wages agreed in CFW WG. Skilled and unskilled daily workers in fisheries sector will benefit from a temporary employment opportunity for 25 days, where the unskilled worker will receive (60 ILS/18 USD) per day and the skilled worker will receive (75 ILS/23 USD) per day. They will be involved in boats rehabilitation. All targeted households will be selected according to the shocks incurred, protection-related, and socio-economic vulnerability criteria. Selection criteria will include: households who are affected by C19’s precautionary measures, ongoing deterioration of the economic situation, and/or unforeseen protection incidents. It is important to note that due to prevailing cultural norms and limitations, women are unequally represented within the fishery, agricultural and herding professions. Women that are active in these livelihood areas will be provided with prioritized support. PUI will work to dismantle the barriers women have to face when accessing the workforce by promoting equal decision making within the household and providing timely awareness-raising within the community in terms of AGD. PUI will mainstream the IASC's PSEA principles throughout the whole action through ensuring the achievement of the following priority outcomes identified by the IASC for PSEA:1. Safe and accessible reporting mechanisms that can receive and respond to incidents of SEA;2. Quality assistance for survivors of SEA;3. Ability to offer survivors prompt, confidential and respectful investigations. The beneficiaries, project staff, members of local partner CBOs, contractors, subcontractors, and all persons involved in the implementation of the activities will be sensitized on the PSEA issues through awareness-raising sessions and distributing leaflets.Senior staff at the level of the mission (i.e. HoM, Field coordinator, HR/Admin coordinator) will be in charge of investigating such SEA incidents in full compliance with safety, privacy, and confidentiality requirements and according to PUI's PSEA policy. Beneficiaries will receive the details of the feedback and complaint mechanism with specific number and email dedicated to SEA incidents through which they can report any incident in safe and secret manner. Coordination will be made with PSEA-specialized NGOs and INGOs at the early stages of the project to ensure rapid response and effective remedy to any potential incidents

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

PUI has a long established presence in the ARA, a deep knowledge of its context, and close collaboration with its diverse population, local CBOs, community leaders and municipalities. PUI’s accountability to the ARA population has been well-recognized for years, including during the war of 2014. The Action’s primary concern is to respond to the affected population’s needs, expressed and identiﬁed during the needs assessment phase, through FGDs and phone surveys. Hence, key community members and potential targeted beneﬁciaries are involved in the design phase of the action and also in identifying vulnerabilities and related needs. All community consultations and assessments equally include people with diverse needs and capacities like PwD, older persons, women and girls. To ensure transparency, PUI will meet with all selected beneﬁciaries and community representative, as soon as the action starts, to inform them about the ﬁnal accepted project activities and explain all different steps. Beneﬁciaries from diverse groups will be asked to actively participate and contribute to the success of the Action. These information meeting will also allow targeted beneﬁciaries to inform PUI and its partners about their situation, needs, aspiration and involve them through a participatory approach. During those meetings, PUI and partners’ staff members will ensure to handout information cards on the feedback and complaint mechanism (FCM) as well as PSEA, and explain the reporting mechanism. Other modalities of communication with communities will also be adopted to ensure access to PwD and people who cannot read or write. It will be highlighted that feedback, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by PUI’s MEAL staff who are independent from the project team. Concerns of all nature, including SEA, be dealt with in respect for the principles of conﬁdentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is crucial in humanitarian aid delivery. PUI is dedicated in ensuring do no harm and dignity to all people accessing services regardless of their personal characteristics. PUI is currently developing a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy and on the planned community consultations, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed Action onset. Some of the key intervention areas will be:• A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis for the project will be drafted Action onset, with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEA is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects for protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEA, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEA reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEA training will be provided to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEA will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries.• Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. At the same time, staff members will be trained on “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “ Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures” . • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some on the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, the Action aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with speciﬁc needs in all aspects of the project cycle. The Action’s participatory assessment is speciﬁcally designed to involve vulnerable groups traditionally excluded from decision making. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI and its partners will analyse possible barriers and enablers in cooperation with the community, CBOs, PDOs and beneﬁciaries with a speciﬁc focus on age, gender and diversity. Furthermore, the Action will pay particular attention to identifying beneﬁciaries most at need by attributing special attention to inclusiveness of vulnerable groups and people with speciﬁc needs. The planned action will target women as well as men with and without disabilities as income providers, to ensure equal access to productive resources, facilitate access to market while promoting joint decision-making in income use. Obstacles to as an example female headed households’ economic participation will be tackled, through engaging actively with the involved beneﬁciaries on challenges and possible solutions. PUI and partners are working to ensure that programmes and services are available and accessible to persons with and without disabilities. Therefore, they work on a non-discriminatory approach, where a person with a disability is able to exercise their protected rights on an equal basis with other members of the community. PUI as humanitarian organization is also committed to protect Rights of Persons with Disabilities and inclusion principles and practices of the most vulnerable. PUI ensures that persons with disabilities and their families are involved and consulted at all stages of the project cycle. For instance, during the needs assessment phase, PUI includes persons who identiﬁes with one or several of the Washington Group Short Set of Questions on disabilities to gather information on barriers, needs and priorities. As a result, PUI and partners will identify and work to remove any barriers that may prevent persons with disabilities from effectively and safely using services and participating in projects as well as build on identiﬁed enablers. During the planning phase, PUI ensured that the disability-related ﬁndings and recommendations from the assessment stage have been carried through into the project design. PUI then, make adaptations when necessary to ensure accessibility and inclusiveness of project services. Further, during the implementation phase, service delivery mechanisms can be adapted to improve access for persons with disabilities. C19 specific issues in terms of access will also be taken into consideration. Specifically, hygiene, prevention, and control measures and protocols will be in place in all planned project activities. The protection and gender analysis will be updated regularly to reflect new emerging needs associated with any enforced changes in the project delivery modalities. These enforced changes in modality with also be part of regular consultations with the communities to identify and assess how they might contribute to the access in humanitarian service provision of different groups of people. Accessible information in different modalities for different groups of people regarding C19 will be provided. Lastly, the continuation of the FRM, regardless of C19 related changes in programming will be ensured.
