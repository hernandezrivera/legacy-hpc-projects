Project Summary

Floods displaced hundreds of people and destroyed at least 60,000 hectares of farmland in Somalia's Middle Shabelle, Lower shabelle and Hiraan region around Jowhar, the regional capital, when the Shabelle river burst its banks last week, Officials from office of the Prime Minister said on Thursday, 3rd June 2021. Women, adolescent girls and children continue to hardest hit by flooding, droughts and displacements in the first and second quarter of 2021. In total 66,000 individuals have been totally displaced from their dwellings out of which 45% are women and girls, while 28% are children. For Beletweyn in particular - Out of the 3,593 individuals that were displaced by the Shabelle river floods, 2795 individuals of the displaced (77%) are female, children and girls.
National (GBVIMS) data in the first quarter of 2021, indicated that over 60% of reported GBV incidents were Physical Assault, 12% Rape, 9% Sexual Assault, 8% experienced Denial of Resources, 6% Psychological /Emotional Abuse, and the remaining 5% is Forced/ Early Marriage. The data showed an increase in the number of rape incidences reported which accounts to 12% compared to the Q4 of 2020 which accounted for 9%. This indicates that women and girls and displaced persons are particularly at risk of GBV. 99% of all GBV reported cases were females, with physical violence being the highest at 60%, followed by rape at 12% and sexual assault at 9%. In terms of case context, intimate partner violence accounted for 64%, followed by child abuse at 12% and harmful traditional practice, including FGM at 7%. 82% of the survivors reporting GBV are IDPs, 17% are from the host communities and the remaining 1 % is returnees, refugees and asylum seekers. This indicates that women and girls and displaced persons are particularly at risk of GBV.
SOYDA is aiming to provide integrated life-saving protection assistance to the floods affected IDPs and host communities in Benadir, Middle and Lower shabelle and Hiran region: The project foresee 14,650 affected people whom 3,500 are men, 7,000 women, 1,800 boys and 2,350 girls in the affected districts. These will constitute 8,990 direct beneficiaries based on 750 men, 3,100 women, 2,280 boys and 2,860 girls. Considering the beneficiary are vulnerable, they will be under care of / or caring for at least one other affected person. On GBV, SOYDA propose to provide immediate lifesaving services and risk mitigation to GBV survivors and vulnerable 2240 women, 230 men, 210 boys and 660 girls through the following interventions:- Provision of Clinical Care for Sexual Assault Survivors (CCAS), Case management (CM), Psycho social support (PSS) and linkages between referral actors and service providers in the area.; SOYDA will provide PSS and counseling to women and girls in the safe spaces and encourage safe networking, on information regarding GBV services through updating and disseminating referral pathway. SOYDA will also distribute menstrual and dignity kits to 1,340 women/girls including GBV survivors, women HHs, elderly, persons with disabilities and minority groups.
Communities, authorities and humanitarian actors have limited knowledge awareness of GBV risks and consequences and have limited practical involvement for GBV prevention and response. Non-GBV humanitarian actors across all sectors, and the local government authorities have identi ed this gap in knowledge and practice. Although SOYDA have been working with GBV AoR to establish referral system and increase awareness of services in target areas, there is still a need to strengthen engagement of communities, service providers and authorities
SOYDA will implement directly in target areas as a local actor with standing presence and will link with the GBV AoR to common strategy for addressing cases of women, girls children and ensuring quality, consistency, coordination in services and adherence to the internationally agreed upon standards on case management.

1. - Projects activities

1. Provision of GBV services for 850 GBV survivors including case management, referral to safe shelter, basic emotional support and health referral to the main health facilities in Daynile, Dharkenley, Kahda districts of Mogadishu, and Afgoye and Marka district of lower shabelle Region, Jowhar and Baladweyne of Hirshabelle state 2. Provision of life saving material assistance 4500 female headed households (dignity kits, emergency/emergency safe kits and transport cost) to GBV survivors based on individual assessments by GBV case workers. The material assistance will also be allocated to provide emergency safe shelter by already identified foster families. Foster families will be on standby in case an emergency safe shelter is needed to secure the protection of women, girls and GBV survivors. It is envisaged that the foster families will host the survivor for a period of up to 3 months. Tailored NFI/Shelter items will be provided to the survivors’ host to assure hospitality in a dignified manner. Provision of life saving material for GBV survivors (based on the IASC guidelines on MH and Psychosocial support in emergency settings Geneva 2007 – layer of the intervention pyramid called basic services and security) 3. Capacity building on Clinical Management of Rape Survivors (CMR) for 40 health staff from 12 MCHs and outreach centers that is managed by SOYDA. Among the participants GBV focal points will be identified and supported to provide case management and referral at the well- functioning MCHs of in Daynile, Dharkenley, Kahda districts of Mogadishu, and Afgoye and Marka district of lower shabelle Region (GBV cluster service mapping will be baseline for the selection of MCHs addition, Under the proposed intervention and in close collaboration and coordination with UNFPA and MoH SOYDA will ensure the availability of complete post rape treatment kits (PRTKs) within the health facilities to increase the provision of compassionate, competent and con\0dently clinical cares services to GBV survivors 4. SOYDA will mobilize 80 PSS counselors to deliver one on one and group psychosocial support and counselling for traumatized women and girls in the IDP camps. The counselors together with community mobilizers will sensitize the community and provide basic psychological support to the GBV survivors, to early or forced marriage and vulnerable girls. 5. SOYDA will conducted awareness sessions for the floods affected communities to raise awareness about services available, Importance of timely of cases and COVID 19 effects and prevention. . Awareness will include: A. Information on how to prevent COVID-19, such as hand washing and physical distancing. 6. A. Information on how to recognize signs and symptoms of the disease and the importance of reporting without fearing any repercussions; C. Information about modes of transmission and risks of infection, to effectively combat myths that Stigmatize children/GBV survivors or children of survivors; D. Dissemination of COVID-19 specific health referral pathways and hotline numbers (Will be done in line with the approved guides.); E. Information and positive messaging to clients, parents and caregivers in distress as a result of COVID-19 due to quarantine, physical distancing and illness of dears ones, including messaging on how to communicate to children regarding COVID-19, to mitigate stress for the children. 7. Conduct 3 days training of 16 female Community Safety Mobilizers on GBV monitoring reporting and referral. The trained Community safety Mobilizers will lead in monitoring and reporting and will link with the existing one stop centers and safe houses as well as other available services for defective reporting

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

For the risk of violence's during distribution, SOYDA will mitigate by ensuring that (1) information on service and vulnerability criteria are well explained to avoid con\0ict from those not eligible, (2) additionally those not meeting criteria if vulnerable or are in need can also be referred (connected to) relevant service providers. (3) SOYDA will also engage the community members in the supporting protection of the vulnerable as well as schedule distributions away from military and (4) where possible, respond to the individual needs to minimize the long movement or long distribution lines / ensure accessible also to people with mobility impairments and/or mobile services to reach those, who cannot access the facilities. SOYDA will mitigate the risk of exclusion of the marginalized by (1) actively reaching out to individuals with disability or (and even partners already working with them) to incorporate their views and provides relevant services (2) ensure the plan include disaggregate outcomes for boys, girls and those with disabilities hence proper representation of all interested group within the society including elderly and vulnerable women. (3) Building capacity if staff to understand and avoid multiple forms of discrimination on the basis of race, colour, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, nationality, ethnic, indigenous or social origin, property, birth, age or other status. (4) Promote general public awareness on information to persons with disabilities about available support and assistive services, including as well as other forms of assistance, support services and facilities. For the risk that bene\0ciaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, SOYDA will (1) Create awareness on the existences and how to use the mechanism (inform and educate the public) (2) Ensure the public access feedback in timely manner or are informed on actions taken based on their complains / reports (3) Ensure the tools for complains are child friendly and also easily accessible even with people with disabilities. (4) When complaints and require investigations SOYDA will ensure it does not put the reporting individual at any risk
