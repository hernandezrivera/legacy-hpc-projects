Needs

In South Sudan, malnutrition rates remain high and a public health problem . According to the recently conducted FSNMS, the results of the IPC report indicate global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates above world health organization (WHO) 15 percent emergency threshold (IPC, Sept 2018). In kapoeta south of eastern equatorial state, a GAM rate of 15.5 percent (11.7-20.3 95% CI) (IPC, Sept 2018) was discovered. The survey further discovered a SAM rate of 4.3 percent (2.1-8.8 95% CI). In Kapoeta south county, the key drivers of critical levels of acute malnutrition are severe food insecurity, poverty, and sub optimal child feeding and care practices which have a direct impact on nutrition status of children under five and women. The most affected by malnutrition are girls and boys less than 5 years and pregnant and lactating women. Further, the high GAM rate has been attributed to food shortages, inadequate provision of treatment and preventive nutrition care services which continue to expose children < 5years and PLWs to further risk of malnutrition in Kapoeta South County. MaCDA is requesting for SSHF fund to address the humanitarian nutrition situation in kapoeta south county and ensure that all malnourished boys and girls have equal access to adequate treatment and preventive nutrition services. MaCDA has active PCA and FLA with UNICEF and WFP respectively for MIYCN, OTP and TSFP activities. This will enable timely request for supplies to respond to nutrition needs in Kapoeta South. MaCDA has proven technical expertise and capacity to provide nutrition interventions to the affected populations with qualified and adequately trained local personnel to provide quality nutrition service delivery to the kapoeta community. In addition, MaCDA employs local community staffs, builds capacity of CHD nutrition staffs through training on CMAM to ensure sustainability of the program. MaCDA works with local authorities and the community to create a sense of ownership of the project,enlighten them of their entitlements and their roles as far as the project is concerned. In addition, MaCDA has qualified technical finance staffs with knowledge on internal controls and reporting on financial accountability. To ensure effective integration during project implementation, MaCDA will ensure that children discharged from OTP are linked to TSFP to maintain a continuum of nutrition care while children 6-59months and PLWs in TSFP will be linked to GFD program and other livelihood interventions so as to improve and increase food accessibility and availability. MaCDA will ensure that children enrolled in OTP/TSFP are screened and treated for malaria, train nutrition staff on available GBV services such as referral of GBV survivors, link with available WASH programs to ensure that all OTP/TSFP sites in kapoeta south have access to clean drinking water for appetite test, hand washing facilities, and latrines. In addition, MaCDA will create nutrition awareness in existing primary and secondary schools in kapoeta south to ensure that nutrition education focusing on prevention and treatment will be initiated among school going pupils and student. MaCDA's nutrition response is aimed at providing and improving the nutrition status of the most vulnerable populations (children <5years, and PLW by delivering high impact lifesaving management of acute malnutrition and increasing access and utilization of integrated nutrition services to the most vulnerable populations to prevent and treat malnutrition.

Activities

1) Conduct routine MUAC screening for 17,000 children < 5 years. 2) Conduct two mass community awareness and seven mobilization meetings with key stakeholders and local leaders. 3) Provide treatment for 800 SAM cases and 2,439 MAM cases among children 6-59months in accordance to the MOH/RSS CMAM guideline in all 9 facilities in Ikotos County. 4) Provide treatment for 1,700 MAM cases among PLWs targetted for in the project. 5) Conduct two trainings for 24 health workers (CHD/SMOH health service providers) on MIYCN. 6) Conduct training of 18 Nutrition Assistants and 27 CNVs on CMAM. 7) Conduct training of 30 IYCF promoters. 8) Form 30 mothers support groups. 9) Conduct training of surveyed nutrition staffs to identify and appropriately refer victims of gender based violence. 10) Provide and deliver routine preventive nutrition messages to caretakers/mothers of children 0-23 months. 12) Conduct regular nutrition education sessions in schools through established nutrition clubs in schools.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Since the project is community based, information will be provided to the beneficiaries during the entire project life. Mother and caretakers will be provided with routine MIYCN messages on proper maternal, infant and child nutrition, key hygiene messages and advice on importance of PNC for growth monitoring of children < years. Further, before treatment intervention is given to children, mothers will be given information on preparation of child feeds, administering the feeding rations and the effect of sharing the child ration. Further, beneficiaries will given information about how food rations will be provided, when and what time so as to ensure all beneficiaries are mobilized to access the service.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The key MIYCN messages in MIYCN activities will be translated in the local languages to ensure that mothers and caretakers understand them clearly.All information related to proper feeding and treatment of SAM and MAM will be translated and provided to the beneficiaries in their local language. Additionally, MaCDA will ensure that all nutrition staffs (Nutrition Assistants, CNVs and field supervisors are recruited from Kapoeta community who are fully versed with local language.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will ensure that all nutrition staffs and beneficiaries are fully equipped with their rights. Staffs will be trained on gender and protection issues including sexual exploitation if beneficiaries and abuses. Nutrition staffs will be trained on the appropriate referral channels and reporting such cases if they are found during the project life.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Yes because, Kapoeta South county has 5 Payams and in each payam a consultative meeting was held where 20 community members were engaged to understand the needs in the county. Further, each Boma had a representative who contributed and participated in the consultative meeting and this was representative of the whole Kapoeta community.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

During the project life, the project will ensure that both local community leaders in Kapoeta South County and women representative are empowered to provide complaints and success ideas to MaCDA technical monitoring teams. This will ensure a comprehensive community participation and ownership of the project. This will be done through holding of stakeholders workshops on quarterly basis in each payam to assess whether the project is achieving its objectives during the course of implementation. In the same workshops, the project focal person will be able to provide accountability in terms of key performance indicators on the program ( i.e, Cure rate, death rate, defaulter rate) so that the community members can devise means of ensuring that they collectively participate in the program implementation effectively.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Planned monitoring and evaluation of the OTP and TSFP sites will be conducted together with CHD staffs, local chiefs and women representatives to ensure that all planned activities are being done according the recommended protocol and that community are informed and aware of the program implementation to improve the sense of ownership of the program by the community members. Further, Women will be particularly encouraged to be part of the monitoring and evaluation activities because they are the majority beneficiaries of the project.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will have two categories of beneficiaries; Direct and indirect beneficiaries; Direct beneficiaries; Children < 5 years and PLWs under treatment for SAM and MAM, Indirect beneficiaries; Mothers and caretakers of children < 5 years.During the project life, the specific needs of the beneficiaries categories will be considered to ensure that all beneficiaries in each category are well catered for to acquire the nutrition service at the OTP/TSFP site. Special needs of children or PLW (pregnant and lactating women) with disability will be key to ensure that all children and PLW in need of nutrition service gets it equitably.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Before, implementation, MaCDA will ensures that all OTP/TSFP sites are mapped in terms of accessibility. In case of far and hard to reach location, MaCDA will ensure to establish new OTP/TSFP site to ensure that all children < 5 years and PLWs access the nutrition treatment and preventive service.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities includes screening, prevention and treatment for malnutrition. During project implementation, all children <5 years and PLWs will be screened equally, those found malnourished will treated with equal treatment rations. All staffs implementing the project will be trained on protection aspects of the project especially follow up on all cases to avoid neglect of beneficiaries, and also avoid to exploit beneficiaries or the caregivers. All mothers and caretakers will be provided with MIYCN messages without discrimination. All children < 5 years of age will be handled with care during screening for malnutrition, OTP/TSFP shelter will be established to ensure that mothers are protected from long cues and direct sunshine.

Yes
