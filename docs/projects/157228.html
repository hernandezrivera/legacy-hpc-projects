End Date

Project Summary

Increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) for the most vulnerable (7000 children, 4000 in Rokoro, 2000 in Golo, and 1000 in Nertiti) children (pre-school and primary school age children and adolescents) affected by new emergencies and ensure continuity of relevant quality education during sudden onset and aftermath of emergencies.
CRS will conduct the following activities:
1- Construction/rehabilitation 30 classrooms in nine schools (18 in Rokoro, 6 in Golo, and 6 in Nertiti)
3- Construction of 10 gender-sensitive latrines for IDP children - 3 in Rokoro, 3 in Golo, and 4 in Nertiti
4- Conduct community Mobilizations sessions on topics around girl’s education and education in emergency initiatives. 2 sessions of sensitization meetings will be conducted at each school in 9 IDP communities targeting 500 community representatives (250 male and 250 female). At least 50% from IDP communities. DRDO staff will lead the sensitization sessions with support of project staff and in coordination with MoE .
5- Provision of a 5-day training for 45 teachers from among the 9 schools (in 3 groups) for 3-day basic teacher training and the 2-day PSS training. MoE master trainers based in Central Darfur will conduct the trainings based on approved Curriculum that includes Basic and advanced teaching methodology and how to conduct PSS.
6- Description Establish 10 classes of Non-Formal Education (Accelerated Learning Program) 4 ALP class in Rokero, 3 in Nertiti and 3 in Golo benefiting 500 (200 in Rokero, 150 in Nertiti and 150 in Golo) school age children.
7- CRS will provide 10 ALP classes for out-of-school IDP children .
8- Training of 180 PTA members (20 from each of the 9 schools) .
9- 9 water tanks for storage, the project will provide one water tank to each school (nine tanks in total) to support sanitation infrastructure of latrines and handwashing stations.
10- Provision of 20 blackboards for constructed and rehabilitated classrooms in targeted schools (4 in Rokero and 3 in Nertiti and 2 in Golo).
11- Provision 400 seating sets to address the needs for seating for new IDP children .
12- Psycho-social Supplies - Football, netball, drawing paper, textbooks

Needs

Gaps in educational services are evident in Nertiti, Rokero and Golo camps, the rate of IDP and host community children out of school ranges from 35% to 45% (UNICEF), with most enrolled students crowded into classrooms and sitting on the ground. The classrooms are constructed from local materials which do not keep out the rain. Latrines are mixed-gender and made with transparent local materials. There is a shortage of learning materials and trained Ministry of Education teachers in the schools. The increase in IDPs coming into the IDP camp in 2017 has further stressed what the learning environment in the camp is able to offer. The needs in Nertiti, Rokero, and Golo particularly centered on the construction of semi-permanent classrooms, rehabilitation of damaged classrooms, need for adequate latrines and sanitation facilities, needs for trained teachers and education supplies. Psychosocial support and school kits will also be a strong need for the children of recently displaced families, thus the most significant needs for the students in this camp are construction and rehabilitation of classrooms, seating in the classrooms, Psycho social support, trained teachers and PTAs. Child protection: Child protection will be streamlined into all of our activities as an underlying/cross-cutting theme. We will make this a core part of teacher training and PTA training, as well as implementing psycho-social support such as child safe spaces (sports, drawing, other activities) to help keep children safe. Another lesson learned on child protection is to coordinate with WFP to provide school meals to keep vulnerable children in school, as food can be an incentive for parents to enroll their children and keep them safe/protected from harmful labor practices. Classrooms made from stone and local materials create dangerous environments for children because the buildings can collapse. This happened in the past and seriously injured 20 female students - thus the construction of new schools will lead to safer infrastructure. Students also need desks so that they can sit down and focus in a healthy, safe, friendly learning spaces. In most of Rokoro, there is currently no school feeding so CRS will coordinate with WFP to address this (and in our 2019 WFP FLA we also have school feeding activities). CRS also wants to be innovative on gender and provide female sanitation kits to female students to help keep them in school.
