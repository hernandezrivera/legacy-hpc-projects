Project Summary

This is a 1 year project aims at providing emergency shelter and not food items to 3091 households (18,550 individuals) affected by the recent floods in Tonj North, South and East and Gogrial East Counties of Warrap State (1500 Women, 1100 men, 500 boys and 1300 girls) through in-kind assistance (100%) while mainstreaming GBV, protection, WASH and health. Beneficiary targeting will be guided by the SNFI Cluster criteria of and in consultation with the affected communities to include; female headed households, persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, children heading households, households with children under 5 years, family household sizes (averagely 6 and above) and or vulnerable households with low purchasing power at risk of GBV. These will be identified after a thorough needs analysis has been conducted to identify priority SNFIs to guide The Organization of Children Harmony (TOCH) to respond with appropriate assistance to address the immediate needs. More so, a risk analysis will be conducted before hand for safety assurance for staff and beneficiaries. TOCH plans to employ an in-kind modality since currently, most markets have been interrupted/destroyed due to heavy flooding and restocking may take some time and this means TOCH will rely on the core pipeline for the SNFIs.
The choice of assistance will be informed by the needs analysis however the recent call by the State Minister for Information (Tonj) also guides this plan. As such, shelter materials like plastic sheets, rubber roles and non-food items including kitchen sets, mosquito nets, sleeping mat, solar lamps (due to the protection concerns being raised recently about the increasing snake bites) and blanket will form the TOCH SNFI emergency kit. The community will receive training from well-trained TOCH staff on how to construct temporary yet dignified structures while more vulnerable categories of beneficiaries like the elderly, female headed households, chronically ill, persons with disabilities will be supported by locally hired semi-skilled labourers at a small fee (guided by the SNFI labour rates) to support at least an estimated 30% of the targeted beneficiaries that fall under the above criteria.
Community engagement throughout the project cycle will be paramount to encourage their participation while promoting ownership and sustainability. Community will be consulted on their needs including defining the targeting criteria and a community committee formed at boma level comprising of 4 women and 3 women will be identified and endorsed by community members who will play key roles in registering vulnerable households, community mobilization, information sharing, supporting the distribution of items, participating in monitoring of project activities and also serving as a complaint and feedback channel. Moreover, beneficiary feedback channels established at the start of the project in consultation with the community to identify and recommend for appropriate mechanism will be important to ensure that the community provide feedback including reporting on fraud, sexual abuse and conduct of staff and or volunteers during the implementation period.
For quality assurance TOCH’s M&E department will be responsible to ensure that project activities are continuously monitored and inform project staff if activities are on track and advise where necessary to ensure project objectives are met. The monitoring team composition will include field staff, community representatives, GBV and protection partners and the findings will always be shared at different levels (beneficiaries, staff, State Focal Point, TOCH Management and SNFI cluster) for learning and future improvements. 1. Needs

The Shelter and Non-Food Item (SNFI) Cluster estimates that 2.1 million people in South Sudan, particularly internally displaced persons (IDPs), will require urgent shelter and NFI support in 2024 due to persistent challenges like communal violence, recurrent flooding, and worsening food insecurity. The states mostly affected included Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Unity, but with significant spill over into Warrap State due to ongoing and compounded inter-communal conflict, flooding, and poor infrastructure.In Warrap State, Twic, Tonj North, and Tonj East counties have been disproportionately affected by both intercommoned violence and seasonal flooding, exacerbating vulnerabilities in access to safe shelter and essential non-food items. Several report including IRNA and IPC Report highlights these areas as having alarming levels of displacement, with many households residing in temporary shelters or makeshift settlements that expose them to health risks, insecurity, and harsh climatic conditions. The market supply chain disruptions because of the conflict in Sudan, the seasonal deterioration of road networks during the rainy season and associated impacts on market functionality, floods, cattle-related conflicts and intercommoned conflicts and violenceThe South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024 further underscores the rising vulnerability of these counties, noting the impact of the Sudan crisis and the potential disruption from upcoming national elections, which may intensify displacement and humanitarian needs. Flooding in these counties has destroyed homes and livelihoods, leading to additional displacement, while access constraints and delays in the humanitarian supply chain continue to hinder rapid responses. In Tonj North, over 22,000 people have been reported to be affected by floods while 6,250 families are reported in Twic County and in need of emergency shelter and non-food assistance according to RRC Warrap state report. More so, 70,000 people have been classified in Emergency (15,000 in Tonj East; 39,000 in Tonj North and 16,000 in Tonj South and 19,000 in Gogrial East Counties) due to floods and are at risk of GBV and WASH and health. It is also reported that the raging floods have resulted into substantial destruction of houses, road networks and destroyed the livelihoods of 10,892 households according to the results of the Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) conducted by South Sudan Red Cross (SSRC) in collaboration with the State department for Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in Warrap state.) In places where water level has receded, humanitarian organizations are responding to assessed needs, in support of the Government. Due to poor early-warning systems, communities have been forced to flee to temporary locations on higher land as the waters filled their homes, leaving behind livestock, businesses, and other household assets that have been destroyed or washed away by floods. The flood waters have remained to date as the rain season continues to enter the fourth month. This means Emergency provision of shelter and non-food items (SNFIs) is urgently needed when the water continues to retrocede at the onset of the dry season (early January) and these displaced households begin to return to their villages so as they are able to put up temporary shelter and live a dignified life. Moreover, construction materials that include grass for thatching the huts have been destroyed while the risk of malaria is very high due to the high mosquito infestation during the rainy season hence a justification why plastic sheets and mosquito nets have been prioritized.

2. Project activities

TOCH proposes an in-kind approach to address the emergency ES/NFI needs of 12,130 most vulnerable individuals in the Tonj North,Tonj East and Twic Counties, Warrap State. The following activities have been planned; (1) Needs and protection analysis; this will be done at the start of the project to determine and confirm the actual needs of the affected communities. More so, a protection risk analysis shall be conducted before hand using the safety audit guideline by IOM to ensure safety of staff and beneficiaries to be served. (2) Community consultations; TOCH will consult with the communities to agree on the selection criteria, explain the project activities including their roles and expectations in the project. Housing, Land and Property analysis shall be done in consultation with local authorities to avoid any probable land conflicts. This consultation is one way of ensuring community participation, ownership and sustainability. This shall also involve community consultations on establishment of appropriate and relevant complaint feedback mechanisms (CFM) (3) Community Committees; TOCH will request the community to identify and form village committees that shall be responsible for information sharing, linking the community with the project staff, beneficiary registration, monitoring and distribution. Gender and diversity inclusion shall be emphasized to ensure different groups including women/youth/PWD are included. They will then be trained on the different roles to undertake including the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and GBV referrals for any GBV risk mitigation. (4) Beneficiary registration and verification; The above identified Village Committees will support TOCH staff to identify , register and verify beneficiaries (5) M&E Data base; After the registration and verification exercise, the lists shall be submitted to TOCH field staff who will do spot checks to ensure the targeting criteria has been followed and vulnerable households/individuals registered for assistance. The lists shall then be submitted to TOCH M&E department where a database will be created and each beneficiary assigned a code. More so, another database shall be created to receive and record the feedback collected throughout the project period and shall form part of the reports and recommendations. (6) Core Pipe line Request; once the needs analysis has been shared with the SNFI cluster, TOCH will submit a request for pipeline to approve and release the SNFIs as indicated in the needs analysis report. (7) Procurement of shelter framing ; (wooden poles, bamboo bundles) and roofing material (grass) will be outsourced locally (8) Secondary transportation of pipeline materials from warehouse to distribution sites using a locally hire transport service provider (9) Distribution of pipeline items and shelter material to the registered and verified beneficiaries (10) Monitoring and Evaluation: Rapid monitoring will be done during the distribution process and continuous monitoring to assess if the project is on track, identify any gaps for improved programming. A post distribution monitoring (Evaluation) will be conducted between 4 to 6 weeks as per cluster recommended timeline to assess the impact of intervention. (11) Reporting; this will be done at different levels. From the field by the field staff to the State Focal Point, Project Manager and M&E Officer, data then compiled, analyzed and reported by the Programme Manager to the Cluster to the cluster and other fora.
