Project Summary

The project aims to improve the quality of the lives of children and adolescents affected by the current crisis in Syria, specifically in Idleb governorate (Idleb 30%, Ariha 25%, Jisr-Ash-Shugur 25%, Binnish districts 20%). The project is built to be linked to HRP objectives by improving access to education services, the quality of the services provided and the systems that support these services. With a commitment to sustainability the response aims to complement existing systems rather than creating parallel ones. The project will contribute to providing the needed education requirement through the following activities:
•Rehabilitating non-functioning learning spaces;
•Supplying students and staff with student and teaching kits - an essential component in the effective teaching and learning process;
•Providing learning spaces with safety and security materials/equipment in order to protect students from dangers they may face in attending school therefore improving access to education;
•Enhancing professional development to teaching staff (active learning, self-learning, life skills).
•Providing incentives to education staff as well as they are an imperative component in the education process.
•Enhancing the knowledge and life skills of communities hence facilitating behavior change in the importance of education and increasing the school enrollment rate.
The response prioritizes the most disadvantaged population groups, in particular those in areas with high severity need and children that are disproportionately underserved. In particular, the focuses will be on out of school children (OOSC) in Idleb, Ariha, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Binnish districts), to ensure that students continue to learn and improve the quality of the provided education.
This project will allow Nasaem to provide a complete program supporting education, as well as improving services provided and ensuring the longevity of the facilities. Child protection, child participation and gender issues will be mainstreamed in all project activities to increase the reach and enhance the impact of the project.
The recent global pandemic of coronavirus 2019 (Covid-19) has put additional stress on humanitarian response in general; the project will ensure the implementation of Covid-19 preventive measures (distribute soap, sanitizer and masks, as well as maintain a safe distance during project activities).
Nasaem will allocate a qualified MEAL team to monitor all the activities that will be undertaken and to measure the impact of the activities implemented, as well as NKO MEAL team will make its best to provide all feedback mechanism tools in all locations that will be included in the project.

4 - Needs

Based on the UNICEF Whole of Syria report, the number of people in need of aid in Syria increased by 21 % from 11.1 million in 2020 to 13.4 million in 2021, with 6.08 million children affected. However, in 2020, an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, is almost certain to increase. As consequence of COVID 19 formal education will continue to have a limited absorption and retention capacity and be carried out in largely inadequate and at times unsafe, learning environments. In Idleb governorate, the devastating humanitarian situation and the absence of adequate support have impacted the education sector and loss of components of the educational process. This lack of support for the education sector has negative effects in the future and causes generations of children to lose their right to education, which may make them vulnerable to ignorance and delinquency or perhaps to be involved in the armed conflict in Syria for males. According to field surveys conducted by NKO team, it is mentioned one of the main challenges to continue the education process is that a lot of education facilities are working on volunteering basis.

5 - Implementation of activities

NKO intends to conduct the activities in separate locations in2022 and 2023. Output 1: Children and adolescents have access to protective learning spaces/materials, and recreational kits to facilitate an effective learning environment. Activities: 1.1 Rehabilitate schools with gender-sensitive equipment/furniture. 1.2 Provision of recreational/learning/teaching kits for affected students. 1.3 NKO will disinfect/sanitize 12 schools. 1.4. Facilitate school transportation to and from insecure and distant areas. Output2: Teachers, school admin staff (women and men) trained and strengthened to effectively teach school age children (boys and girls). Activities: 2.1 Provide training on teaching methodology, PSS, school management, EiE, INEE standards, and hygiene promotion. 2.2 Support teachers and school staff (women and men) with basic monthly incentives. Output3: Locals and IDPs, teachers, parents and children have awareness of the importance of education and received life skill messages. Activities: 3.1 Conduct emergency life-skills session for affected boys and girls, including life-saving hygiene and health messages (including drink clean water, use latrines and wash hands with soap in accordance with INEE standards. Hygiene messages will be tailored to the different needs of boys and girls. 3.2 Deliver emergency life skills and importance of education messages to IDPs and host community members. A trained NKO MEAL will monitor all activities to ensure everything is in place.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: A breach of ethics. - Mitigation measure: Nasaem will train the staff on the code of conduct, child safeguarding, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies and ask them to sign them. - Risk: Staff may find sensitive protection cases. Mitigation measure: Nasaem’s field staff will receive training on the referral of cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to the available service providers in the areas based on the updated service map. The project will priorities the most vulnerable persons (males and females including IDPs), considering the children situations and difficulties within these HHs (priorities to HHs including children with disabilities or chronic diseases), to receive services during this project. The project is designed to enable equal and impartial access through community consultation and participation across age, gender and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups and not to exclude any groups and provide the identified services with dignity and impartiality. Awareness sessions will be conducted about strengthening self-protection mechanisms, rights' assisting, GBV services, increasing knowledge about woman and her main roles and rights. In addition, males will attend sessions about GBV risks to increase knowledge and awareness and to reduce following bad or negative coping strategies. The feedback (complaint) mechanisms will be in place and all beneficiaries must be aware of them.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All staff and volunteers working in this project will be trained on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) aspects to understand, sign and adhere to a code of conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. Ensure measures in case of violation and fight impunity. NKO will ensure that mechanisms are in place for Protection from PSEA and that they are, regularly updated and are functioning effectively. NKO will continue to strengthen coordination with the PSEA network in addressing sexual exploitation and abuse. Respond to all complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can or need to be put in place following existing principles and procedures recommended by the PSEA network. In partnership with PSEA network, identify dedicated focal points and raise awareness about PSEA. Provide clear information that beneficiaries do not have to provide services or favors in exchange for receiving services or accessing facilities. Ensure that vulnerable groups and individuals are able to access services equally and without any discrimination. It will be confirmed that the service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against girls, boys, women or men. Apply the principle of “different needs equal opportunities.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Movement restriction to basic services due to the high increase in the number of COVID-19 cases and the high level of risk. Mitigation Measure: Providing remote response services. continues communication with health directorate to get regular update on the cases. Risk : Safety and health of staff during COVID-19 pandemic. Mitigation Measure: -Prioritize the safety and well-being of staff, specifically those who provide services to survivors of gender-based violence. - Ensure the continued regular supervision of employees and the allocation of personnel to shifts in order to take into account changes in responsibilities. Self-care strategies as part of programs and human resource policy in the organization. Risk : Security concerns Nasaem Khair team will make a continuous assessment on the security situation in the project location.
