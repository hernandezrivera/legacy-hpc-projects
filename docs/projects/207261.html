Project Summary

"In the Rohingya context, SCI implemented nutrition services from 2017 to 2021. Then handed over the direct implementation to local partners and continued the technical assistance and advocacy to improve and continue the quality services to support the sectoral localization strategy.
As the global pioneer of IYCFE and CMAMI, SCI continues counselling services under the health post with no service duplication. The counselling service is a behavior change communication approach, SCI follows the standard protocol of service integration with other sectors to ensure synergistically outcome-based service delivery for the vulnerable populations and avoid service duplication.
SCI will implement integrated preventive nutrition services through 2 health posts and 2 Primary Health Care Center (PHCC) in 4 camps by consulted the IYCF-E global framework integrated approach. The nutrition project will target pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and under-five children who attend the health facilities for clinical needs. The services providing are the screening and management of at-risk mother-child pair in CMAMI, anthropometric and nutritional assessment of 6-59 months children, pregnant women, and lactating mother along with need-based counselling. By participating in the Nutrition Sector JRP, SCI will continue work alongside other nutrition actors to ensure a more comprehensive and coordinated response to the nutritional needs of the affected population by contributing in capacity strengthening, and nutritional counselling services. SCI has a robust Community Health Worker (CHW) team to communicate and maintain the referral pathway to incorporate direct nutrition support with the nearest INF of the health facility that sometimes resolve the programme participants difficulties in accessing services and information.
To acknowledge and respect the nutrition sector strategy objectives (no. 4), SCI is providing services in an integrated way at the same service point with nutrition education during pregnancy and post-natal care, promotion of breastfeeding and appropriate complementary feeding, immunization, treatment of diarrhea with zinc, mental and psychosocial support which can lead the programme to more integrated and sustainable solutions. SCI will contribute its expertise to a broader strategy that considers various aspects of nutrition and health. In addition, SCI has planned to arrange a capacity-building initiative for integrated nutrition facility staff near the SCI health facility to ensure better utilization of quality health, nutrition mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services for achieving synergistically outcome-based service and social well-being. Malnutrition has irreparable negative consequences on infant and child development and could be facilitated by a delay in the physical and mental development that lasts until adolescence and carry difficulties as an adult. For preventing the negative consequences of malnutrition, integrated mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) interventions would be an evidential treatment tool to help to identify the sign of distress of infant, child and their caregivers in early stage through providing psychosocial support at facility and community. SCI will show the eagerness to provide technical assistance to implementing partners in managing NBF children as per sectoral need. As a part of integration SCI will further focus on the Stabilization Centre service in the PHCC level to complement and minimize the pressure of existing SC services in the camp context. SCI will continue leading the IYCF-E technical working group and provide strategic guidance to the nutrition sector and partners for evidence-based decision making and capacity building on IYCF-E, CMAMI and MHPSS intervention."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"The levels of acute malnutrition among Rohingya refugees living in Cox’s Bazar remain high. According to the SENS survey of December 2022, the GAM rate is 12.3%, and the stunting rate is >35% among the 6-59 months children which is alarming. Crude and under-five mortality rates are well, below emergency levels of CMR rates of <1 death /10,000/day and U5MR rates of <2 death/10,000/day. The findings show in the IYCF-E Monitoring Survey, October 2023, that the level of minimum acceptable diet (MAD) among the children aged 6-23 months in the refugee camps is very low only 22.7% (20.0-25.7 95% CI), Minimum meal frequency (MMF) lack is 31.4% which is associated with stunting, consumption of protein as egg and/or flesh food is 34.5%. According to the latest bulletin of the Nutrition Sector, there are 45% of IYCF, 71% of SAM and 62% of MAM children assessed and the management target is achieved with a good cure rate.It is the Integrated Nutrition Strategy (2023-2025) priority is to develop a multi-sector action plan with priority activities for chronic malnutrition (stunting among under-five children in both the host community and camps) and anaemia control and prevention. In addition emphasize the 1,000-day critical windows for pregnant and lactating women, newborns, children under five years, and adolescents to address all forms of malnutrition through IYCF counselling, and health education forums. Utilizing the other established sector platforms for nutrition and health education as an opportunity.There are need of enhance nutritional knowledge, behaviour and practices of parents and caregivers of children aged 0-59 months through nutrition education, counselling and social behaviour change communication (SBCC) sessions to improve dietary practices and ensure sustained changes for cases of malnutrition for the camps and host community, incorporate with the nutrition sector strategy priorities.Despite continuous nutrition curative and preventive services, the trend of malnutrition has been declining slowly in recent years. The reasons for this are multifold and include a limited scope of diversified diet provision, prevailing unhealthy social support and cultural norms and reluctance to compile referral suggestions. Emphasizing preventive integrated approaches delivered at every service point, boosting the messages during sickness episodes when changes of nutrition status faltering are high, and integrated service provision of clinical, mental, and nutrition assessment with counselling avoiding referral time laps will give an edge to accelerate the gain. Addressing these gaps requires service modality beyond a single nutrition service point, at least for preventive components, reaching every possible service facility available for multiple exposers to counselling and follow-up of compliance."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

SCI already supports the localization and rationalization and handed over the direct implementation of nutrition services long ago (September 2021). The organization provides only nutrition counselling services with nutritional status assessment by anthropometric measurement in the health facility and maintaining a strong referral pathway with the nearest INF. In this modality, SCI does not overlap or duplicate any services as the referral mechanism is strongly maintained here for the direct nutrition services from INF. The counselling service is a behavior change communication approach continued by following standard protocol to avoid any documentation overlapping as well. As per Rationalization Principles, SCI has initiated the modification of service provision at the health post and PHCC; As an SAG member SCI has adopted and promoted the rationalization principle. In PHCC and health post, SCI established modified services modality approach including the at-risk mother-child pair services provision in CMAMI, assessment and need-based counselling for pregnant and lactating mother, nutritional status assessment of under 5 years children and maintaining strong referral pathway to the nearest INF and other sectors. Integrated and multisectoral preventive nutrition service provision is encouraged in the Nutrition sector implementation strategy document for achieving the broader outcome. Following the Nutrition sector strategy (2023-2025), The preventive nutrition service will be integrated with SCI-led health facilities that will ensure Cost-efficient integrated service modalities for comprehensive outcome.

3.3 - Age & Disability

"SCI has a comprehensive safeguarding policy and conducts risk assessment of every project at designing stage. The mitigation plan is part of the program and budget accordingly. Safeguarding orientation is part of staff induction. Awareness materials and reporting channels are well circulated and displayed in facilities and participants are informed about the process. There is a dedicated safeguarding team in regional and country office to gatekeeping the policy. Any report or flagging is addressed with highest priorities. A safeguarding report is generated quarterly to be shared among staff, concern stakeholders and donor.Health post services including nutrition corner is designed for all groups of population male, female, person with disabilities (PWD), and all gender orientations (LGBTI). Nutrition service will be the same. The inclusion of PWD is one of the priorities and health posts including nutrition corners will be made structurally suitable for their access and comfort for service seeking. Children with special mental and physical needs will be referred to appropriate service providers for feeding technique support. Community workers will ensure that PWDs are practicing optimal nutrition practices and barriers can be minimized through regular household visits."

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

SCI has an accountability system in place to focus on the areas of improvement and ensure a participatory approach from the beginning to the end of the project. The Core Humanitarian Standards are followed throughout the response programming to build quality processes into work, increase accountability by maximizing the time we must build relationships with affected people and strengthen our learning and adaptive management given the complexity of the response. SCI has adopted a robust Feedback and Reporting Mechanism (FRM). Feedback and reporting channels are used as a means of accountability and to maintain community dialogue and understanding of beliefs, practices, and behavior. Feedback and Complaints (F&Cs) received are processed, analyzed, and referred to relevant sectors, and external agencies (within 24 hours for Child Safeguarding-related and other complaints that require an urgent response). SCI reports on F&Cs through a monthly Accountability Snapshot, and quarterly Accountability report and work with sector-level field staff to close the feedback loop on an individual/community level, as feasible. For this project, multiple accountability mechanisms will be applied to ensure that feedback is actively collected, whilst ensuring the safety of beneficiaries, service providers, stakeholders, partners, and staff.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

SCI is an active member in the ISCG PSEA Network, a coordination platform of humanitarian aid agencies in Cox's Bazar and shares capacity with the platform members. Apart from this mainstream PSEAH SCI promotes PSEAH awareness, prevention, reporting, and response. So, to do this, SCI contributes to staff awareness through trainings (Staff and volunteer inductions within 7 days of joining, 100% staff and volunteer refresher once in a year, PSEAH mandates in the staff meetings, developing learning materials (posters, flyers, videos, Safeguarding Pocketbooks, Volunteer Flipchart etc.), contributing to community capacity by establishing Community Champions etc. Also, SCI supports safer recruitment practices (background check, volunteer vetting, PSEAH statements in job advertisements), PSEAH risk identification and mitigation measures in programme deliveries. SCI have a zero-tolerance approach to any PSEAH concern and confidential, accessible, and culturally appropriate Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms are available. Organizations take the necessary actions after receiving a SEA complaint that are in line with the SOP for the SEA Complaint Referral in the Rohingya Refugee Response in Bangladesh along with the survivor centered approach. There is a dedicated team for conducting safeguarding investigations. SCI also contributes to create partner capacity in conducting safeguarding investigations.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

SCI DRR team has a comprehensive work plan based on risk assessment done in the camps and is actively engaged in mitigating any damage to the facility structures due to natural calamities and also to support in the service continuity in case of any emergency by establishing temporary/mobile service point and fast recovery work to regain the regular services. SCI is Maintaining Climate-resilient healthcare infrastructure and environment friendly programme activities through implementing low-carbon solutions Such as use of solar systems, waste reduction & proper waste management and water supply systems in the health facilities results of strengthening structural resilience through durable and adaptive approaches. SCI will organize workshops for DMCs and vulnerable communities to develop early action plans based on different thresholds, enhancing their preparedness and messaging capabilities. SCI planned to provide orientation on Household Preparedness and Safety Plan based on threshold and triggering settings to empower the community in disseminating this information to a larger audience.
