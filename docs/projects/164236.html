Project Summary

The aims of the project is to 1) promote social cohesion between host communities and IDPs, as well as newly arrived returnees (from inside and outside Syria); 2) provide cash vouchers in order to move from in kind assistance to restricted cash-based transfers and provide sense of dignity to beneficiaries; 3) dedicate special attention to people with disabilities through small scale home rehabilitation in order to make houses more accessible for them. Rehabilitation will consist of upgrading home entrances and bathrooms to increase access and movements inside the houses.
The project will be implemented in Eastern Ghouta, Aleppo and Dara. As per HNO in Duma the current population is 467,925 inhabitants (IDP 19.4%, Returnee 19.2%, Residents 61.4%) out of which 10% has disability; population of Aleppo is 3,933,168 people (IDP 24.7%, Returnee 6.7%, Residents 68.6%) and as stated in HNO, 10% of them reported some kind of disability; population of Dar'a is 1,015,275 (IDP 18%, Returnee 21.8%, Residents 60.3%) with 10% of population with disability.
In all 3 locations COOPI has solid presence since 2018 (EG and Aleppo) and 2019 (Dara). By COOPI will also be able to achieve fruitful synergies with the currently implemented projects focused on protection as the current activites are allowing COOPI to enter in contact with the most vulnerable cases. Additionally, COOPI has already established very good relations with community leaders in the AoI resulting in greater access and acceptance by communities and easier provision of assistance in a timely manner.
The project will contribute to all 3 objectives of the sector, - 1) income generating opportunities, 2) access to social services and 3) promotion of social cohesion - reaching 8.750 unique beneficiaries. For home rehabilitation activities focus will be on residents and returnees, while in all other activities IDPs should also be included.
During the preparation and implementation of the activities, COOPI will ensure that proper coordination with local authorities will be maintained and, at the same time, will deeply involve communities in the design and the implementation of the project. Coordination will be ensured with the Sector and its members but also with international and national actors active in the same areas in order to implement harmonized response, avoid overlapping and promote synergies. Synergies will also be carried out by COOPI through the contemporary implementation of other projects submitted to HRP 2020 taking place in the same governorates of this Action in an integrated approach.

4 - Needs

After 9 years, the crisis in Syria continues to strain the socio-economic foundations of Syrians. HNO data shows that 75% of PwD do not have access (or sufficient income) to medicines and/or psychosocial rehabilitation. This increases their dependence on other HHs members, decreases their self-confidence and mental wellbeing. During COOPI assessment in Eastern Ghouta, the PSS team reported a severe lack of assistance for PwD, which should consist in a set of specialized protection services, including home rehabilitation. HHs with lost or separated family members -consequence of years of crisis- have difficulties finding a functioning system to address their social, legal and safety issues. The presence of IDPs in host communities that are in lack of infrastructure and have been affected by the crisis, produces challenges, lack of access to services and markets for the entire population of the affected areas. This influx of population in already-vulnerable areas also produces important effects inside homes, leading to the deterioration of the family environment and the breakage of the community social fabric. The situation is likely to further deteriorate in the event of a large number of people returning from other parts of the Country or abroad. The most common negative coping mechanism detected by COOPI during the implementation of the current projects (September 2019) are: not buying clothes, not buying uniforms for children attending school, taking loans, requested support from relatives, waiting for aid coming from INGOs and SARC, families sent their children to work. According to HNO ER&L sector assessment in Syria there is a very high demand for vocational trainings. Some urban areas such as Damascus, Dar’a and Aleppo showed very little opportunities for vocational training. As an example, during our work in Aleppo, we have noticed a very high demand for vocational trainings (which COOPI at the moment is not able to respond, not even via referrals).

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented in a) Rural Damascus:Eastern Ghouta,Nashabiyeh where COOPI has an established presence B) Aleppo where COOPI is running two protection satellite centers and C) in Dar’a where COOPI is already implementing small-scale livelihood interventions. • Community based initiatives With the support of the volunteers and the mobile units, COOPI will implement community-based initiatives (CBIs).CBIs will be mostly implemented by the community itself. After the implementation of the CBIs,a report will be produced, which will contain information on activities conducted and a description on its impact. The CBIs can include rehabilitation of communal spaces, repair of public lighting, rehabilitation of the children playground etc. •Small scale home rehabilitation - COOPI will support people with disabilities providing better access to home and improved mobility inside their living space in order to make them more disabled-friendly.The interventions will make PwD less dependent on their family members and help to re-establish a proper degree of dignity.PwD will be considered as possible beneficiaries (for light rehabilitation) only if they prove to own their house.•Cash assistance The vouchers, (following the standards defined in the sectors) will be distributed to most vulnerable families and will be used only for payment of transportation and/or food items in local markets and/or medicines (restricted cash vouchers). Specific case-by- case assessment will be conducted.•Vocational training Will be organized for at least 2.000 beneficiaries.The trainings will be organized in cooperation with specialized institutions, the participants who attended the whole training and passed the final exam -up to 1000 beneficiaries- will receive a small grant to start their own production. •Market revitalization through light rehab of the pre functioning shopsCOOPI will be aiming to support 20 small shops with light rehabilitation in the implementation area

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Key protection risks for staff and beneficiaries could be:• sex exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers;• child labor and early marriages;• violence from beneficiaries against aid workers or among themselves; • interference in the beneficiaries targeting; • beneficiaries list not updated and aid diversion; • hostilities in new accessible areas;To mitigate these risks, the project foresees the following measures: establishment of feedback mechanism, a gender balanced composition of the , training and follow up on code of conduct, training to staff on humanitarian principles, targeting modalities and criteria; periodical verification of list of HHs before and during the project implementation; FGDs before, during and after the implementation; appropriate locations for community events in line with security situation in new accessible areas in consultation with relevant stakeholders; preparation of contingency plans; clear, public and transparent criteria for the selection of HHs; House to house assessment in order to properly select HHs who meet the criteria and who have capacities to utilize delivered assistance.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

During the implementation of the activities,COOPI might foresee these risks:1.RISK:Tension between IDPs and host communitiesASSUMPTIONS:The host communities insist on different initiatives and approaches in implementation of CBIs MITIGATION MEASURES: Transparent process of the selection of the CBIs;2.RISK: The consultation processes with the communities are hijacked by community leadersASSUMPTIONS: Community leaders are not involving the community, especially vulnerable individuals (woman, people with disability).MITIGATION MEASURES: COOPI to request and convince members of communities to be proactive and to express themselves in community preparatory meetings, if necessary COOPI will be acting as speaker for communities. 3. RISK:Voucher fraud and identity theft;Vouchers exchanged for non-authorized items; Sale of vouchers by beneficiariesASSUMPTIONS: COOPI will distribute restricted cash vouchers and beneficiaries will desire/request other itemsMITIGATION MEASURES: COOPI to conduct in-depth analysis to include in the vouchers goods which are appropriate to beneficiary needs; Suppliers to maintain a copy of beneficiary list; Voucher design with unique serial number, beneficiary names already printed from central Database, fast barcode control for duplications/falsification;Social verification through feedback/complaint mechanisms with complaint cards and forms, sms and call system;Cross checking of the voucher with the copy retained by COOPI (signatures/fingerprints, serial number, barcode, COOPI stamp across the two sections) as voucher receipt; Central Database from which Vouchers are printed will be accessible only with password and by authorized COOPI staff4. RISK: High drop-off of the participants on vocational trainingASSUMPTIONS:Beneficiaries lacking interest for the training provided MITIGATION MEASURES:Proper assessment and targeting of the beneficiaries in order to determine which training would be appropriate and interesting for beneficiaries
