Project Summary

The project aims to provide early learning with a focus on social, emotional, and cognitive skills to children affected by conflict in Syria in a collaboration between IRC and local partners to promote resilience and wellbeing in children ages 3-8. Exposure to conflict and the experience of displacement has been shown to have lasting effects on children. Caregivers who are themselves experiencing trauma may struggle to provide children with responsive care. Elevated stress and disruptions to care during the critical period of early childhood can harm their social, cognitive, and emotional development with far-reaching consequences. Studies have shown that children who begin primary school with a developmental disadvantage, are more likely to struggle academically, engage in risky behavior, or drop out of school entirely (Grantham-McGregor et al., 2007).
Supported with providing psychosocial support services, the goal is to prepare a generation of children affected by crisis and conflict in Syria by introducing children to world of nurturing, playing, and learning, passionately created by early childhood specialists, psychologists, play and art therapists, linguists, writers, artists, frontline staff, human center designers, and caregivers themselves.
Throughout the implementation of the project, IRC will regularly work on the technical capacity relevant to Early Childhood Education and Development to support the continuity of this knowledge in the existing systems of the implementing and non-implementing partners under this project
This falls in line with the needs for ECD/ECE that were highlighted in the Education Strategy for Syria, along with Nurturing Care Framework in the humanitarian context that is led by UNICEF and WHO that is focusing on integrating ECD under the different sectors starting with Education.
IRC will target four areas in North Syria, Aleppo where the total PiN (severity 3-5) is 1,128,752 and the number of children with disability is 102,895. Idleb where the PiN is 1,060,750 and the number of children with disability is 1,821. Al-Hasakeh where the PiN is 447,433 and the number of children with disability is 18,172. And Ar-Raqqa where the PiNis 309,924 and the number of children with disability is 15,706 as highlighted in the HNOEducation sector findings for 2023

3 - Needs

The provision of Early Childhood Development Education and Development services in Syria is being neglected by the humanitarian community despite the clear and high needs that were highlighted in the 2022 Syrian Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) with the direct connection with the lack of this service delivery. The layers of barriers, obstacles, and challenges that children and their caregivers face in Syria amply the importance of early childhood education and development for school readiness and there are 6.9 million children (3-17 years) in need of education support. The total people in need that Whole of Syria aims to support is 4.9 million people (severity 3-5) half of this number lives in the areas the IRC is aiming to support. Before the conflict pre-primary education was not common. The vast majority of children start school at six years old. For this reason, the PiN considers 50% of children age three and four years old to be in need of early childhood development as highlighted in the HNO Education Sector Findings for 2022. Young children are unlikely to attend early childhood education due to a lack of ECE centers. While early learning for the age below 6 years old is not compulsory due to its ability to enable children’s readiness for school and enhance life-long learning, it is a priority. The provision of ECD was also highlighted in the Education strategy for 2022-2023 and the Nurturing Care framework Strategy for 2022-2023.

4 - Implementation of activities

1- is the ECED technical capacity sharing delivered by IRC’s technical team, the cornerstone of the project regarding building and strengthening the capacity of the frontlines staff working with IRC and Local partners relevant to the provision of ECED services, this will be done through a long-term Learning Cycle that will be provided for the educators, teachers, and Schools’ leaders. 2- is the integration of ECED with different programs including Child Protection, WPE, ERD and Health into direct service provided by IRC and local partners reaching caregivers and children through home visits, childhood centers, schools, and other facilities delivering the children program targeting children ages 3-8. 3-is Networking and Advocacy work, with the ultimate goal is increasing the provision of early childhood education and development programming in the humanitarian context through supporting a local ECED network that consists of local partners and stakeholders, and working on the Rehabilitation of 15 Schools, taking into consideration improving the physical environment of the wash facilities in the Schools to promote the inclusion and Gender equality, providing the furniture and office supplies, ECD going to conduct TOT school readiness training for the Master Teachers and supervisors of Ministry of Education covering the cost of the training Venues, Lunch, accommodation and transportation for the Trainees.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IRC adheres to a strong internal code of conduct related to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and safeguarding beneficiaries, especially children, as well as a safe environment for reporting that allows for anonymity. Each year, all staff attend a full-day IRC Way Code of Conduct Training that includes discussions on NGO’s commitments to beneficiaries and NGO’s zero tolerance for sexual misconduct. IRC will work with partners utilizing their existing beneficiary mechanisms to establish common standards for beneficiary feedback and ensure protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and accountability to beneficiaries and other stakeholders. This will include capacity-building activities with partners to reach these standards as necessary.
