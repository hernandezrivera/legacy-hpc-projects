Project Summary

This project will mitigate the suffering of populations affected by man-made crises or disasters related to natural hazards, and reflect the revised 2023 Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) Cluster Response Plan ensuring that the crisis-affected populations have access to gender and climate change-sensitive basic water services, adequate gender- sensitive sanitation and hygiene services, risk communication and community engagement (RCCE), menstrual hygiene management (MHM), vector control, and waste management. The military conflict which broke out on 15 April 2023 between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has increased the overall WASH needs to 14.9 million people with the cluster targeting 6.1 million people with the priority to support the newly displaced, people stranded, and families at the gathering points including women, children, elderly, people with disabilities, the sick, and caregivers. As well as people affected by epidemics, natural hazards, and climate change implications (mainly water scarcity and floods).
This project aims to provide humanitarian WASH assistance to 900,000 beneficiaries including 183,600 women, 176,400 men, 275,400 girls, and 264,600 boys with approximately 12 per cent of the target population estimated to be either elderly, people with disabilities, or other vulnerabilities. IOM aims to assist approximately 450,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs) newly displaced by conflict or disasters related to natural hazards with WASH services and improve and sustain access to safe water for approximately 400,000 individuals including returnees and crisis-affected communities. The project will focus on crisis impacted communities in various states primarily in areas where water infrastructure is damaged or available capacity is unable to meet the critical needs stretched by the influx of the displaced. IOM will also support communities to improve hygiene through sustaining access to safe water and sanitation facilities and promoting hygiene messages to address and increase community resilience to malnutrition, disease outbreaks, and other public health related risks targeting approximately 50,000 individuals.
IOM will provide emergency water assistance through the establishment and rehabilitation of water points, access to proper sanitation facilities and hygiene items and campaigns. This will include environmental impact mitigation such as the provision of solar technology to power water points and monitoring ground water to moderate depletion levels. Exit strategies will be developed in more secure states of the country to alleviate aid dependency in protracted emergencies by strengthening self-reliance and facilitating community solutions. The project will ensure dignified and equitable access to basic WASH services through the implementation of early recovery approaches where possible, connecting the need for short-term emergency interventions during the ongoing military conflict with long-term solutions by working directly with local communities to facilitate ownership and accountability. The WASH emergency interventions will gradually contribute to socio-economic inclusion of forcibly displaced populations and returnees by directly increasing access to basic services for the displaced and host communities and indirectly increasing employment opportunities.

1 - Needs

The proposed interventions are based on the gaps in WASH services observed over the last two years (2022 and 2023), exacerbated by disease outbreaks such as cholera, COVID-19, and Hepatitis E, the ongoing military conflict between the SAF and RSF, and the further deterioration of the economic situation in the country. Overall 14.9 million people are in need of WASH services with the cluster targeting 6.1 million people with the priority to support the newly displaced, people stranded, and families at the gathering points including women, children, elderly, people with disabilities, the sick, and caregivers. As well as people affected by epidemics, natural hazards, and climate change implications (mainly water scarcity and floods).In locations where easy access to safe water is lacking, vulnerable communities resolve to collect water from unsafe sources, increasing the risks of water-borne disease outbreaks and exacerbating malnutrition rates among children. IOM seeks to provide WASH assistance across the various crisis-affected states as long as humanitarian access and security conditions allow and is planning to continue its interventions in locations where over 900,000 individuals desperately need safe water, sanitation facilities, and targeted hygiene items, awareness and waste management campaigns. Low levels of awareness on proper hygiene and sanitation, lack of food, potable water and other basic services will severely affect many displaced and mobile populations during this crisis. As the population increases in areas of displacement additional water sources and sanitation facilities will be needed, especially where open defecation is widely practiced and there is a dire need for hygiene awareness campaigns and garbage collection systems. This is especially critical considering the deterioration of the health system in Sudan and the need to avoid numerous health crises in an already volatile context. Therefore, IOM will focus on providing assistance to those most-affected by the conflict in Sudan including IDPs, returnees, and host communities to reduce tension over limited resources and support increased influxes of people from crisis-affected areas/states (especially Khartoum, South Darfur, West Darfur, Central Darfur) that were directly impacted to more secure areas within states or neighbouring states, gathering points along the border including Port Sudan and other strategic areas where new IDPs and relocated population is stranded or is taking refuge. IDP camps, gathering points at borders, and hotspot areas affected by the military conflict will also experience additional risks due to potential floods during the rainy season prompting the need for additional support. Life-saving interventions will be scaled up in the immediate term, including increasing access to water in areas where supply is disrupted including health facilities and the establishment of distribution points, including in gathering and transit points. The chronic underdevelopment of the eastern Sudan region has contributed to the scarcity of water resources and lack of infrastructure severely affecting communities and livelihood opportunities in the area, and is now witnessing increased influx of the displaced. Access to safe water and other basic services remain among the lowest in the country; Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates are above 15% - the international emergency threshold levels. These communities require provision of safe water, sanitation, and promotion of safer hygiene practices which are an essential part of prevention efforts during infectious disease outbreaks such as cholera. Combining improved access to WASH services, infection prevention and control (IPC) measures, and Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) efforts are an effective way to prevent or limit transmission of diseases. Therefore, WASH services will also be provided in these communities to ensure that no one is left behind during the crisis.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Monitor water depletion levels, ground water availability through hydrogeological surveys to improve resource management and evaluate impact of constructing new water points in local environment. 2. Provide access to safe water through water trucking, construction or rehabilitation of water points including hand pumps, water treatment plants, and yards that use sustainable, environmentally friendly power sources such as solar or other green energy technology; in critical locations like health-care facilities/ schools. 3. Draft Standard Operating Procedures for the operation & maintenance of water supply systems and support coverage of O&M costs for water points for the most vulnerable communities who cannot afford water fees and support the establishment of a water fee systems once the sites are handed back to community. 4. Use Community-led Approaches to Total Sanitation (CLTS) to engage the community including equal representation of men, women, and youth groups, in designing and evaluating the responses and supporting construction maintenance of sanitation facilities and promote safe and improved hygienic practices to reduce open defecation. Indicators to mark stages of progress towards achieving open defecation free community will be regularly monitored. 5. Provide households and communities with sanitation facilities, improve community’ approaches to sanitation and address protection concerns by constructing more gender, age, and disability sensitive specially designed latrines, with durable materials to prevent groundwater contamination. Latrines to be constructed in consultation with community members to encourage inclusion, include safety features such as locks & screening and enough lighting to address protection risks. Support MOH to effectively maintain operations at waste stabilization ponds. 6. Assess hygiene practices at household level, communal setups like schools and health centres to increase community awareness of safe improved hygiene practices by conducting hygiene campaigns through door-to-door, focus group discussions, tea talks, broadcasts, Risk Communication and Community Engagement activities that include topics on methods to prevent fecal oral transmission and outbreaks of diseases that may exacerbate public health or malnutrition. IOM’s hygiene teams will be mobilizing women and local community members to validate the key messages and as key promoters of hygiene within the community including providing hand washing facilities and supplies, training communities on critical moments for hand washing, engage in systematic vector control (chemicals), garbage collection campaigns, solid waste management initiatives. 7. Prepositioning Family & Menstrual Hygiene Management kits inclusive of context appropriate items to assist vulnerable households for possible renewed displacement & healthy MHM practices. Girls and women will be organized into groups for training on use of the kits for MHM. 8. Post Distribution Monitoring tools are implemented to measure impact of emergency interventions and gathers feedback from the affected population. Conduct Evaluations to check if materials reached intended beneficiaries. 9. Community members will be trained in establishment of solid waste and water management committees; consisting of various gender and age groups to collect tariffs from community members to support maintenance of facilities& develop sustainable contingency plans. 10. Train female& male hand pump mechanics and chlorination technicians on safe chlorination, water quality monitoring, testing and analysis. 11. Promote capacity building activities for local authorities to improve management and sustainability of water sources. 12. Contribute to the establishment of a WASH pipeline for partners through prepositioning of WASH emergency items. Distribution and hygiene promotion activities will follow national guidelines on COVID-19 preventive measures like physical distancing and PPE usage when necessary.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

New water sources will only be constructed after a comprehensive geophysical study is conducted, analyzed, and ground water levels are monitored. Ground water levels will be constantly monitored and analyzed after construction; for example, through installation of data loggers. Volumes of water extracted will be recorded giving an overview of the extent of extraction from the aquifer versus recharge levels. IOM will also work closely with the WASH Sector, targeted communities, Ministry of Irrigation and Water Resources, Higher council for Environment and Natural Resources, Range Land Authority, Ministry of Agriculture and National Forestry Co-operation to ensure that Integrated Water Resource Management Systems (IWRM) are established. Calculations to deduce water balance will be done as per inputs from these entities to ensure there is no over-extraction of water from the targeted catchment areas. IOM will focus on finding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, scalable, environmentally sustainable and climate smart. Experience has shown that solar powered systems reduce running costs by two thirds unlike motorized pumps, which are also noisy, polluting (emitting greenhouse gases which contribute to climate change) and are highly susceptible to fluctuating fuel availability and prices. The use of solar powered water points will be prioritized where possible especially considering the damage to electricity infrastructure during the ongoing conflict. Hygiene and waste management campaigns will also be designed in a way to encourage land preservation through proper / responsible garbage disposal methods and systems.Where possible studies will be conducted to research on use of biogas from solid waste – this may go a long way in saving trees as an alternative to firewood and charcoal. With improved access to water and a means to support more sustainable agricultural practices communities will also be encouraged to increase tree planting to reduce soil erosion and greening the environment. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth, and mass displacement during this crisis have made the placement and proper management of water resources extremely important to Sudan’s economic development and social cohesion.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Under this project, consultative participatory approaches employed will allow for the communities to participate directly while designing and implementing activities including in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender, age, and vulnerabilities, especially for the distribution of MHM kits and identification of risks associated to accessing WASH services. This inclusive participatory approach assures the inclusion and promotion of ideas from vulnerable women’s and girls’, and persons with disabilities’ voices, who are often excluded from community decisions or underrepresented during decision making processes and face more barriers in accessing services, especially with regard to selecting the locations and features of the latrines and water points. Therefore, consulting all community members will ensure that their needs are not only addressed but protection risks are addressed and mitigated.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Under this project, consultative participatory approaches employed will allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender, disability, and vulnerability. The participatory approach assures the inclusion of beneficiaries and the available avenues of feedback and complaint mechanisms, which IOM and its partners can use to address activity design flaws or other issues. For instance, individualized interviews with vulnerable beneficiaries especially women and girls during certain activities like post-distribution monitoring (PDM) missions opens communication channels for beneficiaries to discuss topics they may not want to discuss in a group setting. IOM is part of the inter-agency coordination groups and will ensure to follow the relevant guidelines on accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms as agreed by the inter-agency groups. As for the prevention of SEA, IOM has zero tolerance policy towards SEA and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA as well as guidelines on how to manage gender-based violence (GBV) related cases during crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to refer GBV cases through the referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to any implementing partners to ensure compliance.
