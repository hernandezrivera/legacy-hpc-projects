Project Summary

There is an acute shortage of Emergency and NFI support for 4.7 Million out of which, 2.7 Million resides in areas of severity. A vast majority (90%) reported unaffordability to cope with SNFI needs, coupled with inadequate shelter. As informed by HNO and through our surveys, there has been an increase of 37% from 2020 to 2021.
The proposed action will provide response to emergency SNFI needs caused by conflict- related displacement, flooding and other shocks and protect the wellbeing and thermal comfort of highly vulnerable IDPs and host communities in NWS, Aleppo Governorate. We plan to focus on 4 sub-districts (Afrin, Al-Bab, Mare’ and Suran). The planned programming intends to be achieved through an integrated program of assistance focused on providing targeted, mixed modality CVA and in-kind NFI assistance to the most vulnerable host and IDP households (HHs) in areas with greatest severity of winter need.
Winter Response (Target 15040 HHs) - For winter response component, we have already secured funding for 3,000 HHs (20% of total planned) through SCHF, with implementation already in process. A proposal winter response has been submitted in consultation with and to BHA for 1,000 HHs (7% of total planned). The planned programming may seek additional NFIs as Gift-in-Kind, if available.
Shelter Response (Target 350 HHs) – For Shelter Response, we intend to reach out to 350 most vulnerable HHs (3,850 beneficiaries), in need of dignified shelter. The assistance would be a direct contracting assistance, which is expected to generate gainful employment for able men and women as well.
Modality – We will utilize a mix modality (CVA and In-Kind) to achieve the targets set for 2022. Primary modality will be CVA through provision of a onetime Winter Assistance valued at USD 150 per HH. Households that have received CVA assistance will not receive in-kind assistance. The CVA would be for targeted winter response items. Direct assistance will be provided for Shelter Assistance by deployment of The selection of beneficiaries will be based on our already developed vulnerability criteria taking into consideration protection issues as well.
Data Collection and Monitoring - The monitoring will be through our well defined M&E systems. Monitoring will be diligently executed starting from Verification, Distribution Monitoring, Post Distribution Monitoring and Satisfaction Surveys. The data will be collected online, primarily using tablets and Kobo tools. We deploy several tools to include Excel and SPSS to analyze and report. A robust system is in place for complaints receipt and redressal, with dedicated teams available for handling and resolving complaints. A robust system of lodging complaints is already in place through communications tools such as WhatsApp, SMS and email. Further the complainant can also lodge a complaint via hard copy and drop it in the drop box placed at our office locations.
Reporting – We will ensure prompt reporting on all of our projects through regular filing of donor as well as 4W reports and ad hoc reporting requirements.
CVA Support Systems – A fully automated and online Cash Voucher System developed internally and utilized for last 6 years will be deployed. The system is flexible to use paper vouchers or cards and can be of single or multiple use. To complement our voucher programs, we have framework agreements with Fund Service Providers that provide cash distributions through formal arrangements. Due to this system, our response time for emergencies range from 5 – 7 days from the onset of emergency.

4 - Needs

As informed by HNO and in-house surveys, there has been an increase of 37% in PINs from 2020 to 2021. All data and surveys report an acute shortage of emergency and NFI support for 4.7 Million, out of which 2.7 Million PiN reside in areas of severity. A vast majority (90%) reported unaffordability to cope with SNFI needs, coupled with inadequate shelter. We have identified 4 potential sub-districts (Afrin, Al-Bab, Mare’ and Suran) as intervention areas. Identified areas have seen surged IDPs, faced with harsh winters and flooding. There are an estimated 109297 PiN requiring NFI support coupled with 208657 that require shelter in Afrin. Further to this, an estimated 52597 PiN require NFI support coupled with 93786 requiring shelter in Al-Bab. This is displaced population that resides in these 2 areas. The planned programming intends to address the needs of approx. (15,810 HHs, 79050 Beneficiaries), accounting for 17% of PIN (NFI, Shelter and CFW). The situation is further economically aggravated due to COVID and rapid economic decline. The coping mechanism are very limited and are limited to, child labor, and purchasing items on credit increasing the beneficiaries vulnerability. There is an urgent need to deploy able workforce through CFW. This will lessen the burden on donor assistance and works towards resilience as well as sustainability with acquired knowledge and skills.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. Moreover, by applying lessons learned from previous interventions, we will engage with local councils, relief committees and other key actors to mitigate risks faced by women and children, in particular, and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries in Syria will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible. Accessibility measures will be built into assistance delivery, including the option for home delivery to beneficiaries unable to safely access distribution points due to mobility or other restrictions. To mitigate against protection risks, we work closely with LCs and other administrative bodies to ensure open and accountable feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries served. Refresher training on NGO PSEA and Child Protection policies were delivered during 2020 to ensure all staff understand how to identify and correctly report any SEA or CP concerns


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Syria faces the most complex humanitarian emergencies laded with ongoing hostilities which resulted in massive numbers requiring assistance. With increased deteriorating situation, the coping mechanisms are failing leading to serious protection issues. This is further complexed by change of roles where females are heading the households, a role traditionally held by the male population. These insecurities comes with protection issues, particularly GBV and child labor. Coupled with increased hostilities and COVID, resulted in negative coping mechanism such as early marriages and child labor. A safe estimates informs that 42% of the affected population faces one or more protection issues. No significant risks were found during implementation of current programming. To counter any risks, we have designed and put in place a strong Protection mechanism, where protection issues can be reported and complaints handled speedily both at field and HQ level. Our staff is continuously trained in PSEA. Variety of mediums have been deployed for complaints including telephone, email and physical receipts.To counter risks, a strong awareness campaigns are designed along with a robust Complaint Redressal Mechanism to handle complaints. A strong team is in place with appropriate trainings and refreshers to handle complaints. All complaints will be duly reported to the funding agencies.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Consideration of the highly volatile situation within Syria has been built into program design. Real‑time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary.The following assumptions have been applied to program design: • LCs and local authorities (including AFAD) remain supportive of the NGOs activities and continue to cooperate with established Standard Operating Procedures designed to ensure targeted and transparent delivery of assistance on the basis of need alone• 100% house-to-house beneficiary verification reduces the risk of inclusion or exclusion criteria while close coordination with other FSL actors reduces the risk of duplication• Established guidelines for COVID-19 prevention measures for all field operations continue to mitigate against the risk of transmission • Security context in targeted areas allows ongoing access by NGO staff. • Female beneficiaries continue to be willing to engage in culturally appropriate livelihood activities.We are continually updating contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in Syria, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Should breaks be experienced due to border closures, contingency plans are to be in place to adapt to the changing context in NWS. These plans detail various scenarios and required actions to mitigate risks both operational and programmatic.
