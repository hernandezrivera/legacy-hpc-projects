Project Summary

Malnutrition remains a major public health burden and a top related cause of death in children under five in South Sudan. In early 2020, the Global Acute Malnutrition prevalence (GAM) was high at 16.2 % (FSNMS Round 24, 2019 report) which is above the WHO recommended threshold of 15%. Humanitarian nutrition partners have made substantial gains to improve treatment of acute malnutrition, however the burden of acute malnutrition remain high with 1.4 million children under five projected to be acutely malnourished in 2021.The World Health Organization (WHO) aims to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving preventive and treatment nutrition services for girls and boys under five years of age affected by severe acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2021. The interventions will directly target 10% of the children under five who suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition with Medical complications in prioritized locations with high burden of acute malnutrition and food insecurity.Through this project, WHO will ensure that enhanced quality lifesaving medical treatments and preventive nutrition service is provided for SAM/MC children through integrated nutrition, health and wash interventions to reduce the burden and prevent unnecessary death related to acute malnutrition. WHO will support in improving nutrition surveillance and strengthen/establish nutrition sentinel sites at health facilities in prioritized locations to provide time critical information on acute malnutrition for prompt action. Capacity building of medical doctors, nurses and other health workers responsible for case management of severely malnourished children is essential to achieve reductions in mortality. WHO will build capacity 120 of health care workers on a) inpatient management of SAM/MC using the SAM/MC package designed by WHO and integrated in the National Protocol (40) b) Maternal Infant and young child Nutrition to promote, protect and support optimal infant and young child feeding(40) c)Nutrition surveillance at health facilities level and reporting through EWARN (40) as well as provide psycho-social support to SAM/MC children on admission.

1 - Needs

Recurrent conflicts in South Sudan and lack of durable peace since December 2013 has led to social-economic marginalization, deprivation and lack of basic services. South Sudan remains among the poorest countries in the world with 85 per cent of its working population subsisting on non-wage agriculture and livestock; the Government’s ability to provide basic services is low and inconsistent across the country resulting in increased number of people in need. In 2020, the country relapsed into the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence in selected hotpots, a second consecutive year of major flooding and the impacts of COVID-19 pandemic leaving an estimated 8.5 million people in urgent need of humanitarian assistance (HNO 2021). An estimated 1.6 million people are still internally displaced and another 2.2 million are refugees and asylum seekers in the region. The country also hosts an estimated 302,137 refugees from Sudan, Democratic Republic of Congo and Ethiopia. While there is hope for relative peace due to the Revitalized Transitional Government of National Unity (R-TGoNU), formed in February 2020 as part of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS). Localized violence continued in the Central and Southern Jonglei and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) in early 2020 orchestrated by tribal youth groups, this is in addition to some of the worst flooding in almost 60 years that caused widespread displacement and suffering among the population. The conflict in Jonglei was characterized by persistent attacks by Murle raiding groups and large-scale offensives by Dinka and Nuer (Lou and Gawaar) forces in the GPAA at the end of February and into March 2020, and again from June to August 2020. The violence left over two thousand casualties, displaced thousands, leading widespread loss of livestock, destruction of socio-economic infrastructures and increased protection concerns in the affected areas. The country is currently experiencing one of its worst Food insecurity situation in addition to pockets of violence illustrated above. An estimated 1.8 million people most of them from Warrap, Jonglei, Lakes, Unity and Upper Nile states and Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) would be in emergency Phase 4 and 5 Food security situation through December 2020 to March 2021, this number will be approximately 2.5 million between April to July 2021 (IPC 2021 report). Consequently, an estimated 1.4 million children are currently facing malnutrition, 313,319 of them are severely malnourished and 1,078,867 are moderately malnourished. As a result, Global Acute Malnutrition in South Sudan remains persistently high, the FSNMS 2019 report recorded GAM rates among children under-five at 16.2 percent above the minimum threshold of 15%. Major causes of malnutrition in the population range from high prevalence of diseases (up to 36%), poor quality and diversity of food, deficiency of water, sanitation and hygiene services, poor access to health and nutrition services, lack of adequate protection mechanisms and COVID-19 related disruptions during most of the year 2020. According to the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster Information System, in 2020, 197,382 children under five were admitted with SAM in outpatient and inpatient programs, this figure represents 68% (292,373) of the total children in need and 74% (268,045) of the cluster target. Aggregate 2020 figure taken from January to December however indicated reduced coverage for both Therapeutic Feeding Programme (inpatient and outpatient) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) from 17 to 10 percent respectively. Responding to the health effects of poor nutrition in the country suffers from poor health systems. South Sudan health systems has been significantly affected by these years of recurrent shocks experienced by the country rendering it incapable of responding adequately to diseases outbreaks.

2 - Project activities

•Training of health care workers from stabilization centers on inpatient management of severe acute malnutrition, maternal infant and young child nutrition,nutrition sentinel surveillance and reporting through EWARN •Provision of training materials and national guidelines on inpatient management of severe acute malutrition to support trainings of health care workers•Provision of stabilization centers reporting tools to improve reporting systems in SC •Integration of child stimulation and early childhood development in the provision of care for children with SAM ( stabilization centers) through provision of psycho-social stimulation tools. •Conduct supportive supervisions and on job training for health care workers to ensure quality service delivery by the front-line workers at stabilization centers.•Improve water quality for safe preparation of F75 and F100 in the stabilization centers through provision of candle filters. •Provision of PPE and hand washing point foot paddle in the SC•Establishment /strengthen nutrition sentinel sites for nutrition screening at the facilities level to provide time critical information on SAM cases for prompt action•Provision of SAM with medical complication kits.WHO and UNICEF strongly coordinate to support stabilization centers, WHO provides medical supplies such as medicines and UNICEF provides nutrition supplies such as therapeutic milk and plumpy nut.

3 - Project indicators

Through this project, WHO plan support treatment of 24,254 children 0-59 months with severe acute malnutrition with medical complications through provision WHO SAM kits which is especially designed to provide medicines, renewables and equipment suitable to treat children 0-59 months with SAM with medical complication and common childhood illness complications, this is enough to treat 50 children with SAM-MC and will be provided on quarterly basis. To improve access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery, 24,254 mothers and caregivers of children 0-23 months admitted in SC will be counseled on MIYCN. WHO will also put effort work on increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions through integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM with medical complications, a total of 73 stabilization centers will be provided with psycho-social stimulation tools, PPE and hand washing point foot paddle.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

WHO’s nutrition interventions are implemented through existing nutrition stabilization centers located in the communities where the target population leave. The Stabilization centers are established with gender and protection lens to ensure ease of access by at risk population especially women and children, for instance distance and average walking time to the Stabilization centers. To scale up and maintain accountability on PSEA, WHO will use its hotlines for reporting as well as also strengthen PSEA mainstreaming through the use of available Information boards, radio messages and community meetings with mothers/care givers on admission with children with SAM/MC at the facilities. Furthermore,the protection component will also consist of a nutrition staff focal points for protection and streamline collaboration/ referrals which will be directly linked to the psycho-social support services provided in the facility. PSEA awareness materials and leaflets will be provided to the facilities and Stabilization centers where they don’t exist.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback from the most vulnerable members of the community will be used to support mainstreaming of vulnerable and at risk groups needs into regular programming and advocate for inclusive programming that takes care of the needs. It will also be used to increase representation and participation of at risk and vulnerable groups through community meetings, direct participation of at risk groups in the day today implementation and monitoring of project activities Where protection risk has been identified in the feedback from affected people, WHO nutrition will use the information to engage the protection cluster to increase protection assistance and safeguards for beneficiaries of nutrition services. This can be in terms of walking distance to the stabilization center, or queuing time at the facility during service delivery to affected people Finally, feedback from vulnerable members of the communities will be used to strengthen and make operational service delivery referral mechanism. Conditions of children with SAM/MC is further aggravated due to service availability gaps in WASH, protection, health, Food security and livelihoods alongside other needs. Using this feedback, WHO will engage cluster partners and refer beneficiaries respectively.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

WHO has a strong framework for humanitarian accountability as such the project will adopt context specific approaches to implement accountability to affected people, and provide beneficiaries information about the project while ensuring the functionality of complains and feedback mechanism with the view of making sure affected people at all levels are consulted, informed, are participating and given timely feedback. Gender and age structured Focused Group Discussions (FGD) will be used to ensure that children,adult as well as male and female gender are able to inform the choices the project will take. Project inception meetings will be used to provide information to Nutrition stakeholders, community leaders and selected beneficiaries. Important information such as the project purpose, target group and criteria, project benefits, donors and how people will participate will be provided during the inception meetings. Meanwhile community meetings will also be used to disseminate project information as well as receive and respond to community feedback/complains

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

WHO will analyze beneficiary feedback with the view of understanding best practices and things that are not working. Consequently, WHO will reintegrate recommended best practices to increase support for affected people. Where feedback exposes gaps in Nutrition programing, WHO will design and implement remedial actions in line with needs expressed by the beneficiaries including provision of clarifications in the case of limited information. Sample remedial actions could be increased participation of affected people, increased engagement of community leaders to create a two-way communication with local partners, community and opinion leaders and beneficiaries. In some cases, beneficiaries’ feedback will be used to strengthen referral mechanisms to ensure affected people access services that are not provided through the project. For instance, networking with the protection cluster and or health cluster on issues related to PSEA and medical attention for children with SAM/MC and their care givers.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

A project risk matrix has been developed alongside risk mitigation measures in accordance with foreseeable risk in the areas of operation.To ensure successful implementation, WHO’s risk management policy will be used to track and closely monitor project activities. As such, all activities planned in areas of operations will receive prior approval from the United Nation Department of Security and Safety (UNDSS) through WHO security focal point who is the principal advisor on security issues, besides the human resource officer who advises and guides on staff welfare and potential risks in field missions, and activities will from time to time advise WHO staff. WHO has very strong policies and internal control measures to manage resources under this project, such policies will be the foundation against which the organization will guard potential losses and abuse of other project supplies. Delivery of supplies to the response locations will be from Juba using UNHAS/chartered flights. WHO operates a TAFF account with UNHAS that facilitates chartering of flights to deliver supplies. Flight 748 is another service provider that WHO routinely uses to deliver supplies and the mobile medical team in the case that UNHAS flights are not available. The Organization currently has a contractual arrangement with reputable and reliable money transfer services that allow for quick movement of cash to field hubs for rapid operations with a turn-around time of 76 hours. Training funds will be delivered through them. In addition, WHO has a Nutrition technical officer who will be responsible for all technical components of the project, the technical; staff will coordinate implementation with state coordinators and the Mobile Medical teams to ensure all implementation risk are identified and addressed in a timely manner.
