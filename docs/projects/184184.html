Project Summary

FSNU Reports From October to December 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr (October-December) season rainfall, continued insecurity and other food security related risk factors, including rising food prices and cost of living, declining availability of milk for both consumption and sale, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season. Humanitarian assistance must be sustained through December 2021 to prevent Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes for 3.5 million people. Livelihoods support is also required for people that are Stressed (IPC Phase 2) or worse.
. As is the case with disasters, droughts and the retained aspect of floods heightens the risk of family separate causes severe distress for children due to the shock and loss of homes and disruption in daily life. The median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence has remained Serious (10– 14.9%) for the past four consecutive seasons (11.8 in Gu 2020, 13.1% in 2019/20 Deyr, 13.8% in 2020 Gu and 12.6 % in 2019/20 Deyr), The country is facing a Triple Threat of drought and the global pandemic COVID-19 contributing towards the deterioration of production outputs, social economics increasing vulnerabilities of households. These culminations result in impact on the states of acute malnutrition situation affecting children and women. As of September 30th, 2021, the nutrition cluster partners reached 244,727 boys and 287,318 girls aged 06-59 months covering 66% of HRP targets. Of which 190,366 are severely acute malnourished children, 341,679 moderately acute malnourished children and 104,017 PLW admitted. In order to improve the situation, integrated programming and linkages to (WASH, Health and FSL) are needed and through early action and preventive interventions to the high risk groups the situation can be addressed effectively. Nutrition cluster needs has been estimated 2.3 million U5 children and PLW of whom 1.14 M need treatment services while 1.16 M need prevention services. Out of the 2.3 million U5 children and PLW in need, cluster partners would target 1.4 million for 2021. Detailed information on the targets will be shared soon. Lower Shabelle districts were among the most areas hosted highest IPDs movement, mainly escaped from relapsing drought waves since Aug 2021,The report highlights that the new drought, COVID19 affected people are still suffering from lack of health Service that accelerated concerns on earlier health problems. there for AYUUB is seeking and proposing with the interventions to Reduce of excess morbidity and mortality due disease outbreak driven by Floods and climatic shocks and conflict IDPs and non-displaced and to Contribute the improvement in physical and mental wellbeing of the population affected by conflict and displacement and nondisplaced; including people with disabilities AYUUB is seeking and proposing with the interventions to Provide and Strength equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (06-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases by targeting 22800 (8,500 Boys, 8,700 Girls and 5,600 PLW) drought affected direct beneficiaries in L/M Shabelle districts. AYUUB intends to provide Mothers MUAC led sessions tat the static outpatient therapeutic centers to ensures mothers gain knowledge and skills in undertaking MUAC measurement at their own, this will ensure better communty caregivers knowledge as well as provide localized way of doing things differently. AYUUB will coordinate with MOH (Minstery of Health) -SWS (South West State) as well as Nutrition clusters to coordinate response activities for maximum impact to beneficiaries
