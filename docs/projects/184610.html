Project Summary

The protection rights and needs of children is estimated at 4 million people, of whom 2.1 M are IDPs and 1.9 M are non IDPs, while 1.7 children and 445,800 women are in need of child protection and gender based violence services of which 40,500 are people living with disability and affected by armed conflict and violence and destitution triggered by climatic shocks, including prolonged drought conditions and floods. People in need of protection include households with significant proportion of persons with heightened vulnerability households with vulnerable members such as people with disability, medical conditions, children, elderly or pregnant/lactating women. The vulnerability is further increased by the Corona virus global pandemic in which these with disability have been adversely affected. The total figure of people in need also comprises of households forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as hazardous child labor, early marriage or family separation, families facing safety concern, at risk of eviction or with unresolved land disputes, without awareness of or access to protection services, and excluded from participation. Children (Boys and girls) including disable continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights including family separation, GBV, physical abuse, psychosocial distress, mental health, child labor, wide spread recruitment and use of children by armed groups, forced displacement, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/or discriminatory practices and exploitation. This situation is exacerbated by the limited availability of basic services, weak governance, negative social norms, and persistent marginalization. Persistent violations of children’s rights continue to be a critical concern in Somalia as children and adolescents are often targets of killing and maiming, abduction, recruitment into armed forces or armed groups, detention, and sexual and physical violence. Armed groups attack schools or hospitals and deny children access to humanitarian assistance. According to the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) report verified 21,560 violations against 18,079 children. In 2021, 1,797 grave violations affecting 1,392 children (330 girls; 1,062 boys) have been documented, with 631 children (97 per cent boys aged 11-17) recruited and used by parties to the conflict in the first half of 2021 alone. This represents a 15 per cent increase compared to the same period in 2020. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of child marriage, sexual violence, and other forms of gender- based violence. According to CTFMR, there is an increase in reported sexual assault against girls by parties to the conflict, rising from an average of 23 reported cases per month in 2020 to 27 cases monthly in 2021 during the same period. The highest cases of rape and sexual violence were reported in conflict and IDP communities in Bakool, Bay, and Hiraan, Thus, girls and boys remain a group particularly exposed to protection risks, such as violence (including GBV), recruitment, exploitation, and neglect). An estimate 40,500 people in need live with disability stemming from physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairments, and as a result, face serious risks of exploitation, ill treatment, neglect and stigmatization. A large portion of the displaced communities in need are members of marginalized groups and disable people, who lack support network in the areas of displacement or residence. In addition to this finding from multi-sectoral safety audits in 2021 indicate specific GBV risks for disable group, women and girls such as sexual harassment and rape. The main causes are inadequate fencing of IDP camps, poor shelters constructed with inappropriate and light housing materials (e.g. dump cloths, plastic sheets and sticks), poor lighting, lack of private spaces for sleeping, over-crowding, and faulty solar lanterns and torches

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

IDPs in the targeted locations are discriminated from accessing public services and are sometime denied access to security due to the displacement. Clan-based discrimination is a common incident in all the targeted camps specially IDPs household from minority groups, people with disability as well as female headed households are particularly affected by the discrimination. Gatekeeping from the landowners and their proxies exploited IDP due to their vulnerability and sometimes subject them to absolute deprivation from aid service or even public services. Sexual exploitation is relation to access and document for humanitarian aid has been identified as common. Sexual violence against minority women and girl’s groups is extremely common and most often unreported due to lack of clan protection. Preventions/Mitigation Measures: HIDIG will have conducted awareness on SGBV and discrimination issues through structured engagement with relevant stakeholders including religious leaders, women leaders, person with disability and business people. We will make concreted efforts to bring local authorities and traditional communities leaders to raise their voices on the clan discrimination and will call for the protection of IDP minority population. IDPs will be empowered to raise their voices on matters that affect them and will be asked to prevention clan based discrimination and calls for minority protection will be raised through out the project period. The Community based Child Protection structure will play a key role in ensuring that all protection issues are identified and reported through appropriate channels. Due to existing community norms; girls and women and people with disability are often subjected to exclusion due to cultural belief dominancy. girls, women and people with disability needs are often overlooked and are not included ion community decision making processes. Children and elder people aren't also consulted in decision making within their communities and their actual needs are also not considered. girls and women are sometimes faced the risk of sexual exploitation due their vulnerability and lack of protection. Mitigation measures: all ages , genders and people with disability will be included in the project activities; awareness will be made on ending gender , age and disability based discrimination. We will ensure to have a balance male and female representation as well as age groups in all relevant activities. Displaced population in the targeted locations face the threat of forced eviction or live in IDP sites where there is exploitation is too common due to lack of secured land tenure for the IDPs.Land owners or their proxies often taken some of the humanitarian support from the IDPs as part of land rental cost. Armed men from both state and non- state actors have multiple of times sexually assaults women and girls within the IDP sites or outside of the camps thereby create a sense of insecurity and fear for IDP populations. Sexual violence against minority women and girl’s and people with disability is extremely common and most often unreported due to lack of clan protection
