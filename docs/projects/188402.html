Project Summary

The proposed project aims to save the most vulnerable civilian population's lives and increase their security by creating an environment with reduced impact of explosive ordnance (EO) in Deir ez Zour (DeZ) Governorate, focusing on the southern sub-districts known as the ‘East Line’. The area has one of the most
complex security settings in Syria, a mix of unmet humanitarian needs, and lacking humanitarian mine action (HMA) actors' presence. Contamination along the East Line has a major impact both on the lives of civilian population, the delivery of humanitarian assistance, and access to vital amenities and critical infrastructure. To mitigate the EO threats in the most cost-efficient and sustainable manner, Team ITF work in conjunction with a national NGO, established by individuals originating from or familiar with the context in DeZ. ITF shall support a national NGO through training and building of its organizational and technical HMA capacities, skills and know-how. The national NGO's three gender-balanced teams, with close ITF involvement, shall implement systematic non-technical survey along the East Line to map and mark
EO contamination, and deliver EO risk education safety messages to at-risk boys, girls, men and women. Thereby, Team ITF will address several challenges that mark this specific context in NES, namely 1) lack of humanitarian assistance, in part due to the unknown presence of EO threats, 2) high risk of death and injury due to EO, which can compound existing great humanitarian needs, 3) lack of sustainable national NGO capacities in NES, especially in the HMA field, and 4) lack of reliable baseline contamination data. This intervention shall also restore the social fabric by actively involving women and men, and ensuring their equal access to resources.

4 - Needs

In Syria, especially in Deir ez Zour, levels of explosive hazard contamination continue to beconsidered as massive. According to the 2021 Syria HRP, one in two Syrian lives at the risk of explosive hazards. UNMAS reports that the destruction or contamination of key infrastructure, such as hospitals, has deprived civilians of basic services, and the presence of explosive hazards is a lethal barrier to movement, the delivery of humanitarian aid, and to those seeking refuge from violence, as well as to those wishing to cultivate their land. In 2018 1,465 mine/explosive remnants of war victims were recorded in Syria by the Landmine and Cluster Munition Monitor from landmines and ERW, though due to underreporting the true figure is thought to be significantly higher. The majority were caused by improvised victim-activated devices or IEDs. In addition to the loss of civilian life, explosive hazards are the cause of lifechanginginjuries, trauma, loss of livelihoods, for instance through farming, or hindered access to medical provision or education. Further compounding the needs of people in NES, are the COVID-19 pandemic and recent economic collapse, leading to increasing poverty rates and major food insecurity.

5 - Implementation of activities

ITF will work with a local NGO to train and equip them initially for the implementation of activities under constant mentorship and oversight, and eventually for more independent implementation. Team ITF shall mitigate the extreme risk of EO injury and death in DeZ East Line area through community-led emergencyEORE delivery and survey, mapping and marking of potentially hazardous areas. Following duty of care and do no harm principles, and to lower the security risks for the programme staff, the action shall be delivered by three mixed-gender teams with access to DeZ but not living there permanently. The teams, however, shall establish a systematic community outreach making sure all population groups are provided with information on the intervention and opportunity to participate meaningfully. Reporting system and data collection shall be based on local communities 'eyes and ears' information feeding key data to inform this intervention. An anonymized complaints mechanism shall also be established. Survey, mapping and marking of EO-affected roads, buildings, infrastructure, and other areas of importance as determined and identified by NTS in close consultation and with participation by the local community and Cantons will be the first activity. This will be closely followed by EORE sessions shall be delivered to increase at-risk groups' knowledge of EO and encourage safe behaviour. As a precursor to all other activities, the teams shall conduct in-depth CL.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1. Presence of explosive hazards limiting access and posing a threat to the physical safety and security to both those delivering and those benefitting from services; R2. Creation of wrong expectations among beneficiaries, who attribute to teams the ability of removing explosive ordnance, leading to frustration between beneficiaries and service providers; R3. Risk of interventions not reaching all vulnerable groups because of lack of understanding of social norms, gender considerations; R4. Familiarization with messages delivered at EORE sessions leading beneficiaries to become overconfident and engage in risky behaviours. R5: Damage to housing, land or properties or eruption of HLP disputes due to survey. M1. Delivery of EORE training to humanitarian personnel to increase awareness of the explosive threat; include first aid component in risk education sessions; delivery of training of trainers (ToT) to ensure large scale awareness and sustainability of efforts; M2. Clear explanation of intervention and objectives to beneficiaries; close cooperation between survey and risk education/community liaison personnel; M3. Involvement of community focal points in the planning and delivery of EORE sessions; tailoring of messages and EORE materials according to the audience; M4. Involvement of survivors as role models for behaviour change; linking EORE with clearance. M5: Clear explanation of intervention to local communities/beneficiaries.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R1: Teams exposed to/guilty of GBV, sexual exploitation, and abuse. M1: training all project staff trained on PSEA, gender mainstreaming, and Do No Harm; ensuring access of beneficiaries and staff to feedback and complaint mechanisms.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Disinterest of local authorities/lack of community acceptance. M1: maintain communication with local authorities and communities, employ lessons learned from previous projects in the area, deployment of community liaison. R2: Inability to ensure relatively safe access of staff to the areas and inability to operate in a manner that does not disproportionately endanger the lives of local staff. M2: Contingency planning, scenario development for remote management with enhanced mentorship and coaching of local staff. R3: Non-compliance of project personnel with SOPs or lower than expectedknowledge and competencies of the staff. M3: Re-training provided to staff failing to live up to the expectations; disciplinary processes for repeated failures to comply; feedback and complaint mechanism in place. R4: Deteriorating trajectory of the COVID-19 pandemic. M4: Adopting social distancing and other recommended measures during the implementation of activities; strict adherence to health prevention measures by the work team and the provision of appropriate PPE, testing kits; encouraging beneficiaries to use prevention methods and social distancing, and include COVID-19 messaging in activities.
