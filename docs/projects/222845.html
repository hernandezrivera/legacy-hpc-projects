Project Summary

This proposed project responds to the critical needs of vulnerable populations in Guit and Leer Counties (Unity State), Aweil North and Aweil West Counties (Northern Bahr el Ghazal State), and Raja County (Western Bahr el Ghazal State). Concern has selected these counties as they experience above-national average levels of vulnerability to natural disasters, severe food insecurity, inter- and intra-communal conflict, high disease burden, high malnutrition prevalence, and an inflow of returnees and refugees from the Sudan crisis. Further to this, Concern is the mandated nutrition partner having agreement with UNICEF and WFP, and there are health service delivery gaps. The presence of significant protection risks, especially gender-based violence, further exacerbates the situation.
The proposed project will build on Concern’s existing Nutrition interventions to integrate Health at twelve static primary health care units (PHCUs) (8 in Aweil North, 2 in Leer, and 2 in Guit), revitalize the Boma Health Initiative (BHI) for community outreach and five mobile clinics primarily targeting refugees and returnees in Kiir Adem point of entry in Aweil North, Athiem in Aweil West, while Agbang, Boro-Medina, and Diem Jallab in Raja county. The project will provide free-of-cost health and nutrition services to vulnerable communities, work closely with and provide support to the County Health Departments (CHDs), and coordinate with the Health Sector Transformation Project (HSTP) to support the uncovered health facilities.
Concern will ensure that the Ministry of Health (MoH's) Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS) is provided to the communities in targeted facilities not covered by HSTP partners. Concern will support the BHI in 10 Payams with 125 BHWs, 12 static health facilities, and 5 mobile clinics. The mobile teams (including Clinical Officers, Nurses, Midwives, Social and EPI workers) will focus on providing services in hard-to-reach areas, generally remote areas more than 10km from fixed health facilities and at the entry point of refugees and returnees. In Aweil North, Concern will support eight static health facilities, 2 in Guit and 2 in Leer. Concern will use SMoH/CHD incentivized staff to provide health services. Concern will be responsible for providing pharmaceuticals and medical supplies and capacity building of health staff. Concern will continue supporting the BHI activities in 10 Payams in 2 counties.
The project will help ensure that people in these communities have access to lifesaving health services, including treatment for malaria, ARTIs, and diarrhoea. Concern will coordinate with other partners to ensure that it targets locations with critical gaps in providing essential services.
Concern will also respond to health emergencies or disease outbreaks and support MoH, WHO, and the Health Cluster for community outreach with the existing staff and supplies from the WHO Core Pipeline.

1. Needs

In 2024, multiple and concurrent disease outbreaks reported and responded, including measles, yellow fever, poliovirus, meningitis, hepatitis E and cholera. The ongoing conflict in Sudan and reported disease outbreaks such as cholera puts the country at risk of imported cases. The most recent WHO/UNICEF estimates of measles and Penta 3 coverage is 72% and 73%. Malaria remains endemic and the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, often exacerbated by frequent flooding events. Findings from the 2024 ISNA show that only 57% of population can access the nearest health facility (HF) in less than an hour, while 44% cited long distance as a barrier to accessing health care. Women and girls in remote and crisis-affected areas facing additional challenges due to, limited trained personnel, recurrent supply chain breaks affecting family planning commodities and essential drugs for clinical delivery assistance and treatment of sexually transmitted infections. 38% indicated that their children were not vaccinated due to lack of a health facility nearby. While HSTP is currently supporting 50% of functional health facilities, 18% are non-functional. The July 2024 GBV health analysis revealed only 28% health facilities provide GBV care, with 43% offering services. Lower-level facilities struggle with staffing and infrastructure, limiting early intervention to referrals.Flood and conflict-affected areas show the lowest health service coverage and poor health status among the communities. IDPs and the host populations lacking adequate treatment and shelter are more exposed to acute respiratory diseases (ARI) and cholera outbreaks. Low vaccine coverage and communicable disease outbreaks both contribute to high GAM prevalence. Key barriers included the unavailability of essential medicines/treatment, long waiting times, transportation fees, and long distances. Concern will continue supporting five health facilities in Aweil North: Mareng Akok, Malek Miir, Akuak Malual, Lueth Ngor, and Rup Mading, which are not supported by HSTP. The CHD in Aweil North highlighted that 22 HFs are not supported by HSTP or the CHD. 10 of these HFs have concrete buildings, while 12 have damaged infrastructure due to conflicts, flooding, and lack of resources. Concern will target three more HFs in Aweil North, which are not covered by HSTP, have concrete structures, have a high catchment population (51,920 people), and lack community outreach initiatives. In Guit, more detailed assessments indicated that basic infrastructure (buildings) is much lower than in Aweil North due to the prolonged impact of conflict and flooding, resulting in displacement. Therefore, Concern will target two facilities, Nying and Kurlatjor Chuoke PHCU. In Nying, PHCU Concern is providing only OTP/TSFP, and upgrading of the facility infrastructure is ongoing with existing funding from Irish Aid. In the Kurlatjor/Chuoke PHCU case, where Concern supports OTP/TSFP in a temporary structure built with local material using local construction, a tent will be needed to ensure that health services meet basic requirements. The protracted conflict in Sudan has disrupted significant cross-border trade flows and pipelines, including the main oil pipeline connecting South Sudan to international markets. UNHCR and partners have reported increasing arrivals through the Boro Medina entry point in Raja and Kiir Adem entry point in Aweil North. These arrivals require urgent health, nutrition, and protection assistance, and it is crucial to begin providing these services from the point of entry as they transit through Raja. Unlike other entry points along the border, Boro Medina has no current humanitarian response aside from one national health actor. Concern is proposing to establish health and nutrition mobile clinics that provide primary healthcare, nutrition, sexual and reproductive healthcare services, and protection services, including psycho-social support (PSS).

2. Project activities

CO1: In NBeG (Aweil North and Aweil West) Concern will support eight static HFs and two mobile clinics in remote locations addressing refugees and returnees and BHI with 105 Boma Health workers (BHWs). In Unity (Guit and Leer), Concern will continue to support four static health facilities and BHI. In WBeG (Raja), Concern will support three mobile clinics at the entry points of refugees and returnees. These HFs will provide BPHNS as per South Sudan MoH guidelines. In all 12 static HFs, Concern will second SMOH/CHD staff and pay their monthly incentives as per the South Sudan MoH standardized rate in all mobile clinics of Concern and supported static HFs. Concern will provide surveillance reports and respond to any suspected outbreak immediately. Concern will train and support EPI vaccinators in their supported health facilities to conduct outreaches in hard-to-reach areas so as many children are vaccinated as per immunization schedules; the vaccinators shall use vaccine carriers to collect vaccines from CHD cold chain in Aweil North, Leer, and Guit. Concern will continue to support CHD in the immunization campaign at all times. Clinical staff shall be trained on clinical management of rape (CMR), Integrated Management of Childhood and Neonatal Illness (IMCNI), Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC), rational drug usage, community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN). Concern will liaise with the Health Sciences Institutes for Nursing & Midwifery in Wau and Aweil to recruit high-quality midwives and nurses.CO2: Concern will provide health services through 12 static health facilities and five mobile clinics in areas not covered by HSTP partners and CHD. Concern will leverage its recent experience with the BHI in Aweil North/West. The BHWs will be linked to the 12 PHCUs managed by Concern and will support integrated community case management (iCCM), health education, nutrition screening, and referrals. They will also be trained in reporting disease outbreaks. The static health facilities and mobile clinic site will provide health services to communities per MoH’s BPHNS. Concern will provide health services like outpatient treatment of common illnesses, including malaria, ARTIs, and diarrhoea, in coordination with SMoH/ CHDs. Reproductive health will ensure attendance by pregnant mothers in remote communities at ANC, skilled birth attendant deliveries, and attendance at PNC across all locations. Community health activities will focus on improving behaviors that reduce the risk of common diseases and will be linked with nutrition and WASH interventions. Concern’s health teams will respond to scale-up health responses that integrate with its nutrition response in 12 health facilities. Although Concern does not implement specific services for survivors of SGBV and mental illness, Concern has a referral system in place to identify and refer such cases to other Health and Protection partners. CO3: Concern will work towards health system strengthening, including capacity building and collaboration with CHD in static facilities and mobile clinics across all locations and training CHWs to treat common childhood illness. Concern will continue with regular community consultative meetings and ensure equitable distribution of limited resources like health facilities and mobile clinic sites. Feedback, complaint, and response mechanisms (FCRM) will be implemented to provide timely feedback and adaptation to community needs and the needs of persons with special needs and GBV survivors. Frequent and regular joint supportive supervision visits with CHD will be conducted quarterly. On-the-job training will be provided to health workers during joint supportive supervision, and an action plan will be shared with the health facility in charge of follow-up. Concern will hold quarterly stakeholder review meetings to review achievements, challenges, and plans.
