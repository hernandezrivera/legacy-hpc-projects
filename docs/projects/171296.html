Project Summary

In line with the WASH cluster objective #2 and country strategic objective #2, the action aims to ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient sanitation services with dignity for the un-served, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza strip through ensuring basic WASH services for conflict-affected, un- or under-served and vulnerable populations and institutions in the Gaza Strip. The proposed intervention responds to the SDG #6. SDG6 aims to ‘ensure available and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all’ and includes targets for universal access to drinking water, sanitation and hygiene for all by 2030.
The project aims at reducing the public health and environmental pollution risks that may occur as a result of wastewater percolation from existing cesspits in the target locations in Rafah and Gaza City. It will benefit nearly 28550 people of which 49% are women in four Neighborhoods in southern and northern parts of Gaza strip namely in Tal Sultan North-west of Rafah, El-Zaytoun South of Gaza, Aldaraj w Alsabra middle of Gaza and Shejaiya in East of Gaza area. Total number of beneficiaries in each location can be listed as follows: Tal Sultan 5000, El-Zaytoun 18200, Aldaraj w Alsabra 350 and Shejaiya 5000. Moreover, the project will indirectly benefiting all people in the target four locations through direct pollution prevention of their areas and also prevent groundwater pollution.
According to the information obtained from CMWU and PWA during the coordination meetings organized with them on 11 and 12 October 2020 respectively, these information were updated again on 14 October 2020 in coordination with PWA, CMWU and area focal point in Rafah and Gaza and they identified the four locations listed above which are in need of sewer system to connect more HHs in order to avoid flooding of wastewater to neighborhoods and to coastal aquifer. The need of each location can be summarized as follows: Tal Sultan needs 2500m UPVC sewer line and 85 concrete manholes and connecting 200 HHs. Aldaraj needs 100 m UPVC sewer to connect 15 HHs. El-Zaytoun needs 2000 m UPVC sewer line and 70 concrete manholes and connecting 140 HHs while Shejaiya needs 400 m sewer to connect 35 HHs. Accordingly, the project will contribute by supplying and installing 5000 m of UPVC sewer lines, 170 concrete manholes and 390 house connection. Moreover, the project also aims to stop further transmission of COVID-19 in Gaza and reduce the risk of exposure to the COVID-19 through the distribution of dignity hygiene kits to people at higher risk of catching the COVID-19 disease in four targeted locations in addition the project will provide awareness and capacity building campaigns for local communities on best hygiene practices and will provide capacity building for municipal, CMWU and PWA staff (both male and female) on the operation and maintenance of wastewater sewer lines and wastewater operation and maintenance in general. The project will contribute also in closing down all the cesspits in the target locations to stop further pollution of aquifer and prevent public health risks, hence, reducing ensuring no harm principle.
The project design has considered gender mainstreaming and also mainstreaming of protection principles including meaningful access, safety and dignity. PHG will adhere to the protection principles throughout the implementation of the project and will develop protection monitoring, vulnerability mapping and impact assessment to ensure project compliance with these principles.
PHG will also implement the project and will be responsible for its overall management and reporting toward the donor. PHG will ensure the quality implementation and management of project in accordance with its ISO9001 - 2008 standards and will ensure the timely implementation of the project in an efficient manner within budget limit and will allocate relevant qualified human resources.

1 - Needs

Over the past decade, sewage systems are a life hazard that affects the lives of Gazans, and the problem was made worse by the damaged infrastructure due to recurrent hostilities, especially after the war in 2014. The continued political and physical isolation of Gaza has exacerbated the severity of these issues, as the restrictions in place limit control over and access to its natural resources, thereby inhibiting its ability to cope with and adapt to these challenges. however, other issues such as insufficient and unreliable electricity, import restrictions on equipment, and funding shortages to infrastructural reform have all exacerbated the presence of functional sewage systems in many areas.On average, it is estimated that the total volume of wastewater generated in the Gaza Strip is about 48.54 MCM/year. Currently, access to sewerage facilities across Gaza strip varies from areas where the households are served by sewerage systems, to areas where there is no sewerage system at all. According the (PCBS,2017), about 85.1% of households in the whole Gaza Strip are connected to wastewater networks, while about 3.7 % were using porous cesspits and about 0.7% were using tight cesspits. The wastewater water crisis is reflected on the public health and on the surrounding environment causing serious illness and pollution due the overflow of the wastewater. Accordingly, an urgent intervention is required to increase the coverage rate, particularly in areas with no sewerage infrastructure. Targeted locations have been facing a growing environmental crisis for many years now. Based on Gaza WASH Vulnerability Map, PHG will target Tal Sultan neighborhood in Rafah, (El-Zaytoun, Aldaraj w Alsabra, Shejaiya) neighborhoods in Gaza area which are considered one of the densely populated and marginalized areas in Rafah and Gaza. Currently 2,600 households in these 4 neighborhoods have no access to wastewater networks and rely upon cesspits to dispose of household waste. These cesspits contaminate the water aquifer and cause regular sewage flooding along the roads. The project aims to improve the environmental conditions through the installation of the sewage networks in the internal streets of targeted neighborhoods and back filling the sewage cesspits which are the main pollutant of the aquifer which negatively affect the environment and health.In order to prioritize the areas with high humanitarian needs, PHG has carried out several meetings with relevant stakeholders, including CMWU, PWA, Municipality of Gaza. Both CMWU and PWA has recommended Rafah governorate and Gaza governorate as priority areas. Regarding Gaza, the daily volume of wastewater generated is about 70 m3, where 86 % of household are served by sewage networks and the remaining 14% uses cesspits as a mean of disposal. Whereas, about 20,000 m3/day are generated in Rafah city with a coverage rate 83% of the total household. The areas of high priority in Rafah and Gaza are densely populated and Lacking sanitation service in the project area. According to PWA and Municipality of Gaza, there are frequent complaints of citizens in that region due to the daily overflow of sewage from cesspits. Due to proximity of the aquifer to the target area, the wastewater seeps through the soil layers to the aquifer causing deterioration in the groundwater quality which is reflected by high levels of Nitrates (NO3). Consequently, the target community is suffering from serious health problems, especially the children. Furthermore, the population in that region suffers from the financial burden of emptying cesspits from time to time and inability to pay these costs. Therefore, sewage water floods streets leading to critical environmental and health conditions on the population and the surrounding areas.

2 - Activities

All project activities and interventions have been reviewed, evaluated and coordinated with the relevant stakeholders, including CMWU, PWA, Municipality of Gaza and WASH cluster to avoid and overcome work duplication, as well this cooperation and coordination will be maintained and improved at implementation stages at all levels with all stakeholders and organizations. The approach of implementation for this project will starts by formulating a project committee that consists of 5-7 person , which will include representatives from the community , women , municipality , local committees , the role of the committee is to assist in collecting contribution if any (local members) , also will facilitate work of the project staff in the field particularly to solve any conflict if raised through implementation period , the women whom representing CBOs and community will be active and expected to contribute equally in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project side by side with the committee members, they will be given a significant role in implementation from the beginning . the design of the systems will be done under the supervision of PHG , and expected to be supported by CMWU ,PWA ,Funder representative, and member form the community whom will be as members in a steering committee of 3-5 persons , they will be updated/reporting back in periodically meeting by PHG engineer / head of office /representative for the progress and obstacles faced through implementation , and recommendations form next periods , the SC will support and help to find solutions to improve work and progress . the tendering process will go through the PHG procurement system, procurement plan will be designed for this purpose. The M& E officer at PHG, will follow implementation stage by stage through the M&E module and will insure achieving outputs and will measure indicator on regular bases. The main activities that will be implemented under the project which will target the neighborhoods of Tal Sultan neighborhood in Rafah, (El-Zaytoun (Tafesh st., University College st., Al-Saeed Mosque and Samam st.), Aldaraj w Alsabra, Shejaiya-Al Karama) neighborhoods in Gaza area can be summarized as follows: 1- Supply and installing of about 5000 meters of UPVC sewer pipelines with different diameters, 2-Supply and installing of about 170 concrete manholes with different sizes in the targeted areas 3-Supply and installing of about 390 house connections the targeted areas. 4- The provision of 700 household hygiene kitHygiene kits will be distributed to the households of beneficiaries living in the target areas. Beneficiaries will be identified by the CMWU and Municipality in Gaza in cooperation with established WASH cluster. Vulnerable groups including Women will be included in the Consultation.The hygiene kits will include jerry cans, soap for hand washing and laundry, toothpaste. Moreover, the menstrual hygiene management material will also be part of the hygiene kit. The distribution will be done in a transparent manner with items displayed on a banner.The specific needs of vulnerable groups in the community, such as women, children, pregnant and the elderly, and those with special needs will be strongly emphasized.PHG will maintain a safe modality with enhanced measures according to COVID-19 health recommendations, this will include controlled management of crowds, adjusted spaces to ensure physical distances and shading in open areas, strict use of PPEs, and enhanced hygiene and cleanliness measures. 5- Hygiene promotion awareness campaigns at community levelThe project plans to target and reach 550 individuals (275 men, 275 women ) from the target area and representing a group of households and reflecting the different social backgrounds, economic groups, age and gender. through 4 capacity building and awareness campaigns, knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) of the targeted communities in Rafah and Gaza city will be carried out in order to collect information on existing hygiene practices, solid waste management, excrete disposal, quality of water used for drinking purposes and handling of water at household level. Following the analysis of KAP, Hygiene promotion approach will be designed.PHAST (Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation) will be adopted for the implementation of hygiene promotion. The methodology will focuses on participatory learning and will aim to empower communities to manage their water supply and to control sanitation related diseases by promoting health awareness and understanding. This approach contributes to community empowerment since the target people will participate in their own projects at different levelsTopics will focus on: a) how to maintain proper hygiene practices in an daily life , b) maintaining safe water storage, c) water treatment, d) transmission routes for water and sanitation diseases and how to prevent the infection, e) when to wash one’s hands and how to apply hand washing techniques, f) proper waste disposal, and, g) how a woman should maintain proper hygienic and menstrual management.The proposed modalities are will improve the sanitation services in the target areas, protect the aquifer from pollution due to cesspits and raise the economic standard of living for the region’s residents.The aim of these activities are to address the problems identified under the needs assessment which is related to poor water quality and water borne disease infections especially for children as well as those obtained from the women and men interviews during the assessment.

3 - Indicators

The indicators are: - 4,750 households in the targeted areas are connected to a sewage network. - Waterborne diseases affecting children under 5 years old and women in the targeted areas is reduced by 15%. - Knowledge of 550 locals / staff including women on WASH related issues is improved. – 700 household hygiene kits distributed. - 5kms of sewer pipelines, 170 manholes and 390 house connections are installed in the targeted areas. - 50% of cesspits (195 cesspits) are closed in the targeted areas. - The financial burden required to emptying the cesspits will be reduced by 300$ a yr. - Public health and personnel hygiene for 4,750 families (28,550 people of which 13,990 is women) is promoted. PHG will conduct risk and community assessments prior to project implementation to verify each indicator of the project to ensure cost-effective and timely project implementation. Moreover, PHG will conduct interviews with relevant stakeholders, municipalities, CMWU and PWA staff for verifying baseline data and baseline indicators. End line surveys will be also conducted following project implementation in order to measure the impact of the project on targeted communities especially the impact on women and children. The results will be reflected in the final project report. Data on monitoring progress to realize the outputs will be secured from the weekly and monthly technical progress reports as well as financial reports that will show project expenditure and compare it with progress. Management will be informed in case of any deviation from the planned targets to enable them from taking timely action to modify plans and make needed consultation with donor and other relevant stakeholders in case there is any big deviation. Moreover, monitoring progress will be ensured through adequate reporting on the established activities and indicators in the project document by using information from the following sources: a) Baseline data collected and reported. b) Data from feedback and complain mechanism created by the project to ensure compliance and accountability c) Monitoring data coming from direct monitoring and follow ups from frequent monitoring field visits conducted by PHG teams. This includes all information, observations that would be gathered during these visits, will be documented, and processed according to PHG internal result monitoring system. d) Inputs and feedback from project Committee meetings on the overall implementation of the project. PHG will document all information, observations that is gathered during these meetings and use it to ensure better informed decision making and project planning and implementation. e) Gathered data segregated by gender and analyzed to assess the level of project impact on gender and to make sure that target number of female beneficiaries are met during the implementation. f) End line data collection and analysis to report on the project impact on the target beneficiaries. g) Monthly meetings with local stakeholders to follow up on the implementation of activities and to address any potential obstacle during implementation. These tools together with baseline and end line data analysis and KAP assessment performed by PHG project team will be used to derive activity monitoring and reporting to management, donors and stakeholders on monthly, midterm and final project reporting periods or as established in the project agreement. PHG team will liaison with all stakeholders including WASH cluster, PWA, CMWU for any arising issue during implementation and will take proper actions to address these problems. Finally, PHG will ensure timely reporting both narrative and financial to donors and relevant stakeholders as per the project agreement and will ensure that reporting quality is met through its quality management system. This will be ensured through the Monitoring and Evaluation officer who will follow up with project manager to ensure compliance with the quality and timely reporting.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PHG aim with all of its projects is to ‘Do-No-Harm’ and to maximize positive impacts. In conjunction with this policy, PHG has ensured these practices by focusing on female heads of household, empowering the most vulnerable of families. By doing community outreach, PHG is able to help normalize female participation in public spaces. By providing home visits involving female evaluators, women can feel more safe in their own homes.PHG’s two-fold aim with all of its programs is to ‘Do-No-Harm’ and to maximize positive impacts. For this project, PHG will ensure that beneficiaries, staff, and partners will be protected as much as possible from contracting or spreading COVID-19 by taking all safety precautions and measures throughout the entire project phases, including interviews, needs assessment, field visits, distribution of material, and technical implementation. Throughout the whole implementation phases and project activities PHG will be followed a clear protection approach to ensure a solid engagement at the community level by local community committees. Their role throughout the whole project will be that of guaranteeing compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female-headed households, households with people with disabilities), moreover the name and personal data of beneficiaries will be kept confidential and safe from unintended use by applying effective data protection measures, prevention and response measures will be put in place in case of physical/sexual violence arising from lack of WASH facilities, in coordination with protection actors, Exposure to safety threats will be considered through the process of hygiene and dignity kits distribution, ensuring that beneficiaries will not be under any risk of theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst receiving their hygiene kits home through door-to-door distribution of hygiene and dignity kits will be taken in account. Furthermore, the hygiene kit size will be taken in consideration, ensuring that beneficiaries are able to carry home with no additional burdens (physical or financial) and without the assistance for others to carry them and in case they need help, an assistance should be provided for free and the hygiene promotion sessions will be conducted based on the culture respect and religious inclusion and that there is a proportionate number of female hygiene promoters.To avoid discrimination the proposed actives were tailored to target the most marginalized and vulnerable groups in the community in terms of sanitations. It will be ensured that the process of beneficiary registration and documentation is inclusive through applying a systematic outreach, gender balance will be ensured in the distribution teams and hygiene promoters through assigning enough women available as a contact point for women in the community, specific needs of the vulnerable group’s women, girls, older persons will be checked and provided e.g. sanitary pads and adult diapers in hygiene kits and provide additional personal hygiene items for persons with incontinence or severe diarrhea, an inclusive orientation session will be conducted on WASH services and how to use hygiene kits to ensure equitable access to all types of activities, the information on WASH will be distributed through various communication means to reach the broader community and to account for different literacy levels eg. door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms and the hygiene Promotion messages will be simple to make it understandable for the children.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposed activities were developed through regular contact with beneficiaries and recent needs assessments, in coordination with the Palestinian Water Authority, CMWU and Area Focal point in Rafah and Gaza. In line with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on the accountability to affected population, PHG is engaging households in the intervention design. As women and girls in sanitation bear most of the burden of carrying, installing sewer systems and connections to the household level, their involvement is crucial to effective WASH and sanitation projects. Women also have the most responsibility for sanitation, hygiene, health of the home and the children. Moreover, inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Community-based WASH Committees and PHG staff will ensure that the installing of the sewer systems in Rafah will include adequate safety, dignity and Do No Harm. The approach of implementation will starts by formulating a project committee that consists of 5-7 person , which will include representatives from the community , women, municipality, local committees, the role of the committee is to assist in collecting contribution if any(local members) , also will facilitate work of the project staff in the field particularly to solve any conflict if raised through implementation period , the women whom representing CBOs and community will be active and expected to contribute equally in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project side by side with the committee members, they will be given a significant role in implementation from the beginning . During the awareness sessions PHG team will ensure that health and hygiene will not be considered as a “women issues”. In this way, men and boys will be encouraged to be actually engaged in their promotion, thus contributing to the improvement of the health and hygiene in their households and communities. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level. PHG staff and project committee role throughout the whole project will that that of guaranteeing compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female farmers, female-headed households, households with people with disabilities). Beneficiaries will be consulted from the project onset regarding their preferred type of assistance and will be invited to participate in fully empowered committees. Awareness raising: in order to maximize the project’s impact, beneficiaries will be informed and sensitized by PHG staff and project committee on the prevention of major health related risks and best hygiene practice.In line with human rights PHG will be followed a clear and accessible grievance mechanism will be set up for receiving suggestions and complaints and assisting in detecting any misconduct, Feedback mechanisms will be confidential that the beneficiaries are not required to reveal their identity to the project staff, all the staff including hygiene promoter, workers and hygiene kit distributers will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, all staff, hygiene promoter, workers and hygiene kit distributes will receive a training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and the full rights of beneficiaries for receiving WASH assistance will be preserved through strictly confirming to all implementing stuff as the beneficiary don’t have to pay or provide services to anyone in exchange for Wash services.
