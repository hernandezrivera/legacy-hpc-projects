Project Summary

REACH proposes activities focused on the provision of enhanced humanitarian coordination, information management and assessment support to the humanitarian community. Within a context of reducing humanitarian resources and increasing needs, REACH aims to continually inform and enable evidence-based decision-making within the humanitarian coordination structure and individual agencies for strategic prioritisation and programming. This will be facilitated through the provision of robust and reliable data on the most vulnerable populations and locations, to ensure critical life-saving assistance is facilitated where required. In order to achieve this goal, REACH aims to conduct regular monitoring of humanitarian needs across the country, needs assessments and information management support to clusters and coordination structures to identify locations at risk of excess mortality to inform ongoing resource prioritisation decision-making. Additionally, REACH proposes to continue engagement in a national-level assessment (the Intersectoral Needs Assessment) to effectively inform the 2025 Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC), including reliable data on people identified in need of humanitarian assistance.

1. Needs

According to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), an estimated 9.4 million, including 337,000 refugees and comprising 76 per cent of South Sudan’s population, will need humanitarian assistance in 2023. These figures represent an increase of approximately half a million people in need from 2022 . The key drivers of need in 2022 – including climatic shocks such as flooding and drought, conflict and violence, disease prevalence, economic crisis, and depletion of livelihoods – have not abated and are expected to continue, re-occur, or worsen, leading to a further deterioration of the humanitarian situation in 2023. Additionally, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), 6.31 million people were facing high levels of acute food insecurity in 74 counties (out of 78 assessed counties) between December 2022 and March 2023, with an expected deterioration of conditions during the 2023 lean season, putting 43,000 people at risk of famine-like conditions in three counties in Jonglei and Unity .The need for coordinated and effective collaboration within the humanitarian coordination architecture and the use of accurate, reliable and comparative analysis is critical within this context of increasing needs and reducing resources to ensure prioritisation and response planning efforts are strategic and evidence-based.

2. Project activities

REACH will continue to actively and strategically engage with the inter-cluster response through its role as co-chair of the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG) alongside OCHA to directly inform the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) prioritization and decision-making process regarding responses in locations of high needs. In addition, REACH will continue to provide dedicated and ongoing analysis and information management support to key clusters and working groups, including the FSL, Nutrition and Protection clusters, the Cash Working Group and the Communication and Community Engagement Working Group (CCEWG). A key part of REACH’s engagement with the response coordination structure will be the provision of timely and reliable data on prioritized, hard-to-reach locations through the implementation of an Emergency Rapid Response Mechanism (ERRM). The ERRM workstream will be implemented through the deployment of Rapid Needs Assessments (RNAs) in response to sudden crises and rapidly evolving humanitarian needs. Implementation of RNAs will be determined in coordination with the NAWG and other emergency response actors, as well as through REACH’s ongoing monthly Area of Knowledge (AoK) data collection. These assessments will inform analysis in counties assessed to be at risk of excess mortality in the 2024 IPC.Additionally, REACH, in partnership with OCHA and IOM and as part of the Intersectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA) Technical Working Group (TWG), will support with conducting a representative multi-sector needs assessment, building on the ISNA in 2023, which will inform the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan. The findings from this assessment will be key to strengthening evidence-based prioritization of resources in a context where the current humanitarian needs are higher than ever before.
