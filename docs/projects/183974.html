Project Summary

The main aim of the project is to provide community support to IDPs, returnees, returnee IDPs and host communities in South Darfur. The project will help strengthen the capacity of communities to manage resources and peacefully resolve conflicts, and enable them to identify and prioritize investments in their areas using a participatory and community-oriented approach to project identification and management. The project will contribute to long-term support for development and reintegration at the community level, peacebuilding and social cohesion in areas of return. Establishment of focal points and information centers after consultation with communities to select a location, creating an accessible space for the provision of humanitarian assistance in conjunction with a local NGO, where our organization acts as a teaching partner for Empowerment of Women through Enterprises and Gender Equality organization (EWEGE), will create all conditions for the provision of relevant support to the beneficiaries. Long-term development assistance will increase economic independence, legal and social literacy, prevent and respond to SGBV, mitigate the impact of conflicts in project communities, and increase the ability of communities to act as agents of their own defense, as well as manage resources and resolve disputes peacefully. Additionally, a communi-ty monitoring and maintenance system for community facilities will be established and communi-ties mobilized to maintain community facilities.
Project activities are designed to be long-term and sustainable solutions for women and girls, IDPs, returnees and host communities through capacity building and training to continue to prevent and address any protection challenges beyond the deadline project, including returnees, at risk and / or with special needs through the provision of comprehensive protection assistance, including gender-based violence. The implementation of this project will allow beneficiaries to gain access to appropriate mechanisms to prevent further displacement, protect rights and provide an opportunity to find long-term solutions to problems. For a clear understanding of how the program is being implemented and the feasibility of providing this type of assistance, all project activities will be monitored and evaluated throughout the implementation period. The project impact analysis will be used to formulate policy and make further decisions on the method of intervention based on the needs and requests of the beneficiaries. The proposed methodology and an integrated approach to the implementation of project activities will contribute to the early achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals.

1 - Needs

In 2020, Sudan faced flooding, the COVID-19 pandemic. Precarious situations, severe economic downturns, and lack of income opportunities disproportionately affect women and girls, who usually bear the brunt of adverse coping strategies. The dire economic situation in Sudan is currently unstable and continues to deteriorate, contributing to poor living conditions, limited livelihood opportunities, increasing the risk of protection, violence, exploitation and limited coping ability. Access to justice and government social safety nets remain weak in conflict - affected areas, especially in South Darfur State. As such, the overall protection needs of women and girls in Sudan remain extremely high in 2022. Due to the recent escalation from January to April 2021, about 237,000 people were displaced in West, Central and South Darfur (Source: IOM). They are now looking for temporary shelter and support in neighboring villages, public / government buildings and existing camps / IDPs. Historically, refugees continue to arrive in South Darfur. The bulk of these people - 65% - are women and children who are forced to build life in a new place. The state's IDPs mostly stay in camps in vari-ous localities, especially around major cities in the state such as Nyala. In the state of Southern Darfur, beneficiaries can be roughly divided into two categories: vulnerable long-term displaced IDPs and the host community. Displaced persons continue to cite the deteriorating security situation and recurrent intercommunal violence as a constant threat to their safety in displaced areas and as an obstacle to their return to their villages and camps from which they were redisplaced. Gaps in the following types of protection services / assistance - in IDP camps / locations and areas of return - are most acute: case management and referrals to protection specialists or humanitar-ian organizations; legal assistance on civil documents, arbitrary detention, family law, housing, land and property issues; psychosocial support; raising awareness of protection issues; Moreover, due to years of conflict, areas of displacement and return often lack basic public infrastructure as well as mechanisms to enable community members to self defend and resolve community disputes, and promote peaceful coexistence.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project involves the implementation of targeted activities with the following results: 1. 10000 beneficiaries will be covered by monitoring activities in the field of protection, an analysis of the situation will be carried out and actual needs identified; 2.7500 beneficiaries will raise awareness through information sessions on protection and peacebuilding; 3. 3,800 members of the community (women and men) will be trained in the prevention of gender-based violence, risk reduction and/or response and 7500 beneficiaries will raise awareness as a result of information sessions on prevention of SRS; 4. 4000 beneficiaries will receive individual legal advice; 5. 8000 beneficiaries will receive psychosocial support; 6. 3000 beneficiaries will be referred to other humanitarian or government organizations. 7. Creation of 2 women's rehabilitation centers. 8. 2,400 women will receive dignity kits Community and household level protection monitoring in areas of displacement and return enables: identification of protection needs, concerns and threats; analysis of protection needs/challenges and recommendations for the way forward; and evidence-based advocacy on critical protection issues and barriers to durable solutions. • Protection monitoring enables provision of specialized protection services and assistance in response to identified needs, including case management and referral, legal aid/documentation, psychosocial support, awareness raising, and cash-based assistance for protection outcomes. • Vulnerable people in displaced and returnee communities have access to strengthened community-based protection structures/committees, reflecting their AGD composition, thereby enabling community members to be agents in their own protection. • Community-based protection structures/committees are supported to perform essential protection functions, including development of community protection plans, identification of vulnerable persons with specific needs and referral to protection/humanitarian partners, early warning on conflict triggers, receiving complaints and feedback on humanitarian, peacebuilding or development assistance, and awareness raising. • Community-based dispute resolution structures/committees are supported to undertake conflict analysis, stakeholder mapping, and conflict resolution through capacity building. • Community support projects are implemented with strong engagement of communities in the identification, design and management of the project, and contribute to mitigating, preventing and addressing conflict triggers between different communities, and ultimately ensure peaceful co-existence. For its part, the Foundation will provide in kind resource assistance in the form of materials, equipment, etc. Community members will be directly involved in the implementation of community projects and contribute their Guidance Note on the Selection and Retention of Partners for Project Partnership Agreements treasure in the form of labor resources. In this way, communities affected by displacement will increase their ability to identify, prioritize and solve collective problems.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” understands that communities living in countries affected by conflict are more vulnerable to the impacts of climate change. Armed conflict destroys infrastructure, weakens institutions, impedes access to basic services and erodes social cohesion - all critical to human resilience and adaptation to climate change. Communities are on a survival rope in the face of these complex crises. Given that the protection of civilians remains the highest priority in the implementation of this project, it also takes into account environmental lenses that will contribute to the effective management of natural resources, which, among other things, contribute to the restoration of basic livelihoods. In the implementation of this project, the Foundation operates with one of the humanitarian principles – "Do no harm", which is part of the concept of reducing the impact on the environment.Thus, the training programs will include mandatory items that train the project beneficiaries in waste management, waste separation and disposal (including construction waste), training in cleaning their local area, careful attitude to natural resources (plants, water, land). When training beneficiaries on water chlorination, the aspect of chlorine handling and disposal will certainly be included, and when training communities to install water pumps, emphasis will be placed on how to install pumps so as not to harm the environment. In the training centers, to train opinion leaders, a separate course on environmental protection will be developed and conducted, which includes hygienic aspects, cleaning and separation of garbage, careful handling of nature, water resources, etc.In the context of climate change, the carbon cost of international deployment will also be taken into account when defining the workforce. The project will employ mainly local staff, with a limited presence of international staff. Local staff will be trained in the principles of environmental protection. Also during the project, the staff of the Foundation will conduct a systematic analysis of the cost-effectiveness of the impact of this humanitarian operation on the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

People affected by conflicts, riots and natural disasters are almost always exposed to high risks of violence (physical, psychological and sexual), rights violations, coercion and exploitation. Individual and community protection capacities, as well as national protection mechanisms, are often compromised or insufficient to cope with the protection risks posed by ever-emerging threats. Thus, the situation in the field of protection will be taken into account separately, including when interacting with stakeholders and government officials. When developing and implementing the project, the location of the facilities where the project is being implemented will be taken into account, the distance of routes from real or potential threats, such as: violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence and attacks by armed groups.Protection risks and violations will be determined by characteristics such as minority status, gender, sexual orientation, age and other diversity factors. Thus, the central role of protection in this project is, together with the communities, to identify groups at high risk in the field of protection, to find out the types and causes of such risk, and also to analyze these features, taking into account external factors that affect the protection mechanism itself. In addition, this approach involves identifying specific weaknesses that give rise to such risks, taking into account the unique experiences of men, women, girls and boys, such groups as the elderly, people with disabilities, LGBT and other groups.A wide range of representatives of the local community will have the opportunity to participate in the decision-making process in an inclusive and non-discriminatory manner that has a real impact on the provision of a humanitarian response. The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” in this direction will use communicative approaches, assuming the presence of clear and direct feedback between the staff who develop and control the program, and the communities themselves. The project also plans to improve protection by differentiating the risks (needs) of the local community, which will allow for more targeted assistance. This will help, among other things, identify the underlying drivers of conflict and thereby improve the protection of the local community, while avoiding the harm that humanitarian action could otherwise do.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

During the implementation of the project, all activities will be carried out in compliance with the basic humanitarian standards of quality and accountability to the affected population. Actions and approaches will enable local communities to participate in key decision-making at all stages of program implementation: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and revision. During the project, beneficiaries and stakeholders will have the opportunity to contact the management of the Foundation by telephone, e-mail, or through social networks. Also, at the locations of the project implementation, "feedback" boxes where you can leave requests will be installed. All complaints are brought to the attention of the management of the Foundation, and those who applied are provided with a response (if they have left their contact details). If this is a complaint, then it is communicated what measures were taken. Information about the start, implementation and completion of the project will be published on the organization's website, pages in social networks, and also posted in local media.
