Project Summary

According to the humanitarian needs overview, there are 5.8 million people in dire need of GBV prevention, mitigation and response activities majority of them women and girls. Half of the affected population is women and girls, and 6% of those are female heads of households. Women and girls continue to suffer disproportionately from GBV and violations of basic rights due to conflict, insecurity, overcrowding in IDP sites, forced relocations and returns, lack of livelihood opportunities, lack of community awareness on women’s rights as well as lack of access to response services. They are also exposed to survival sex and SEA risks due to lack of access to aid and resources.
CARE is proposing an intervention to provide immediate and urgent SGBV priority services to conflict affected IDPs, IDP returnees and host communities within North Gondar Zone, Addi Arkay Woreda; North Wollo Zone, Kobo Raya, Meket, Habru Woredas and Woldiya town; South Wollo Zone, Dessie town and Dessie Zuria Woreda; Afar region, Zone one Chifra and Zone 4 Gulina Woreda. These woredas have been classified as ‘priority one’ for emergency response by the national GBV cluster AoR. Meanwhile, the woredas have also been in loss of lives, incidences of SGBV and displacement of households.
In addition, CARE is proposing to intervein in drought affected areas of East Harargeh Zone Chinaksen Woreda; and Borena Zone Wachile, Dilo and Dubluk Woredas

1. - Projects activities

1. Provide PSS services to women and adolescent girls including through women & girls friendly spaces and community-based structures. CARE will provide PSS services to women and girls affected by war trauma and experiences of gender based violations. A total of 5000 women and adolence girls will be reached through group and individual counseling sessions. 2. Conduct trainings on GBV for frontline GBV service providers (including social workers, health professionals, legal professionals etc from NGOs and government) on topics such as CMR, PSS, PFA, case management etc.- CARE will provide this these trainings to a total of 300 service providers, the trainings will result on addressing secondary trauma, better treatment of affected community, better treatment of SGBV survivors, better understanding of referral pathways and would be a great importance for women and girls affected by the conflict. The training on PSS will be for CARE staffs, CARE volunteers and partners (WCYA officers, police, health care providers and legal actors). The training will be provided by CARE’s PSS-Specialist. In addition, a follow-up support and guidance will be provided for staffs and partners working on PSS and SGBV cases. After the training, participants will be able to provide home visits, hold basic trauma healing sessions, raise community awareness, assist women and girl survivors of SGBV, supervise and hold sessions in women and child friendly spaces and all in all provide PSS service. Individuals that have sever MHPSS needs will be referred to Hospitals that provide Psychiatric services. 3. Conduct public community awareness raising campaigns on GBV & PSEA- CARE will coordinate with Woreda WCYA or Woreda food security task force in identifying protection risks and needs of these at-risk groups, and plan interventions that strive to reduce their exposure to GBV and other forms of violence. Once risk factors are identified community discussion topics will be developed. Safe site and number of gatherings for discussion will be selected considering COVID 19 physical proximity restrictions.4. Procurement & distribution of Dignity KitsIn times of crisis, women and girls need basic items in order to interact comfortably in public and maintain their personal hygiene, particularly menstrual hygiene. Without access to culturally appropriate clothing and hygiene items, the mobility of women and girls is restricted and their health is compromised. Priority items from the recommended dignity kit will be procured and distributed for 40,000 women and girls of reproductive age with disposable sanitary pads, body soap and underwear. Recipients of these kits will be provided to IDP household members residing in some of the worst affected areas within the selected Woredas. Selection of Kebeles and subsequent beneficiaries will be done in consultation with community representatives and government officials. Distribution of kits will take place in pre -agreed locations where safe passage of beneficiaries can be guaranteed and as much as possible will link to the provision of other emergency response services. Through the distribution of dignity kits, CARE will promote self-esteem, confidence, mobility and safety of women and girls of reproductive age. Distribution will including women in the process of selecting the distribution points and dates, provide information prior to the distribution (what, when, where, how) so women and girls can plan to collect their dignity kit safely and discretely, organize the distribution in a discrete place, by women staff, avoid locations a long way away from shelters as these may increase GBV risks. CARE will take maximum precaution for COVID 19 risk mitigation by maintaining measures of physical distancing, CARE will provide physical protective equipment for health and safety of staff engaged in distributions and gatherings.5. 3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

CARE is committed to saving and protecting lives of affected communities. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities is one of the core values in CARE. CARE Ethiopia will highly involve target affected communities in conducting the community consultation, during implementation of the actual work, and in monitoring and evaluation. CARE will organize regular and ongoing meetings with committees of affected communities in coordination with the Woreda Women and Children Affairs Office. CARE staffs will closely work with beneficiaries, committees and representatives, government community development agents (DAs), community health workers (HEWs), and people in the local administration. This will create a good opportunity for CARE to consult with beneficiaries and key informants during the implementation of activities and stay updated on the situation of community and the different needs. CARE will establish complaint and feedback hearing committees where cases will be directly referred to and responsive action is taken. Should serious cases arise that are beyond the capacity of the committees CARE will facilitate referrals to official government structures for remedial actions.
