Project Summary

Communities in Southern Ethiopia are facing the worst drought in decades. The failure of two consecutive rainy seasons has pushed pastoralist and agro-pastoralist communities towards severe food and livelihood insecurity. Loss of crops and livestock means that an estimated 1.8 million people are in urgent need of humanitarian support.
The drought has adversely affected livestock production and productivity resulting in occurrence of livestock diseases, poor livestock body condition and death of animals. Viable pastures within local rangelands are highly depleted, leading to a sharp decline in animal productivity. Cattle with poor body condition cannot be sold in the local markets, severely affecting income opportunities and purchasing power of local communities. Household cash availability levels have been negatively affected by declining livestock prices and extremely low agricultural production.
18,000 households (117,000 individuals) will directly benefit from the drought response project. The response aims to enhance the resilience-building capacity and support the livelihood restoration efforts of drought-affected pastoral communities. The project will last for six months and will benefit 3,000 drought affected Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) households and 15,000 host community households. The beneficiary breakdown is as follows: 750 IDP households and 5,250 host community households in Dawa, 750 IDP households and 5,250 host community households in Liben, 750 IDP households and 2,250 host community households in Borana and 750 IDP households and 2,250 host community households in South Omo.
The project will focus on access to supplementary animal feed and cash transfer assistance. The activities are informed and aligned with the Agriculture Cluster objectives and framework priorities, including protection of core productive assets and access to livelihoods of crises affected households, and the Ethiopian Cash Working Group (ECWG) guide on cash transfer programming.
The project is planned to be implemented in four zones of three Regions in Southern Ethiopia. The reasons for the geographic focus are: 1) severity of the drought and needs of the target population; 2) HEKS’s presence on the ground – where the organization has logistics set-up, staff that know the context and culture, institutional relationships – and capacity to respond to the drought; 3) regular engagement of HEKS in local coordination platforms; and 4) sound relationship with the local government. The project will promote localization by working together with local partner Agri-Service Ethiopia building on previous joint interventions and thus developing the capacity of the local partner to deliver emergency responses. The project will also work with Dorcas building on existing partnerships and expertise.
The project will be implemented in locations that do not increase vulnerabilities, do not facilitate forced return, and will be implemented with careful planning to ensure that all targeted groups are consulted, engaged and involved for assistance. The project will ensure the engagement of women, people with disabilities, elderly people and other vulnerable groups in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the projects inputs, outputs and results.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1: Animal feed distribution to 9,000 families (58,500 people)The project will distribute 2.5 kg of concentrates feed per day for 30 days to be used by 9,000 families (58,500 people) to feed 27,000 core breeding animals (3 animals per family). These animals will support families that have lost their livestock and enhance livelihood restoration. The intervention will be coordinated with the Woreda government officers. Beneficiary selection will be carried out by a team composed of representatives from woreda pastoral development offices, community representatives including kebele administration and rangeland councils, development agents assigned in each kebele and the HEKS emergency team. The key targeting considerations (80% women, 25% people with disabilities) are based on community dialogues that identify the most vulnerable families in the community, such as women-headed households, families with children with Severe Acute Malnutrition and people with disability or families who have members with disability. The committee is mandated to discuss and agree on additional targeting criteria and identify eligible households. Overall, HEKS will oversee the process on the ground to ensure fairness and transparency. Costing Methodology is based on the HRP 2022 (Draft), revised in line with the inflation. Activity 2: Multipurpose Cash distribution to 9,000 families (58,500 people)The project will include Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to 9,000 households (58,500 people). Cash is the most effective and flexible mechanism to urgently address needs of people affected by the drought as it provides maximum flexibility, choice, and dignity to buy items are most urgently needed. Banking service providers will carry out direct cash distribution to reach targeted people. In collaboration with local government authorities, the project team has verified that selected target areas maintain functional markets that are suitable for unconditional cash interventions. The transfer value is 2,000 ETB or $40 for one round. MPCA beneficiaries will be selected through a well-established process. HEKS/EPER will conduct a community sensitization event on the selection criteria (see details below) and obtain validation by community representatives. Targeting committees (with community leaders/representatives, government authorities and IDP representatives) will be established in each location where the activity takes place. The committees will be responsible for beneficiary registration and verification. The committees will also share the final list with the financial service provider, establish compliant feedback mechanisms (suggestion boxes, posting whistle-blowing contact details for anonymous calls and text messages, and provide feedback on relevant project measures). Capacities of MPCA project staff on protection mainstreaming will be strengthened during their project induction sessions and through on-job mentoring by experienced HEKS/EPER staff. MPCA project staff will sensitize beneficiaries on prevention of GBV and protection risks throughout activities. Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be carried out in each project location as part of this activity.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

In order to respond to the humanitarian situation in the target area, HEKS will promote coordination and collaboration with different organizations that are present in the zone. The Food Security cluster cluster platform and the technical Cash Working Group (CWG) will be used to actively communicate with other organizations and for sharing and exchanging information, expertise, resources, and reporting and/or briefing. HEKS will work with relevant government offices/agencies, public service institutions such as Development Agents (DA) or extension workers, different community traditional institutions, NGOs, financial service providers, local leaders and local groups with roles and responsibilities in humanitarian crisis preparedness and response. Effective coordination among these various responders will contribute to successful preparation and response to the crisis. HEKS will collaborate to promote coordination to eliminate gaps and duplication in service, determine an appropriate division of responsibility and establish a framework for information sharing, policy agreements, program collaboration and joint planning. The project team will implement the activities in collaboration with local government partners (livestock office, disaster risk management office etc.). Regular consultations with other stakeholders working in the same area will be sought in order to bring about complementarity. The project partners will work with protection partners in the area through referral pathways. Finally, the project team will strictly coordinate and work in complementarity with other humanitarian partners operational in the area, in order to avoid duplication of efforts and ensure the highest coverage of gaps.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

HEKS has a strong commitment to receive and respond to beneficiary feedback in a safe timely manner, including being contextually relevant and in line with literacy levels and language diversity. Local leadership and community members contribute to assessments, monitoring and evaluation, as part of key informant interviews (KIIs), focus group discussions (FGDs), and post-activity / end line data collection. Such participatory data collection mechanisms will ensure that all categories of affected persons (including women, youth, persons with disabilities and older persons) are actively involved providing information and feedback about whether the interventions are effectively addressing their specific needs. Anonymity will be guaranteed in this process. Collected field data will be analyzed in a gender and age disaggregated form by M&E and technical teams. Community sessions (potentially including single-sex sessions) will be organized to share and discuss the monitoring results. These sessions will also be used to assess the opportunity of adjusting the interventions (e.g. content/format of hygiene promotion sessions) to better address priorities/needs of different social groups. Different groups of beneficiaries/project participants will thus have the opportunity to provide feedback as appropriate. This will entail informing beneficiaries, either directly or through their community leaders, during regular community sessions or meetings, of any change in project strategy or activities that follow specific feedback / complaints (that is, closing the feedback loop). Since 2018, HEKS is a member of the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS Alliance) and is thus committed to complying with the nine standards of the voluntary code to improve the quality and effectiveness of all its humanitarian aid projects worldwide. HEKS will make sure that beneficiaries know they have a right to equitable and safe assistance, where and how to obtain it. In line with the organization’s Human Rights Based Approach, HEKS is committed to ensuring that the proposed project is based on a participatory and empowering approach that enables and encourages affected populations, especially marginalized individuals, to play an active role in the project and in decision-making processes that affect their lives. This translates into putting place in the targeted locations specific mechanisms to provide timely and accessible information, and to receive and respond to beneficiary feedback throughout the duration of the activity. Gender-appropriate feedback will be ensured through ensuring feedback locations are safe and accessible to women; through deployment, where possible, of female staff; separate FGD with women. Age-appropriate feedback will be enhanced through ensuring feedback locations are safe and accessible to people from different age groups, including the elderly. HEKS will also provide opportunities for women, people with disabilities and older people to hold positions of responsibility and be represented on community-based committees including as community focal points for assessment teams. An accessible, well understood and confidential mechanism, including during Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM), will be set up for receiving feedback (suggestions and complaints), with a clear follow-up system on all reports. HEKS’s feedback and complaint mechanism includes information sharing on: project objectives, outcomes and outputs, implementing agency and donor; selection criteria and mechanisms for beneficiaries; composition of the cash distribution; livelihood restoration activities to be carried out. The validation and agreement with the main stakeholders on the beneficiaries selection, verification, cash distribution place and time will take place through meetings with community members, local authorities and cluster representatives.
