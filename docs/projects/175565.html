Project Summary

In the recent needs assessment undertaken by Rural Action Against Hunger (RAAH) in Yei and Kapoeta in December 2020; WaSH needs emerged high amidst other multi-sectoral needs. Over 80% of households surveyed had poor WaSH services evidenced by health facility data of increasing cases of watery diarrhea, pneumonia; and the long ques sited in the few protected water sources such as boreholes and yards. Majority of the population used unsafe water mainly from the rivers/pond/ open charcoal water catchment areas. There were serious disturbing concerns of targeted GBV meted on young adolescent girls as they usually support water collection in the families. There were long ques where girls and women seen struggling for 3-4 hours to access protected water sources (estimated to be 5% i.e. boreholes and solarized piped water) and over 90% ponds and rivers where animals and human beings crumble for water especially in the peak dry season of January to May yearly in Kapoeta region are unsafe for household use. The scarcity of safe water in Kapoeta region negatively impacts on the hygiene standards of the most vulnerable households especially children, lactating mothers, elderly and persons with disabilities. Since March 2020, the shutdown of schools and the economy to prevent the spread of Covid 19 in the Kapoeta region and Yei areas close to the border, have recorded a high number of unwanted teenage/child pregnancies according to a health expert-Kapoeta and Yei county health authorities. There are also cases of child abuse in pursuit for daily bread where young girls below 18 years are traded in marriage in exchange of animals for the family survival in Kapoeta. In RAAH’s interaction with the community, this practice has fermented violence amongst some families when these forced marriages were perpetrated by few advantaged community members and were crying when schools would be opened to keep the children out of the early childhood marriage they have seen grow over the period. In Kapoeta North, there were no visible toilets and over 90% of the population were practicing open defection. Where as in Yei, 60% of protected water sources i.e. handpumps were not functional and the major reasons attributed to it was lack of spare parts in the local market and County Rural Water and Sanitation. Over 35% of the county population mainly relies on unprotected sources of water such as rivers, ponds, shallow wells. RAAH’s assessment of the sanitation and hygiene situation in Yei county was relatively better as compared to Kapoeta region. However, new voluntary IDP, returnees and refugees families that returned from the diaspora lacked toilets estimated to be 5% of the population and Yei county health data reviewed reflected commonly morbidities as watery diarrhea, ARIs, pneumonia, cough and malaria. The link to these preventable illnesses has always been an integration of main stream WaSH services across HHs, communal and institutional structures to adequately address the route causes and link services with Health, FSL, GBV responses
RAAH proposes integrated WaSH lifesaving and resilience interventions in the locations of Yei and Kapoeta North counties targeting 21,894 persons (3,649 HH) hailing from returnees, IDPs, refugees and host community with extreme vulnerabilities i.e. women, men, boys, girls, and PWDs to reduce the acute shocks, and chronic stresses already identified in IPC 2020/2021 and REACH data of multiple vulnerabilities (food insecurity, undernutrition, trauma of armed conflicts, inadequate health care etc.) in line with the strategic priorities and objectives of WaSH cluster.
The most vulnerable groups been targeted include: children under-fives, pregnant and lactating women, elderly and PWDs for a scaled-up response through provision of minimum famine prevention packages with targeted WaSH NFIs distribution; operation and maintenance of WaSH infrastructure [repairs of BH, dams, digging toilets, and training of WaSH coms]

1 - Needs

Inter and multi-sectoral humanitarian needs continue to grow to insurmountable scale in Republic of South Sudan despite the relative peace in parts of the country since the signing of revitalized peace agreement in 2018. Floods, drought, cattle raiding, revenge killings, communal insecurities using mainly fire arms amongst targeted communities and Covid-19 pandemic vices have enormously contributed to watered down efforts in basic social service delivery, displacements, rise in mortality and morbidity rates[ REACH, HNO 2021 and OCHA humanitarian access overview]. Like many parts of South Sudan, Kapoeta North- predominantly semi-arid belt is faced with food insecurity, rise in malnutrition cases, inadequate WaSH services, worrying trends of GBVs and as well as Yei county. Humanitarian access and quick deployment of aid workers over time has been meted with pockets of armed attacks in these locations where lifesaving services are needed most. Kapoeta North and Yei Counties are in (IPC 4+); RAAH’s needs assessment undertaken in Kapoeta and Yei in December 2020 reflected high WaSH needs. 80% of households surveyed in Kapoeta and Yei county health data had an increasing case of watery diarrhea, pneumonia; and was projected to be high in this dry spell Feb-May]. There were very long ques along protected sources estimated to be 5% i.e. (borehole and motorized/solarized pump piped water). Over 90% of the community accessed unsafe water from river/pond/dam catchment. According to Save the Children Protection Specialist in Kapoeta, young girls and women experienced disturbing concerns of targeted GBV meted along the water points. A mother confirmed to RAAH WaSH team of having to wait for 3-4 hours to look for water especially in the dry season. Long ques were seen around the few protected water sources; and water collection containers were visibly seen dirty. In the dry season (January to May) the common ponds and rivers totally dry up, the few pumps are overstretched in use and break often as well as the scramble for water for humans and animals increase causing violent conflicts especially in Kapoeta region. The scarcity of water in Kapoeta region, has negatively impacted on the hygiene standards of the most vulnerable households especially children, lactating mothers, elderly and persons with disabilities. County Education authorities in Kapoeta and Yei reported that when schools were shutdown to prevent the spread of Covid 19, the number of teenage pregnancies has risen inclusive child abuse a factor that is fermenting intercommunal violence for few parents in Kapoeta who value education as opposed to early marriages. In the six villages visited by RAAH in Kapoeta North (Lomeyen; Mosingo; Najie; Karukomuge; Chumakori and Paringa payams) visibly there were less than 4% toilets available and over 96% of the population were practicing open defection. Where as Yei county mainly a green agro-ecological belt has a number of water sources and receives rainfall twice a year. RAAH teams interaction with County Rural Water and Sanitation, revealed over 60% of the protected water sources such as water handpumps were not functional and the major reasons were attributed to lack of spare parts in the local market and in the County Rural Water and Sanitation department. Sanitation and hygiene situation in Yei county were relatively better compared to Kapoeta region. Yei county in the Q.4 of 2020 according to UNHCR and protection partners reported having received at least 5% of returnees, refugees and IDPs from Uganda, Congo and Sudan. These vulnerable families lack sanitation facilities of there own and the copying mechanisms shared by local authorities is that some of these families do share toilet facilities with neighbors and other practices open defection. 0ver 40% of the county population access unsafe water from swamps, rivers, shallow wells according to our key informants’ interview in Lasu; Mugwo Otogo; and Tore payams of Yei county.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RAAH upholds strict measures to ensure beneficiary rights are upheld, including the right to information, gender inclusion, dignity, safety, and avoid harm. Our staff and prospective beneficiaries sign standing agreements before being deployed to service area and expected to be good ambassadors in PSEA. We use various stakeholder engagement platforms such as meetings, seminars, workshops to point out the risks of PSEA and implore the local authorities/leaders to be good advocates of ensuring the rights of the beneficiaries are upheld. RAAH employs the following principles on Gender throughout its programming across thematic sectors to enhance gender transformative approaches: 1. Respect of individual rights according to international humanitarian law, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). The main idea is to treat people regardless of their sex, ethnicity etc. 2. Inclusivity = equal opportunities: affirmative action to get more women in decision making: - awareness raising and education; - being open to accept women in the process. 3. Non-violence: seeking the non-violent solutions 4. Pro poor focus 5. Respect for positive cultural values (the negative are against the basic human rights) 6. Promotion of women as equal partners (in relationships) 7. Encouraging family values 8. Non-stereo typing

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will use a range of feedback and complaints mechanisms such as pictorial fliers to share WaSH advocacy information for most vulnerable members who are able to see, assign an interpreter for verbal talk for those able to hear in the local language to be able to receive their feedback with a dedicated staff (both male and female). A help desk will be assigned at every critical WaSH service point to address these concerns e.g. WaSH NFI distribution. Also referral path for protection concerns for further case management will be shared to the beneficiaries incase RAAH staff are unable to offer support with explanation to the vulnerable member to ensure continued involvement and adequate WaSH service delivery. Two way feedback information sharing will be encouraged from the most vulnerable persons, project team and local authorities and active involvement in the project activities for all representatives [women, girls, boys, men, elderly and PWDs] will ensure dire needs will be voiced and jointly service level improvements will be avenued.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Generally, RAAH has put in place flexible critical project information sharing platforms with all the stakeholders being mindful of ease of access, affordability and timeliness. The project will first use project initiation/inception meetings platform to share on WaSH needs in the community, proposed strategies of how to address these needs while involving women, girls, boys, men and persons with disabilities; and the roles of each gender groups. Then the communities opinion will be sought on their preferred feedback mechanisms and they will be also shared already in place feedback mechanisms as toll-free lines, help desk, suggestion boxes, and referral path ways in the multi-sectoral cluster to get assistance and other technical networks. Democratically, most preferred and convenient feedback mechanisms affordable to women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, and men will be agreed. RAAH will then encourage the assigning of gender specific focal point leads as initial contact points for the most vulnerable to quickly voice their concerns and during wider stakeholder project review meetings; the information shared [tracker] by the different gender groups will be used for joint decision making. All most vulnerable persons i.e. women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, and men will be represented in the various WaSH committees [ water user committees, hand pump mechanics, community hygiene promoters]. All these structures will be empowered to share information with RAAH technical WaSH project team and RAAH will then coordinate with the cluster, line ministry and partners for possible support and advocacies for any of the shared needs un addressed with the aim of improving equitable access to WaSH services in the various location where the WaSH infrastructures are commissioned. RAAH’s monitoring and evaluation (M & E) function will particularly be one of the useful tools for feedback and information sharing in the project. The M/E unit regularly talks to beneficiaries, provides adhoc information on success factors on uptake of WaSH services to all gender groups, gaps and learnings are shared for improvement. Also, the inbuilt involvement of gender representative community WaSH committees represented by [women, girls, boys, men, elderly and PWDs) in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation activities will facilitate local knowledge sharing and learning a very useful tool for improving the project environment success factors. Women, girls, boys, men, elderly and PWDs will be engaged in identifying safe location of new WaSH facilities with technical support from RAAH team, be part and parcel of joint monitoring; and all feedback obtained will be used to refine the WaSH services provision structures for the benefit of all target population and entire community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

At critical service points such as project inception meetings, sighting of WaSH facilities, WaSH NFI distribution, GBV safety audits and Joint monitoring there are critical service gaps that come up and we this information to engage key stakeholders to address the service gaps; through our operation and maintenance activities, and redesigning of the project will always be possible. RAAH WASH team will actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that response and complaint are streamlined, and dealt with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn) from affected individuals regarding matter arising concerning WASH and WASH related issues (GBV, Nutrition and Health) that may have human rights, health or other implications, and employ measures for handling these shall be adapted accordingly. The affected populations will be allowed to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have reached on monthly basis.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

RAAH an active member of humanitarian community has ascribed to various protocols to ensure protection of most vulnerable members of the community and have their human rights upheld. In this regard, RAAH will undertake WaSH safety audits to evaluate the level of accessibility and safety of existing water and/or sanitation facility and its surroundings, and to identify possible changes or improvements. The audit identifies simple changes to facilities and their surrounds that can improve their usability for a wide range of users, thus avoiding the need for separate ‘special’ provision. The audit will also be a way for stakeholders to consider any risks to safety from using the WaSH facilities and to consider if anything can be done to reduce the risks. Accessibility and safety audits will support engagement of persons with disabilities to participate in each stage of the process, including the training and when doing the accessibility and safety audit and use the feedback to improve barriers to accessing WASH services. Also with the help of protection tip sheet, RAAH will ensure women, girls, boys and men’s different risks and potential rights violations in conflict and disasters can be minimized through prioritizing safety & dignity at WaSH NFI distribution points, home to home hygiene campaigns and at water points. This project has been designed with ingredients of identifying and addressing the most likely physical and psychosocial risks for different groups while accessing WaSH services. For instance to reduce GBV cases around WaSH facilities, RAAH will encourage vulnerable girls to walk in groups while collecting water; empower WuC to sensitize the community on prevention of negative GBV risks commonly practiced around the water points. In all the service points such as sanitation facilities; they will be having lockable doors, disaggregated for all genders, siting of the facilities will be in secure locations and arrange equitable access to WaSH services in proportion to need and levels of risk, by all gender and age groups. All the project kicks off; various stakeholder engagement platforms such as gender specific review meetings, help desks at service points and assigning of each gender group representation in the project implementation and monitoring will help ensure all affected gender and age groups feedback is obtained and their unique help needs are addressed. Already this project has provided for effective participation and empowerment of women, girls, boys and men in different age groups to protect themselves and claim their rights, including freedom from harm and the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education; and be in-charge of monitoring the benefits received by women and men, girls and boys, and compare these to the expressed needs and priorities of the different groups. All feedback obtained in the course of provision of the WaSH services will be points of further engagement with priority project stakeholders for joint decision making, service improvement and refinement of the project within known constraints such as cost, time, scope and quality.
