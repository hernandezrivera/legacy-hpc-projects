Project Summary

The project focuses on ensuring and maintaining access to essential life-saving health services and vital public health interventions for refugees and closest host communities living in 13 very high/high risk localities of the 8 states. The functionality of the clinics through provision of free-of charge health services will be supported in areas (not covered through partners), with medical supplies.
The provision of essential PHC services and referral system to PHC centers and hospitals, including availability of free-of-charge medicines is vital to ensure the access to essential health care services for people in need of humanitarian aid, as well as addressing the impending public health threats such as COVID-19.
Most of the essential medical supplies (IEHK, and Lab reagents) will be procured internationally from WHO pre-qualified suppliers meeting the international quality standards and donors requirements. the kits are adapted to the protracted nature of Sudan crisis and to the local morbidity profile considering the most common diseases. The kit include a balanced mix of medicines responding to the demographic specificities with almost 70% women and children and their specific health need; pediatric formulas for children bellow 5 years of age, medicines for ante, post-natal care, and essential (first line treatment) medication for adults acute and chronic diseases.
Case management trainings, Rapid Response Teams in charge of assessment and initial response to public health events such as alert investigation and initial response will be implemented by WHO, weekly EWARS (early warning) data will be collected and analyzed.
To support timely investigation and initial response to recurrent outbreaks; specific laboratory confirmation kits will be procured along with other needs.
Around 1 mln people will indirectly benefit from the project implementation.

1 - Needs

In October 2020, UNHCR indicated that, there have been nearly 992000 refugees and asylum seekers in Sudan 74% of them are South Sudanese who arrived and continue crossing the borders to Sudan since Dec 2013, most of the South Sudanese refugees are settled in White Nile camps 261,000, East Darfur 70,000, West Kordofan 62000 and 38000 in South Kordofan, in addition to; unverified number in Khartoum. In November 2020, military confrontations between federal and regional forces in Ethiopia’s Tigray region, which borders both Sudan and Eritrea, led the Government to declare it in a State of Emergency. Since then, Ethiopia’s Tigray region has reportedly continued to be affected by incidents of armed conflict. Since early November, Eastern Sudan received large numbers of refugees through at least three border points, and there are currently no clear signs that this refugee flow is coming to an end. The population in the Tigray region of Ethiopia includes approximately 600,000 people dependent on food relief assistance (more than 400,000 food insecure, 100,000 IDPs and 95,929 Eritrean refugees). In addition, 1 million people receive safety net assistanceAs of 22 November 2020, and within just two weeks, Sudan has received over 40,000 new Ethiopian refugees.The country faces multiple challenges, including over 200% annual inflation, currency depreciation and fuel shortages, further compounded by lockdown restrictions due to COVID-19 earlier in 2020. According to the latest International Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report food insecurity levels are the highest ever recorded in Sudan. The 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that over 12.7 million people are in need of assistance, including one million refugees, an increase of a third compared to 2020.The situation of the refugees shows continuing influx of arrivals with very poor living condition as they are accommodated at congested camps or at public buildings, and market shelters, most of the new arrivals having traveled for an extended period of time, carrying few personal belongings and in need of urgent humanitarian assistance shelter, water and sanitation, health, protection, food. The ongoing influx of refugees added to already existing caseload has stretched the needs for basic services including health in 8 states prioritized by WHO for urgent humanitarian support (White Nile, South and West Kordofan, North Darfur, East Darfur, Khartoum, Kassala and Gedarif states). The burden posed on the existing public health facilities in 8 states serving the local community and the refugees which have been tremendous; the health status of the refugees in these settings is poor due to unmet of basic services including nutrition and food security in addition to access to secondary health services especially for people with non communicable diseases, The poor living conditions, including access to safe water and proper sanitation, overcrowding, heightening the susceptibility to communicable diseases, The assessments showed that the gaps on the access to quality and lifesaving emergency health services are significant in 8 states, where refugees are living in several locations. The health services need to be augmented by support to existing health facilities to cover the needs of an increasing number of people coming to Sudan. The health sector response (HNO and Ethiopian response) has a comprehensive strategy to address the urgent and essential needsThe most common diseases amongst the refugees are Diarrheas, Acute Respiratory Infections, Malaria and Skin and eye infections, diseases related to poor access to water and sanitation, Support for diseases confirmation and public health interventions (surveillance, case management, referral of emergency cases, health education, vector control, vaccination, and inter sectorial coordination) became challenging with the steady increase of refugee’s caseload and decreased humanitarian funding for Sudan

2 - Activities/Outputs

• Procurement and distribution of emergency medicines ( for most common diseases), medical supplies including cholera kits, and laboratory reagents (PHC level) enough to cover essential needs of 650,000 people in (White Nile, South and West Kordofan, North Darfur, East Darfur, Khartoum, Kassala and Gedarif states). Rapid Diagnostic kits for malaria and trauma surgical kits • Rehabilitation of isolation centers with semi permanent (corrugated iron sheets and cement base) for emergency care of cases, fences, latrines, incinerators, shaded area for nutrition, health promotion and vaccination • Operational support for the running of 20 emergency clinics in 8 states to enable these clinics to provide free of charge emergency integrated PHC services. • Provision of medical equipment, clinic furniture, basic diagnostic tools and surgical instruments for the functioning of the clinics• Training of medical staff categories on case management, surveillance and infection prevention control.• Training of CHWs to deliver integrated health promotion messages at clinics including psycho social counseling and support on mental health • Conduct alert investigation missions, including collection of samples and their transport for confirmation, identification of source, initiation of response through outreach and active case finding• Strengthening of EWARS for weekly collection of epidemiological data and enhanced detection of communicable diseases outbreaks• Implementation of public health screening at entry points including nutritional status, vaccination status, HIV/AIDS tests• Training of community volunteers and supervisors on water sampling, protection of water sources, HH level water safe management, and integrated vector control.• Conduct water sampling for quality testing• Conduct regular vector control, with a focus before and during rainy season when the campaigns will be conducted at 2 weeks interval (WHO guidelines) when health awareness and education messages and material will be disseminated to communities• Conduct refugees SAM training for the health staff , working in the targeted TFUs to provide quality services• Joint supportive supervisions with MOH at least once every 3 months, to include on the job coaching aiming to improve adherence to treatment protocols and the quality of services. Patient satisfaction/ Exit interview to be conducted during each supervision visit so communities’ perception and needs are addressed and included into future plans.
