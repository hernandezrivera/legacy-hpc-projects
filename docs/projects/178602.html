Project Summary

In the poultry sector:
The objective for this sector is to contribute to the reactivation of poultry farms for reproduction and food security, and to support the poultry sector through the following activities:
1- SDH will support 100 poultry farms selected in each region according to specific criteria where the farmers will be responsible for raising the distributed chicks until they could financialise their farms by the chicken products.
2- Purchase (chicks - sawdust - medicine - fodder supplement - charcoal - power source (solar power)- vitamins and sugar supplements.
3- The other sub-activities will be (chicks & charcoal distribution, supervise the feeding process, monitor the overall condition of the poultry, provide healthcare for the little chicks, carry out immunization during the life of the little chicks under 6 weeks according to the vaccine program.
* A portion of the production estimated at 56,000 chicken (70 * 800) will be returned and prepared for distribution in camps for IDPs and people in need according to the selected criteria by SDH.
4- Support owners of the poultry farms by animal (feeding, breeding, diseases) training.
5- Distribute hatchers to breeders working by solar power, which have egg-laying chickens.
In the livestock sector:
The activities under this section aim to support the livestock herders, living in the North of Syria. which provides to preserve the health of livestock, increases production, and grants economic security for the working families.
1-support 500 families have livestock according to criteria.
2- SDH will provide these families with necessary supplies for the livestock, such as fodder, fodder supplements (Salt and vitamins) for three months, vaccines campaigns, and veterinary services.
Training for the herders will carry out by veterinarians; The training provides the herders with information about The care for livestock, the methods of forming the fodder mixing, and the main diseases that affected the livestock and its treatment.
In the vocational training sector:
in order to alleviate the unemployment rate, which is significantly widespread in northern Syria, and provide high-quality local products
Training (500) beneficiaries, especially the female ones, through a series of vocational courses in order to meet the local market need
In the agriculture sector:
1- Cultivation of 500 hectares of wheat, chickpeas, Bean,.….ect
2- Cultivation of 4 hectares of various vegetables (cucumber, tomatoes, okra, zucchini, pumpkin green pepper)
3- Cultivation of 150 hectares of mix-fodder for animals. The crops are alfalfa, Jalban, and Barley.
3- Increasing the agricultural experience for the BNFs and reduction of unemployment.
In the Food sector:
The objective of this sector is to provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people to alleviate suffering.
1- Distribute food basket for 50,000 families in North of Syria.
2- Distribute ready eat ratio for 50,000 families in North of Syria.
3- Distribute cooked meals for 50,000 families in North of Syria
The fund for the Project is secure for 10% and unsecured for 90% and it is 100% FSL sector.

4 - Needs

10 years of the crisis, the food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of The situation continues to deteriorate drastically with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, compared to 9.8 million in the previous year. The food insecurity PiN has risen by 45 percent in the last year.• At least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, an increase of 57 percent compared to 2020,of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure. Food prices in Syria have increased dramatically in 2020 and WFP’s national standard reference food basket price in November 2020 was 251 percent higher when compared to November 2019.The agriculture sector, which was once a cornerstone of the Syrian economy, has been severely impacted, with half (i.e. 50 percent) of national grain requirements met through imports.• According to Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO), high cost and limited availability of quality crop and livestock production inputs, damaged irrigation infrastructure across the country, degradation of livelihoods and income-earning opportunities, coupled with the direct and knock-on effects of Covid-19 and climate-induced shocks continue to drive more people into food insecurity. (2021 Needs and Response Summary)

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities are expected to cover the period till the end of December 2021. The project start date has been set taking into consideration the urgent need for support livestock and chicken herders during the winter months. During the lean period, farmers rely on animal feed for their livestock also, cultivate the multi-crops for themselves also, we will distribute food Basket for the emergency status of IDPs. All project activities will be implemented directly by SDH within the target areas and will involve and cooperate with local partners in all stages of the implementation process. A team will be assigned including a project manager and field coordinators (agricultural engineers and veterinarians) and we will work to include employees in the staff for supporting the families (most of which being female-headed). Contracts will be made with specialized instructors to provide support in training workshops. Purchases will be carried out in a transparent manner and in accordance with the procurement strategy approved by SDH and will be overseen by the relevant specialists to get the best quality. The local team will be responsible for the implementation of all phases of the project _ procurement, distribution, monitoring and evaluation, the preparation of periodic reports, and enacting any necessary updates at every stage. Trainers will be hired to add value to the planned business plans and ensure quality standards. Follow-up training will also be undertaken up to several weeks after the training to assess if beneficiaries have continued to practice their livelihood activity with good demonstrated skills. SDH will work closely with the project manager to fill the gaps of the FSA sector.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: community tension due to service delivery locations/types of services offered not consistent between communities. Mitigating Measures: Ensure local authority actively engage with communities to communicate to the local population the reasons for prioritizing the selected areas. Monitoring: SDH will continuously monitor the reaction of communities to services provided, and mitigate any disputes by reaching out through FGD's, communities and local leaders. Risk: Barriers to access for marginalized groups Mitigating measures: Specific measures are taken to ensure input flow from marginalized groups; Feedback informs planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Mitigation measures are brainstormed with the community to ensure equal access to SDH activities. Monitoring: FGDs, feedback mechanism Risk: SCI or partner staff violating SCI CSG rules harms children; damages the reputation of the organization; Mitigating measures: All SDH staff, volunteers and representatives are trained on and sign the Child Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct, as well as on reporting mechanisms; Risk: Affected populations and SC staff and volunteers, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment. Mitigating measures: Training will be provided to SDH staff and volunteers partners on SDH ’s Anti-Harassment Policy and Code of Conduct and the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through SDH ’s PSEA focal points. Overall Monitoring: As some activities harbor high risks for both beneficiaries and humanitarian workers (including risk of sexual exploitation or harassment), SDH will be trained on potential risks and how to mitigate them and provided with confidential complaint mechanisms to report

SDH field staff have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over vulnerable communities/beneficiaries that hold PSEA risks. SDH has institutionalized policies on PSEA, Whistle-blower, Code of Conduct, Gender, Safeguarding and Complaint, and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM). Proposed activities have been designed and are implemented with a standardized andcohesive approach through Standard Operational Procedures guided by the principles of “Do No Harm”, con¦dentiality, impartiality/non-discrimination, the best interest of the child. Gender, age, and diversity are sensitized Mitigating measures: 1) Effective and comprehensive mechanisms established to ensure awareness-raising on PSEA amongst personnel; continuous training on the topic provided by dedicated staff members. 2) Effective capacity building & training on GBV & PSEA principles for all staff is ensured. 3) Ensuring regular monitoring and evaluation through impartial assessments of needs and risks, vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting, and beneficiary/staff feedback mechanisms. 4) Ensuring provision of information to communities and people affected by a crisis about SDH; its principles, how it expects its staff to behave, the programs it is implementing and what they intend to deliver as well how to report concerns and complaints. 5) Effective community-based CFM, including victim assistance isestablished and monitored for all persons (gender & age).

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Stakeholders will take part in the project with all the power they have. - The security situation of the region is relatively stable. - The project steps will be implemented within the designated time frame of the project Risk: Security risk, Some diseases occur, The quality of chicks are bad, Lack of materials in the area mitigation: Provide vaccinations, health care and supervision of the poultry farms by veterinarians, We will establish the center in relatively safe areas, Permanent sterilization and no access but to specific persons, We will check them through our team of doctors, Dealing with dealers can secure these materials. Assumption: Availability of materials and livestock inputs in the area. Risk: Lack of some materials or livestock inputs in the area. mitigation: dealing with suppliers that provide and deliver livestock inputs in the target area. Assumption: Dealing transparently with the team and the beneficiaries or traders. Risk: Problems between staff and stakeholders. mitigation: Selection of trained personnel to deal with beneficiaries. Assumption: Improve the health of livestock and increase production. Risk: Death of livestock and low productivity due to disease outbreaks. mitigation: Regular vet check-ups for livestock and immediate treatment in the event of an outbreak of disease. Assumption: Cultivation of land and harvest of crops. Risk: Occurrence of land fires and theft of harvests. mitigation: Periodic visits by field coordinators and the M&E team to ensure that the process of agriculture is going well.
