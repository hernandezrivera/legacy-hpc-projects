Project Summary

Over a decade of sustained conflict and conflict-related injuries, exposure to explosive ordnance and psychological harm, malnutrition, and disruptions to prenatal care and early childhood development have led to very high incidence of impairment and disability in northwest Syria (NWS). According to the 2023 Protection Analysis Update (PAU) conducted through the eviction monitoring tool run in 16 Reception Centers, the most affected population groups, such as pregnant and lactating women (88%), destitute older persons (75%), widows (50%), unaccompanied and separated children (44%) and person with disabilities (44%) where also identified as those most exposed to at heightened risk . According to a survey conducted by the Protection Monitoring Analysis Working Group in NWS in July 2023, which focused on the impact of escalation in hostilities, the deterioration of the security situation and other challenges faced related to protection and recovery, the most significant protection concerns for children during displacement were: violence against children (48%); children showing signs of psychological distress (44%); and concerns regarding Gender-Based Violence (GBV) during displacement. The survey indicated that 82% of respondents were concerned about the heightened risk of labor exploitation faced by women and girls, including those with disabilities. The potential increase in domestic violence in camps or reception centers was a serious concern raised by 68% of respondents. The risk of early and forced marriage was a pressing concern during displacement, with 64% of respondents expressing worry. Housing, land and property (HLP) and civil status documentation (CSD) issues remain key challenges, compounded by the lack of a recognized legal framework across NWS. In fact, one of the most significant consequences of the February 2023 earthquakes was the loss of personal documentation which resulted in obstacles to accessing humanitarian aid, reception centers, etc. In the NWS context, characterized by the lack of the rule of law, loss of documentation exponentially exacerbates existing protection risks. In the Protection Cluster’s report on escalation of confrontations, 53% of the KIs stated that losing their documents had been one of the most significant impacts of forced displacement . Referring to the Northwest Syria PAU 2023, protracted conflict and displacement have rendered NWS particularly susceptible to the perils of Trafficking in Persons (TiP) especially in informal settlements inhabited by internally displaced men , women, and children. Alarmingly, these risks remain among the most overlooked, understudied, and inadequately addressed in NWS.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

IOM will continue to provide and expand its provision of integrated protection services to conflict-affected populations across NWS through various interventions including: • Establishment and strengthening of protection, adolescent/ youth groups and child protection committees for identifying and addressing protection issues within their localities. • Carry out safety Audits to identify emerging Child Protection and GBV risks • Designate and train protection focal points within the community to act as first responders and refer individuals to appropriate services. • Provision of structured and unstructured Psychosocial Support (PSS) activities • Develop awareness raising activities to address protection risks (including GBV, CP, MA, HLP/CD, legal, Peacebuilding) • Build capacity of local partners to promote ownership and sustainability of the response to protection needs. • Support with cash for community-based structures and initiatives • Conduct parenting programs to strengthen the caregiving environment for children. • Provision of CP and GBV prevention and response services including case management • Referrals to specialized/non-specialized response services • Individual legal sessions including legal assistance on eviction alternatives, mediations, etc. • Provision of cash and in-kind assistance for GBV, CP and legal case management services • HLP Documents Safeguarded: the legal advisors will raise awareness on the importance of HLP documents safeguarding to prove ownership and assist the affected people claim their rights in future as a step towards transitional justice. • HLP Due Diligence for XB sectors to verify property rights, prevent conflicts, and implement safer programs aimed at enhancing the protection and well-being of affected populations. • Conduct initiatives to enhance rule of law and transitional justice• Conduct protection monitoring (on-site and mobile teams); Overall, in 2024 IOM will directly support 40,000 beneficiaries (9,049 men, 8,951 women, 11,059 boys and 10,941 girls) across NWS Syria through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions, targeting the most vulnerable including unaccompanied and separated children, persons with disabilities and the elderly.
