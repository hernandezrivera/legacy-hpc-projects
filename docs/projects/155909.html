Project Summary

The project will provide life-saving humanitarian food assistance to 1500 HHs (1050 IDPs/450 Host), through a restricted voucher modality (E-voucher) with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food by a monthly 50$ which will last for 6 months with the total of 300$ for each HH.
In coordination with GBV protection sub-cluster, GAN will allocate 1% of the project beneficiaries for the survivors, also GAN will target the beneficiaries of the livelihood program to alleviate the burden and get more benefit from the programs in coordination with the FSL and ER clusters.
And will provide livestock/poultry to 800 HH (560 IDPs / 240 host) in order to avoid any internal conflict and conflict between IDPs and host communities.
The project will continue for 5 months.
Each head of HH will receive a certain number of livestock or poultry in addition to orientation sessions during the first preparatory month.
The beneficiaries will be divided as follows:
200 families will benefit from sheep (2 sheep - Provided you are pregnant)
200 families will benefit from goats (2 goats - Provided you are pregnant)
400 families will benefit from the chicken (9 chicken and one rooster)
The quantities of feed will be distributed starting from the second month and in three batches, as follows:
150 kg per month for beneficiaries of sheep and goats
100 kg per month for beneficiaries of chickens
In addition to the follow-up service by a veterinarian during the project period.
The project aims to provide the support for the rural areas in Idleb and Aleppo, the goats distribution will be prioritized for those who have previous experience in livestock breeding and lost their goats during the crisis whereas the distribution of the chicken will be prioritized for those who have previous experience in poultry and lost their chickens during the crisis.

Needs

An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies.Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations.With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines , the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary thus demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria. Estimates of 478,283 people living in last resort camps in the North West and North East Syria who have very limited opportunity to access a diversified diet; and is expected to be at a higher degree of food insecurity.The IDPs and returnees who are at a greater degree of vulnerability. By residence status from the assessed households’ 33.4 percent IDPs and 41.2 percent of returnees are food insecure. Additionally, 29 percent of returnees are adopting emergency coping strategy as compared to 18.7 percent reported across all groups at national level. The households headed by Persons with Disabilities and the older people are at a greater degree of vulnerability due to the stress on their capacities to earn their living and afford their basic needs.200,000 breeders’ households are suffering from the loss of productive assets (livestock) and damaged infrastructure (land, irrigation infrastructure, facilities, etc.) which is affecting both farmers and breeders. (HNO2019)In the Afrin area, 76,491 families are in desperate need of food assistance, of which 7.8% are female-headed households, in the northern governorate of Idlib, there are 122,494 families in need of food assistance, of which 13.7% are female-headed households (FSa and FSLa 2018 - IMMAP)Throughout the conducted assessments by GAN in 2018 in Idlib and Aleppo, the provinces are in extremely needs of the cultivation and livestock programs due to the nature of the ground and the recent IDPs movement to the provinces form rural areas likes (Rural Damascus, Homs, Eastern Idleb and north of Hama)

Implementation of activities

1- Monthly food assistance for the assessed food insecure people:(In Afrin and Jandairis)The project will provide life-saving humanitarian food assistance to 1500 HHs (1050 IDPs/450 Host) HHs in order to avoid any bias and internal conflict between IDPs and the host communities.The project will last for 8 months through a restricted voucher modality (e-voucher) with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food by a monthly 50$ which will last for 6 months with the total of 300$ for each HH.Each HH head will receive an e-voucher card to with guidance package (includes the assistance terms, list of vendors, list of items, instructions, and voucher NFC card) and orientation sessions during the first preparatory month.The HH head will be able to use the e-voucher card immediately after the training sessions at the beginning of the second month.Market assessment, selecting beneficiaries, contracting vendors, contracting the e-voucher service provider, recruiting M&E officers, training the vendors and beneficiaries on the cards and distribution of terminal POS to vendors will happen together during the first month.Starting from the second month, the beneficiary will be able to buy with 50$ his/her needs among 40 specified spices with restricted amounts that are allowed in the e-voucher card to fulfill the calorie standard set by the WFP.Compared to other alternatives of disbursing cash, including paper vouchers, GAN chose to implement through e-voucher system based on many reasons, such as saving the dignity of beneficiaries and creates a safe space for IDPs to be engaged with the host communities,as to prevent of food basket selling which is frequently happened. the e-voucher system increases the variety of food spices which has a good health and nutrition impact on beneficiaries for example they will be able to buy fresh meat, chicken, and vegetables beside what they need of groceries, at the same time the system will prevent buying not allowable items such as cigarettes. However, GAN conducted a feasibility study which will be externally attached under the name (Comparison between Food basket and e-voucher). the preparation time for this process will be as or less than the food baskets preparation time as for our past experience.M&E plan will be conducted during the project with PDM and a clear complaint mechanism for beneficiaries against vendors and employees.2- Livestock and animal feed distribution: (In rural areas of rural Aleppo and rural Idlib)The project will provide livestock/poultry to 800 HH (560 IDPs/240 host) in order to avoid any internal conflict and conflict between IDPs and host communities.The project will continue for 5 months.Each head of HH will receive a certain number of livestock or poultry in addition to orientation sessions during the first preparatory month.The beneficiaries will be divided as follows:200 families will benefit from sheep (2 sheep-Provided you are pregnant)200 families will benefit from goats (2 goats-Provided you are pregnant)400 families will benefit from the chicken (9 chicken and 1 rooster)Market assessment, beneficiary selection, contract vendors, M&E staff, and beneficiary training will occur together during the first month.The quantities of feed will be distributed starting from the second month and in three batches, as follows:150 kg per month for beneficiaries of sheep and goats100 kg per month for beneficiaries of chickensIn addition to the follow-up service by a veterinarian during the project period.The M&E plan will be implemented through the project with the PDM and a clear complaint mechanism for beneficiaries and employees.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:-Security situation does not deteriorate. -Adequate presence of vendors to support the voucher program.-Stable food prices in the targeted locations.-Project is accepted in the targeted locations Risks:- Target locations are inaccessible for our staff due to security incidents; if this happens, we will monitor the security situation on a day to day. Should the situation not improve, we will work to revise our action plan.- Communities and key stakeholders reject our project/plan; if this were to occur, begin the process of selecting new target locations.- Local markets cannot provide supplies/services/labor/goods; we will continually assess the market, and if an incident occurs that causes a sudden shift in the market (non-availability, increased cost), consider changing the locations to where markets and vendor with sufficient capacity are available.- Some local authorities may have restrictions on using e-vouchers. GAN has already activities in the targeted locations with no objections from the local authorities to use this system. If this were to occur, as a mitigation measure, we will use paper-based voucher system that can replace the e-voucher system or change the location based on the situation.
