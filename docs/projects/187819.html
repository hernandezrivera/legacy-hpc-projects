Project Summary


HIA will continue using the Rapid Response Teams (RRT) approach to increase equitable access to comprehensive nutrition services. Each RRT will consist of two Community Health Worker (CHWs), Data entry, nurse and one nutritionist in line with Standards Operating Procedure (SOP) circulated by nutrition cluster. As such, HIA will continue support ten RRTs in line with nutrition cluster approach and two primary health care centers in NWS, Idleb governorate, covering the following Sub-districts: Qourqeena, MaaretTamsrin, Armanaz and Bennsh. These ten RRTs will be covering the following communities: Torlaha, Khirbet al Kahtib,Koknaya, Sardin, Rabeeta, Htan , Qalb Loze, Baliya, Dier Seeta, Abarita, Toum, Ram Hamdan, Habat, Boayba. These communities have 81,605 toal population. There is constrained access to nutrition services in the targeted communities. Moreover, the areas are highly populated with Internally Displaced Persons (IDP)s and considered as underserved.
The following programme will be implemented under the Rapid Response Team modality.
The RRT will conduct Households visits on daily basis targeting 30-40 PLW and CU5 in average to implement the agreed program mentioned in RRT SOP. The RRT will be in close contact with HIA field team and supply chain central team to ensure that the distribution of nutrition supplies will be monitored on regular basis and the reporting process will be done through supply management monitoring tool in HIA.
Community based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) Community based screening treatment and referral of SAM and MAM children, pregnant and lactating women to the respective treatment facilities. The RRT will implement the simplified protocol modified by nutrition cluster COVID 19 Task Force in addition to scaling up scaling up Family MUAC approach at household level within COVID 19 Pandemic
Promotion of optimal Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E): targeted one to one counselling on the individualized IYCF subjects and dissemination of IYCF-E messages in the focus groups will be undertaken The RRTs will be integrated with health services by conducting awareness sessions in the health facilities and for those beneficiaries who are seeking the primary health care services along with referring the complicated cases to the health facilities.
Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMNCI): detection of the danger signs in children under five, provision of the first aid and referral to the hospital system.
Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care(bEmONC: Provision of Basic Antenatal and Post-natal Care services for all pregnant women as agreed by RH Technical working group and RRT TWG and mentioned in RRT TOR.

Child Protection: identification of children, pregnant and lactating women with the visual signs of abuse and violence, first physiological aid, referral or connecting with the respective PSS services.

4 - Needs

actually, Poor infant feeding and caring practices will adversely deteriorate the levels of the already precarious acute and chronic malnutrition among children aged below 5 years in Syria. Similar effect will be potentiated by maternal anemia and malnutrition. Therefore, the already low levels of poor infant feeding and caring practices will continue to be evident if relevant response actions are not implemented at scale.The results from the most recent SMART survey report done in Aug-2021 indicated that the prevalence of moderate stunting (<-2 z-score) among children aged 6-59 months was 13.1 % whilst prevalence of severe stunting (<-3 z-score) was 6.6 % . And Prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) based on the percentage of the median and/or oedema was 1.4 %. According to the last surveillance community report in 2021, the GAM prevalence in Harim District is 2% and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) among pregnant and lactating women ( PLW) is 13.75 % Idleb district has MAM prevalence rates of 13.5 % among PLWs. All this point to a deteriorating nutrition situation compared to previous years. The uptake of optimal Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices was also found to be sub-optimal. For instance, the exclusive breastfeeding rate was found at 41.4 % whilst the Minimum acceptable diet (MAD) among children aged 6-24 months was 39.7 %. Poor uptake of optimal IYCF practices is a major driver of malnutrition in NW Syria
