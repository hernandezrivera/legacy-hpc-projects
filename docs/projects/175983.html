Project Summary

The population affected by Conflict, acute watery Diarrheoa, Malnutrition, Food insecurity & hunger, flooding and COVID-19 pandemic in Counties of Yirol East and Yirol West in Lake State, Lainya, and Terekeka counties in Central Equatoria in South Sudan ,have improved access and utilization to Safe Water, Sanitations and Hygiene services, Mitigate GBV risks as well as enhanced WASH resilience.
VISH will achieve this WASH interventions through:- Identification beneficiaries, mapping and rehabilitations of broken hand pumps and shallow wells , constructions of new water points in communities with critical Water need, water quality testing and monitoring, constructions of community emergency latrines, and contractions of institutional latrines at nutrition site , Provision WASH supplies( Soap, buckets & Jerrycan, chlorine tablets, Dust bins, hand washing containers, Hand sanitizer) to SAM/MAM care taker and PLWs, Trainings ( Hand Pump Mechanics , Water User committees,Hgyiene promotion volunteers, Nutrition Assistants and volunteers), and Hygiene promotions ( dissemination of IEC materials on COVID-19 and Hygiene practices, radio spot and talks shows, hand washing, Hygiene campaign and sessions , Events ( World Water and Toilet Day, Hygiene weeks)
Therefore ,VISH requesting for $ 500, 000 from South Sudan Humanitarian Fund to scale up WASH interventions for 32,687 People ( 5448 HHs) projected to be in IPC 3 & 4 in the 4 Counties mentioned above inorder to improve access and Utilization of WASH services to prevent the spread of acute Water Diarrheal Diseases , COVID-19 pandemic , and reduce malnutrition , food insecurity and hunger to the vulnerable Internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, refugees and host communities in South Sudan

1 - Needs

The living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people and 310,000 refugees and Asylum seekers will be will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in South Sudan in 2021 (HNO South Sudan ,2021). Only 36 per cent of households have access for improved water source and could only spend 30 minutes in fetching water without any protection concerns while 64 % of people either rely on unimproved or surface water sources in South Sudan. Also, the quantity of water available for use per person per day is below sphered standards in many locations in South Sudan, with 17 % of people unable to collect enough water for drinking and 39 per cent of people reporting insufficient access to water for handwashing. Similarly access to sanitation remained low in South Sudan, with 77 % houses holds without access to latrine, with only 17 % of households owning a latrine in their compound, 4 % shares latrine, and 2 % use a communal/institutional latrine and also with over 73% of the households practicing open defecation. WASH coverage in the 4 proposed counties, Lakes State (Yirol East and Yirol West), and Central Equatoria (Terekeka, and Lainya, ) are equally critical low.The alarming WASH situation in South Sudan is attributed to the prolonged conflict, economic instability, displacement, widespread flooding, outbreak of COVID -19 pandemic, breakage of WASH infrastructures, population pressure on existing WASH infrastructures and weak Government structures. Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions across South Sudan and contributing to the highest child mortality rates in the. It is also Predicted to push an estimated 1.4 million children under age 5 and 483000 pregnant or Breast-feeding women to acute malnutrition in 2021 (IPC report, 2020). In 2020 Some 70 per cent of household’s report members affected by a water or vector-borne disease. Lack of access to water has led to tension and conflict between communities over competition of already scarce resources. Furthermore, COVID-19 are anticipated to persist and, Women and girls face will continue to have increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence while collecting water far from their homes. Women, children and people living with disabilities to risk of violence and discrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements due to the open defecations.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

VISH will ensure through the, project inceptions and review meetings, trainings and complaint feedback mechanisms, relevant information on protection against sexual exploitation is disseminated to the beneficiaries to ensure sexual exploitation and abuse is reported timely. County and Payam leaders, beneficiaries, Boma chief and staff will also be trained on PSEA guideline to be able to identify and report sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by any staff, authority or partners. Additionally, VISH will roll out its whistle blower policy or guidelines, conflict of interest policy and ensure all staff members and its service providers sign and adhere to the organization code of conduct. Hence, no tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

VISH will establish and strengthen a transparent complaint and feedback mechanisms in all project sites. Through the community project committees, the most vulnerable persons(individual living with disabilities, not literate, women and girls) will be prioritized. The feedback platforms will promote beneficiaries to share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. During community engagement, mobilization and sensitization, any feedback shall be transmitted to the vulnerable person. The project committees will be empowered to monitor, report and represent the needs of the vulnerable persons and will be facilitated to meaningfully participate in coordination mechanisms with humanitarian agencies and other. VISH will promote harmony among the beneficiaries and services will be provided to every beneficiary regardless of status (physical state, race, tribe and ethnic dimensions). Further, the beneficiary satisfaction survey will be conducted which allow for in-depth assessment of how services are benefiting the targeted beneficiaries

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information’s related to project will be shared by VISH with the beneficiaries during project inceptions meeting, project launch, and project review meetings and training. However, VISH will ensure two-way transparent feedback and/or complaint mechanism system for the beneficiaries to share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. VISH will also continue to strengthen the current complaint feedback mechanisms desk to receive feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries about the services, and the feedback and comments of the beneficiaries shall be analyzed and used for decision making, on community engagement, mobilization and sensitization , information sharing to increase service uptake, .

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

VISH will ensure beneficiaries are involved right from the start of the project in planning, during implementation and monitoring. A transparent feedback mechanism system will be established and strengthened in all 4 counties, so that beneficiaries can share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. The complaint feedback mechanisms/desk established on each project site will receive feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries about the services, and the feedback and comments of the beneficiaries shall be collected, analyzed, and used for decision making to improve or modify the strategy when appropriate. During Community engagement, mobilization and sensitization, any feedback shall be transmitted to the beneficiaries. The community project management committees, county RRC focal point person, water use committees, Hygiene promoters, hand pump mechanics will be empowered to monitor, report and represent the needs of the beneficiaries and will be facilitated to meaningfully participate in coordination mechanisms with humanitarian agencies and other stakeholders. Further, the beneficiary satisfaction survey/assessment will be conducted in all selected VISH project site and finding will be used to improve the service delivery.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

VISH recognize that, the quality, timely and inclusive protection services are critical in effective humanitarian response, and will therefore map all the protections partners and available referral pathways, which includes medical care, legal support, mental Health psychosocial support, where women and GBV survivor seek for support for their project related concerns.VISH will also train its staff on protection mainstreaming and ensure incorporation of protection measures within its project activities. This include facilitating the distribution during the early hours of the day to ensure household can return home safely and project venues are accessible to all people despite gender, age and diversity (e.g., ethnic groups, political or religious beliefs). All form of sensitization of beneficiaries will include protection messaging, e.g., around decision making, gender and child protection. Selection of beneficiaries from different communities will also be considered to reduce tensions and conflicts and complaints and feedback mechanisms entail a referral mechanism should any protection-related feedback need the involvement of protection actors for follow up

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

VISH will carry out sensitization campaigns to inform targeted communities and its leaders of the objectives, agreed upon selection criteria, feedback mechanisms, and process of verification and distribution. Targeted beneficiaries will be amongst the most vulnerable households and hardest to reach populations. The selection criteria will take into account the following vulnerable groups: • newly arrived IDPs with no assets; • child-headed households• families who have lost assets and food during fighting or flooding • female-headed households (with additional vulnerabilities)• households hosting several IDPs. • Families with malnourished children under five years• Caregivers• Unaccompanied or separated children• families with persons with disabilities• older persons at risk and persons living with a serious medical condition
