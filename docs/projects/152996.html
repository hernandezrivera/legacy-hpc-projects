Project Summary

The project will: -
1. Improve access to integrated emergency health and shall focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality affecting the most vulnerable communities.
2. Detect, prevent and respond to epidemic diseases outbreaks through multidisciplinary program.
3. Promote healthcare services integration with Nutrition, WASH and Protection especially for GBV to enhance intervention effectiveness and its impact on the most vulnerable.
4. The project will integrate the ongoing development focused intervention to narrow the gaps in health care provision and to ensure outbreaks and other emergencies impacting on health to be timely and effectively prevented, addressed and contained

Needs

Since the onset of the civil war in South Sudan in December 2013, Jonglei has been one of South Sudan’s most conflict-affected states. Nearly five years of continual national-level war, combined with long term structural-level communal violence, has led to generational spans of personal insecurity. Compounding this situation, Jonglei also suffers from one of the highest level’s health related in the country, with a majority of its geography located within high-risk diseases outbreak. Stretching east of the Nile River and south of the Sobat River and Pibor river Corridor, Jonglei’s vast plains are highly prone to flooding in the wet season, drought in the dry season. As such, Jonglei maintains one of the highest proportions of population suffering from diseases in South Sudan. Continual conflict and structural food insecurity create prerequisite conditions for widespread population displacement. At present, Jonglei hosts the second highest amount of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the country, at an estimated 363,399 people (OCHA, September 2017 - 2018)2. Akobo, Uror and Nyirol Counties, located in northeastern Jonglei State alongside the southern bank of the Sobat River and near the border of Upper Nile State, was last directly impacted by conflict in April 2018. Clashes between opposing parties on waat payam, resulted in population displacements towards Wech Jal and Kaikuiny Payam where a majority of population respondents still reside. However, conflict in neighboring counties earlier in the year, ongoing conflict within Akobo, Uror and Nyirol today, and the possibility of continued conflict in the surrounding areas, means that the displacement dynamics in and around areas will remain in flux in the near future, with a strong potential for secondary and tertiary population displacements. Population profile:The majority of households encountered composed of women, children and elderly persons. Eighty-nine per cent of IDPs originated from Uror and Nyirol County itself. Origin is commonly defined as where parents are from and can differ from main the location of habitual residence. Most IDPs cited Nyirol as pre-displacement county of residence the sudden nature of the displacement left many families separated. :The main health concerns in Akobo, Uror, Nyirol were reported to be Malaria, diarrheal diseases, pregnancy related conditions, TB and Cholera and its thought to be induced by rampant displacement.

Emergency health protection for people with disabilities
