Project Summary

Awerial is a County in Lakes State of the Republic of South Sudan. It is composed of eight Payams namely: Abuyung, Bun-Agok, Dor, Magok, Nile, Puluk, Alel I and Alel II. It is bordering Yirol to the West, Tali to the South-West, Terekaka to the South and Jonglei State to the East. Awerial and Jonglei has been divided by River Nile and the Great Sudd swamps with rich aquamarine. Awerial covers an area of approximately 4,659.10 sq. km square kilometers and has consists of Two-counties: Awerial North and South Counties with a population of 47,041, of which 23,299 are male and 23,742 are female. It is inhabited by the Dinka (Jieng) Aliap, currently more vulnerable to lack of safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene services, diseases, or even consequently death. There are IDPs and Host Community with inadequate WASH facilities. Similarly, there is food shortage, shelters, NFIs and other early recovery livelihood programmes.
The main core of this intervention that the Project seeks to address is the inadequate WASH facilities and health outcomes currently faced by target populations in Awerial South County of Lakes State due to inadequate and unsustainable water, sanitation and hygiene facilities and practices. Most of the water points available in the target areas is collected from surface run off, River Nile and is at extremely high risk of being contaminated and/or polluted, making it of very poor quality for human consumption. This is exacerbated by unsafe water handling practice along the water chain – from source to consumption - with water carrying and storage equipment (predominantly 20 litre jerry cans) not properly cleaned and no clear separation of water for animal and human consumption at household level and limited water treatment before consumption. Within the target community, there appears to be limited awareness and appreciation of health risks associated with drinking unclean water without first treating it. From a social/public health perspective continued poor hygiene practice can leave user populations at great risk from, and contribute to the spread of water borne diseases most predominantly diarrhea. The target is to addressing the high incidences of vulnerability and the limited access to safe drinking water and sanitary services to 132,875 people in needs.

Plan fields

1 - Needs

1-Unsafe drinking water: People in Awerial south county have no access to safe potable drinking water and use the unprotected water sources.2-Poor sanitation: People in Awerial south county have no access to hygienic latrines and open defecation was observed by the assessment team as common exterta disposal practices.3-Poor hygiene: Overall the education level revealed to be lower among these communities of Awerial South County. Many families lack Hygiene Kits. Hygiene promotion activities are carried out only in planned settlements and the rest areas (mostly scattered) are left out.4-Inadequate latrines: Open defecation practice has been noticed as one of the very common phenomena particularly among host community and IDPs who live with the host communities inside the villages. Most families lack latrines for defecation. 5- Poor hand washing practices: Many people are not using soaps as safe hand washing practice at the critical times. During the assessment, the teams visited randomly few schools in every payam and none of them were found to having hand washing facilities as well as the knowledge identified limited about safe hand washing practices and majority of them were not washing their hand during critical times6- Inadequate WASH facilities: There are few clean water points and boreholes in Awerial South County. Inadequate potable safe drinking water points, unsafe hygiene and sanitation behavior and practices, prevalent open defecation practices, and poor personal and environmental hygiene and sanitation conditions are some of the serious WASH challenges for the IDPs and host communities

2 - Activities

1-Sanitation and hygiene promotion; Hygiene Promoters (HPs) and health extension workers (HEWs) will be mentored through training to equip them with knowledge and skill of hygiene and sanitation. Hygiene and sanitation education will be given at religious institutions, community social occasions and house to house visit. In each Boma, health clubs will be established comprising of both men and women; trained and will exercise the hygienic practices and utilization of sanitation facilities effectively.2-Drilling and maintenance of Boreholes: WECSS will drill and repair boreholes in order to increase access to clean water points. Animals drinking points will be separate. This is to avoid contamination of water points.3- Capacity building: Training will be organize for the water and sanitation committees, pump mechanics, care takers and village hygiene communicators by identifying the critical gaps in operations, management, and hygiene and sanitation promotion. Joint monitoring of water sources at the spot level including the beneficiaries, and other stakeholders will be conducted. Through these women, men, boys and girls will be empowered for long lasting utilization of water points through ensuring environmental sustainability.Construction of latrines: The presence of a sanitary latrine should promote good hygiene behaviour. WECSS will promote construction of sanitary Latrines to avoid risk of cholera and other diseases.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, the beneﬁciaries' rights will be legally recognized. It is the main driving force in this project design and implementation plan. To reduce existing gender disparity, harmful traditional practices and protect the environment from degradation, due attention will be given in awareness raising. All beneficiaries including minority and vulnerable groups will participate in planning, implementation and evaluation in water, hygiene and sanitation throughout the project timeline. Women will be encouraged to actively get involved in the committees and village hygiene promoters. Their needs and expectations are keys during the project implementation.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, the project is designed to beneﬁt both the IDPs, Returnees and Host communities. The project will improve the health status, reduce women workload and provide time to get engaged in agricultural and other income generating activities. It will also reduces the medication costs. In general, it will increase the overall production and productivity of the target populations. The projects required to maintain the desired social values, traditions, institutions and other social characteristics. The Water User Committees, Caretakers and Hygiene Promoters will be elected by the community democratically using acceptable social norms. And the training of members will help the beneficiaries to improve their knowledge and develop rules like: setting water fetching time and repair fee for the proper management of water points based on the culture and values of the community.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project is linked to community-led structures and mechanisms. Community based approach will be adopted as the most effective means of empowering communities to manage their own water sources and sanitation facilities. Active participation of County stakeholders and the beneficiary communities will be ensured from the very beginning and at every stage of the project cycle. The beneficiaries are the main actors of the project in implementation, monitoring and evaluation and managing of water sources and their sanitation facilities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with national objectives that ensure social protection for all vulnerable groups have access to Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). The project is aligned the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 6) that envisions universal, sustainable, and equitable access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene, as well as the elimination of open defecation by 2030. This goal is aligned with national objectives of promoting sanitation and hygiene, clean water access for all and clean environment.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is suﬃciently safe/stable. WECSS will use a community- based and participatory approach by linking the following to long-term durable solutions efforts;1-Form Water User Committees (WUC); The Water User Committees will ensure that even after WECSS leave the area, the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene standards are maintained. Each Water User Committee will consists of up to ten members with at least half being females so as to encourage gender balance. They will be given refresher training and support them with caretakers kits. They will be tasked with powers to let the community collect user fees for borehole maintenance and fencing of water point by community around water source.2-Monthly meetings by trained Water User Committees (WUCs): WECSS will ensure that these committees conduct monthly meetings to review Sanitation and hygiene at water points and sanitary facilities, share minutes of the meetings with community and disseminate information on water use, sanitation and community overall hygiene. 3-Form Community Hand Pump Mechanics (CHPM): These will be tasked with maintenance and repair of small damage to boreholes in the communities.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the continuity of the project after it completion period of implementation is important. All projects are designed to have long lasting positive impacts and changes in the life of the community. Before the implementation of project, WECSS will organised consultative community meetings, enlighten the community about the project benefits. Community contribution and sense of ownership will be enhanced for the sustainability of projects during execution of project at various stages. The trained WASH Committees, village hygiene Promoters and care takers groups will have full authority in order to operate, manage and promote clean water supply, hygiene and sanitation facilities after completion of all planned project activities; such as construction of standard water points and sanitation facilities.
