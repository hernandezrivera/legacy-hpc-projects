Project Summary

Access to safe and sufficient water is below expected levels thus the immense need for drilling and rehabilitation of new additional hand pumps that are aimed to reduce the risk of looming water-borne diseases like cholera outbreak among the prone disease outbreak areas in Greater Yirol and Awerial counties amongst others in former Lakes State. Therefore coordinated distribution of water treatment supplements and the training of households on safe water chain to ensure safe water consumption will further reduce the risk of waterborne disease outbreaks in key selected counties that has a history of a range of public health hazards/diseases. Through recruitment of local volunteers, training and creation of hygiene awareness messages and sessions will ensure increased practices of appropriate hygiene behaviours. Additionally, support of sanitation activities both at household and health institution level will ensure proper safe disposal of human excreta and reduce health risks linked to open defecation and disease outbreaks i.e. cholera. Through this project, Help e.V intends to maintain its humanitarian response team on the ground which will continue providing a complete package of WASH services to; girls, boys, women and men affected by WASH emergencies from both IDPs and host communities in Awerial/Mingkamman and Yirol. Below are some of the key planned activities:
• Drill new boreholes on need-basis
• Repair and rehabilitate existing WASH assets (broken boreholes/water-yards) at the state level.
• Train water user committees, pump mechanics and equip them with toolkits for Village-level operation and maintenance (VLOM).
• WASH NFI distribution to targeted beneficiaries including the provision of WASH NFI protection kits to OTP/TSFP
• Address the Menstrual Hygiene Management gaps in schools and community: Sensitize and advocate and create national awareness for Safe, dignified and inclusive MHM in the country.
• Construction of gender-segregated and inclusive WASH facilities in institutions and the community.
• Create forums and innovative mechanisms for peace reconsolidations and co-existence within the waring communities in the state/region.

1 - Needs

Greater Yirol and Awerial continue to be amongst the marginalized region where fewer coverages are seen by humanitarian actors in terms of responding to existing and increasing Humanitarian needs. Approx. 250,000 people in Greater Yirol including Awerial are in urgent need water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) support. Approx 80,000 IDPs in Mingkamman are also in critical need of WASH services since the pull out of many humanitarian actors in Awerial. The needs are closely related to the conflict, internal displacement and the economic downturn, which have all put a further strain on the limited existing WASH services in the region. It’s estimated that approx. 80% of the targeted population do not have access to improved sanitation. Half of the population does not have access to improved water sources, with about 35-40% of the existing boreholes estimated to be non-functional. In some of the recent past countrywide estimations, the average waiting time at water points is 20 to 30 minutes moreover, walking time to and from water source is 45 minutes to 1 hour in many of remote locations and in some areas of Yirol East IDPs walk up to 1hour or more to access water, exposing many girls and women to SGBV related violence. Average daily water consumptions per person are 8-13 litres which are below the desired standard. There is a shortage of water containers.Open defecation is persistent in approx. 60% of settlements in the counties. Only approx. 35% of the population has access to a borehole within 30 minutes’ walk. Some references indicate that in Greater Yirol, the percentage of people defecating in the open is above 60%, but 30% have pit latrines, while the other 10 % use public latrines either in the markets, school, church or health facility. Open defecation is seen along the roads, footpaths and near the fields. General hygiene condition of the population and especially children is alarming.Due to budgetary constraints by few actors in Awerial and Yirol, there are insufficient hygiene promoters in the existing remote communities to cover key affected populations in need of hygiene messaging and community mobilization. Communities’ access to safe and sufficient water is below expected levels thus the immense need for installation of additional water points and rehabilitation of existing but broken water points to help reduce the risk of looming water-borne diseases like cholera outbreaks (as seen in the recent past) among the prone disease outbreak areas in the region. Despite efforts by few existing actors/partners, there still exist major gaps in standard accessibility to general WASH infrastructure in these communities, coupled with poor handwashing practices where soap or alternatives are not commonly used in larger portions of these communities due to lack of related WASH knowledge and financial constraints. In Yirol West, for example, it is reported that only 9.7% of the population has access to safe drinking water and only 34.1% take additional measures to clean their water collection equipment.

2 - Activities

1. Borehole drilling & pump Installation: Drill and install boreholes with Handpumps in assessed and selected location as per sphere standards in Greater Yirol where the needs have been established to be huge due to the vast population distribution across Yirol East and West in including Awerial in general.2. Borehole repairs and rehabilitations: The trained mechanics will liaise closely with the established WUCs at community levels while collaborating with relevant county water departments/selected communal leaders to identify broken pumps and follow-up on timely maintenances/repairs thereafter. 3. Conduct Pump Mechanic Training: Newly installed boreholes and existing boreholes that were drilled a couple of years back all need a new set of pump mechanics and technicians to maintain and service the boreholes to ensure the communities continue to benefit from clean water. The training will be conducted in collaboration with the County office for the Ministry of water resources and infrastructure. 4. Household Latrine Construction and decommissioning of filled up latrines: Household latrines will be constructed in the existing IDP settlement in Mingkamman and the host communities in Greater Yirol applying CLTS approach and the by use of locally available materials. Initially commissioned latrines will be decommissioned when filed up at different intervals. 5. Construction of drainable Latrines in selected locations: Construct various blocks of drainable latrines in selected institutions i.e. Primary Health Centres, markets or schools.6. Train local Hygiene Promoters at village level: The Hygiene Promotion technical will lead in the training of additional working groups of hygiene promoters in various cycles to enable an increased coverage of messaging and information sharing on hygiene practices in both the IDP and host communities so that messages reach as many persons in the targeted communities. 7. Water User Committee Training: Train additional Water User Committee (WUC) members on maintenance and management of water points, maintaining of hygiene standards around water installations and take lead in spearheading on cross-cutting issues; gender, conflict resolution at water points.8. Distribute WASH NFIs: Distribution of Hygiene NFIs (buckets and Jericans) to households to enable target families to store water and reduce the frequency of times women and girls transverse into long distances in search of clean water.9. Messaging & IEC Activities: Intensify hygiene Promotion and sensitization activities in the targeted communities by trained & qualified Hygiene promotors with direct support form WASH technical leads through campaigns and triggering of Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) approach to raising best hygiene practices and communities’ full participation.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Before implementation attempts, as an organizational culture; we aim at adequate consultations and involvement of all relevant stakeholders; including target populations and various categories including women, youths, old people national local authorities, other humanitarian actors, relevant sector departments and ministries. Given that Help has been active in supporting the population in the selected locations, there already exists a high level of acceptance by all stakeholders including the target communities/groups and thus Help aims at keeping and maintaining this attribute. Additionally, Given the nature of climatic conditions in South Sudan, Help e.V strives at reaching high targets in the dry season in all hard to reach location then minimize movements when the selected locations are inaccessible due to flooding in the wet season. Additionally, has invested inadequate means of mobility by ensuring adequate availability of appropriate vehicles and qualified drivers which ensures personnel shall be safely and timely moved and humanitarian supplies be shipped to remote locations with minimal impediments in terms of movement and access.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

•By regular field monitoring and follow up visits to the field and meeting with communities by the senior management team from country office for direct engagement with targeted communities where feedback is sort on the ongoing activities and how they evaluate their support from the organization. •From the experience with such remote communities, the community members tend to be confident in providing sincere and critical feedback to the visiting senior management teams than to the routine field staff who are in the communities on regular basis providing regular services. Thus regular spot-checks and field support visits by senior management (SMT) are key to standard compliance ensuring quality service provision to the target populations.•Via RRC representatives at the community levels who carry out separate monitoring visits to the target communities to ensure that the communities receive quality services as stipulated by the given humanitarian standards including sphere standards guidelines. RRC in-turn will provide structured feedback to the Help e.V management for relevant actions •Due to very low literacy levels in the target communities, focus group discussions will be formed and hold regular sessions every three months in selected locations to which will enable communities to share their feedback openly and with utmost honesty. The facilitators will be from the local population and they will be fully inducted on the local communities feedback mechanism and their objectives. This will pave way for feedback from communities and recommendations for improvement. Help will also collect feedback through informal conversations and meetings with the Relief and Rehabilitation Committees (RRC). Via local authorities and field visits by programme or senior management staffs.
