Provision of critical child protection related services to most vulnerable children, adolescents, youths and caregivers in conflict and flood affected counties of Wau, Aweil East, Nagero, Fangak, Pochalla and Abyei municipality of Western Bahr El Ghazal, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Western Equatoria, Jonglei States and Abyei Administrative Area respectively.

Basic Info

Project Name

Provision of critical child protection related services to most vulnerable children, adolescents, youths and caregivers in conflict and flood affected counties of Wau, Aweil East, Nagero, Fangak, Pochalla and Abyei municipality of Western Bahr El Ghazal, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Western Equatoria, Jonglei States and Abyei Administrative Area respectively.

Start Date

01/01/2023

End Date

31/12/2023

Project Summary

Through the project Hold the Child will provide life savings and support services to the most vulnerable, conflict, flood, COVID 19 affected girls, boys, adolescents, youths, women, and men in South Sudan who are exposed to child protection risks in targeted areas (targeting Host Communities, IDPs, Returnees and persons with disability). For the effective delivery of the services Hold the Child will closely with other partners under the Protection cluster and be supported by the CP AoR and other technical working groups like CMTF, UASC, PSS Working group, CAAFAG working group among others. Hold the Child will work closely with other sectors not limited to food security, livelihoods, education, health, nutrition, WASH, shelter and settlement and camp management as per CPMS 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28 respectively. The project too will focus on other key activities of the CP AoR that are enhancing the localization agenda since Hold the Child has the knowledge and strength to continue in CP coordination in WBeG CPWG, participation in various capacity-building opportunities, ensuring the quality of CP services and contributing in resource mobilization.
The services will be delivered through different modalities namely: static, outreach, and mobile. The project will be provided through the following key sub-thematic services to at-risk and vulnerable children, adolescents, youths, people with disabilities (PWDs), and community members.
1. Provision of comprehensive case management including through cash-based assistance (CPMS standards 18, 11, 13, 20);
2. Provision of family tracing and reunification for UASC (CPMS standards 13, 18);
3. Establishing and strengthening referral mechanisms (CPMS standards 14, 16, 17);
4. Provision of quality age and gender-appropriate individual, group, and specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and parenting skills through community-based interventions including mobile teams to extremely vulnerable girls, boys, and their caregivers (CPMS standards 10, 15);
5. Conduct child protection and COVID-19 awareness-raising & prevention messaging activities for women, men, girls, and boys including children with disabilities (CPMS standard 6).
6. Capacity building to frontline service providers and volunteers on Child Protection including PSS, case management, general CPiE, and COVID-19 (CPMS standards 2, 18);

1 - Needs

South Sudan with continued conflict and instability combined with flooding has resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacements to which Wau, Fangak, Pochalla, Nagero, Aweil East and Abyei municipality have heavily contributed. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. In addition, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns to certain areas. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return (most in Wau and Raga), populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. A deterioration of people’s physical and mental well-being, living standards and coping mechanisms is expected in 2023. Children and adolescents in South Sudan continue to be exposed to a wide range of protection risks including, risks of family separation, recruitment, and use of children by armed forces and groups, psychosocial distress, sexual violence, neglect, abuse and exploitation. The COVID-19 outbreak protracted situation, community displacements, flooding and ongoing conflict have exacerbated this situation; hindering adequate, safe access and full utilization of lifesaving humanitarian services in South Sudan. Over 2.01 million people are currently internally displaced in South Sudan. Most of them have settled in former Protection of Civilian sites (PoCs) in areas of Wau and Nagero contributing, collective sites, or are living out of camps/with host populations, requiring Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) services to meet their basic needs.As per the WFP situation report for February 2022: Tensions remain high around Tambura, Western Equatoria State, where levels of food insecurity are projected to be severe, with Nagero county ranked of extreme concern. According to the 2021 FSMNS+, an average of 7 percent of children drop out of school due to various reasons including high costs associated with education, forced/early marriage, distance to school, and cattle-keeping responsibilities. Many of them, especially girls, may never go back to school due to teenage pregnancy or early marriage, while boys engage in child labour, including recruitment by armed groups and community-based militias.In reference to the South Sudan Flooding Situation Report of 12 November 2021 by the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group, Some 780,000 people were reported as affected by flooding as of 12 November. At the time of reporting, people in Jonglei (Fangak and Pochalla) are the worst affected (278,000 people), Northern Bahr el Ghazal (Aweil East) (65,000), Western Bahr el Ghazal (10,000) among others. The flooding has displaced thousands of people, many of whom have taken refuge on higher ground within their county, with many sheltering in churches, schools and public spaces.2022 saw devastating waves of clashes in Agok between the Twic (Warrap), Ngok (Abyei); In February OCHA estimated 50,000 IDPs in Warrap (Twic) & Abyei town North of AgokIn reference to South Sudan HNO 2022, estimated that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. As per Protection Cluster 2021 analysis: People with disabilities face challenges to access services, with greater challenges experienced among women and especially children with disabilities (75 percent perceive having less access to services, food, and education than children without disabilities). Anecdotally, there are greater protection risks for women and girls with disabilities.Over 37,490 cases (17,700 girls and 19,790 boys) as unaccompanied, separated or missing and at-risk most vulnerable children as per to UASC report for caseload summary of 11th November 2022, while 21,380 children (10,057 girls and 11,323 boys) as active caseload.Due to the above needs, there is a great need for continued Humanitarian Response in the targeted affected counties within South Sudan.

1. Provide comprehensive case management, including cash-based assistance. Target 800 children (400 girls and 400 boys). 2. Provide family tracing and reunification for UASC, including identification, registration, documentation, interim care, family tracing, follow-up visits and reunification, reintegration follow-ups and case closures. Target 320 children (160 girls and 160 boys) 3. Updating and strengthening the referral pathways to enable the implementation of case management services across the targeted counties. Target 2 referral pathways. 4. Provide quality age and gender-appropriate individual, group and specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) through community-based interventions including mobile teams to extremely vulnerable girls and boys. Target 53,000 children (26,500 girls and 26,500 boys); 5. Provide quality age and gender-appropriate individual, group and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and parenting skills through community-based interventions including mobile teams to parents and caregivers. Target 11,000 adults (6,500 females and 4,500 males) 6. Conduct child protection and COVID-19 awareness-raising & prevention messaging activities to women, men, girls and boys including children with disabilities; Target 54,120 children (27,060 girls and 27,060 boys); 11,400 adults (6,700 females and 4,700 males) 7. Capacity building to frontline service providers, and volunteers including government line ministry social workers on Child Protection, including PSS, case management, and general CPiE. Target 400 adults (200 females and 200 males) Despite the reduction in the spread of COVID-19 Hold the Child will continue adapting specific modalities and methodologies in the delivery of the outlined activities. The modalities include door-to-door, use of phones, especially for case management, use of the available hotlines and helplines, for instance, the GBV hotlines, use of help desks, individual support, especially on MHPSS, through different channels of communications including through existing community-based child protection groups and focal points that already have the trust of the community, radio programs, social media, bulk SMS and outreach volunteers, special consideration to children with disabilities among others modalities.With geographical presence and more than 10 years of technical expertise Hold the Child will continue providing child protection critical services to support the most vulnerable children and their caregivers across South Sudan

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

By providing Cash and Voucher Assistance Hold the Child will be ensuring programming supports local markets through multiplier ways. There will be regular market monitoring to ensure that beneficiaries are still able to access basic items with the cash value transferred and to monitor any impact on the market as a result of the increased demand generated from the program. The program will carry regular food commodities and analyses that will enable informing the possible adjustments that will be done to this particular output. While Hold the Child will be working closely with the World Food Programme and other partners that are implementing a similar programme hence this will inform the project on how to deal with specific challenges that might arise from the implementation of the Cash assistance.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

South Sudan has been facing economic hit challenges that have resulted to continues fluctuation of prices of goods and services at the market level. Through the provision of Cash and Voucher Based Assistance, it will ensure improved access to basic food commodities available in local markets in the targeted areas of the project. In addition, the assistance will enable the most vulnerable children and their caregivers to meet their basic needs and avoid negative coping mechanisms that might result due to food insecurity. Through the provision of unconditional cash, beneficiaries are provided with increased choice, flexibility and meaningfulness in meeting their food needs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) and marginalized groups will be consulted during the project cycle and their contribution and participation will lead to decision making; the project identifies disabled people's increased risks, and project identifies and addresses barriers to participation faced by people with or without disabilities. Availability and accessibility of complaint mechanism to all including persons with disabilities, that will be confidential and responsive; reduction and awareness on exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, and flood-affected areas and policy orientation for all the staff with supervision.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Hold the Child will demonstrate its commitment to the affected populations by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated across all project stages i.e. inception with key stakeholders, recruitment & staff induction pieces of training, activities & outreaches, monitoring & evaluation, performance management.Hold the Child will help the affected communities claim their rights through information and documentation, and support efforts to strengthen respect for rights. Hold the Child will have a duty to advocate for the full respect of children’s and human rights and compliance with international law that supports a stronger protective environment. All children and other targeted beneficiaries should be able to access solutions (such as legal actions at local, national or international levels) and claim legal rights (such as inheritance or restitution) that may influence their ability to protect themselves and to claim other rights. Children should be supported to become informed and engaged citizens. Hold the Child will implement simple, accessible, child-friendly, anonymous programme feedback and reporting mechanisms at each site. Affected populations will/must be aware of how to use the mechanisms and what response they should expect when they raise a concern or report an incident. All general programme and Hold the Child’s feedback will be addressed promptly and shared with staff and associates in team meetings and performance reviews following the principles of confidentiality and need to know. A friendly feedback and complaint mechanisms such as suggestion boxes, one on one, a focus group discussions among others will be explored in all our target areas. Regular field visits will enable to have open meetings with the communities of the affected populations, therefore the beneficiaries will be always be given a chance to express themselves safely. Reports will be collected from the community-based protection members who are close with the beneficiaries hence strengthening the linkages of feedback and complaints. Such measures will include stakeholder advocacy sessions, focused group discussions etc. issues raised will be examined and actions are taken on a progressive basing along with project implementation. Hold the Child will work with key stakeholders to support integration efforts in line with the triple nexus continuum (emergency, resilience and peacebuilding especially in relation to HLP). All activities in the project will be guided by HCO’s Child Safeguarding Policy and making sure all frontline service providers sign a Code of Conduct and measures to ensure adherence, therefore making sure that beneficiaries are always protected in reference to “do no harm” principles

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Hold the Child has adapted mandatory reporting of PSEA related incidents mandatory in the project cycle. All staff, associates and volunteers must receive regular and refresher training on child safeguarding and PSEA, do no harm, psychological first aid, and risk assessment among other key pieces of training not forgetting on data management for the targeted beneficiaries by utilizing data management systems like the CPIMS++. South Sudan continues to experience Sexual exploitation and abuse as major critical issues; Hold the Child through this project will have specific measures and activities that will be tailored throughout the life cycle of the project that will focus on prevention, mitigation and response. Key policies within the organization like child safeguarding policy and PSEA will be disseminated to the beneficiaries through hand-outs, through IEC materials that will have key information on PSEA and how the beneficiaries can safely report cases. Through awareness as part of key activities that will be implemented and referrals will all contribute beneficiaries accessing the information on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All beneficiaries' feedback will be paramount and will be taken into special consideration through the project cycle, hence this will be directly informing in the project designing, in implementation and in monitoring.’
