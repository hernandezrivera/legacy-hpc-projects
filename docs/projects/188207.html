Project Summary

As part of its education response, PUI is targeting vulnerable and underserved communities with a bundle of activities, with the overall objective of enhancing the access to quality education provided in protective and suitable learning environment for affected children, to ensure that they don’t become a lost generation. Indeed, with the protracted crisis approaching its eleventh year in Syria, an entire generation of children and youth is being subject to persistent lack of opportunities to access quality education. This situation could have long-term negative consequences, affecting children’s mental health and wellbeing and preventing them from developing in good and safe conditions, as well as gaining essential life skills and knowledge.
The proposed project in brief:
- Will target locations where high education needs are documented by Education Sector.
More than 77% of the locations have a severity score of 3 (Tafas, Eastern Ghariyeh, Al Mayadin, Abu Kamal, Sbeikhan, Khasham, Talbiseh, Suran, Madiq Castle, Oqeirbat, Arjel, Um Tinet Simaan, Dayr Hafir
Darayya, Duma, Sabka and Maadan), while 18 % have a score of 4 (Deir-ez-Zor, Mahin, Ein Terma and
Khan Shaykun). PUI also will target Dar'a city which has as score of 5 (4.55% percentage from all areas).
- The cost per beneficiary is 36$ for remedial classes, 48$ for Self-Learning Program, 60$ for Early Childhood education, 4$ for awareness sessions, 6$ for back to learning campaigns, 120$ for teachers’ capacity building and 60000$ for the school rehabilitation activity. The cost per beneficiary is around 104.185$ (Calculated by dividing the total budget by the total number of people the project will target.)
- It is estimated that around 4,762 individuals of targeted beneficiaries may have disabilities.
This project aims at encouraging out of school students and dropped-out students to resume their education through tailored back to learning campaigns and empowering those children with self-learning program conceived as transition programs to enable them to catch-up and reintegrate the formal education system after this transition period.
In addition, PUI will assist children in their education by providing them with kits, education supplies and remedial classes to cover for any gaps and ensure every child’s educational needs are met and to prevent them from dropping out of school. Furthermore, PUI will contribute to enhancing the quality of education by providing capacity building activities to teachers and MoE staff, training them on active learning, PSS, and life skills. PUI will also initiate an awareness campaign for children and their parents as well as teachers on health and hygiene, with a specific focus on COVID-19 protection and Mine risk education. Lastly, in parallel to the above-mentioned activities, PUI will ensure that children have access to safe and protective learning environment where they can access quality education by rehabilitating damaged schools in targeted locations.
PUI is planning to conduct Remedial classes, back to learning campaigns, Self-Learning programs, Awareness sessions, Early Childhood education and capacity building for teachers in all the targeted areas while the school rehabilitation will be conducted in all areas except for Khasham, Talbiseh, Suran and Dayr Hafir areas as there is no need identified since PUI has already conducted school rehabilitation activities in those areas.
PUI will create Parents & Teachers Association (PTA) in each school, to support an inclusive participation of the community in the designing and monitoring of the activities. The PTAs will consist of teachers working in the targeted schools and parents of school students. The PTA committee will consist of around 10 to 15 members from both genders and diverse age groups.
PUI will be training 120 young volunteers to participate in the BTL campaign. Those volunteers will be working for two months.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022, more than 7.6 million children between the age of 3 and 17 and in need for education assistance. The protracted nature of the conflict, economic duress and the COVID-19 pandemic, continue to impact overstretched education services. With 25 verified attacks on schools and seven verified instances of military use of schools in nine months, attacks affecting schools remain high. Self-reported attendance at national level is 82%. Economic factors and negative coping mechanisms, including children working to support the household, continue to be a key reason why children are not attending school as most often education services are not flexible enough to accommodate children working to support their households. In addition to distance traveled, children face potential risks when traveling to school which may limit access to education services. At national level, the data reveals that 18 per cent are out of school and half of the children who are not in school have never enrolled. Also, the formal system is unable to absorb and retain all school age children. Many students learn in unconducive and/or unsafe learning environments. At the national level there is an average of one operational classroom for every 54-school aged child. Many children in school continue to have reduced learning time due to multiple shifts and school suspension. PUI did not do a need assessment in the areas as the needs are similar to the general ones mentioned in the HNO 2022.

5 - Implementation of activities

In terms of organizational capacity, working in Syria since 2008, PUI directly implements integrated programs, including education and protection, livelihood, WASH, shelter and NFI components, with the overall objective of bringing the Population of Syria back to their initial standards of living, to foster self-sufficiency and to promote social cohesion. Through the implementation of this integrated response, PUI gained good acceptance in its areas of intervention and increased the positive impact of the interventions implemented. PUI always tends to create synergies between its interventions; the proposed intervention will therefore complement other interventions implemented integrated areas. The design of the response is based on consultation with key informants from local stakeholders and the targeted communities to design the best way of implementation without leaving any one behind. Also, PUI introduced several tools to ensure monitoring of implementation at all stages of project. Moreover, coordination at field level with local stakeholder and humanitarian partners and Education sector working group is ensured by the Local representative officers to share knowledge and to avoid duplication of provided services. Furthermore, PUI coordinators work closely with other sectors when needed. Lastly, PUI education staff directly monitor the activities while the independent M&E team conduct regular monitoring visits and evaluate the impact of each activity

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Security situation deteriorates and learning facilities in some locations became insecure.(Likelihood: Low. Impact: High)Mitigation measures: Assess the situation in targeted areas before the intervention; learning facilities can be de-conflicted using the de-confliction mechanism; Safety protocols are set in the learning facilities; Raise awareness about existing protection and health service providers where victims can seek help. Risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to inequality of services provided.(Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High)Mitigation measures: Perform conflict analysis before planning intervention; Try to provide equal services in areas where conflict exists; Ensure local community anticipations to ensure the buy in and build element of success. Risk: Incidents of child labour, youth marginalization & GBV may take place.(Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High)Mitigation measures: Raise the awareness on existing service providers where victims can seek help; Enforce policies with/through all partners to prevent such incidents; Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train staff on code of conduct; Address any SEA complaints.

PUI has well-established PSEA policy and repotting methods for any breaches.
