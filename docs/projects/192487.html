Project Summary

The goal of this project is to strengthen protection for women survivors of violence during the complex humanitarian crisis in Lebanon. Under the Emergency Response Plan (ERP), this project responds to critical protection gaps in GBV prevention and response by targeting underserved Lebanese women, migrant women, and Palestinian refugee (PRL) women in Lebanon. UN Women oversee and monitor the overall project implementation, providing technical assistance. Services will be provided directly by KAFA, with support from the Lebanese Union for People with Disabilities (LUPD).
The GBV prevention component will respond to the objective 1.1: measures are in place to prevent and mitigate the risks of GBV among the most marginalized communities. It will include:
i) Outreach campaign with messaging on GBV, including services available
ii) Awareness sessions raising for GBV interventions, with a focus on women with disabilities
The outreach campaign will seek to increase women-at-risk and men with access to information about how to prevent and stop gender-based violence, as well as the services available to them. The campaign will have a two-prong approach, utilizing both multi-media platform, and traditional communication materials, including fliers and leaflets in high traffic areas such as health centers. Given the fuel and electricity crisis, expanding outreach interventions to women who are not online or have access to internet and mobile phones, is essential. The awareness sessions will focus on two target groups – women-at-risk of experiencing violence, and men who might become perpetrators.
The protection service component will respond to objective 4.1: Survivors and individuals at risk have access to quality specialized GBV services. It will include:
i) KAFA’s emergency helpline (03 018 019)
ii) Holistic and comprehensive case management services for women survivors
iii) Emergency safe housing
iv) Specific case management for women survivors with disabilities
KAFA’s emergency helpline is a well-established service, operating since 2016 and has been inundated with a high case load, since the beginning of the pandemic. It is available for women survivors of violence 24- hours/7 days a week and will be capacitated to expand its reach. Women will receive counseling and referrals to case management and other services. The case management is led by highly experienced and trained case workers and will take place in women safe spaces in Beirut, Bekaa, and Saida, safe housing/shelters, and in case of emergencies, remotely. Specific staff and interventions will be offered to women with disabilities, including ensuring that physical centers are accessible, and staff are equipped with information and tools to best support their needs. To operate effectively in the dual emergency context of the COVID-19 pandemic, and fuel crisis, support will be provided to ensure the continuity of remote services and access for women survivors, including internet packages, mobile applications, and alternative means of power sourcing.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Under sector objective 1.1, women’s awareness sessions will aim at empowering women and strengthening their confidence at home and in their communities by focus GBV related topics. The awareness sessions targeting men will work with men to challenge gender stereotypes and redefine traditional and toxic masculinities that contribute to gender-based violence. Under sector objective 4.1, the case management services include social care services, psychological and psychosocial interventions, legal assistance and one-time allocations of emergency protection cash. The integrated approach is regarded as a best practice in responding to GBV and other forms of abuse, as it enables survivors to access a range of services in one location, reduce trauma and receive specialized care. Ultimately, it engages and empower women to move away from abuse and to move forward with their lives.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

This project will use gender sensitive approaches to ensure women’s protection, such as prioritizing the recruitment of female frontline staff, monitoring women’s utilization of protection cash assistance, and adopting new outreach strategies to identify migrant women left behind and not accessing services and assistance available. In addition, UN Women has strong policy and prevention mechanisms in place to prevent SEA in humanitarian response context. Under this project, all partners – KAFA and LUPD- must meet UN Women’s minimum standard requirements for PSEA including having a PSEA focal point, staff mandated orientation/trainings and ensure beneficiaries have access to a safe and confidential referral mechanisms.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Ensuring a feminist approach and women’s empowerment is at the heart of this project, whereby the needs and perspectives of female affected populations will be prioritized. This is an incredibly important issue of all recipients of aid, and particularly critical for projects targeting survivors and victims of GBV. Accountability for affected populations will be integrated into every aspect of UN Women’s project monitoring and evaluation framework. The project will ensure that there is a complaint and feedback mechanism so that all women can voice their concerns and resolve disputes with regards to the delivery of aid. PSEA will be integrated into these mechanisms. In addition, a steering committee made-up of migrant women beneficiaries from the targeted communities will be the main forum for regular community-led feedback. Their needs and suggestions will help guide project implementation to keep agencies accountable to migrant women.
