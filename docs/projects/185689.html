Project Summary

In 2022, Tdh will focus on implementing a multi-sectoral response to children&caregivers from both Rohingya and host communities. Children&caregivers were involved/consulted in different stages of this proposal, to capture the gaps&needs in different layers of the communities.The project offers comprehensive CM services, integrated MHM services, MHPSS, family support&positive parenting services, strengthening Community-based Child Protection Mechanisms with capacity building of local actors, transferable/life skills trainings, and strengthening adolescents’ participation in social cohesion. These activities aim for sustainable outcomes in the mid-term, especially by strengthening the community structures and local capacity. Tdh programming is age-gender-diversity inclusive, ensuring equal and impartial access to assistance and services especially by vulnerable groups and people with specific needs through scaled-up outreach. Focus will be given to vulnerable children at risk or survivors of child abuse, child marriage, child labor, trafficking as well as unaccompanied and separated boys and girls, children with disabilities, child survivors of sexual abuse and GBV. Tdh will take all necessary measures to improve children, adolescents and adults’ access to existing resources and opportunities. The barriers which prevent adolescents, and children with different abilities from accessing the services will be assessed and acted upon accordingly. A gender lens is applied to all aspects of Tdh response, and messages on prevention and response to GBV will be mainstreamed.
As per the needs and lessons learnt from COVID-19 access impediments; Tdh will continue developing modalities to mitigate against interruption of activities and build the capacity of community volunteers. MHM is a significant concern in Cox’s Bazaar. Tdh currently operates 2 Primary HealthCare Centres in Teknaf; providing 24x7 service (including SRH) to Rohingya and Host Communities covering all age groups. In 2021, Tdh’s SRHR activities started with awareness raising on MHM, key messages dissemination and the distribution of MHM dignity/hygiene kits. In 2022, Tdh will scale up the SRHR response through gender-responsive,adolescent/youth-friendly services in the camps. Activities will offer knowledge &awareness on how to manage menstruation effectively and hygienically while increasing the access to Tdh community facilities to wash/dispose of used sanitary materials in appropriate ways. Tdh will also address societal beliefs&taboos surrounding menstruation. All activities will ensure the do-not harm principle by prioritizing the safety and dignity of women and girls. Tdh will continue its sensitization regarding safety measures before and during natural disasters, including disaster risk reduction, resilience building, applying measures to prevent family separations, the activation of LCCMPs and/or ECCCs, PERU teams, and mainstreaming of COVID-19 messages.
Tdh will continue to be an active member of CPSS and multiple inter-agency coordination fora; will liaise regularly with the local authorities and will focus on partnership development with local CP actors. Tdh has an office in Dhaka, where Tdh will continue cooperation with relevant governmental authorities. To strengthen child safeguarding measures including PSEA, Tdh will continue its child-friendly, accessible feedback and reporting mechanisms through direct consultations with children. TDH M&E Plan comprises of routine monitoring of project outputs and broader effects periodically, encouraging project quality improvements and learning to a wide range of stakeholders, including beneficiaries. Tdh will encourage the local participation continuously, to promote local ownership, and ensure the sustainability. Besides this intervention, Tdh’s existing camp and community structures are constructed according to the safe and green engineering guideline and environmental impact of the project will be minimal.

1 - Identified Needs

Both in Rohingya and host communities, COVID-19 and prevention measures exacerbated existing protection concerns and needs, with a social & economic impact that exhausted households’ existing coping mechanisms, placing children at increased risk of child marriage, hazardous labor, trafficking and getting pulled out of school, among other concerns. With high dependency ratio (ISCG, 2019, Innovations for Poverty Action-IPA-2019) in both the Rohingya and host community, lack of a reliable source of livelihoods forces female household heads to fall into exploitive relationships, such as survival-driven transactional sex, indicating the need for more livelihoods support. Relatively, child labour involves children as young as 7 years old; 3% of children 5-17 years are reportedly working, with 51% in the worst forms of child labour (JRP2021). In the host community, 6% of the assessed households reported at least one member under the age of 18 already married or about to get married. 8% reported the presence of at least one child working; 52% percent of working children in hazardous work. In the camps, the most reported child protection concerns are Child Labour (64%), neglect (59%), Child Marriage (57%), risks of trafficking (32%), depression (27%), separation of children (25%) and physical abuse (23%). The key causes of child marriage in the camps are 49% economic reasons, 39% family pressure/force, and 28% cultural acceptability (Child Protection Assessment in Rohingya Refugee Camps in Cox’s Bazar). This indicates a need for a holistic response which eases both economic tensions in families as well as behavioral change and norms in communities. 34% of Rohingya households reported needs of children in their community not to be adequately met, while it is 51% of households for the host community, with the top unmet need was education at 33% and 58% for both communities respectively (J-MSNA November 2021). Additionally, household poverty was the most common reason given for the increase in child labor at 70%, lack of access to education (48%) and sick or elderly caregivers (40%) in the camps. With MHPSS activities remaining reduced or suspended, together with limited access to child, women and girls’ safe spaces and learning centres, the mental health of children and adolescents were highly affected. Psychosocial distress increased since the start of COVID-19 with signs of emotional or behavioral trouble; disrespectful behaviours was the most reported (26%), followed by selfisolation (22%) and then aggressive behaviours, self-harm, lack of self-care, and unwillingness to listen to caregivers (all at 20%) (Child Protection Assessment October 2021). Unaddressed psychological distress over the long-term has been known to contribute to an increase in GBV, intimate partner violence, and child abuse. The overcrowding of camps coupled with women and girls’ restrictions to move freely has a profound impact on women and girls’ sense of dignity, safety and hope for the future as well. The security environment in and around the camps also impacted negatively on the wellbeing of both Rohingya refugees and vulnerable host communities particularly for women and girls, limiting their access to public spaces and basic services; including MHM services. The 2019 REACH Menstrual Hygiene Materials Assessment report states that 68% and 58% of households reported receiving information on menstrual hygiene materials management and menstruation; and almost 90%, expressed the interest to receive more information on how to properly use, clean and dispose menstrual materials. Lack of adequate MHM facilities and services has consequences for women and girls' health, and, at the same time, often leads to reinforce taboos and shame associated with menstruation. This shows that there is a need to provide integrated MHM services in more consistent, structured and systematic way.

2 - Activities or outputs

Comprehensive Case management service for children and adoloscents, and access to child protection appropriate services including alternative care, and Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) services. Provision of structured MHPSS activities to Rohingya refugee and host community boys, girls, adolescent boys and adolescent girls. Activities will utilize an enhanced community outreach strategy to reach out to, mobilize and retain the most difficult to reach children and adolescents, including children living with disabilities, working children, adolescent girls as well as those coming from disadvantegous socio-economic backgrounds. Provision of one-on-one counselling based on Problem Management Plus (PM+) methodology (the structured MHPSS support to caregivers &parents) Strengthening the capacity of community-based child protection committees, local actors and community groups for DRR, community resilience and self-protection. Sensitization of traditional leaders, religious leaders, women leaders, and other key community stakeholders on gender equality, menstrual health, girls empowerment and child participation. Organize community dialogues through community-led initiatives on protection, gender equality and menstrual health. Deliver MHM curriculum to adolescent girls and female Caregivers to Rohingya refugee and host communities at Tdh PHCs, Tdh Community Centers and Host Community secondary schools respectively. This activity will be in direct cooperation with GBVSS, and the suggested cirricular by the cluster will be used. Establishment of 2 "Safe MHM Service Point" within Tdh Health Centers; to be accesible by young women and girls to wash or dispose of their used sanitary materials in appropriate ways. Scaled up community outreach, social inclusion activities, awaraness raising activities, focusing on vulnerable groups and people with specific needs, to reduce stigmatization and discrimination Provision of family support and strenghening services to female & male parents and caregivers, through both static and mobile modules: The Parenting Skills Program (PSS) is a structured module of Tdh, incorporated under structured PSS module "Program Management Plus strategies (PM+)". These two structured modules mainly target the children and their caregivers to be equipped with problem management skills ,and PSS particularly targers the caregivers with various of stress management strategies and awaraness about child rights, to prevent and mitigate the violence against children. One Positive parenting module under PM+ is 8 sessions in total, delivered once per week over a 2-months period. Capacity building and development of partnership strategies for local protection actors, including child protection CBOs and volunteers (non CP actors and CP actors), and with the the host community school teachers, administrators, and managers on introductory packages specifically designed to ensure ethical CP referrals and to cover existing technical capacity gaps respectively with a commitment to follow ups with government and national partners. Also maintain the coordination and linkage with Tdh inter-sectoral programme and ISCG regularly for mainstreaming the interventions. Development and implementation of life skills, transferable skills, self-reliance and skill development package for adolescents to reduce the risk of negative coping mechanisms. Facilitation and implementation of Rohingya and Host Community youth-led and girls-/women- led comunity-based initiatives for the purpose of enhanced youth participation and social cohesion. Enhancing Accountability to Affected Populations (cross-cutting): Tdh follows an inclusive and iterative approach with a user-centric design aiming to integrate the feedback of child and adult beneficiaries.
