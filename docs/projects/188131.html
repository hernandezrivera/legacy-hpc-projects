Project Summary

Humanitarian needs within proposed project locations of Masyaf and Salamieh Districts of Hama governorate continue to be high and complex. A key driver of vulnerability for both displaced and resident households, as well as returnees, is limited and stalled income levels leading to negative coping mechanisms and tenuous food security levels. These economic impacts exacerbate hardships and increase humanitarian needs in the food sector; thousands of new families have been rendered vulnerable and need aid. Loss of hope, dignity and social cohesion are emerging risks that threaten any future stability.
Youth, women and children face acute vulnerability due to a reduction in basic social services, and negative coping strategies include: reducing spending on NFIs to meet food needs, taking on debts/loans, skipping payments of debts/rent to support other needs expenditures and selling productive assets.
Humanitarian programming needs to actively balance between meeting immediate humanitarian needs, while simultaneously contributing to local resilience and transitioning off exclusively humanitarian assistance in order to obtain a decent standard of living. Helping community members earn a living is therefore a key pillar of any intervention in the current Syrian context.
Community-based Cash for Work (CB-CfW) programming represents an innovative modality for cash programming inside Syria. First piloted by AKF in 2015, CB-CfW is a joined-up approach that places community organizations at the centre of design and implementation of humanitarian programming while at the same time providing temporary livelihood opportunities to vulnerable HHs able to work.
AKF has been implementing Community-Based Cash for Work (CB-C4W) projects for eight years. Through this project, AKF’s extensive experience with various CB-C4W projects will be expanded to address required social services in Masyaf and Salamieh districts. The main objective is to sustain and recover the economic livelihoods of crisis-affected people especially women and youth through:
- Providing emergency employment through CB-C4W activities for 2000 individuals, of which 60%, 80% and 4% will be females, youths and PwDs, respectively.
- Implementing 10 CB-C4W projects which aim at improving basic social services delivered to the population within Masyaf and Salamieh Districts.
Strengthening the civic capacity in the delivery of public services by involving local communities and Local NonGovernmental Organizations (LNGOs) in planning, implementing and monitoring the CB-C4W projects.
Each direct beneficiary household will receive monthly cash estimated at 200,000 SYP. Cash transfers will be provided through AKF’s cash transfer platform via the First Micro- Finance Bank (FMFB). CB-C4W projects and lists of beneficiaries will be informed by the Project Steering Committee (PSC) and community organizations (e.g. LNGOs).

4 - Needs

10 years of Syrian crisis continue to erode the socio-economic foundations of the Syrians, in parallel to the Lebanese financial crisis, the rapid depreciation of the Syrian currency, inflation, and the entry into effect of new trade restrictions in June 2020. The COVID-19 pandemic led to extra stress on social and basic services while depriving Syrians of jobs and livelihoods. All contributed to contraction of the economic activity, affecting people’s ability to sustain adequate living standards, seek decent livelihood opportunities, access social services, and further undermine social cohesion(HNO 2021).ERL needs remain large across the country, with an estimated 11.6 million people 51% Female needing dignifying ERL support. This is exacerbated by a severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital (UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio- Economic Impact Assessment). About two-thirds of households lost at least one income sources, and half lost 75% to 100% of their income. 14.6 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance – a 21% increase compared to 2020 – with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. Over 90% of the population is estimated to live below the poverty line (2021 Needs and Response Summary Report). While there is a lack of in-depth information on Masyaf district, the 2021 HNO statistics indicate that negative coping strategies are a major problem across the country.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will provide wage-earning employment in 4months to 1,000 vulnerable individuals, 60% females, 80% youth, 4% PWDs, and 30% IDPs and returnees. Each beneficiary will receive monthly cash estimated at 200,000SYP through opening bank account at AKF’s financial service provider the First Micro Finance Bank (FMFB). LNGOs-CBOs will implement the project by assessing communities’ needs designing CB-C4W activities, identifying target groups. AKF will provide training on identifying the needs, designing quick impact project, gender equality, and management of CB-C4W projects. AKF will provide management and oversight during the implementation. Direct beneficiaries will be identified and given by LNGOs/CBOs and verified by an elected Project Steering Committee(PSC) based on clear criteria with a focus on women, youth, PwDs, IDPs and returnees. Process and outcome monitoring will be implemented by AKF directly and include baseline and end-line assessments, labour market price analysis, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries, post-project monitoring and FGDs with project stakeholders. The project adopts a gender and protection-sensitive approach based on AKF’s Do No Harm and safeguarding policies. AKF’s local community-based approach, integrates conflict-sensitive programming to identify unintended results of targeting strategies, sectarian fragilities, promote transparency and ensure inclusive community participation for vulnerable women, youth, PwD, and elderly

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

A:Workers may be subject to discrimination by residents of areas targeted by CB-CFW due to negative cultural perception of some work types. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Low.Community organizations will raise awareness of work type among targeted communities to identify vulnerable individuals. Regular and confidential monitoring and reporting will help identify exploitations cases to take relevant actions.B:Gender-related cultural and traditional norms may limit women participation in some works like solid waste collection. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Female trainees will be asked preference of work/task to ensure their comfort. AKF will maintain regular monitoring of women contribution and a complaint reporting system, and facilitate gender equality awareness-raising to facilitate positive interaction. Teams will have mixed genders and female leaders.C:Increased risk of inter-community tension where CBOs members may be accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries. Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. AKF will communicate the project objectives and targeting approach and criteria, oversee the selection process and ensure implementation based on the selection criteria.D:GBV and job associated hazards. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Workers will be given protective equipment. Women will work close to their residence to be familiar with the environment, and ensure easy access; minimize the risk of GBV, reduces time burdens, and facilitate family care responsibilities


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk 1: There is a risk that the project activity may lead to safeguarding issues for women involved in the project. Risk 2: The project activities time and locations might be unsafe for the vulnerable groups especially for women and children. In line with AKF safeguarding policy, manual and standards, the project PSEA strategy that will ensure the following mitigation measures are in place: • Train all project staff on safeguarding policies. • Ensure all involved partners adhere to AKF's Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct • Develop gender-responsivereferral pathways and ensure project beneficiaries and vulnerable groups are aware of safeguarding policies and can access reporting mechanisms. • All data collection will consciously abide by robust ethical standards, guided by AKF’s Codes of Conduct and Safeguarding Policies. • Conduct an assessment of safety aspects in spaces used for activities implementation as well as the surrounding environment. The project will adopt measures and mechanisms to protect benefciaries and hold superiors and subordinates to account alike. AKF follows specifc process and procedures to integrate safeguarding in the Project Cycle which are: • Determine expected safeguarding risk using (Risk Register tool) • Determine safeguarding measures to reduce the risks through (mitigation plan and indicators). • Evaluating safeguarding at the project Cycle before, during and after implementing the project, using Project Cycle Check list.
