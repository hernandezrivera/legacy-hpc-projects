Project Summary

In this project Rural Health Services (RHS) proposes interventions aimed at improving access to and provision of timely integrated lifesaving primary healthcare services to under-served crisis-affected IDPs and host populations in the hard-to-reach rural locations in Pibor (Jonglei State), Tonj North (Warrap State) and Raja (Western Bahr ElGhazel State) counties in South Sudan. The project will include establishment and operation of 5 static facilities (PHCUs) and 10 community-based mobile clinics. Of these 2 PHCUs and 4 mobile clinics will be in Pibor, 2 PHCUs and 4 mobile units in Tonj North and 1 PHCU and 2 mobile clinics in Raja County. More than 50% of the populations in the target locations are in dire need of health services. In fact about 24,000 people were classified in Catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity exacerbated by the recurrent sub-national and localized conflicts, widespread insecurity, atypical floods and aggravating COVID-19 scourge. South Sudan’s COVID-19 cases are cumulatively 8,414 with the death toll at 100 as of 4th March 2021(Worldometer.com, 2021). The leading causes of mortality and morbidity are malaria (43%), ARTIs (pneumonia) and AWDs and Epidemic prone diseases (measles and COVID-19). As a result, maternal mortality rates (789 deaths per 100,000 live births) and U5 children mortality rates (90.7 deaths per 1,000 live births) rank very high due to surge of vaccine preventable and communicable infectious diseases.
The established facilities will provide OPD consultations, ANC & PNC, clean, safe and hygienic deliveries by skilled birth attendants, EPI and mass vaccination of children under 5 years, Screening, treatment of MAM cases and referral of SAM cases , integrated disease surveillance & response (IDSR)/EWARN, hygiene promotion and health education, training of key project staff in preparedness, response and control of epidemic prone disease outbreaks with the focus on, malaria, and pneumonia, cholera, measles and COVID-19, clinical management of rape cases (CMR) and basic mental health psycho-social support (counselling). Other activities will include; management of war wounded injuries, provision of basic & minimal initial HIV/AIDS/TB services, distribution of LLITNs and micro nutrient supplementation, deworming, growth monitoring and nutrition assessment children less than five years.
In all the project targets 9,200 people in Pibor 5,219 in Tonj North and 1,581 in Raja County making up a total population of 16,000 direct beneficiaries plus an estimated 6,500 indirect beneficiaries at an approximate cost of 738,000 USD.

1 - Needs

The vicious conflict in South Sudan has largely – and for long - distracted service provision and led to wanton destruction and neglect of key sectors like health, resulting in record unmet needs in health care and other critical sectors, and has eroded people’s resilience more so the vulnerable and hard-to-reach marginalized young women, girls and children as well as people with disabilities. The target areas for this project are among the worst hit locations in the country. For instance Pibor, Tonj North and Raja counties are among the 24 most affected counties in IPC phases 5, 4 and IPC AMN Phase 4 of acute food insecurity. This is based on the estimated more than 50% of the populations being in dire need of essential services, a situation which is exacerbated by intermittent and sub-national conflicts, widespread insecurity, and atypical floods as well as aggravating COVID-19 Pandemic scourge. As a result, mass displacements, livelihood collapse and disrupted humanitarian food aid delivery have had devastating consequences on the already needy populations. COVID-19 has affected 8,414 cumulatively since April 2020 (Worldometer.com, 2021). The floods further hindered access to these locations with more than 60% of roads rendered impassable. The average GAM rate among children less than five years (U5) in Pibor, Tonj North and Raja counties is well above the 16% emergency threshold. It therefore goes that most of the U5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are likely to be malnourished in 2021 due to decreased coverage and uptake of nutrition services dictated by conflicts, flooding and COVID-19 impact (HNO, 2021). Pibor with estimated population of 222,297 among whom 88,919 are considered to be in dire need of health services, 49,000 people are displaced from the Western Pibor payams (Gumuruk, Lekaungole, Veterth & Pibor) to areas adjacent to UNMISS and several sites within Pibor Town (about 34,000 individuals (11,443 households) and 15,000 IDPs fled to Maruwa hills, Labarab camps and Boma areas by Aug. 2020 (HNO, 2021). In Tonj North County (population of 256,629 people and 81,614 PIN) with 16,480 IDPs (2,744 HHs) fled from affected parts of Rual-Bet, Pagol, Manalor, Akop, Aliek, Kirik and Awul payams to seek refuge in safer parts of Rual-Bet, Pagol, Manalor, Athieng-Puol, Aliek Centre, Krirk and Warrap Town by Sept-Dec. 2020. (IOM’s DTM, Nov, 2020). Raja County with 58,073 people out of whom 11,615 are in need, more than 9,000 individuals (1,957 households) are affected by flood related displacements due to heavy rains and overflow from flooded rivers onto lowlands of Timsah, Firka and Siri Malaga. The IDPs sought refuge at the adjacent safer locations in Timsah, Firka and Siri Malaga highlands at public places; Mosques, Schools or Churches for temporary sheltering. Most IDPs were Children (61%) and adult (40%) mainly women (60% of the adults) and some elderly persons. The floods further hindered access to these locations with all seasonal roads made impassible. (IOM’s DTM, Sept., 2020) Children, women and persons with specific needs (old, physically disabled, pregnant women, and child-headed households) suffered from exhaustion, diarrhea, lack of food and drinking contaminated river water with dead bodies and increased risk exposure to COVID-19 pandemic scourge, very limited or no public or households latrines (open defecation practices) and only 35% of population access safe drinking water. Reported pressing humanitarian needs are health, WASH, Food, protection and ES/NFIs exacerbated by compounding shocks; floods, sub-national conflicts, macro-economic crisis and COVID-19 impact (HNO, 2021). It is therefore evidently clear these areas need quick and tailored emergency humanitarian interventions especially in the critical areas of health, WASH, nutrition, NFIs and protection.

2 - Project activities

Through this project, RHS will establish and run 5 static primary healthcare units (PHCUs) and 9 community-based mobile clinics in the 3 counties including 2 PHCUs at Thangajon & Maruwa and 4 mobile clinics at Teneth, Manydakel, Manyabol and Labarab in Pibor, Firka PHCU and Timsah Mobile Clinic in Raja and 2 PHCUs at Manalor & Warrap and 4 mobile clinics at Awul, Pagol, Rualbet and Aliek in Tonj North. These will provide community-based outreach healthcare services to vulnerable; IDPs and host populations. The project design focuses on provision of integrated essential health services in line with national and international humanitarian standards and based on the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, seasonality and trauma related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, and responsive activities to people in need paying considerable attentions to vulnerable groups (women, children and person with disabilities). This aims to reduce excess morbidity, mortality and enhance considerable resilience to compounding shocks to vulnerable women, men, girls, boys and persons with specific needs. This is linked to Health Cluster objectives (Cos) especially CO1 which strives to reduce excess morbidity and mortality caused by common communicable and epidemic prone diseases. Our static PHCUs and mobile clinics will provide OPD consultations, antenatal care (ANC) and postnatal care (PNC) conducting clean, safe and hygienic deliveries by skilled birth attendants, mass vaccination of U5 children screening, treatment MAM and referral of SAM, integrated disease surveillance & response (IDSR)/EWARN, hygiene promotion and health education, training of key project staff in preparedness, response and control of epidemic prone disease outbreaks focusing on, malaria, pneumonia, cholera, measles and COVID-19 linked to CO2: Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations. We shall pay special attention to management & referral of SGBV especially clinical management of rape cases (CMR) and basic psycho-social support (counseling) as linked to CO3 (Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders). Our integrated approach to healthcare will also include management of war wounded injuries, provision of basic & minimal initial HIV/AIDS/TB services, screening services (RDT) to pregnant women, distribution of LLITNs and micro nutrient supplementation, deworming, growth monitoring and nutrition assessment to under 5 children. The target population is 16,000 direct beneficiaries (54% children & 46% adults of equal gender proportions and 15% PLWDs). This includes 9,000 people from 88,919 PiN in Pibor, 5,219 people from 81,614 PIN in Tonj North and 1,581people from 58,073 PIN in Raja are targeted. About 6,500 indirect beneficiaries from the surrounding communities are expected to benefit from the proposed interventions. With these proposed interventions, RHS hopes to significantly to reduction of excess morbidity and mortality caused by common communicable and epidemic prone infections in the three counties at a projected cost of 738,000 USD.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RHS has developed a PSEA policy guide that is used to ensure protection against any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. This guides all workers, collaborators and consultants in the course of delivering health services. RHS will sensitize all the recruited health workers and the community health committee members to be guardians of the PSEA policy in the field so as to maintain regular reporting of all incidents. The CHCs will be charged with alerting their communities of the availability of the avenues for reporting all incidents on the matter and will be assured of the protection of all whistleblowers.We shall keep abreast of all new knowledge and updates on the matter and this will be communicated effectively and timely to all stakeholders in the areas of operation. In addition RHS will establish and support various women and girls friendly spaces to advocate for their rights and enhance access to relevant information regarding available care services with appropriate information exchange network and referral pathways.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

RHS will include representatives of persons living with disabilities, pregnant & lactating women (PLW), girls and illiterate members of the community in the proposed CHCs. Through these people’s representatives, the project will ensure all voices are heard at all times and their complaints analyzed and responded to in the most efficient manner. RHS project will dis-aggregate beneficiary data by vulnerability, preferably, using the Washington Group Short Set of Questions (WGSS). The illiterates will be encouraged to utilize the proposed suggestion boxes to deposit any confidential information written for them by trusted persons.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Before the project commences, RHS will directly engage beneficiaries to first ascertain the needs of various vulnerable groups in a gender and age sensitive manner with considerable attention to persons with specific needs. In collaboration with relevant stakeholders our teams will directly incorporate and use the beneficiaries’ representatives (women, youth, persons with disabilities and others) and local authority leadership structures to facilitate and enhance a joint sensitization on the project detail including issues related to implementation, monitoring, evaluation and reporting. Suggestions boxes will be established in all the proposed facilities to ensure beneficiaries who feel threatened because of the information divulged are protected. This will mutually enhance effective and efficient complaints, feedback mechanisms about the project content, context and scope. Information obtained from beneficiaries will be kept with prudence, integrity and utmost confidentiality to ensure maximum protection in all aspects during and after project period. In addition our community health committees (CHC) approach used in Manydakel and Teneth in 2019 will be employed to ensure joint monitoring and regular feedback in the course of project implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

This is an emergency humanitarian intervention. RHS team will meet with beneficiaries from the word go to bring all stakeholders at the same wavelength. Thereafter, all feedback from beneficiaries regarding the quality and quantity of services will be treated with all the attention it deserves. It will then be analysed and appropriate course of action adopted. The response approach will include complaints that will be addressed immediately, those that will require wider community engagement and recommendations that will be forwarded to the Health Cluster and the Humanitarian Coordinator for further engagements. In this way all stakeholders will receive firsthand information on aid effectiveness and or ineffectiveness in the current form it is being offered.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project is based on a protection risk analysis because it pays speciﬁc attention to the needs of the different groups in a gender sensitive manner. Other cultural sensitivities and variations will be put in much consideration during implementation of the project. Speciﬁc respect will be given to each beneﬁciary to take part in the decision making process. We also take special precautions to protect children especially unaccompanied minors in the IDP Communities. This project will involve communities and households in particular, because it has been designed to protect the vulnerable household members especially women, girls and children from sexual exploitation and abuse. Complaints that will not reach through the normal channels from the supervisors and project oﬃcers shall be received directly by the M & E Field visitors during project progressive evaluation meetings. After proper investigation of the alleged issues, the head oﬃce shall communicate appropriate feedback outcomes to both beneﬁciaries and partners including the corrective measures taken, equal involvement and participation of the beneﬁciaries in decision making, giving them opportunity to employment and empowering them to avoid and report violence against other more vulnerable groups (women, girls, boys and disable groups) within the community while keeping referral pathways (information exchange) so confidential and influential at informed-decision making. The project will identify, address and monitor environmental, attitudinal and institutional barriers and enablers to participation for people with disabilities and older persons, and will dedicate budget for removal of these identified barriers

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

RHS recognizes the role of food security in health. We therefore encourage communities to practice activities that promote food security, livelihoods, resilience and self reliance such as home vegetable gardening, poultry keeping, fishing and small scale entrepreneurship. These simple methods will improve home income and effectively lift communities from IPC 3, 4 and 5.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

This project will include a wide range of responsive activities that are equitably tailored to each vulnerable group's need. The project M & E will include segregated responsibilities for managing complaints, feedback and response for each vulnerable group to enhance equitable access to such basic care services. RHS will involve all stakeholders to ensure that these procedures are conformed to in the course of delivering humanitarian services.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Through the nutrition component of this integrated project, RHS PHCUs and community based mobile health facilities will regularly screen all children less than five years for malnutrition using the MUAC technique. Based on the screening outcomes, our personnel will classify them according to the level of malnutrition to determine MAM or SAM cases. Appropriate response will then be undertaken including treatment of all MAM cases and referral of SAM cases to the next echelon of healthcare. There shall also be preventive measures such as mass deworming and Vitamin A supplementation. Using the same results, RHS will undertake sensitization and training of pregnant and lactating mothers as well as other care providers on the best nutrition practices including IYCF. This will consequently create access to the much needed nutrition services and will reduce excess morbidity and mortality due to malnutrition related sequelae among U5 children and PLW in the target project locations thereby effectively decreasing GAM prevalence.
