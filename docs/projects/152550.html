Project Summary

This project will respond to the need of the most vulnerable 40,000 individuals (9,600 men, 14,400 women, 6,400 boys and 9,600 girls) in Greater Equatoria, targeting with static response Mundri E/W and Tambura counties (WES) and with mobile response Lainya (CES) and Magwi county (EES). The project targets the most vulnerable IDPs (60%), returnees (30%), and host population (10%), particularly focusing on people with protection concerns and acute needs.
LCED will contribute to save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys (SO1) and protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys through provision of specialized and integrated services (SO2). This will be done through in-kind distributions of S/NFIs, including replacement of S/NFIs which have outlived their life span, to the most vulnerable households, including pregnant and lactating women (PLW), elderly and disabled persons, and children under 5. In some remote areas shelter framing material is available but not accessible to the beneficiaries (women, elderly), who experience protection concerns on the road, including harassment and looting, and it is not possible to implement a CBI due to lack of functioning markets. In such areas LCED will procure shelter framing material locally for estimated 15% of beneficiaries. Local material is preferred to pipeline items due to higher quality and lower cost of local supplies and transportation, and the possibility of revitalizing the local market.
LCED will continue and strengthen collaboration with other S/NFI, WASH and Protection cluster partners. As SiFP for Greater Mundri for the S/NFI cluster and DFC focal point for UNOCHA, LCED will engage new S/NFI partners with static presence in the area, offering capacity building and conducting joint interventions in order to improve the S/NFI response in the area. At the same time, LCED will strengthen the collaboration with SFPs in CES, WES and EES, in order to improve humanitarian coordination and avoid duplications in Tambura, Lainya and Magwi counties. Since 2014, LCED collaborated with WVI, Medair, IOM and Titi Foundation for static and mobile response in Mundri E/W, Mvolo, Tambura (WES), and Lainya, Wonduruba and Kajo-Keji (CES). As former member of the SAG, LCED is well positioned to lead humanitarian interventions applying the cluster methodology.
Additionally, LCED will continue incorporating protection in its activities, involving partners such as NP, through community sensitization on GBV, incorporation of risk mitigation measures, and referrals. LCED will also participate in multi-cluster needs assessments, leading them in Greater Mundri, and refer needs and gaps to the ICWG for mobilization of specific clusters. Finally, LCED plans to integrate the S/NFI activities with FSL and WASH, as member of these three clusters. Distribution of S/NFI and WASH NFIs to vulnerable displaced population will be integrated, and vulnerable communities will be targeted with rehabilitation of assets (water points and latrines) and construction of emergency shelters.
CBIs will combine S/NFI and FSL, in order to revive the local market economy for both food and non-food items, considering the risk of people reselling the NFIs in order to buy food. LCED will contribute to support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks (SO3) targeting 15% of beneficiaries with restricted, unconditional cash vouchers. This will be implemented only in areas where the population has access to the market but no purchasing power. A market assessment was conducted in Mundri W/E counties (Oct‘18) and the results were shared with the S/NFI cluster. After defining the ES/NFI package for the targeted beneficiaries, according to their needs, LCED will engage suppliers in the targeted locations to set the price of the items, according to actual prices in the market.

Needs

(a) Multiple displacement during 2017-18, due to conflict and lack of food, especially in remote areas of Mundri E/ W and Tambura (WES), Lainya (CES) and Magwi counties (EES).(b) In many counties of Greater Equatoria there is a consistent influx of returnees in some targeted locations (Magwi, Tambura, Mundri East), since the peace process began; returnees are both from neighbouring counties and countries such as Uganda, DRC and CAR.(c) Massive loss of property among IDPs and host community has occurred in many locations of Greater Equatoria, leaving most of the population exposed to human and environmental hazards, living in dire and undignified humanitarian conditions.(d) The main needs are food, shelter and viable WASH solutions.(e) In some cases, especially in areas such as Lainya and Magwi, returnees find their shelters destroyed or occupied and the only choice they have is to share the little resources with others (IDPs and host community).(f) Remote areas of the targeted counties (especially Tambura, Magwi and Mundri E/W) have not been accessed for most of 2018.(g) Most households are in dire need of basic NFI such as mosquito nets, plastic sheets, sleeping mats, blankets, solar lamps and kitchen sets. This is due to displacement, looting and deterioration of items, lack of access to the markets and little purchasing power.(h) An estimated 20-30% of the household targeted have already received S/NFI assistance in 2017-18, however, the items received are damaged due to the fact that they have outlived their life span, and the beneficiaries are still in the same condition of vulnerability. In March 2018, LCED successfully replaced the plastic sheets for IDPs residing in a collective center in Mundri town, which LCED had targeted in April 2017 with distribution of emergency shelter material and construction of temporary structures.(i) Most local markets have collapsed, while the functioning ones have heavily inflated prices and items are scarce, especially in the remote locations of Magwi and Tambura counties. There is need to boost local markets and revitalize the local economy with cash based interventions, and similar approaches to increase community resilience and improve livelihoods.(j) In some remote areas there is lack of access to locally available shelter framing material, especially for women and elderly people, experiencing protection concerns on the road, including harassment and looting, and it is not possible to implement a CBI due to lack of functioning markets.(k) There is critical need for emergency shelters among IDP households, especially for female-headed households, in order to ensure protection, and dignified and healthy living conditions. Pregnant and lactating mothers, children below 5 and elderly persons are more exposed to and affected by environmental hazards where in lack of proper shelters.(l) Women and girls are greatly exposed to GBV when they sleep under the tree or in overcrowded shelters. Lack of women and girls’ purchasing power often make them victim of GBV, since men ask for sexual favours in exchange for goods or services (e.g. building their shelters). (m) There is need for basic NFIs among displaced population, especially for pregnant and lactating mothers, children, elderly and sick people to avoid spreading of diseases by sharing NFI such as kitchen sets among too many people.(n) Child-headed households and elderly people are often not able to live in dignity, because they are not strong enough to cater for their needs (e.g. build a shelter, collect shelter materials, etc.). In many remote areas they do not even benefit from humanitarian assistance because the distribution point is far from where they live, and the items never reach them.(o) High food needs pose a risk of reselling the NFIs and shelter material acquired from the local market in case of CBIs, or using the money for different purposes, in case of distribution of unrestricted cash vouchers.

Activities

LCED will implement three main activities: (1) in-kind distribution targeting the most vulnerable displaced households, using items from the S/NFI cluster core pipeline, including replacement of S/NFIs which have outlived their life span (2) in-kind distribution targeting the most vulnerable displaced households combining pipeline items and local shelter framing material, and (3) CBIs. In-kind distribution will be combined with distribution of WASH NFIs and hygiene promotion campaigns, while construction of emergency shelters, especially in IDP camps/collective centers, will be paired with rehabilitation of assets including water points and latrines, taking specific measures to reduce protection risks (e.g. gender-segregated latrines, provision of solar lamps, etc.) Sub-activities for 1 and 2;a) Identification of, through community consultations.b) Combined market and multi-cluster needs assessment in the targeted locations, with the involvement of locally hired enumerators, in order to identify IDPs/returnees/conflict affected population in dire need of S/NFI, areas where in-kind interventions are most needed, and where it is possible to combine NFI, WASH and FSL interventions.c) Referral to ICWG and OCHA in case of needs/gaps which LCED is not able to cover.d) Verification/registration of beneficiary HHs as per S/NFI cluster prioritization, with the assistance of the RDCs. This activity will include verification and registration of beneficiary HHs to be targeted for construction of emergency shelters, and those for the replacement of non-functional S/NFIs.e) Distribution of NFIs and emergency shelter material, combining framing material locally procured and from the pipeline supply where local procurement is an advantage and CBI is not an option, or only pipeline items where local procurement is not viable. All distributions will be combined with distribution of WASH items where needed, and will include beneficiary sensitization on the use of the items and on risk of GBV, with the assistance of protection partners.f) Construction of emergency shelters as demonstration for the most vulnerable HHs, and provision of technical support for the other HHs, in order to ensure that the shelters follow the Sphere standards. g) PDM in locations where activities were implemented, with the assistance of other S/NFI partners where applicable, following the cluster methodologies. Sub-activities for CBIs:a) Market assessment and identification of beneficiaries, suppliers and viable modality of interventions according to needs and social dynamics, through community consultations. Identification of areas where it is advisable to combine FSL and S/NFI interventions.b) Engagement of local suppliers (both for S/NFI and FLS where possible), to sign a contract with LCED, after defining a voucher package for beneficiaries, with prices.c) Distribution of restricted, unconditional cash vouchers the most vulnerable and marginalized groups, targeting female-headed households.d) PDM/market monitoring, involving community members as LCED volunteers, who will lead community consultations. Risk- Mitigation strategy (Market assessment, Mundri E/W, Oct. 2018):1) Insecurity to access the market - Involvement of RDCs for early warnings, and of local authorities to advocate for free movement and access to the market for the population.2) Increase in commodity prices resulting from cash assistance - Work with local authorities/community leaders to continuously assess the market and report any price manipulation. Engagement of local suppliers and project beneficiaries throughout the project cycle.3) Unintended use of cash provided (by men) towards marriage or alcohol - Target only women, in order to ensure that the assistance will go to their households and not other families.4) Unintended use of cash provided towards food items - Provision of restricted cash vouchers, and combination of S/NFI and FSL cash-based assistance.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The LCED field officers will collaborate with the Relief Distribution Committees (RDCs, including representatives of the most marginalized groups), local authorities and community leaders in order to share with the beneficiaries and the rest of the community information on the assistance provided, including:-information on the organization mandate, prioritization criteria and size of the intervention (including resource and access constraints) with the population at the time of the assessment/verification/community consultations, in order to manage their expectations,-information on the use of the NFIs and shelter material during the distribution, and instructions on how to use the complaint desk/feedback mechanism, and how to report eventual cases of misconduct by the LCED staff or volunteers and GBV to the relevant people/authorities. This will include informing the beneficiaries about their rights and entitlements, from the beginning of the project.-information on the physical support offered to the most vulnerable households to build their temporary shelters, and technical supervision for all other households targeted by the same intervention.-Information on lessons learnt and key actions to implement changes, conveyed by the RDCs after the PDM

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Different gender and age groups will receive different information according to the assistance they will be provided. Specifically, community sensitization on GBV, in relation to creating a safe environment for women and girls in the collective centers, will be done using gender-tailored messages for men/boys and women/girls.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

During the assessment and verification, separate FGDs of women and youth will be conducted separately from the others, in order to give space to these two categories to express their opinion, which is not possible in a mixed group, due to cultural norms. During the distribution of items, the beneficiaries will use separate queues by gender, in order to avoid harassment towards women, while the LCED volunteers will handpick the most vulnerable people including PLW, elderly persons and disabled persons, and assist them in receiving and transporting their items. PLW, elderly persons and child-headed households will be assisted not only with distribution of items, but also with transportation of the items to their place of residence, while the LCED volunteers will build emergency shelters for those targeted (other beneficiaries will only receive the shelter material and technical advice on how to build the shelters). Constructing temporary shelters will be a priority to reduce the risk of GBV towards women and girls. This will also be paired with community sensitization on GBV and creation of Relief Distribution Committees (RDCs). At least 30% of the members of the RDCs must be women, and 30% youth, in order to ensure that needs of all categories (men, women, boys and girls) are taken into consideration during the implementation of different project activities. Additionally, crowd controllers, enumerators and volunteers that LCED will hire locally should be at least 30% women, in order to ensure an income for both genders. Finally, the beneficiaries receiving cash vouchers will be only women, in order to ensure that the assistance will go to their households and not another family, considering the polygamous nature of many marriages. Also, targeting women with CBI will empower them economically, and as it has been observed in previous project, it will allow them to maintain themselves and their children without having to rely on friends and relatives.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Community consultations do not only include the community leaders, but also Focus Group Discussions with representatives of the vulnerable groups (women, youth, elderly and disable persons), in order to make sure that views, challenges and needs of such categories are addressed. Also, in each distribution location LCED will form a Relief Distribution Committee (RDC), including IDP/returnee/host community leaders, women representatives and youth representatives from the IDPs, and sub-chiefs. The RDCs provide great insight for activity planning and project design, as well as context-specific information, changes, and early warning in case of conflict.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The Relief Distribution Committees (RDCs) will work with LCED to assure that IDPs, returnees and host communities are aware of the priorities for the selection of beneficiaries for distribution, and how vulnerable households have been verified. They will also assist the LCED team in the distribution of emergency shelter material and NFIs (including a complaint desk) and they will mediate in case of discontent among the population in regards to the prioritization of the beneficiaries.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To ensure timely, efficient and effective implementation of project activities, continuous monitoring and evaluation visits will be conducted time to time with the active participation of all key stakeholders including the beneficiary communities, local leaders and S/NFI cluster monitoring team. To ensure participation of all groups, all data collected on project activities will be segregated by gender, age, vulnerability status among others. Data collection tools will be designed in a way to allow participation of all categories for example Focus Group Discussions will be done according to gender to allow women and girls express their needs freely.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project considers the protection risks for the most vulnerable groups: women and girls, children, elderly persons. Women and girls are greatly exposed to risk of GBV when they sleep under the tree or in overcrowded shelters. Related to that, the lack of women and girls’ purchasing power often make them victim of GBV, since men ask for sexual favours in exchange for goods or services (e.g building shelters, collecting framing material). Children and elderly people are also exposed to other form of violence, and they are not able to live in dignity, because they are not strong enough to cater for their needs.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

LCED considered access barriers for different population groups, and will put in place specific measures to overcome them. For instance, in many contexts of South Sudan women and youth are not included in community consultation, or are not able to express themselves in mixed groups (with men or elderly) due to cultural norms. Considering that, during the assessment, verification and PDM, separate Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with representatives of men, women, boys and girls, as well as elderly and disabled people, in order to identify their needs. The LCED will make sure to always have at least one female staff member in the field, in order to conduct FGDs with women.Furthermore, in many remote areas children and elderly people do not benefit from humanitarian assistance because the distribution point is far from where they live, and the items never reach them. Therefore, PLW, elderly persons and child-headed households will be assisted not only by distribution of items, but also with transportation of the items to their place of residence , while the LCED volunteers will build emergency shelters for those targeted (other beneficiaries will only receive the shelter material and technical advice on how to build the shelters).

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Addressing inequality and protection threats is an essential component of this project. Firstly, women might face protection threats while sleeping in the open or in overcrowded shelters. Considering that, one of the project priorities will be to ensure that vulnerable groups such as women, girls and children do not sleep in the open, in order to reduce the risk of GBV towards women and girls. Secondly, all activities will be paired with community sensitization on GBV and creation of Relief Distribution Committees (RDCs), with the aim to reduce dangerous behaviours/habits and find common strategies to reduce inequalities and protection threats within the community. Finally, the lack of women and girls’ purchasing power often make them victim of GBV, since men ask for sexual favours in exchange for goods or services. A Therefore, LCED will target women for a tailored CBI, in areas where they have access to the market.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

LCED conducted a needs assessment using the S/NFI Cluster assessment tool to identify the different S-NFI needs of the targeted population. Lists of the verified beneficiaries shall be shared with the community leaders prior distribution. During distributions of S-NFI assistance, distribution centres and modalities shall be agreed upon by all the stakeholders including the target population, local authorities and community leaders to facilitate access of all categories of people. Special categories of beneficiaries (pregnant women, lactating mothers, the elderly and the sick) will be supported to access services. M&E Tools; M&E Database, Household survey questionnaires, Focus Groups Discussion guide and observation, activity reports and PDMM&E Indicators:• % of males and females participating in community meetings• % of males and females registered as supported with S-NFI• % of beneficiaries reporting that their views were considered• % of beneficiaries participating in the design of the selection criteria• % of beneficiaries reporting that special categories were supported to access assistance

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety and security concerns of the targeted population will be jointly identified during community meetings. LCED will work closely with local authorities to protect beneficiaries of S-NFIs from any possible threat within their community. M&E Tools; Individual interviews, Key Informant questionnaires, FGDs, Reports, Complaint Feedback/suggestion box and PDMM&E Indicators:• % of beneficiaries reporting to be safe with the distributed S-NFI items• No. of males and females reporting reduction in security threats• % of beneficiaries reporting that the distribution locations were safe and secure• No. of GBV referrals

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

LCED in accordance with the AAP guidelines will ensure that project beneficiaries are treated with fairness at all times by staff and community leaders as is embedded in all project activities. LCED will ensure that priority needs of the marginalised population are identified and addressed through support/referrals. The community will be allowed to suggest possible ways of helping the marginalised groups’ access S-NFI services. Clear information about the modalities used in selection of beneficiaries and distribution of S-NFI items will be shared with the community to ensure satisfaction of beneficiaries. Lastly, the complaint feedback desk will be put in place to address any challenges encountered and also provide feedback.M&E tools; Individual interviews, Key Informant questionnaires, FGDs, Reports, Complaint Feedback/suggestion box, and PDMM&E Indicators:• % of targeted population reporting being excluded from accessing assistance• % of targeted population reporting to have observed special categories given consideration during the project activities• % of targeted population reporting that the beneficiary selection criteria was fair• % of individuals reporting that information about assessment/verification and distribution processes was clear for everyone• % of individual reporting that they were respected by staff and other community leaders• % of individuals reporting that their views, complaints and feedback were addressed• % of beneficiaries reporting that they were satisfied with LCED interventions
