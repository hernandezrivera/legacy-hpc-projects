Project Summary

UNICEF will implement 3 main activities: 1) Emergency MPCA to highly vulnerable households within one month after the crisis to address critical and essential needs to save lives; 2) provide at least 3 months of MPCA to highly vulnerable families affected by the crisis to meet their multiple basic needs to enhance safety nets and protect assets; and 3) facilitate referrals (Cash plus), that is vulnerable households with specific needs are referred to Cluster sector specific cash intervention. A fourth activity that this project contributes to is systems strengthening for the National Cash Transfer Programme drawing upon lessons learnt.

UNICEF will provide graduated cash payments to targeted households to build lessons to feed the capacity building of the national social protection system. Targeted households will receive graduate payments based on the number of children in the household. The payment value for the households will be aligned to the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (NIS 754) for activity 1 and Minimum Expenditure Basket (NIS 1,080) for activity 2 as defined by the Cash Working Group. UNICEF will also have a referral or cash plus component that will refer beneficiary households with children in need of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support to Child Protection partners for support and follow up. Beneficiary households in need of services available through the Gaza Protection Consortium (Disability Rehabilitation, MHPSS) will be referred to them and other service providers, if there is sufficient capacity, using existing cluster-based Protocols and Guidelines. UNICEF and GPC have a programmatic mutual working relationship given that they currently have received funds from the same donor where indicators between the agencies have been harmonized and they are currently working together to strengthen referrals across UNICEF and the GPC agencies hence UNICEF will capitalize on this existing relationship further to strengthen synergies
The UNICEF cash programme uses a two-pronged approach for addressing both immediate and mid-term needs of children living in poor and vulnerable households. The referral or ‘cash-plus’ approach provides immediate support to children living in poor and vulnerable households to meet their basic needs (based on gender and age requirements) as well as connecting them to a package of essential services, while the systems strengthening of the national social protection system offers a pathway to protect vulnerable households with children in the medium-to-long term but at the same time strengthening the national system to be shock responsive and adaptive. UNICEF will also make efforts to refer households with potential to available economic empowerment and livelihoods activities to ensure self-reliance by the targeted groups, which is one of the sustainable exit pathways.
The main eligibility criteria for the programme are that a household should be living below the poverty line and have children between 0 – 17 years, and should be shock or crisis impacted. The prioritization criteria will include:
o Households affected by an acute shock
o Households with children with disabilities (CwD)
o Female and Child-Headed Households
o Households with PwD which have children
o Households with chronically ill member(s) which have children
o Households with children of school going age (in and out of school)
o Households with children who are at risk of falling/sliding back into the negative side of the CSI, rCSI, FCS scales
o Households with malnourished children, lactating and pregnant mothers, at risk of malnutrition and children with nutritional deficiencies (stunted, wasted & anemic cases) located in areas with multiple deprivations.
o Households with children with protection needs, including those subjected to violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation, in particular children involved in child labor, those prone to child marriage and children with severe psychological distress.

1 - Needs

The Child Sensitive Emergency Multi-Purpose Cash-Plus & Shock Responsive Social Protection Strengthening Programme in the State of Palestine will be an innovative integrated programme to address shock/crisis, economic and social vulnerabilities of children and their families. The programme will provide a lifeline for vulnerable families with children through emergency cash transfers to meet basic needs; offer cash-plus referrals and linkages with information, knowledge and services on health and nutrition, and child protection; and strengthen the shock responsiveness and inclusiveness of the national social protection system through capacity building and systems strengthening. Childhood poverty manifests in different forms, including a lack of access to quality health; inadequate water, sanitation and hygiene; education; poor nutrition; and association with violence and neglect. As such many households are pushed to negative coping strategies such as using poorer food substitutes; selling assets; removing children from school; and child labor. UNICEF’s Child Sensitive Emergency Multi-Purpose cash transfer programming will tie into other programme objectives to support overcoming other childhood deprivations such as malnutrition, child labor and enrolment of children in early childhood education in emergency situations. Due to the on-going conflict, occupation and restricted mobility in some parts of SoP, jobs and entrepreneurship opportunities are limited. In this situation, monetary and multi-dimensional poverty continue to impact the survival, development and protection of children, adolescents and women. Hence UNICEF’s referrals under the cash-plus component seeks to link targeted groups with income generation opportunities. Prior to the COVID-19 crisis, both poverty and extreme poverty had increased throughout the State of Palestine since 2011, particularly in the Gaza Strip. The COVID-19 pandemic and the May 2021 escalation in the Gaza Strip are believed to have caused significant increases in household vulnerability. The national poverty rate was at 29 percent in 2017 and rose to 31 percent in 2020. Around 16.8 percent of the population was living in deep poverty in 2017. The escalations in the Gaza Strip have resulted in worse social conditions in the Gaza Strip. The World Bank estimates suggest that the 2021 escalation, alongside the protracted crisis, has pushed poverty in Gaza Strip to 59 percent in 2021 (using US$5.50 a day (2011 PPP) international poverty line). Poverty rates are higher among larger households, in 2017 poverty rates for households with no children were 23 percent, reaching 56 percent for households with nine or more children. The national multi-dimensional poverty incidence in 2016/2017 was 24 percent, with 45 percent in the Gaza Strip. Child multi-dimensional poverty incidence in 2021 (cc-MODA) was found to be at 32 percent of children in the State of Palestine, with household wealth having the highest association with child multidimensional poverty with over 60 percent of children in the poorest wealth quintile also multidimensionally poor. Another important indicator illustrating child well-being and social inequalities is stunting (nutrition), with 8.7 percent of children in the State of Palestine stunted in 2019/20 . Stunting rose from 7.4% in 2014. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) or wasting also affects 1.3 percent of children in the State of Palestine in 2019/20. Wasting rose from 1.2 percent in 2014. The level of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rose from 0.3% of children under five in 2014 to 0.6% in 2019/20. Wasting is the most immediate, visible and life-threatening form of malnutrition. It results from the failure to prevent malnutrition among the most vulnerable children coupled with inadequate water, sanitation and hygiene. Operationally, UNICEF will also draw upon partners’ data on children attending health facilities for treatment of stunting or wasting in the Gaza Strip. This complex humanitarian crisis coupled with structural needs will contribute to further worsen the socioeconomic conditions of the affected population, especially the most vulnerable groups: women-headed households, persons with disabilities, refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), people affected by conflict-related violence and those already living below the poverty line. Many households in the Gaza Strip (32 per cent) reported in 2019/20 receiving more than one type of social protection benefit, reflecting the high levels and possibly multiple forms of deprivations suffered, which qualify households for benefits. However, as much as there are always several social protection programmes implemented by various actors, they are not specific to vulnerable households with children hence UNICEF’s focus to a child sensitive emergency multi-purpose cash transfer approach. The current crisis is noticeable in the increased demand for Child Protection and Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) services. Following the outbreak of the pandemic in Palestine there was an increase of demand for services to respond to children experiencing violence at home1. Data documented by the Sawa counselling helpline, reveals an increase in violence in the wake of the escalations and the pandemic including SGBV, violence against children (VAC), sexual exploitation and abuse (SEAH) and violence against women (VAW). Information from more recent assessments undertaken by the GBV sub-cluster member organizations, as well as data drawn from programme and service delivery, confirms that women and girls in the State of Palestine face widespread and varied risks of GBV. Different sources reviewed after the 2021 May escalation found that, based on preliminary data and lessons learnt from previous escalations, hostilities exacerbated gender-specific risks and vulnerabilities and resulted in a higher scale of humanitarian needs among women, girls, men and boys in Gaza.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will have three main activities: child sensitive multipurpose cash transfers within one month of the emergency; 3 months of MPCA to highly vulnerable families affected by the crisis to meet their multiple basic needs to enhance safety nets; and protect assets, and referrals or Cash-plus component. The communities affected will be engaged in identifying their referral needs and activities for onward support by relevant service providers. The referral needs identification will be done through engagement all socio-economic groups at community level during various project monitoring activities and the sampling for engagement will ensure that all groups are fairly represented in the samples. Activity 1: Emergency MPCA to highly vulnerable household within one month after the crisis to address critical and essential needs to save lives UNICEF plans to support 3,000 girls and boys in shock affected households with multi-purpose cash grants to help them meet their gender and age sensitive essential needs. The cash transfer value for the project will be determined from the CWG SMEB of NIS 754. The transfer values will be different per household since it will be determined by number of children between 0<17 years in household. This will be disbursed within the first month of the emergency as a first line of response. Based on the lessons learnt during UNICEF’s Phase 1 of cash transfers, UNICEF will widen scope of household categories for different amounts so that transfers are proportional to dependency ratio. The proposed categories are in line with the MoSD household categorization of NCTP program. The amounts will be reviewed and rounded off (in NIS) so as to ease process of cash out since getting smaller denomination bills can be a challenge. UNICEF is planning to continue using mobile cash token modality to pay beneficiaries owing to positive feedback from beneficiaries, ease of business process, and compliance with UNICEF’s internal controls. However, UNICEF will monitor the Financial Service Provider performance and beneficiary feedback to determine the suitability of the FSP and the modality. If there are serious challenges in meeting programme objectives with the proposed modality, UNICEF would consider changing the modality and FSP. Activity 2: As a second Line Response and depending on socio-economic vulnerability, UNICEF will provide at least 3 months of MPCA to highly vulnerable families with children affected by crisis to meet their multiple basic needs to enhance safety nets and protect assets. The transfer values will be based on the GCWG MEB of NIS 1,080 calculated against the number of children in household. UNICEF will coordinate with MoSD and all cash actors through the National Cash Working Group and GCWG and the West Bank to avoid double dipping of beneficiaries and ensuring harmonization of transfer values. Beneficiaries' lists will be shared and compared using, following standard data sharing protocols across agencies. Beneficiaries’ consent will be sought at registration stages and verification exercises to ensure confidentiality and privacy is maintained. As part of the Grievance Redressal Mechanism (GRM), the Inter Agency GRM hotline will be used as well as UNICEF’s GRM platform and SAWA hotline which is focused on cases of Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). These platforms are set up for beneficiary households with opportunities to provide unsolicited feedback and seek advice and support. Information on how to use these platforms will be communicated to beneficiary households through mobile SMSs and flyers prior to start of the project. Activity 3: referrals (Cash plus ) for vulnerable households with specific needs to cluster specific cash interventions, e.g. health, food, protection and social protection to include Nexus related programming, (that is boys and girls benefiting from cash-plus activities/referrals such as Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). UNICEF’s cash-plus approach ensures that targeted children and their families are able to benefit not just from the cash grant but a package of essential gender and age sensitive services including nutrition, child protection, PSEA and awareness sessions. Under activity 3, UNICEF plans to use social and behavioral change communication strategies to focus on key messages around health, nutrition, education, child protection, PSEA and WASH. For children identified with these vulnerabilities either individually or at the household level through verification, PDM or case management follow up, UNICEF will support counselling on nutrition and health (covering issues such as dietary diversity, the importance of breastfeeding, maternal nutrition, and vitamin supplementation, hygiene practices); child protection (to prevent violence at home and in communities); and awareness of PSEA. UNICEF with partners during monitoring activities will identify families and children with referral needs and provide direct support. The plan is also to link targeted children and their families with other UNICEF ongoing interventions within the same localities to ensure that they are able to receive multi-sectoral support. The target population for activities 1, 2 and 3 are the same. However, the specific referral services and support for MHPSS would be guided by the needs of children in each family. These needs would be identified during beneficiary verification exercise at the start of the response program and referred using standard Protocols and Guidelines. Services which UNICEF partners do not provide such as for medical support, disability rehabilitation or legal advice may be referred to non-UNICEF partners such as through the partners who are members of the Protection Cluster and/ or Gaza Protection Consortium. These referrals will benefit from the complementary work under ECHO grant to get data protection agreements in place for referrals and to be guided by protocols and guidelines.

3 - Indicators

The child sensitive emergency multi-purpose cash programming will be monitored through Grievance Redress Mechanism, Baseline Survey, Payment Verifications, and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), and End Line survey with the targeted households leading into a lessons learnt event. The UNICEF monitoring tools are designed in a way which enable effective, meaningful and mainstreamed participation of elderly, people with disabilities, women, men, girls and boys in the entire program implementation and monitoring processes. The various monitoring processes are as outlined below, these indicators include the Gaza CWG standard indicators and UNICEF specific indicators. 1. Grievance Redress Mechanisms/ Complaints Feedback Mechanism (CFM): This is a continued usage and management of already existing CFM structures in place for UNICEF current actions so as to enable a two-way communication between project participants and the project team. Currently UNICEF uses three different platforms which include: a partner CFM, InterAgency Hotline and the UNICEF State of Palestine email. For PSEA cases and related issues UNICEF uses a dedicated PSEA Sawa hotline and these will continue to be used. 2. Baseline survey: This is going to inform the current households status and aid in deciding benchmarks or targets for the project. 3. Household verifications: All registered households to be targeted by the response will be verified 100% by UNICEF and its third-party monitoring service provider so as to ensure that risks of exclusion and inclusion are mitigated. This is also a fraud risk mitigation measure for potential ghost households. 4. Payments Verifications: This is an exercise aimed at ensuring that 100% or a certain agreed proportion of the households are called and visited to verify if they have received their cash entitlements in correct amounts. This is also a fraud risk mitigation measure for potential ghost households receiving payments. 5. Post-distribution monitoring (PDM): This is aimed at informing program in terms of do no harm (DNH), cash usage and prioritization, household satisfaction, cash adequacy, access to basic needs, household spending per sector versus the MEB calibrations as well as identifying areas of improvement for the program. 6. End line: This will be aimed at measuring the impact of the action interventions and would be done at the end of the action cycle, at least a month after the action is completed. The results will be compared against the baseline. 7. Lessons learnt event: This is key in informing new program designing, ongoing programs and other cash actors, including MOSD, on good and bad practices regarding multipurpose cash transfer. The topics to be covered will include looking at the entire project cycle answering the following: What went on well? What were the challenges encountered? What needs improvement? What could have been done differently? What lessons were learnt? Any innovation opportunities? Results of the lessons learnt will be disseminated at working group forums, such as the GCWG, national cash working group and the Social Protection, Cash and Voucher Assistance Working Group. A lessons learnt document will be produced and shared with other actors, donors and the MoSD. The proposed various monitoring activities above are aimed at mitigating various risks which among them include fraud, diversion, ghost beneficiaries and misuse of the cash entitlements at the expense of deserving targeted children. Overall, UNICEF will use GCWG standards and its own internal indicators below to measure the project success: Activity 1 and 2• % of households supported with emergency multi-purpose cash transfers who report being able to meet their basic needs and lifesaving services as they define and prioritize them (all/most/half/some/none)• % of targeted households who have reduced Coping Strategy Index (rCSI) as a result of provided cash• % of households by Livelihoods Coping Strategies (LCS) phase (Neutral, Stress, Crisis,Emergency)• % of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age, and disability) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner• Total monthly expenditures by sector relative to MEB sectoral components/amounts• Number of households reached by cash transfers through UNICEF supported programmes• % of the target population with acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS)• # of highly vulnerable HHs in oPt affected by shocks and stresses due to crisis and natural disaster receive at least 3 months of MPCA to address their multiple pressing needs and decrease the incidence of negative coping strategies Activity 3• # of highly vulnerable households /individuals in SOP –have access to referrals pathways for sectoral needs, social protection, including Nexus and development programming.• % of beneficiaries referred report having received satisfactory services as they define them (very satisfied/satisfied/not satisfied)• # of households reached through Social Behavior & Change education and awareness raising sessions on various themes (health, nutrition, education, child protection, PSEA and WASH)

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The provision of cash empowers the affected population to decide on their own how to meet their needs using available local market facilities. It also complements the provision of in-kind assistance and services delivery during emergencies. Cash modality enables the right to choice, it is dignified, boost the local economy, it is deliverable timely and at low cost hence value for money as opposed to other modalities. Overall, markets in Gaza strip are fully functional hence cash is noble to support these markets and a good practice towards market based approach strengthening in emergencies and early recovery initiatives.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Beneficiaries will receive child sensitive emergency multi-purpose cash assistance through a self-managed SMS. The beneficiary will have control over how they access their cash. The provision of Emergency Child Sensitive Multi-Purpose Cash provides a dignified response modality. The modality chosen is accessible within walking distance by targeted population and these are cash out retails or merchants where the entire community do regular purchases within their communities. The cash out process is discrete since the platforms being used are used by any other community people hence does not expose the affected population to risks as opposed to in-kind distribution which is visible and affected people are gathered in crowds. By virtue of its discreteness and affording the right to choices and decisions to prioritize, cash modalities gives the affected people the dignity, in-kind support would otherwise not afford hence no harm. The fact that it is a market-based approach, also promotes local markets and mitigates harm which comes with other modalities. As part of the entire cash monitoring process, UNICEF asks questions relating to identifying risks and any harm which may result from the project be it conflict at community and household level, market distortions, safety and security issues so as to ensure that redress actions are taken based on the risk matrix and mitigation measures in place. All monitoring tools and activities will be undertaken with “do no harm” lens so as to ensure safe programming.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will target households who would have been shock or crisis affected and they are vulnerable households with children. UNICEF’s targeting is impartial and non-discriminatory and does independent monitoring processes including household vulnerability verifications so as to ensure that most vulnerable and people in need are targeted and included in the humanitarian cash assistance.
