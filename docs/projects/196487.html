Project Summary

Silk Road seeks to increase the food production to finding solutions supporting the agricultural and livestock products where 151500 direct beneficiaries will be tragted through our FS interventions. In terms of the livestock support projects, cow, and sheep breeders will be supported to create a dairy value chain that starts from the milk production till the stage of marketing for the final products in the local market. the cow and sheep breeders will be technically supported. Fodders will also be purchased to be distributed to the cow and sheep breeders. There will be another support for dairy product manufacturing, by providing small grants with vocational training to the women who produce yogurt and cheese. The last step of the dairy value chain support is providing plans and facilities to market the final product by establishing local selling points in the targeted communities. The products will be freely distributed through the selling points, to pave the way to move to the selling phase. In terms of agricultural support. We seek to support vegetables, lentil, barley, wheat, and olives farmers in Idleb, and Aleppo country sides with agricultural inputs.
Basically the seclection critera for the beneficiary farmers comes as follow:
2. Have access to land with 4000 M² -15000 M² (the priority for smaller owners).
3. Families headed by women.
4. Families have people with disabilities.
5. Families with children under 5 years old.
6. Families with large size above six members with low income.
6. Families with elders above 60 years old.
Silk Road will enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuate discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation.
All project staff and stakeholders will share an ethical responsibility for mainstreaming protection across the humanitarian response, including general and sector staff, programming staff, advocacy staff, design, monitoring and evaluation staff, and their managers.
The priority will be given for the following categories:
- IDPs families with the focus on IDPs Families headed by women.
- Women/Boys-headed households.
- Families with large size above six members and do not have a breadwinner.
- Silk Road will take into consideration girls, separated or unaccompanied girls and boys, orphans, women heads of household, lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer/questioning (LGBTQ), and people with disabilities.
As well as the criteria will focus on GBV survivors and Women and girls at risk of GBV:
Women/girls head of households, Women/girls with physical/mental disabilities, Widows, Divorcees, Pregnant and lactating women/girls, Survivor of torture (e.g. in detention), and Unaccompanied or separated girls.
These inputs are seeds, fertilizers, land ploughing, pesticides, and yield mechanical harvesting. We have a plan to activate the wheat and olive value chains. As the wheat value chain starts by purchasing the wheats from the farmers directly to benefit from the added value. Technical support will be provided also through the farmer's school establishment, besides that field visits will be conducted by our technical agricultural expert team to follow up the case of the crop with the farmers step by step. Agricultural brochures also will be distributed to the farmers to raise awareness and guide them to the correct scientific methods which must be applied. Projects will support livelihoods in the targeted areas, by providing cash for work activities.
Furthermore, the IDPs in the targeted locations will receive a ration of the final production by taking a part of the total production from the targeted farmers according to agreement signed with them, those amounts of agricultural products will be distributed for the most affected people in the communities and camps in northwestern Syria areas.

3 - Needs

According to 2023 HNO overview 15.3 M out of 22.1 M of Syrian population are in need for the humanitarin aid, in other words, almost seven out of ten people in Syria need assistance. All the population groups identified in the 2023 HNO are worse off in 2023 than in 2022, here the need for the humanitarian responce clearly appares . The agriculture sector, which was once a cornerstone of the Syrian economy, has also been severely impacted. Silk Road had conducted Needs Assessment in 33 communities many people are facing shortages in food items in the assessed locations due to lack of money to buy food and the high price of food when available. The available food in these locations come either from markets or lending food to IDPs from markets or host communities. In case of food access difficulties, IDPs can cope this challenge by reducing the numbers of meals eaten in a day. The main sources of income for families are Agriculture and livestock breeding, and for the displaced families is daily working and Aid. Where people declared that agriculture consider main source of income in this area. Regarding theAgriculture situation the most important crops grown in the covered communities are 44% wheat, 15% potato, 10% barley, 8% winter vegetables, 6% summer vegetables, 5% bean, 4% beas, and 3% chickpeas and lentils. The number of the livestock in the targeted area decreased about 20% comparing with the last year because of high prices of the fooder and the vaternarian medicins .

4 - Implementation of activities

Regarding livestock project - Purchase and distribute the fodder to the cow and sheep breeders where we are palning to target 550 breeders- Purchase and distribute the group of vitamins, medicine required for the cattle health in parallel with the technical support - Support marketing the dairy production produced by the targeted women by establishing celling points and creating a special brand for the dairy products the livestock support activity will be in Alrouj plane where the number of livestock is very high there. Starting by distributing those products freely to the most vulnerable people in the first stage to secure smooth transferring for the selling stage later. - Building the capacity of the local council in the targeted community: this will be achieved by technical training courses in the targeted location. As the agricultural projects will be implemented as the following. - Purchase and distribute of seeds for the farmers high quality Hybrid seeds will be distributed to farmers by in-kind distribution modality for seeds. - Distribution the pesticides (insecticides- fungicides) and agricultural fertilizers (Organic fertilizers-urea and compound fertilizers - amino acids). - Farmers will be supported also by lands ploughing for two times. - Farmers will be supported with yields harvesting services by hiring daily workers. this kind of activities will be implemented in Azaz, Harim amd Idleb distrcts where we are planing to support 2200 farmers in the targeted areas.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

We assume that the security situation is stable in the targeted areas but the measures will be taken in case of risks as the following: 1- Prevalence of infection with COVID 19 virus in the targeted areas: 1-1 Provide the field teams with the safety equipment (Masks, gloves, personal hand sterilizers) 1-2 Door to door distribution of the agricultural inputs. 1- 3 in case of activities needs people gatherings like field days we will following measures: - Emphasis will be placed on maintaining social distance with a constant awareness of it. - Providing personal safety equipment such as masks and rubber gloves. 2: Corruption in procurement by employees, suppliers or contractors or change in required specifications. Mitigation Measures: 2-1 Application of procurement policies and procedures 2-2 Continuous follow-up of procurement 2-3 Check before receiving 2-4 Establishment of a Procurement Committee 2-5 Using Goods/services received notes 3- Increased armed conflict due to increased tensions between different groups. This could delay, pause, or suspend programming.Mitigation Measures: If this reaches Silk Road’s project towns, they will pause activities and wait until the situation on the ground becomes safer. 4: Target areas are inaccessible or insecure because of the advance of armed groups and/or aerial shelling. Mitigation Measures: 4-1 Ensure there is a process established that allows consulting the communities.
