Project Summary

"The Oxfam developed the project proposal in line with its global and country vision. This proposal also has developed following the JRP 2024 objectives (#3 & 5) as well as SCCCM sector objectives # 04: Provide life-saving shelter, NFIs and LPG support to households affected by disasters and other sudden onset events and ensure emergency preparedness. The prioritization of activities and targeted beneficiaries is based on the priorities expressed by the community, protection monitoring data, MSNA, assessment report and insights from Oxfam field teams. During the project period Oxfam will ensure smooth coordination with the GoB, SCCCM sector including other appealing and implementing partners at sector and camp level.
Becoming an active member of Energy and Environment Network, Oxfam will ensure its intervention following the guidance of EEN network. To enhance coordination between Oxfam Protection team and EEN, Oxfam will ensure the following steps. Firstly, a dedicated staff under protection who will be responsible to facilitate regular exchange of information and updates among Oxfam protection team and ENN. By attending regular meetings, workshops, and conferences arrange by EEN and discuss on shared goals, challenges, and innovative solutions. Oxfam will ensure submission of sector introduced report like 4W and if any other. Through the project we are targeting to achieve the following outcomes as mentioned below.
1. Strengthen the capacity of the inclusive and integrated community-based solar light repairing and maintenance on street solar light maintenance and repairing.
2. Enhance the technical skills on repairing and maintenance of solar lights facilitating on hand training.
3. Reduce the protection risk at night specially for girls and women’s.
4. Ensure safer use of WASH facilities and a safer movement at night specially for girls and women including persons with special needs.
Oxfam has installed 747-unit communal solar lights in camp 3,4,12,19 and 22 including host community Hnila and Whykong union. We would like to continue our intervention in those same area accordingly. Through the project 20 Rohingya community people per camp a total 100 will be train up on solar light repairing and maintenance. Oxfam will provide all the logistical/ spare parts support to make the non-functional/partial functional and or missing lights. As a result 49,786 refugee community and 7500 host community people will be benefited."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"The prioritization of activities is based on the priorities expressed by the community, protection monitoring data, MSNA, assessment report and insights from Oxfam field teams.Now in its sixth year, the protracted crisis is a protection crisis with over 931,574 Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar. The JMSNA’23 by the ISCG highlights protection concerns as a top priority in the refugee camps (48%). The stateless Rohingya live in extremely overcrowded camps. Protection risk (60%) as top priority in the refugee camps compared to host (33%). The Concerns included crime and violence (48%), potential affiliation with criminal groups (25%) and restriction of movement (25%). 37% of refugee households reported feeling unsafe after dark; 50% of hosts reported feeling very safe compared to refugees 22%. JMSNA report). The main reasons include presence of criminal groups and unfunctional solar light, only 48% are fully functional, 13% partially and 30% non-functional and 10% missing (EEN report), A 13% decrease from 2022 to 2023 in female family members feeling safe at night using communal latrines (WASH Sector). During dark times and if there are no lights, some men are involved in locking the latrine doors while women and girls are inside and take advantage of the situation to commit gender-based violence. (The Joint Protection Monitoring Report (Protection Sector, April-June 2023).Site Management and Energy & Environment Sector are limited in repairing non-functional SSLs. The care and monitoring of the lights require a community-led approach to develop shared responsibility and co-ownership of community facilities like solar lights. Considering the above mentioned needs and priority Oxfam propose the intervention. "

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"The project will prioritize the protection of affected populations and contributing to protection outcomes in humanitarian actions through preventing, minimizing and or mitigate the consequences of protection risks. By designing and implementing all activities, will consider protection risks and potential violations and maximize the protective impact of aid programming. The Oxfam will continue to build the capacity of IPs to prioritize rights, safety and dignity and avoid causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation and empowerment throughout the project. A continuous assessment of the project activities, services, plans, monitoring, and evaluation will conduct with protection mainstreaming lenses to identify existing limitations and gaps of the program and effective plan to overcome identified limitations. Regular review of protection mainstreaming will help the program to identify the progress, changes, new opportunities, possibilities of intentional and unintentional harm etc. and take necessary actions to minimize the negative impact of the program, maximize positive impact and mitigate the limitations and gaps including possibility of causing harm through program cycle and activities. Oxfam’s protection mainstreaming is ultimately about rights-based, reliable, and quality humanitarian programming that considers age, sex, disability, specific needs, and cultural appropriateness throughout the project. Regular community consultations, risk assessments men, women, boys, and girls, including PWD, diverse persons to identify and mitigate risks, and ensure safety, dignity, and rights."
