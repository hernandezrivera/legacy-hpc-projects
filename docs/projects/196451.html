Project Summary

WWGVC plans to intervene in Aleppo, Deir-Ez-Zor, and Ar-Raqqa governorates restoring community
services in rural and underserved areas. In line with the WASH sector's objectives, the interventions identified focus on resuming the supply of sufficient and safe water to 109,000 individuals in 2023, reducing the impacts of the financial burden of purchasing trucked water, and the health concerns associated with unmonitored water sources for domestic use amidst the recent cholera/AWD outbreak. WWGVC will coordinate closely with the local water authority of each governorate to assess the infrastructure damage and the possibility of rehabilitation, solarization, and resuming operations. Detailed assessments will also be conducted prior to the prioritization and selection of locations. The assessment will develop a baseline regarding the status of WASH infrastructure and services, the severity of its impact, and the presence of vulnerable individuals that will inform the prioritization decision at the community level.
Water systems in the targeted areas are either out of service or partially functioning with a limited capacity of regular maintenance. The improvement in service provision is achieved by the light rehabilitation of pumping stations, provision and repair of electromechanical equipment, rehabilitation of boreholes, and decentralized water networks rehabilitation ensuring water delivery to the affected population. These activities will ensure water systems are operational in all the targeted communities of the project, delivering sufficient water to the households connected to the water networks. Moreover, WWGVC will also support the Water Boards in the targeted governorates on the operations of pumping stations through alternative power sources to diesel. This will be achieved through the polarization of pumping centers, or connection to a nearby grid where feasible and efficient. In parallel, WWGVC will work closely with the Water Boards of each governorate to build their capacity on water systems management and water safety planning, including training of technical staff and pumping stations operators, along with the provision of equipment, tools, and consumables for an improved and more efficient maintenance of water systems and monitoring of water quality. The above hard component activities will be integrated with awareness campaigns on hygiene promotion (HP) including awareness on cholera prevention to all the targeted communities, associated with the distribution of WASH NFIs to 41,000 individuals or 8,200 vulnerable households.
The project activities are targeting needs that are similar across all the subdistricts targetted in all three governorates. These include, but not limited to: 1. Damaged pumping stations and catchment areas of water sources prohibiting collection and delivery of water through the networks. 2. Damaged water networks reducing the water system's efficiency in delivering the needed amount of water collected. 3. Low efficiency of water systems due to lack of power by the water authorities. 4. Inability to afford hygiene items for the most vulnerable populations increasing the threat of spread of water-borne diseases. 5. Reduced awareness and care towards proper hygiene practices caused by the worsening living conditions and limited access to water.
The project will be implemented directly by WWGVC field staff in the selected governorates, in coordination with the relevant stakeholders including the WASH sector, SARC, Ministry of Water Resources, and Ministry of Education.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, 13.55 million people are in need of WASH assistance in Syriarecording a 2.6% increase from 2022. 52% of the population relies on unsafe alternative water supply modalities. Lack of power supply is significantly reducing the functionality of water systems. This is the situation in the targeted subdistrict of Atareb (WASH severity 4), Daret Azza(4), Jebel Saman(2), Menbij(3), and Tal Refaat(3) in Aleppo, Ashara(3) in Deir Ez Zor, and Sabka(3) and Maadan(3) in Raqqa. Pumping stations are available, but they lack enough power to deliver water to the beneficiaries. Similarly, in other areas, water systems still lack maintenance and have incurred damages during the conflict. Support to the rehabilitation of water systems is still a high need in Aleppo (Al Khafsa(3) an As-Safira(2), Khanaser(3) and Menbij(3)), and Deir Ez Zor (AL Jalaa(4) and Tabni(2)). While all the areas targeted are in need of network rehabilitation to repair the damages and reduce the leakages that are causing the inability of water pressure to be maintained and there delivered to the beneficiaries. In addition, the overall deterioration of water quality and quantity paralleled by very poor sanitation conditions, lack of awareness and bad hygiene practices are directly contributing to the steep increase of public health risks, as demonstrated by the recent outbreak of AWD/Cholera, keeping hygiene promotion and distribution of WASH NFIs a priority.

4 - Implementation of activities

The implementation of activities will be done by WWGVC Field teams in each governorate in areas under GoS control. A:1 Coordination with the local stakeholders, SARC, line ministries, and the WASH sector will be undergone and approvals to be obtained. A2: Light rehabilitation and repair of water pumping stations including testing of water source, the rehabilitation of structural elements and the supply and installation of electromechanical equipment including pumps, power generators, electric panels, and other accessories.A3: Decentralized water network repair targeting areas with recently rehabilitated/equipped pumping stations.A4: Equipping small to medium pumping stations and boreholes with power-generating solar panels.A5: Capacity building of water board technical staff and pumping station operator on water systems design, operations, maintenance, and water quality, in addition to the provision of tools, equipment, and consumables.A6: Conduction of hygiene promotion campaign and awareness on water-borne diseases - including cholera - in addition to water conservation and safe water management through mass media, FGDs and street theater, associated with the distribution of WASH NFIs.A10: Monitoring, evaluation, and learning: all activities will be implemented through local contractors and monitored by WWGVC technical staff. Baseline and engline assessments will be conducted to measure impact, as well as post intervention monitoring conducted by WWGVC MEAL staff.

Risk: GBV/PSEA. Mitigation measures: All staff is aware of and trained on the code of conduct. Regular training sessions is done with staff. Sensitize the community about PSEA and how to report misconduct. Allegations of PSEAH cases are received, addressed, and responded to according to an internal system respectful of safety and confidentiality principles. In addition, WWGVC takes proactive measures at every stage of project implementation to avoid causing harm inadvertently whilst mainstreaming best practices of safe programming.
