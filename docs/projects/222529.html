Project Summary

In this Appeal, Community Initiative for Development Organization (CIDO) proposes an annual integrated emergency health response in Akobo & Nyirol Counties of Jonglei and Twic county of Warrap States. This aims to accelerate comprehensive integrated emergency health response that is mindful of needs of the most vulnerable populations including Returnees, IDP, PWD, women, men, and children while improving equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations through 3 health mobile clinics and 2 static health facilities (i.e. 1 PHCC & 1 PHCU). Total of 98,217 people (28,482 men; 23,572 women; 23,572 boys & 22,590 girls) including PWD costing 1,200,000 USD are targeted with considerations underpinning the design of this response of choice of locations where gaps are evident acknowledging a targeting criterion that is responsive to the needs of the most vulnerable groups. We will consider integration with other clusters such WASH, Protection, Nutrition and Food Security & Livelihood while ensuring effective coordination with health cluster and Ministry of Health at all levels ranging from national, State and county to maximize efficiency and impact. This will deliver essential health services as per the national standards centered on the context epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, seasonality and trauma related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, response and mitigation of activities responding to people in need, with attention to access for vulnerable population such as women, children as well as people with special needs will be ensured. The response aligns with Health Cluster Strategic objectives CO1, CO2 & CO3: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations, reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases and enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. Specifically, the response will deploy, 3 mobile health clinics. One (1) mobile clinic in Akobo East, 1 in Nyirol of Jonglei while 1 in Twic county of Warrap States and 2 Static health facilities (1 PHCC and 1 PHCU both in Akobo East and West of Jonglei). CIDO will collaborate with development partners and other national health programs to transit from emergency aid to sustainable, routine health services, aligning with the humanitarian-development-peace nexus framework aimed at building a resilient health system capable of meeting both immediate and long-term health needs in line with South Sudan National health policy 2016-2026 and 2023-2027 health sector strategic plan. Efforts to engage communities and existing health partners and key stakeholders will be mounted through planning and organization of emergency health services to be entrenched in the cluster coordination mechanism at all levels to enable them support. CIDO will deploy and capacitate qualified health workers to foster key priorities for 2025 that includes expanding essential primary healthcare, improving immunization, prepositioning medical supplies, detecting and responding to disease outbreaks, enhancing referral services, implementing health cluster GBV action plan and supporting the South Sudan MHPSS Strategic Plan 2024–2028. The AAP mechanisms will be adhered to, to enable beneficiaries provide feedback within the response catchment and easy Communication and Community engagement. CIDO will optimize its 10 years’ experience to provide humanitarian services, technical expertise and exploit its capacity

1. Needs

The current health landscape is grim, with malaria standing as the leading cause of death and illness, affecting half of the population. Low measles vaccination rates lead to frequent outbreaks, and cholera and Hepatitis E cases persist. Infant and child mortality is high, compounded by the 2022 floods, which affected over 900,000 people and displaced 140,000 across 29 counties. In 2024, Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) estimates that 9 million people need humanitarian assistance. Out of these, 6.3 million need health services, with the health cluster targeting 3.2 million while CIDO proposing to reach 98,217 individuals. The case load is attributed to multiple emergencies, including disease outbreaks, floods, and conflict-related displacements. Furthermore, in the proposed response locations, poor access to health services, a limited number of health workers and lack of access to health services have contributed to the worst health indicators trend in the world, with a child mortality rate of 78 deaths per 1,000 live births. Around 75 per cent of all child deaths in South Sudan are due to preventable diseases, such as diarrhea, malaria and pneumonia. Akobo, Nyirol and Twic North counties apparently aren’t left out in the current statics indicating that South Sudan has a maternal mortality ratio of over 1,223 per 100,000 live births – one of the world's highest maternal mortality ratios (MMR) (MMEIG, 2022). A shortage of human resources and skills, referral constraints, financial constraints, challenges with availability and last-mile distribution of supplies, floods, and insecurity characterizes the weak health system at both facility and community levels. Many of the health tragedies in the projected locations occurs because children lack access to quality health care, vaccinations, proper food, and clean water and sanitation. The many deaths of children are attributed to minimal efforts to address preventable communicable and infectious diseases. Fragile and conflict-affected situations in the response locations further complicate the survival of children. The estimated 40% of the under-five deaths occurring during the first month of life reflects the high risks associated with delivery and the immediate post-delivery period for both the mothers and the new-borns. Low birthweight, pre-term or small for gestational age, birth asphyxia, perinatal and neonatal infections. South Sudan is also facing acute food insecurity; integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projected that over half of South Sudan’s population experiences high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC phase 3 or worse) according to the projection for April to July 2024, including 2.366 million people in IPC phase 4 (emergency) and 79 000 people in IPC phase 5 (catastrophe). Notably, in the ongoing health response by CIDO in Akobo East, indicates that Malaria, AWD and RTI are the leading morbidities hence need for accelerated efforts to address the trend. As we speak, influx of returnees are still experienced. CIDO will utilize its operational and static presence in Akobo, Nyirol and Twic Counties with field offices where it is a health implementing partner providing primary health care services through rotational mobile health clinic, Food security & Livelihood, nutrition sensitive advocacy programming and providing shelter nonfood Items services to reduce morbidity and mortality among the vulnerable populations. This immense static presence and experience with community mobilisers and vaccinators already present in the 3 counties supported by CIDO will be exploited to ensure equitable access to quality health services in the response catchment area offered to the vulnerable population. CIDO will invest strongly in collaboration with clusters such as WASH, FSL, protection, and static nutrition partners in the respective counties to enhance quality integrated health services both at community and facility level

2. Project activities

The following activities will be undertaken through the response period: - Conduct OPD consultation, Conduct Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities, Clinical Management of Rape, Ensure community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities, Carry out Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response, Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population. Conducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support
