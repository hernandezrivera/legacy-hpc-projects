Project Summary

The project will improve living standards, strengthen accountable life-saving service delivery, providing a path for durable solutions for conflict-affected IDPs in sites, site-like settings and host communities through a comprehensive CCCM response. By scaling up on-site and mobile approaches, supporting advocacy, service monitoring, community engagement, and site development will ensure dignified living conditions.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

IOM CCCM will target 172,500 people in need through a comprehensive CCCM response that encompasses site planning and improvement, community engagement, information management, coordination, and capacity building. Through its expertise in site design and development, IOM has led several site assessments across government-identified areas to establish service areas with improved living conditions for displaced communities. IOM will expand development of sites that meet improved standards, while also capacitating local governance structures, partners, and government counterparts to ensure a principled and durable response. Through community engagement and community participation, the project will support governance structures in sites and site-like settings to promote meaningful participation and inclusiveness, equipping these structures to support the community and service providers while ensuring protection and social cohesion. Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms will be established in line with protection principles and in consultation with communities to provide feedback within a reasonable timeframe. In hard-to-reach areas or in areas of new displacement, mobile teams will provide coordination support and advocacy, capacity building of community self-management structures as well as support reception of new arrivals. As CCCM Cluster lead agency, IOM will also assume the responsibility of CCCM provider of last resort. IOM will engage in regular CCCM partner and service provider meetings to ensure response consistency across sites and between actors.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Output 1: Support pre-existing governance structures and create Community Committees to ensure needs highlighted by IDPs – particularly those traditionally underserved – inform the work of humanitarian actors in the site/area to avoid duplication of services.Utilize a variety of communication methods to engage all sectors of the community, following an analysis of access to information sources.Provide support to site/area led initiatives to engage marginalized groups in participating in empowering activities, including livelihood opportunities. Output 2: Implement Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM) and awareness raising around Linha Verde. Provide trainings on mechanisms to partners at site level, as well as PSEA and PFA trainings.Carry out intentions/perception surveys and satisfaction surveys, service monitoring, site profiling – 3Ws and RSOs for sites. Advocate for people in need in line with their expressed needs.Ensure site planning coordination of partners implementing physical works in the site.Establish site level coordination mechanisms for service provision.Ensure inclusion of IDPs living outside sites in the response.Disseminate information on COVID-19 and rumour tracking to community members. Output 3: Coordinate and carry out site planning with government and humanitarian partners to identify viable land for new sites and agricultural land; provide capacity building trainings with government technicians on site planning and demarcation. Ensure that damaged shelters are fixed, drainage is in place and working, and implementation of accessibility works for PWDs, ensuring safety and that infrastructure is physically, socially and culturally appropriate for residents. Provide training and ongoing technical support including on-the-job supervision to CCCM partners for camp management, site planning, information management, community engagement and registration. 5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Assessments will be conducted to determine community dynamics, including factors exacerbating marginalization, to ensure activities do not contribute to further discrimination or marginalization. The project will promote meaningful particpation of marginalized communities in decision-making and community governance structures. Through Communication with Communities, IOM will ensure marginalized groups are reached with all public awareness messages and information on activities and services, and accessible complaints and feedback mechanisms will enable marginalized groups to raise issues of protection or exclusion. Prior to interventions, Camp Management teams will consult with different actors - community leaders, state and local authorities, OCHA and partners - regarding camp administration and land usage to ensure that the displaced population are not forcibly evicted from the site. Where returns are affected by HLP issues, Camp Management will link them with HLP actors for assistance. CCCM will participate in inter-agency safety audits to identify protection risks and address related issues. Camp Management will collaborate with protection and security actors in developing community-based protection measures that enhance the safety of women and girls, and engage with women’s governance structures, representatives, and IDPs to identify and address safety concerns. IOM will ensure site planning and care and maintenance reduces GBV risks, including solar lighting and elimination of dead-end corridors, and will refer GBV cases to GBV actors for assistance through referral pathways.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will focus upon both the physical and structural barriers faced by persons with disabilities, and ensure that engagement with people with disabilities is not ad hoc, but rather one of meaningful partnership, collaboration, and empowerment. At the structural level, IOM will work to ensure that people living with disabilities are represented in community structures, such as through a Community Disability Group, and that nominated members of such groups are facilitated in meaningfully participating within wider community governance structures. The project will use assessments to understand principal barriers to inclusion of people with disabilities and understand how support can help to overcome these. In site design, IOM CCCM team account for structural elements to improve physical accessibility for persons with disabilities, to include construction of bridges and ramps, whilst advocating for adaptations in WASH facilities. In terms of physical infrastructure, community members with disabilities will be invited to meaningfully participate in providing feedback on proposed accessibility infrastructure. Within all efforts, IOM will work with protection partners to obtain information on people with disabilities to ensure that they are also factored in service response and monitoring, and ensure that baseline and endline data for the project is clearly disaggregated.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Site planning is crucial to reduce the risk of creating a negative impact on the environment. Through thorough assessments of the area prior to implementation, the site planner will be able to adjust for environmental degradation of resources such as water sources and trees, whilst utilizing established techniques to effectively control loss of natural vegetation cover, soil erosion, and slope protection whilst implementing site care and maintenance. IOM CCCM will also work with WASH partners to ensure proper sanitation management as regards waste, latrines and drainage to prevent contamination of groundwater. IOM CCCM will also involve communities – particularly host communities – in key decisions relating to local management and extraction of natural resources, which will also contribute to peaceful co‐existence between the displaced and host population. Community Engagement throughout the project will also contribute to strengthened community buy-in to environmentally friendly practices through community committees. In addition, the interventions will use locally sourced materials wherever possible to reduce the transportation requirements and to ensure that existing natural materials are sustainably utilized and appropriate for the context. Labour based interventions will be prioritized reducing use of fuel and increasing community participation in the sites.
