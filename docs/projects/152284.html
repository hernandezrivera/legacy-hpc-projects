Project Summary

CARE Nigeria has been implementing the SAFPAC III project from early January 2018 in Borno and Yobe States, followsing a similar model to Chad, DRC and Mali SAFPAC projects. Health facility, community, and provider attitude assessments were followed by trainings for service providers and clinicians in key SRH clinical competencies and training for community facilitators on engagement and reflective dialogue to overcome social norms and barriers that inhibit women and girls’ access to family planning and post-abortion care. Routine clinical skills assessment, supportive supervision of providers and community facilitators, and improved stock management and supply chain are all considered in the model. Given the shortage of the functional health facilities in Borno and Yobe State, CARE Nigeria has been working to address key MISP objectives in its approach, including clinical management of sexual and gender-based violence. Through this initiative, CARE is supporting 20 Health Facilities in Borno State, and planned to reach 4,800 new family planning users by December 2018 (with 60% using a LARC method), and 280 PAC users. From February, 2018 to June, 2018, CARE has reached a total of 1157 New Family Planning Users (24% of target); 113 LARC users (4.6% of target); 6 PAC Users with 2 PAC Users leaving with a FP method.
SAFPAC IV
CARE Nigeria will position SAFPAC phase III to scale-up for Phase IV with an additional 10 Health Facilities and expanding FP/PAC services to the second worst crisis-affected state in the Northeast - Yobe. Following the same strategy for SAFPAC phase IV, CARE will support a total of 30 Health Facilities (25 in Borno State and 5 in Yobe state), with the aim to reach 22,000 new family planning users by December 2021; 11000 (50%) targeted for LARC method and 1,290 PAC users.

Needs

The humanitarian crisis in north-east Nigeria, now in its ninth year, remains one of the most severe in the world with over 1.7 million persons remain internally displaced, and an estimated 7.7 million men, women, boys and girls in acute need of protection and health assistance (OCHA March 2018). Reproductive Maternal, Newborn Child Health (RMNCH)North-East Nigeria has some of the worst maternal health indicators in the country which have been further aggravated by the crisis. The most recent Nigeria Demographic Health Survey (NDHS 2013), reveals that the North-East has a high unmet need for modern contraceptives at 17% and extremely low Contraceptive Prevalence Rate (CPR) of 3% compared with the 10% national average. At least 98% of women of reproductive age in Northern Nigeria do not use contraceptives. While the majority may be unaware of the benefits of FP, unmet needs sit at 20% with those aware and willing to use contraceptives having no access to these services. The resulting effect is that the region has the highest number of children per woman (total fertility rate) of 6.3 compared to the national average of 5.5; and with a very high Maternal Mortality Ratio of 1,538 compared to the national value of 576. Only 20% of pregnant women deliver with a skilled birth attendant and teenage pregnancy sits at 39%, representing a major health concern due to its association with higher morbidity and mortality for both mother and child. Educational levels of females in Nigeria are low, with only about 62% having at least some primary school education (only 30.6% in the Northeast). This, along with rural residence and high levels of poverty are known to be associated with use or non-use of health services. There is a concentrated HIV epidemic in Yobe (5.3% prevalence) and generalized epidemics in Borno (2.4%) and Adamawa with 1.9% prevalence (www.naca.gov.ng). The protracted crisis with the disruption of the health system has only increased the exposure and risk of men, women and new-borns to preventable complications and death.

Activities

Increase access to respectful care and SRHR services (FP, PAC/SAC and CMR) for all women and girls in 30 health facilities (25 in Borno state and 5 in Yobe state)CARE Nigeria will work in close partnership with State Ministry of Health in Borno and Yobe states for provision of enabling environment – Health facilities and qualified local staffs - to deliver quality FP/PAC services. Community engagement on FP/PAC awareness through Local CSOs.In line with the National Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus, CARE Nigeria will continue to adopt the localization efforts, and will work with 2 CSOs to undertake community engagement sessions in all the catchment areas of the 30 supported Health facilities, to increase awareness, knowledge and utilization of FP/PAC, including referral of clients to the FP/PAC services in the supported Health facilities. The awareness sessions will focus on addressing community barriers and misconceptions around FP/PAC based on wrong social, cultural, religious and traditional beliefs and practices, and that Communities are aware of their SRHR rights and are engaged and involved in addressing social barriers and gender norms and hold health systems and local authorities accountable (Community Engagement: demand creation and accountability)Equip Health facilities to provide high quality of FP, PAC/SAC and CMR services and counseling and ensure proper stocks management in conjunction with community representatives. CARE will train service providers in order to strengthen their capacities to ensure effective stocks management, ordering, consumption, requisition and monitoring stock level, including availability of supplies for provision of services and monitor their utilization.Advocacy activities at all the targeted sites. CARE will engage the services of high-level 12 member traditional and religious leaders recently inaugurated by His excellency, the Health commissioner of Borno State with a mandate of undertaking SRHR to advocate for a pro-SRHR agenda to influence local policy and advocacy issues aimed to address the socio-cultural and religious barriers, which are negatively affecting the uptake of Sexual reproductive and family planning services in the targeted States. The high-level religious team, selected from across the tribes and communities in the north-east Nigeria, are well respected and highly trusted by their communities. Joint monthly supervisory support visit with SMOH and partnersCARE will coordinate with District Health Management Team (DHMT) and local partners on joint monthly supportive supervision to supported Health facilities. This will provide an avenue to review conditions of the sentinel site visited, including review of data collection, use of standards of protocols, service provider attitudes and performance and provide on-job-training besides documenting to provide solutions to issues faced by the health facility teams.Additionally, through this visits, CARE will highlight infrastructure capacity issues facing the FP clinics in all the supported Health facilities, and will work closely with SMOH and LGA representatives, including the DHMT to devise appropriate measures to provide FP spaces in all the supported facilities. Adolescent and Youth friendly spaces:CARE will work in the supported Health facilities and communities to support provision of adolescents and youth friendly services. This will be done through assessment of skills and attitudes of healthcare providers for provision of AYFS while fostering a supportive environment enabling adolescents and youth to access to SRH especially FP and PAC is all the supported 30 Health facilities. Provision of IEC materials and HMIS tools.CARE Nigeria will continue to support provision of Information, Education and Communication materials on FP/PAC services, and Health Management Information Systems – data collection tools and reporting platform (DISC) for efficient and effective data collection, management and analysis
