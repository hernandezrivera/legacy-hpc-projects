Project Summary

The overarching objective is to provide agricultural based livelihood support to women and youth in herding households affected by man-made and natural shocks.
This project falls under Country Strategic Objective 3: “Strengthen the ability of acutely-vulnerable Palestinian households to cope with protracted threats and shocks” of the HRP 2018-2020 as it improves the coping abilities and reduces shocks and environmental hazards of herders and specifically women in herding communities.
The project also falls under Objective 1 of the Food Security Sector: “The resilience, food security and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected.”

Needs

From the findings of the focus groups, meetings and training sessions that FAO team had conducted with herder communities in cooperation with MoA field extension departments in the different governorates of the WBGS over 2018, it was apparent that female and male herders are suffering from limited access to public services, weak adaptive capacity and asset bases, and a high reliance on social safety nets. In addition, poor herder families, especially those who are female headed, suffer from the lack of economic capacity to obtain the necessary inputs and basic requirements for meaningful farm and animal health management. This situation has been compromising the productivity and profitability of meat and dairy production. The majority of the processing and marketing work load is performed by women, who become more negatively impacted by the reduced production, productivity and quality; resulting in a significant reduction in the ability to meet the basic food security and livelihood needs of their households. Including dairy production This description highlights a herder’s reduced ability to respond to shocks and hence vulnerability to displacement risks. Low resilience herders are at risk of adopting negative coping strategies if no immediate assets protection and livelihood resilience building activities are provided. Herders rely on the income they achieve through selling live animals, while women in herding communities sell processed dairy products in local markets and urban centers of nearby cities. However, herders, women in herding communities and consumers are suffering from the lack of community spaces/markets with minimal facilities and hygiene standards to enable the necessary stability and profitability. This usually results in higher transaction costs for the herders and women in herding communities, higher prices for the consumers in addition to the associated health and food safety risks.1. The project aims to improve herder’s resilience and protect their rights to stay in their communities through tailored set of interventions addressing the below listed threats/needs, which were defined through in depth consultations with the herding communities. The scope of the project was also coordinated with MoA including the gender unit and the Livestock Subsector Working Group under the FSS. Shortage and fragmentation of grazing area due to increasing drought and land confiscation. This forces herders particularly women and children to walk longer distances and take more time to feed their animals, which affects the time and energy remain available to undertake other household responsibilities.2. Shortage of Water: In most of the herder communities, there is shortage of water. The filling points are usually far from the residence of the herders, which forces them to take water for their animal use and forces women to walk long distances to the filling points to bring water for drinking and domestic use. 3. Herders are ill equipped against winter storms and cold, which leads to animal sickness and lamb mortality. Additionally, inappropriate shelters enable water to enter sheds leading to an increase of cleaning work and in some cases, this leads to women getting sick. 4. The highly demanding work of maintaining livestock that women bear. Women are responsible for milking and dairy processing; they are also responsible for the manufacturing including hygiene and sterilization procedures. 5. Marketing the livestock products as most of the markets are restricted to men, and there is no space for women to enter such markets and sell the processed dairy products. 6. Unemployment among youth due to lack of productivity within the herding communities.

Activities

All project activities will be coordinated with MoA and further detailed and designed in consultation with the beneficiary communities. Operational coordination aimed at avoiding duplication of efforts and maximizing synergies will take place through the Livestock Sub-sector Working Group under the FSS. In addition, FAO will assume overall responsibility for implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of the project and the appropriate use of funds. Meanwhile, FAO will seek qualified implementing partners with established experience and expertise in both West Bank and Gaza Strip as per FAO rules and regulations. In order to ensure optimal outreach, ownership and sustainability of the projects results, the project will be implemented through engaging local partners in the design, implementation and M&E phases. A Letter of Agreement with at least one local NGO will be signed in order to partner with in support of activities 1.1 and 1.6 (below). The selection of the local NGO partner will be according to FAO global rules and regulations. FAO will partner with Al Dahrieh municipality for the implementation of activity 2.1. Prior to any project implementation, FAO will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with line ministries and with other organizations working in the same area and sector of activities. Output 1: Coping capacities enhanced and resilience against environmental shocks improved for women and youth in the targeted communities in the WBGS. Activity 1.1 Distribution of drought tolerant seeds, in accordance to MoA procedure, in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Tubas, Nablus, Jenin.Activity 1.2 Rehabilitation of rangeland in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Ramallah Nablus.Activity 1.3 Conduct awareness-raising campaigns and technical and organizational support to herders in order to promote the production and use of silage as an alternative and sustainable animal feed in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, North Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Tubas, Nablus, Jeircho, Jenin, Rafah, Khan Younis, Middle Area, Gaza, North Gaza.Activity 1.4 Rehabilitation and/or construction of 200 water cisterns.in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem.Activity 1.5 Rehabilitation of animal sheds in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, North Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Tubas, Nablus, Qalqilya, Jericho, Jenin, Rafah, Khan Younis, Middle area, Gaza and North Gaza 1,500 beneficiaries. Activity 1.6 Distribution of small scale farm management inputs (disinfection preventive material, salt blocks, wool and hooves secateurs) in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Rafah, Khan Younis, Middle area, Gaza and North Gaza.Activity 1.7 Distribution of dairy processing equipment. in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem.Activity 1.8 In full coordination with the MoA, an effectiveness assessment on artificial insemination will take place. Based on results, artificial insemination for small ruminant in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, North Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Tubas, Nablus, Qalqilya, Jericho, Jenin, Rafah, Khan Younis, Middle area, Gaza and North Gaza. Output 2: Access to market is improved for herders’ households engaging women and youth in WB Activity 2.1 Enhancing and enabling the market accessibility for women and youth through up scaling the capacity of the livestock market in Al Dahrieh through promoting their participation in local exhibitions and providing them with spaces to sell their products.

Post distribution monitoring will be conducted by interviewing a sample of female and male beneficiaries in order to monitor use of the inputs distributed, prospects for sustainability of the intervention as well as overall satisfaction of beneficiaries as part of FAO’s accountability to beneficiaries. Project impact will be assessed by comparing the results of a baseline and end line survey. Collected information will be gender dis-aggregated. Project activities and achievements will be monitored consistently to ensure continued alignment with the FSS response plan and the FAO Country Programme Framework 2018 2022.All project activities and results will be coordinated with the FSS to avoid duplication of emergency support efforts. Relevant results, analysis and lessons learned shall be shared with all Food Security Sector stakeholders. In addition, FAO will maintain coordination with the protection cluster and the Humanitarian Country Team in order to ensure alignment with and support of advocacy initiatives. A Grievance Redressal Procedure on the basis of FAO guidelines and taking into account local best practices has been established and is currently functional. It includes clearly defined responsibilities and follow up channels, starting with the officer in charge of receiving the feedback/complaint, and the proceeding processing procedures, up until final decision and the subsequent follow up. The procedure includes several well-defined steps tied to specific time frames including: preliminary review for eligibility, verification process and resolution and will be publicized among targeted communities in the context of beneficiary selection processes, post distribution mounting as well as in the context of partnerships agreements if and when relevant.. Major monitoring and evaluation milestones: 1) Successful completion of the project’s activities should be accomplished 12 months into project implementation. 2) All inputs should be fully operational 12 months into project implementation. 3) Beneficiaries will be contacted for an impact evaluation after project completion, to measure the overall sustainability of the intervention as well to verify whether all structures are in use. Impact evaluation will specifically target females in focus group discussions, and female experts if/when needed will conduct these.
