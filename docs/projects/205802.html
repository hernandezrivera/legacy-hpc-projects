Project Summary

The project will be aligned to Health Cluster objectives of providing quality health care to crisis-affected people, preventing/responding to health threats, strengthening the health system. It will realize an effective coordination and integration between development and humanitarian action by complementing the ongoing program in support to the local health system when this latter will result not sufficient to respond to people needs, targeting the most neglected groups in the most remote sites, in particular in case of emergencies increasing the demand for assistance and disrupting life-saving services. This will be ensured by proposing a range of different services provision modalities (static and mobile), to overcome the accessibility constraints affecting current health care coverage at all health system level and/or resulting from emergency events, in particular for those people whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) expose them to additional vulnerabilities, challenging their access to services. For this purpose, an integrated health – protection intervention will be realised, through the opening and sustain of reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both clinical care and psychosocial support. In general, project impact will be maximized through a multisector approach, with the result of services being highly comprehensive, as addressing health conditions and the inter- related complications (ie malnutrition), and offering quality care, being informed by hygiene promotion and IPC principles, becoming the first setting for an effective prevention of the main infectious diseases. At this regard, the project will guarantee timely and effective prevention, containment and management of outbreaks. Surveillance and early warning will be strengthen, with the engagement and coordination of various stakeholders.
CUAMM will promote existing SMOH/CHD led coordination mechanisms (County Quarterly Review Meetings, State Humanitarian Meeting, State Health/Nutrition Cluster) strengthening and expansion, as privileged platforms for multisector dialogue, with SMOH/CHD and its partners interacting with and harmonizing the interventions of various actors pertaining to other clusters and bringing in their resources and expertise for effective addressing health problems rooted causes, in the framework of SMOH/CHD overall development and emergency response plan
In general, project design and implementation modalities will promote the proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities and this will facilitate the selection and implementation of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable intervention options, focusing on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better and more sustainable outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries. Local actors’ technical and operational support will be one of the core strategies informing the proposed action, contributing to its effectiveness and to the quality of supported services and activities. Amongst these actors also local NGOs/associations, to be mapped, appraised and the accompanied in taking over the implementation of an increasing quote of field based activities, facilitating the realization of the recommended integration with other health determining sectors.
The project is expected to reach 30000 people, out of whom 16% will be U5 children and 50% female, with at least 1% of patients accessing outpatients services being people with disability (300).

1. Needs

LAKES STATE is priority humanitarian setting, with 5/8 Counties in severity 4 and 510.000 people in need 510.000 (PIN2023). It is and borders ìareas prone to flooding (in 2021, 4/8 Counties were targeted by SSHF emergency interventions) and this, plus high internal conflict, have deprived families from their subsistence resources, hampered provision of/access to quality services and lead to internal migration. As per IOM Mobility Tracker 13, Lakes State is hosting 1750.000 IDPs. On top of them, about 4000 returnees’ families from Sudan. Matter of concern is IDPs burden over hosting families, whose resources are limited, because of raids/lootings, environmental factors. This is more concerning when considering that the majority of households are headed by women, more exposed to violence and likely to be food insecure. Poor nutrition intake and WASH conditions are key determinants of high morbidity and mortality, especially amongst mothers and children.In such complex context, the following needs were identified.1_Host/displaced communities don’t have regular access to quality health care, as confirmed by Lakes State low health indicators: ANC4 21% (ANC1 64%, 68% drop), institutional delivery 25%, curative consultation rate <1 (DHIS2 2022 numerator, National Bureau Statistic denominator). PHC HFs are inadequate in number, distribution and conditions to provide preventive/curative services, situation even worsened since 2022, when the 27% of those previously externally supported were left out of any funding mechanism. Some areas do not count any functioning HF, people travel long distances to get assistance (which is not even reachable during certain period because of hard environment). Boma Health Workers network is limited, deployed volunteers are not sufficient to cover the entire territory and security/environmental factors make them preferring to stay closer to main villages. The existing still functioning HFs, besides not being sufficient to respond to increasing demand, are working discontinuously and under standards, hosted in poor buildings, missing key equipment, quickly running out of essential supplies and managed by unskilled personnel. These conditions challenge the quality and the comprehensiveness of provided services, rarely addressing disability, mental health, GBV, nor offering adolescents assistance. 2_Poor access to/utilization of health services plus peculiar environmental conditions, makes the State prone to outbreaks (cholera, measles). Prevention is not consistently realized. EPI coverage is insufficient (71% Penta3, 42% Measles): some antigens are provided in small quantities, not sustaining daily activities nor allowing prepositioning, HFs’ vaccinators are reluctant to conduct outreaches because of lack of reliable transport and motivating perdiem. Mass vaccination campaigns have disrupted routine vaccination, creating the precedent of unsustainable costs, forcing health authorities/staff to keep revising daily immunization calendar and confusing the population, seeing vaccination as an extraordinary event rather than a routine. Limited HFs/BHWs spread over the territory affected health education coverage, with perpetuation of poor health seeking behaviours, including open defecation, missed soap use. 3_Although CHDs and related supervision/coordination mechanism being in place, these are weak to create conditions for responsive and resilient health system. Integration between different sectors (WASH, protection) and health is sporadic, affecting local authorities’ capacities of addressing the above mentioned problems. Similarly, emergency response is consisting in vertical/one shot interventions, focused on the contingent issue, instead of more concertated efforts, with longer term perspective in terms of reconstruction and preparedness. Humanitarian and development partners’ effective dialogue is rare, as well as local health authorities have weak capacities to orientate and play a role in it

2. Project activities

Activities align to HC orientations to achieve sectorial objectives and respond to identified needs. Targeted PHC Facilities/villages will be selected with the HC, after needs assessment and discussion with beneficiaries. Priority will be where existing services are not adequate, as neglected by funding mechanisms, overloaded because of IDPs/outbreak/flooding/violence, damaged by adverse events. Because of the peculiar status of health care providers, direct service delivery will be preferred.SO1: improving essential quality life-saving services (including EmONC) accessibility. The BPHNS will be provided to different extent at different levels (Continuum of Care) through different delivery modalities, bringing them closer to people, entry point for PWD, people with mental disorders and GBV survivors, who will be offered generic assistance and tailored care to their specific needs, eventually referred as per locally defined pathway. ACTIVITIES: 1.1Targeted PHC HFs will be enabled to provide the due/life-saving services by addressing the gaps hindering their attendance capacities. Health staff will be hired/trained/supervised, essential drugs/supplies procured/distributed, complementing what provided in kind by other actors, old/looted equipment replaced, damaged infrastructures repaired. 1.2Free of charge ambulance service will be sustained for lifesaving referrals, contributing to vehicles costs and staff reward/training. 1.3BHWs network will be expanded and sustained for ICCM/health education, with the engagement/training/equipment/supervision of more volunteers, whose distribution will be tailored to that of targeted communities. 1.4CHDs will be supported to deploy mobile clinical teams to cope with lack of HFs or with existing HFs discontinuous functioning or poor responsiveness; dedicated staff will be hired/trained and provided with supplies, equipment, transport/communication means. The services to offer will be defined under HC guidance.SO2: outbreak prevention, investigation, integrated response. ACTIVITIES: 2.1Static/outreach routine immunization will be guaranteed at all targeted PHC HFs and included in the mobile clinics package, by hiring vaccinators, advocating UNICEF/WHO for regular antigens provision and taking care of their storage/distribution, running different transport means to bring immunization to remote site. 2.2CHDs will be sustained for surveillance (including IDSR) with distribution of GL/tools, surveillance officers training/mentoring, transport and communication means. 2.3In case of outbreak, responsive/mop up campaigns will be organized for containment/prevention, while RRT will be activated for case tracking and first line management by engaging/training dedicated staff and providing due suppliesSO3: health system strengthening through humanitarian-development nexus. CUAMM will integrate the emergency intervention to the ongoing development program, without duplication, to promote synergies and optimise available resources. ACTIVITY: 3.1Reinforcement of multidisciplinary supervision/coordination mechanisms for stakeholders’ concerted/multisector actions under CHD/SMOH stewardship. Health actors will interact with those of other clusters for integrated activities contributing to SOs achievement (SO1_ Malnutrition prevention and treatment, Protection for GBV comprehensive attendance; SO2_WASH for IPC). CUAMM projects are designed and implemented with SMOH/CHD to ensure intervention endorsement and local actors’ involvement, with constant communication flow and open discussion. Implemented actions quality will be guaranteed with technical/operational support to SMOH/CHD, while services responsiveness/acceptability will be promoted engaging beneficiaries in the planning and monitoring (community feedback mechanism) phases and local NGOs/associations during implementation
