Project Summary

United Nations Association of South Sudan has been engaged in monitoring of refugee returnees in collaboration with UNHCR since 2018. Various assessments conducted and shared with UNHCR demonstrated a consistent increase of an average 43.61% in spontaneous returnees from neighboring countries of Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya, Uganda, Central Africa Republic and Democratic republic of Congo. UNASS seeks to provide emergency lifesaving SGBV, Child Protection, Psycho Social Support and Trauma healing Services for Refugee Returnees and transit/returnee route communities. UNASS is to target refugee returnees spontaneously returning to South Sudan from target locations in three states (Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria and Western bar el Gazal State) to include; entry border points through Boro Medina point to Raja then Wau and Aweil (mainly Plata and Ambororo returnees from Sudan, Chad and CAR), Lobone Ngom-oromo and Nimule entry/exit points in Magwi county, Kajo-keji-Moyo exit/entry points in Kajo-keji county and Kaya-Oraba-Lasu exit/entry points in Yei County(Uganda, DRC and CAR returnees routes). UNASS bears in mind existing international and regional refugee protection mechanisms. UNASS is intent on furthering prevailing mechanisms in regards to issues of refugee returnees such as the Global Compact on Refugees which acknowledges that local partners have a role to play in measuring the impact of hosting, protecting and assisting refugees, in order to assess gaps in international cooperation. Through its refugee returnee engagements as a lead member of a network of 18 NNGOs on the Global Compact on Refugees and Statelessness in South Sudan; UNASS has acquired unique capabilities in rendering support to these persons of concern. Through this intervention, UNASS targets at least 15,000 (6,000 women, 3,000 girls, 4,000 men & 2,000 boys) refugee returnees with enhanced returnees’ access to territory with dignity and respect for their rights and enhanced protection and access to lifesaving SGBV, Child Protection, Psycho Social Support and Trauma healing Services and support to at risk vulnerable returnee and returnee routes/host communities to sustain their capacity to cope with protection significant threats. Through border monitoring, returnees will be able to access the territory with dignity and their rights are respected, returnees and host community empowered and outreach strengthened and assistance provided to persons with specific needs. There will be prevention of and response to Sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), protection of children from neglect, abuse and violence, Psycho Social Support and Trauma healing services and durable and humanitarian solutions made available to returnees for a period of 12 months. UNASS is to contribute to cluster objectives 2 & 3

1 - Needs

UNASS bears in mind existing international and regional refugee protection mechanisms. UNASS is intent on furthering prevailing mechanisms in regards to issues of refugee returnees such as the Global Compact on Refugees which acknowledges that local partners have a role to play in measuring the impact of hosting, protecting and assisting refugees, in order to assess gaps in international cooperation. UNASS is a lead member of an 18 NNGO network that aspires to the realisation of the Global Compacts on Refugees and Migration as well as ending Statelessness by 2024 and has build robust capabilities in indentifying, analyzing and seeking durable solutions to these persons of concern The ongoing violence and crimes have affected almost all families and the price paid incredibly high with loss of lives, infrastructure, assets, physical psychological abuse and loss of livelihood. People lost their homes and have stayed in temporary camps (PoC and IDPs camps), fled across the borders etc for years. Displacements coupled with challenges in ensuring physical protection have made the life difficult for many. Violence, deprivation and suffering have negatively affected the mental health of this vulnerable population, especially women and children. UNASS and Partners are planning to benefit from their diverse expertise and experience to provide a comprehensive response. Experience of violence and trauma has put large part of the target population, especially women in extremely vulnerable positions. The process of healing from a conflict is long, and the first emergency needs include support to cope with experiences, and ways to build resilience moving forward. Both individual and community psycho-social support is needed in displaced, returning and remaining communities. This complex situation requires a multi-sector response, which can be met by the common response from the UNASS.Open borders and access to the territory by South Sudanese returnees’ faces challenges. Notably, capacity to handle movements efficiently and in line with international principles needs reinforcement and capacity to register returnees rapidly must be built. In this regard, protection monitoring, protection interventions and up-scaled outreach are needed. Needs of vulnerable returnees need to be addressed in the delivery of protection services and community empowerment activities. Risks for women and girls are exacerbated by overcrowding, community tensions, and the limited opportunities of many households to meet their needs. Some have resorted to survival sex and early marriage. Medical and other support services for survivors of SGBV are lacking. According to the UNASS returnees Assessment at major entry/exit border areas, challenges facing children include separation from families, child labour, and exposure to physical and psychological violence and limited access to child protection services.hrough this intervention, UNASS targets at least 15,000 (6,000 women, 3,000 girls, 4,000 men & 2,000 boys) refugee returnees with enhanced returnees’ access to territory with dignity and respect for their rights and enhanced protection and access to lifesaving SGBV, Psycho Social Support and Trauma healing Services and support to at risk vulnerable returnee and returnee routes/host communities to sustain their capacity to cope with protection significant threats.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

UNASS subscribes to the human rights perspective which recognises that all human beings, including refugees, asylum seekers, migrants, and specifically UASC are equal and have the same right to protection. The child and other human rights are at the center stage of our Child protection project and this is to always be amplified and recognised in all our project engagements, products and services.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The designing of this project followed a number of community consultative meetings conducted to identify needs of affected population, all age groups and sex were consulted and the activities and resources requested are adapted to the needs and preferences of girls, boys, women and men of different ages. The targeted actions address the needs and priorities as well as the discrimination faced by specific groups of women/girls/boys/men.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is linked to the South Sudan Refugee response plan and the Child Act (2008) and the 2011, undertaking between the National Council for Child Welfare and MoGCSW on Protection of Separated and Unaccompanied Children.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to national objectives (1) providing humanitarian assistance to persons in need especially though faced with disasters such as armed conflict; (2)to keep a register of orphans, child-headed households, disabled and other children in particular need of material and other assistance within each area of jurisdiction and monitor and assist them in order to protect them from abuse and enable them to grow with dignity and develop their potential and self-reliance captured under the Child Act 2008.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

UNASS had a number of consultative meetings with the communities involving different population groups and analyzed the needs and dynamics between women, girls, boys and men in different age groups. The risks were identified and measures factored in to mitigate the potential risks to ensure that this project reaches the intended target groups. Ensuring effective coordination and consultation between returnee and host communities on the best way to engage will create the necessary foundation for a safe and stable environment. Peace building is to be a key component of this intervention that is to ensure returnees engage with host communities in positive activities aimed at transforming their communities and relations.Enhanced vertical linkages and expanded horizontal linkages through a process that enables returnees and host community participation in selection of participating county/payam and establishment of full county/payam buy-in and the transparent identification of projects involving a wide spectrum of community members and local authority officials.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The proposed project is to assist in reducing and preventing the longer-term effects of crises and assist in recovering the community from the conflict. Beneficiary and community participation to ensure project ownership and accountability shall be achieved through community contribution in the project implementation cycle. Peace building activities are to form part of the project in order to provide sustainable lasting solutions to communities in addressing some of the drivers to situations pose a protection risk to children, girls, boys, women and persons with special needs. The project recognises that Sustainability of the protection and promotion of rights must be enjoyed and easily accessed by UASC and hence durable solutions suggested to enable maintaining the level of necessary care and protection that is afforded to these children such as the planned family reunification

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Instances of exposure of displaced and conflict affected women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements is to be mitigated by the project team working closely with protection committees when issues of GBV or child abuse are encountered. Sexual exploitation and abuse have two dimensions, from the community or by the implementing partners. UNASS has a clear code of contact and policy on sexual exploitation/abuse, all our staffs are always oriented and provided with the policy document and any offender will be punished accordingly.UNASS will be working with protection actors on the ground to mitigate barriers to health care for SGBV survivors, mapping of existing services and referral pathways available to SGBV survivors and orienting health care workers on the needs and services of SGBV survivors. The risk of Contamination of targeted project areas with explosive hazards is to be addressed through close collaboration with the protection cluster in creating awareness about safety measures to take regarding UXOs within the project areas.
