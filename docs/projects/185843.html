Project Summary

As the coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic sweeps across the world, it is causing widespread concern, fear and stress, all of which are natural and normal reactions to the changing and uncertain situation that everyone finds themselves based on the HRP 2021 ( Borno, Adamawa and Yobe).
Girls and boys in north-east Nigeria continue to face multiple protection risks and violations of their rights. The threats and risks posed by these protection risks have been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic which has not only negatively affected delivery of life-saving child protection services but has also weakened the capacities of families and communities to care for children. The COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the situation of children living without parental care, especially those for boys who had been sent away by their families for religious education purposes as well as the situation of children on the streets. There is a strong need to build the capacity of child protection actors, particularly NNGOs, in providing family tracing and reunification services.
SCI will continue to;
• Provide CP services; to children and their caregivers, Case management including alternative care support
• Strengthen capacities of community based mechanisms and government capacity including COVID – 19 guidelines and adaptations,
• Prevention of, and response to GBV and
• Provision of psychosocial support (PSS) & specialized services eg (MHPSS) to children and their caregivers.
Considering the impact of COVID-19, on people’s mental health, SC will be monitoring and responding to protection needs of children and families together with national authorities.
SC will be providing information and guidance to staff and the beneficiaries we serve. SC will continue to mainstream CP across all sectors and all phases of project
implementation in save the children to maximize the benefits of save the children intervention and reduce possible risks against boys and girls.
As part of the implementation, SC common approaches such as parenting without violence (PWV) and Steps to protect children in conflict. In the phase of implementation, COVID-19 adaptation for child protection case management that must have both child protection and safety at the core including activation of remote case management supervision and coaching whenever there is an out break or lock down of COVID_19.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Northeast Nigeria is in its 11th years which has taken a heavy toll on people in the BAY states the most. This is as a result of Violent attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAG) which is escalating more and more. This has significantly affected the well-being of children threatened them with multiple protection concerns. According to HNO Nigeria 2021, about 1.4 million children (41% girls, 59% boys) living in locations with limited or no core child protection services and community-based prevention and response to protection risks. The Humanitarian need overview further revealed that an at least 17,528 girls and boys (of whom 28% girls, 72% boys) including 484 children with disabilities (43% girls, 57% boys) and 577 unaccompanied and separated children (46% girls, 54% boys) face different kinds of child protection challenges. These comprise risk of abuse, neglect, and exploitation. Preventing and mitigating these risks will require timely child protection services. Children identified access to recreational activities and life-skills education as priority services primary to their well-being, particularly in the COVID-19 pandemic context where education and social activities have significantly declined. The COVID-19 pandemic has further weakened coping capacities for families to provide and care for children; poor households in north-east Nigeria face more difficulty in accessing food as food prices and annual inflation increase, and food availability is expected to decrease. Protection risks are heightened for girls, mainly adolescent girls who are most at risk of sexual violence and as a result of the pandemic, are also at a high risk losing out on education opportunities amongst denial of realization of other rights of whom 27% are in Adamawa, 55% in Borno and 18% in Yobe, (HNO Nigeria 2021). The impact of the humanitarian crisis in the conflict-affected BAY States presents one of themost significant vulnerabilities to the spread of COVID-19. The situation is a complex, protracted crisis with huge developmental needs, damaged infrastructure, lack of governance, and the rule of law outside Maiduguri and major garrison towns. The most vulnerable population groups are girls, boys, women, the elderly including those with disabilities, and people with chronic medical conditions. The biggest fear is the effects on the decreased immunity associated with diseases and risks typical of humanitarian contexts such as malnutrition, measles, malaria, HIV. This is particularly true in the conflict-affected states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY states), which have been affected by over eleven years of violent conflict. Save the children seeks to improve impartial access to services for girls and boys to their basic rights and strengthen the overall protection environment. SC will be responding by providing interventions such as psychosocial support through building the capacity of caregivers to provide PSS at home, house to house visits while maintaining IPC guidelines in the coming months where CFS might not be open because of lockdown or security threat and establishment of CFS post COVID – 19. Save the children will conduct a risk analysis and review child friendly spaces activities to meet the PSS/life skills needs of girls and boys including those with disabilities at both individual and family level, alternative care arrangement/placement or provision of foster parents, strengthening community based protection structures, as well as prevention of and response to GBV and further provision of more advance mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS).

2 - Activities

• Case management Support (CP/GBV) Identification and provision of multi-sectoral case management service to unaccompanied, separated and other girls and boys at risk including child headed household and those living with disabilities, survivors of SGBV, children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG) etc. If CAAFAGs are released, they will be placed under SC trained standby foster parents in line with their best interest and with full compliance of COVID-19 (PPES for CP staffs, remote case management where there is lockdown or restriction etc). Special consideration will be given to children with disabilities • Alternative care arrangement/placement of children on the principle of necessity which means children can only be placed in alternative care if it is really necessary, it must be in the child’s ‘best interest’ and The principle of appropriateness which signifies that children should only be placed in alternative care that suits their individual needs and situation. Alternative care is intended to provide protection, well-being and stability while a permanent arrangement is made • Provision of more advance/specialized mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) service to boys and girls.• PSS structured activities: Age appropriate and culturally sensitive orientated activities for boys and girls while aligning to IPC guidelines and physical distancing. Where there I lockdown, PSS activities will be provided through house to house by a train caregivers or community based volunteers, remote and non-contact methods e.g Through phone calls.• Life skills will be provided by train staffs to support children (boys & girls) enable them deal with specific (complex) or challenging life situations and circumstances, help them aspire to a bright future and how to work towards achieving it. The life skills sessions will equally help them for growth, development, management and survival. All life skills activities will be carryout in compliances with COVID-19 protocol. • Capacity building/training for child protection partners, social welfare staff on safeguarding, child protection related areas and COVID-19.• Positive Parenting /parenting without violence sessions to caregivers and foster parents• Sensitization and awareness rising session to populates on child protection key child friendly messages including gender Equality, gender base violence and COVID – 19 IPC guidelinesOther activities • Capacity building for women, men, girls and boys including those with disabilities community-based structures including community stakeholders on child protection related topics, COVID-19 prevention and Gender equality.• Advocacy to the government on Child protection, Gender equality and COVID – 19 related issues and continued collaboration with the MWASD on case management and other CP services provision to Girls and boys including those with disabilities and Female and male caregivers.• Update the referral pathways in the respective locations through desk reviews using non-contact methods in line with COVID – 19 guidelines and referral of vulnerable children to access inter-sectoral and other specialized services

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Save the children will ensure protection is well mainstreamed across different activities such as case management, PSS activities and other. This will be achieve by ensuring there is prioritization of safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm, ensuring meaningful access for assistance and services in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination), setting-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints (Accountability) and Participation & empowerment.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Save the children have Feedback response mechanism (FRM) which served as avenue to raise concerns about breaches in policies and commitments raised by affected populations. Feedback of the community members will be capture through different mechanisms such as our central toll free line, frontline staff and during meetings with community members. Staff and volunteers will be train on the importance of feedback in improving program quality. Regular sensitizations will be carryout on the use of the mechanisms. For proper documentation, all feedback received are being documented in the complaints feedback database. This served as a tracking tool for monitoring closed and open feedback for cases that have not received responses for the concerns raised.
