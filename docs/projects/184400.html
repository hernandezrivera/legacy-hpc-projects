Project Summary

IOM humanitarian response strategy (HRP) for 2022 commits to improve access to emergency and lifesaving primary healthcare services among the most vulnerable and crisis-affected populations in the targeted locations. IOM will utilize Essential Package of Health Services (EPHS) framework to achieve efficient health delivery system. IOM will prioritize training and capacity building of the health care workers. IOM will also ensure minimum standards for infrastructure are met to reach people with lifesaving health care, this will include to maintain and repair of structures. IOM will also preposition essential medicines, medical supplies for common outbreaks. Medical equipment, Personal Protective Equipment (PPEs) and IPC supplies will also be prepositioned in regional hubs to ensure uninterrupted supply to all IOM supported fixed health care facilities, outreach, and mobile health services. IOM will support the operation of 32 fixed health care facilities with integrated community outreach, and 15 mobile clinics in targeted areas. In total, the 15 mobile clinics and the network of outreach activities attached to the fixed HFs will be operational in areas that are hard-to-reach, remote, conflict-affected, and newly liberated areas. IOM will collect weekly diseases epidemiology and surveillance data in the health facilities to enhance the country’s surveillance and emergency preparedness. Outreach activities operated from the fixed HFs will be conducted twice a week in the catchment communities. Fight against vaccine preventable disease is priority for IOM, measure will include support for routine vaccination thus increasing immunization coverage. IOM will also provide integrated nutrition services including screening of children under 5 years and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) for malnutrition and therapeutic services for children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) through integration of outpatient therapeutic programmes (OTPs) activities in the health facilities and mobile outreach services. IOM will support provision of maternal health care and newborn care including safe deliveries, emergency obstetric care. IOM will also undertake risk reduction and community engagement (RCCE) both at the fixed facilities by conducting health education, health promotion with information education and communication (IEC) materials, and hygiene practices that contribute to excess morbidity and mortality. Referral pathway will be established for SGBV survivors from the community level to the health facility levels through working with the community members and the local authorities. Identified SGBV cases will be provided initial clinical management. IOM will contribute to promoting well-being of boys, girls, men and women in Somalia by providing access whilst integrating mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) into primary healthcare and community-based psychosocial support. Further, IOM intends to continue its technical leadership and support in the coordination of active MHPSS actors in the national response to the COVID-19 pandemic and humanitarian crises in the country. IOM will support a network of selected and trained community volunteers both Community Health Workers (CHWs) and Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) at the community level. IOM supported fixed health facilities will also be the point of referral for further management of cases identified by mobile teams. Identified severe cases including SAM cases, obstetric and neonatal cases, rape cases, identified during mobile outreach services and by the CHWs and CNVs at the community levels will be referred to the fixed health facilities. IOM will support establishment of feedback mechanism, at the community level. This project will be part of IOM’s integrated emergency response program which includes health, nutrition, shelter and NFIs, camp coordination and management, WASH, and displacement tracking and monitoring and protection activities.

1. - Projects activities

IOM proposes to implement the following activities across Jubaland, Southwest State, Puntland, Somaliland, and Banadir: Activity 1: Operate 32 fixed health facilities (HF) with integrated community outreach, and 15 mobile teams in response to acute emergencies and to larger, hard-to reach areas with dispersed populations. The HFs and mobile outreach clinics will provide emergency and essential lifesaving primary healthcare (PHC) services in crisis-affected communities, prioritizing hard-to-reach, newly liberated areas or underserved regions which lack consistent access to primary healthcare services. IOM will also support rehabilitation and renovation of health facilities infrastructure including entrance rumps (where there is need) to facilitate and improve access to facility services for people with reduced mobility and vulnerable population including elderly persons.Activity 2: Preposition and supply essential medicines to health facilities and mobile sites. In line with essential package of health services (EPHS) framework, essential medicines will be available uninterrupted in health facilities across the selected facilities. Further supplies will be strategically stored in regional hubs as buffer or provide emergency support in cases of emergencies or outbreaksActivity 3: Support fixed health facilities to have and provide Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BeMONC) especially in locations lacking maternity and delivery services in target locations by providing necessary equipment and capacity for safe delivery.Activity 4: Training and capacity building will be provided to both clinical and non-clinical staff 15 (80 female and 70 male) will received theory and practical sessions as well on job coaching. Training will cover mostly common conditions seen at facility, disease outbreak response, national treatment guidelines, IPC, trauma and emergency management, maternal child healthcare.Activity 5: Provide health promotion and behavior change communication by strengthening health education at facility and community level and to sustain community awareness to drivers of health.Activity 6: Conduct various trainings and supportive supervision on MHPSS (basic PSS skills, referrals, etc.) for 150 primary health care workers (76 women, 74 men) at 10 static health facilities and mobile outreach clinics; and 50 essential community volunteers /workers (25 women and 25 men; to include protection actors, community leaders, etc); to mainstream MHPSS, raise awareness on importance of apppropriate diagnosis, care and treatment of people with mental illness accessing primary healthcare services at health facilities in IDP settlements and to promote health-seeking behaviors.Activity 7: Provide lifesaving vaccination to hard-to-reach children so as to increase immunization coverage among vulnerable population and help mitigate against communicable disease outbreaks. Villages will be mapped and ensure that all children are vaccinated. This will be achieved by bringing together community leaders, local authority and community health workersActivity 8: Support and mitigate the impacts of COVID 19 by supporting and running of selected isolation centers. People will be screened, provided with health information and treatment of confirmed and suspected cases as per protocol. The project will support inclusion of people with disability by ensuring they consulted and represented in project design, development, implementation, and evaluation. Physical barriers to access the health will be removed and access facilitated by ensuring trumps are in place. More importantly health staff will be sensitized to identify people with disability so to give priority to reduce their wait time. Behavior changes and communication among health staff will be strengthened to identify and change processes that support unconscious bias toward people living with disability.

Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. • IOM will ensure health facilities are secure with perimeter wall and minimum and security guard on duty to protect women and girls from risks of sexual violence/exploitation when accessing services.
