Project Summary

The objective of the intended project is to contribute and improve the living conditions in the targeted IDP camps through strengthening the predictability and effectiveness of service provision and protection for displaced communities with the aim to reach durable solutions through strengthening community-self management and in addition to that the project will strengthen coordination and information sharing among all stakeholders. Through this proposed project activities, DRC will ensure that all the displaced communities are enabled to partake meaningfully in the governance structures and management of the sites. This project will also encourage camp residents to participate community led care and maintenance activities aimed at improving safety and hygiene in sites, reducing protection and health risks.
DRC will work with the camp management committees to facilitate site maintenance through cash-for-work while utilising members of the IDP community by enabling them to earn temporary income. Through this project, DRC will establish or strengthen an inclusive community governance structures and thematic committees at each site level, the engagement of these structures in CCCM coordination mechanisms alongside active humanitarian partners in the respective sites and representatives of relevant local authorities, as well as through continued support for capacity building, mainly through the delivery of trainings adapted to each type of participation (thematic IDP committee members, site governance members, local authority representatives). The proposed project will enhance Complain, Response and feedback mechanisms.

1. Needs

According the National CCCM Cluster, over 908,800 people remain in 142 formal and informal displacement sites, predominantly in Unity, Upper Nile, and Central Equatoria states as of September 2023. These individuals face extreme vulnerability due to the lack of viable alternatives. Self-se􀆩led, unplanned IDP sites, often overlooked and underserved, pose an even greater risk of exclusion from essential services. In 2023, 58 per cent of displacement sites (hosting 327,215 people) did not receive Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) support, resulting in limited access to coordinated services, community support, information, or site maintenance. Additionally, over 400,000 projected returnees from Sudan in 2024 will need CCCM assistance in the transit and reception centers near the Sudan border. Based on monthly site verifications, the CCCM Cluster anticipates that around 20 per cent of these returnees will settle in existing IDP sites throughout the country. This highlights the critical role of CCCM support in addressing the needs of both IDPs and returnees to ensure their access to life-saving assistance and protection. The integration of these additional returnees in existing IDP sites will require significant efforts to provide decent living conditions, protection, and humanitarian assistance. Coordination and resources will play a pivotal role in addressing the challenges presented by this evolving situation. According to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview, 9.4 million people (9.06 M South Sudanese IDPs and 337K refugees) are currently in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 500,000 people have been observed since 2022. 1.19 million people (1.10M IDP + 0.09M returnees) have been identified as in need of CCCM critical interventions out of the total 9.4 M people in need. An estimated 0.953M people will be targeted through CCCM cluster interventions in 2024. Of this, DRC is targeting to reach 150,000 by the end of 2024. Protracted displacement is a major driver of humanitarian needs, as significant coping strategies of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) are eroded, and unresolved security and livelihood obstacles mean that women, men, boys and girls are likely to remain vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. According to the National CCCM Cluster, women, girls, and people with disabilities, who comprise 15 per cent of the total IDP population in camps and camp-like settings, as well as those living outside of camps with host populations, are even more vulnerable. The ongoing conflict and flooding have forced hundreds of thousands of people to remain in displacement sites across the country requiring Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) services to meet their basic needs. Following the sub-national violence in parts of the country, DRC conducted several protection and CCCM assessments which have identified significant protection violations, occurring both during the displacement journey and in the area of displacement. Women and girls continue to face threats to their safety and security arising from early marriage and sexual exploitation/Gender-Based Violence (GBV) as they lose livelihoods and access to education, which is not prioritized by their families.DRC through its static, roving and mobile responses have identified that coordination of services especially in hard to reach areas continues to be problematic, with yet insufficient information sharing among service providers, and weak feedback mechanism between the local authorities, humanitarian organizations and the IDPs. Critical needs in the IDP sites are lack of information on service provision at the site level, HLP issues, and low level of community participation, and most of the IDP sites do not have fencing or proper lighting at night and often overcrowded, that can further elevating safety concerns

2. Project activities

DRC will undertake the following activities that are in line with the CCCM Cluster strategic objectives. Coordination: DRC will continue managing the current 19 IDP sites and further strengthen its coordination, and monitoring of services at site level to ensure that minimum standards are met. In hard-to-reach areas that are remote and difficult to access, the Mobile Response Teams (MRT) take charge of organizing coordination meetings with other humanitarian actors. This helps facilitate the seamless provision of integrated assistance and allows for the exchange of valuable information derived from Camp Coordination and Camp Management assessments.Governance and Community Participation: DRC will ensure the establishment or strengthening of an inclusive (men, women, youth, PSNs, minority, etc.) community governance structures in all managed sites. DRC will implement a system wherein community leaders are established or strengthened in every site or camp under its management, with the aim of fostering inclusivity across various segments of the community. Additionally, the DRC places great emphasis on promoting accountability to the affected population by establishing a Feedback, Complaint, and Response Mechanism (F-CRM). This mechanism is facilitated through dedicated F-CRM desks, complaint boxes, or workers. DRC ensures that all complaints and feedback received from beneficiaries are duly acknowledged, referred to the appropriate service providers, and followed up until their resolution. DRC will also improve information sharing, awareness raising and sensitization of beneficiaries living in sites on services and activities available at the site level.Information management: DRC creates, maintains, and distributes various information in the displacement sites. These include service mapping, 5Ws, population headcounts, site profiles, and more. DRC collaborates with a wide range of stakeholders, including humanitarian actors, UNMISS, OCHA, as well as the state and national level CCCM cluster. By sharing information, the DRC aims to enhance coordination and support in humanitarian efforts.Capacity development: To ensure the successful implementation of high-quality CCCM Programs, DRC prioritizes capacity development for all stakeholders through training on basic CCCM to community leaders, partners, and local authorities to enable their participation in, and eventual take-over of the management of the sites. This includes providing material assistance to equip leaders with necessary resources to enable them undertake their responsibilities. By focusing on capacity building, DRC aims to improve the effectiveness and sustainability of CCCM initiatives.Protection Mainstreaming through safety audits: DRC will carry out a safety audits jointly with protection/GBV partners intention surveys. The primary aim of these safety audits is to identify visible risks and evaluate the specific vulnerabilities of women, girls, boys, and men to these identified risks. Furthermore, the audits aim to determine the necessary measures to mitigate these risks effectively. The safety audit evaluates GBV risks associated with the physical structure and arrangement of sites/camps, availability of resources, and the provision of humanitarian services. Site Maintenance: DRC implements site maintenance initiatives primarily through cash for work modality, utilizing the daily wage rate established by the CCCM cluster to maintain the upkeep of the sites. The community leaders, in collaboration with the broader community, determine the specific activities to be undertaken. These activities typically encompass a range of site maintenance tasks such as bush clearance, drainage reopening, dyke and water retention basin upkeep, as well as foot bridge construction and repair. Where funding permits, DRC will carry out flood mitigation measures such as creation of new drainages and re-opening of existing ones, maintenance of access roads, retention dams, etc.
