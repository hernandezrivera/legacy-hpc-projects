Project Summary

This project has three components of the protection, the first component is the child protection: North Darfur: Atina, Kabkabyia, Um baru and Tawila; South Darfur: Alwihda, Kass, Marching, Neteiga and Umdafug ). This includes procurement and distribution of tents for emergency child friendly spaces(CFS) with furniture, stationeries, and equip them with solar power system and computer, procure and distribute recreation Psychosocial support (PSS) and dignity kits tents for emergency response, provide structured and sustained family based psychosocial support services and community engagement and awareness, capacity building activities and mentoring and coaching, for government, HDPO staff and community based child protection structures as well as assisting children with special needs and disabilities specially girls.
The second component is general protection( North Darfur: At Tina, Kabkabyia, Tawila and Um Baru; South Darfur: Alwihda, Neteiga( Khorabeche), Umdafug, Marching( Manawashi) , Kass and Umdafug; includes supporting the persons with specific needs with referral to specialised services providers , support the communities and stakeholder in legal assistance and specific groups that will be supported through psychosocial support, the project will also include the cash support for identified with special needs to get their needs, this project activities will also train community groups aiming raising their protection awareness associated with community based structures establishment including reconciling committees and youth networks where government authorities and peace partners.
The third components(GBV): The proposed activities for GBV will complement HDPO’s interventions on protection issues with implementing awareness raising campaigns on GBV and its consequences on women and girls and the whole society in 5 localities in South Darfur: Alwihda, Marching, Neteiga, Umdafug and Kass and 4 localities in North Darfur:, Kabkabyia, At Tina, Tawila, Um Baru. Also, through these campaigns we will focus on raising the awareness on available support and services for GBV survivors in the targeted localities. The campaigns will involve large number of people and will include public events that engage men and boys in preventing GBV. The HDPO will also use social media, radio and TV to reach various segments of the community. It is expected to reach at least 6000 persons through this activity directly and indirectly.
In addition, HDPO will implement community sensitisation sessions in the targeted localities. These activities involve a limited number of people (less than 50) on topics like GBV and health, domestic violence, child marriage etc. It is planned to reach 2000 people through this activity.
Furthermore, HDPO will establish 6 community based protection networks in the targeted localities with attention to gender balance (15 members in each CBPN). The CBPNs members will be appointed in consultation with the community and will be directly involved in the awareness raising activities and referral of disclosed GBV cases to available GBV services. The 90 members will receive trainings on GBV, safe referrals of survivors and basic psychosocial support (PFA).

1 - Needs

The major causes of vulnerability in North Darfur and South Darfur are conflict-driven, economic crisis, and family instability. These have resulted in the migration of millions of inhabitants from the place to other to camps in urban areas' outskirts, with minimal livelihood resources. Many IDP camps have existed for over 17 years, becoming increasingly violent and volatile with reports of various human rights violations. Further movement of populations is likely to occur due to political tension and instability in hotspots like Darfur. This increases civilians' vulnerability, particularly women and children, to protection risks such as SGBV, family separation, child recruitment, and psychosocial and physical harm. Perpetual risk of hunger caused by food insecurity and flooding may lead to more child abuse, neglect, and exploitation. Children are forced out of school to fend for families through petty trade and domestic/unskilled labour. Displacement will also further contribute to the breakdown of the community self-protection systems. The conflict recently broke out in North Darfur Sand mainly in Tawila locality, leaving serious protection issues, putting extra overburden on the lives of the vulnerable people especially people with special needs across community structures; hence this project aims to support those categories and provide the essential interventions. Humanity for Development and Prosperity Organization (HDPO) works in ( North and South Darfur) with IDPs, returnees, and host community. Services in the IDP camps are severely limited, particularly regarding education. The majority of children can be found wandering within the camp, leaving them more vulnerable and exposed to protection risks like exploitation and child recruitment. Per unpublished records from agencies in affected areas, children in 4 out of 5 families are subject to violence, abuse, and/or harmful cultural practices, such as child abandonment, neglect, child marriage, corporal punishment, and SGBV. Effects for girls can be as serious as unwanted pregnancy and miscarriage, child mortality, STIs, and serious injuries. Children are also less likely to be sent to school when they must travel through highly insecure areas daily, coupled with transportation costs and the undervaluing of girls’ education. 58% of community members do not know the exact age to get a girl married. Moreover, the majority of Sudanese nationals do not have identification documents like birth certificates. Gender analysis reflects the change in gender roles and labour division due to the camps' lack of work opportunities. Many men leave the camps to look for money to support their families, resulting in women assuming households’ heads' responsibility. The number of female-headed households among IDPs is about 31%, much higher than that of returnees (22%) and settled communities. As a result of these pressures, many women find it difficult to function psychologically and socially. In cases of child-marriage, women, as well as the children, do not have decision-making powers. 70% of female respondents mentioned that the decision to marry depends completely on the father or other male family members, leaving out women, who are most likely to know the interests of the child in terms of needs, challenges, and priorities, or even approximate age of the girl child.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The inequalities are sustained in cultural and traditional gender norms and dynamics that can perpetrate, reinforce and forgive the use of GBV to maintain the disparity in power relations. Women and girls are often at the receiving end of violence, depriving them of their rights over and ability to access, control, manage and benefit from natural resources. Especially in situations where women directly rely on natural resources for their livelihoods and well-being, economic and other forms of GBV can effectively reduce victims to either total dependency or poverty, which can, in turn, result in reinforcing patterns of physical violence and sexual exploitation of women and girls

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Monitoring protection activities will be essential in informing stakeholders about the magnitude and nature of protection challenges facing IDPs and returnees for purposes of taking informed decisions and strategies in meeting the protection needs of vulnerable people. Through a participatory process, HDPO will develop a monitoring plan with a set of activities that will be applied depending on contextual and operational factors. Protection monitoring will take place within camps of IDPs and returnees as well as among host communities. The activities will focus on physical location and layout of camps, justice and legal facilities, health facilities, situation of vulnerable groups, physical protection, GBV, sexual exploitation and abuse, psychosocial assistance, and social welfare among others.HDPO will employ several integrated tools to conduct effective protection monitoring such as observation, survey, focus group and individual discussion, profiling, household interviews, referrals and key informant interviews.Protection monitoring activities will be conducted with respect to confidentiality, protection of sources of information, sensitivity to feelings of victims and witnesses including respect to national and local authorities.Activities will include;• Direct contact of victims or witnesses by field staff or volunteers.• Spot checks including camp settlement, walk about home visits, observation and taking notes.• Survey of areas of displacement, return or settlement in total or random as the case may require through field visits.• Informal and formal /structured interviews with individuals in the affected communities selected randomly.• Regular meetings with humanitarian (cluster) agencies and local authorities for information sharing and review.• Review and analysis of reports and documents by other parties such as press reports, protection reports, situation reports.• Reviewing camp security and police security incidence log books.• community, camp management and focus group discussions with IDPs, returnees, host communities and local authorities. For purposes of promoting early warning systems and networks, HDPO train volunteers in early detection of possible violations of rights and reporting through its established reporting / communication system. HDPO shall also ensure that it strengthens its cooperation with other humanitarian organizations, local authorities and leadership and the community to help in promoting our information gathering network.We will engage strongly in capacity building of our contacts who will generally include representatives from IDPs, returnees, host communities, Civil society, journalists, local leaders, security officials which will help in strengthening detection, reporting advocacy of these groups on matters of protection. It will also help in enriching our information gathering network for purposes of follow up and timely action. HDPO shall also participate actively in protection sectors.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

HDPO will ensure its Safeguarding strategy is implemented and hence ensure that all staff is trained in SEA. Give staff access to the Global Learning and Development Centre's Learn and Connect program to take the mandatory e-learning course on PSEA( UNHCR).rain all staff in PSEA and be seen to take specific steps to prevent it. For example, discuss PSEA action plans in-office meetings, meet with government officials to discuss prevention, discuss the issue with partners, etc. Strengthen community engagement and awareness-raising. Critical activities include running awareness-raising sessions with women, girls, boys, and men in communities of concern and other stakeholders (including implementing and operational partners, government officials, civil society organizations, and companies in the private sector). To prevent SEA effectively, all staff and humanitarian workers must make sure that PoC is aware of their rights and entitlements. PoC should know that they are entitled to assistance, that they should never be expected to provide any form of sexual favor, and that demands for such favors are strictly prohibited. They should also know how and where to report SEA incidents safely and confidentially when they occur.
