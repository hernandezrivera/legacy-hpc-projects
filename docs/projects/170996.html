Project Summary

On 5 March 2020, the Palestinian Prime Minister declared a State of Emergency across the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) in order to curb the spread of COVID-19 pandemic. The government has temporarily closed schools and other educational institutions. This closure has negatively impacted on all the school children and in specific it has bad effect on children who are already at high risk of dropping out of school. To ensure better and safe return to schools for those vulnerable children, PSCF will provide children with different learning opportunities that improve their academic attainment.
Within this project, PSCF will focus on improving the academic achievement of children who dropped out of schools and children at high risk of dropping out, through removing barriers to regularly receive education services as their peers. The project will be implemented in the east areas of Gaza Governorate specifically in Al Shajaia, and Al Zaitoon in full cooperation with the Ministry of Education (MoE).
The project duration will be 12 months. It will target 500 children including children with disabilities aiming to enrich their knowledge and skills to catch up with their learning and to make use of opportunities to develop their performance. The project activities will be carried out at 2 local CBOs located in the east Gaza governorate. To achieve the aim, this project will involve the following activities:
- Contracting with 2 CBOs located in Al Shajaia and Al Zaitoon to be hosting centers for the implementation of the project activities.
- Providing catch up sessions targeting OOSC and children at high risk of drop out.
- Rehabilitate 8 school to facilitate the access of children with disabilities.
- Develop 1 education studios to prepare audi-visual lessons.
- Enhancement of the learning environment through provision of assistive devices, and provision of educational kits and school uniforms.
- Structured group psychological support sessions (PSS) in addition to a few complementary psychological activities for out of school children and children at high risk of dropping out.

1 - Needs

The education sector has been hit hard by the spread of COVID-19 pandemic in Gaza Strip. A strict lockdown was imposed by the local authorities in Gaza, following the detection of the first COVID-19 cases outside quarantine centres on 24 August. The authorities in Gaza have taken some measures to curb the pandemic included the closure of all education facilities. According to the OCHA report (2020), stated that all education facilities across the oPt have been closed. These include 3,037 schools, of which, 2,300 are in the West Bank and 737 are in Gaza, along with 2,017 kindergartens (KG). A total of 1,430,000 children are affected as a result, 57 per cent in the West Bank and 43 per cent in Gaza.The closure of schools will affect negatively the children’s learning outcomes and it will lead to increase in school drop-out as a result of this massive disruption to education access. As a response to school closure, the MOE prepared plans and moved to remote learning to ensure the continuation of education. However, this effort fell short of being sufficient. Many challenges were faced by teachers, students, and parents. These challenges hindered access to education and substantially impacted the quality of education for vulnerable communities. In specific, most students do not have access to e-learning because of some obstacles mainly involve infrastructure, weak internet networks, and power outage. According to the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics, only 30 percent of households in Gaza have a computer, while 73 percent have a basic internet connection, used for computers or smart phones. According to a June 2020 report by the Palestinian NGO Network, Gaza’s internet network crashes more than ten times per hour on average. Among other factors that affect negatively in education and led to school dropout is disability. The UN Women report titled “COVID-19: Gendered Impacts of the Pandemic in Palestine and Implications for Policy and Programming” that issued in April 2020 reported that almost half of the population in the West Bank and Gaza are children (2.3 million), where gender inequalities also exist. While 95 per cent of female children and 93 per cent of male children are enrolled in primary education, 38 per cent of children with disabilities are out of school. Young girls between the age of 6-15 who have a disability are less likely to be enrolled in school (30 per cent compared to 22.5 per cent of boys). Moreover, only 61 per cent of children are enrolled in secondary education where one out of four 15-year-old boys are out of school.Recent statistical report was received from the MOE showed the statistics of OOSC who dropped out of school in 2019-2020 in the east Gaza Directorate. The report pointed out that 1433 (553F & 880M) were dropped out of schools from grade 1-10. A total of 546 children (454M & 92F) aged from 9-15 years dropped out of schools. The report also shed the light on the Children with disabilities who dropped out of school in 2019-2020. A total of 614 children with disabilities (316 boys and 298 girls) dropped out of schools. Most of them suffer form speech difficulties, visual or hearing impairments, or physical disability. Thus, this project will provide the children at high risk of dropping out or dropped out already with the beginning of the academic year aged from 9-15 years with learning opportunities that improve their academic attainment and facilitate their return to schools, as well as developing and enjoying psychological well being.

2 - Activities

Before designing this project, PSCF has communicated with the MoE to identify their needs and priorities. The ministry provided us with the latest statistic report finalized in 2019-2020 showed the number of children who dropped out of schools in the east Gaza Directorate of Gaza strip, in addition to the reasons that led children to drop out of schools. Based on the report and recommendations of MoE, PSCF intends to work with the children who are at high risk of drop out and children dropped out of schools in the last year for reasons related to their health conditions, economic situation, psychological disorders, low academic achievement or having a disability. The intervention will target children aged from 9-15 years. The main activities that are proposed to be implemented include: - Conduction of catch up sessions in Arabic, English and Math for OOSC and children at high risk of drop out from grade 4-9.- 50 children will be re-integrated in the governmental schools.- Rehabilitate 8 schools: the rehabilitation includes toilet for children with disabilities, ramps, and handrails. - Developing 1 small studio with needed equipment to help teachers prepare and broadcast audio-visual interactive lessons.- Provision of education kits, uniforms, and assistive devices.- Providing individual and collective counseling sessions for target children. - Building the capacity of 200 male and female teachers in 10 schools through conducting 10 training courses (20 hrs per each course) on developing and implementing contingency plans, such as alternative education pathways, to mitigate impacts.- Deliver 12 awareness sessions for 200 teachers and 40 school students on better preparedness and response capacities improvement, including first aid training.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

During project implementation, PSCF will examine the internal environment of hosting CBOs to ensure healthy, safe and suitable premises for the project implementation of the activities to accommodate the children. Periodic visits will be conducted to ensure that the project locations are healthy, clean and safety. PSCF will ensure that the hosting organizations has a first aid package to be used in the emergency cases. In addition, PSCF will prepare code of conducts which will be read carefully by the staff before contracting with them. This code organizes the relationship between the employees and the beneficiaries. Moreover, the activities shall be culturally appropriate, ensure the quality of interaction and will be gender sensitive (In case of any disturbance regarding the mixed-gender teaching classes or psychology support).

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Throughout the project, teachers, including females, will be empowered to effectively respond to the education needs of the vulnerable target children including children with disabilities through communication and learning methods. The project psychosocial counselors will be supported to respond to the protection and psychosocial needs of children. Also, special arrangements will be set to ensure that cases of violence or abuse, including GBV, affecting children with disabilities are referred appropriately for the psychologists to receive support.
