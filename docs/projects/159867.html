Project Summary

This proposed response plan will focus on addressing the nutritional needs of the most vulnerable groups of the population in Aweil East, Aweil South Rumbek east and Panyijiar especially U5s and PLWs. The IRC will implement the proposed emergency response in 2020 for 12 months through strengthening the existing 68 static nutrition sites (65 OTPs and three Stabilization Centers), and by deploying six mobile teams in hard-to-reach areas (two in Aweil East , two in Panyijar and two in Aweil South) to improve the nutrition response coverage through rapid response mechanism. IRC has field offices located in Aweil East, Aweil South, Panyijar, Rumbek and Pamir and has longstanding relationships with the government, local authorities, and communities in these geographic areas. Furthermore, IRC provides health and nutrition programming in each of these states(Unity and NBEG), offering a foundation upon which to build strengthened nutrition service. This project will address life-saving humanitarian needs in Aweil East, Aweil South and Panyijar Counties, which are some of the counties with chronically high GAM rates of above the WHO emergency threshold. In Panyijiar County, GAM levels have consistently been above 15% as a result of insecurity and influx of IDPs from Leer and Mayendit counties as aggravating factors. In Rumbek East, IRC has scaled up the nutrition response from 3 to 23 OTP and the coverage of services increased the number of acute malnutrition enrolled in the nutrition program. The deteriorating food security in Rumbek east will continue aggravating the nutrition situation in the county. The project will integrate the health, child protection, WASH and food security sectors supported by UNICEF, WFP, OFDA, and HPF in the counties. The main components of this project are 4 and they include;1) Management of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) among children under five years of age; 2) Management of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) among children under five years of age; 3) Management of malnutrition among Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW); 4) Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCFE). Treatment will directly target children under 5 years of age (without discrimination between boys and girls) and PLWs. IRC will reach a total of 21, 611 (AE: 7,353, AS: 4,748, RE: 5,305, Pany: 4,205) SAM children; and 41,155 (AE:13,933, AS: 7,571, RE: 7,064, Pany: 12,587) under-five and 1,4704 (AE: 4,678, AS: 2,590, RE: 5,467, Pany: 1,970) PLW with MAM. Similarly, IRC plans to reach a total of 39,843 (AE: 12,315, AS: 2,1309, RE: 13,629 , Pany: 7130) primary caretakers of 0-23 months old children through MIYC and 140, 589 (AE: 66,005, AS: 4,748, RE: 38,488, Pany: 14,788 ) children 6-59 months though vitamin A supplementation. The project will work along with the health sector through a collaborative approach to ensure stabilization kits for SAM with medical complications are provided. Nutrition Cluster partners will provide malaria screening and referral/treatment for SAM and MAM cases. Through layering and collaboration of activities, the WASH Cluster will support partners in the county to ensure the provision of clean and safe water for OTP/TSFP services, including appetite tests, hand washing, and appropriate ablution facilities. FSL Cluster partners in the county shall conduct general food distribution sessions that target households with SAM and MAM beneficiaries. Throughout the project cycle, community outreach and mobilization will be conducted to ensure early detection and improved coverage of the nutrition services in the targeted areas by the mobile and outreach teams.

1 - Needs

The food security situation in South Sudan is expected to deteriorate in the post-harvest period of new year (Jan-April 2020) as household food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) are likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. The slight improvement in the food security situation in the 2019 lean season compared to the same period in 2018 is attributed to reduced insecurity after the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan in September 2018, which has led to better access to livelihoods and markets. Additionally, humanitarian access has improved during this period, further contributing to the increased access to food, nutrition and livelihood support, as well as service delivery. However, acute food insecurity persists, driven by localized conflicts, climatic shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality. According to the recent IPC for Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN) scale 58 counties including NBEG (all counties of NBEG) are classified as IPC AMN Phase 3. The overall nutrition situation during the lean season of 2019 is Critical and worse compared to the same period in 2018. About 1.2 million children 6-59 months are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2020 based on the results of the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS), and few SMART surveys conducted during the validity period before the analysis. The flooding in June 2019 has affected 46,680 individuals (7,780 HHs) across the greater Aweil region, displacing affected persons from their homes, destroying food and non-food items, submerging water sources (wells, hand pumps) and aggravating the already deteriorated food insecurity situation. Access to a nutritious food source has been affected, with livestock killed in the floods and farming lands disrupted. 100% of the 10,017 HHs interviewed as part of a multi-sectoral assessment conducted in Aweil in June 2019 reported that they have less than one-month worth of food stock available, and 80% reported that they consume one meal a day. This is compounded by the ongoing malnutrition crisis in the country. .According to the FEWSNET South Sudan Food Security Outlook report (June 2019-January 2020), although there are anticipated slight improvements in the food insecurity situation by the end of 2019, these improvements are expected to support mainly Stressed (IPC Phase 2) outcomes in some areas of the country, and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) will remain widespread, with several counties expected to continue to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes (including Aweil East, South Rumbek eats and panyiajar. The nutritional situation in in the proposed counties rages from Critical to serious nutritional status . The GAM rate in Aweil east is 15.5%, Aweil south 14.8%, Panyijar is at 17.2 % and Rumbek eats is rate of 11.2 %. The current IPC also projected that the nutrition situation during the lean season is expected to be worse across most of the counties. According to the FSNMS Round 24 report, the GAM rate at the national level had significantly increased from 13.1% to 16.1% that indicates the nutrition situation is still worsening. This is attributed to food insecurity, increasing market prices, insecurity, the lean season, sub-optimal Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practice, high mortality, and morbidity. According to the nutrition cluster in 2020, an estimated of 292,373 SAM under-five children and 1,008,696 under-five and 469,792 PLW with MAM need humanitarian nutrition assistance in the country. Similarly, in the four-county that IRC is proposing to provide nutrition response to an estimated 21,611 SAM under-five children and 41,155 under five with MAM in need of nutritional assistance in the year 2020.

2 - Activities

In the targeted period of one year, the nutrition Activities will mainly include the ones listed below; however not only limited to the listed ones because of layering and collaboration with other clusters. The primary activities will include;1-Provision of integrated CMAM and MIYCN service at 68 OTP/TSF sites and inpatients service at 3SC. 2-Transportation of nutrition supplies: provision of one to one /group counseling on key MIYCN messages to primary caretakers of 0-23 months children. This will involve the propositioning of nutrition supplies in hard to reach areas. 3- Formation of mother to mother support groups: This will involve training and establishment of mother to mother support groups (MtMSG) at the health facility and for community outreach. 4-Early detection and acute malnutrition and provision of timely life-saving nutritional treatment and care.Screening/testing and treatment of malaria and other illnesses (URTI, UTI) in outpatient therapeutic feeding programs (OTPs) among children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM). 5-Training of health workers on CMAM and maternal-infant and young child feeding (MIYCN): All the facility (stabilization center, OTP, and targeted supplementary feeding program) health and nutrition staff will be trained on the new South Sudan CMAM and MIYCN guidelines. 6-Training of community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) on screening, detection, and referral of malnutrition.7- Provision of soap for hand-washing: Caretakers with children in OTPs will be provided with hand-washing soap and will be oriented on hand-washing and safe preparation of food. 8-Initiation of breastfeeding and the provision of nutrition education in health facilities/maternity wards. 9-Cooking demonstrations: This will involve demonstrations on cooking and consumption of nutritious foods during pregnancy and complementary feeding.8- Establishment of kitchen gardens: The program will establish kitchen gardens at OTPs with hand pumps as part of demonstrating to caregivers how to cultivate nutritious foods. Provide rapid malaria attest for all SAM children and provide malaria treatment according to the national protocols. 9- Procurement of Stabilization Center (SC) infection prevention and control supplies. This includes detergents and other supplies in infection control at the SC.10-Conduct of health and nutrition campaigns: This includes national immunization days and nutrition calendar events. 11-Train all nutrition staff on GBV and establish a referral system and linkage between nutrition and GBV program. 12- Provide ECD training materials for all CNV's CNW and SC staff and provide ECD at the community level and counseling for SAM children and caretakers at SC and Link all acutely malnourished families to GFD.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The IRC also prioritizes highly vulnerable groups, including children and women, to improve their access to services. Caregivers will be encouraged to bring boys and girls U5 for screening and enrolment, should they meet the admissions criteria, and unaccompanied children of both genders will be linked to protection services as appropriate. Community and facility-level health education sessions will be provided to both genders to ensure each is aware of the health services available to them and their families. Further, PLWs and their male partners will be engaged in counseling sessions on optimal feeding practices in IYCF to promote positive behavior and healthy relations in the home. Additionally, the IRC works at the community level to minimize the vulnerability of women to gender-based violence (GBV) by minimizing the need to travel to receive services, through mobile teams. Information, education and communication materials developed will also be translated in the local language to ensure retention and will also be developed to ensure they are age-appropriate and gender-specific. Hence messages will be tailored to target women, men, boys, and girls. During a crisis like food insecurity and flooding, the vulnerability of women and children, especially girls, have only increased in an already patriarchal society. This intervention will support the immediate and urgent needs of PLW and adolescent girls and reduce their isolation and help them to build relationships and links to services. The IRC will engage and sensitize communities on GBV and the importance of gender equality, as well as work with the male group. The IRC will also ensure that there is gender balance in bodies that represent the interests of the community to ensure that women’s voices and concerns are at the forefront of community involvement throughout service provision.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The IRC will work closely with the local administration, including local chiefs, community leaders and the County Health Departments (CHDs) to ensure OTPs and TSFPs are open and accessible to community members at appropriate times (open when there is adequate daylight to ensure the safety for women and children accessing services). In order to ensure our services remain as responsive, relevant, and appropriate as possible, timely community feedback mechanisms will be established through meetings with key members (MTMSGs, CNVs, etc.). All information collected will be reviewed and used, as appropriate, to adjust and improve the intervention. The IRC will also conduct post-distribution monitoring to assess the beneficiary satisfaction levels for commodity actions.IRC will seek and consider the views of beneficiaries and community members throughout the project to ensure that their feedback and complaints are addressed in an effective and timely manner. The IRC has an established complaint feeding mechanisms at all OTP sites and beneficiary satisfaction interview is done quarterly by the nutrition staffs and M&E officer in the respected counties.
