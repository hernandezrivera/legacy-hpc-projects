Project Summary

BZ is proposing to implement "Small Grants Project" with an aim to support establishing 150 small businesses in Afrin for 150 families (e.i. 900 persons). The projects will focus on supporting the food security and livelihood sector in the region and supporting the local market in order to stabilize the markets and income of the people (IDPs/Host) in the targeted.
The project will include
1- Market assessment to identify current market needs and build on the ﬁndings to adapt the curriculum and selection criteria.
2- Later, the project will then inform interested community members on the ﬁndings and selection criteria;
3- Registration of the candidates who meet the criteria and start delivering specialized training on business and project management in three cycles. Each cycle will be for 50 participants from the 150 families.
4- By the end of the training, the project team receives business ideas ﬁlled out in a simple proposal form, from all trainees; and start analyzing and evaluating them according to a set criterion.
5- The project team will work with beneﬁciaries on developing business feasibility studies and setting business work.
Participants will be divided into two groups:
1- The ﬁrst one includes participants who developed a sustainable-proﬁtable project idea, and they will receive an in-kind grant to establish their business.
2- The second group includes participants who require further training and coaching to develop their ideas to a sustainable-proﬁtable project. The project will provide more in-depth training for these group members until their projects are ready for the establishment, they will then receive the in-kind grants and establish their businesses.
The project's team will make an agreement with local suppliers to provide equipment and raw materials to beneﬁciaries instead of giving cash grants. This will make sure that beneﬁciaries will hold up to the establishment of the business. The project's team will accompany beneﬁciaries through all procurement process and will continue providing on-the-go support for a period of three months after establishing the businesses.
We will target 50% females and 50% males during participants’ selection; this will guarantee that grants will also be distributed by the same percentage (50 females and 50 males). On the other hand, our beneficiaries are 50% IDPs and 50 % Host community and the B&Z team will take into consideration the PWDS in the selection criteria. They will work on have joint projects between the two communities to ease tension between the two communities.

Needs

According to “WoS Food Security and Livelihood Assessment (FSLA and CFSAM) - 2018”, number of civilians in Afrin, Aleppo reached 190,000 individuals (76,000 were identified as people in need); 30% of the population in this area are IDPs (most of them from rural of Damascus) while there are another 20% of returnees (who lack financial capacity to secure their needs), this means that almost 50% of the population is depending on humanitarian assistance. According to the same assessment, the percentage of people who has food security is “0%”, while 60% has marginally food security, and 10% in severe need for food security. Although the area was an agricultural one, and population of Afrin worked in agriculture and livestock market for decades; yet the area lives now in severe need to secure the population’s food; this goes back to the Syrian crisis and its developments; this development put Afrin in 2018 in a battle zone, and destroyed its heritage as “area number one in producing olive oil in Syria”. On the other side and according to the HNO 2019, the people in need for food in Afrin are 97336.According to BZ’s field team who conducted a basic needs assessment; the need for small businesses that focus on food production chain is very high (this includes: raw material sale, farmer support, livestock farms, seeds, fertilizers, pesticides and vaccines, irrigation systems, solar power, and selling points). shifting population from dependants on humanitarian assistance to self-dependence is also very required to take the next step toward early recovery from war results.

Protection Risk Analysis

BZ works through its programs to ensure beneficiaries’ safety and security, and integrate all necessary measures into activities to prevent any physical and psychosocial harm. All BZ centres has complaint boxes, all staff members are educated on complaints policy, and code of conduct (CoC should be the signed by each staff members after attending a session with HR), M&E and operations follow up on with staff to ensure full respect of Code of Conduct and protection measures through frequent meetings with beneficiaries, satisfaction surveys; and follow up on complaint cases with BZ main office discreetly, this ensures that the project complies with safety and protection measures during all its phases.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks are:1- There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. The Likelihood is Medium. The impact is High. The Mitigating measures are 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed; 2. Ensure consultation with communities perspectives; 3. Carry out safety audits for beneficiaries’ access to site locations. 2- Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across member. The Likelihood is High. The impact is High. The Mitigating measures are 1. Agree to minimum assistance package across the sector. 2. Assess and validate any changes before they occur. 3. Identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. 3- The risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. The Likelihood is High. The impact is High. The Mitigating measures are 1. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2. Train staff on the code of conduct 3. Code of conduct in all staff contracts Address any SEA complaints.
