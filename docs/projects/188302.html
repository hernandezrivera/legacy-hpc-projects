Project Summary

The designed project seeks to empower the child protection system within the current Nutrition Centres of Northern Western Syria, by ensuring that CP concerns and considerations are taken into the Nutrition sector of the humanitarian response to maximize the impact of the CP services in these communities and minimize the risks to children which may exacerbate from programming without children’s safety and will be taken into consideration.
MUSA will work on the following activities:
1-Integrating Child-friendly spaces with 6 Nutrition centers: Mercy-USA will target to engage 6,000 children in recreational, sustained child protection programmers.
2- 300 children will receive case management services through the Nutrition centers: Mercy-USA will employ a professional team in each center who can handle 50 cases(1 caseworker per center).
3-A specialized Child protection &PSS training to PSS workers; to ensure adequate programming: 12 PSS workers (2 from each center) and 6 caseworkers (one from each center)
4- A specialized Case Management training will be conducted on 6 caseworkers.
5- 2,000 parents will receive specialized PSS training using SHLS curricula and awareness-raising sessions to prevent and respond to child protection issues.
the project will take into consideration gender-sensitive and disability-friendly that would ensure the protection needs of boys and girls. as well as the needs of children with disabilities allowing adequate access to the Nutrition centers.

4 - Needs

Based on the HNO 2022, grave violations against children remain a significant concern including the risk of being killed, injured, recruited, used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted, and sexually abused. By Sep 21, 1,440 grave violations were verified by the MRM.Girls and boys continue to experience persisting violence including physical, emotional, sexual, and domestic violence. Insecurity, economic hardship, and Covid-19 exacerbate child protection concerns. These fueled harmful coping mechanisms, and increased child labor and child marriage, putting adolescent girls at particular risk.Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 26 percent of surveyed households, demonstrating the increasing cumulative toll on mental well-being with immediate and if not addressed, lifelong consequences. Numerous unaccompanied and separated children or children living with older caregivers, or living in institutions are at increased risk of exposure to violence. While MUSA has conducted an RNA in 6 N. centers in NWS, findings were as follows:-Mothers preferred to have a CFS inside the canters that provide safe spaces for their children through receiving the services & expressed their wish in receiving training on Parenting skills that support in dealing with their children.-Children have no access to play and PSS opportunities: Which could add a burden on their parents and the host families and they lose the opportunity to engage in safe,fun, and child-friendly activities.

5 - Implementation of activities

MUSA will use its current Nutrition centers in Idleb& Aleppo that target CU5& PLWs to integrate CP activities into N. activities which ensures the provision of integrated safe services to the vulnerable people in the same area.The PSS activities will target 7,500 children between 2-8 years in addition to storytelling. 2 PSS facilitators will be responsible for conducting the PSS activities for children in each center and 1 PSS worker will conduct parenting skills sessions, The Parenting Skills intervention will promote the well-being of children through improving parents’ stress management, fostering positive parenting practices, and providing parents with strategies to support children’s and adolescents’ psychosocial needs(12 sessions for 2000 parents who have children U12 years of old).2000 individuals will be reached through awareness-raising to prevent and respond to CP issues,18 workers will receive CPIE, PSS&CSG training. 6 CWs will receive CM training and will follow the cases until closing targeting 300 children.12 advocacy meetings in CP issues for Humanitarian workers and CPC. A performance measurement plan will be developed to guide the implementation. Performance tracking of indicators will be conducted through a specially-designed performance tracking tool adapted to the performance framework. complaints and FARMs output monitoring, satisfaction surveys, will be conducted routinely to assess whether the targeting process was effective, transparent, and fair.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Specialized CP services (CM) for girls and boys are available in targeted locations - Protection risk: Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys, and their families if staff are not appropriately trained or if client data is not managed appropriately. - Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High, mitigating measures: 1. ensure training packages are in line with CP minimum standards and consider remote managed context 2. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received anddemonstrated proficiency in training modules. 3. CP messaging to non-Protection actors.Strengthen human resource capacity to respond to CP concerns in Syria - Protection risk: Challenges in measuring the impact of capacity development; compromised quality of training due to contextual constraints; high turnover of staff - Likelihood: Low. Impact: High - Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure training programs are designed to assess the impact of the training. 2. Link with other capacity-building initiatives to ensure synergy 3. Support development of a cohort of core child protection trainers in Syria 4. Encourage follow-up, mentoring, and coaching initiatives. Equip the Nutrition centers with CFS equipment - Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members - Likelihood: High. Impact: High -Mitigating measures: 1. Communicate regularly to the community about the available services. 2. Provide training to staff and service providers for PSEA, COC, and CSG policies.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ercy-USA will maintain protection mainstreaming in all project steps as follows: • List of PLWs and Children under 5 will be identified, including disabled and chronically ill children and mothers.• Monitor unaccompanied and separated children admitted into nutrition programs and coordinating with staff in terms of defaulters; • The project is designed to identify any sign of abuse or violence among PLW and children, all cases will be referred to pre-identified centers• A confidential complaint and protection reporting mechanism system will be designed and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents or violations.•Mercy-USA has different channels for information dissemination which includes but not limited to WhatsApp groups, IEC materials, fixed posters and banners •PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (track and report):- PSEA posters will be distributed to beneficiaries- PSEA banners will be placed at the project site/community center (or other service centers)- PSEA videos will be shared with beneficiaries on social media platforms / played at service centers- PSEA messaging/videos and hotline information will be shared with beneficiaries through SMS/WhatsApp messaging• All project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies, in order to ensure full adherence with Do No Harm principle with strict dealing with Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and GBV issues


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: • A safe environment allows the staff to implement the activities (low airstrike bombing). • No delay in the provision of financial resources. • Engagement of local community with the committees. • Commitment of partner groups to Mercy-USA’s SOP. • Availability of services providers or services required as part of the case management plan. Risks: Safeguarding incidents (Medium) Mercy-USA has well-established. safeguarding allegation system that is accessible by children, families, and community members. Each activity is assigned an HR officer is made aware of how to report incidents through multiple ways: online system, verbal communication with HR staff, and complaint boxes. Community acceptance (Medium) Before engaging in any new community Mercy-USA teams establish formal relationships with independent civil entities. The risk to personal safety of field staff (Medium) Mercy-USA has a duty of care policy and all staff is trained on safety measures including emergency locators.Security incidents (Medium to High) Mercy-USA has a strong security department with qualified staff that monitors the security situation and put in place prevention and response plans .all staff receives S&S training. Poor staff capacity (Medium) Mercy-USA invests in 4 weeks of training of staff when they join, and ongoing coaching and supervision processes are in place.
