Project Summary

FSNAU post Gu 2020 assessment report indicated that 5.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance across Somalia this was due to the combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation,COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks , In addition, 849,900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021. The 2020 Gu season exhibited erratic performance. The rains started as early as late March in many parts of Somalia, then intensified and expanded in April, with heavy rain leading to riverine and flash floods. However, rainfall in May was widely below average. Severe riverine and flash floods in April and early May caused significant population displacement and damage to property, infrastructure, farmland, and crops.
Despite multiple shocks in 2020, high levels of sustained humanitarian assistance and government support have played a critical role in stabilizing and preventing worse acute food insecurity outcomes in Somalia. According to the Somalia Food Security Cluster, an average of 1.85 million people received food assistance monthly from April to August. Approximately 400 000 people also benefitted from government-led rural and urban safety net programmes. In April, government has also enacted a 20-100 percent tax exemption on imported food commodities in order to mitigate the socio-economic impact of COVID-19 on the population. Based on results of assessments conducted in July and August 2020, approximately 1.3 million people are currently facing Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or 1) higher) outcomes through September 2020 in the presence of humanitarian food assistance. An additional 2.5 million people are Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people experiencing acute food insecurity to 3.8 million
COVID-19 and Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2020. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly.
HIDIG project will therefore, intends to provide immediate access to unconditional and conditional cash assistance to 7,200 most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in IDPs and host communities, it will also provide capacity enhancement, farming tools as well as farm inputs to these target farmers in Beletweyne as well as agricultural seed inputs and tools to increase their farming production thus improving the nutrition of the target population in Bakol and Hiran region. The project will provide cash for work to enable provide immediate access to 800 household income thus improving the overall well-being of the target communities. The project will ensure inclusion of poor women, girls including disable population thus ensuring protection of the vulnerable household in the target location.HIDIG will ensure to coordinates the foods security and livelihood activities as well as the protection actors closely and concern ministries at regional and federal level to ensure better information sharing.

Project Activities

1.Provision of emergency unconditional cash transfer to 7,200 (5,000 IDPs and 1,200 HC) vulnerable IDP and host community households considering 60% of the beneﬁciaries registered as vulnerable women household affected COVID-19 and desert locust in Bay, Bakol and Hiran region south central Somalia. Each household will have received 84 USD per months for 3 months’ period. The unconditional cash transfer will provide immediate life-saving and life-sustaining opportunity among the vulnerable communities in emergencies hence this will be enhancing the household food basket thus improving the livelihood. 2.Provide cash for work activities to 800 men and women household heads and enable create short term job opportunities and create income to the vulnerable drought displaced and AWD affected IDPs and host communities to enable improve the community livelihood structure hence provide alternative livelihood. Each of the cash for works (CFW) will be given $ 5 per day for 24 working days for four months on communal asset rehabilitation, this will enhanced their livelihood of the target communities 3.Sensitize vulnerable household beneﬁting from unconditional cash transfer on nutrition and hygiene promotion for food preparation to enable reduce malnutrition and disease hence improve community living standard. 4.Conduct training to capacity building farmers (48 men and 62 women household headed) on proper preparing, harvesting as well as management of farm inputs storage hence enhancing their knowledge and thus inculcate skills for their livelihood in futures

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Due to the triple affected of locust infestation, flooding and global COVID-19 pandemic many vulnerable households in Bay, Bakool and Hiran region have been widely affected and could not afford the basic needs. This patterns, and the power balance within the families. Elderly people have also been affected due to lack of essential food and medicines and reduced availability of care. Therefore, the project will particularly target these vulnerable groups, which includes women, children, elderly persons and ethnic minority groups in the target regions in South central Somalia, people with disability will be considered in the inclusion of the project beneficiaries to enable benefits from lifesaving assistance to protect them from further harm and refraining them from adopting negative coping mechanisms. Direct beneficiary’s selection will be ‘inclusive’ and based on the consultation with communities by the respective local administration following the humanitarian principles. A proposed list of beneficiaries (from community consultations) will be validated by the project in a door-to-door beneficiary verification exercise. Beneficiary selection shall not be restricted by documentation provided available with the beneficiaries and solely the selection criteria shall be followed. Local authorities and relevant government departments will be consulted for final selection of beneficiaries at the targeted areas. Additionally, affected communities and local government authorities will be engaged in identification of the beneficiaries most in need. The selected beneficiaries will be registered and provided with unique cards for future verification and monitoring purposes. The cash will be transferred through mobile company/financial service provider which will be selected based on availability of network and/or services, readiness and ability to provide a prompt delivery, and reasonable service charge, thus HIDIG organization propose an inclusive assistance through provision of unconditional cash assistance and conditional cash grant is the most appropriate intervention at this immediate response stage and will allow beneficiaries to priorities their most pressing need

Mitigation Measures

This project targets areas with high concentration of IDPs including Baidoa and Beletweyne and pastoralist in Ceelbarde and Rabdhure therefore possible community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations who may face animosity from host communities who already feel overburdened and with limited services. With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing permanent structure or own a land for food security and livelihood center may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care. To overcome these challenges, HIDIG will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive access to services within the IDP camps by establishing cordial relationship between them and with the host communities and local authorities. Based on the food security and livelihood Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced and host communities will be consulted about needs and assess whether education interventions could cause tension, harassment or conﬂict between the IDPs and host communities. Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. HIDIG intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of IDPs beneﬁciaries and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor health and food security and livelihood infrastructure, many centers do not have proper planning so that those with disabilities can easily seek services. However, HIDIG will ensure services provided reach the most vulnerable IDPs and pastoralist in the far areas through identiﬁcation and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the cash collection sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement on better livelihood. Building community structures including local temporary spaces will provided in consultation with community/member who owns the land to ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conﬂict and violence. Communities ﬂeeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate food security and livelihood services without any discrimination .Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence
