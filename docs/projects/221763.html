Project Summary

Tonj East and Tonj South in Warrap State has constantly experienced adverse multiple forms of humanitarian emergencies instigated by the protracted intra-communal violence, influx of returnees and refugees from neighboring Sudan, insufficient rainfall and flooding that led to crops yield failure. About 421,059 people (276,371 in Tonj East and 144,688 in Tonj North) are living in the areas. The animals-crops husbandry and fishing are the main sources of living and economic activities in the region. The poor household food security, suboptimal MIYCN practice, improper child and maternal care, lack of access to clean water, lack of sanitation, increased prevalence of diseases and conflict resulted from cattle raids are the underline causes of the malnutrition and aggravating factors which puts at risk the nutritional status of the vulnerable population. High levels of malnutrition and low access to Primary Health Care services for mothers and children, mostly due to inaccessibility, health activity funding constrained, poverty and limited awareness on health risks, contribute to the extremely alarming levels of child morbidity and mortality in the target counties. Under the Country strategic planning framework of the multi-year FLA and PCA for nutrition program, SSGID is the Implementing Partner in Tonj East and South since Jan 2018, after SSGID- CCM Foundation merged with SSGID in March 2023. This ensuring the effective and continuum management of both MAM and SAM cases. The current active PCA and FLA with UNICEF and WFP supports 23 fixed OTP/TSFP sites (13 in Tonj East/MLH, 10 in Tonj North, and supported 5Stabilization Centers, 3 in Tonj East including Marial Lou and 2 in Tonj North). With the looming situation of acute malnutrition, SSGID submitted proposal to WFP and UNICEF to continue support the same facilities with nutrition intervention in2024 to 2025. The organization has also secured its parallel funding from Italian Agency for Development Corporation (AICS)which supports 4 nutrition outreach sites in hard-to-reach locations in Tonj East and Tonj North Counties and this grant will end on 31st March 2024.With this proposed HRP 2024, SSGID aims to directly reach additional 147,994 (40%) of 366,967 people in needs in the two Counties. In Tonj East (57,639 U5) – 34,583 girls, 23,056boys, (29,086 Adult) - 17,452 Lactating Women, 11,634 Pregnant women while in Tonj North(41,319 -U5)-24,791 Girls, 16,528 boys, (19,951 Adult) – 11,971 lactating Women, 7,980 pregnant women who will directly benefit from the project. The budget needed to augment our interventions and to meet the needs of these targeted beneficiaries in both Counties is $ 375,883.51 in cash. SSGID will intensify on MIYCN key message counselling, GBV messages, kitchen gardening and family MUAC training for caregivers/mothers who will be self-referring their sick children to the nearby nutrition site for prompt management by skill trained health workers and viable referral system for very sick children will be in place between low- and high-level nutrition sites. Nevertheless, the shortfall of nutrition supplies, insecurity, funding constrained, calamitous events like (flooding & draught)plus conflict of interest from the parties may hindered the smooth implementation of this proposed scale up nutrition interventions to the affected population in the targeted county of Warrap State.

1. Needs

Tonj East (TE) and Tonj North (TN) are two of the six Counties of Warrap State with an estimated population of 421,059 people (276,371 in TE and 144,688 in TN), The latest IPC 2023 result stated that, about 295,029 children to be affected by acute malnutrition and in urgent need of nutrition service in Warrap state – accounting for 18% of the burden of acute malnutrition countrywide. Low Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD), Minimum Meal Frequency (MMF), Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD); high prevalence of diarrhea, Malaria and Acute Respiratory Infection (ARI) and poor access to improved sanitation are the major driving factors of acute malnutrition in the State. The two counties of (Tonj East and Tonj North) were classified in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious) between July and September 2024. About 37%of the population in the State was said to be in Crisis (IPC Phase3) or worse acute food insecurity between September and November 2023. However, improvement has been expected in 2 counties of Tonj East and Tonj North during the post-harvest season between October 2024 to March 2025 but this will not result in change of IPC AMN phase classifications. An estimated 44% of the population of Warrap state which inclusive of Tonj East and Tonj North has been projected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase3) or worse acute food insecurity between December 2024 and March 2025. Deterioration was expected during the lean season between April and July 2024, with about 55% of the state’s population inclusive of Tonj East and Tonj South will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. In line with the HRP2024-2026, SSGID aims to continue interventions that address the nutritional needs of children under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs). The nutrition needs for girls, boys and women will be prioritized through provision of child and women friendly spaces at all the nutrition sites, as well as toys and recreational facilities in the stabilization centers to enhance quick recovery and cognitive development for children admitted in the programme. Powerful synergies between nutrition and Primary Health Care will be ensured through effective integration of services, viable referral system and promoting a community-based approach and by training health/nutrition workers on CMAM, integrated MIYCN, water and sanitation (WASH) and Gender-Based Violence. Efforts will be exerted on strengthening the capacity of the County Health Departments and local actors to deliver a multi-sectoral and harmonized approach to reduce malnutrition and change the nutritional behaviors of the community through implementation of nutrition sensitive programme like kitchen gardening and cooking demonstration sessions. Nutrition outreach sites will be supported in areas without fix centres to ensure easy and convenient access. We will conduct joint supportive supervision and monitoring with stakeholders for effective and quality service delivery to the intended vulnerable groups.

2. Project activities

In line with the needs identified, SSGID proposed to support the implementation of the following activities; •Establish demonstration kitchen gardens, promote seed multiplication in all the nutrition sites and in the community by providing initial variety of vegetable and fruit seeds for mother-to-mother support groups and community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) for planting.•Strengthen family MUAC screening for early detection of malnutrition among children under five by their caregivers and active screening of both children under five and PLW by CNVs.Support MIYCN counselling, community promotion breastfeeding events (World breastfeeding week) in the proposed Counties.•Severely acute malnourished children admitted in outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP)/Stabilization centers (SCs) will be screened and treated for malaria, diarrhoea and pneumonia and given WASH kits, and sensitize communities on hygiene and sanitation. •Massive screening and supplementation of vitamin A and deworming twice a year for all the children 6-59 months in the community will be conducted by trained CNVs. •Integration of education activities in provision of care for children with SAM in OTP and SCs by creating recreational ground and availing early childhood development materials (ECD) materials for stimulation. •Scale up integrated community outreaches (immunization, growth monitoring and antenatal care) with provision of ready to use therapeutic food (RUTF), ready to use supplementary food (RUSF), corn soya blend plus plus (CSB++) and routine medications at 4 sites in TE and 4 in TN to increase service coverage. •Mainstreaming of child protection through creation of child and women- friendly space (breastfeeding corners) and conducting audit assessment on gender-based violence (GBV) risk mitigation at all nutrition sites. •Nutrition assessments (SMART Survey) would be conducted in the counties in order to obtain the update and reliable data for planning, monitoring of the nutrition situation and for appropriate responses. •Joint nutrition coordination and program review meeting with the government, implementing partners and donors will be conducted.
