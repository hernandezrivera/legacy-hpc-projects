Project Summary

In the years 2024 and 2025, the Shafak Organization proposes a project aimed at mitigating and responding to protection risks faced by conflict-affected individuals in theAfrin, Jarablus, Al-Bab, and Azaz districts of Aleppo governorate, as well as in the Harim, Ariha, Jisr Ash Shugur and Idleb districts of Idleb governorate. The initiative seeks to empower local communities and individuals affected by conflict in Northwest Syria, fostering their role in societal building and determining their future.
The project will provide protection services and emergency assistance to 88,500 internally displaced persons (IDPs) 77895 and members of the host community 10605. The comprehensive protection services will encompass mitigation and response, as well as specialized and non-specialized protection services. The overall target includes 32500 Male, 56000 Female project target and 5000 Persons with disabilities project target.
The proposed project will offer gender and age-appropriate general protection services, including structured and recreational psychosocial support, recreational and mental health psychosocial support activities, case management, life-saving assistance, referral to specialized services, awareness-raising on protection-related topics, life skills and vocational training, legal assistance, and legal awareness sessions on housing, land, and property rights and child safeguarding. Community committees will be established and trained to enhance protection through CPMS, safe identification, referral, and communication skills.
To ensure inclusivity, Shafak will maintain a gender-balanced team and deliver training on housing, land, and property rights and child safeguarding to legal staff and community actors in Syria and Turkey. The services will prioritize vulnerable groups, such as women-headed households, children-headed households, persons with disabilities, elderly women and men, families with children at risk of child labor, unaccompanied orphans, and newly displaced individuals without financial means.
In emergencies, the project activities will swiftly respond to the urgent protection needs of newly displaced people, offering a protection emergency package, including psychological first aid, referrals, and emergency case fund support. The initiative will uphold principles of equity and non-discrimination, respecting the basic social and human rights of participants while considering the cultural, historical, and social specificities of the targeted populations. All static centers will be rehabilitated and equipped to be disability-friendly spaces.
In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs. (HNO, Summary, 2024). Within the protection sector, numbers indicate an increase in the number of PiN in 2024 to 2,375,850 in Idleb, and 3,566,142 in Aleppo who are in dire need for protection services.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project activities will be implemented through static community centers, protection tents, and outreach teams. These include establishing 4 community centers, providing of PSS through CM services, Provision of cash assistance and to at heightened protection risk individuals and HHs for mitigation to 2000 individuals, distribution of in kind assistance for women and children at risk offering general case management services to 1000 individuals, and facilitating Individual PSS sessions for 1500 BNFs. while group-based activities for 15000 people. Awareness sessions on protection topics and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will reach 45,000 individuals. provision of consultation and information sessions conducted in line with HLP AOR communication with communities strategy to address questions related to the safeguarding process and Civil Status Documentation (CSD) will be delivered to 22,500 people, and the legal team will provide counseling and assistance, including civil documentation and HLP issues, to 2700 beneficiaries. Additionally, 550 staff and humanitarian workers will receive training on HLP, CSD, gender, and protection mainstreaming. The project will support 15 community-based protection structures and conduct Protection Mainstreaming (PM) assessments in cooperation with the PMAWG in 50 communities. Monitoring visits will be conducted by male and female Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) staff using various tools. The Protection Risk Analysis identifies potential risks, such as uneven access to services, tension in the community, exploitation or abuse by staff, and risks related to providing information about documentation. Mitigation measures include consultation with at-risk communities, establishing selection criteria mechanisms, clear communication about available services, and training staff on Codes of Conduct (CoC) and PSEA. The PSEA strategy addresses the risks of sexual exploitation and abuse, focusing on awareness sessions, staff training, and the establishment of reporting mechanisms. The Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures section highlights potential challenges such as limited access due to insecurity, disputes between host communities and IDPs, and outlines mitigation strategies like close monitoring, security-related SOPs, and clear verification processes.
