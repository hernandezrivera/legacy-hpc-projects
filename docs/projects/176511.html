Project Summary

Providing Multi-sectorial response to refugees in Unity State.
The overall objective of the proposed intervention to protect refugees and provide multi-sectorial support in term of Shelter, CCCM, Protection, Food security and Livelihoods in Pamir and Jamjang Refugees camps (DRC will support new arrivals with emergency shelter materials and for protracted refugees continue to partner with UNHCR in meeting outstanding and emerging shelter gaps. In CCCM for Ajuoung Thok and Pamir refugee camps, DRC will continue providing essential camp management services. These will include strengthening community engagement by building the capacity of the refugee leadership structures to ensure that they are empowered and self-reliant.
In FSL, DRC will continue to commence the food security and livelihoods activities by providing skills development through vocational trainings, supporting natural resource management by introducing new seedlings and agroforestry approaches to save protect the environment and creating tree nursery as well as building on capacities to produce more energy saving stoves to reduce the travel time for collecting firewood’s. Agriculture inputs will be provided to woodlots committee to establish the small gardens in the plots close to their wood lot areas. Additionally, start up kits besides the skills development’s trainings will be provided in order to allow graduated beneficiaries to start the business in respective disciplines.
In protection, DRC will ensure protection services are available to women and girls, boys and men. DRC will facilitate access to life-saving protection assistance by working with community networks to identify and strengthen positive coping mechanisms and develop mitigation strategies for identified protection risks.

1 - Needs

An estimated 310,000 refugees have sought refuge in South Sudan. The majority of refugees coming from South Kordofan and Blue Nile states in Sudan fled into Unity and Upper Nile states in South Sudan during the period 2011-2012. In 2020 the number of refugees got slightly increased since South Sudan maintained an open-door policy during the COVID-19 pandemic. Pariang county in Unity State currently hosts 82,591 refugees in Ajoung Thok and Pamir refugee camps while Yida settlement has an estimated number of 39,000 refugees. As per the 2021 HNO analysis, the humanitarian needs in Pariang county are less severe compared to 2020; nevertheless, Pariang hosts a significant number of refugees and their needs will require to be addressed urgently. As the situation in Sudan remains non-conducive for returns, refugees remain in need of camp management services while to ensure ownership and peaceful coexistence amongst the refugee community structures and host community the community engagement should be strengthened and information sharing should be timely and accurate. In addition, participation of women in refugee community structures remains low so there is the need to continue supporting these structures to represent the needs of the different groups in their community. Due to the current COVID-19 reality, refugees require information and inclusion on COVID-19 messaging as well as being able to express their concerns and provide their feedback. Apart from the refugees who are currently in South Sudan, there are approximately 2.2 million South Sudanese refugees in neighboring countries. Despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, UNHCR’s position on refuge returns states that the conditions are not yet considered conducive for the safe, dignified and sustainable returns. However, an estimated 110,000 refugees returned to South Sudan in a self-organized manner during 2020 driven by family reunification, limited livelihoods opportunities and insufficient access to basic services in the countries of asylum. Almost one in five refugee returnees reports not having access to their houses due to damage or being occupied by others. When arriving back to South Sudan after protracted displacement, refugee returnees lack adequate information about their areas of return and available services. Due to the high needs of the population in South Sudan and the limited available resources, refugee returnees face challenges receiving basic services in their areas of return. This increases the vulnerability of the returnees who are vulnerable or with specific needs as they might not be able to continue receiving the level of assistance they need in return locations.

2 - Project activities

For new arrivals and relocation caseloads, DRC shall focus on the provision of timely emergency shelter materials after the refugees have been received by both DRC and UNHCR in the relevant camp. The intervention shall involve the distribution of emergency shelter materials (either plastic sheets, shelter tool kit and wooden poles) or family tents. Referral cases shall be dealt with on a case by case and materials provided shall be determined by UNHCR jointly with DRC. For protracted refugees, DRC will continue to partner with UNHCR in meeting outstanding and emerging shelter gaps, through participatory approaches in which beneficiaries contribute and oversee a substantial part of the shelter developments. DRC’s role in the process will be to mobilize beneficiaries to make mud bricks and construct walls in addition DRC will provide tools and access to marram and water, alongside supervision and technical support. Beneficiaries classified as persons with specific needs (PWSN) including women (in particular female-headed households), child-headed households, older persons, and persons with disabilities shall be provided with complete transitional shelter. The criteria have been developed to help identify those most in need of assistance from the moment they settle in Jamjang refugee camps. DRC and UNHCR shall work closely on the verification of individuals to be assisted ensuring the most in need are prioritized for shelter assistance.As the camp management in Ajoung Thok and Pamir refugee camps in Unity state, DRC will continue providing essential camp management services. These will include strengthening community engagement by building the capacity of the refugee leadership structures to ensure that they are empowered and self-reliant. Wherever possible, refugees and host community will participate in common activities to ensure peaceful coexistence. DRC will continue to commence the food security and livelihoods activities by providing skills development through vocational trainings, supporting natural resource management by introducing new seedlings and agroforestry approaches to save protect the environment and creating tree nursery as well as building on capacities to produce more energy saving stoves to reduce the travel time for collecting firewood’s. DRC will also provide agriculture inputs to woodlots committee to establish the small gardens in the plots close to their wood lot areas. Startup kits will be provided to the skills development graduates to allow the beneficiaries to start the business in respective disciplines.DRC general protection activities will ensure protection services are available to women and girls, boys and men. DRC will facilitate access to life-saving protection assistance by working with community networks to identify and strengthen positive coping mechanisms and develop mitigation strategies for identified protection risks. DRC will conduct protection assessments and monitoring to identify PSNs, provide individual protection assistance, strengthen referral mechanisms for specialized services provide group and individual psychosocial support and case management services for GBV survivors including operationalization of women and girls friendly spaces. Additionally, bringing together the host and refugee populations in joint discussions and encouraging them to collaborate within and outside of agency initiatives, the relationship between the refugee and host communities shall be strengthened and peaceful coexistence will be promoted.

Yes

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DRC conducts regular awareness raising with beneficiaries on PSEA, including reporting mechanisms, as well as provides information on PSEA to beneficiaries in multiple formats.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Considering the importance of access to information and feedback provision, DRC will operate the complaints and feedback and Community-Based Protection desks in the two camps, ensuring people are aware of where and who to approach when they need support, and/or where to channel their complaints and feedback. DRC will also maintain roving complaint and feedback desks in outer camp zones apart from the static desks in the camps, in order to support community requests for services and information. Through verification visits to verify requests for ad-hoc CRI and material items and other formal and informal interactions with persons with specific needs, DRC will also ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members of the community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through the coordination meetings with the community governance structures DRC provides information about the project and the complaint and feedback mechanism. In addition, DRC conducts community awareness sessions with the communities to ensure a two-way communication: provide information to beneficiaries and receive their feedback. Regular activity recording with Internews/Jamjang FM is another activity DRC implements to engage communities, disseminate information, and respond to community feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During the design phase of the project, DRC engages community and other relevant stakeholders in the need assessments and uses their feedback to design the project activities based on their needs. The regular engagement of communities and stakeholders informs the project design, their prioritized activities and modality of assistance. DRC has a functional Feedback, Complaints, Response Mechanism (FCRM) that allows beneficiaries to give feedback and complaints regarding the activities being implemented in their respective locations. The DRC F-CRM desks are set up to receive complaints and questions from beneficiaries and follow-up with feedback, including referrals to external service-providers or to the DRC protection desks when appropriate, either immediately or within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the nature of the complaint. DRC has a standardized FCRM form where all feedback and complaints are captured. All data captured on the FCRM forms is then uploaded onto a password protected database where all feedback and complaints are tracked until they are closed. Furthermore, beneficiary complaints and feedback will also be captured when conducting post distribution monitoring (PDM). Beneficiaries will be able to provide their opinions on how DRC interventions can be improved and they will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or make a complaint.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.1:DRC is protection focused organization and takes significant measures throughout the project cycle. DRC will conduct the registration of beneficiaries in their respective locations in order to reduce the risk of being exposed. DRC will also work closely with local leaders, project management committees and beneficiaries to identify near by location for the distribution where beneficiaries are not posed for any protection risk. Distribution time and entitlements would be shared a head of distribution to the beneficiaries in order to allowing them to receive the assistance on time. DRC field staff will closely work and assist beneficiaries to receive their entitlements in all location. DRC adheres the do no harm principles and demonstrates in its all projects. 8.2: Women and girls face an increased risk of sexual violence when accessing humanitarian assistance. Considering this, DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming across its programming and seek to reduce/mitigate protection risks that may be incurred during the course of the project. Examples, of steps that will be taken by DRC include engagement of beneficiary members around selection of the locations for distributions and screening of locations with a GBV risk assessments.8.5:DRC will carry out sensitisation among its own team members to raise their awareness about the need to engage members from all segments including those with disabilities in the consultations/project implementation. DRC will also carry out home to home visits to ensure the inclusion of community members who may be unable to leave their household due to physical or cultural/social restrictions.8.6:DRC has strong complaint response mechanism in all intervened areas where beneficiaries’ have access to information and provide feedback, register their concerns by using CRM system. DRC has placed CRM boxes and displayed banners for the easy use of CRM systems. Beneficiary are oriented on the use of CRM where they can share their concerns. DRC field staff conduct the joint community sessions as well as individually share the CRM and its way of working.8.7:DRC understands the data protection sensitivity and did not collect the sensitive personal data and all the data is only used for the respective projects. Before collection of data, beneficiaries are asked about the concerns and the use of data. 8.8:Due to the risk of tension between the host and refugee community over resources and services. DRC will target both the host and refugee community in this response and all actions will be implemented through a conflict sensitivity lens. In addition, DRC will implement peaceful coexistence initiatives that support the development of positive relations between the host and refugee community
