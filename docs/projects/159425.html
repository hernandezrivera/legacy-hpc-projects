Project Summary

In response to the continued growing humanitarian crisis as a result of conflict in South Sudan, Confident Children out of Conflict (CCOC) is proposing the following interventions to expand lifesaving Child protection response services to vulnerable populations in Juba, Central Equatorial State. The complexity of the operating environment requires multifaceted approaches that are tailored to each location including: FTR, CB PSS, PSS and maintaining a functional interim care Centre for UASC and most vulnerable children in need of protection and response services in Juba Central Equatoria State. The proposed one year child protection in emergency intervention is designed to: • Increase access for UASC and OVC to life saving child protection services i.e. FTR case management process, Psychosocial support (PSS) services through CFS including Non CFS psychosocial support (CBPSS). Integrate protection monitoring and mainstreaming into the Child Protection in emergency actions to improve the overall context analysis and integration of protection principles in the intervention that will be scaled up to respond to the identified needs. • Enhance understanding and analysis among staff and communities and other actors on the child protection context and needs. CCOC will utilize and increase on the existing internal emergency response capacity to staff and be able to provide timely, effective and efficient Child protection (FTR), PSS and ICC services for UASCs during the project period. This will offer an opportunity to provide much needed CPiE humanitarian services in Juba IDP urban settlements to areas with limited child protection services and in addition, strengthen Child Protection interventions in Gumbo, Mahad, IDP camps and the Host communities in Juba city. The child protection intervention has been specifically designed to be integrated within CCOC’s existing child protection program in Juba City, which are aimed at strengthening community based protection mechanisms (CBPM) and facilitate access to information and appropriate services. • Through existing Community Protection Committees (CPC), provide a platform to establish community based child protection mechanisms (CBCPMs), Child Help Desks and Child Rights clubs (CRC’s) in primary schools to facilitate the identification of and response to child protection concerns, the development of community based mitigation plans, prevention messaging (e.g. on forced early marriages etc.) and child protection surveillance mechanisms. In addition, the CBCPMs will identify and refer vulnerable children in need of individual support. • Operate six community based safe healing and learning spaces for children that facilitate their recovery, restore a sense of normalcy, and promote their cognitive, physical, social and emotional development and provide parenting support. • Youth/Adolescents engagement to provide peer support, recreational activities and referral to other multi-sector support services as an entry point to develop future intervention with Children Associated with Armed Forces/Groups and school dropouts. This proposed child protection project will continue to strengthen the resilience of Children, caregivers and their families. CCOC’s own experience testifies to post-traumatic distress occurring in high numbers in war-affected children. Children affected by conflict need to be assisted to use and rely on adequate coping mechanisms, which include developing self-confidence and expression of emotions. An integrated approach to ensure broad community support for psychosocial support to both children and their caregivers is critical to ensure children are adequately cared for. CCOC’s ongoing work through child friendly spaces will continue but this project will ensure they are more deeply rooted and owned by the community.

Implementing Partners

CCOC’s care for children depends on close relationships with many actors in the state. In addition to the State Ministry of Social Development CES (who bear the primary responsibility of care), quality care for children depends on good bilateral and multilateral relationships with partner agencies. CCOC will continue to build strong relationships with agencies such as ISRaAID, NP, Save, IRC, ICRC, USRATUNA, UNICEF, UNFPA, UNHCR, and Intersos to ensure cases referred for interim care and safety are resolved appropriately and in a timely manner. CCOC also remains committed to strong coordination through the child protection sub clusters and its working groups at state and national levels. CCOC will also continue to play a central role in coordination in the locations of operation through camp partners’ meetings, and the Urban Actors Group which frequently provide solutions to cases outside CCOC’s area of expertise. For FTR, CCOC will coordinate with the FTR WG, Save the Children, and UNICEF. Additionally, CCOC will mentor at least 3 SMoSD CES social workers in training foster families and supporting family tracing and as well care for UASC.

1 - Needs

Since the signing of the Revitalized Agreement of the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) on 12 September 2018, South Sudan has seen a gradual stabilization of the security situation in some areas previously affected by conflict. However, OCHA estimated 7.2 million people needed assistance, up from 7.1 million estimated in the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview. Displacements remain high, with children being those most affected, including through lack of basic safety and security, separation from family, and access to core health and education services. In reference to UNICEF South Sudan protection briefing note as of August 2019, 19,920 girls and boys have been identified and registered as unaccompanied, separated or missing, of which approximately 8,250 children still in need of family reunification. Currently at Confident Children out of Conflict (CCOC) interim care center, the total caseload is 90 children (61G & 29B) and of all the registered cases, 48 percent (43 children – 30G & 13B) are unaccompanied minors and 52 percent are separated and other vulnerable children referred by partners for protection and interim care center services (according to CPIMS as of 7th, November 2019). In 2018 and 2019, over 80 cases both boys and girls were referred by partners on transit during reunification process benefited from interim care services (accommodation, feeding, psychosocial support services while at the center). This huge number of children in need of child protection services shows that the protective environment around children has been compromised due to the impacts of decades of civil conflict.Under these circumstances, abandoned and neglected children, especially girls and women have become vulnerable to SGBV. Though the extent of SGBV is unknown, there is a large amount of evidence that sexual gender based violence is an ongoing risk for girls and women throughout the country. Besides, the economic hardship and lack of survival opportunities, has forced people to engage in negative coping mechanisms, such as child marriage, survival sex, and violence. CCOC will continue to maintain the interim care center providing accommodation, food, psychosocial support and comprehensive case management services for UASC and other vulnerable children in need of protection. Through this project, CCOC will increase access for UASC and OVC to life saving child protection services i.e. FTR case management process, psychosocial support (PSS) services through CFS including Non CFS psychosocial support (CBPSS), and enhance the understanding and analysis among staff and communities and other actors on the child protection context and needs. CCOC will also provide a platform to establish community based child protection mechanisms (CBCPMs), Child Help Desks and Child Rights clubs (CRC’s) in primary schools to facilitate the identification of and response to child protection concerns, the development of community based mitigation plans, prevention messaging (e.g. on forced early marriages etc.) and child protection surveillance mechanisms.CCOC will continue providing child protection services in central equatorial state specifically Juba county in payams of Kator, Rejaf and Juba town. Internally displaced people living in Mahad IDP camp and Don Bosco IDP camp in Gumbo and underserved host communities such as Lologo, st. Mary cemetery, salakana and korobou are targeted for this project.

2 - Activities

CCOC will identify, document and provide Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) and follow up services for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC). The Interim Care Centre (ICC) will remain operational in Juba providing accommodation, meals, medical care, clothing, and other assorted material assistance for the UASCs & OVCs identified by CCOC or referred by other child protection partners. In addition, CCOC will provide comprehensive case management support activities for UASCs and OVCs under ICC and those in the communities including referrals for specialized services. CCOC will also increase on the existing emergency response capacity of the staff and Community Based Child Protection Networks (CBCPN) and animators to be able to provide timely, effective and efficient Child protection through training on case management, PSS and CPiE. In addition, CCOC will conduct training for caregivers and non caregivers on CPiE and positive parenting skills sessions to create a protective and safe environment for children. Structured community based psychosocial support activities will be provided for children of different age groups with the aim of facilitating their recovery, restore a sense of normalcy, and promote their cognitive, physical, social and emotional development. CCOC in conjunction with CBCPCs will integrate risk reduction action and monitoring plans to address and monitor the specific child protection concerns and also conduct awareness raising session on child protection in the communities. Lastly, CCOC will mainstream child protection and psychosocial support activities in schools through capacity building events like training teachers and PTA/SMCs and organizing debates, music dance and drama activities with child protection risks mitigation messages. Quarterly, child protection awareness raising campaigns will be organized in schools targeting adolescents and youths. Besides, Child Rights Clubs (CRC) and Child Help Desks will be established in primary schools of the targeted communities to facilitate the identification of and response to child protection concerns

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

In reference to the Transitional constitution of the Republic of South Sudan 2011, the rights and freedoms of individuals and groups enshrined in the constitution shall be respected, upheld and promoted by all organs and agencies of Government and by all persons. Therefore, beneficiaries’ rights will legally be recognized for instance; the rights to life and human dignity, personal liberty, rights of women, rights of the child, freedom from slavery, servitude and forced labour amongst others.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, throughout the project planning and design, CCOC applied a participatory community approach, where each participant became an important contributor to the designing of this project. This gave the beneficiaries a feeling of ownership, and it built a strong base for intervention in the communities. For instance, the CCOC field social workers used the established community based child protection committees to ascertain the problems facing children and possible solutions to address these problems. The CBCPMs prioritized child protection due to the negative coping mechanisms of conflict affected communities such as early childhood forced marriages due to the hard economic situations. Therefore, Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) and Other vulnerable children identified in the IDP camps and host communities will benefit from the interim care centre (ICC) services-providing residential care and rehabilitation services, including appropriate referral mechanisms and psychosocial support. Conflict affected children of all age groups in the communities will have access to or benefit from community based psychosocial support activities such as Early Childhood Development activities and child friendly space activities in IDP schools and host communities to restore a sense of normalcy, promote their cognitive, physical, social and emotional development. The caregivers will benefit from the parenting skills sessions and awareness raising campaigns which will enhance their capacity to take care of their children.Through the monthly community coordination meetings that gathers community leaders such as chiefs, priests, house heads, CCOC used such forums to involve these leaders to openly discuss the possible solutions to child protection concerns, and their recommendations are the ones used in the designing of this proposal

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project is directly linked to community systems which are informal. CCOC has a well-established community based child protection mechanisms (CPCPMs) such as the child protection committees, focal points, community leaders and PTA members, Child Help Desks and Child Rights clubs (CRC’s) in primary schools. These community led structures facilitate the identification of and response to child protection concerns, the development of community based mitigation plans, prevention messaging and child protection surveillance mechanisms.To ensure long-term interventions, CCC applied a participatory community approach, where each participant became an important contributor to the designing of this project. This gave the beneficiaries a feeling of ownership, and it built a strong base for long term intervention in the communities. This project will also empower community based child protection mechanisms through refresher training on child protection in emergency settings to continue to prevent, respond, report cases of child abuse in the communities and refer such cases for psychosocial support and comprehensive case management including referrals for specialized services. The caregivers will also be trained on parenting skills and continuous awareness raising campaigns on child protection will be conducted in the communities for a durable solution for preventing violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect against children. The community systems in conjunction with CCOC staff will operate community based safe healing and learning spaces for children that facilitate their recovery, restore a sense of normalcy, and promote their cognitive, physical, social and emotional development and provide parenting support so that children will realize their full potentials in life.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes! The project is aligned to objectives of the National Social Protection Policy Frame Work (NSPPF) of the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare since the ministry bears the primary responsibility of social protection. The ultimate goal of the NSPPF is to enhance the capacity of the South Sudanese people to manage socioeconomic risk and volatility by increasing their resilience to shocks; help the poor and vulnerable avoid destitution by ensuring more equitable resource distribution; and improve access to opportunities generated by economic growth. Through the project activities, CCOC will be complementing to the work of Ministry of Gender, child and social welfare in terms of child protection for conflict affected populations.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Although the gun shots and bombardments have ceased in Juba and in most parts of the country, the protective environment for children and women is satisfactorily stable and safe. According to the daily security update by NGO forum, there is high level of armed robbery in Juba and this has been caused by the worsened economic hardship and lack of survival opportunities. The economic hardships have also forced people to engage in negative coping mechanisms, such as child marriage and survival sex especially families in the internally displaced peoples camps and host communities in Juba. Under such circumstances, children need to be protected from violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Training and sensitizing internally displaced persons from the targeted communities on child protection is an assured way of preventing violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect against children because caregivers would have gained more knowledge on positive parenting skills. The establishment and training of Community Based Child Protection Mechanisms in form of committees gives opportunity for preventing and responding to child protection concerns since these committees are members of the communities whose capacities in child protection would have been enhanced. Hence, even though the project comes to an end, the community has mechanisms in place to ensure that the protective environment around children are safe and secure. Most importantly, the project design fits well within the national development vision and has high level of support because this project ensures all rights of children are realized. CCOC will also continue to seek for further funding from other potential donor to sustain the project.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The risk of lack of access to / awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents will be mitigated through organizing monthly school out-reach campaigns to reach out many pupils with child protection messages. Mobile awareness raising sessions will be conducted in the IDP camps and in the host communities to reduce the risk of lack of access to awareness on child protection services.Though the conflicts have ceased in most parts of the country, the risk of targeted project areas being contaminated with explosive hazards will be mitigated through putting security response contingency plans in place and conducting mine risk education for children in schools and caregivers in the communities so that they are not exposed to explosives.The risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project area will be mitigated through liaising with other child protection partners especially ministry gender child and social welfare to relocate and take care of the UASC and OVCs hosted at the CCOC interim care center or solicit for more funding to find another safe and secure environment for the children.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The established community based child protection mechanisms will act as a platform through which vulnerable community members receive feedbacks. The CBCPMs members comprises of community leaders, social workers, teachers, women group leaders, priest, and the police. The teachers can manage to translate information and give feedback about services to the illiterate individuals. The women group leaders can also disseminate information to the women and girls through women and girls friendly spaces sessions on available services on child protection. Through the religious gatherings, the priest and community leaders will disseminate information or feedback to the community members because such events gather different people within the communities irrespective of their socioeconomic status and physical abilities. The awareness raising sessions and campaigns will also give chance for community members to ask any question in regards to child protection services and prompt responses or feedbacks are given immediately. To ensure the most vulnerable members of the community receive feedbacks on the services provided, the CBCPMs members will also be trained on good communication skills.
