Project Summary

Through the 2021 humanitarian response plan for the year 2021, The Rescue Mission intends to achieve to WASH cluster objectives which are: 1) provision of access to basic services in emergency with main focus of providing access to safe water, adequate sanitation and hygienic behaviors to mitigate and reduce high levels of malnutrition, water related diseases (ARI, AWD, cholera etc) and 2) Mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence violence (GBV) issues among the affected population in targeted locations in Juba, Kajo- Keji, Terekeka and Morobo in Central Equatoria States.
The proposed intervention will reach a target of 40,000 vulnerable people (men 9169, women 10380, boy’s 9971, girls 10480). Among internally displaced persons, returnees, and host communities including people with disabilities in the targeted locations. The activities will include, Construction of new water points; rehabilitation of water points, Construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in health facilities, Nutrition centers, Schools and community level; installation of hand washing facilities to promote hand washing; hygiene promotion education through door to door visits, participatory hygiene sessions in public places for effective behavioral change; distribution of WASH NFIs to the most vulnerable.
Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits will be provided to adolescent girls and women of reproductive age group accompanied with menstrual hygiene management sessions. TERM will ensure ownership and sustainability of the response through involvement of the targeted community throughout the life cycle of the intervention. All the WASH temporary and sustainable services will address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an accountability to affected populations (AAP) community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectoral evaluations and analyses. This project will focus on the various needs of men, women, boys and girls identified through focus group discussions (FGDs) with all the sex and age groups, people with disabilities and pregnant lactating mothers incorporating gender dynamics and specific needs of different groups into needs assessment and response recommendations. Throughout the response life cycle, TERM team will coordinate with the authorities, community and other implementing partners on ground who are implementing WASH, nutrition, health, and protection for integration of activities to ensure maximum utilization of resources. Post-distribution (PDM), post- intervention (PIM) monitoring will be done across the project done where accessible and secure. PDMs/PIMs gives clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports will provide clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve overall project quality.

1 - Needs

South Sudan communities continues to suffer in 2020 by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. According to South Sudan Humanitarian Need Overview 2021, an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities. Only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64 per cent of people across the country either rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41 per cent), take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19 per cent), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4 per cent). The proportion of households relying on surface water in Central Equatoria are 41 per cent. The quantity of water available per day is also below standards in many locations, with 17 per cent of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39 per cent of people reporting insufficient access to water for handwashing. Access to sanitation remained low, with only 17 per cent of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound, 4 per cent have access to a shared latrine, and 2 per cent have access to a communal/institutional latrine. This means that 77 per cent of people do not have access to a latrine. WASH needs are also high among IDPs and returnees in non-camp settings and among their already stretched host communities.Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases, a health-related WASH needs arose in 2020 as a result of COVID-19 and will persist into 2021.According to Tearfund and IMA assessment of needs in Kajo keji some boreholes give out muddy water and many times women and girls have to travel to distant places to get clean water. For most households, even in cases where bathing water is collected from the hand pumps, there is a lack of safe bathing facilities. Use of rivers and swamps for bathing imposes severe risks of safety and security for women and children including the risk of sexual and gender based violence, Across different payams, 30% of the respondents indicated that they take 15-30 minutes to reach their nearest water point. Further to this, 24% indicated that they take 30-45 minutes to get to their nearest water point. Access to water remains at 19% of the respondents taking 1-2 hours to reach their nearest water point .Majority of the people (59 %) indicated that they wash their hands with any water only regardless of whether it is clean or dirty. This leaves the communities predominantly susceptible to diseases such as COVID-19 A recent (Jan 2021) IRNA in Lainya identified access to safe clean water remain challenging as displaced persons has resorted to use of untreated surface water as some functional water points produce brown and turbid color water due to rust pipes.In Mangala IDP camp, the flood affected displaced population are in dire need of basic WASH services, the population has continued to increase as oppose to the previous setting of the camp hence rendering the services inadequate, there is a general insufficiency in sanitation and safe and adequate water exposing women and children to high risk of GBV and abduction including risk to water borne diseases

The project will also reduce the risk of GBV arising from women and girls walking long distances in search of water.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TERM has a comprehensive PSEA policy that all staff are trained on and special consideration shall be made to have it applied to all the persons involved in the program implantation through training and emphasis on community awareness shall also be done on the information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The project team will conduct PSEA training for different groups of the community including Chiefs, Local authorities, IDPs, Returnees and host communities males and females from different age groups and people with disabilities shall be engage, The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation and TERM will ensure that project staffs are oriented about protection mainstreaming on the inclusion and ethical conduct, including prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse focusing on the vulnerability of women and girls.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

To avoid exclusion and marginalization, all gender age groups will be given a room to participate in all consultations and decision-making platforms throughout the project beginning from assessment, baseline, project design, implementation and closer so as to be able to include diverse opinions and views of the beneficiaries TERM will consult separately with girls, boys, women and men (including older people and those with disabilities) to ensure that activities are designed to provide equitable access and reduce incidences of violence and exploitation. At the design level, the project will ensure that girls, boys, women and men (including older people and PWDs) have access to appropriate and safe services with risk analysis done prior to rehabilitation or establishment of WASH infrastructures, aid distribution events and public gathering etc . At the implementation level, the project will ensure that girls, boys, women and men (including older people and PWDs) have access to feedback and complaint mechanisms so that corrective actions can address their specific protection and assistance needs. TERM will ensure frequent collection of feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries through appropriate feedback collection mechanisms, such feedback shall be used to improve the design and the services being provided. The project staff will always carryout consultation with various groups among the beneficiaries to ensure no one is left out. During the project life cycle, a periodic monitoring, collection of feedback and complaints shall be established, this will help TERM to determine areas of improvement/adjustment. It also gives clear view of whether beneficiaries are comfortable with the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. Outcomes from feedback and complaints shall be documented, lessons drawn and recommendations will be used to improve planning, design and implementation modality of the project.
