Project Summary

The Darfur states remain one of the areas worst-affected by the ongoing conflict which erupted in April 2023 between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), which is heavily impacting civilians and has triggered in Sudan the world's largest displacement crisis. The project's overall objective is to provide emergency health, nutrition, and pss services to crisis-affected populations in Central and West Darfur, IDPs, and host communities, by ensuring i) basic lifesaving integrated health services are supported and provided to IDPs and host communities; ii) significant access to essential integrated prevention and management of acute malnutrition for the targeted population at HF and community level and iii) communities are involved in the improvement of their health, nutrition and psychosocial status, selecting and training community volunteers, providing health, nutrition and pss awareness to targeted communities and providing protection training and service mapping.
PUI will continue to support the already supported Mother and Child Centre (MCC) in Geneina as the main primary care service for mothers, conducting maternity care, such as ante-natal care (ANC), post-natal care (PNC) and Family Planning. Given the context evolutions in the whole Darfur states, with increasing health, nutrition and MHPSS needs while access is improved, PUI will also start supporting one additional HF in Zalingei, Central Darfur. Zalingei’s PHC will provide free primary healthcare, including Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services, to its catchment population area, while Geneina MCC will more specifically target women of reproductive age, PLWs and children under 5, providing SRH minimum essential package and integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI).
PUI will ensure the provision of essential pharmaceutical commodities following standard quality process for purchase, storage, distribution and management, and non-medical equipment and supplies to health facilities, their financial support and necessary rehabilitation. PUI will continue to coordinate with UNFPA and WHO for the provision of SRH and IEH kits. PUI will provide support on outbreak preparedness and routine immunization program, ensure the training and capacity building of health staff, support access to quality healthcare services, including (SRH), and referral systems, provide PSS services. As per MISP recommendation, Clean Delivery Kits will be distributed to PLWs in communities where access to HF and skilled birth attendants is not ensured to reduce risk of maternal and neonatal mortality. As per SOPs and safe and dignified programming principles, menstrual hygiene kits will be provided to displaced teenagers (14 – 25) and women under 25 coming to the HFs seeking care.
2 OTPs will be set inside the supported HFs to provide treatment to children under 5 suffering from SAM without complications, and will be accessible by both IDPs and host communities. PUI will liaise with UNICEF and WFP for nutrition commodities provision. SAM with complications cases will be referred to Geneina’s Stabilization Centre (SC), set in Geneina Hospital and supported by MSF, and to Zalingei SC, set in Zalingei’s hospital, which World relief is planning to support. PUI will support SAM with complications referral to higher level of care with cash for transportation, as well as health and SRH referrals. Two Mother Support Groups of 10 mothers each will be established. Under the supervision of PUI health and nutrition officer, they will be provided with sensitization on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) best practices, including exclusive breastfeeding, and cooking demonstrations on how to prepare nutritious food from locally available ingredients.
PUI will provide Psychosocial Support Services, at HF and community levels.

Implementing Partners

PUI will work in partnership with a national NGO on this project, yet to be identified, to ensure local acceptance, transferring of skills and sustainability. 3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

PUI projects and activities are designed and implemented around an integrated approach, respecting and integrating Protection as a cross-cutting theme to ensure an intervention quality requirement. As per the guidelines of the Global Protection Cluster (GPC), protection principles are incorporated into each single project, and promote meaningful access and safety and dignity in all activities.The following pillars of Safe and Dignified programming are mainstreamed throughout all sectors and activities, and all PUI staff have or will be trained and will receive refreshment training on Safe programming principles and safeguarding policies:: 1. Prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks2. Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services -in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who maybe particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3.Accountability: set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints4.Participation and empowerment: support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to claim their rightsTo prevent causing harm and ensure the safety of the beneficiaries, PUI will conduct sessions on safety measures to adopt during the repairs and after the work. These will also be developed with the aim to reinforce the beneficiaries’ autonomy and capacity to protect themselves in case of endangerment. PUI will ensure creating a safe and dignified environment. PUI aims at identifying the most vulnerable individuals or groups when leading assessments and surveys, to be able to appropriately analyze their needs and feedbacks, and PUI will develop strong accountability mechanisms as well. PUI will develop efficient accountability mechanisms, with the local authorities and community leaders. PUI will apply the “Do No Harm” principle across all its activities. PUI will also ensure that the supported health facilities are safely accessible, particularly for the vulnerable groups. An entrance accessible for people with disability, and a safe waiting area will be dedicated to women. Across all its intervention at health care facility level, the health and nutrition staff will able to identify unattended cases in need of PSS and baseprotection assistance such as child protection related issues, GBV cases, and severely traumatic experiences. PUI will activate the identified and relevant referral system when necessary.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

All PUI projects are deeply rooted within the communities where they are implemented, involving community stakeholders, leaders, staff and volunteers at every step of the way, from design to implementation, through focus groups discussions, key informant interviews, surveys, etc to ensure the community’s ownership of a project and related activities, their success and sustainability. To ensure accountability, community feedback mechanisms, with several entry points such as complaint/suggestion letter box, sometimes helpline or such as protection / helpdesk desk and accountability desk dedicated to receiving and sharing information about beneficiaries' concerns regarding effective and efficient implementation of activities are put in place. These mechanisms are regularly monitored during the project implementation, on a monthly basis, and the monitoring outcome taken into account to provide guidance if or how activities have to be adapted, for the overall project's quality and effectiveness improvements.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

PUI will ensure that the field staff share with the community PUI’s commitment to accountability to the affected population on issues such as staff recruitment and training, code of conduct, information sharing and transparency. All PUI staff receive the organization Code of conduct and all PUI ethical policies, including PSEA. PUI has a zero tolerance towards all forms of abuse, exploitation, mistreatment and moral or sexual harrassement. All persons entering in contact with Première Urgence Internationale collaborators, members, partners, suppliers, associates, and beneficiaries in relation with its activities must be protected to the greatest extent possible against deliberate or involuntary actions and failures which would expose them to risks of abuse, harassment or sexual exploitation and any other prejudice. PUI will ensure cases can be reported. All representatives and collaborators of Première Urgence Internationale undertake in all circumstances to respect the provisions, norms, and commitments to report any concerns, suspicions, or allegations of PSEA and any violations of this Policy in accordance with the wisthleblower mechanism and recommended timelines, as detailed in PUI Ethical Framework. The signature of the code of conduct includes a commitment to respect all policies and to maintain high ethical standards. PUI will set up a CFRM adapted to the local context to collect the views and complaints of a wide range of stakeholders (affected populations, communities, local civil society actors, international humanitarian actors, local authorities or national governments and donors) and take appropriate action. It will be designed in line with the protection principle of Do No Harm, in consultation with partners and affected communities, taking also into account available internal resources (financial and human) and with the objective of gradual scaling up. PUI thus aims at guaranteeing the availability of appropriate Feedback/Complaints channels, while ensuring effective complaints handling, in order to avoid creating frustration or mistrust with regard to the proposed mechanisms.Depending on the context, this mechanism consist of i) Community Mobilizers, who would be important messengers conveying feedbacks and complaints from the population, themselves being part of the community ii) Complaint boxes, especially in health facilities ii) Regular consultations with community focal points / community committees iv) Focus group discussions led by PUI teams.Whatever the mechanism(s) chosen, PUI will inform the population of the existence and functioning of the complaint and feedback mechanism. This information will be disseminated through several channels (i.e.through community relays, during surveys etc.) in order to ensure that the targeted community can make use of it as soon as the first activities are launched and Inform the population in the intervention area of the anti-fraud, anti-corruption and PSEA principles.
