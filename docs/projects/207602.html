Project Summary

• Better access to clean water by installing 11 deep tube wells and maintaining 90 existing tube wells and tap stands, reaching 9,000 people. AAB will also carry out water quality testing on the standard of contamination-free of the water and apply corrective measures where necessary, benefiting 5,000 people.
• Increased sanitation facilities by constructing 16 twin-pit latrines and 16 bathing spaces for over 600 people. We will ensure the facilities are well-lit for the use of women and adolescent girls at night by installing solar lights. Volunteers, supported by ActionAid, will repair and maintain the existing latrines and bathing spaces, benefiting 5,000 people.
• Improved sanitation and waste management by cleaning drainage networks to benefit 15,000 people.
• Better hygiene practices and waste management by raising awareness of at least 5,000 people and distributing menstrual hygiene materials (including sanitary pads, soap, and sandals) to at least 1,000 women and adolescent girls.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

• Lack of accessible sanitation and hygiene facilities: Since 2018, there has been a 3% increase in the use of improved sanitation facilities to 11.6%. However, areas of high density are putting pressure on existing Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) facilities. In addition, as per MSNA findings, 38% of women in camps are not feeling safe using community latrines at night. Besides, 5% of host community members are using unimproved latrine facilities.• Poor solid and human (excreta) waste management system: Only 51% of refugee households dispose of wastage in designated areas. Only 50% of faecal sludge treatment plants in Camp 11 & 12 meet the acceptable standard.• Inadequate safe water facilities: Whilst the number of refugees with access to safe water has increased significantly between 2018 and 2022, the quality of the water is of concern: 8% of community water sources and 39% of household water sources are contaminated with unsafe levels of E Coli. Cases of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and scabies have increased significantly in the past year and there have been incidences of dengue fever in the camps as well. According to MSNA findings, residents of FDMN Camp 12 are getting only 9L of water per person per day. As per sphere standard, a minimum of 20L of water per person per day is required.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

According to the Rationalization Principles, AAB contacted the WASH Sector earlier after getting its fund for WASH activities for the year of 2024 and onwards. Sector, with the consultation with AFA (IOM), decided to provide the responsibility of D block of Camp # 12 to AAB. Since AAB has been delivering WASH support in Camp # 12 from long ago. Which is aligning with Principle # 3, as AAB is going to be WASH focal of D block of Camp # 12 (one agency per block) and AAB is implementing multiple projects (CBP, GBV, CwC, Skill Development etc.) in Camp 11 and 12 (which will reduce overhead costs and allow AAB to follow integrated approach). AAB also fulfilled minimum basic requirements as implementing partner. Besides, AAB ensured funding for three (3) years to deliver WASH services and is trying to increase the funding amount for ensuring sustainability, predictability and continuity.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

ActionAid Bangladesh (AAB) will arrange solar street-lights adjacent to WASH facilities for avoiding potential threats or violences at night against girls, women, and children. AAB will construct gender-segregated latrines & bathing cubicles and distance of facilities from the shelter will be minimal to make safe & accessible for women & adolescent girls. AAB keeps the provision of distributing MHM kits to host community to address protection mainstreaming issue. Project team will ensure operation & maintenance activities of WASH facilities to keep those functional and to protect from privacy issues & ensure dignity (proper locking system, no leakage on fencing sheet etc.) of women & adolescent girls, while using latrines & bathing cubicles. AAB will consult with women representatives from community to select site or take any initiative regrading WASH services. AAB will also involve women participant to manage solid waste through CfW (Cash for Work) for empowering them economically.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Ensuring accountability within the WASH project stands as a pivotal cornerstone. A robust system has been established to uphold this accountability. Volunteers are strategically enlisted for every sub-block, undertaking consistent visits to gather beneficiary feedback and complaints. These inputs are meticulously documented in a dedicated register book, managed, and maintained using KOBO tools. Additionally, beneficiaries hold the option to engage directly by visiting either the AAB camp office or the site management office to voice their feedback or register complaints, fostering a transparent and accessible avenue for their contributions to the project's improvement. The AAB MEAL team handles these inputs. Comprehensive training on CFRM, safeguarding policy, PSEA, SHEA, and child protection is mandatory for all WASH project staff and volunteers. Awareness sessions empower beneficiaries to grasp CFRM principles, enabling active and informed contribution to the project's success.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

All of ActionAid’s humanitarian responses follow strong safeguarding protocols, which all partners are also required to follow. AAB take a survivor-centered approach to safeguarding & PSEA, which gives women and girls a voice to speak out against violence and report complaints. AAB has strong policy about PSEA, Safeguarding & Child Protection and takes zero tolerance action against the abuser. AAB arranges refresher training on PSEA, Safeguarding & Child Protection policy on a regular basis for staff and volunteers. AAB has a hotline number to take complain against PSEA breacher.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

AAB will keep a lumpsum budget for emergency preparedness and response, such as tie down the roof of WASH facilities in monsoon. It includes contingency plans as well, like purchasing aqua-tabs for crisis moment etc. AAB has a plan to do some plantation activities and will produce fertilizer from organic waste through MRF (Material Recovery Facility).
