Project Summary

Despite healthcare and hygiene advancements, refugees remain exceptionally vulnerable. Reliance on humanitarian health services, coupled with epidemic-prone diseases beyond COVID-19, such as cholera, diphtheria, dengue, and scabies alongside natural hazards like fires, cyclones, and monsoon rains, contributes to internal displacements. This complex crisis emphasizes the ongoing need for sustained engagement by World Health Organization (WHO).
WHO, as the lead health agency, supports Government of Bangladesh (GoB) in partner coordination, disease surveillance, and outbreak response. This continues through 2024 aligned with the 2023-2024 Health Sector Strategic Plan including rationalization. This strategy incorporates insights from other pertinent strategic documents, ensuring a well-informed, evidence-based response to the evolving crisis dynamics.
WHO is committed to maintaining integrated disease surveillance at 60 sentinel sites in camps, emphasizing communicable diseases. The organization aims for the prevention and early detection of emerging threats, monitoring the health of the Rohingya population through established reporting systems like EWARS, DHIS-2, HeRAMS, and analyzing key health indicators from sector partners via the 4Ws. A robust system for preventing, detecting, and responding to outbreak-prone diseases will be upheld, utilizing platforms such as EWARS and Community Based Surveillance.
WHO is committed to training government personnel and healthcare workers on key health topics, encompassing disease surveillance, laboratory diagnostics, and case management for communicable diseases, neglected tropical diseases, NCDs, with a priority on mental health. The organization will enhance partner capacities for delivering quality essential health services at all levels, addressing critical gaps such as safe blood transfusions to minimize obstetric risks in the targeted population.
Furthermore, WHO continues to strengthen capacity of 60 laboratories operated by health sector partners through supplies, including essential equipment, consumables such as Rapid Diagnostic Tests, and crucial reagents. The focus extends to the upkeep and expansion of the sole molecular field laboratory at Cox's Bazar Medical College, managed by the Institute of Epidemiology, Disease Control and Research. Aligned with its commitment to a comprehensive health response, WHO is leading testing expansion initiatives in critical areas such as Hepatitis C, Antimicrobial Resistance surveillance, Cholera culture, HIV External Quality Assurance, and emerging diseases. This strategic approach ensures timely responses while upholding standards of quality and sustainability beyond the emergency phase.
Additionally, WHO is committed to initiating behavior change through risk communication and community engagement. Prioritizing high-risk groups, including those with NCDs, older persons, and people with disabilities, involves identifying and eliminating barriers.
WHO and partners collaborate with government health authorities to introduce new vaccines (HPV and TCV) in Routine Immunization in both refugee and host populations. The goal is to achieve >95% vaccination coverage for preventable diseases like Diphtheria, Measles, and Rubella to minimize outbreak risks. Being endemic population, the mass campaign with Oral Cholera Vaccine will be rolled out to sustain the population immunity and prevent Cholera outbreak.
Existing health programs have made strides in delivering primary health care services for refugees and host communities, but a gap exists in addressing chronic conditions like NCDs including mental health. WHO is committed to bridging this gap by enhancing the capacity of HCWs around management, providing essential diagnostic equipment and medicines for integrated clinical services for NCDs including mental health.
WHO is committed to providing comprehensive support to strengthen governance and leadership in Health Sector operations

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Refugee Health Status: Overall, the estimated Crude Death Rate (CDR) of 2.1-2.4 per 1,000 among refugees compares positively with the national CDR of 6 per 1,000. However, Maternal Mortality Rates remain high among refugees at approximately 280-300 per 100,000 live births compared to national figures of 123 per 100,000. Hypertensive disease and post-partum hemorrhage accounted for most of the deaths. Through 2022- 2023, the most common causes of morbidity were skin diseases (20%), and respiratory tract infections (16%). Other illnesses, like acute watery diarrhea, dengue and NCDs e.g., hypertension and diabetes featured as the top reason for consultation. Disease burden related to Hepatitis / STIs, and HIV continue to remain high in Rohingya population as compared to host community. A recent survey (May 2023) by WHO estimated scabies prevalence at 40% in the camps underscoring its burden on the health systems. Against this background, WHO priority scope of work is focused on strengthening the capacity and availability of NCD services, supporting control of scabies and other communicable diseases like STIs/HIV/ Hepatitis. The organization will continue with capacity building, scaling up screening and referral services, strengthening monitoring, improving diagnostic capacities and support access to medicines for communicable & NCDs. Health threats/Risks: The risk of infectious disease outbreaks, especially for Cholera, reported in small clusters often and natural hazards such as monsoons, cyclones, and landslides remain a concern. Being endemic population, Oral Cholera Vaccination campaign, is required with gap of three years to protect the population. Transmission continues in camps following massive Diphtheria outbreak in 2018. In Nov 2022, WHO in collaboration with GoB conducted a wide age range Penta/TD vaccination campaign in response. The pattern of dengue transmission appears to be changing with increased incidence in each seasonal surge. Other infectious diseases requiring keen attention are hepatitis and STIs, further assessment is required to map the burden of the problem and ensure timely access to treatment services. All the above risks underscore the need for WHO leadership to maintain strong disease surveillance, adequate preparedness capacity, and leadership in coordinating the response to these issues, and contingency supplies for responding to health emergencies due to both biological and non-biological hazards.Health Resources and Services: In the refugee response, there are about 50-60 partners providing a range of life-saving essential health services including diagnostic capacities through a network of about 72 health posts, 45 primary healthcare clinics, and 3 secondary care facilities of varying capacities. Access to tertiary healthcare is organized through patient referrals to health facilities in Cox’s Bazar township and Chittagong. WHO will continue to purposefully direct its interventions to maintain access to essential services through these facilities that have been prioritized through the Health Sector rationalization process. At the community level, the health education and behavior change communication for health promotion is delivered through a network of 1700 trained Community Health Workers (40% from the host community) translating to 1 CHW per 550 people- closely approximating the standard coverage of 1 CHW per 500 people (CHWG/UNHCR March 2023). These CHWs continue to require support with essential equipment for disease screening and referral, training, and supervision. As of September 2023, there were about 1200 Skilled Birth Attendants in the camps (37%-Medical Officers, 26%-Nurses, 37% midwives) resulting in a coverage of 23 SBA per 14,260 compared to the standard of 23 per 10,000 (Health Sector 4W, Sept 2023). WHO’s capacity-building intervention will target health workers in the camps to enhance quality and standards of practice.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

As the Health Sector Lead agency, WHO has collaborated with the partners to ensure a prioritized response based on the recommendations of the health facility rationalization. In follow-up to translate the strategy into practice, WHO’s program is wrapped around the prioritized health service delivery platforms to ensure efficiency, avoid duplication, and promote equitable access to comprehensive services. For instance, all gap-filling support for essential medical commodities will be provided in coordination with the health sector’s priority, and identification of learning needs and training participants will be managed through the health sector. WHO maintains close collaboration with other partners whether bilaterally or through the coordination platforms and ensures the actions are complimentary to existing programs to promote programmatic longevity.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

WHO encourages the incorporation of cultural and gender-sensitive approaches in healthcare services. WHO will conduct need assessments and analyze the findings by collecting data on age and gender through different programs. In addition, will continue to monitor gender sensitive indicators in vaccination program through life course like proportion of male to female vaccination across all age groups to ensure equitable access and socio-cultural barriers are addressed accordingly through Community Health Working group. WHO applies gender analysis in all the trainings/workshops conducted by WHO to ensure female participation and empowering them with knowledge and decision-making capacity. Findings from different assessments and surveys are utilized to design and tailored the policies to overcome socio cultural barriers to access health services like promoting physical activities in women to reduce the risk of noncommunicable diseases particularly Diabetes and Hypertension. Furthermore, WHO support the activities to improve the access to Sexual and Reproductive Health services and continue to provide technical assistance on clinical case management of rape and intimate partner violence in both the Rohingyas and host community. Furthermore, disease burden related to HIV continue to remain high in Rohingya population as compared to host community. WHO is spearheading testing expansion initiatives in critical areas like HIV External Quality Assurance, and other emerging diseases. This strategic approach aims to ensure not only a timely response but also to uphold the standards of quality and sustainability, transcending the emergency phase. WHO will capitalize on service delivery to gender diverse population, supporting gender perspectives within health facilities and supporting establishing necessary linkages. WHO through the health sector will continue to ensure that facilities have at least one female doctor.

3.3 - Age & Disability

WHO advocate for persons with disabilities to have equitable access to effective health services are included in health emergencies preparedness and responses; and can access cross-sectoral public health interventions to achieve the highest attainable standard of health. Strategic Tool for Assessing Risks (STAR) assessment by WHO in health facilities for refugees recommended one entrance per facility with ramps for disabled. Ramps and path to be finished with material that are non-slip, surface free of irregularities with minimal joints with handrails on both the sides. Focus is provided on NCDs services including mental health and vaccination across life course for the elderly and disabled population. WHO led health sector works to ensure the humanitarian response to the Rohingya crisis is inclusive for men, women, boys, and girls with disabilities and older persons, at all age.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project incorporates integrated activities related to the health sector strategic planning. Findings from the independent community assessments regarding service availability for both the Rohingya and host community population will be utilized to enhance quality of health response as one of the key objectives of AAP. WHO will provide comprehensive technical guidance to the Risk Communication and Community Engagement Working Group for effective communication with communities. Additionally, WHO will seamlessly integrate the principles of AAP into discussions with the Community Health Working Groups, ensuring a synergistic approach to effectively address health care challenges and promote community wellbeing. Key focus will be provided towards guiding principles of Gender Action Plan (2021) and Accountability to Affected Populations (APP) Framework.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) of the communities by staff from the United Nation and partner agencies is a grave violation of their commitment to serve and protect vulnerable people, in both humanitarian and development settings. Additionally, Sexual harassment (SH) of their workforce is a serious failure of their duty of care. As part of culture change to prevent and respond to SEAH, WHO has launched a new policy on preventing and addressing all forms of sexual misconduct on 3 March 2023 and is operative from 8 March 2023. In the journey towards zero tolerance for any form of sexual misconduct, for inaction against it, and for any form of related retaliation, WHO is taking a strong and proactive approach to engage with SEAH victims/survivors and ensuring their safety and well-being through the implementation of PRSEAH Three-Year Strategy (2023-2025) and close collaboration/ contribution to PSEA Network and IASC joint initiatives to ensue zero tolerance of SEA across within WHO and contribute to system wide improvement. WHO has a PSEA focal person who ensure to address cross cutting issues within and beyond health sector. PSEA, and related trainings are mandatory for every WHO staff, consultants, and volunteers within WHO and are critically monitored.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

WHO Emergency Sub Office spearheads the Emergency Preparedness and Response Technical Committee to coordinate with Health Sector, Civil Surgeon’s Office - Health Emergency Operations Center (HEOC), and Office of the Refugee Relief & Repatriation Commissioner to scale up the preparedness and response readiness capacity of WHO Cox’s Bazar and the Health Sector, including the health facilities in the Rohingya camps to key hazards identified in the Multi-Hazard Response Plan and specific contingency and response plans for other hazards including biological such as cholera. Besides that, technical assistance is provided to Health Sector, Mobile Medical Teams, Emergency Preparedness and Response Technical Committee members to guide and calibrate their public health interventions to respond health emergencies and disasters within the framework of disaster risk management.Furthermore, WHO remains actively engaged to detect and monitor potential health threats through EWARS, conducting comprehensive risk assessments to identify hazards and vulnerabilities, providing a foundation for targeted interventions. During crisis, continue to provide support to Mobile Medical Teams ensuring a rapid and coordinated response to health emergencies. WHO will continue to preposition stockpiles for emergency use in camps. In addition, WHO will continue to monitor activities under domains of WASH FIT which includes water, sanitation, and health care waste management in health facilities in camps. In addition, build the capacity of health care workers for safe disposal of infectious and noninfectious waste generated by health facilities in the Rohingya camp settings thus minimizing the impact of waste generated by health facilities on the environment and on climate change.
