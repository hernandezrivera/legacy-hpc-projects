1 - Needs

According to the latest comprehensive assessments, some 1,000-1,500 in GCA and 7,500-9,000 in NGCA vulnerable families (mainly non-displaced, but also returnee and IDPs), living along the “contact line”, need humanitarian shelter assistance for the repair of their homes. In GCA, most of the cases, especially in Donetsk oblast, were covered by the State Emergency Service of Ukraine, leaving however part of the caseload still eligible for the complementary shelter assistance.In analyses, , shelter is identified as the second highest priority for residents in NGCA.In terms of winterization, the percentage of HHs reporting limited access to fuel for winter in GCA remains high in 0-5 km zone as in previous years. The continuing coal blockade with regard to NGCA led to a significant reduction of available coal on the market. State subsidies for utilities and heating have resulted in only a slightly positive impact for limited number of HHs as compared with the gradual increase of fuel expenditure. (In fact, the amount of funds available for subsidies and the number of eligible HHs are being reduced with each new heating cycle). The heating costs, at USD 560 (Shelter Cluster, 2020), 4 tons of coal heating fuel requirements, representing more than 40% of the annual income. The COVID-19 pandemics has weakened the state’s ability to support the most vulnerable families with subsidies, which only exacerbates vulnerabilities. It is also important to add that these subsidies do not provide a long-lasting effect, as the received amount is spent straight away in the respective winter season. The amount of the subsidy does not exceed 70 EUR per household that does not cover the minimum amount needed for the heating season. There is also a problem with limited access, in some places, especially isolated settlements, there is no appropriate transport logistics and residents do not have the opportunity neither to travel to receive subsidies to local administrations nor to purchase the solid fuel. These isolated settlements remain a priority for humanitarian assistance in the GCA, as identified by the Shelter/NFI Cluster. The shelter team plans to help the most vulnerable households without gas heating system or other sustainable heating system.The most conflict-affected people, particularly the elderly and vulnerable households, rely heavily on the government’s social protection scheme as their main source of income (HNO 2020). People’s savings and reserves are exhausted, and those most in-need are being forced to stretch already limited resources or simply go without. The systematic analysis of the impact of the conflict in eastern Ukraine by Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Cluster, Socio-Economic Summary Report shows a clear link between the conflict, COVID-19 and the worsening socio-economic situation. Nearly half of rural households and one-third of urban households in GCA of the two conflict-affected regions do not have adequate supplies for the winter (REACH). Many inhabitants, mostly elderly people with small incomes, many with disabilities, are not able to purchase necessary food and non-food items to maintain decent living conditions in their houses that suffered war-related damage. Thus, considering the fact that during winter in Ukraine the temperatures can go regularly below -20°C), life preserving and lifesaving assistance remains urgent and time-sensitive. Based on recommendations from the Shelter and on its own updated needs assessments (age, gender and diversity-sensitive) PIN will target the most vulnerable households and individuals, considering cumulative vulnerabilities (e.g. elderly people who are isolated in poverty and without caregivers, impoverished female-headed households with care dependency, individuals with a non-recognised disability or other factor of health vulnerability not receiving social benefits or other minority households/individuals with insecure tenancy or housing arrangements etc).

2 - Activities & Outcomes

With the above needs/strategies in mind, PIN proposes the following shelter/NFI activities for 2021. Outcome 1. Satisfied essential shelter and NFI needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict affected population through monetised/in-kind assistance and contingency1. Providing solid fuel & heater distribution (GCA and NGCA)• PIN Shelter team plans to cover needs for winterization with solid fuel to the most vulnerable inhabitants of the frontline zone. The fuel will be delivered to the most vulnerable populations in underserved communities. Delivery of 4 tons on average of solid fuel for each HH will be carried out exclusively within the 5 km LoC, on both sides of the LoC. Average cost per HH is $5602. Providing winterization cash grant transfers (only GCA)• PIN will provide conditional winterization cash grant. This intervention will enable beneficiaries to meet their basic winterization needs by purchasing solid fuel and etc. Average grant size is $250 Outcome 2.Provided adequate transitional solutions [monetised or in-kind] related to shelter and NFI needs meeting minimal international and national shelter standards1. Implementation light and medium repairs (LNGCA and DNGCA)• Materials and labor for light and medium scale repairs on conflict-damaged private homes and multi-storey buildings, for essential purposes and for the most vulnerable, mainly consisting of roof patching, installation of high quality plastic/PVC windows with frames, full rehabilitation of roofs, light wall repairs, doors, and/or some light interior work. Average value of light repairs set at $400, and $1500 for medium repairs. • Facilitation of community-based energy efficiency pilot solution(s) in selected hard-to-reach and isolated community(ies) to improve in particular house insulation and sustainability e.g. through empowering local mechanisms as exit plan.. Outcome 3. Provided/upgraded permanent shelter solutions for the most vulnerable conflict affected population1. Implementing structural repairs ("heavy repairs") LNGCA• Materials and labor for heavy scale repairs and reconstructions (using existing foundations) of conflict-damaged private homes of the most vulnerable consisting of rehabilitation of exterior/interior walls, roofs, windows, flooring, electricity, plumbing, etc, for safe and dignified housing. Average value of repairs set at $4000.
