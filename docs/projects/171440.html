Project Summary

Resilience and protection have always been pillars of the agricultural sector. The First Strategic
Objective identified by the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) in the National Agricultural Sector Strategy
Update in August 2020 was, "The resilience of female and male farmers on their lands is enhanced."
Additionally, the Jordan Valley has always been a priority area for authorities and donors, due to the
vulnerability of populations living in the area; this is due to the challenges and risks that include
restrictions on movement, construction, and rehabilitation activities, as well as the demolishing of
farmers’ and herders’ livelihood facilities. These challenges are currently further exacerbated due to the
movement restrictions and health risks of the COVID-19 pandemic. In line with these objectives and
challenges, and in partnership with the Palestinian Youth Union (PYU), this project will focus on
supporting the resilience of vulnerable agricultural and Bedouin communities by rehabilitating
agricultural roads, protecting agricultural lands, improving access to water for agriculture, supporting
youth in implementing agricultural activities, and providing support to women CBOs. The project will be
carried out in Area C in the northern Jordan Valley in Tubas Governorate. PYU is a key local partner
due to their expertise in working with agricultural-, women-, and youth-focused CBOs. They also have
expertise in, and took the lead in carrying-out a study on, engaging youth in agriculture.

1 - Needs

Secours Islamique France (SIF) and the project partner, Palestinian Youth Union (PYU), conducted a joint needs-assessment through field visits to the project locations on Sep. 19, 2020 and Sep. 29, 2020. During these visits, SIF and PYU staff members met with Village Councils of Kardala, Bardala, and Ein Al Beida, and of Bedouin communities. SIF and PYU also visited the proposed project locations to determine the needs in the areas and devise the most impactful project activities.A quick survey was conducted with each village council using a template. The aim of filling the survey template was to develop a profile data for each community and collect their respective urgent needs; the session to fill the survey was facilitated by PYU. In addition to the survey, focus group discussions were conducted with community members and village councils to identify the most urgent agricultural and livelihood needs. The identified needs included: irrigation water carrier lines, agricultural roads, water storage facilities, and protection and rehabilitation of agricultural land.SIF and PYU also met with representatives from women cooperatives in the area who talked about their work, products, working environment, and the challenges they face (including COVID-19 impacts, mainly on input prices and marketing).The engagement of the various stakeholders mentioned above, during the design phase of the project, ensures that the planned activities respond to the urgent needs in the targeted governorate. Through coordination with the FSS Cluster and Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), we also ensured that the activities are in line with the priorities identified in FSS and MoA strategies.This project was initially informed by the "Cluster Strategic Plan for Tubas and North Jordan Valley," which includes a strategy and work plan for 2019-2022 that aims to identify the needs, gaps, and priorities, and addresses challenges. Coordination started with FSS and MoA on Sep.17, 2020 to validate the initial selection of locations and interventions. As endorsed by MoA, coordination with the Agriculture Directorate of Tubas and Jordan Valley started on Sep. 24 and a meeting was later conducted on Sep.29 (meeting minutes availble in the Documents Tab).In 2018, PYU led a study to investigate unemployment among fresh graduates and the opportunities to engage youth, females, and males in the agriculture sector. Upon this study, a National Program Framework to engage youth in the agriculture sector was developed (2018 – 2023); the framework was developed with the participation of different authorities, including MoA, Ministry of Social Development (MoSD),Ministry of Labour, and a number of local NOGs working in the agricultural sector. The program has a vision toward a young Palestinian farmer who contributes to achieving food security and self-sufficiency for the family and society, and aims to find solutions to the unemployment problem of young graduates in Palestine, enabling them to work in the agricultural sector. This project aims to support this vision and encourage youth to participate in a number of agriculture activities through a cash-for-work intervention.

2 - Activities

The project is in line with HRP 2021 and falls under the first eligible action under the HRP Allocation, Food Security: “Support vulnerable Bedouins/Herders/Farmers/Fishers affected by reduced capacity to acquire basic inputs or access markets as needed to protect their livelihoods.” Main activities per outcome and output: Outcome 1: Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for farmers, herders, breeders, and fishers, including promotion of technical capacities and small scale production units, and using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience.Output 1.1: Affected and vulnerable farmers of the northern Jordan Valley have improved access to their lands.Activity: Rehabilitate 4km of agricultural roads in Ein el Beida and Ibziq, based on completed needs assessments. (Beneficiaries 140 HH; 337 females, 349 males – 322 children, 339 adults, 25 elderly)Output 1.2: Affected and vulnerable farmers and breeders have increased protection for their agricultural land in order to ensure their food security.Activity: Implement collective fencing for 150 dunums of agricultural lands in Bardala, Ein el Beida, and Kardala. . (Beneficiaries 40 HH; 96 females, 100 males – 92 children, 97 adults, 7 elderly).Activity: Protect grazing land in Bedouin communities by distribution of 30 tons of seeds. (Beneficiaries 100 HH; 241 females, 249 males – 231 children, 243 adults, 18 elderly).Output 1.3: Affected and vulnerable farmers of the northern Jordan Valley have improved access to water.Activity: Procure and install 2 km irrigation water carrier lines in Kardala and Bardala, serving farmers and agricultural land, based on completed needs assessments. (Beneficiaries 50 HH; 120 females, 125 males – 115 children, 121 adults, 9 elderly).Activity: Install two Metal Reservoirs with 250 cubic meter capacity each in Ein El Baida, serving farmers and agricultural land. (Beneficiaries 50 HH; 120 females, 125 males – 115 children, 121 adults, 9 elderly).Activity: Rehabilitate 1000 cubic meter capacity Metallic Reservoir in Kardala, serving farmers and agricultural land. (Beneficiaries 50 HH; 120 females, 125 males – 115 children, 121 adults, 9 elderly).Activity: Supply plastic water tanks with 5 cubic meter capacity to 25 beneficiaries in Bedouin communities in northern Jordan Valley. (Beneficiaries 25 HH; 60 females, 36 males – 58 children, 61 adults, 4 elderly).Outcome 2:Support to non-agricultural livelihoods, and Income Generating Activities (IGA).Output 2.1: Women in the northern Jordan Valley are more secure in their livelihoods by gaining support to increase or maintain their incomes, especially during COVID-19.Activity: Provide guidance and inputs to two women CBOs on health and safety for food processing in the time of COVID-19. .(20 females/adults) Outcome 3:Providing support through unrestricted cash modalities.Output 3.1:Youth in the northern Jordan Valley are more secure in their livelihoods. Activity: Implement cash-for-work activities related to collective fencing for youth, to support them in engaging with agricultural activities. (10 male/adults). Through the extensive experience of PYU working with youth in different communities and based on previous needs assessment PYU had conducted in cooperation with MoA, MoSD, MoL and other organization a need to engage youth in the agricultural sector was identified. PYU will design cash-for-work activities and urge youth to participate in implementing agricultural activities and in order to in re-highlight their role in the agricultural sector through their implementation of activities related to fencing and growing fodder seeds and increase their employment opportunities in the future. Keeping in mind that the crisis of COVID-19 increases the unemployment rates and decreased household income in most communities. A project steering committee will be created at the take-off of the project where members representing different stakeholders will be nominated and selected according pre-set criteria. The Project steering committee will support in selection of individual beneficiaries (where applicable), facilitate project's intervention and visibility in the locations; ensure the community contribution; facilitate the follow up and implementation of the activities and participate in evaluating project's intervention.All construction and rehabilitation works will follow national standards and guidelines and will be implemented in close coordination with PWA and MoA and relevant Directorates in Tubas and North Jordan Valley. SIF engineers will be conducting technical assessment visits to locations in order to develop designs, BoQ and other tender documents. Directorates will be a main partner in the tender opening sessions, follow up field visits and final handover. . All safety measures at the construction site will be included in the contracts and will be the responsibility of the contractor and monitored by SIF engineers. As most interventions will be implemented in Area C; SIF will conduct legal awareness sessions in coordination with NRC for farmers and herders and other stakeholders of the project over 5 sessions at the beginning of the project.SIF will be indicating a DO NO HARM principle: Both organizations will analyze and anticipate the range of implications actions may have on the protection of different groups and their environment. The participation of vulnerable groups in project planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation will help ensure the project is responsive to protection concerns. SIF had developed a new Child Safeguarding Policy in 2020, and will abide by it throughout this project. Also, project-related contracts include standard clause on PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

SIF interventions in the Palestinian Territory have a principled approach of individual and group physical and psychological integrity, selfrespect, and empowerment. The project aims to enhance the quality of life of Palestinian communities and is in-line with SIF values, especially alleviating the suffering of the poorest, respecting human dignity, and the beneficiaries’ diversity (cultural, religious, or social).Human dignity is highly respected by SIF and inherent to every human being, inalienable and independent of the state and occupation. The necessary realism of proper humanitarian assistance cannot merely be reduced to technical know-how bereft of ideals; thus solidarity and respect to human dignity are paramount. Development is not solely in economic terms; it has a combination of social, political, financial, natural, physical and spiritual expression. SIF core values are impartial, independent and neutral and interventions are grounded in the idea of solidarity with the people it helps. The focus of the projects is in building the local capacity and resilience of communities by providing support to vulnerable populations and enabling them to better prepare for and respond to the situation on the ground. This project brings unity in taking pride, being honorable, noble, and worthy, bringing a sense of pride to the beneficiaries to till their lands.SIF also abides by the principle of Do No Harm; we will respect a low profile during implementation of activities to avoid any harm coming to beneficiaries.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

All individuals in targeted communities will have equal opportunities to apply to be beneficiaries for relevant activities. For example, for the land fencing activity, all targeted community members in urban areas that fit the eligibility critera will have the opportunity to apply; and for the distribution of seeds and 5m3 water tanks activities, all Bedouin community members that fit the eligibility critera in the targeted areas will have the opportunity to apply.
