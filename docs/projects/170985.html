1 - Needs

A protracted protection crisis continues in Palestine, which remains largely attributable to Israel’s ongoing occupation, now in its 53rd year, violations of international humanitarian and human rights law (IHL/IHRL) and continuing Palestinian divide. Twenty-twenty brought a deterioration in the severity of protection risks in Palestine driven by the outbreak of COVID-19 and related movement restrictions, school closures, job losses and socio-economic distress. The administrative vacuum, because of the PA halt in coordination with Israel in response to threat of annexation, has introduced additional challenges. UN-OCHA estimates that at least 2.1 million Palestinians, 60 per cent in the Gaza Strip and 40 per cent in the West Bank experience, or are at risk of conflict, violence and forcible transfer, and will be in need of protection assistance in 2021.Between 1 January 2020 and 24 September 2020, there were 521 incidents of settler violence in the West Bank, which resulted in 97 Palestinian being injured, 160 vehicles vandalized and 5,517 trees vandalized. Yet, as the Government of Israel (GoI) failed to meet its 1 July 2020 deadline to declare De-jure Annexation, settlers have shifted their focus from committing acts of vandalism and violence to establishing new facts on the ground that would advance De-facto Annexation. To appease settlers, the GoI has expedited controversial settlement expansion plans and settlement infrastructure projects that will further fragment the West Bank and pave the way for annexation, while putting more families at risk of dispossession, displacement, loss of shelter, loss of livelihood, poverty and food insecurity.During this period, Israel also demolished 525 structures in the West Bank (including 80 donor-funded), displacing 123 households that consist of 692 people (including 353 children). These demolitions will increase as settlement expansion plans and infrastructure plans progress. These demolitions took place in spite of an unprecedented public health crisis, in which Palestinians needed their shelters for social distancing and quarantine, and WASH structures to maintain proper hygiene, in an effort to prevent further proliferation of the COVID-19 pandemic. NRC and ICLA partner organizations noted an increase in the use of Military Order 1797 in Area C, which allowed for the expedited demolition of new structures within 96-hours of construction, hindering the provision of humanitarian assistance. Also, due to Amendment 116 of the Planning and Building Law, which imposes greater punitive measures against individuals who construct “illegaly”, there has been a surge in self-demolitions in East Jerusalem. Furthermore, the Israeli Civil Administration established new permit regulations for construction in Area C, which renders it more difficult to obtain building permits, and a new permit regime for access to the Seam Zone, which has been marked by an exponential increase in rejected permit applications.In light of these developments, as well as: 1) the worsening COVID-19 pandemic, which brought forth a slew of discriminatory emergency regulations; 2) the looming threat of annexation; and 3) the halt in coordination, NRC foresees a significant increase in demand for legal aid in 2021.

2 - Activities

This project is designed to challenge these policies, through employing a four-pillar approach, comprising of: 1.Immediate legal response; 2. Preventative legal response; 3. Policy change response; and 4. Stakeholders’ capacity-building PC continues to engage in the 1st pillar of immediate legal response on behalf of Palestinian HHs and communities before Israeli courts to challenge policies that are absolutely proscribed in international law. The aim is to assist Palestinians to challenge demolition orders or other violations through the court system in order to provide beneﬁciaries with temporary reprieve, and allow for additional measures to be considered. This is done with the hope that respect for international law will pervade in the meantime and the violations will be suppressed. Furthermore, the Israeli judiciary system is the only option for domestic recourse to justice and international law which prescribes that one must exhaust all domestic legal remedies prior to litigating cases on an international level. As a result of PC’s ICLA programme, in 2020 so far approximately 83.89% of beneﬁciaries in Area C and East Jerusalem have remained in their homes due to legal representation provided through PC. Under the 2nd pillar, preventative legal response, forced displacement is challenged through consultations and the delivery of legal information to communities and beneﬁciaries. This response focuses on the identiﬁcation of beneﬁciary needs and pro-actively addresses obstacles towards access to justice. The 3rd pillar aims to achieve a policy change response through humanitarian focused advocacy, public interest cases (PICs), research, and engagement with UN mechanisms. Ultimately, the aim is for international law to be applied to Palestine in a way that harmonizes Israel’s current discriminatory policies and laws with IHL and IHRL norms. Speciﬁcally, the success of PICs is inter-dependent upon the inﬂuence exerted by the UN, EU and Third States to obtain favourable statements and resolutions founded on IHL and Human Rights principles, which in-turn inform the stance of the parties to the litigation. PICs consequently, and due to their principled nature, have a signiﬁcantly longer duration than individual cases, often lasting upwards of 10 years without a ﬁnal ruling; thus, preserving the status quo for Palestinian communities and avoiding large-scale eviction, demolitions and conﬁscations pending resolution of complex issues of IHL and human rights law by the court. PC is currently supporting 90 PICs in the WB, including East Jerusalem that challenge 54 principled issues pertaining to the Palestinian peoples’ fundamental human rights. These cases aim to establish positive precedent in Israeli courts, which would assist pending cases with slim margins for successful closure. Of the 13 PICs closed in 2019, 9 had successful ﬁnal outcomes, meaning that 69.23% of these HLP and Residency cases were closed with favourable rulings that did not result in forcible displacement / transfer. Through the 4th pillar, PC contributes to the Palestinian Authority (PA) capacity building and the coordination of the legal aid activities with legal professionals and local communities. Ultimately, ICLA’s work is aimed at contributing to the development of a sustainable legal aid system for Palestinian legal actors. This will be done by enhancing the capacity of the PA to work on the legal response in Area C through the Anti Wall and Settlement Resistance Commission; in East Jerusalem through raising the coordination capacity of Jerusalem Governorate, complemented by the PC co- chairing a sub-working group of the Protection Cluster, the Legal Task Force (LTF), which is the main coordination platform for the legal responses of local and international actor in the ﬁeld of legal aid

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In order to prioritize safety and dignity of Palestinians at risk or affected by forcible transfer over other considerations, ICLA will conduct protection risk assessments (Do no Harm), adopt conflict and context sensitive approach in dealing with beneficiaries, ensure informed beneficiary consent and acting on instructions of beneficiaries, securing confidentiality and data protection. In addition, ICLA will use additional protection technical expertise to supplement its interventions; coordinate with specialised protection actors to assist with conducting protection risk assessment of ICLA beneficiaries, carry out combined ICLA/protection campaign around protection issues; in collaboration with gender / child protection actors, conduct joint legal and protection research around a particular protection issue or risk in order to inform programming or recruit staff with expert protection skills in NRC. For example, although the legal framework might enshrine certain rights and entitlements that NRC would ideally like to pursue (e.g. strategic litigation), NRC will not do so, if the process of accessing justice were assessed as likely to do harm to the claimants.NRC’s Protection Policy, Gender Policy, Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy and guidance notes and tools including Core Competency Quality Assessment tools and the Protection Resource Pack all contribute to reinforcing and providing guidance on how to action the protection policy. NRC’s Code of Conduct holds staff, partners and associates (e.g. contractors) accountable, while projecting a common set of standards to the beneficiaries being served. PSEA prevention and response training is part of the induction for all staff. At the community level, ICLA will support multi-sectoral, sustainable, and integrated programming with other humanitarian actors (e.g. through West Bank Protection Consortium), which will allow people to preserve or increase their resources and opportunities, thus making them more resilient, and more likely to find durable solutions. ICLA will ensure community engagement on different levels of project cycle management and echoing community feedback. ICLA will conduct needs assessments, service mapping and where relevant, refer beneficiaries to other NRC core competencies or other partners providing specific services. NRC ICLA will support OCHA in the assessment, dissemination of information and implement referral pathways in order to ensure coordinated prevention and response to emergency. ICLA will actively and regularly participate in the Protection Cluster, lead Legal Task Force and collaborate with other Clusters and actors (e.g. UNRWA) on awareness raising, sensitisation and information dissemination activities to ensure that people have meaningful access to information and feedback mechanisms. NRC Advocacy unit will seek to maintain the humanitarian situation in West Bank on the international agenda in an effort to shape priorities and address the impact of the GoI policies through an evidence-based approach with the consent of the individuals involved, while upholding the do no harm principle.NRC has a global policy on “Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Sexual Abuse (PSEA) & Sexual Harassment Policy”, where each NRC staff in Palestine and implementing partners are obliged to sign as part of the agreement/contract. One of the articles that NRC’s partners should agree upon before projects start implementation is the “Code of Conduct for non-staff representing NRC or accompanying NRC on missions”, as sexual relationships between NRC personnel and beneficiaries of assistance undermine the credibility and integrity of the work of NRC and are prohibited. In achieving accountability and transparency, NRC’s complaints and feedback policy is complementary to NRC’s Protection Policy, Code of Conduct, Child Safe Guarding, Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse documents, Anti-Corruption and the Security and Risk Management.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

ICLA’s main priorities will be to empower women, men, girls and boys to have equal access to information and services. ICLA will develop information and communication products to support vulnerable groups to access basic services, rights and entitlements (e.g. online, social media, WhatsApp etc. methodologies to overcome Covid-19 restrictions). ICLA will also use a community-based and participatory approach to identify and train members of the communities to disseminate information, recognise protection concerns and refer cases to specialised service providers. Legal assistance including representation: ICLA will use judicial and administrative remedies to support access to justice for the most vulnerable including women, children and people with disabilities. Approaches and procedures will be selected on a case-by-case basis to allow for a customised response that meets the specific needs and interests of the beneficiary(s). In this respect, carefully selected and trained fe/male officers may provide legal information and advice, facilitate negotiations, and if relevant, provide legal representation. Capacity Building: ICLA will also provide technical support (sensitisation, training, orientation, etc.) to local NGOs, youth and women leaders and community representatives, as well as humanitarian actors with a view to strengthening their capacity to ensure protection for IDPs/refugees and support host communities. Particular emphasis will be placed on IHL/IHRL, rules and regulations about the rights and issues of women and other vulnerable groups. Subject to needs assessment, ICLA may also provide financial and organisational support to implementing partner to strengthen their capacity and ensure sustainability of the intervention. Advocacy: Research and technical support on legal status, freedom of movement, access to rights and services and adequate standard of living, including housing will be used to inform the major protection actors, donor community, and other relevant stakeholders, with the respective analyses of the current protection situation, strengthen the networks and seek allies in order to influence policies and decision making in relation to the targeted population. ICLA recognises the fundamental need to address the different impacts of conflict and disasters on men, women, boys and girls and promote the potential for positive transformation of gender norms. With regard to the situation in the West Bank, women are disproportionately affected by the loss of homes resulting from demolitions and forced evictions and often face more dire consequences due to the denial of residency rights, such as their inability to enter the workforce due to movement restrictions. Jerusalem residency rights convey access to social welfare entitlements available to all of the city’s residents.Theresidents. The provision of legal aid can serve to alleviate the psychosocial impact of the threat of displacement by reassuring the entire family that the demolition or eviction is being challenged, providing at least temporary protection at no cost to the family. Additionally, delays in demolition and eviction can be for many years, allowing families time to plan for a future displacement. In the longer term, the project’s delivery of legal aid to help women, men, girls and boys secure housing, land, property, freedom of movement and residency rights will improve gender equality and contribute to a more prosperous and empowered Palestinian society.ICLA’s Mobile Legal Aid Clinic is specifically designed to reach marginalized communities. In the second quarter of 2020 alone, 206 paralegal services were provided through the MLAC in three locations to 167 beneficiaries (80 Male, 87 Female). Out of these beneficiaries, 76 were from female headed households and all of them have financial hardship. See the Indicators section for more data.
