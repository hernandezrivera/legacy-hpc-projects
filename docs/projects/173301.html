Project Summary

HEKS will continue to be the shelter focal point of catchment 7 & 8, which covers camp 14,15 & 16. As HEKS already implements TSA I in Camp 15 in 2019 and 2020 and have very good knowledge of the shelter situation and good working relation with SMS, CiC and other actor working on those camps, for 2021 HEKS aims to support 21,597 households in camp 14,15 and 16.
Assistance will include but will not be limited to: Emergency shelter assistance including RDV, strengthening referrals through Catchment FP, Supporting EVI with shelter materials delivery and implementation, Providing TDK, awareness and technical support during the response, Distribution of COVID NFI for quarantine facilities and families on quarantine and TSA2.
In all the activities, HEKS will ensure PwSN, older people or women head of HHs will receive homedelivery support and labour assistance in the construction. Distribution points will have priority lines for older people, women and PwSN with shaded area and place to seat. HEKS will follow SNFI Sector guidance and will ensure appropriate coordination with other partners working in the same area.
Staff will be trained on Gender and PSEA to ensure proper gender and protection mainstreaming.
Households will be trained in DRR and shelter reinforcement Special focus will be provided on women participation. HEKS will ensure monitoring and technical support for the HHS during the implementation.
Materials will be received from IOM common pipeline for all the activities, cost of materials is not considered in the budget.
A mixed approach will be used to receive complaints. Real-time feedback will be collected by the volunteers during household visits. During distributions, a help and complaint raising desk will installed. PDM will allow beneficiaries to place their complaints. Aactivities will be monitored by M&E team. Findings will be used to improve the activities.

1 - Identified Needs

As HEKS/EPER already implements TSA I in Camp 15, HEKS/EPER has very good knowledge of the shelter situation in the camp. For TSA I, detailed surveys were conducted, which includes a Shelter Survey (October 2019), Post Distribution Monitoring (November 2019), TSA I Performance Monitoring (November 2019), and TSA I Technical Assessment (February 2020). Following coordination with the Shelter/NFI Sector, main findings from those survey were, insufficient materials, safe cooking space was often missing, bracing was rare, larger families received same amount of assistance, TSAs will continue being the principle shelter approach. TSA I was implemented in the targeted camps from early 2019 to early 2020. More than one year have past and those households didn’t received any shelter materials for improving the shelter structure. According to MSNA, need of shelter materials is 1st priority out of 5, 69% of households faced issues with their shelter in the last 6 months. 51% issues with their roof, 17% walls, 15% floor/plinth, 10% damaged/rotten materials. 28% households not having made any improvement in last 6 month, reasons are 36% did not received any support from any humanitarian organization, 23% HH’s have no money to pay, 15% HH’s could not access to materials. Additionally, during lockdown situation Rohingya’s ware not able to access in local market to purchase shelter materials.The MSNA 2019 and Light Multi-Sector Needs Assessment 2019 (Light MSNA 2019 – based on a smaller sample than the MSNA 2019) indicate an ongoing need for kitchen items, clothing, bedding and lighting. Both data sets show that not all households have the same needs. To respond to this finding NFIs will be provided through a voucher system, which allows the refugee population to choose the items they need most from a predetermined range. In 2020, due to the COVID-19 restriction, NFI assistance was focused on the assistance to the older people, to ensure they can isolate themselves and therefore reduce exposure to the virus. As COVID-19 precautions need to be taking into consideration also in 2021, HEKS will ensure security measures in NFI distribution as well as in any other shelter activities. 38% of households reported facing mobility challenges inside and/or outside their shelters. The response shall be addressed with shelter-level site improvement with focus on the accessibility of PwD. 58% reported to not have enough light.27% of Rohingya refugees mentioned clothing and household cooking items as their priority needs.The Focal Point system is intended to improve emergency coordination and response including RDV. CFP will support the CiC/SMS agency with the development of emergency response plans for moderate and major events. CFP will also have a role during extreme events, specifically a cyclone, in accordance with the 72h Cyclone Plan. CFP will refer protection and gender The monsoon response will be coordinated at camp level by FPs/CFPs. CFP.In Rohingya community women are excluded from decision-making systems, and their lack of access to reliable information, consultations and feedback mechanisms limits their ability to influence both prevention and response plans. The inclusion of women leaders, networks and volunteers in the planning, implementation and monitoring of response plans will have a strong impact on making these right-based strategies, inclusive and effective. HEKS/EPER will ensure the most vulnerable households, including female-headed households, still have access to materials and support to make shelter improvements and to prepare for the cyclone season.
