Project Summary

The effects of Covid-19, Floods and droughts has resulted in massive challenges pertaining to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene. Approximately, 4.6 million Somalis are still in need due to insufficient access to portable water and inadequate sanitation services of the affected population in many districts, thus negatively affecting the country’s public health and the dignity of highly vulnerable and displaced communities. This spike in numbers compared to 2020 is mainly exacerbated by the situation developed as a result of Covid-19 and its rapid transmission. Lack of adequate WASH services to the population is a main cause of spread of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD), leading to malnutrition in the country and contributing to increasing the existing stunting rate of 25.3% in children below 5 years of age. It is also a cause of relapse (ADD) when treated patients and children returning to their communities where safe water and sanitation coverages are limited, resulting in limited sustainability of health programs.
According to JMCNA, approximately 8.9 million people are affected by the prevalent poor WASH conditions in Somalia. The methodology used for the PIN calculation estimated that 4.6 million people are in catastrophic (10%) severe (9%) and extreme (18%) need of humanitarian WASH assistance.
The population in need of adequate WASH services in 2021 includes approximately 1.16 million IDPs 3.36 million host communities, 53,906 Returnees and 28,002 Refugees. Among the people in need, 2.9M are children, 0.7M are women and 0.7M are men. Among those groups, the cluster also recognises that 15% are persons with disabilities and 4% are elderly persons who will require specific attention. Persons with disabilities face additional challenges and barriers. Barriers include environmental barriers, which inhibitive access to WASH facilities, as well as social barriers, leading to discrimination within communities and further aggravating the risk of exclusion to WASH services. Physical barriers are often due to the design and distance to water and sanitation facilities, which makes it difficult for persons with disabilities to have equal access to WASH services. The lack of consultation and engagement with persons with disabilities in WASH designs, limits the opportunity for an inclusive response and contributes further to the marginalization of this vulnerable group.
Only 2 per cent of households across Somalia report availability of water and soap at latrines and 52 per cent at home. In case of non-availability of soap, people wash their hands with soap substitutes (40 per cent). Other coping mechanisms include borrowing cash or hygiene materials (12 per cent) or relying on humanitarian assistance. Only 45 per cent of households have access to Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) material at home or can access it through their local market.
STS proposes a WASH project targeting IDPS and Vulnerable host communities in Janaale and Howlwadaag in Marka district, and Afgooye district of lower Shabelle, WarSheikh and Balaad of Middle Shabelle, Dhusamareeb of Galgaduud region and Kahda and Deynile of Banadir region.
This proposed WASH project by STS will contribute to the WASH cluster objective: Deliver life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute needs among most vulnerable settlements and communities (including men, women, boys and girls) in a dignified manner.
This proposed project will also contribute to the overall specific objective of: Provide integrated WASH and Health response in 74 districts to reduce the excess morbidity and mortality rate from preventable crisis-driven diseases and public health outbreaks among 2.8 million most vulnerable people; by the end of 2021.
The WASH Project will target 28,000 with access to clean and safe water, proper hygiene and sanitation, hygiene promotion and awareness and prevention of poor hygiene associated diseases like Acute watery diarrhoea, Cholera, as wells prevention of COVID-19.

Project Activities

STS will undertake the following WASH activities that will directly contribute to the WASH cluster objectives: Deliver life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute needs among most vulnerable settlements and communities (including men, women, boys, girls and disabilities) in a dignified manner; as follows:1. Deliver Emergency Water Services through:Emergency water trucking with temporary distribution systems for 2 months minimum;Household Water Treatment and Safe Storage (HWTSS) for 2 months minimum;Well disinfection and chlorination;Extension/rehabilitation of existing water supply system;Provision of water containers for transport and storage;Monitoring of water quality at HH level and/or community level2. Deliver Sanitation services through construction/rehabilitation of sanitation facilities targeting communities who do not have any access.3. Deliver Hygiene Promotion Activities through Distribution of hygiene kits and Hygiene promotion campaigns.4. Restore Access to Basic Sanitation Including institutions by undertaking the following activities:Construction/improvement/rehabilitation available water sources (Shallow Dug wells, Boreholes, Spring etc) to ensure it is protected in compliance with cluster standards.Borehole rehabilitation with full compliance to cluster guidelines.Establishment/Rehab of small-scale water supply systems including pumping, elevatedtanks, distribution systems.Establishment of water committee to ensure proper maintenance of water services.Strong Disaster Risk Reduction component in flood/drought prone areas.5. Related to WASH Safety Index sub-indicators.A strong protection/safety lens will be applied to reduce risk of violence against women and children, and men or other vulnerable groups (ageing and disability). Consequently, we avoid gender blindness in programming through a consultative process with men, and women. Equally, all ages within the target age bracket will be given equal opportunity to be represented as well as those with disability and from corner clans. Marginalized groups and minority clans will be given equal opportunity to participate when accessing WASH facilities:Latrines build with solid walls and locks at doors;Latrines with internal light (or alternate options if relevant);Gender separated latrines (or alternate options if relevant);Toilet at reasonable walking distance;Water point at reasonable walking distance;HH consulted in design, locations and delivery of water; (considering ageing and disabilityas a vulnerability as well)HH consulted in design, location and delivery of sanitation services (considering ageingand disability as a vulnerability as well).6. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) for hygiene items such as soap, menstrual hygiene products, chlorine [1] for household water treatment, and water transport and storage containers, and/ or for water and sanitation services

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Unconditional Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) for hygiene items such as soap, menstrual hygiene products, chlorine [1] for household water treatment, and water transport and storage containers, and/ or for water and sanitation services. This is because in some locations distribution of in-kind hygiene kits and menstrual hygiene products will be difficult to access for the most vulnerable IDPs and host communities due to insecurity and cultural inappropriateness of the distrubtion. However, Unconditional Cash can bridge that gap and provide traceable access to acutely needed WASH services.

Mitigation Measures

Beneficiary selection will be systematic to ensure vulnerable persons, such as marginalized minorities, single/child headed households and Mothers/caregivers/PLWs with disabilities are included. • Different sociodemographic groups will be consulted on their specific WASH requirements and preferences for distribution points.• Outreach messaging will be respectful and inclusive to ensure that beneficiaries from different sociodemographic, economic and cultural groups know about WASH services, distributions times/sites. 3a. • Safe programming and mainstreaming gender at all stages will be ensured. The integration of STS Protection staff in the project team will help ensure the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. • STS will engage with local IDP and community leaders and community representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect WASH practices, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build beneficiaires self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. • STS will train all staff involved in the WASH sector on psychosocial support (PSS) including identification and support for disabled beneficiaries. 3b. • STS staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. •STS staff and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries will receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). 3c. • STS will locate distribution points in locations that are accessible by vulnerable population groups from the catchment population. 4b. STS will maintain a complaints and feedback phone number that allows anonymous submission of feedback. Timely corrective response or feedback will be maintained.
