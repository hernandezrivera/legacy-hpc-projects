Project Summary

In line with the Protection cluster strategy for 2021, ACTED will implement its mobile responses in locations with emergency needs and in hard-to-reach areas to contribute towards meeting the need of over 5 million people facing protection risks in South Sudan. Through this project, ACTED and its partner CINA will target up to 63,960 beneficiaries with varied protection services ranging from awareness raising, protection monitoring and distribution of kits to strengthening referral systems and direct case management.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, there are currently more than 5 million people, including 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers in South Sudan, in need of protection services. Majority of the people in need are women, 57% and children 52 % as they are disproportionality affected by violence and persistent insecurity. Other vulnerable groups include elderly and people with disabilities who often struggle to access the limited services provided to the general population. These groups often experience crises very differently due to their different roles within the family and society, as well as unequal power dynamics that exist between and among them. These groups also tend to be excluded from decision making due to deeply rooted imbalances or have limited mobility that prevents them from accessing crucial information and react rapidly if necessary. Disputes over use of resources and access to limited services continues to negatively influence coexistence of the communities, and affecting displaced communities in particular, endangering peaceful coexistence with host communities. There is an urgent need to contribute towards early identification of issues, mitigation and peaceful resolution of conflicts between communities. Living in displacement exposes women, girls and boys to additional risks including gender based violence connected to shelter structures, lack of privacy, site layout or uneven distribution and control over resources. Formal access to justice is limited and women are hesitant to approach community based GBV response mechanisms, as the leaders involved in case mediation are mostly men with little training on ensuring confidentiality, privacy, and dignity of the survivors. As a result, there is an urgent need to strengthen the ability of the community based structures to actively participate in prevention and response to GBV. Reporting of sexual violence, including rape and sexual assault, remains low due to fear of retaliation, stigma and discrimination survivors face within their communities. Increase in behavior changing activities is therefore crucial. Furthermore, there is substantial evidence, including in ACTED rapid assessments, indicating girls’ movements are severely restricted and subject to heightened risk of SGBV once they begin to menstruate due to cultural perception of their maturity connected to their ability to have children. There is a particular need to assist adolescent girls in displacement to help them manage their needs and menstrual hygiene in dignity. To address these needs, ACTED will continue to integrate protection services into all of its mobile interventions in 2021 in line with the cluster objectives.

2 - Project activities

In line with the Protection cluster strategy for 2021, ACTED will enhance the provision of protection services through their integration with the mobile responses in locations with emergency needs and in hard-to-reach areas. ACTED previously implemented protection activities through its local partner CINA. The activities ACTED implemented under protection section were reflected in reporting to the cluster by ACTED’s implementing partner CINA. In 2021, due to the increase in needs, ACTED expanded its protection services mainstreamed across its response and will be directly implementing various activities. These activities include provision of protection monitoring in locations where ACTED will provide emergency response, as well as in areas of high return for IDPs and refugee returnees. In these areas, ACTED will provide multi-sectoral services to reduce suffering of the population with a specific focus on women, children, elderly and people with disabilities. People with special needs identified through protection monitoring will be further assisted through in-kind support and referrals to ensure immediate assistance and prevent harm. ACTED aims to directly reach at least 2,000 beneficiaries through this assistance. At the same time, ACTED will aim to enhance peaceful coexistence of host communities and returnees through improvement of community infrastructure that could be the cause of competition (eg. Water resources) between communities, supporting intra and inter community dialogues and provision of in-kind support to conflict resolution mechanisms as well as creating understanding through conflict analysis. At least 3,800 beneficiaries will benefit from the support of peaceful coexistence. ACTED will aim to improve livelihood opportunities of displaced populations through community-led livelihood initiatives to improve coping mechanisms, increase resilience and increase protection of the often disenfranchised groups. The livelihood interventions support at least 30 beneficiaries per mobile intervention (660 in total). At the same time, ACTED will support creation of women friendly spaces, awareness raising activities on GBV, supporting peer to peer counselling, strengthening of referral pathways to ensure improved community led response. ACTED will aim to reach at least 11,000 community members to change attitudes towards GBV and strengthen community led response mechanisms. At the same time, to improve protection of adolescent girls in displacement, ACTED will aim to distribute in-kind support to at least 1,000 girls (dignity kits, hygiene kits and other support based on needs). At the same time, ACTED will conduct border monitoring to inform the overall humanitarian response and improve access to basic services, including protection, for refugee returnees along border with Uganda. ACTED will conduct border monitoring and protection assessments on 8 border crossings in Easter Equatoria State. ACTED will utilize the collected information for further advocacy with cluster partners and other organizations to ensure coordinated and rapid response to emerging needs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During sensitization of beneficiaries on CRM mechanism, ACTED informs beneficiaries about PSEA and provides clear instructions on how to report such cases utilizing CRM present at the site of implementation. ACTED explains how such sensitive cases are handled to encourage reporting.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As per its standard operating procedures for CRM, throughout the project, ACTED will ensure that multiple channels of communication (e.g.: help desk, staff registering complaints, hotline etc,) are available for ACTED to receive feedback from communities including the most vulnerable members such as individuals living with disabilities and individuals who are not literate. Furthermore, ACTED will have at least one women staff member in help desks to encourage women to register their feedback to ACTED.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the implementation project activities, beneficiaries will be sensitized on the complaints and response mechanism, and on how to submit concerns or complaints to ACTED. Moreover, help desks at project sites will be installed at central locations and equipped with visibility materials.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ACTED’s independent AMEU will continuously register the complaints and feedback of the target population and insert them into ACTED South Sudan’s learning database. The findings will be discussed at least on a monthly basis with the project implementation and project development teams to ensure that the feedback is incorporated into the ongoing project as well as for the design of upcoming projects.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

ACTED counts on a robust internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA. All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which includes ACTED’s Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy and Child Protection Policy. The staff will receive refresher trainings on these policies on a regular basis. ACTED will take the necessary measures to ensure that the communities receiving services and items will have safe access to distribution sites by selecting an accessible location. Vulnerable and/or marginalized groups will be included in community consultations to ensure they are included in decision-making processes and receive the assistance provided by ACTED. ACTED will conduct inception meetings with external stakeholders including local authorities to ensure that the scope of the project and any selection criteria is widely understood to prevent further interference.
