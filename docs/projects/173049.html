Project Summary

A successive series of economic and political crises have reinforced the context of humanitarian crisis in Sudan. Although the signature of a Peace Agreement by some armed groups on October 3rd is encouraging, the situation remains fragile and volatile. Among others, the recent conflict in Ethiopia and the influx of refugees into Sudan represents a major challenge for a country in political transition.
Specifically, South Kordofan is highly vulnerable and weakened by several years of war that have aggravated structural development problems that were previously present. The crisis has led to a breakdown in the administrative system and the deterioration or collapse of public services in the region.
The presence of PUI through medico-nutritional assistance interventions makes it possible to follow the evolution of the needs of the most vulnerable populations. Through the monitoring of activities by its teams at the field and coordination level, the work in collaboration with the health and administrative authorities, and the different NGOs , PUI reaffirms the fact that it is essential to continue supporting the most vulnerable populations through the improvement of access to free and quality medico-nutritional care in the South Kordofan, in particular children and women by extending its coverage through the reinforcement of the mobile strategy.
Contribute to reducing the morbidity and mortality of the population affected.
Improve access to totally or partially free, quality medical-nutritional care for the populations

1 - Needs

The continuing political tensions in Sudan, as well as the current economic crisis, have a direct impact on the living standards of the Sudanese people. Inflation has now reached over 80%, the prices of basic foodstuffs continue to rise, fuel and cash are increasingly difficult to find, the country's debt stands at 60 billion dollars and unemployment persists. Last September, the new government launched an emergency plan to revive the economy, focusing on four areas: the fight against unemployment, the transition from humanitarian to sustainable development, price stabilization and the strengthening of economic institutions. However, maintaining the country on the American blacklist of states supporting terrorism represents a major obstacle to the country's economic recovery (complexity of international transfers, reluctance of foreign investors) and the health crisis linked to the COVID 19 epidemic is contributing to worsening the country's economic situation. The successive economic and political crises have reinforced the context of humanitarian crisis in Sudan. According to UNOCHA, 9.3 million people will need humanitarian assistance in 2020, i.e. 23% of the country's population compared to 5.4 million in 2015; the economic crisis has pushed several million Sudanese into poverty and worsened the situation of already precarious populations. Impact on the standard of living of the population: as mentioned above, several million people are now in need of humanitarian aid as a direct consequence of the economic crisis. In the face of galloping inflation, households are now spending more on basic necessities, leaving other items of expenditure considered of secondary importance such as education, health or drinking water. According to UNOCHA, 77% of households spend more than half of their budget on food purchases. More and more people are no longer able to meet their needs, and this problem is particularly acute for the most vulnerable groups of people, including refugees and displaced persons. According to UNOCHA, 2.7 million children suffer from acute malnutrition. This reinforces the humanitarian needs already present in the past: malnutrition and under-nutrition are still present, unemployment and lack of resources in the countryside push people to settle on the outskirts of cities, thus reinforcing the inability of public services to meet their needs. Impact on security: The context of political and economic instability has contributed to an upsurge in banditry, violence and tensions between communities. These dynamics have resulted in a significant deterioration of security in the country, particularly in the outlying areas of the South (Darfur, Kordofan). The violence is fueled by inter-community tensions and land conflicts, which have been on the rise in recent months, as well as by banditry, which has always been present in the outlying regions, but which is increasing as a result of the "security loosening" felt or experienced following the change of regime and the economic crisis. Occasional attacks against the population push people to flee in search of security; for example, the clashes of January 2020 in Geneina (West Darfur) caused the displacement of up to 48,000 people.Impact on services, particularly the health and education systems: The crises that have impacted Sudan in recent years have led to a deterioration of public services, particularly in the health sector. The decrease in drug imports is particularly significant: they fell from $221 million to $124 million between 2017 and 2019 due to a lack of resources, in addition to the country's internal obstacles making drug supply more complex: irregular and insufficient supply between the different levels of the health system, particularly between the chief town and health centers, and the rainy season, which makes some health centers inaccessible for several months. 2 - Activities/Outputs

Activity 1: Strengthening the technical platform of three health facilities Improving access to care will take into account the specificities of the intervention area. The health centers will be supported by PUI according to their capacities in light of the humanitarian context through the support of partially free health care to the population (FEFA and children under 5 years old) if possible, and will also depend on the humanitarian access of certain areas. To date, 3 health centers have been identified: Tabassa Health Center, Banat Al Murib Health Center, Al Murib Health Unit. These centers will also be targeted by TGH for EHA outcome. This targeting may change in a few months, depending on the support of certain NGOs, which fluctuates in Sudan. At this level, it will be a question of strengthening the access of the population to the :- Curative health care (management of cases of disease) - Preventive care (vaccination, prenatal consultations, post-natal consultations, family planning) - Reproductive healthPUI will strengthen the technical platform of the health structures supported notably through the provision of medicines to health centers according to the country's import rules for medical consumables and small equipment. This strengthening is intended to ensure that beneficiaries have access to the treatments prescribed by health workers and to prevent breakdowns in essential molecules. Within this framework, PUI will also train health workers on supply chain management, compliance with drug ordering procedures and also on rational prescription of these drugs. Donations of medicines will be supplemented by a supply of small medical equipment and equipment (consultation table, delivery table, small medical materials such as stethoscopes, blood pressure monitors, scales, measuring rod, etc.) in order to guarantee the minimum equipment for the realization of preventive consultations, curative consultations and deliveries. This element is essential in the context of reducing the risks of maternal-infant mortality.Finally, in order to improve retention and reduce absenteeism of qualified staff in health centers, induced or aggravated by the crisis, PUI will provide financial support for the payment of staff salaries according to the national grid in force in the country and harmonized with other partners to avoid competition, with a view to ensuring the attractiveness of the positions and also to enable the recruitment of missing staff where necessary. This financial support will consist of a monthly incentive bonus that will be added to the salary that health personnel should receive. The absence and/or lack of qualified personnel (especially female personnel) impacts the quality of primary health care, particularly reproductive health care activities and the use of traditional birth attendants in precarious health conditions, due to the lack of access to functional structures for pregnant women. In connection with the current Covid-19 pandemic and the recurrent epidemics that the country may face (cholera), the health personnel and centers will be equipped and the staff trained (see Activity 2) in ICP equipment enabling them during consultations and waste sorting, to be effectively protected (masks, gloves, gowns, alcoholic solutions...) and to have at their disposal the necessary equipment to carry out sorting, collection and adequate management of waste in order to prevent and fight against infections. Medical waste will be incinerated according to the standard procedure at the health center or hospital level (at the district level). 5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

PUI is strongly committed to monitor the potential environmental impact of their interventions. The organization will implement a waste management plan that aims at mitigating the risks associated with hazardous waste. These risks could be significantly reduced through simple measures (reduction and sorting of waste, reduction of storage and transportation, training and sensitization). The reduction of waste is also encouraged by reducing the amount at source (choosing products that generate less waste, equipment that can be reused).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

PUI strive to implement the principles of cross-cutting protection in the implementation of their activities, namely : - The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries- Ensure effective, ethical and meaningful access to the assistance delivered- Accountability, participation and empowerment of people During the construction and rehabilitation of the various targeted services (health centers, latrines, schools, etc.), special attention will be paid to the accessibility of these sites to people with reduced mobility. Specific adjustments will be made to the facilities in order to guarantee identical access to all persons from the community. When delivering information messages (selection of beneficiaries, presentation of intervention modalities, complaint mechanism, etc.), organizations will ensure that the information is heard and understood by all, without retention or bias. Their representativeness and consideration will therefore be ensured at all stages of the project cycle. Prevention of negative effects of assistance provided PUI will take particular care to avoid any negative effects or tensions that could result from the process of selecting beneficiaries and/or localities to be targeted ("do no harm" principle). The inclusion of communities in the decisions helps prevent unintended negative impacts while guaranteeing the dignity and supporting the legitimacy of local leaders. Access to assistance and services is tailored to all individuals and groups in order to prevent potential restrictions in accessibility caused by physical or social vulnerabilities that beneficiaries may suffer. PUI will ensure that the most vulnerable people are not left out of the selections and have equal access to the services targeted by this project by, for example, conducting individual household visits and collecting potential complaints and claims regarding vulnerability criteria and the selection process.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The complaint mechanism is an essential component of the accountability of programs at PUI. The mechanism covers a wide range of stakeholders, starting with affected populations, communities, local civil society actors, international humanitarian actors, local authorities or national governments and donors.Complaints are a formal expression of dissatisfaction or dissatisfaction with something and / or resentment of someone who calls for an answer. They may to carry on:i) The quality of the work carried out on the program. This may include: timeliness, safety and relevance activities. This includes complaints about targeting, the quality of items or services, and the failure to keep promises.(ii) The behaviour of staff or volunteers. This may include: a lack of respect for the members of the community; allegations of corruption, nepotism, favouritism or discrimination; or allegations of allegations of sexual harassment, abuse and exploitation. The process is carried out in real time to allow external stakeholders to express their concerns, complain or provide feedback on PUI activities in a given field at any time. Three different "entry points" are used for this purpose: the establishment of a telephone number, a "complaints box" in health centers, and a dedicated e-mail address for collecting comments.The PUI complaint mechanism is characterized by four criteria, it is: 1) Clearly defined: since the term "comments" is a broad concept, the complaints mechanism is framed in such a way that stakeholders are fully aware of "what they can comment on".2) Inclusive: the mechanism allows all key program stakeholders to access and use it. Special attention is given to " give voice" to the most vulnerable people in the communities and those who may have difficulty for some reason in expressing their reactions/views.3) Safe and confidential: Individuals, groups or entities must feel safe in their interactions with the mechanism set up by PUI. The potential fear of retaliation is clearly addressed and mitigated.4) Responsive: PUI has a responsibility to provide stakeholders who provide feedback with an effective response to the comment. Received.Trainings related to the MEAL Pack are regularly carried out by the HQ or the field. In fact, these tools are already understood and appropriated by the teams. However, discussions are regularly organized to discuss ways to improve the mechanisms in place and to adapt them to the context and needs.
