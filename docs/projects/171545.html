Project Summary

The overall goal of the project is to improve vulnerable women’s access to quality multi sectoral services including multisectoral SGBV services, cash enablement, and sexual and reproductive health services and foster women-led humanitarian response (including in response to COVID-19) in oPt. The project responds to HRP Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
This would be achieved throughout three results: 1) To Improve vulnerable women's and girls' access to quality multi sectoral health and protection services 2) To improve the quality of SGBV services provided to vulnerable women and girls 3) Advocate for equitable access to healthcare and protection services, especially for the most vulnerable population to both SGBV and COVID-19 pandemic, through meaningful women’s and girls´ participation.
UN Women will capitalize on the achievements and lessons learnt of the last years interventions in addressing SGBV needs, supporting partners to improve quality, accessibility and responsiveness of the services to the needs of those who are most vulnerable, including to COVID-19 and to conflict-related consequences. UN Women proposes to implement this 12-month project to improve most vulnerable women’s and girls´ accessibility to multi-sectoral healthcare and protection services, cash based initiatives, decision-making, awareness-raising and accurate information on COVID-19, on SGBV, and on service availability.
UN Women’s interventions will be carefully tailored to address women’s and girls’ needs following rights-based and survivor-centred approaches. Interventions are designed to address the needs of the most vulnerable women and girls i.e. women with physical and intellectual disabilities, elderly women and those with non-communicable diseases, women at risk of forcible transfer, women heads of households, women SGBV survivors, etc.
Through its local partners in the oPt, UN Women will work to couple SGBV services with cash assistance and cash for work programming to ensure a maximized benefit for women most affected by conflict and COVID-19. Capacity building, case identification, management and referral will be also strengthened with a special focus on community protection mechanisms to deliver healthcare and protection services. At the same time UN Women will support access to comprehensive, accurate and updated information to partners on COVID-19 and its gendered implications. This information will be disability-adapted and produced in several cultural and age friendly formats and disseminated to the population through the CSOs, and the media.
To change social norms and behaviors around SGBV and women’s protection, men and boys will also be engaged through positive masculinity trainings and specialized trainings on GBV and GBV services.

1 - Needs

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), as of 1st October 2020, there are 51.062 confirmed cases (47.987 in the West Bank including East Jerusalem and 3.075 in Gaza). 52.29% are females, while 45.77 are males. 80% of people infected are between 0-49 years and the other 20% are over 50. COVID-19 cases across the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) in September rose by 40%, and by 84% in the Gaza Strip. Based on UN Women’s research on the gendered impact of COVID-19 (May 2020), the ongoing COVID-19 crisis has created and exacerbated gender-specific risks and vulnerabilitiesThe COVID-19 pandemic has increased protection concerns. Domestic violence against women and children has increased during the emergency as a result of “increased psychological and economic pressures”. In fact, COVID-19 has left many victims of family violence trapped at home with a violent perpetrator at a time of severely curtailed contact with those outside the household. Fifty-three per cent of service providers reported an increase in domestic violence with psychological violence being the main type experienced During the month of June, Sawa documented a 30 per cent increase in calls over May 2020 with 102 Women reporting physical, verbal, and emotional abuse from across the oPt. Based on preliminary data collection and meetings that UN Women had with more than 30 women led organizations and women’s leaders in the West Bank and Gaza, the ongoing COVID-19 crisis has created and exacerbated gender-specific risks and vulnerabilities. As a result of the declaration of an emergency and the restriction on movement, an increase in domestic violence was reported by service providers. Relevant hotlines have experienced increased calls regarding SGBV in addition to the psycho-social challenges and violence that were already present due the conflict and Israeli occupation (UN Women Rapid Assessment Covid-19, May 2020).According to UN Women recent Rapid Gender Analysis of COVID-19 pandemic in Palestine (May 2020) , under the current COVID-19 crisis, gender inequalities are not only increasing, but roles of women, girls, men and boys are also shifting and creating new dynamics which are being shaped by a series of overlapping economic, social and political factors. Survivors of GBV have witnessed their access to services badly affected by the pandemic. During the first two months of the lockdown, MoSD has halted all field visits and some CSO service providers have suspended essential services, including group therapy sessions and case conferences, due to capacity issues. Services were later resumed as MoSD got an approval from the council of ministers to consider GBV services as essential services during emergencies. In April 2020, 28 per cent of service providers reported a decrease in the reporting of GBV attributing this to movement restrictions and fear of COVID-19 which limit women at risk’s abilities to leave home and seek help. GBV victims without a phone, internet, or awareness to seek support cannot access vital services. Victims concerns about their protection can hinder them from reporting cases and seeking support. Without peer support to encourage victims to seek help, abuse is more likely to continue unreported. (UN Women Gender Alert August 2020)Currently, women represent nearly 60% of workers in the care sector in Palestine. Women also comprise 70% of frontline health workers. As the outbreak evolves, this is implying a heavier load of treatment and care, particularly for women frontline healthcare and social workers. It also implies that the face greater exposure to COVID-19. Stigmatization is also influencing health workers. Both male and female frontline health workers are increasingly exposed to isolation and ill-treatment in some cases. Measures taken to prevent COVID-19 infection are reinforcing patriarchal norms and traditional perceptions. For women and girls, that already experienced strict control over their movements, lockdown and social distancing limits their social freedom, access to services and resources and engagement in the community (UN Women rapid gender analysis of COVID-19). According to the GBV AoR Helpdesk given the increase in reports of GBV, ensuring that women and girls can access GBV support services remains a critical and lifesaving activity. women’s access to safe health services and to accurate information on COVID-19 remain an area of priority. Also according to UN Women’s assessment of impact of cash for work programming on women, The Cash for Work opportunities did not only support women financially, but also positively impacted their social lives. The assessment results showed that 93.8 per cent per cent of women respondents indicate that the CFW benefited them in many different ways including participation in decision-making in the household, decreasing tensions in the household, gaining elevated social position in the household, and reconnecting with society. means that the average household income doubled as a result of the cash for work initiatives.Against this backdrop and continuing its strategic cooperation with humanitarian actors (mainly sectors and clusters) and CSO partners, UN Women proposes to implement a one year project to a) improve vulnerable women's and girls' access to quality multi sectoral health and protection services b) To improve the quality of SGBV services provided to vulnerable women and girls especially women GBV survivors and women affected by COVID-19 c) Advocate for equitable access to healthcare and protection services, especially for the most vulnerable population to both SGBV and COVID-19 infection through meaningful women and girls´ participation.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Do No Harm As a member of the Humanitarian Country Team in the oPt, UN Womenoperates under the SPHERE principles. Do No Harm is a key humanitarian principleunder this framework, and guides UN Women’s programme development andimplementation. Thus, UN Women ensures that assistance does no harm, orminimizes the harm that may invariantly be done. In this regard, UN Women will workwith partners to ensure that the cash for work is coupled with protection-relatedinterventions such as GBV case management and awareness raising of men and thatto ensure a protective and enabling environment for women with disabilities, whereincome becomes an enabler to live a life that is free from abuse. On communitysensitization, UN Women will support the partners to carefully develop their messagesand pre-test them with the target groups and with victims/survivors to ensure that theywill be effective to the target audience and will help prevent any unintended harm. Asfor psychosocial and legal counselling, UN Women will ensure it is provided as perIASC guidelines and that of the MHPSS WG and the legal task force

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UN Women and CSO partners will adopt a survivor-centred approach in supportingwomen and girls with disabilities including victims and survivors of GBV. This willensure that survivors’ rights and needs are prioritized first and foremost. The survivorcentredapproach aims to create a supportive environment in which the survivor’srights are respected and in which women survivors are treated with dignity andrespect. The approach helps to promote the survivor’s recovery and ability to identifyand express needs and wishes, as well as to reinforce capacity to make decisionsabout possible interventions. Through this approach, women will be provided withoptions and will have access to information instead of being told what to do. -Theproject will include several activities that focus on GBV prevention such as cash forwork; an intervention that was assessed by women beneficiaries as decreasinghousehold tension . Providing economic opportunities to women also countersexisting trends of negative coping mechanisms emerging from dire lack of resourcessuch as substance abuse and school dropout. Coupling cash for work schemes withGBV services will empower women and girls with disabilities seek solutions out ofabusive environment and become active actors in building their communities. Otheractivities include awareness raising that target women and men (also caregivers ofWGWDs) which aims at addressing root causes and promoting behavioral changesthat can limit GBV against women and girls with disabilities fromoccurring/reoccurring. Some of the interventions such as psychosocial support willaddress risk-taking behavior and conflict-preventive measures. UN Women will alsosupport building the capacity of local actors in the area of GBV prevention and PSEAto ensure ownership and sustainability of the approach. -Given the multisectoralnature of the interventions, UN Women will ensure to coordinate the projectimplementation with the humanitarian clusters namely the Protection Cluster led byOHCHR, the GBV Sub-cluster led by UNFPA, the child protection WG (led by UNICEF),the Disability working group, and the Cash for Work working group led by Actionagainst Hunger to avoid duplication, ensure proper protection mainstreaming,emphasize complementarity and maintain the spirit of delivering as One. UN Womenwill also ensure to team up with different civil society organizations as partners in theimplementation of the project; capitalizing on their different expertise and knowledgeof the affected communities and populations. Civil society partners will be responsiblefor coordinating the implementation of the activities with different community-basedorganizations and civil society coalitions.
