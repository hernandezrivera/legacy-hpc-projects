Project Summary

People with disabilities and older people are the most vulnerable community has identified through different studies and research since the Rohingya influx and significant natural disasters such as fire outbreak, monsoon flash flood, and biological pandemic diphtheria and COVID. Considering their inclusion in a humanitarian setting, Age and Disability Working Group (ADWG) was formed in 2019 under Protection Working Group (PWG). The four founding organizations CBM, HI, HAI and CDD consecutively provided financial and technical support to ADWG to ensure age and disability inclusion throughout the response.
CBM has taken lead in seeking funding from one of the founding member organizations of ADWG to the relevant core HR (ADWG Coordinator) cost along with technical advisory roles within all four core members for the effective coordination and delivery of age and disability inclusion technical support. It also seeks funding for the implementation of activities to steer forward the agenda of age and disability inclusion in the humanitarian coordination system which includes influencing the inclusive humanitarian policy environment in the humanitarian response and providing inputs into Humanitarian Response Plans, Preparedness Plans align with Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU) of Protection Working Group (PWG) and other policy documents and producing good practice documentation and guidance’s for the different sectors, working groups, and government authorities. The project will take into consideration the four principles of protection to ensure the prioritization of safety and dignity of gender-inclusive people with disabilities and older people through providing guidance on universally accessible designs and onsite support for ensuring accessibility and safety of camp shelters and WASH infrastructure.
CBM will also provide capacity development to the protection sector through the ADWG platform on inclusive GBV programming to ensure that all age and gender groups of people and persons with disabilities are able to access protection services with dignity. The project will also seek to raise awareness on the collection of Sex, Age, Disability Diversified Data (SADDD) through Washington Group Questions (WGQs) and on inclusive targeting and registration procedures to ensure that beneficiary targeting does not cause more harm leading to discrimination and further stigmatization of persons with disabilities and older people. To conduct research on barriers that hinder meaningful access and participation of older people and persons at the 9 camps where representation gaps are in place based on the ADWG services matrix. The findings from such activities will be shared by the ADWG at various sectorial as an advocacy tool for ensuring meaningful access and participation, providing recommendations and guidance to mainstream agencies to promote meaningful access for all in line with the Sustainable Development and its Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) - leave no one behind. Considering Accountability to Affected People, the project will ensure guidance on accessible feedback mechanisms as well as ensuring that project information is communicated in accessible formats. Guidelines and tip sheets on the inclusion of older people and persons with disabilities in different sectors will be developed and shared by the ADWG. The project will promote the empowerment of persons with disabilities through providing training on advocacy to the Disability Self Help Groups to advocate for inclusive practices addressing the glaring gaps within all the camps and host communities.
The proposed action intends to improve access to basic and specific needs of people with vulnerabilities including disabilities and the elderly, emergency relief response following any future natural disaster. Considering frequent natural disasters, the project also includes a 5% contingency budget based on a risk compliance contingency plan followed by the CBM crisis

1 - Identified Needs

The project needs have been identified through observation during the implementation of current activities, surveys, and study results conducted by humanitarian agencies in coordination with the Age and Disability Working Group (ADWG). The needs of people with disabilities are insufficiently included in humanitarian action for effective response: Underlying risk factors for people with disabilities including women and children with disabilities are inadequately addressed. Due to its unique geographical location, Cox Bazar and its surrounding Upzillas are very prone to natural disasters (such as flash floods, torrential rains, typhoons, landslides, etc). More recently, the frequency of natural disasters has been increasing significantly with moderate to severe intensity and causing a significant impact on human life and local community development. People with disabilities are not yet systematically included in disaster preparedness efforts. Information on disaster preparedness is often not available in accessible formats, so people with disabilities are less aware and prepared for a disaster. During a disaster situation requiring evacuation, people with disabilities are more likely to be left behind, injured, and even killed, and they may be separated from family or support persons, or their assistive devices may be lost or broken. The ADWG has noted an improvement in the inclusion of persons with disabilities and older people in the Rohingya Response. However, research studies have shown that there are still a lot of gaps in the inclusion of persons with disabilities and older people. Findings from the Age and Disability survey by REACH (My 2021) found that, fifty-two percent (52%) of the Persons with disabilities reportedly faced barriers related to mobility in shelters, and 76% around camps, particularly among older persons with disabilities. Female older persons with disabilities were found to be disproportionately affected by this gap. The majority of the older people and people with disabilities struggle to access essential services and community participation because of stigma, hilly/flood-prone terrain, lack of adapted facilities, and inclusive interventions. Older people and persons with disabilities are facing difficulties in accessing complaint and feedback mechanisms meaningfully. There is insufficient data on gender-based violence towards women with disabilities and older women in the camps. The NGA survey 2021 conducted by HAI further found that 37% of older people face problems buying food from e-shop/market, 94% of the older people currently have no income; 38% cannot reach the distribution point and 25% face problems carrying their food ration. It was also noted that 38% of the older people having disabilities cannot reach static health centers. 85% of the older people reported not having been consulted by the Humanitarian agencies regarding the services they provided. Gaps in rehabilitation services and representation of persons with disabilities-focused organizations were identified in 9 camps (2w,4E,7,10,20,20E,21,23, and 25) based on the ADWG services matrix and Rehab and Assistive Devise services gap mapping of PWG. There is therefore a need for a well-coordinated approach by the ADWG towards the provision of technical support for Age and Disability Inclusion in the Rohingya humanitarian response to meet the identified gaps.The ADWG was established in 2019 with an overall objective to promote an inclusive humanitarian response toward persons with disabilities and older people in the refugee camps and host communities. It is comprised of four technical members; HI, CBM, Centre for Disability in Development (CDD), and HAI.
