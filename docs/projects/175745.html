Project Summary

It is estimated that the total number of people in need of critical health services for 2021 in Fangak, Koch and Rubkona counties is 306,199 (Male:149,942; Female:156,257), out of which 58091 (Male: 29,580; Female: 28,511) are children under 5 years old (U5). An unusually high level of flooding in 2020 and now in 2021 continues to exert pressure on the already fragile health systems in Fangak and Koch counties. With basic social facilities destroyed such as WASH and health and nutrition facilities, the affected population is in critical need of health services. Bentiu Protection of Civilians (PoC) is transitioning to an internally displaced (IDP) camp and there could be possible challenge in accessing quality health service. Malaria is very high in Bentiu PoC during the raining season and health facilities are usually congested which make following social distancing difficult.
The project will target a total of18,283 (Male:8227; Female: 10056) people as direct beneficiaries, particularly women and children and other underserved community members. The health service will be through Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs), Primary Health care Centers (PHCCs) and Mobile health units (MHUs). Services will particularly focus on addressing the top three morbidities of malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), and acute respiratory infection (ARI), as well as prevention of epidemic-prone diseases. Likewise, there will be a focus on maternal and child healthcare, routine immunization, disease surveillance, and infection prevention and control.
The project will deliver health responses to meet health cluster objectives with focus on strengthening local capacity to prevent, detect and respond timely to outbreaks and public health risk events. Where possible, the project will involve integrating of health activities with Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), and Protection sectors to provide holistic lifesaving interventions. Strong referral mechanisms will be enhanced toward the care of vulnerable people with specific needs like SGBV survivors, mental health and SAM children U5 with medical complications. Training for staff will be provided on key health and nutrition topics and other integrated programs. Outreach interventions will be organized to reach hard to access locations from the PHCU sites through MHUs. Activities will be monitored through coordinated strategies jointly with the County Health Directorate (CHD), Ministry of Health (MOH) and UN Agencies. Within WR, activities will be monitored by project staff and the Area Coordinator with support from the Juba office through Supportive Supervisions. Reports will be shared with the Health Cluster and MOH through the DHD. Community based compliant mechanisms will be established within the health facilities and at community level to ensure accountability to the affected population.
To respond to these immediate health needs, this project will ensure scaling up of lifesaving emergency health service to populations in need in each of the target locations.

1 - Needs

Some 2.4 million people are in need to emergency health care services in 2021 in South Sudan. The total number of people in need of health services in Fangak County are estimated to be 67,125 (Male: 35,590; Female: 31,535) out of which 14,090 (Male: 6,639; Female: 7,451) are children U5. In Koch County, 96,257 people (Male: 47,787; Female: 52,470) of which 15,266 (Male: 8,374; Female: 6,892) children U5, need health support. Only 10% of children under 12 months and 11% of children U5 in Fangak County have received DPT3/PENTA3 and measles vaccinations in 2020. In Koch County, 22% of children under 12 months and 32% of children U5 have received DPT3/PENTA3 and measles vaccination in 2020 . IRNA conducted in early February 2021 in Fangak County revealed that an estimated 48,103 individuals are directly affected by flooding of which 15% are believed to be children U5. Families are living with no access to WASH and health services. Flooding continues to exert pressure on already fragile health systems, and with already destroyed basic social facilities, the affected population is in critical need of support. World WR’s facility in Buom was affected by flooding and damaged and was relocated to Noor village where most families have relocated. Other PHCUs affected in Fangak County were Tiep, Wicmoun. The major drivers of crisis in Koch County are unprecedented flooding due to the heavy rainfalls mid last year and continued inter-communal fights causing subsequent loss of livelihoods and properties. An IRNA conducted in August 2020 indicated that an estimated 11,600 individuals are in critical need for assistance as a result of the flooding that affected areas of Ngony, Kuachlual, Gany. Jaak and Baow payams. An ICCG monitoring and assessment mission done in February 2020 indicated that there is an estimated population of up to 762 individuals settled in the area of Leah in Gany Payam do not have any access to a health facility and are in critical need for Health services. The Bentiu PoC is transitioning into an IDP camp and there is anticipation among the IDPs about a deterioration in security which would cause many to relocate back to their homelands. The total number of people in need of health services in Rubkana County are estimated to be 142,818 (Male:70,566; Female: 72,252) out of which 14,090 (Male: 14,567; Female: 14,168) are children U5. Bentiu PoC in Rubkona County has been facing problems of malaria outbreaks for the past four years; last year this problem worsened. Likewise, weak linkages of referral mechanisms and supply chain mechanisms, limited technical capacity of staff and a high need for maternal, new-born and child services are some of limiting factors to accessing quality health services. Poor waste management and the lack of WASH facilities in health facilities and a weak diseases surveillance system, timely reporting and monitoring mechanisms are some of the challenges faced. In 2020, the Bentiu PoC was the site where the first COVID-19 case was reported in South Sudan. However, there is still a big gap in the community: COVID-19 measures are not strictly practiced, lack of face mask for the most vulnerable and community perceptions. With a second wave of COVID-19 infections within the country, there is critical need to intensify Risk Communication and Community Engagement activities.

2 - Project activities

WR plans to address health needs in the targeted areas in the following way:• Risks of epidemics will be identified and controlled at the very lowest level through active disease surveillance and control mechanism in place in the health facilities. Health services will increase awareness on COVID-19 and provide integrated COVID-19 message at community and facility level. PPEs for frontline staff will be provided and 68(39 Male; 29 Male) health and nutrition staff and nutrition volunteers will be trained on epidemic prone diseases and outbreak surveillance. Activities will include malaria prevention and response. Data will be collected using standard forms provided by the MOH and the Health Cluster. 100% of data will be submitted into the IWARS/DHIS for analysis and decision making at the Health cluster level. • This project will provide immunization services through routine vaccination services and immunization campaigns. Basic Emergency Maternal and New born care (BEmONC), including building the capacity of skilled birth attendants to perform deliveries services and new born will be provided. The project will train 23(Male:11; Female: 12) health facility staff on basic BEmONC, clinical management of rape (CMR), post abortion care (PAC) and clean delivery. • Primary healthcare services including life saving and essential health service, communicable and non-communicable diseases and will be providing through the PHCUs, PHCCs and MHU in all locations. WR will procure supplementary drugs for emergency medical support, obtain emergency health and reproductive kits from the core pipeline, and will maintain an uninterrupted pharmaceutical supply chain at all health facilities. • Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) will be mobilized to provide health education at community level on topics related to communicable disease. Health promotion activities will be dependent upon weekly disease epidemiological trends of health centers. CHVs will also be mobilized to do active case finding and early referrals to health facilities. They will conduct awareness campaigns for community leaders and other stakeholders on health education sessions focusing on hygiene and sanitation and handwashing when appropriate. • WR will also intensify the identification and appropriate referral of patients with mental health problems for treatment and psychosocial support, and support 50 survivors of rape with clinical management and referral for psychosocial support and community mobilization and sensitization on SGBV. • Community based feedback and compliant mechanisms will be strengthening in all health facilities and community level to ensure accountability and service satisfaction of the affected population.• WR Project staff, project managers, Area coordinator and program Director will regularly conduct supportive supervision at health facilities and follow up of the implementation of the health services. Support will be provided to field staff from Juba office through capacity building and mentoring. Health officers will be provided with updates on new protocols and guidelines, and will make sure that all those tools are available at the facility level.• Strong referral mechanisms will be enhanced toward the care of vulnerable people with specific needs like SGBV survivors, mental health and SAM children U5 with medical complications. The project will provide CMR service to 50 Survivors of SGBV and will train 23 (Male:11; Female 12) on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR). • Where possible, the project will involve integrating of health activities with Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), and Protection sectors to provide holistic lifesaving interventions.• To respond to the immediate health needs of the community, this project will ensure scaling up of lifesaving emergency health service to populations in need including people living with disability and elderly in each of the target locations.

3 - Project indicators

This Emergency health project will be collecting data with appropriate data collection tool and will be submitting different reports to different reporting stakeholders. Weekly IDSR report will be submitted in to online EWARS system on timely basis. Other monthly progress reports, 4w and 5w report and quarterly report will also be submitted to health sector and other funding agencies. Reports related to national immunization campaigns and survey where WR will be participating and leading will also be shared to responsible actors for analysis and subsequent use. There will also be other specific reports that will be developed and shared depending on the specific activity. The objectives of this project will be aligned with the health cluster objectives. • The first objective of this project will be: To Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities in Koch and Fangak counties by end of 2021. To measure the success of this project, the following indicators will be used: Percentage of children (male and female) aged six months to 15 years who received measles vaccination on completion of a measles vaccination campaign. WR will reach more than 95% children through vaccination campaign in the project area, percent of alerts, rumors and outbreaks will be reported and investigated within 48 hours of notification.• The second objective of this project is: Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems. To measure the success of this project, the following indicators will be used; Percentage of deliveries conducted by skilled birth attendants, number of OPD consultations, number of mentally ill persons receiving MHPSS service through referral, number of health facilities providing SGBV health and MPHSS services, number of Community health Workers assigned per 1000 people, Percentage of less than one year old children who received PENTA3 in each location, number of health facilities providing BEmONC services, percentage of health facilities which have Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) protocols and mechanisms in place, Percentage of functioning health facilities provided with supportive supervision. • The third Objective of this project is: Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly. To measure the success of this project, the following indicators will be used; Number of cases of SGBV survivors who received CMR services at health facilities in one-year period, number of persons with disability receiving health services at facility and during outreach programs, number of facilities that have community feedback mechanism in place.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

World Relief takes Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) seriously at all levels. All staff are trained on PSEA and must sign on to the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. WR will also build the capacity of the health facilities and nutrition sites to treat women and girls who are survivors of rape and other incidents of sexual or gender-based violence. For the community to report any incident of PSEA, WR will establish community-based beneficiary feedback mechanisms and will do consultation with community members and community leaders on how reporting should be done, to whom it should be reported and how the compliant is handled. The community will be clearly informed that any reported case will be kept confidential and will be dealt by responsible individuals at WR. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations is mitigated by ensuring that program teams which consist of combinations of locally hired national staff, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. WR will continue to leverage its relationships to advocate for the safety of beneficiaries regardless of their tribe or political affiliation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

People living with disabilities are often discriminated against in a society. Health care access is especially critical to this demographic, yet they often are not able to seek any support from humanitarian actors because of stigmas. Through health project staff, WR will work to identify program beneficiaries with disabilities early on and help them get the necessary health support. Identified cases will be given priority attention and enrollment into any of the programs to get the appropriate service support needed. People living with disabilities might find it difficult to access health facilities and WR will ensure accessibility to the health facility is fully possible for people with disability. People who are not able to travel and need household visits and support will be identified based on their needs and will be followed-up with accordingly.Vulnerable individuals, including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities are likely to be discriminated against in accessing health services. Distance is a possible barrier to inclusion. The project team will ensure that these individuals are particularly targeted during registration. The project staff will be inducted on protection mainstreaming to ensure that inequalities are minimized, inclusion in project activities is enhanced and all population groups are protected from exploitation and abuse. This will be achieved by ensuring equitable gender inclusion, targeted inclusion of people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups including children and elderly individuals. Community leaders, religious leaders and local leaders will be consulted and advised as to the focus of the program’s intervention related to the inclusion of people with disabilities, and will be requested to provide their full support in WR interventions related to case identification and referral. They will be requested to participate in meetings and group awareness raising sessions. Beneficiaries living with disabilities will also be consulted to give feedback on the service provision and will be part the planning and service improvement process. Using the project staff, project report will disaggregate data of persons living with disabilities and keep record to be shared with relevant actors.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The community level engagement is materialized through conducting regular meeting with the volunteers and community-based committees as well as focus group discussions. WR sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meetings at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plans and opportunities, community concerns, and risks. This feedback is discussed with the management team in the country office and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs. A variety of mechanisms are used to give beneficiaries a choice in how to provide inputs and express concerns. • First, local staff hired at the community level, are trained on how to receive feedback from participants, serving both as staff and as trusted community members. • Second, program management staff at the field level conduct regular Focus Group Discussions with project participants and relevant community members and leaders. To ensure the comfort of project beneficiaries, various discussion sessions are conducted with segregated groups of individuals with particular sex, age, and minority (e.g. non-dominating clan or sub-tribe) identities. • Third, community health workers are able to receive feedback from participants one-on-one, both at health facility level and during home visits in settings where participants may feel safer sharing their comments in the privacy of their own home. This also ensures that elderly, disabled, or other people confined to their homes have the opportunity to submit concerns. • Fourth, beneficiaries are informed of their right to submit complaints during community-level meetings in case they feel more comfortable in a group setting or directly to a WR staff member at a project site.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The various partners and stakeholders, as well as the beneficiaries themselves, will be asked to participate throughout all stages of the process from needs assessments and inputs on project design, beneficiary selection to building the work plan, to scheduling and designing trainings, to monitoring and evaluation exercises. Affected populations will be involved in the project in a few main ways. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community level volunteers contributing to the health objectives. The volunteers will be involved in project design, implementation, and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-based committees, which are comprised of members of both genders, elderly, youth, people with disabilities, and of minority groups where relevant. The community level engagement is materialized through conducting regular meeting with volunteers and community-based committees as well as holding focus group discussions. WR sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meetings at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plans and opportunities, community concerns, and risks. This feedback is discussed with the management team in the country office and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Women and young children particularly are at risk as a result of project activity implementation. Rape and sexual abuse when walking to health facilities is especially of concern. The project team will discuss with the Boma Health Teams (BHT) to ensure and encourage men at each household to escort women and girls in their travel to health facilities to reduce the potential for sexual and gender based violence and other protection concerns. Vulnerable individuals, including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities are likely to be discriminated against in accessing health services. Distance is a possible barrier to inclusion. The project team will ensure that these individuals are particularly targeted during registration. The project will employ one protection officer and two assistants. Their role will be mainly to ensure inequalities are minimized, inclusion in project activities is enhanced and all population groups are protected from exploitation and abuse. This will be achieved by ensuring equitable gender inclusion, targeted inclusion of people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups including children and elderly individuals. The project staff will be inducted on protection mainstreaming in addition to the above. With the recent surge in COVID-19 cases, the project will make sure the response takes into account preventive measures through procurement of PPE for frontline staff, increased community awareness through messaging and distribution of IEC materials, and screening of clients at all service points. Intensifying Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities, including where possible distribution of face mask to vulnerable populations, will be prioritizedSafety of beneficiaries is of paramount importance to project implementation. World Relief has a security focal point who works closely with other protection actions; a security risk analysis will be conducted to ensure safety of staff and beneficiaries. This will be particularity so for distributions which act as pool factors and have a potential of exposing beneficiaries to attacks and injury.
