Project Summary

Cabo Delgado province is experiencing protracted armed conflict since 2017. The conflict has led to government building and civil property destruction including killing, abductions, rapes, and extreme violence against civilian which deteriorated the already existing dire humanitarian needs. Since the onset of the conflict, over 1.3 million internal displacements have been reported in northern Mozambique. As of end of September, 577,545 people remain displaced and 610,732 people returned to their areas of origin. The displacement has led to loss of livelihoods and increased vulnerability among conflict-affected people.
Although humanitarian assistance tends to be directed towards IDP sites and camps, both IDPs in displacement areas and in host community needs protection and humanitarian assistance including access to information to exercise their right. Moreover, it appears that over half of those who are displaced live in host community including 137, 000 in Pemba city according to the 2024 Mozambique Humanitarian Needs Response Plan (HNRP). Unfortunately, this has not necessarily translated to increased access to assistance and protection to the IDPs in host community. These factors have increased the need to develop context-specific operational responses that address the needs of displaced persons and uphold their basic rights, irrespective of the displacement settings, through improving their access to information, protection, services, and meaningful representation.
In the framework of this project, NRC aims to address the protection and humanitarian needs specifically to the IDPs living in host community for both Ancuabe and Metuge. The implementation strategy will adopt CCCM Urban Displaced Out of Camps (UDOC) approach for community support. It will explore how CCCM skills and resources can be applied and adapted to address the needs of displaced populations in non-camps setting. The UDOC approach will be focusing on the establishment and support of coordination platforms, facilitating the inclusion of affected populations (particularly the most vulnerable) in management of the displacement response, and on developing two-way communication mechanisms between the affected population and all other stakeholders.
The overall objective of the project is to improve the living conditions of IDPs out of camps and members of vulnerable host communities. While specific project objectives are the following:
• Support community leadership to engage with local stakeholders to actively take part in area coordination mechanisms.
• Establish and implement community-based feedback and complaint mechanisms.
• Support centralised service provision through coordination with local authorities and partners to overcome services gaps in the targeted areas
To achieve these objectives, NRC will a adopt three components of UDOC approach.
- Area based approach which entails to providing centralized support in a define specific geographical area of project implementation with gaps in community engagement, communication, coordination including community-based approach.
- Stakeholders’ engagement to strengthening local governance and participatory structures for handing over displacement management to the local community and empowering them to engage directly with local authorities.
- Integrated programming seeking to provide multi-sectoral services that addresses a range of (though not necessarily all) needs with consideration of the whole population of the target area (including all affected people regardless of their legal status, risk category, nationality, etc.).
The project will target all the group including children, pregnant women, youth, adult, elderly, PWD, females, male and other genders. The project activities will take please in the pre-selected location, Ancuabe and Metuge main village.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

NRC will implement a context specific UDOC programs by adopting an area-based approach, which will focus on defined geographical area or community rather than a specific sector or a particular target group and attempting to address a multitude of challenges associated within the defined area. The area of intervention is selected from among the most vulnerable areas, where there is a large ratio of displaced persons to host community. The ABA will be oriented for the participation and engagement of the community. This means not only sharing information but also enabling the active participation of local stakeholders (including local authorities, local organisations, and the displaced and host communities themselves). It will adopt a community-based programming by using participatory tools to engage the community at all phases of project implementation. Prior establishment of ABA coordination structure including committees and government infrastructures, NRC will try to work with already existing structure, revitalising them if not longer operational or strengthening if are wreaks. The following steps will be taken: · Consulting with the structures of government, in particular city authorities. · Engaging with effective members of civil society, such as national NGOs and faith-based groups. · Taking time to understand existing dynamics and structures, such as local legislation. These are the local mechanisms which should ensure information provision and communication with communities, as well as coordination of service delivery to the affected populations. The project will engage with multiple stakeholders including municipal and/or district level authorities, departments and institutions, civil society and non-governmental actors, private sector, and academia. NRC will establish the ABA coordination structure and committees and government infrastructures with the ambition to be developed in collaboration with, and handed over to, the community structures to ensure long-term sustainability and build preparedness. 4 ABA coordination structures will be established, 2 for Pemba city and 2 for Metuge and management committees with 50% women representation. The coordination structure and committees will function in accessible space (schools, existing government, or community meeting centres) where the displaced households residing In Pemba and Metuge can easily get access. NRC will boost the structures for potentiality on serving number of different functions including scale and type of emergency/displacement, security and access, local culture, and requirements of the displaced, type of service needed and referral mechanisms that will be done through established community-based complaint mechanisms. It will also provide a safe space for people to access information and provide feedback on services, and for community members to meet with each other and with service providers. The coordination structures will work within and among the affected communities and they will be based within communities. They will play key role in communicating with communities, identifying vulnerable households, supporting coordination, and building the capacity of participatory community structures. Different members will have different skills and roles, but all will be adept in community-based work.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Throughout its programming cycle, NRC actively assesses and addresses protection risks affecting specific vulnerable groups within the broader target population. Among these groups, women and girls are identified as particularly susceptible to the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse during beneficiary selection processes. The absence of adult male household members or the presence of dependent children can further amplify these risks. In Mozambique, women and girls frequently face gender-based discrimination, experiencing marginalization in household and community decision-making and lacking control over their resources and opportunities. Men and boys, on the other hand, may be more vulnerable to recruitment by armed groups. Indeed lack of livelihoods opportunities and economic prospects in Cabo Delgado have led to recruitments of marginalized youth who built high expectations around the exploitation of natural resources and the gas industry, according to the IOM. Additionally, societal expectations, such as the pressure on adult men to provide conspicuous dowries for marriage or to support their entire households, may compel them to take risks that can create protection concerns, such as early marriages. Elderly individuals and persons with disabilities are also at a higher risk of marginalization and exclusion. Due to a combination of socio-cultural stigmatization and physical barriers limiting mobility, persons with disabilities often remain unseen in the community, confined to the domestic sphere. NRC's efforts aim to recognize and mitigate these specific protection risks for these vulnerable groups. Finally, the project is aimed at taking place in urban displacement locations, where displacement affected populations are being hosted by local populations. To this effect, the project will be cognisant of particular vulnerabilities of both displaced but also their hosts, who have seen their often-meagre resources strained due to population influxes. Community structures established will ensure that both populations are adequately represented and put in responsibility roles and will ensure selection of participants is reflective of the area’s demographic profile, to avoid causing harm. Complaints and feedback mechanisms, which will be made available to communities throughout the project life will be a key outlet for these communities to hold NRC accountable.At the beginning of interventions, focus group discussions and key informant interviews will ensure to adopt a gender segregated approach and eventually population status disaggregation to ensure to capture each population fragment’s need, freely expressed.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Equal representation in the different community groups will be ensured through democratic election processes. To guarantee that the groups with special needs, such as women, elderly and persons with disability, are heard and issue are addressed, specific coordination/support groups with that objective. Referrals to relevant Clusters and its partner organizations will actively and systematically be done within the existing coordination structures at Pemba and district level.This intervention’s approach hinges on the participation and empowerment of communities targeted. Community-based assessments will ensure to include all representative elements of the area and will use adaptive methodologies to provide a safe space for communication and identification of needs. Representative community structures will be empowered to speak on behalf of their peers and identify and remedy community challenges that affect the area as a whole and to raise these issues with local authorities. Services delivered will be tailored to the specific needs of the area in questions; needs which will have been ascertained through participative means.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The various community groups shall represent the different groups and their specific needs. Any overarching coordination body shall have representation of these groups so that their voices shall be heard. Any concerns in terms of access or any other barrier shall be addressed through specific support. Depending on the nature of the challenge this can be provided in-kind, cash support or through community engagement and awareness.Community engagement, information campaigns, etc will also be conducted through door-to-door approaches and mobile teams will ensure that any person can be accessed regardless of contextual or physical barriers.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Due to the nature of the proposed activities under this sub-sector, the environmental impact is relatively low. Nevertheless, whenever necessary, NRC will prioritize the use of Cash to give flexibility in the assistance including on the material production for information sessions, campaigns, and awareness. This approach reduces the usage of items such as plastic material. In addition, mitigation measures leading to reduction environmental negative impact will be put in place for all NRC interventions including west management points.
