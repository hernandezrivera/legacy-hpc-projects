Project Summary

Children in Pakistan are the mostly neglected and vulnerable group of the society. According to national census, at least 40% of the total population are under the age of 18 years. Only 34% of children under 5 are registered at birth. About 3.3 million are involved in labor. The situation caused by the Covid-19 has significantly affected the children to access their right to education, health, protection and psychosocial development. According to a United Nations report published in June 2020 it is stated that the Covid-19 pandemic was likely to push more than 120 million South Asian children in poverty within the next few months. A part from it concerns regarding the increasing violence against children during the pandemic have also been reported, where children were being subjected to physical torture by financially and mentally depressed adults. According to another survey ‘Cruel Numbers 2019’ an annual report published by the Society for the Protection of children rights, 08 child abuse cases were reported every day in Pakistan during the year.
Two of the proposed 3 districts i.e. Dadu and Larkana have been ranked on ‘severity’ of o4 in the severity map under Protection sector of the Pakistan 2021 humanitarian Sector Plan. It is well known fact that due to displacement/relocation of families caused by any reason such as pandemic or floods the young children especially girls become more at risk of violence, abuse and exploitation. Sindh Government through the legislative cover i.e. Sindh Child Protection Authority Act 2011, has already defined the child protection measures, but unfortunately due to lack of awareness and resources at local level those measures are not fully implemented. It is therefore, measures to prevent abuse, exploitation and violence against children need to be strengthened in coordinated manner to provide safe access to the protective services.
The proposed project will directly contribute to the both sectoral objectives of the Protection sector with specific reference to the sectoral activities number 11, 12 and 16. The project will provide immediate protection services in proposed three districts to 10500 children.
IRC’s senior management staff member, who is trained in child protection in emergencies (CPiE) will directly oversee the project. The project will conduct a rapid assessment to select the locations for setting up 30 static PLaCES (Protective Learning and Community Engagement Services) and 30 outreach centers in program districts. In this regards, 60 child facilitators and Child Protection Monitors will be hired. The PLaCES shall register the children and women and will engage with them to provide them psychosocial services, recreational, learning and sports activities in center. The PLaCES will be directly linked with district based child protection mechanism including the social services provided by the state departments i.e. Pakistan Baitul Mal, NADRA and other such departments. One Child Protection Officer in each district shall be hired for engagement with authorities, oversee the implementation and technical input to teams in PLaCES. S/he will be responsible for 30 centers in each district. Parallel to that the Child Protection officer shall make efforts to strengthen the coordination system at district level, by facilitating the meetings of districts child protection committee and district child rights monitoring committee. IRC has already presence in project district so it has an advantage of enjoying good work relationship with the district authorities pertaining to child protection. The media campaign shall be launched through local cable TV message, social media and FM radio to disseminate the child protection messages. From the first day of the project social welfare department / Sindh child protection authority staff at local level will be engaged and on completion of the project the services will be handed over to them for continuity.

1- Project Activities

The overall purpose of the activities is to support the Community-based mechanisms are established in support of a strengthened protective environment for children and women in project areas through enhanced coordination and information management of child protection services. .Following activities shall be carried out in 03 proposed project districts. o Establish and operationalize 30 PLaCES and 30 outreach services/locations for providing critical protection and social services to children and women, including children with disabilities.o Conduct 3-day training for project staff on key components of project, delivery of services in PLaCES and managing outreach services, reporting and responding to protection issues, application of SOPs on unaccompanied and separated children and related standards and principles in child protection response.o Organize sports week/ 03 events in the community for delivering key messages on child protection, peace, child marriages and social cohesion.o Establish/ strengthen Child Protection Committees in project area by arranging broad community meetings with engagement of Social welfare departmento Organize cluster-based training events (02 Day each) of CPC members on identification of missing and unaccompanied children and referral of cases, and girls/boys who are at risk of violence, exploitation & neglect.o Disseminate information on Child Protection, GBV, Reproductive health, immunization, breastfeeding & nutrition for women participating in activities in PLaCES thorough posters, local cable TV, FM radio and social media.

2 - Project Indicators

o 60 number of static PLaCES and outreach services established in project areas to provide case management and referral services for 10500 of children and women. o Number of enrolled children (60% girls) actively participating in sports and recreational activities at PLaCES.o Number of staff including: child facilitators and monitors for PLaCES (at least 50% female), and other community volunteers having knowledge and skills on the provision of appropriate psychosocial support, taking guidance from IASC standards. o Number of events organized in the community for delivering key messages on child protection, including child marriages and promoting social cohesion.o Number of Child Protection Committees (CPCs) formed and are actively engaged in identifying cases of missing, unaccompanied girls and boys and girls and boys who are at risk or are victims of violence, exploitation and neglect.o Number of CPC members (at least 50% females) have received training on identification and referral of cases of separated or unaccompanied children and cases of violence, exploitation and neglect.o At least 50% of population of project areas has received information on child protection including GBV through media.
