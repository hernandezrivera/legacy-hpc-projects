Project Summary

Objective 1: Provision of protection training to primary school and health care Staff and teachers in recognizing and responding to protection incidents and concerns and becoming community focal points (CFP) for referral mechanism and basic principles of inclusion.
Objective 2: Strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors/service providers, to assess, prevent, mitigate and respond to needs of persons with disabilities, elderly and people with chronic illness.
Objective 3: Provision of capacity building and support to disabled people community based organizations (CSOs) on improvement of their referral mechanism and basic principles of inclusion
Objective 4: Enhance capacity of community members to sustain self-protection through awareness on existing community-based protection mechanisms, awareness on conflict resolution/mediation and peaceful co-existence messaging through focus group discussions and sensitization campaigns.
Beneficiary profile and numbers: Children, 1920, 1920 caregivers of the children, 72 teachers/school staff, 100 DPO/CSOs staff, 100 MoH staff, 72 aid agencies staff, 26,000 community members with and without disability.

Needs

Reliable data on persons with disabilities in Somalia is limited but the available information is critical to ensuring exclusion and the danger of physical, institutional and attitudinal exclusion of persons with from political, social and economic life are eliminated. It is likely that persons with disabilities in Somalia add up to as much as 20 per cent or more of the population , (701,636 individuals in Somaliland ). While progress has been made in the way frameworks and policies address the issue of inclusion and mainstream protection, stakeholders continue to face difficulties to translate those policies into action. To ensure no one is left behind and ensures safe and equal access to assistance for the most vulnerable, States, donors and humanitarian actors must increase their ability to provide an inclusive and mainstreamed humanitarian emergency response. In Somalia, persons with disabilities, elderly and people with chronic illness are currently facing significant obstacles in timely and meaningful access to basic and lifesaving humanitarian assistance offered by many humanitarian actors for many reasons such as such limited skills and capacities of humanitarian actors in understanding their basic and specific needs, person with disabilities and mobility restriction remain invisible or confined to their home/camp due to their functional/activity limitation and unmet rehabilitation needs etc.

Activity 1:2: Formulation and signing of collaboration MoU’s health, schools and NGO administrations.Activity 1:1: participatory assessment baseline barriers/facilitators (institutional, environmental and attitudinal barriers) in emergency response.Activity 1:2: Develop capacity building and sensitization action plan together with the stakeholders.Activity 1:3: Design a service mapping for the existing services, with a specific focus on protection, education, health (mental health, psychosocial) and social service providers.Activity 1:4: Provision of 4-day training on the basic concepts of protection, (service mapping, case identification, referrals, advocacy, protection law, UN SGBV guidelines, feedback mechanisms etc..Activity 1:5: Design, production and distribution of IEC materials in the supported schools, health facilities and humanitarian agencies. Activity 1:6: Promote construction and rehabilitation of public facilities (playground by building ramps, wheelchair accessible doors, contrast marking of the steps and wall edges, or levelling off the ground) for easy and safe access to health and education environments to persons with and without disabilities.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed project is designed to move towards a community based risk management approach in delivering inclusive protection support services rather than direct intervention. The aim is to increase community resilience, and to ensure the continuation of the project’s impact after the project ends. Training on protection referral mechanism and creation of the community focal points amongst them is one of the elements that will ensure the longer-term impact of the project. Furthermore the minor disability-inclusive and safe playgrounds rehabilitation work planned in the project will last after the project ends and are aligned with the MoH and MoE strategy for schools and health facilities rehabilitation. The creation of socio-recreational will aim promoting long-term cohesion and self-confidence. Through this project, HI seeks to reaffirms the central role that the community themselves, and in particular women, can play in conflict prevention and in the political and socio-economic reconstruction of their society.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Field experience and observations indicate that persons with functional limitations, including persons with disabilities and injuries, older people and other most vulnerable people are often at risks of, Lack of effective participation/discrimination, neglect sexual, emotionally or psychologically abuse, resulting from physical environment that is not accessible, lack of relevant assistive technology (assistive, adaptive, and rehabilitative devices) and negative attitudes of people towards disability . Solutions, 1) capacity building, training, mentoring, coaching and sensitization of all stakeholders on disability 1) in areas with recurrent displacement, collect data on the number of persons with disabilities, and type of disability, alongside age and gender data plus any special needs they may have, 2) prioritize persons with disabilities in reunification efforts, particularly those dependent on caregivers, 3) Ensure disability questions are included in rapid assessments and target households with disabled people during interviews, focus group discussions or other assessment activities, 4) to improve access promote inclusive accessibility by construction and rehabilitation of public facilities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Persons with disabilities are extremely vulnerable to protection violations ranging from physical, sexual and emotional abuse to lack of access to justice systems and documentation. Children with disabilities are three to four times more likely to be physically or emotionally abused. Solutions include, 1) Target male and female persons with disabilities of all ages in all protection monitoring initiatives, 2) Assist persons with disabilities with communication difficulties to complete relevant forms and help all persons with disabilities to replace lost documentation and access justice and other systems, 3) Provide all information in simple language, using at least two means of communication (oral and written) and ensuring it reaches people who cannot leave their shelters/temporary homes. 4) Include persons with disabilities, persons with injuries and caregivers in psychosocial initiatives.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

a) Protection assessments and improve the quality of assessments, integrating protection risks self-identified by persons with disabilities, older people and other most vulnerable people and their communities in order to improve its analysis. b) Operationalise inclusion legal frameworks via creating awareness, training, coaching and mentoring of PWDs, teachers, health officials and community. c) Promoting awareness and capacity building training on needs and inclusion, communities need increased access to information on available services as well as access to referrals to services in order to best care for their needs. d) The project will pursue protection by presence and the strengthening of humanitarian, health and education actors to become community focal points (CFP) for referral mechanism and basic principles of inclusive service delivery and e) Rehabilitation and/or construction of accessible public facilities.
