Project Summary

Since September 2017, Mdm shifted its operational procedure to transit from direct implementation of services to support the existing health facilities. The main objective is to insure accessibility and availability of basic services to the community.
The modality aims to help the existing health system rebuild its capacities and to respond to the needs of the community.
This approach gives more sustainable solutions to respond to the needs of the community and insures the recovery of the system and the services.
The project will address, throughout close collaboration with health authorities (MoH, DoH, districts) accessibility, availability and quality of healthcare services at primary level, including integration of GBV and MHPSS in Kirkuk and Ninewa Governorates with possible extension to central Iraq (Anbar Governorate). The health facilities will be selected based on criteria agreed with the health authorities, with a minimum of one PHCC per district. The support with medicines will focus to avoid shortage of the essential medicines for the treatment of common diseases including the supply of the Center of chronic diseases.

Needs

Since decades, Iraq is suffering from political instability, economic sanctions and violent conflicts. The health system has suffered from neglect, migration of health workforce, disrupt of the supply chain, physical damage of hospitals and health facilities. (Ref:Laith R S: . Human Resources Issues in Iraq’s Health System: A Proposed Reform Plan. JOJ Pub Health. 2017).Iraq is suffering from a double burden of diseases: In addition to a high prevalence of infectious diseases (diarrheal diseases including cholera, acute respiratory infections, typhoid, TB), the country has also a high prevalence of non-communicable diseases (Diabetes, Hypertension, cardiovascular diseases). Accidents, cardiovascular disease, and strokes are the leading causes of mortality in Iraq. The IS crisis of 2014 has severity affected the Health System. affected areas has been dramatically disrupted by the crisis:• Hundreds of health facilities in Anbar, Ninewa, Salah al Din and Diyala have been damaged or destroyed. • The health facilities that are fully or partially functioning are overstretched• The displaced populations have placed a heavy burden on existing health infrastructure in the areas hosting the displaced. • Thousands of health professionals have been displaced• Supplies of essential medicines and medical supplies have been disrupted.The main concern regarding the access to health is the lack and the inadequacy of health services: too expensive (private), of poor quality (public), and overcrowded (public). Actually, in Iraq, there are seven million individuals in need to healthcare assistance. Medicine shortages are a major concern in many public health facilities, particularly for treating chronic diseases. Due to the displacement and to conflicts, the needs to MHPSS are relatively high in Iraq. Based on MdM activities in 2018, respiratory tract infections, and non-communicable diseases (diabetes, hypertension and cardiovascular diseases) are the main causes of morbidity.

Activities

Activity 1.1: Support the existing health facilities in the targetted areas to restore its capacity to provide quality basic primary healthcare serices, including reproductive health, mental health and psychosocial support.Activity 1.2: Provide trainings to the health providers in supported facilities and at district level: on-the-job coaching and supportive supervision. Training on pririty topics in collaboration with the DoH. Activity 1.3 : 1 Visit per week per health facility to monitor provision of basic services. Activity1.4: provision and distribution (including monitoring) of essential medicines, basic equipment and consumables. Activity 1.5 : Build and strengthen partnerships with health authorities at the central and local level through supportive supervision : Joint monitoring visits(1 per 2 months)Activity 1.6 : Support the disease surveillance system: EWARN reporting Activity 1.7: Health and Hygiene Promotion: Awreness raising on hygienic practices, MHPSS, SRH and GBV

Protection Risk Analysis

Tensions between IDPs, returnees and host com-munities for access to services.Tensions between levels of health authorities may result in political driven decisions taken at central level which do not respect the needs of population, IDPs, returnees.Difficult coordination among actors involved in the provision of services, e.g referrals.

Mitigation Measures

Clear and solid communication and information on the project non-discriminatory approach, reinforcement of health workforce on humanitarian values and non-discriminatory intervention principles.Build institutional partnership with the authorities at different levels: Central level- Governorate level and district level.Increase coordination with all actors on the ground: INGO, LNGO, authorities and communities.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The project engages with several actors from community to national level, along with Primary Health Care centers and Ministry of Health / Directorates of Health. The capacity strengthening is to focus on Health Facilities and Health District Authorities level which involves National Authorities to some extent. Capacity strengthening is one of the core component of activities, to ensure ownership and sustainability, while responding to immediate needs of conflict-affected populations and ensuring quality of healthcare services provided.During this project, MdM will support the local system to resptore its capacity to respond to the needs of the community in the affected areas. Will engage the community to adopt healthy behavior and environment. At the end of the project, MdM will ensure that the health facility has enough resources to provide quality services, accepted by the staff, the community and the authority (Health facility score > 60).
