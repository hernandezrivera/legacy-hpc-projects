Project Summary

CRS proposes to support 22,22 vulnerable ind. in most-affected communities in Ayod and Akobo Counties and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) through the drilling of 18 new and rehabilitation of 42 existing communal boreholes, utilizing flood-resistant designs, to serve 22,222 vulnerable individuals. 50 water point user committees (WPUC) and 10 pump mechanic groups will be trained to manage and maintain the water schemes. The project will also construct water troughs at all water facilities to provide access to drinking water for small ruminant animals, reducing the required amount of time and distance travelled to access water. The project will also integrate these WASH activities with other livelihood interventions to promote multiple water uses. By engaging vegetable producers to grow crops and fruit seedlings near water facilities, enhancing the food and nutrition security of affected communities. These activities will complement and amplify the impact of planned BHA and WFP funded programming in Ayod, Akobo, and Pibor, including resilience building, livelihood, food security and nutrition activities. CRS has active field bases and extensive experience in all of these counties and GPAA. Increased access to clean water for personal and domestic use, will enhance the uptake and sustain the use of promoted hygiene and sanitation best practices, contributing to improved nutrition outcomes in the target communities.

1. Needs

South Sudan has experienced three civil wars and decades of multi-level, violent conflict between the government and its opposition as well as sub-national anti-government insurgencies, inter-communal conflict over resources, and rivalries among ethnic groups. This violence, paired with recurring climatic shocks, has traumatized and displaced populations; disrupted agricultural production and adaptive capacity; eroded trade, markets, and basic services; exacerbated food and nutrition insecurity; and increased household dependency on humanitarian aid. Recurring climatic events including drought, flood, livestock disease, and crop pests have also negatively affected production. The target counties are recognized as intersectional priority level 1 per the WASH Cluster targeting guidance based on FSNMS. These counties are all likely to experience IPC 4 (food security emergency) or worse outcomes in lean season 2024. (Per IPC relaease 6 Nov 2023).The target counties are historically under-developed, isolated, and seriously affected by insecurity and intercommunal conflict. The routine revenge killings and cattle raiding displaces communities, forcing them to concentrate in smaller locations with limited access to clean water and power, hygienic sanitation facilities, etc. Climatic shocks such as unseasonal rains and dry spells as well as crop and animal diseases and pests have reduced food availability. This has contributed to predicted Critical (IPC AMN 4) acute malnutrition situations in these counties throughout 2024 (IPC release, 6 Nov 2023). Most communities have limited access to clean water, relying on seasonal streams, unprotected wells, and water ponds for their WASH needs. The number of protected water schemes available are in short supply due to poor surface and groundwater source potentials. In addition, these protected water schemes are often overburdened, nonfunctional, or contaminated. The high reliance on unimproved, open water sources compounds the risk of waterborne and diarrheal diseases in the region. The low access to quantity and quality water also contributes to low rates of hand-washing and other hygiene practices, impacting the health and nutrition of many, especially children. The high unmet community needs for access to clean water has significantly contributed to the emergency conditions in these counties.

2. Project activities

CRS proposes to support 22,222 vulnerable ind. in most-affected communities in Ayod, Akobo, and Pibor Counties through the drilling of 18 new and rehabilitation of 42 existing communal boreholes. 50 water point user committees (WPUC) and 10 pump mechanic groups will be trained to manage and maintain the water points. CRS will also construct water troughs at all water facilities to provide access to drinking water for small ruminant animals, reducing the required amount of time and distance travelled to access water. The project will also integrate these WASH activities with other livelihood interventions to promote multiple water uses. Engaging vegetable producers to grow crops and fruit seedlings near water facilities will enhance food and nutrition security. Improved water supply will complement and amplify the impact of CRS’ planned BHA and WFP - funded programming in Ayod, Akobo, and Pibor, which includes resilience building, livelihood, food security and nutrition activities. Increased access to clean water for personal and domestic use, will enhance the uptake and sustain the use of promoted hygiene and sanitation best practices, contributing to improved nutrition outcomes in the target communities. While the geophysical and hydrological characteristics of the terrain makes borehole drilling a complicated process, CRS South Sudan has its own drilling rigs and experienced drilling crew to meet the task. Prior to the drilling, geographical survey will be conducted to assess potential water source locations and maximize success rates in securing productive boreholes. Water quality tests will be conducted at each site to ensure safety for human consumption and use. As needed, CRS will install surface water treatment (SWAT) plans.CRS will do hygiene promotion using Wash'Em and distribute standard hygiene kits and MHM supplies to most vulnerable HHs, prioritizing women and girls. CRS will conduct regular safety audits at all water points. CRS has worked in greater Jonglei for over 10 years, meeting a range of humanitarian needs, inc. food assistance, livelihoods, nutrition, health, social cohesion, and conflict mitigation. CRS has years of extensive experience implementing comprehensive WASH programming in these counties. This project will complement recent and ongoing BHA-funded resilience, food security, and nutrition programming. CRS will coordinate with local actors, WASH and Nutrition Clusters, State and County authorities, and other implementing partners. The proposed project will integrate WASH interventions with planned food security and nutrition activities to help enhance acceptance and adoption of promoted sanitation and hygiene practices, reducing the rate of malnutrition due to waterborne diseases. In addition, layering the WASH interventions with ongoing and planned gender, livestock, and social cohesion programming will facilitate greater impact. Participation of women in WPUCs as committee members, pump mechanics, and caretakers will improve agency, empower them to take on leadership roles and participate in community decision making, and improve access to information and income. The project will apply a conflict sensitive, gender-responsive “Do No Harm” approach, considering the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, girls, and PWD. CRS has strong acceptance, and a stellar reputation with local communities and stakeholders in these areas, and will leverage our local experience, reach, and expertise in this challenging context. CRS has local and intl staff expert in emergency WASH implementation, and maintains robust systems to ensure accountability and program quality. CRS' established supply chain and logistics capacity, including warehouses at field locations across these counties, to purchase and deliver required supplies and equipment during the intended time frame. CRS will ensure staff, office space, and vehicles are shared across programs to maximize cost effectiveness.
