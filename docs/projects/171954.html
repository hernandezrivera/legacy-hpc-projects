Project Summary

Launched in March 2016, the NMS has proven to be an effective tool to monitor the situation of the IDPs. The NMS reports are used by a wide range of state and non-state actors in Ukraine to develop strategies and plan activities with real-time data about the most pressing needs of IDPs and host communities in Ukraine.
Building upon the experience gathered during the past 18 rounds of data collection, IOM will continue gathering, analysing and sharing information on socioeconomic characteristics of IDPs across the country, including, amongst others displacement trends and movements, return intentions, employment, financial situation and basic needs, access to social services, preferred durable solutions and assistance needs. In close consultation with the Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine (MoSP), the Ministry of Reintegration of the Temporarily Occupied Territories of Ukraine (MRTOT) and government and non-government humanitarian and development actors, IOM will work to include new key indicators that will inform the programming of government and international actors, taking into consideration all three dimensions of the Humanitarian, Development, Peace Nexus (HDPN). This will be aimed at creating a solid background for more sustainable, durable and context-sensitive around the principles of doing no harm (DNH) and accountability to affected populations (AAP).
The proposed project will also contribute to strengthening the capacity of MoSP, MRTOT and government and non-government humanitarian and development actors to conduct assessments on IDPs in a harmonized and systematized manner.
IOM intends to further localize the NMS agenda by piloting the introduction of critical elements of community ownership for data collection in priority areas. Community ownership will be encouraged through the engagement of youth and local authorities in the data collection and validation process.
This process will serve to build the capacities of local stakeholders to better identify the needs of their communities to enhance emergency and recovery responses. To promote data sharing and foster harmonization of the data and analysis collected within NMS, IOM will work closely with Ukraine’s MRTOT to further align these activities with those of the Government of Ukraine and other relevant humanitarian and development stakeholders through the existing coordination system. This coordination will allow to support the assessment of progress against the ‘State Target Program for Recovery and Peacebuilding in the Eastern Regions of Ukraine (STP)’ and the ‘Strategy of Integration of Internally Displaced Persons and Implementation of Long-Term Solutions to Internal Displacement until 2020 (IDP Strategy)’ approved in December 2017 by the Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine to provide an overarching national policy framework for peacebuilding, recovery and IDP integration.
Additionally, IOM will conduct a pilot IDP profiling exercise with the aim to establish the boundaries of credible IDPs’ numbers in target locations to improve humanitarian targeting and PIN calculation for planning of the response. IOM will also contribute to data sharing and coordination by organizing and hosting a series of initiatives including main humanitarian and statistical stakeholders to review methodologies and findings and share experiences.

1 - Needs

According to the Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine, more than 1.4 million people are registered as internally displaced persons (IDPs). Half of the registered IDPs permanently resides in the GCA in Donetsk and Lugansk oblasts. In line with the latest NMS Survey findings, 80% IDPs have been staying at their current place of residence for over three years thus the internal displacement can be considered protracted. The situation of protracted displacement requires actions of the humanitarian actors to prevent the negative effect of the conflict, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic. The provision of humanitarian assistance is highly dependent on trends and patterns of human mobility. Humanitarian actors require information on the locations and compositions of the affected population in order to deliver services and respond to needs in a timely manner.The monitoring of displacement requires a flexible information management system and adaptable methodologies with the capacity to regularly provide adequate information to facilitate the delivery of services and target responses in line with needs in a timely manner. IOM will continue using the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), a global IOM tool designed to regularly capture, process and disseminate information to provide a better understanding of the evolving movements and locations, numbers, vulnerabilities and needs of the displaced populations.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: Humanitarian stakeholders plan their assistance programming based on strong field evidence. Output 1.1: Enhanced data on the socioeconomic characteristics of IDP and returnee communities in Ukraine are available to government and humanitarian actors. Activities under Output 1.1: • Update and tailor NMS survey questionnaires in coordination with government authorities, humanitarian actors and in line the strategic focus of the NMS round;• Carry out sex and age disaggregated data collection, including but not limited to telephone survey, focus group discussions and face to face interviews. Data gather will take place on a quarterly or semesterly basis; • Combine data obtained from multiple sources, namely: administrative data; data from focus group discussions and key informant interviews; data of sample surveys of IDP households and local population; telephone interviews with IDPs (numbers are from National IDPs database provided by the MoSP); and available relevant data from other sources;• Analyze the data collected, including disaggregation by gender and age parameters and produce semesterly reports;• Present the key findings of the NMS round and disseminate the report to relevant stakeholders. Outcome 2: Community ownership over the data collection process, analysis and validation of NMS data is increased Output 2.1: Youth in target locations are trained to become field enumerators and monitors. Activities under Output 2.1:• Coordination with target communities and selection of youth to be capacitated;• Conduct trainings for selected youth on data collection, conflict-sensitivity, PSEA, communication techniques and MHPS first aid• Deployment of trained youth for data collection on thematic and/or rapid assessments Output 2.2: Trained youth collaborate with local governments to validate data Activities under Output 2.2: • Organization of data validation sessions (FGDs) based on the interaction between trained youth and local authorities;• Organize workshops to discuss ways of possible future collaboration between trained youth enumeragors and local government counterparts. Outcome 3: Humanitarian and development actors have a realistic understanding of the proportionsof IDPs and returnee communities. Output 3.1: A new profiling of IDP population in selected locations is conducted Activities under Output 3.1: • Develop a methodology to conduct IDP profiling;• Conduct a pilot baseline assessment in selected communities• Conduct Key Informant Interviews with relevant local stakeholders for the validation of data • Draft a report on the approach adopted for IDP profiling and present the findings• Organize coordination and consultation meetings among humanitarian and statistical stakeholder to foster data alignment
