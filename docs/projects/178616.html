Project Summary

• All the targeted sub-districts fall under severity scores 4-6 based on the HNO 2020 severity master sheet with education sector specific and severity scores of 3-4 based on HNO 2021 Education Sector PiN severity.
• The cost per beneficiary is $52.
• Beneficiaries with disabilities is estimated at 15%.
• Total funding that ZOA received in 2020 was $4,439,160 of which $1,331,000 was SHF funding.
• Total secured funding for the year 2021 is $3,888,000
• The total number of beneficiaries that ZOA reached for all sectors was 35,081. In 2020 ZOA did not implement major education projects.
ZOA has been able to secure about 40% of the proposed budget through institutional fundraising and private donors.
10 years of conflict have led to the fracturing and under-resourcing of the education system, consequently limiting the ability of children to access education. COVID-19 has added another layer of complexity, further acerbating education constraints. This project is aimed at increasing access to quality education for the most vulnerable children affected by conflict in target locations. Proposed activities are rehabilitation of schools/classrooms to increase access to protected, safe and friendly learning spaces and to ease overcrowding and encourage smooth return to schools; improvement and rehabilitation of gender-sensitive WASH facilities, provision of education supplies for both students and teachers; provision of school furniture, school bags and teaching/learning materials; teacher training and capacity building; and PSS activities together with the provision of COVID-19 PPE. Proposed target locations are underserved areas in the governorates of Damascus, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, and Quneitra.
Through the project ZOA is going to target 19,245 (without double counting) beneficiaries, taking the special needs of the PWDs into consideration in its activities. In the event of limited funding, ZOA will prioritise areas falling under higher severity scales. The project targets 5 different schools in 4 different governorates.
The core beneficiaries of the project are 5-17 school age children and teachers, who will benefit from the main project activities. While parents will benefit from awareness sessions. Apart, Parent Teacher Association will be established per school and 1 parent engagement initiative will be supported per school/community.
The project is in line with the Education Sector’s objectives since it is focusing on the Sector Objective 1 “Availability & Access - Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth and Objective 2 “Quality - Enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth.” Also, the project aims at responding to HRP 2021 Specific Objective 3.1: “Improved access to basic services, electricity, water, education and health.”

4 - Needs

After ten years of crisis, sufficient availability and access to continuous protective quality education remains a critical need in Syria. An estimated 6.9m people (97% children) need humanitarian education assistance. The sector is not able to provide a 2021 estimate of out of school children, however, in 2020, an estimated 2.45m children were out of school and 1.6m children were at risk of dropping out. The number of children in need of education support in Syria has reached catastrophic levels due to protracted crisis, displacements, destruction of schools, closure of learning facilities due to COVID-19 and economic instability (HNO 2021). The capacity building of the teachers and school personnel are also great needs in providing quality education (HNO 2021). Students with disabilities need to have access into schools, therefore rehabilitation is needed in this aspect. More over, educators and school staff need to have proper training for inclusive approaches, for sufficient knowledge and skills to provide suitable assistance (HNO 2021). Infrastructure survey (Nov. 2020) findings indicate that if the total number of 6-17 years old children is divided by the total number functional classrooms, it is estimated that there is 1 classroom per 53 children. Overcrowding is a great concern as it compounds the challenge of providing a conducive learning environment to mixed-aged, mixed-ability students, many of whom may have socio-emotional needs. It also limits the ability of schools to enact COVID-19 mitigation measures (Education Sector Findings 2021 HNO). ZOA has discussed priority needs with the Ministry of Education. Activities proposed are based on needs assessments and ZOA’s technical capacity.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Children continue to be exposed to multiple protection risks and severe rights violations as an impact of the crisis. New and protracted displacement, exposure to violence, increasing poverty and an inadequate access to services- even the most basic necessities, heavily effect the wellbeing of children. Specific vulnerabilities and protection risks for children include domestic and family violence, sexual violence and harassment, forced/ early marriage, child labour including in its most dangerous and hazardous forms such as recruitment, begging and heavy industrial work. Disruption of traditional safety nets, lack of livelihood opportunities and sudden displacement have aggravated protection concerns and led to violence, neglect, exploitation and abuse resulting in negatively impacting the psychological wellbeing of children and their families. Children are also facing limited care and support from parents and experiencing separation from their primary caregivers. Additional risks are: 1) Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls and boys (and their caregivers). Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1. Provision of specialised education services; 2. Provision of trainings. 2) Risk to physical safety of education service providers if perceived to be interfering in family-based concerns. Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1. ZOA/partners will continue involvement of coordination with relevant authorities and school administration; 2. ZOA/partners will train and coach educators and staff to ensure that communication with the family is professional and not confrontational.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risks of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services; Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: 1. ZOA/partners will provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA; trainings for all locations are planned to take place at the beginning of the projects; 2. ZOA/partners will communicate regularly and clearly to the Education Directorates and schools administrations about what services are available and for whom they are intended; 3. ZOA/partners will reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE. 4. ZOA/partners have beneficiary feedback mechanisms, an important component of AAP. It is possible to alert ZOA about abuse via the internet, hotline, social media and complaints boxes.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Risk of renewed conflict in areas where we plan interventions, especially in Aleppo and Quneitra; Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. ZOA/partners will monitor the political / security situation to be able to adjust operationally to this risk. 2. ZOA/partners would adjust operational plans for the locations in question. R2: Risk of access and/or delay of approvals by related ministries, resulting in a delay of the start of operations Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. ZOA/partners will prioritise implementation through local partners; 2. selecting partners with good relationships with the authorities in selected areas; 3 increase coordination and plan ahead of time. R3: Risk of not responding to the real gaps. Likelihood: Low. Impact: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Include the beneficiaries during design of project; 2 FGDs with different age groups. R4: Risk of fraud and corruption. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Clear communication with target populations on their entitlements using multiple channels; 2. ZOA has a clear conflict of interest policy in its code of conduct; 3. Procurement, Logistics, Fraud and Corruption Policies in place for the mitigation of fraud and corruption risks; 4. All staff are trained on policies that address fraud, corruption and abuse; 5. All financial transactions are documented and filed; 6. Regular finance spot-checks and unannounced audits are carried out. R5: Risk of exchange rate instability. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Continuous monitoring of the markets; 2. Transfer funds into Syrian banks according to the needs. 3. Hold maximum possible amounts in Syrian banks in hard currency.
