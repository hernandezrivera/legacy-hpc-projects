Project Summary

This project is aiming to provide the required support to the landmine and Explosive Ordnance (EO) affected people in Sudan through raising the awareness about the threat of landmines and explosive ordnance, release land previously contaminated or suspected to be contaminated with landmines/EOs; opening roads/routes contaminated or suspected to be contaminated with EO; and provide the required support to the humanitarian actors to deliver the aid to the affected populations. The project also aims to support the government of Sudan to meet its obligations under the Ottawa Treaty (landmines ban treaty). The government of Sudan is obliged to meet its commitment under article 5 of Ottawa treaty by April 2023. Also, this project is aiming to support the landmine/EO survivors to reintegrate socially, physically, and economically to their communities.
UNMAS in cooperation with the mine action implementing partners will release 8 million square meters of land contaminated or suspected to be contaminated with EO and open about 100 kilometers of roads and routes for productive use. About 350,000 people will receive risk education awareness through direct sessions or indirect sessions such as radio broadcasting or community campaigns. Also 200 people from the community leaders and humanitarian service providers will receive safety training and risk education.
The project also will provide the required training and courses to enhance the capacity of 200 people from the national implementing partners including personnel from NMAC and national NGOs.
The project also targets 205 landmine/EO survivors or victims through different interventions to reintegrate them to their communities.

1 - Needs

A number of long-lasting conflicts has led to the contamination with anti-personnel and anti-tank mines as well as explosive remnants of war (ERW). According to the HNO severity analysis, 47 localities were reported to be impacted by landmines/ERWs, out of which 42 are ranked as being highly, very highly, or severely impacted. South Kordofan maintains the highest contamination records of landmine and ERW, followed by Blue Nile and West Kordofan, while Darfur states are affected by ERWs only. Mine contamination remains one of the key concerns with an estimated 2.7 million men, women and children at risk of injury or death from mines. IDPs, returnees and refugees are at most risk due to the limited information/awareness about local landmine or ERW contamination within their settled areas. Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) remains insufficient to the scale of needs, building resilience and adapting safe behavior is essential for communities’ safety and wellbeing. Mine contamination also limits access to food and livelihoods as people cannot fully engage in agricultural and animal grazing activities. It also puts people at risk when fetching water or collecting firewood. According to IMSMA there are about 30 square kilometers of land in 232 locations across Sudan confirmed to be contaminated by landmines/ERWs, additionally, it is estimated that thousands of kilometers of roads suspected to be contaminated with landmines/ERWs blocking access to services, socioeconomic activities, or delivery of humanitarian services however the actual level of contamination is not yet fully known due to accessibility and movement restriction. Hence the presence of landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) not only poses a direct threat of killing and injuring the local population but it also limits access of the Humanitarian Aid reaching the vulnerable communities, its effect is further hampering early recovery and development efforts.The recent involvement in peace talks and the political situation in Sudan has opened further humanitarian access to the areas that were previously not accessible. SPLM-N Malik Agar controlled Ullu and Ingasana of Blue Nile is now fully accessible as per the Juba Peace Agreement while the SPLM-N Al Hilu controlled parts of South Kordofan and Blue Nile allow limited access to the aid actors; in the UNMAS mission to Kauda it identified heavy air-dropped bomb contamination within the center of the territory and mines contamination in the areas in the vicinity of the frontlines. As more humanitarian activities are expected in this area, urgent assessment and clearance of roads/routes in opening humanitarian corridors is required. Furthermore, 2,196 casualties were registered as a result of landmines or ERW victims, including 1,573 people who have been injured and 623 fatalities. Children and women accounted for approximately 84% of the known victims of ERW’s and UXO’s. The highest number of victims were registered in South Kordofan state, followed by South Darfur and Blue Nile.As of UNAMID's drawdown by end of 2020, UNMAS is extending its mandate to cover the entire Darfur; despite the ERW response by UNAMID, the areas under SLA-AW control have only recently become accessible, requiring urgent ERW assessment and clearance. Sudan has a commitment to comply with the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention (APMBC) under Article 5 to clear all known landmines by April 2023 where GoS remains committed to meeting this deadline through their National Mine Action Center (NMAC). With the provisioning of technical assistance and fund mine action activities, UNMAS is required to further strengthen/enhance mine action coordination and national technical capacity of NMAC and national mine action operators in Sudan towards Sudan free of Mines and ERW.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1- Provisioning of Explosive Ordinance Risk Education among 350,000 people at risk to facilitate the adoption of risk-avoiding behaviors among the vulnerable population including refugees, IDPs, and returnees. This will be achieved through conducting mine risk education (MRE) and awareness raising including direct sessions and community-based risk education CBRE; printing MRE material for distribution; dissemination of MRE messages through radio broadcasting to reach communities in restricted and remote areas.Provision of mine risk education to populations living in proximity to the threat of explosives and humanitarian workers will help reduce risk of death and injury from hazardous landmines and unexploded ordnance as a result of acquiring necessary knowledge and behavioral changes. 2- Release Land for productive use through land release operation to contribute to the protection of local communities by ensuring improved access to basic services; restoring livelihood capacities as well as safe access of the humanitarian actors to enable the delivery of life-saving aid. Release lands previously contaminated or suspected to be contaminated with the explosive ordnance for productive use through the following activities which will contribute to release about 8 million square meters of land and open 100 kilometers of roads: Survey activities to Identify and record areas with landmine and ERW contamination through non-technical and technical survey (NTS & TS); clearance of landmines and ERW from lands that confirmed to be contaminated with landmines and ERWs; conduct verification and/or clearance of routes suspected to be contaminated by landmines and ERW; removal of landmines and ERW through EOD spot tasks.3- Improve the life conditions of 205 people from the explosive accidents survivors and promote their socio-economic reintegration and positive perceptions within the community; equal participation of landmine/ERW accidents survivors in the communities will be achieved through provision of medical care, prosthetics and assistive devices, psychological counseling, and income generation kits as well as the collection of data about the landmine/ERW survivors and registration of these data into the mine action systems such as IMSMA or any other government systems; the promotion and advocacy with the governmental entities for rights of PWD.4- Provision of technical and financial support to the national mine action center (NMAC) to enhance their capacity to manage a prioritized principled and efficient mine action programme. This will be achieved through provision of advisory to the NMAC through UNMAS professional personnel; conducting the required training and courses to about 200 people from the national authorities including the NMAC and the partner’s NGOs; and providing the required financial support to allow the NMAC to do its technical and managerial roles.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNMAS will ensure that mine action activities are carried out in compliance with the National Technical Standards and Guidelines (NTSG) and International Mine Action Standards (IMAS) for what concerns environmental preservation. UNMAS and NMAC will identify and assess relevant environmental aspects and determine appropriate and effective measures to mitigate adverse impacts of explosive detonations on soil, air and immediate surrounding environment. This will include the inclusion of environmental requirements in the selection of implementing partners and their incorporation in SoP, proposals, and quality assurance monitoring plans.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Mine action is basically a protection intervention aiming to protect people lives from the threat of landmines and explosive remnants of war, MA sub sector ensures equal participation, consultation and inclusion of affected populations in the planning and implementation of mine action programmes as well as ensuring that the different exposure to and knowledge of risks related to explosive ordnance among women, girls, boys and men from diverse groups is incorporated into risk reduction efforts, including clearance and risk education. It will also facilitate and support multi-sectoral assistance to victims of explosive ordnance that is responsive to the needs of women, girls, men and boys from diverse groups, and advocate for the mainstreaming of gender and diversity considerations in national mine action policies, institutions and programmes. Additionally, mine action implementation partners are encouraged to ensure they “do no harm” to the environment or livelihoods, and avoid increasing the long-term vulnerability of affected communities. Therefore, this project considers protection mainstreaming through all phases of the project starting from the project concept throughout project design, implementation and closure of the project.
