Project Summary

UNESCO has been providing support to the influx of refugees in Cox’s Bazar since 2018.
As part of the JRP 2022, UNESCO proposes to accelerate non-formal vocational education of youth (aged 19-24) from the camp and the host community in Cox’s Bazar, providing them with alternative learning pathways. Proposed interventions will create educational, social and economic opportunities for marginalized youth and their family members, with a specific focus on women and youth with disabilities, allowing them to respond to economic, cultural, and social challenges which have increased due to the COVID-19 pandemic and recent natural disasters.
Specifically, UNESCO proposes to educate 3,000 youth (50 per cent female) aged 19-24 from the camp and host community through non-formal vocational education in local market demanded areas that enables them to contribute to their self-dependency, support their family members, reduces their vulnerability to risks and increases protection, helps them identify their career path, and contributes to the sustainable development of their communities.
Specifically, UNESCO proposes to provide 1,500 marginalized youth (50 per cent female) from the camp community of camp 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 5, 8E, 8W, 20, 20-extension, 21, 22, 24 in the Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila (managed by UNICEF and UNHCR) with non-formal vocational educational classes on repairing solar power, plumbing, tailoring/sewing, and English language training through a comprehensive programme (40 hours) will be delivered in Burmese and English in the Rohingya camps and Bangla and English in host community. Career guidance will be provided during and following vocational educational classes which will help learners to find suitable internship, apprenticeship, and job opportunities, identify a career path, and enable them to access guidance regarding their rights and remuneration.
For the host community, UNESCO proposes non-formal vocational education classes (40 hours) for 1,500 learners (50 per cent female) on food processing, hotel hospitality including English-language training, housekeeping, and mobile phone repairing which will be delivered in Bangla.
The learning materials (online and offline) for non-formal vocational education will be developed/contextualized in consultation with the Skills Working Group, Youth Working Group, and the Gender Hub in Cox’s Bazar.
Existing learning facilities operated by UNICEF, UNHCR, and other agencies will be leveraged to carry out in-person classes and training of trainers as appropriate. As part of the non-formal vocational education, each learner will be provided with a certificate and subject-based toolbox that enables her/him to practice their acquired vocational education.
Responding to the significant levels of stress and trauma experienced by marginalized youth from the camp and host community caused by poverty and distress that youth live in, UNESCO proposes to include elements of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) training in its non-formal vocational education classes to provide learners with the help that they need and deserve.
A showcasing event, organized in Q3/2022, will bring together the different education stakeholders and private sector entities, showcasing good practices and results of the non-formal vocational education programme and acquired skills by learners. In parallel, a career fair will be organized which provides graduates with internship, apprenticeship, and job opportunities in the local labour market.
In coordination with the District Bureau of Non-formal Education as well as camp authorities, UNESCO proposes to carry out training through blended approaches (in-person and online) leveraging a training of trainer approach that engages local NGOs (e.g. SKUS, MUKTI, PRANTIK, ACLAB, BRAC). Relevant and updated education materials will be developed, contextualized, translated, made accessible and digitalized (online/offline) for each course.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar is among the lowest-performing districts of Bangladesh in terms of education and skills training. Cox’s Bazar is historically lagging behind both in the economic performance as well as the human development index. Already before the pandemic, about 33 per cent and 17 per cent of the population live below the poverty and extreme poverty lines, respectively. Humanitarian actors are providing the populations of Cox’s Bazar with a wide range of humanitarian support and services, however, it is estimated that only four in about a 100 refugee adolescents and youth have access to any form of life-skills and/or vocational education (Oxfam, 2019). The Rohingya population consists of a large working age population with 42 per cent adult, 55 per cent children and only 3 per cent elderly. Almost three-quarters of this population has never been to any formalized education institution as they self-reported to have either received only informal education (48.65%) or have not received any education at all (23.53%)(Food Security Cluster, 2020). Insufficient access to education combined with the lack of income-generating opportunities renders many dependent on aid, making them highly vulnerable to exploitation, especially children and youth. Since the start of the pandemic in 2020, the Rohingya and host communities have been experiencing the impacts of the containment measures and the contraction of the local economy as a result of successive nationwide lockdowns. The localized impact of COVID-19 in Cox’s Bazar pushed millions of people back into extreme poverty in 2020. The few selected opportunities for vocational education and learning for marginalized youth of the host and camp community have been significantly impacted by the pandemic. Alternative online platforms for non-formal vocational education are extremely limited and often outdated. Whilst the macroeconomy in Cox’s Bazar has started to gradually recover, the micro economic level is still faced with the impacts of the COVID-19 shocks, especially for the most vulnerable low-income communities and refugees, who have been hit hardest by the crisis. Local labour markets face skill shortages in the recovering economy particularly in the areas of food processing, tourism, tailoring and electronics-related businesses, services and occupations. The low performance is even more evident when considering gender parity and women empowerment. There is significant discrepancy in terms of gender parity in labour market and educational attainment. Women account for only one third of the youth employed in Cox’s Bazar, which falls to only one fifth in the adult age group. To a large extend women are disproportionately engaged in low productivity agricultural sector and low productivity occupations leading to low earnings. Already before their closure due to the COVID-19 outbreak in March 2020, significantly lower proportions of children with disabilities than children without disabilities were found to have been enrolled in formal and informal learning centres. Youth with disabilities face additional challenges that compound their marginal status as they generally do not have access to vocational education opportunities and are often isolated from their peers which reduces their social network and opportunity to participate in society and the labour force.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1. Developing, contextualizing, translating, and digitalizing (online/offline) accessible learning materials for 8 non-formal vocation education courses for camp youth on repairing solar power, plumbing, tailoring/sewing, and English language training, and for host community youth on food processing, hotel hospitality including English-language training, housekeeping, and mobile phone repairing all of which are providing alternative learning pathways for the Rohingya refugee and host community youth. Activity 2. Course-based and pedagogical training of 8 master trainer (4 female) and 24 trainers (12 camp and 12 host, 50 per cent female) on developed non-formal vocational education courses (alternative learning pathways). Activity 3. Conduct of in-depth non-formal vocational education and provision of toolboxes (basic equipment to carry out vocation, e.g. sewing machine and relevant accessories for tailoring students) to 3,000 marginalized youth from diverse backgrounds (50 per cent female), including 90 youth with disabilities, from host and camp community. Activity 4. Establishment of online (WhatsApp/Facebook/Google) and offline communities of practice (CoP) to allow for exchange among benefiting youth within the area of non-formal vocational education. Activity 5. Conduct 6 learning-sharing workshops (full day; 3 in camp, 3 in host) among learners and trainers to which all benefiting youth from the respective community will join and share their experiences. Activity 6. Conduct of 1 advocacy workshop showcasing good practices of non-formal vocational education (successful alternative learning pathways) to sector stakeholders, as well as in parallel, a career fair to provide graduates with internship, apprenticeship, and job opportunities in the local labour market. Activity 7. Monitoring and evaluation of programme activities and their effectiveness through a monitoring team as well as through a reporting and feedback sharing mechanism that allows for the anonymous submission of feedback to UNESCO. Representatives (female and male) from benefiting youth group will also be included in the monitoring and evaluation team.
