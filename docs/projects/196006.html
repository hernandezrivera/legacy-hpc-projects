Project Summary

To increase Rohingya refugees and host communities equitable access to and utilization of comprehensive and high-quality sexual and reproductive health services (SRH) that save lives UNFPA will continue to strengthen its implementation of integrated SRH and GBV services for women and girls of reproductive age through health systems strengthening and community engagement in 22 health facilities across 11 camps and surrounding host communities. UNFPA will strengthen it's coordination and partnership within a multi-sectoral approach that includes strategic collaboration with the Gender based violence [GBV] Sub-Sector, Community health workers [CHW] Working Group, and government actors, under the direction of the Health Sector and key players. The operationalization and implementation of the recently approved, government-led Cox's Bazar Family Planning Strategic Plan (2022-2025) will be a major focus of UNFPA's strategic, technical, and programmatic guidance pertaining to the provision and delivery of a continuum of care package for essential SRHR services to the last mile. UNFPA will guarantee the continued availability and accessibility of family planning and other SRH-related commodities, supplies, and equipment through enhanced supply chain management in order to satisfy the unmet needs of refugees and surrounding host communities. The project will centralize facilities and community-based family planning, improve and maintain the quality of services through strengthening referrals and transfer of high risk and emergency obstetric cases, availability and accessibility of life-saving SRHR commodities, client-centered quality and respectful midwifery-led antenatal and postnatal care, safe birth, menstrual health, cervical cancer and obstetric fistula screening and treatment, adolescent SRHR services, Maternal and Perinatal Mortality Surveillance and Response, and Evidence based SRHR Information Management and quality assurance through mentorship and support to service providers. The capacity of health practitioners and community health workers to deliver integrated, client-centered, high-quality services, such as family planning and health response to GBV in health facilities and protection spaces such as WFSs, WLCCs, and Youth Centers, will be enhanced. Additionally, these integrated services will reinforce and expand existing behavioral modification strategies.
Through capacity building, stockpiling of supplies, and the design of SRHR policies, UNFPA will enhance its preparedness to fulfill SRHR demands during natural disasters and disease outbreaks. To ensure continuity of comprehensive integrated SRHR service provision, UNFPA will continue its preventative and response interventions, including the operationalization of Isolation Treatment Centers [ITC] and provision of integrated SRHR/GBV services in health facilities, communities, and women, girls, adolescent and youths protected spaces.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

According to population projections for 2023, there are around 1.44 million persons in need of humanitarian aid in the Cox's Bazar area, of which an estimated 352,971 (Rohingya refugees 233,455: Host communities 119,516) are women of reproductive age (WRA) in need of SRH services. However, SRHR services are not accessible 24/7 at all facilities, and there is inadequate capacity to offer emergency obstetric care to ensure safe births.Maternal, stillbirths, and neonatal deaths, accounted for 23% of the EWARS reported mortality, 17% of women received at least 4 antenatal care visits by time of delivery according to a Health Sector Bulletin (Sep 2022) report. Approximately 70% of births now occur in health care facilities, a number that has increased over the last years. To address the gaps and maintain advances, it is imperative to strengthen the health system and provide assistance to all SRHR partners in order to assure the delivery of high quality services. In addition, in light of the SRHR morbidity associated with cervical cancer and obstetric fistulas, there is an urgent need to scale up midwifery-led, enhanced cervical cancer and obstetric fistula screening and treatment throughout refugee and host communities, including targeting older women.Access to family planning is still a major challenge due to barriers at facility and community levels. Addressing of these barriers including provision of community based family planning services and health education, training of service providers and provision of family planning commodities will be strengthened through implementation of the Cox's Bazar Family Planning Strategy (2022-2025), which has been approved by the government in October 2022. In addition, access to SRH services that are adolescent youth-friendly and sensitive in current health facilities is limited, therefore the capacity of health service providers to provide these services will be strengthened at community and health facility levels. In the spirit of leaving no one behind, it is crucial to improve initiatives to meet the SRHR requirements of additional vulnerable populations, such as persons living with disabilities.UNFPA is devoted to facilitating access to high-quality health care services that are tailored to the most pressing needs. Access to secondary care and referral links is essential, and partners must have available resources for medical referrals. In the framework of the COVID-19 pandemic, the Health Sector plan considers a base-case scenario, as stated in the WHO Strategic Preparedness Plan (2022), taking into account all available data and the characteristics of the COVID-19 virus. In this scenario, UNFPA will reduce COVID-19-specific resources and incorporate them into the broader health care infrastructure. UNFPA is focused on tackling cross-sectoral concerns (protection, gender, diversity) with shared mandates, in addition to ensuring the availability of basic health services. In addition, UNFPA will guarantee minimum readiness, advanced preparedness, and contingency actions for all common risks that have the potential to have adverse effects on public health. This will entail enhancing the capacity for maternal and perinatal mortality surveillance and response, as well as stockpiling essential medical commodities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNFPA project takes an integrated approach to provide the delivery of comprehensive and integrated SRHR-GBV and adolescent and youth-friendly sexual and reproductive health care to refugees and surrounding host communities. UNFPA will also strengthen the quality of SRH services across all health facilities and partners as per the health sector rationalization recommendation. As the coordinating agency for the SRH working group, UNFPA will coordinate and collaborate with all health partners, stakeholders, other sectors, and Cox's Bazar-level government authorities. In addition, UNFPA and all of its implementing partners will maintain a well-coordinated and amicable relationship with the government, including camp-level CiCs, and will work with other partners and stakeholders, engaging in all camp-level coordination platforms. As UNFPA continues to receive multiyear financing from some of its donors, implementing partners will be assisted to maintain continuity, predictability, and sustainability of services.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

UNFPA, as a lead agency in working to respond to and reduce gender-based violence, continuously analyzes the different needs, roles, relationships, and experiences of women, girls, men and boys in the assessment, planning, implementation and review of the assistance (including protection). UNFPA believes that the promotion of gender equality in programming not only increases the effectiveness of humanitarian action but also upholds our moral and legal obligations to protect the dignity and rights of all people including PWD to ensure dignified impartial access by operating according to the principle of non-discrimination and bolstering our mandates to serve the most vulnerable individuals who are at risk of being left behind.

3.2 - Gender

UNFPA will ensure Gender mainstreaming and integration in all its humanitarian programming in the following ways: Ensure that the specific needs, capacities and priorities of women, girls, men, and boys are identified and that comprehensive and integrated SRH/GBV services help persons and groups most in need; Informing women, girls, men and boys of their entitlements and available resources and engaging their participation and supporting women’s leadership in programme design. UNFPA will ensure its commitment of reaching the most vulnerable populations by enhancing the safety and dignity of the targeted populations. Along with providing key information and awareness sessions, a timely health response to survivors of sexual violence (CMR) including updated GBV referral pathway, the programmes will support the beneficiaries to understand and identify the referral services related to GBV, SRHR, and MHPSS available in the community to enable them to seek inclusive and non-biased services.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

UNFPA will ensure its commitment of reaching the most vulnerable population by enhancing the safety and dignity of the targeted populations including children and adolescent girls and boys as well as persons living with disabilities and older persons. UNFPA as a GBV and SRHR agency works to protect persons, including young people, from all forms of violence, abuse, and exploitation including gender based violence and harmful practices including child marriage. Along with providing key information and awareness sessions, the programmes will also support the beneficiaries to understand and identify the referral services related to SRHR, AYSRH, GBV and MHPSS available in the community. As a result, this will enable them to seek services when needed. In accordance with that, UNFPA will ensure the inclusion of all age and gender groups to make the programme inclusive and non-biased.

3.4 - Diversity

UNFPA services target all persons in need without discrimination including women of reproductive age, adolescent girls and boys, adult men and women as well as key populations such as sex workers and survivors of gender based violence. UNFPA will continue reinforcing partnerships with the organizations working with the Gender Diverse Populations (GDP) and capacity development on SRH/GBV, AYSRH and PSEA. The ethnic, key populations and religious minorities will continue to be in special focus and be involved in the SRH services delivery and access.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNFPA along with its implementing partners (IP) ensure a people centered approach to achieve better health outcomes and embrace our collective commitment toward the accountability to affected populations (AAP) framework as it ensures the Rohingya, and host communities provide invaluable influence, meaningful participation and decision making in the design, implementation and monitoring of program and services. UNFPA will ensure that the people's voices are listened to especially through the regular community engagement strategies including conducting courtyard sessions targeting different members of the community like men, adolescent boys, adolescent girls and women. Client Satisfaction Surveys and suggestion boxes will be in place to solicit, hear, and act upon the voices and priorities of affected people and establish the level of client satisfaction with the quality of services being delivered at UNFPA supported facilities including health facilities and WFS.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. As the coordinators of the SRH WG on behalf of the health sector, UNFPA aligns to the health sector emergency and response plan which brings together all the health sector and SRH working group partners with collective efforts to enhance the preparedness and response readiness actions to minimize the direct and indirect impacts of natural hazards including cyclones and monsoons. Through the SRH working group, UNFPA will ensure that emergency response teams have midwives to respond to SRH/GBV related emergencies, there is adequate SRH contingency stock through prepositioning of emergency reproductive health kits and commodities, and training of emergency response teams on the minimum initial services package for reproductive health in emergencies. In addition, SRH partners will be encouraged to ensure the reinforcement of the SRH infrastructure to withstand the negative weather effects and other disasters like cyclones, given the increasing impacts of climate change on the country.
