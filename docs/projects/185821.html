Project Summary

This project presents an innovative approach aimed at increasing the resilience of host communities and refugees, which 1) in-creases the adaptive capacity of host communities and refugees by supporting the adoption of climate resilient livelihood solutions and the by strengthening local market systems, 2) enhances the absorptive capacity of host communities and refuges by providing cash support and in-creasing awareness of the target groups and 3) fosters the transformative capacity of the target groups through community restoration and awareness raising activities. The objective of the project is to increase resilience of the host communities and Rohingya refugees, in Cox’s Bazar, by improving food security, reducing disaster vulnerability and increasing social cohesion.

1 - Identified Needs

Helvetas and Concern (Alliance 2015 partner organisation) surveyed over 600 households of refugees and host communities in the district. The decreasing trend of poverty incidence before the pandemic is expected to be temporarily reversed because the pandemic threatens the liveli-hood of many people. 43% of those interviewed said they were eating less now, when compared to the period before COVID-19. 51% stated that COVID had negatively impacted the financial situation of the household, with virtually all respondents (94.5%) saying their ability to earn an income had been affected by COVID-19. When data is disaggregated by gender it is possible to verify a gender imbalanced impact. Female respondents were more likely to say their household was eating less than male respondents were (48.1% compared to 38.2%). There was an even more striking difference between men and women in terms of their response on quality of the food ingested with 60.6% of women saying it had gotten worse compared to 41.5% of men. Those living in the camps were also more likely to give a negative response on quantity and quality than those living in the rural (host) communities. The emotional strain of COVID-19 is affecting everybody. Nonetheless, those living in the camp settings were more likely to identify impacts on their personal wellbeing than those living in rural (host) communities, the study shows that 79% of respondents stated they were worried (81% in camps) and 55% stated trouble sleeping (58% in camps). 34% of respondents stated that community arguing increased with COVID-19 (value identical for respondents in camps and host communities) and respondents identified the elderly (72%), women (67%) and refugees (67%) as the groups affected the most by the pandemic. The project will make sure that beneficiaries and stakeholders receive information on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) along with existing complaints and feedback and response mechanism (CFRM) to ensure that beneficiaries and stakeholders can provide feedback or raise concerns or complaints on project implementation. All complaints will be treated confidentially by a designated CFRM Committee and addressed in a timely and appropriate manner, in order to close the feedback loop, and mitigate further damage related to potential risks. In the project at least 50-70% beneficiaries will be selected as women to increase the women empowerment & involvement in the homestead gardening. The project will calculate the male & female ratio within the beneficiaries list and beneficiaries training receive master role during the project monitoring & analysis the project beneficiaries’ data.
