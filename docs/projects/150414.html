Project Summary

The intervention will target HHs, primary schools and rural clinics in unserved communities in Area C to contribute at the improvement of the water quality consumed and hygiene practices. Water filters at the point-of-us and water-saving hand-washing stations will be installed, and good hygiene/sanitation promoted in institutions and HHs, especially those practicing open defecation. Furthermore, the project aims to complement the interventions that other organizations foresees to implement. Capacity building of local authorities and institutions will be built, in order to be more effective and coordinated. Data will be analyzed and shared in a systematic way, with the development of scientific papers and evidence-based advocacy material

Needs

Access to adequate sanitation and safe household / personal hygiene is a concern in communities facing water shortages in the West Bank. Despite the foreseen realization of the “Technical Master Plan” in Area C elaborated by PWA, AAH and GVC in coordination with UNICEF, 76 communities in Area C or some parts of them will still remain unserved and not connected to a piped water network. In addition, the number of communities awaiting to be connected to the network has been reducing, but still not negligible. For their water supply, the HHs in these communities mainly relies on rainwater harvesting (runoff) or/and trucked water from filling. A systematic water quality surveillance Area C communities in Area C in April – September 2018 proved that – regardless water source and quality at the point of delivery – water for human consumption is very often contaminated when consumed in a HH. Indeed, it is affected by cross contamination during collection and storage (presence of TC in 99% and EC in 50% of samples), likely related to both equipment/assets used and their cleanness. Additional contamination is present also inside the HH before consumption when a household treatment takes place (presence of TC in 89% and EC in 25% of samples). The latter is due to poor hygiene practices, and exacerbated by inadequate hand-washing, inappropriate cleaning and storage of cups and dishes, and poor attention to protect water quality. The study excluded that water treatment was not effective (treatment effectiveness always assessed and only few cases of residual contamination registered). Consumption of biologically contaminated water may have relevant health impacts, especially on vulnerable groups. Children under the age of 5 are particularly vulnerable to waterborne diseases, as well as pregnant women and breast feeding women who could expose fetus to impair growth, or contribute to stunting. Approximately 75% of families in the area of intervention (estimate based on data collected in sample communities) have children under the aged of 5. Various primary schools and clinics in Area C are unconnected and have access water that does not meet safe drinking standards, and lack of proper hand washing facilities or hygiene education increasing their risk to public health diseases. These facilities are relevant not only due to consumption of potentially contaminated water by vulnerable groups, but also as meeting points and strategic places to promote awareness and better practices.In September-October 2018, 175 households were assessed in Area C of Hebron governorate. The assessment confirmed that Open Defecation was a common practice. Indeed, 14.2% of people had no latrines at all, while 64.1% very basic latrines, often not properly used. 69.3% of the people reported not to have possibility to hand-washing in the toilet, confirming a hygienic risk for public health. It is assumed that the practice of Open Defecation is common for 10% of beneficiaries – entailing approximately 340 individuals/45 HHs, who do not have/will not have connection to the piped water network and are located in isolated rural areas, an assumption confirmed by WASH Cluster partners active in this location.Finally, currently water treatment experiences at the HH are quite limited and not well documented, due to some failures in the past and a certain skepticism related to O&M and acceptability. Data in this regard are few, methods for analysis and information management in use different and inconsistent, and sharing limited.

Activities

Activities are designed and will be undertaken in coordination with WASH cluster members and relevant strategic documents. Output 1 Water quality for human consumption and hygienic/sanitation conditions are improved at the HH levelA1.1 Baseline assessmentThe assessment will evaluate access to water,sanitation and hygiene practices in HHs in unconnected communities in Area C, to identify those at risk of open defecation and poor hygiene. A1.2 Installation of HWTS and training in 550 HHsHousehold Water Treatment and Safe Storage (HWTS) unit with Gravity Driven Membrane (GDM) technology minimizes risk of cross contamination before consumption. GDM filtration is a physical and biological treatment, needs very limited operation and no consumables nor chemicals. It has successfully been tested for years in Africa,and introduced in Area C in 2018 with very promising results. A1.3 Collection and analysis of water samples in HHs coveredWater samples will be regularly collected from raw water, treated water and at the point-of-use (at the cup), and analyzed to assess water quality (biological and physical parameters). A1.4 Promotion of hygiene and sanitation practices with kit distribution, especially for HHs practicing open defecation.Promotional activities with behavior change approach (RANAS) will be carried out in a culturally appropriate manner to systematically analyze and tackle open defecation and practices contaminating water. Specific sessions – with hygienic kits distribution – will target mothers and children (5 18Y). Output 2 Water quality for human consumption and hygiene are improved at the institutional levelA2.1 Baseline assessmentPrimary schools and clinics in the area of intervention will be assessed, with a special focus dedicated to availability of water treatment and appropriate hand-washing stations. A2.2 Installation of HWTS and training in 10 schools/clinicsHWTS units for institutions – designed according to the number of users – will be installed in identified locations. Personnel will be trained for basic O&M. A2.3 Installation of HWS with water consumption minimized,training and hygiene promotion in 10 schools/clinicsHand-Washing Stations(HWS)with water treatment and recirculation will be installed in identified locations. Personnel will be trained for basic O&M. Users–both children and patients–will receive dedicated hygiene promotion sessions, tailored on various user groups. Output 3 Capacity of authorities and institutions in water quality treatment and surveillance, and data management and analysis is enhancedA3.1 Capacity building about methods for water quality analysis, and design of a surveillance systemField visits with authorities and WASH cluster members will be organized. 3 workshop about theoretical and strategic aspects of water quality surveillance for rural and isolated communities will be organized–1 for Jordan valley,1 for the South area, and 1 at the national level–and 1 in-service lab training about analysis will be organized in coordination with PWA and the WASH cluster. A3.2 Capacity building and experience sharing in the WASH cluster about water treatment1 workshop to share experience and relevant findings–at both national and international level–of water treatment technologies for unconnected and unserved communities will be organized in coordination with the WASH cluster. A3.3 Open Data sharing for evidence-based advocacy and capacity building about innovative data management and sharing optionsData in open access with data disaggregation will be published, allowing evidence-based advocacy by other WASH cluster members.1 workshop about open data,PROs and CONs, options available, and applicability for WASH in oPt will be organized in coordination with PWA and the WASH cluster.Data are collected with a methodology developed with international experts, checked and triangulated.An independent data verification process will ensure the reliability of the findings.
