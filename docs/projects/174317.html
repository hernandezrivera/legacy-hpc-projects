Project Summary

OVERALL OBJECTIVE:
Strengthen access and improve inclusive, quality and equitable basic education for 616,187 conflict-affected displaced, returnees, host communities, school-aged girls and boys aged 3 to 17 years and an additional 15,000 youth in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states of North-East Nigeria.
SPECIFIC OBJECTIVE:
1. Strengthen basic education for 616,187 children (ensuring 50% girls, 130,460 children who are 3-5 years old and 20,000 children from Tsangaya and Islamiyah schools ) through the provision of essential teaching, recreational materials, support to enrolment drive campaigns (EDC) and Girls for Girls (G4G) programme.
2. Improve the capacity of 12,324 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support, education in emergencies, inclusive education, quality Early Childhood Development (ECD), classroom management and pedagogy (including Teaching at the Right Level/TaRL to improve learning outcomes).
3. Increase access to safe learning spaces with gender-sensitive WASH facilities and water for the benefit of 438,300 children.
4. Provide access to learning, training and skills development for 15,000 youth (50% females; aged 15-24)
5. Enhance the response and protection capacity of education service providers in school governance in emergencies through establishing and/or activating Monitoring of School-based Management Committees (SBMC) in targeted LGAs and building the capacity of 822 SBMC (40% females; 4,110 members) on SBMC effectiveness and provision of education services to out-of-school children, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and children with disabilities with a focus on hard-to-reach areas.

1 - Needs

In 2020, according to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), 8.7 million people in Nigeria (5.1 million children) need humanitarian due to the complex crisis entering its eleventh year. 1.7million people are internally displaced, predominantly in the north-eastern states of Borno, Yobe, Adamawa, and new displacement continue due to ongoing insecurity. More than 59 per cent of those affected are children, including many unaccompanied minors or children orphaned by the crisis. More than 1.2 million children need urgent education services. Institutional and human capacities are overstretched to respond to the educational needs of children and youth in Northeast Nigeria. An estimated 19,000 teachers are displaced, and 645 teachers have been killed since 2009. Approximately 1,200 schools have been damaged or destroyed or are used for military purposes. In the Northeast, education facilities are often explicitly targeted by non-state armed groups in Nigeria. The deliberate targeting, burning and looting of schools began in 2009, with some schools repeatedly attacked over the years. Human Rights Watch reports that in some cases, students recruited by non-state armed groups attacked their school and kill their classmates and teachers. From December 2013 to June 2015, the UN Secretary-General reported that all schools in the north-eastern states were closed. Currently, in Borno, 824 out of 1,359 government schools remain closed. More than 5,202,000 children are out-of-school in Northeast Nigeria of whom more than 1,400,000 children are out-of-school in Borno state. In Borno, there are 27 LGAs, out of which four are inaccessible. Among the 23 accessible LGAs, many wards and communities are inaccessible as well. The Nigeria Education Data Survey (NEDS) 2015 shows the scope of damage caused by decades of development neglect combined with the recent education crisis in Northeast Nigeria. The conflict further weakened an already weak education system and has pushed more children out of school, which has increased children’s exposure to violence, psychosocial stress, economic exploitation, recruitment by armed groups, and early marriage. The effective provision of equitable, inclusive and quality education can be a game-changer turning years of underdevelopment, poverty, violence and radicalisation into self-empowerment and a brighter socio-economic future. To increase access to education to 616,187 children (52% girls), UNICEF will construct/rehabilitate 1,833 learning spaces. As part of the support to inclusiveness, UNICEF will construct and/or rehabilitate 1,461 gender‑sensitive WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces benefiting a minimum of 438,300 children (with an average of 300 children per school/TLS). Expand upon the radio-learning program for increasing access to education for 200,000 children, and this will supplement the reduction of class-time learning resulting from shift-operated classes due to COVID-19. To improve knowledge and skills of 12,324 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support delivery, education in emergencies, inclusive education, quality ECD, classroom management and pedagogy (including TaRL to improve learning outcomes). To increase access to education for out-of-school children, UNICEF will support the provision of educational opportunities for 20,000 Tsangaya and Islamiyah children in partnership with State Ministry of Religious Affairs (SMoRA) and Borno Islamiyah School Association (BOSISA). To enhance the response and protection capacity of education service providers in school governance in emergencies and effectiveness, at least 822 School Management Committees (SBMC) (4,110 SBMC members) will be trained for improved capacity in education-in-crisis, education for vulnerable children including girls, and the role of community in providing education services to out of school children.

2 - Activities

In 2020, UNICEF will focus on increasing access to quality and inclusive basic education for 616,187 school-aged conflict-affected, displaced persons, returnees and host community children (50% girls) and an additional 15,000 youth (aged 15 - 24) in the targeted LGAs in three states (Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe). The specific activities include the following: ACCESS: 1. Construct/rehabilitate 1,833 learning spaces.2. Construct and rehabilitate 1,461 gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces with connecting to water sources.3. Expand radio learning curriculum and access through USB recordings of national curriculum subjects for 200,000 children for continuous learning QUALITY: 1. Train 12,324 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support, education-in-emergencies, special needs education, quality ECD and classroom management and pedagogy (including TaRL to improve learning outcome). 2. Provide essential teaching, learning and recreational materials to school-aged children: (a) 7,778 Recreational Kits, (b) 700,000 school bags, (c) 2,961 ECD kits and adolescent kits and (d) 361,371 school uniforms, sandals and socks. Response Capacity of Education Service Providers: 1. Provide access to learning, training and skills development for 15,000 youth (aged 15-24).2. Establish/reactivate at least 822 SBMCs in conflict-affected schools. 3. Train 4110 SBMC members on SBMC effectiveness, protection of schools and provision of education services to out-of-school children, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and disabled with focus on hard-to-reach areas.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

UNICEF considers protection of all persons affected and at risk when planning humanitarian response and protection is central in UNICEF’s preparedness and life-saving activities throughout the duration of project. The population at risk would be identified considering the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons belonging to sexual and other minorities. Primary consideration will be given to UNICEF’s accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed action is defined by daily interaction and collaboration with affected communities, including support to community-based structures and their participation in public awareness-raising, referral processes and collaboration in identifying and implementing initiatives which contribute to improving the protective environments. Furthermore, UNICEF ensures independent consultations with specific groups, including women, girls and children, to ensure their voices are heard and ideas are incorporated into all activities. As part of the emergency response activities, beneficiaries will be consulted through regular monitoring visits and post-distribution monitoring exercises, during which individuals will be consulted on matters including the quality and relevance of assistance received. UNICEF short text messages (SMS) based U-Report tool will be used to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback or complaints. The U-Report 24x7 Hub receives complaints, reports and feedback from communities, analyses the data and shares with relevant sections in UNICEF and its partners for action. As reports and complaints regarding other services handled by different agencies come in, they are shared with the Sector to take action. Each case is followed up to provide feedback to the community members who laid complaints.
