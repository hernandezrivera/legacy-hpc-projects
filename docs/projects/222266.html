Project Summary

UNHCR, as the protection lead agency of the protection cluster, will continue implementing core protection activities in Cabo Delgado, Mozambique, while seeking durable solutions for the affected communities. The context has its complexities. As per latest DTM figures, 576,280 people returned to northern districts of Cabo Delgado, and 541,400 IDPs are still displaced in a protracted situation within the province. In this changing context, new attacks perpetrated by the NSAGs continue to happen and consequently causing fresh displacements. While drafted this proposal, 14,000 IDPs are living in open air in Chiure district, displaced by NSAGs presence in Chiure and Ancuabe districts in the last days.
UNHCR aims with this project to support with the provision of protection services, all affected communities, including IDPs, returnees, and host communities. The latter is considered key in areas of displacement, as conflict over resources and services can hinder the response. People in Cabo Delgado had been facing several risks since the conflict started which includes abduction, kidnapping, enforced disappearance, arbitrary or unlawful arrest and/or detention, attacks on civilians and attacks on civilian objects child and forced family separation, child, early or forced marriage, forced recruitment and association of children in armed forces and groups, gender-based violence, Impediments and/or restrictions of movement and to access to legal identity, remedies and justice, presence of other explosive ordnance, psychological/emotional abuse or inflicted distress, forced eviction or destruction of personal property, torture or cruel, inhuman, degrading treatment or punishment. In this framework, UNHCR protection response encompasses:
1) the access civil documentation (birth certificates and Identification cards);
2) protection monitoring analyzing trends and identing the main protection needs;
3) housing, land and property, in particular security of tenure.
4) legal assistance, counselling and representation, including in transitional justice; 5) community-based protection (CBP) activities focusing on the specific needs of the individual (youth, persons with disabilities and elderly), but also in the community (protection desks, community volunteers, peacebuilding and, explosive ordnance risk education);
6) GBV services, mitigation, prevention, and economic women empowerment.
7) the provision of capacity-building of duty- and rights-bearers, while ensuring accountability to affected populations, PSEA safeguards, localization, and age/gender/diversity approach that provides specialized responses for the most vulnerable groups.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project seeks to address the protection needs and risks of the displaced people, returnees, and host communities in Cabo Delgado Province and seek durable solutions for them. It will also take into consideration accountability to affected populations (AAP), an inclusive age/gender/diversity (AGD).The proposed protection activities include mobile caravans on civil documentation, legal assistance, protection monitoring and community-based protection (CBP). CBP projects encompasses peacebuilding activities, support to persons with specific needs (PwSNs) and youth, MHPSS, establishment of community protection desks and outreach volunteers equipped with protection knowledge to identify, refer cases while monitoring the protection situation of the communities. It will also includes explosive ordnance risk education - EORE. There will be also the component of Housing, Land and Property (HLP), which entails, legal assistance, security of tenure including legal land documentation, due diligence, legal advocacy, awareness raising and HLP capacity-building of duty- and rights-bearers. All the above-mentioned activities target the protection needs and risks of the most vulnerable groups, such as persons with disabilities, elderly, youth, girls and women.Additionally, the project aims to establish vital GBV responses, focusing on risk mitigation and prevention in IDP sites, transit, urban, and hard-to-reach contexts. Targeting women, girls, men, and boys the project also prioritizes specific vulnerable groups identified by recent UNHCR GBV assessments, including adolescent girls, women and girl-headed households, survival sex workers, LGBTIQ+, persons with disabilities, and male survivors. Adopting a mobile approach, GBV services will be flexible and widespread, conducting safety audits to tailor responses to each community's needs.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection is at the core of every UNHCR intervention in Mozambique and globally. Its community-based and AAP approaches involve regular community engagement (CE), two-way communication with communities, and the implementation of complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFM), and through its leadership of the Cabo Delgado CE/AAP Working Group. UNHCR ensures that people are at the centre of its interventions, providing feedback before, during, and after, mitigating the risk of acting in a way that causes harm or is not in the best interest of the population. Risks are identified through various means, including protection monitoring, participatory assessments, GBV safety audits, complaints and feedback mechanisms, partners and community volunteers trained on identification and referrals of people with specific needs and facing protection risks, consultations with affected population, two-way communication with communities and community engagement. 1) Risks addressed by the Protection response: Lack of civil documentation, Lack of access to justice, Conflict/tensions between communities, Lack of knowledge of rights, Lack of access to services and assistance by vulnerable groups. 2) Risks addressed by HLP response: Forced eviction, Illegal occupation, Illegal expropriation, Land conflicts between communities, Lack of community knowledge of HLP rights. 3) Risks addressed by GBV response: Rape, Sexual Assault, Forced Marriage, Physical Violence (including intimate partner), Denial of resources, opportunities or services, Psychological/Emotional violence. This project has also recognized risks associated with Trafficking and Exploitation, as well as LGBTQ+ Discrimination and Violence, extending its focus to other vulnerable populations, including men, women, girls, and boys who are at risk of experiencing GBV.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

UNHCR, with a dedicated GBV Unit of national and international staff, has institutional expertise and GBV technical capacity supporting the project (across all its components) to ensure that the safe and inclusive participation and empowerment of women and girls is mainstreamed across all project activities. Women and girls, as well as older people, people with disabilities, and other people with specific needs, will be consulted via participatory assessments, ensuring that an age, gender and diversity (AGD) lens is applied across all activities according to UNHCR’s AGD Policy. Furthermore, all project staff and partners are trained on GBV/PSEA core concepts and referrals. GBV risk mitigation is mainstreamed in all UNHCR interventions across all sectors/clusters, as required by UNHCR’s Policy on the Prevention of, Risk Mitigation and Response to Gender-based Violence.Protection: Through its CBP component, UNHCR dedicated programs for assisting people with disabilities (PwDs) and older people; chairs the Cabo Delgado Disability Working Group; and provides of capacity-building to duty and right-bearers for protection and inclusion of PwDs and older people. Through its legal component, it ensures inclusion of PwDs and older persons.HLP: Through its HLP capacity building of duty- and rights-bearers, HLP advocacy, land tenure security assistance, HLP legal assistance, and HLP due diligence, all community members benefit equally, regardless of age, gender, or vulnerability. HLP advocacy and capacity-building, count on a specific module regarding women’s HLP rights, in an attempt to counter harmful, gender-biased customary land practices.GBV: ensures meaningful access to assistance by employing a survivor-centered approach, emphasizing the identification of heightened protection risks across diverse principle and an age, gender, and diversity approach guarantee inclusive engagement. Mainstreaming risk mitigation, in line with the UNHCR Policy on the Prevention of, Risk mitigation and Response to GBV, recognizes GBV services as lifesaving and further ensures accessible assistance. Through extensive training and collaboration with clusters, the project integrates GBV considerations into various sectors, fostering participation and empowerment. This comprehensive strategy exemplifies UNHCR's commitment to robust protection measures, specifically addressing the unique needs of women and the elderly.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Protection: Through its CBP component, the project includes specialized assistance for people with disabilities (including assistive devices and rehabilitation services), and capacity-building of rights- and duty-bearers for inclusion of people with disabilities in community decision-making processes and protection and assistance mechanisms, respectively. UNHCR also chairs the Cabo Delgado Disability Working Group. Protection monitoring and participatory assessments also organize dedicated FGDs with people with disabilities to understand their specific protection needs, and ensure they are mainstreamed in all protection responses.HLP: Through its HLP capacity- building of duty- and rights-bearers, HLP advocacy, land tenure security assistance. HLP legal assistance, and HLP due diligence, all community members benefit equally, regardless of age, gender, or vulnerability, including people with disabilities, ensuring their secure access to land.GBV: The initiative collaborates with communities to involve persons with disabilities in GBV program design. UNHCR co-chairs the Persons with Disabilities Working Group,coordinates Cabo Delgado responses, ensuring safe spaces and specialized GBV services for accessibility. Current services for survivors with disabilities include distributing mobility aids, with the proposed project enhancing GBV support through case worker training.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNHCR ensures environmental considerations across the project. The project is not material assistance or infrastructure based so the risks are less significant. However, materials used for safe spaces are local, natural, and sustainable, the work on the spaces is conducted directly within the community. Also, the project tries to ensure sustainability and localization of all inputs including furnishings for safe spaces and re-usable material masks for project participants.
