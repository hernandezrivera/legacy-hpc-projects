Project Summary

Integrated emergency nutrition interventions for children under the age of 5, pregnant and lactating women and Internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the former Unity and the former Eastern Equatoria states.
The project will support CMAM sites through strengthening community outreach and referrals, and access affected communities to maternal infant and young child nutrition programs. The intervention includes a cash component where mothers with malnourished children will be provided with cash to facilitate movement, especially those that stay far away from treatment facilities. The project will also work with communities to prevent under-nutrition. CARE will strengthen nutrition situation assessments, reporting and accountability mechanisms within the areas of operation including monitoring and evaluation.

1 - Needs

he current implementation peace agreement that was signed in August 12th 2018 is perceived to be encountering challenges. Thus insecurity and violence is predicted to likely to continue in various parts of South Sudan continue to cause humanitarian needs across the country. This will be leading to massive suffering among vulnerable people, specifically children, pregnant and lactation women. In former Unity State, the food security situation has slightly improved compared to same time last year, and an estimated 660,000 people, representing 62.3% of the State population are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) The food security situation remains a concern in the four counties of Mayendit, Guit, Koch and Panyijiar in former Unity State, where Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes have persisted due to pockets of insecurity, cattle-raiding, high food prices, flood related destruction of crops and disruptions to market access. Despite the projected improvements in food security Between January and April 2020, acute food insecurity is expected to persist in Leer, Rubkona, Pariang, Mayom counties. During this period, an estimated 490,000 people will be in Crisis. Furthermore, Between January and April 2020, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity is expected to persist in Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Rubkona, Pariang, Mayom, and Abienmhom given availability of some harvest, fish, livestock products, wild foods, and continued access to markets and HFA early cereals depletion resulting from flood-related damages to crops and Fall Army Worms infestations. Furthermore, expected insecurity and cattle-raiding is likely to disrupt regular access to assistance and markets. The FSNMS R24 results indicated that GAM in Pariang and Abienmhom is 23.3%, Mayom and Rubkona 15.5% while SAM stands at 3.5% in Pariang and Abienmhom and 2% in Mayom and 2.1% in Rubkona.In the August 2019 IFSPC report; an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). Compared to the same period in 2018,there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity by an estimated 5%. However, high levels of acute food insecurity still persist in the country. In both periods, these estimates are in the presence of humanitarian food assistance. In the projection period of September to December 2019, the food security situation is expected to improve as seasonal harvests become available. During this period, an estimated 4.54 million people (39% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food. On the other hand, the post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation is predicted to deteriorate as household food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) are likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity (IFSPC 2019). In former Eastern Equatoria State, an estimated 560,000 people (52.6% of the State population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in August 2019. Ikotos, Kapoeta South, Lopa/Lafon, Magwi and Torit counties are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). In these counties, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are driven by low cereal stocks and high food prices. In the projection period of September to December 2019, an estimated 380,000 people, 35.5% of the State population, will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. This reduction in the number of populations in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity is attributed to the seasonal availability of harvests and livestock products.

2 - Activities

1. Increased access to maternal infant and young child nutrition programs preventing under nutrition including cash transfer for 12,500 boys and 12,500 girls aged 0-59 months, as well as 13,500 pregnant and lactating women at community and facility level across 8 counties in Unity and Eastern Equatoria states access maternal infant and young child feeding messages or interventions - 15,000 children U5 and 7,500 PLW in 4 counties of unity state access BSFP as per current FLA with WFP 7,500 caregivers of children 6-12 months receive cash vouchers for complementary feeding - 51 private traders/millers trained and registered for nutrition cash transfer - Test children with MAM and SAM for malaria and treat them - Improved hygiene and care seeking practices among caregivers of children 0-59 months 2. Deliver quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition to 8000 boys and 8,100 girls 0-59 months and 21,500 PLW in Unity and Eastern Equatorial States - Increased coverage of existing CMAM sites through strengthening community outreach and referrals of 6,425 (3337 girls) SAM and 8,005 (4,042 girls) MAM and 5,134 PLW for CMAM services in 8 counties - Improved case identification and referrals of SAM and MAM cases for CMAM - 35 Health facilities supported to integrate CMAM services into basic care packages - Improved capacity of Health care providers and government facilities and institutions in CMAM programming - Improved and reliable buffer pipeline for CMAM commodities to prevent stock out - Feedback and complaints mechanisms for CMAM services provided to beneficiaries.3. Strengthen nutrition situation assessment reporting and accountability mechanisms in areas of operation as well as monitoring and reporting - Participate in nutrition surveillance activities including FSNMS and coverage surveys to inform response, caseload estimation, IPC, decision making and advocacy - Strengthen project monitoring and situational reporting - Participate in state and national level Nutrition Information technical working groups - Monthly & Quarterly reports collected and shared with cluster and donors for nutrition situation and performance monitoring - Information and data provided for quarterly IPC analysis from four SMART surveys - Feedback mechanisms instituted including review meetings with CNVs and Care group leaders as well as suggestion boxes at PLW BSFP distribution points 4: Increased access to safe and integrated nutrition, health education and WASH FSL responses in counties with critical levels of acute malnutrition - In collaboration with education partners, raise awareness in schools on the importance for early detection, screening and treatment of acute malnutrition.4. In collaboration with education partners, raise awareness among school children and teachers on targeted beneficiaries and use of therapeutic/supplementary food issued-that is like medicine and intended to severe and moderately malnourished children (OTP and TSFP) and not for sharing with other family members - Children with malnutrition tested for malaria and treated where positive - Nutrition sites provided with safe water for washing and appetite test to promote hygiene - Linking with targeted distribution for livelihoods and GFD responses for malnourished children based on criteria for targeting including CMAM exits - Provide WASH –NFIs supplies (soap, jerry cans) to the nutrition sites/communities by linking with UNICEF/IOM WASH NFIs.
