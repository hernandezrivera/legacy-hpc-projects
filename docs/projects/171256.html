Project Summary

Somalia Education sector still remains fragile as a result of multiple crises, including long standing political instability, environmental stress and weak governance structures. The country has lowest enrollment rate in the world. Out the 4.9 million school-aged boys and girls, only 1.5M (30%) are believed to be school (35% girls and 41% boys). Despite sustained intervention by numerous actors, major gaps still persist in the sector which limits effective access to quality safe education. School children often face challenges of frequent displacements and high household vulnerabilities due to recurring shocks which hinders sustained enrollments. The FSNAU post Gu 2020 assessment report indicates 2.6 M internally displaced population in the country of which 1.1 M are school-aged children. The Protection Return Monitoring Network (PRMN), 2020 report indicates the year 2020 had the highest number of displaced people recorded since 2017. 893,000 people were displaced this year of which 357,200 (40%) are school-aged children. Consequently enrollment rates remain as low as only 15 % for nomads, 24 % for IDPs and 34 % for rural children. The COVID-19 epidemic has led to disruption of learning due to unplanned prolong closure of schools. This negatively impacted school aged boys and girls who missed learning opportunities for long period. Even when schools reopened, safety of school children remains a big concern as most schools are not able to meet the requirements of safe back to school guideline on Covid-19 prevention. The EIE Cluster need Analysis 2021 report estimates that 1.4 million children in crisis affected communities (45% girls) will need Emergency Education assistance in 2021. Of these, 1,139,632 are children from non-displaced families and 300,398 are children from displaced families while 216,004 are school aged children living with some form of disability. Equally, CARE Somalia led Need assessments conducted in the project target areas of Sool, Sanaag, Bari in July 20, established December 2019 PAWAN cyclone and strong winds during 2020 Gu rains, caused severe destruction in 29 schools. The Cyclones and the winds have blown off the class roofs and caved in walls of many classes. The ministry of Education have appealed for rehabilitation of affected schools. The assessment further established public schools in rural areas are faced with structural challenges which also inevitably limits Education access for children from disadvantaged families. This includes; inadequate learning spaces, inability of parents to meet education costs including fees and teaching learning materials, shortage of WASH facilities and supplies, scarcity of qualified teachers and teacher incentives. In addition 73% of the assessed schools revealed the school stakeholders - teachers and CECs are not versant with child protection and school safety practices including awareness on Covid-19 Prevention. 12 communities reported attacks on schools in Sanaag and Sool regions where local clan and militia conflicts have surged in 2019. Ministry of Education also lacks capacities to deliver education services for children from disadvantaged communities in hard to reach areas.
In line with Somalia EIE cluster strategy, the proposed CARE response aims to improve equitable access to safe quality education and enhance retention for vulnerable displaced children, destitute children living in host communities and children living with a disabilities in areas affected by crisis with aims of: Objective 1: Ensure emergency and crises affected school aged boys and girls have access to safe and protective learning environment. Objective 2: Ensure vulnerable boys and girls are engaged in life-saving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion. The target regions are; Bari, Sool and Sanaag. CARE is implementing emergency intervention covering other sectors in these regions to meet the organization integrated multi-sectoral strategy

Project Activities

The intervention will address barriers limiting access to quality safe education for both displaced and non-displaced host community’s children affected by natural and manmade crisis while equally seeks to address enrolment gaps between girls and boys in the target areas. The project will target 8000 school aged children (4000 boys, 4000 girls and 800 children living with disabilities) who are either out of school or at risks of dropping from schools. The project activities will take into consideration the needs of both boys, girls and children living with disabilities to provide tailored response to the different gender groups while also giving gender desegregated data on number of person reached through each of the activities. More importantly, the project will mainstream the HRP 2020 strategic priorities pillars including CVA, protection centrality, localization, community participation, gender mainstreaming, accountability and advocacy. The response will undertakes wide range of activities prioritized by EIE cluster and Somalia Ministry of Education for Education in Emergency sector aimed both at improving and expanding access to quality education and enhance retention for crisis affected school aged girls and boys from disadvantage hard to reach communities. In addition a number of activities aimed to enhance child protection, promote safe learning environment in schools and augment safe hygiene and sanitation practices in schools including prevention of COVID-19 spread will equally be implemented. In line with the Somalia National Disability framework and EIE cluster guideline on children with disabilities, CARE will ensure the project target children with disabilities through improving the learning environment in local schools to make it conducive for integration with normal children. The design of all proposed activities will take into consideration the needs of the disable groups. The exact activities to be undertaken are as follows. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will provide Teaching Learning materials through Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA), to 4000 school aged children ( 2000 and 2000 girls ) selected from most disadvantaged households who will newly enroll in schools. The project areas have vibrant markets which can have good supplies of Teaching Learning Materials. (TLMs) as local traders are able to quickly stock adequate TLMS supplies once demand is created through local Cash injection. Currently, the major problem facing the vulnerable households (IDPS and rural host communities ) in the area is widespread loss of income sources as most lost significant portion of their livestock’s to the persistent droughts in the last five years. This has considerably reduced the purchasing power of the vulnerable households, forcing many of them to prioritize the little cash they can access to meet basic needs –Food, Water, medicine- at the expense of the Education. Subsequently most parents in the project target areas cannot adequately meet the costs of Education for their children specially fees and purchase of Teaching Learning materials. The use of Cash Voucher will not only help parents to acquire TLMs for their children but do this at the most convenient time of need. In addition provision of teachers incentives through direct cash transfer to teachers on monthly bases will reduce the costs of school fees in many schools located in the IDP and rural pastoral rural settlements. In addition, CARE Somalia has been implementing large scale CVA based intervention a cross most parts of Somalia for the last five years, including the three regions targeted by the project. CARE CVA interventions covers sectors of Food security, Livelihoods, WASH , Nutrition and Protection. The adoption of CVA in the EIE sector will widen the organization foot prints and deepen its experiences and knowledge on CVA applications across diverse sectors of programming. On the other hand, the local target communities are well versed with the approach of organization using CVA to provide humanitarian assistances. As the result, CARE envisages their would be minimal resistant’s / challenges from the communities in adopting this kind of support. Furthermore, the use of CVA would save operational costs and time for the organization to speed up timely implementation of project activities as it avoids the tedious logistic work involved in the purchase, delivery and distribution of in kind supplies. CARE also anticipate the use of the CVA under this program will ffectively reinforce its other cash based relief and recovery programs as the children from the families who are benefiting from such response will equally benefit from the CVA in Education support. This will inevitably enhance the recovery rates of the affected families as the money that would have been used for Education is saved for other households needs especially like acquiring productive assets during recovery.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Cash Voucher Assistant based intervention to provide Teaching Learning Materials will only be applied in schools where this is possible which will be informed by market assessment to be conducted before the implementation of the activities. Most of the major settlements in the project target areas have functional markets and CARE has been doing Cash based intervention using both direct Cash and Voucher paper to target beneficiaries in sectors of Food security, WASH, Protection and Nutrition. The implementation of the CVA component will be coordinated closely with MoE and EIE cluster and Cash Working at Sub cluster level and it will done in areas where market are accessible and teaching and learning materials are available throughout the year from shops near the schools. Comprehensive feasibility market assessment will be done, greatly borrowing from past CARE Market assessment experiences. Finding will be documented and shared with EIE cluster. Priority area will be given to to schools located close to major market centers where children and parent can easily and safely reach to redeem the vouchersBased on a market assessment conducted, information on the cash delivery mechanism, the transfer voucher value based on market prices, the items included in the voucher, the number of transfers as well as the timing of the transfer(s), and monitoring of voucher use will be clearly notified to the cluster through regular briefs which will be coordinated with other Cash actors specially the Cash Working groups which CARE is already an active member.

Please provide further explanation

The Somalia Recovery and Resilience Framework (RRF) developed in 2018 calls for a collective vision strategy of addressing recurring drought crisis and emphasizes on durable solution approach to tackle perennial problems of human displacements including IDPs and Refugee returnees in the country. The long-term need for continued learning and development of children in protracted crises is best delivered where there is a link between immediate Education in Emergency support and Ministry of Education led long planning and financing of the sector through coordinated engagement between state and non state actors involved in delivering education services in the country. Despite being critical need, education remains among the least funded sectors in Somalia. Education sector is one of prioritized sectors earmarked for the new way thinking of delivering humanitarian assistance. In recognition of this, CARE will put a great consideration linking this response to Education sector strategy pillars on durable solution as outline in the Somalia durable solution framework. This will include strengthening government line ministry of education both at state and federal level to steer and provide sound education management leadership in the sector. The project will equally coordinate with other critical education actors the country including the line Ministry, donors and partner agencies to sustain education services and ensure the most vulnerable and hard to reach children are not left behind in the education. Under its Long term Education Program, CARE will equally advocate for long term funding of education sector in the country alongside sustaining short term measures to address needs arising during the emergencies. CARE has already secured funding for a number of multi-year education project under its development program from various donors which covers the project target areas. These include EU funded Waxbarasha waa Iftiin (Education is light) project and DFID funded Somalia Girls Education Promotion project (SOMGEP ), Under its EIE sector, CARE is also implementing ECHO funded Emergency Project and Education Cannot Wait project which are providing synergy to the long term Education project. As the result the schools that will be targeted under this proposed response will in the long run be absorbed in other CARE Education development programs once the project ends, hence laying foundation for durable solution approach. Already CARE is providing wide range of long term teacher incentive support through its long term education projects. Under its strategic multi-sector approaches, CARE will make use its education program to facilitate access to wide range of critical service to address the needs of children affected by crisis. . Already the schools targeted in this project are located in location where CARE is conducting emergency and development based programs covering sectors of WASH, Nutrition, Health, Food security and Protection, hence the children benefiting from this response will equally benefit from other CARE responses. This linkage would certainly go along way addressing the diverse and complex multi-sectoral needs of affected people including development, hence building on the durable strategy approach. CARE will also use the project as launch pad to advocate with donors and Ministry of Education to jointly plan and coordinate together to solicit for more funding to address existing gaps in sectors. Other durable solution approaches to be taken by CARE under its Education Program include support to the development of Federal and state level Education management information system for schools, documentation of best practices, linking teachers recruited under this project to be included in the mainstream country workforce.
