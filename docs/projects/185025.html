Project Summary

This 24 month intervention will be implemented within 11 villages in the Circle of Gourma Rharous, operating in areas where IR Mali maintains an presence in emergency programming, seeking to engage communities in recovery programming through a triple nexus approach. This intervention will seen to achieve the following:
Outcome 1: Improved use of safe and gender-sensitive WASH services
Following assessment, 5 non-functional water systems will be rehabilitated and upgraded into solar powered water systems and 3 new water points will be installed to increase access to safe and potable water.To ensure continued functionality of systems post-intervention, water management committees (composed of men and women) and village repair mechanics will be identified and trained on management and maintenance techniques and equipped with necessary tools.
In ensuring inclusivity and sensitivity, identification of sites to be targeted will be conducted with men and women, primarily considering protection needs and viewpoints of women and girls so as to mitigate against GBV and CP risks that may arise during water collection. At selected sites, solar powered lighting will also be provided. To ensure accessibility for older persons and PwD the following actions will be taken with regards to the water points:
- Textured gravel will be deposited around the water point to guide people with visual impairments. They will walk on the gravel until they reach the taps.
- A ramp will be installed to facilitate access for people with physical disabilities.
- The interior space of the infrastructure will be large enough to accommodate movement of a wheel chair if needed.
- One tap will be at a lowered height
200 HHs will be targeted for installation of gender-segregated latrines. Consultation with W/M/B/G, older persons and PwD will take place in order to ascertain a culturally appropriate latrine design. Alongside this, community hygiene and sanitation kits will be distributed to support improved environmental sanitation in targeted communities.
In collaboration with technical services, hygiene and sanitation sensitisation sessions on best practices will take place. Additionally, consultatively selected community members will be identified and trained as on promotion of culturally appropriate hygiene practices as community hygiene promoters (CHPs). They will design action plans that they will mobilise in seeking to improve hygiene and sanitation within their communities.
Outcome 2: FS&L are supported for crisis-affected HHs
This result will seek to:
- Reduce the use of negative coping mechanisms amongst targeted HHs
- Increase ownership of productive assets (infrastructure and livestock)
- Ehance food and livelihood security
This result will seek to enhance livelihood coping capacities of those targeted combining the restoration of productive assets including the rehabilitation of ponds, irrigated perimeters and market gardens, provision of inputs such as seeds and production equipment including floating cages for fishing, training on technical production routes, and livestock restocking.
This component of intervention is proposed with conflict sensitivity at the centre of the approach, reaching the different socio-economic and ethnic groups that are present within the targeted areas. Following assessment of required work, 1 water pond for pastoralists, 60 hectares of farmland for rice production to be irrigated through solar powered motorpumps and 5 vegetable gardens with water supply will be rehabilitated and restored, floating cages for fishing will be provided to fishermen. In doing so the most vulnerable HHs identified within the targeted villages will be engaged in Cash-for-work activities to increase immediate coping capacities. Where HHs are identified and are not able to engage in CFW, they will be granted unconditional cash (160 HH). In line with cultural norms in the area it is envisaged that CFW will be undertaken by men.

1 - Contexte

What is the rationale for the project, the why of the project?In a recent briefing on the 'Humanitarian Situation in the Sahel with a focus on food security', OCHA stated that 2020 is set to be the worst year in a decade with regards to food security for the Sahel Region, and the WFP warned that without sustained humanitarian support that also looks at long-term resilient food systems, the Sahel Region will witness a humanitarian catastrophe. the outbreak of COVID-19 exacerbates an already deteriorated and complex humanitarian crisis in Mali where conflict, poverty and climate change combine to place over 6.8 million people in need of urgent humanitarian assistance, a 58% increase in need since March 2020. The following situation analysis presents the context in which this intervention will take place within the Region of Timbuktu, Mali: - Conflict: As conflict continues to escalate in Northern and Central Mali, population movement as a result of violence has now escalated to the highest level since the 2012 crisis. As of July 2020, 287,496 IDPs are now registered in comparison to 147,861 for the same period last year. In combination with the effects of climate change, destruction of productive infrastructure, displacement of farmers from their land for extended periods of time, and cropland abandonment due to presence of armed groups and restrictions on movement that they place on communities, are impacting the ability of populations to meet basic subsistence needs, contributing to increasing food insecurity levels. - Food Security and Livelihoods (Conflict, Climate Change + COVID-19): Recurring and successive effects of climate change on a population dependent on rain-fed agriculture are intensifying within Northern Mali, deepening food insecurity and impacting livelihoods. Recurring and worsening flooding, insufficient rainfall and water supply, degraded land, limited access to inputs and low skill levels in adapting to climate change are core issues. According to the WFP’s HungerMap of October 2020, 68% of the Malian population have insufficient food consumption, with marked deterioration in food consumption being noted. Data from IR Mali’s needs assessments show that the lean seasons lasts between 7 to 9 months in Gourma Rharous. Combined conflict and climate change impacts have been exacerbated by the COVID-19 outbreak and forecasts for food insecurity have been revised, with those forecasted to be in food insecurity rising to 2.7 million people this lean season and the severity of food insecurity deepening, particularly in Northern and Central Regions. In the Region of Timbuktu, HHs will be in IPC Food insecurity phases 3 – 4 (crisis and emergency) this lean season. According to FEWSNET, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are ongoing in the Liptako-Gourma region which will continue until September. - Protection and Social Cohesion: Women and children in Mali are disproportionately affected by ongoing protracted crises and escalating humanitarian needs, and as one of the most vulnerable social demographics harmful practices and violence against women and children, particularly girls, are driven largely by harmful traditional and cultural beliefs and practices. Poverty, gender discrimination, structural power relations, lack of access to basic services, non-application of legal texts that protect women and girls, reduced livelihood opportunities and negative coping mechanisms are some of the further contributing root causes. COVID-19 is exacerbating protection issues country-wide; child labour is significantly increasing with differing impacts on boys and girls, GBV, sexual exploitation and abuse, intimate partner violence, stigmatisation of those confirmed or suspected with COVID-19, and heightened vulnerabilities of persons with disability are also increasing in prevalence. In Timbuktu, the protection context is further complicated by the presence of conflict; cases of CP and GBV violations such as recruitment of children and youth into armed groups, EFM, forced marriage, and violence against women are regularly reported, however higher levels are unreported. High levels of trauma and violations of human rights are also present. The presence of armed groups has resulted in health centres and schools and additional movement restrictions on women and girls. Increasing community conflict has also led to weakening of social cohesion between different community groups, disputes in relation to basic services such as water regularly take place. Increased IDPs and returnee presence have also further aggravated tensions, increasing pressure on scarce resources. The findings of a context analysis jointly conducted by Islamic Relief and Save the Children in July 2020 in the circles of Gourma Rharous and Douentza have highlighted the drivers of the conflict from local and regional perspectives. According to the findings the most important dynamic of the conflict are related to land and natural resources.
