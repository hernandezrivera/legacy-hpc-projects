Project Summary

The project objective is to increase viable inclusive protection opportunities and the overall psychosocial and emotional wellbeing of conflict-affected women and youth by empowering them to play an active role in their communities and feel valued for their contributions which will include the participation of local communities, and coordination with other organizations operating in the same or near area.
Silk Road Organization will establish three safe spaces in Tell Abiad, Suluk and Ras Al Ain communities for people who will be involved with the project and will be managed by Silk Road Organization team and this safe space will provide protection activities i.e: PSS, life Skills & informal activities for Youth Male and female aged 15 -24 years old and adults ) as per each age group requires such as regular drop-in sessions which include different recreational activities for (children with age from 10-14 years old) that will create the emotional balance to individuals reflecting that to the family and community levels.
Increment of the beneficiaries’ capacity will positively affect the level of the protection awareness as well as mapping available external services will reduce the risk towards targeted beneficiaries in vulnerable communities; the drop-in sessions is a kind of the Psychosocial support sessions with the theme of recreational activities where the sessions theme is built to help the children and the Youth to open up and share their feelings through (music, drawing, dancing for children and suitable ones for Youth such as chess, Football, etc.) in order to support to balance their emotional status and hence pass this balance to their families contributing to create a better psychological environment at home.
The curriculum will be IRC curriculum. This project will provide the informal education sessions aiming to cover the gap between peers which is created due to the displacements resulting the negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage, recruitment and labour while life skills courses to women and youth strive to burden the risk towards them that came due to ignoring women and youth category abilities of being leaders and engaged with the communities. Safe space will be the place to manage and initiate a community projects (Community projects will be resulted according to the LS sessions and beneficiaries engagement to decide the most important community project shall be done in their area such as Solid waste collection, Hygiene campaigns, Back to learn awareness sessions and flyers distribution, Afforestation campaigns) which will increase the linkages and the access to the community by beneficiaries aiming to re-integrate Internally Displaced People with the host communities that will lead to promoting the positive coping mechanisms as we are planning to focus on providing sessions on numeracy and literacy and facilitate referrals for Accelerate Learning Programs and catch-up classes For the non-formal education. Relating to community engagement, we are planning to engage in dialogue and discussion around key topics such as child marriage, child labour, arming children and other risk factors. A collective way to do this is to engage the community through protection risk mapping and developing a risk mitigation action plan together with the community and clearly outlining the role of the community. Community stakeholders such as the local council, the Mayors for areas don’t include LCs and other local authorities to be well trained to identify adolescent and youth at risk and facilitate referrals for PSS and other services we are providing which will support us to gain more beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

Many families returned to Tell Abiad and Ras Al Ain districts and their countryside, in addition to the displacement of families from Ain Issa camp, Manbij and Ain Al Arab cities to Tell Abiad. Most of the 18,000 people who were living in Tell Abiad returned back to Tell Abiad city or areas sounding. Safety and security issues are the most important requirements in the current situation. According to Silk Road need assessment, the number of people in need of protection and education in the targeted communities of this action is high adding to that to the factor of limited access of humanitarian actors in the targeted areas which affected people living in these sites to be not provided with multi-sectoral assistance and face major gaps in the provision of essential services (referrals). Youth are forced to hold adult responsibilities, boys and girls have to care for siblings while empowerment opportunities decreased towards women especially for those who are heads of households due to the pressure that they are facing to continue the care work beside Lack of positive coping mechanisms due to the high drop in accessing the skills up opportunities that may lead to increasing the number of women and children at need to protection services.Civilians continued to go through multi-faced protection-related risks including displacement and human rights violations. These resulted in over half of the Syrian being displaced facing challenges to re-integrate with the new hosting communities in terms of social cohesion mechanisms, forcing the affected population to resort to negative coping mechanisms as alternative means for survival. In addition to the physical and psychological trauma, these injuries and deaths can have severe long-term socio-economic implications on the family structure. The lack of appropriate services further increases these vulnerabilities and can result in negative coping mechanisms.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1: Conducting vulnerability Assessment: Silk Road will conduct a vulnerability assessment to identify the most vulnerable, in need of protection support and or emergency support.Activity 2: Establish and maintain the three Safe Space in the target location for girls, boys, youth, men and women. Two safe spaces will be established based on clear selecting criteria ِ(Accessibility and safety within the centres).Activity 3: Recruiting, training and managing coaches/ Protection Assistant on our Policies and regulations “Child Protection, Child Safeguarding, CoC…etc.Activity 4: Regular drop-in sessions. Silk Road will provide Cultural & Recreational Activities for both Male and Female of all age( Learning to draw/carve, learning how to using household waste such as paper, cardboard and plastic to create a useful and unique product, Embroidery and knitting, fun games like Chess, music and games for children under 10 years old etc.Activity 5: Providing psychosocial support activities to Female and Male aged 15 to 24 years old beside Parenting sessions that will be provided to the Women above 25 years old. Those will be regular sessions dealing with 3 pillars of PSS (Communication, social and emotional) in addition to providing parenting sessions.Activity 6: Distribution of dignity kits for the most affected female above 19 years.Activity 1.2.4: Engage the NES Protection Working Group referral pathway to refer beneficiaries with specific needs beyond the scope of Silk Road provisionActivity 7: Creating broader social mobilization and awareness on child and women protection issuesActivity 8: Mapping of services and referral pathway.Activity 9: Life skills coursesActivity 10: Providing non-formal educationActivity 11: Peer to peer dialogue sessions.Output 12: Vulnerable members of the community are supported in the design and implementation of events for broader community participationActivity 13: Supporting women and youth-led community projects.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1- Code of Conduct and/or Child protection incidents; Silk Road has a COC and Child protection and child safeguarding policies in place that have been signed by all the staff focusing on the frontline workers in prior of starting direct activities with beneficiaries, including all procedures for reporting directly to the head office. in order to increase their awareness to the cases they might face in the field and know correct practices and procedures they have to follow and reduce the risks on themselves, the affected beneficiaries and the organization as well with their correct reaction.Risk 2- Discrimination between the beneficiaries: Silk Road Organization staff are well trained to be professional in involving both categories of beneficiaries (IDPs and host community) in the same PSS sessions that will create a smooth communication channel between them and increase the communication flexibility to share their emotions and support each other (Peer-to-Peer support).Risk3 - Content of message is not fully adapted to the social and cultural environment and creates misunderstanding or even tensions and has repercussions on the organisation implementing the campaign or /and the Media chosen is not the most effective in the specific environment/ community:- Silk Road will consult with population groups and communities on the most effective way of delivering messages; consult existing assessments on communication to communities (MSNA); resort to the knowledge of local counterparts and social workers in the communities.Risk4 - The population targeted by the intervention is put at risk as a result of the location of the facility; lack of consideration for the accessibility to services and lack of consideration of contextual information on the location and the local dynamics. The safe spaces will be established based on clear selecting criteria (In terms of accessibility the centres shall accessible to all beneficiaries houses in selective and known areas.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1- A safe environment allows implementing the activities by having safe access to the safe space by beneficiaries and staff (low airstrike and less fighting between armed groups)2- The curricula are used will be engaging and suitable for all age categories for children, women and youth Risks:Risk1 -Security incidents affect project activities (beneficiaries, staff, safe space and/or relevant facilities): Silk Road field team has been trained security conflict dynamics analysis and they have the full rights to have the decision on the ground. A security officer is in place to follow up with the team with all relevant support in terms of building the field team capacity, following up their issues and analyzing the field situation in a regular and incident basisRisk2 -Personnel with critical skills needed for the project cannot be found: Silk Road will overcome this risk by: - Re-process if necessary- Broader advertising- Use local ads via local councils- Evaluate strengths and weaknesses well in applicants- Provide training for new employees after assessing what they need.Risk3 -Inflation, especially the cost of equipment, furniture and etc, may result in higher than planned prices. If prices spike too high, then Silk Road may not have the budget to complete the project / Market system disruption. - Silk Road will monitor inflation and prices and include this risk in its contracts with service providers. If inflation rises, they will work with the donor to re-align the budget and program accordingly. - We will include the ability to ensure procurement as a criterion in the contractor's bid analysis. - We will Consider using fixed prices and placing this risk in the contractor's contracts. - We will Conduct a strong and constant market monitoring before starting any local procurement to have early warning of potential lack of supplies, and also map out alternative supply.
