Project Summary

The Syrian crisis has led to a huge deterioration in most of the basic services in the targeted area, especially concerning health sector. In particular, there has been an increment of cases with late diagnosis of cancers, specifically breast cancer, due to the lack of awareness programs and medical equipment for early detection. Particularly, women are affected by the insecure conditions from Syria being restrict to travel in order to access different health facilities and ensure periodical checking.
As a consequence, breast cancer represents 30% of cancer cases diagnosed among women. Its mortality ratio amounts to around 24% while its crude incidence rate and ASR are 29 and 43 respectively per 100,000. According to the National Cancer Registry, 73% of the cases are detected in stage III and stage IV, when the recovery rate is extremely low. OCHA estimates that the number of women in the age of 40-70 who have been diagnosed with breast cancer in northern Syria amounts to around 840,000, while the latest WHO data published in 2017 show that deaths by breast cancer reached 1,613 cases, representing 1.17% of total deaths in Syria.
Breast cancer recovery rates in Syria would be higher if breast cancer screening programs were conducted. The biggest challenge is constituted by the large number of cases of late diagnosis due to the lack of early screening programs, combined with awareness raising.

Implementation of activities

1. Establish a caravan center (Khayr4 center) to conduct early screening programs for breast cancer:1.1 Provide purchase requests with the needed devices and caravan;1.2 Implement the procurement process for items monthly according to WATAN's procurement policy;1.3 Clinical examination for women of 40 years or older in Khayr4 center performed by nurses; 1.4 Radiology diagnostic for women of 40 years or older in Khayr4 center performed by a radiologist;1.5 Conduct biopsies and appoint a pathologist to examine the results in cases suspected of being abnormal;1.6 Conduct awareness-raising sessions on the importance of early detection of breast cancer;1.7 Provide salaries for the staff. 2. Establish a referral system for the breast cancer cases that will be detected during the screening 2.1 Determine a list of local hospitals for surgical treatment of breast cancer cases detected during the screening;2.2 Follow up these cases during and after treatment. 3. Monitoring and evaluation throughout the cycle of the project

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers:- Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL).- Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG) and humanitarian leadership in their efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria.- Reporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase.Gender Based Violence GBV:- Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package.- Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.- Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor.- Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services.Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA:- A PSEA policy describes standard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures.- Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.- Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic.- Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff.- Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver.- Design and implement appropriate programmes based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and feedback mechanisms.
