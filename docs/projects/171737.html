Project Summary

Somalia’s 2.6 million IDPs and an increasing returning refugee population are in dire need of durable solution that gives them a way out from the associated protection risks, so far IDPs are far more vulnerable to mass scale, unrelenting and brutal forced evictions, to pervasive gender based violence trends. IDP children are the most vulnerable group and in emergencies they are exposed to far greater rights violations, civilians on the move are witnessing unprecedented impediments to their freedom of movement, the decades long proliferation of explosive hazards coupled with conflicts have forced those fleeing to use unconventional routes, avoid main supply routes and are exposing themselves to greater risks.
AoR Coordination meeting report in 2020 showed that 1.87 million children including 10% of children with disabilities are in need of immediate GBV and child protection services, Lower Shabelle is listed at priority one for these development. CTFMR (January to June 2020 report) verified 1,774 grave violations affecting 1,613 children (303 girls) of which 34% (545,) and 30 girls were cases of recruitment/use, 34% (555) cases of abduction, above 50% were reported from Lower Shabelle region alone.
In this dire protection environment Somalia is witnessing its first mass refugee return since the late 1990s, refugees are forfeiting their basic rights to return to Somalia in their masses. Conflict over power in Somalia between the allied AMISOM/SNAF troops and the hardlined rebel group have led to outbursts of violence, mass displacement and a myriad of protection violations, coupled with mass refugee returns 2017 is anticipated to continue in the trajectory set by political process and military operations set in 2017 unaddressed these trends will result in a deterioration of the humanitarian situation when the caseload of needs is substantially higher. In recent years in Bay and Lower Shabelle regions of Southwest State of Somalia, the number of cases related to Gender Based Violence in which women, girls and children were abused have increased in resulting in victimization of the vulnerable IDPs and stigmatization of the survivors that has long term impact on their lives. SOYVGA seeks to address such problems in the communities particularly the IDPs who are susceptible to GBV violations.
The thirty years of conflict in Somalia has led to the increase of human rights abuse, particularly the rights of children. During the war, most of the communities were sheltered the Internally Displaced People experienced the worst situation of rape, sexual abuse, economic exploitation, torture, etc. Women and children rights were abused on a daily basis without concrete actions taken as a deterrent to this inhumane act. Cognizant of the growing needs of these highly vulnerable group, SOYVGA has been working with the local authorities, the business communities and local Chamber of Commerce in south central of Somalia in implementing skills training
The proposed project aims to improve, advocate and mobilize for better mechanism and policies that enhance the protection of Women and Girls including persons with disabilities. Enhancement of protection laws and justice system to provide better environment in which at risk population groups live. Decrease and eradication of GBV during and after famine, in IDPs and in hosted communities. .Awareness raising campaigns, and promotion of gender-equality. Offering community resilience programs, educating and empowering the community in holistic way. Empowering women/Girls and the disabled to speak against violence. Training community members to provide psycho-social counseling to victims of GBV. GBV survivors in line with the essential needs of women, men, girls and boys for those affected conflicts and natural disaster Gender Based Violence through multi sectoral response.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash interventions of the project will be used to support the livelihoods needs of the the GBV survivors particularly in economic support for people who have been faced with GBV violations who most of the time are stigmatized because of their status in the community as the result of the violence.Cash transfers will be made for selected GBV survivors to meet their basic needs of which they could not seek assistance due to stigmatization. The selected beneficiaries will be enabled to use the cash for meeting their needs that will also improve their livelihoods amongst the communities some of who may be hostile to GBV survivors.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Prior to the design of the project, SOYVGA conducted market assessments in the some of the target districts in Bay and Lower Shabelle regions to assess market functionalities and the prices of the essential commodities in the regions. The aim was to identify gaps in market functionalities and how it could meet the cash interventions proposed for this interventions.
