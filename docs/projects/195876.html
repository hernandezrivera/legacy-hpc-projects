Project Summary

HNO and HRP highlight that 15.3 million people need humanitarian assistance in 2023, an increase of 0.7 million from 2022.
The expanding impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and the Cholera spread have further complicated the lives of people in humanitarian settings in northwest Syria.
While the number of positive COVID-19 cases has steadily decreased, there is a continued need to focus on treating and mitigating the spread of the virus and its impacts on displaced people and other vulnerable communities, including the healthcare workforce, and supporting scale-up of the public health response

3 - Needs

The overall humanitarian situation remains dire in northwest Syria (NWS), with the population reaching approximately 4.4 million people as of June 2022. Humanitarian needs continue to be driven by the impacts of over a decade of hostilities exacerbated by economic deterioration and multiple displacements.More than 2.8 million are Internally Displaced People (IDPs), many of whom are living in overcrowded locations with limited access to essential services. As of June 2022, 1.8 million IDPs were living in 1,434 camps or informal sites. Women and children represent 79 percent of this caseload., SoP has been addressing the crucial needs for (Food, and NFIs items) of IDPs in Idlib since 2016 As the number of Syrian IDPs in the area continues to grow and further needs are being identified by SoP teams through periodic Field assessments and post-distribution monitoring, SoP now sets the goal to continue the targeted Food Baskets and Bread bundles to Syrian IDPs and Host in the targeted area and reduce negative coping strategies To Assess the situation in Northwest of Syria under three different sectors and propose optimal response modalities and develop operational plan there, Space of Peace Organization conducted a multi-sectorial needs assessment in Idlib district in Early 2022 in Harim, Idleb, Ariha, and Jisr-Alshogour.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

A lack of food and NFIs, WASH facilities and shelter create a myriad of protection concerns, especially for vulnerable groups, such as the elderly, women and adolescents. SoP will continue to monitor protection risks, working with protection colleagues if specific issues arise and will ensure that any activities including targeting, distribution and monitoring do not increase these risks. Due to security threats from the conflict such as most relief assistance is being delivered in a low-profile manner through the door-to-door processor in public buildings where IDPs currently reside and in Camps This distribution modality helps to limit the exposure of beneficiaries and the local association members. Where it is deemed appropriate by the local associations, there are distribution points where beneficiaries convene to collect distributions. In some areas, public distributions are unavoidable but care is taken to arrange them at what is considered the safest possible location and time of day When distributions are done in a more centralized manner, utilizing information solicited from beneficiaries communities, SoP will ensure that they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms or physical ability are taken into account when planning for the distribution.
