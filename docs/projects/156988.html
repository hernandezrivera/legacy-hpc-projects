Project Summary

According to SAMS strategy in integrating GBV services within all SAMS health facilities and supporting the Women and girl’s safe spaces, SAMS through this project aims to Provide support to survivors of sexual and gender-based violence, as well as their extended networks (families, communities, relief workers, care providers, and civil society), in Northwest Syria.
Through achieving the following objectives:
Objective 1: Improve the access to specialized quality GBV services in the health facilities, will be achieved through:
1.1 Provide specialized GBV services (including case management and referral to health and mental health, psychosocial support) to survivors (Women and girls) in 16 health facilities in north west Syria.
1.2 Provide GBV programming for adolescent girls through group discussions, activities and distribution of IEC materials within 16 health facilities in north west Syria.
1.3 Provide safe spaces for women and girls through supporting Women and Girls Safe Spaces in Northwest Syria.
Objective 2: Enhance strategies to empower women and girls and raise awareness about GBV in North West Syria
2.1 Provide GBV empowerment activities to the survivors and their extended families through group discussions, activities and distribution of IEC materials.
2.2 Provision of Awareness Raising activities aims to aware the women and girls from some topics including (GBV, Reproductive Health, and Family Planning)
Objective 3: Build the capacity of the Service Providers will be achieved through:
3.1 Provide trainings for service providers (CMR, community health workers, GBV, GBV guidelines, ethical considerations of working with survivors of violence)

Needs

Now approaching its eighth year, the humanitarian situation in Syria continues to deteriorate, the civilians continue to bear the brunt of a conflict marked by unparalleled suffering, destruction and disregard for human life. The effect of eight years of crises have depleted the coping strategies of millions of people, stretching their resourcefulness to its absolute limit. In this situation, the large number of women headed households, children, older persons and persons with disabilities, especially those without adequate family or community support, are particularly exposed to heightened risks and are increasingly forced to resort to unsustainable and unsafe means of survival, including gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe. The length of the crisis and deep-rooted social norms, in conjunction with the mounting lawlessness in some areas, are normalizing this violence, resulting in the detrimental psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights. The fear of sexual harassment (on the way to school, in the market, at distribution sites, etc.) as well as sexual violence, often associated with kidnapping, is a concern raised by consulted women and girls and contributes to psychosocial stress and limits their movements. Moreover, the shame and stigma surrounding sexual violence means that survivors often do not talk about violence when it happens. Adolescent girls and young girls can experience sexual harassment and violence in situations such as when working on the land/ farm, in shops, and in instances when affected by child recruitment. Household level gender roles continue to change with negative implications within families and communities. Although women have more responsibility, this does not infer authority. Women have taken on additional roles traditionally associated with men, including as heads of household and principal breadwinners for their families. This contributes to increased pressure and stress for women to provide for their families as they juggle commitments within the home and workplace. Consequently, they are exposed to increased risks of violence against them within the family and at their workplace. Where GBV services exist, women and girls have confirmed that they have a positive impact on their lives, with women and girls’ safe spaces serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services (health, psychosocial and legal support) and other needed services (e.g. vocational and skill training). (HNO 2019).

Implementation of activities

SAMS will implement the project activities as the following:1. Establish safe spaces in the health facilities if needed.2. Hire the staff to run the project teams in health facilities and Safe spaces: Case Managers, CHWs, and PSS workers.3. Provide specialized GBV services to survivors (Women and girls) in the Health Facilities safe spaces and WGSS, including case management and referral to health and mental health, psychosocial support.4. Provide GBV empowerment to survivors (Women and girls) and their extended families and through group discussions activities and distribution of IEC materials.5. Provide GBV programming for adolescent girls through group discussions, activities and distribution of IEC materials within 11 health facilities in north west Syria.6. Introduce service providers to best screening processes and techniques.7. Provide outreach activities through community health workers in 15 communities in north west Syria.8. Provide training to the health service providers in CMR protocols, GBV, CHW modules, first responder training, self-care and PSEA.9. Organize self-care and staff care activities. Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Sexual Exploitation and abuse by Facilities staff toward beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Set PSEA Policy and effectively implement the policy in Hiring the staff and oriented them on the policy, training them, and make sure every staff sign the policy, 2. Effective complaint mechanism in place, 3. Awareness raising for beneficiaries on PSEA, 4. Clear complaints channels for beneficiaries and distribute brochures and leaflets in all places in the facilities (waiting rooms, entrance, Etc.).Risk: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Possibility: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centered services 2. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral.Risk: GBV service providers’ safety and security because of their role and PTS for dealing with violence. Possibility: High. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: Self-care strategies as part of the project activities.Risk: Impact of activity is minimal on our population off concern. Possibility: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measure: Ensure training programs are designed to assess impact of the trainings. Risk: The Women and girls are prevented from attending the sessions, by their husbands, or brothers or fathers. Possibility: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Coordinate and communicate with the community leaders to identify our work. Risks: Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services. Possibility: high. Impact: high Mitigation measure: assure that our confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place.Risk: Part of the populations in the targeted communities may not welcome our intervention especially GBV services. Possibility: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Awareness raising about our project in the community. 2. Engaging men and community leaders and informing them about the services.Risk: Women and adolescent girls with child-bearing responsibilities might have difficulty accessing services in the WGSS. Possibility: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measure: Equip all WGSS with child-care services

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Control by unaccepted groups / entities or GoS. Mitigation measures: SAMS will be re-running all anti-terrorism/background checks on all staff, volunteers, partners, hawala, and other vendors associated with the current award. Additional Money Transfer Verifications will take place as well where SAMS has added further anti-fraud verifications conducted by SAMS staff in Syria and Turkey before monthly disbursal of salaries to recipients. Separately, SAMS MEAL staff are continuously running their own separate spot checks, analyzing a sample of the payroll, conducting staff interviews, and verifying the salary amount received, in addition to their regular visits to the facilities, where they observe staff presence and conduct inventory checks. Risk: Attack by GoS and its allies / others.Mitigation measures: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation.Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salariesMitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the trainings regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible.Risk: Abduction (kidnapping)Mitigation measure: Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Security Briefing; Counter Abduction; Abduction Management training for staff and partners.Risk: TheftMitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for SAMS facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports; Security Briefing for staff and partners.
