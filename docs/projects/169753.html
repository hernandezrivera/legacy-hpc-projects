Project Summary

UN Women will extend its cash for work programming to women affected by the blast in the areas of Karantina and Bourj Hammoud. It will engage women to support with the clean up and recovery effort – specifically on issues of plumbing, painting, and sanitation. Cash for work rates will follow previous rates set and used by the LCRP livelihoods cluster unless guidance is otherwise provided. The programme will run for three months, offering two groups of 300 women (for a total of 600 women) opportunities for 4 weeks each, with one week prior to deployment focused on issues of safety, hygiene and COVID compliance and training on the tasks being performed. The women engaged will be selected from the blast affected areas, and from households impacted by the blast (e.g. households that lost a breadwinner due to the blast and may be residing outside of the blast site). Clean up and recovery work will be identified and coordinated with local municipalities, UNDP, ILO, UN-Habitat and other relevant actors. Clean up and recovery work will specifically target the homes of female headed households and elderly women, who may be more comfortable with female lead support teams. The selected women will compliment ongoing cash for work initiatives by ACTED, ILO, UNDP and others.

1 - Needs

According to preliminary assessments, following the blast both women and men of affected households adopted negative coping mechanisms related to food consumption, mainly in order to cope with the harsh economic situation. Reduced spending on food was the most reported coping strategy by both the refugee and host community in June 2020.However, gender disaggregated data shows women are more food insecure - that 66% of women, compared to 43% of men, eat less preferred food, 85% of women compared to 57% of men are limiting the portion sizes of food at mealtime while both men and women limit their intake in order for smaller children to eat or reduce the number of meals they eat. Data also shows a disproportionately high number of female headed households remaining in the explosion affected areas (close to 50% of the initial sample of households surveyed by the Lebanese Red Cross). In addition, older women living alone make up a sizable percentage of those whoremain in the radius of the explosion site, and of those immediately affected by the explosion.27% of female respondents were living alone, of which almost three quarters were over the age of 60.

2 - Activities

1. Conduct one week training programmes for 600 women on safety, hygiene and COVID-19 compliance 2. Working with municipalities and partners to identify clean-up and recovery priorities to ensure a coordinated response3. Cash for Work for 600 women to support the clean up and recovery efforts (indirectly benefiting 3000 people (family members)) 4. Wherever needed, create linkages to referrals funding under the protection section (including PSS services, GBV case management)
