Project Summary

In Northeast Syria (NES), the economic downturn, exacerbated by the devaluation of the SYP against the USD, has far-reaching implications for people’s ability to meet their daily needs. The most commonly reported obstacles to meeting basic needs were the gap between wages and rising prices and the over lack of employment opportunities, while access to livelihoods has consistently been highlighted as the first priority need of host communities and IDPs alike. Production losses in the agricultural sector severely affect the estimated 18% of the employed population working in the agricultural sector across NES. The death rates and/or destocking reduced farmers’ abilities to generate incomes, and therefore have long-term implications for their livelihoods. In Northwest Syria (NWS), the aftermath of the earthquake on the livelihoods and the local economy in the area, compounded an already critical economic and labour market situation by exacerbating the income/expenditure gap caused by the 2022 reductions in subsidies, general price increases, exchange rate volatility, and the global economic outlook affecting remittances and return rates. In fact, prior to the earthquake, Aleppo and Idleb governorates OCHA informants, reported the access to income generation activities and livelihood opportunities as the top-ranked unmet need, a condition which further hindered the households’ ability to cope with the average inflation of 90% and devaluation of the national currency and Turkish Lira throughout 2022 and the related food price increase. The earthquake had a significant impact on agricultural livelihoods and production in Northwest, where 72% of the population rely on crop and livestock production. Additionally, the livestock value chain, source of livelihood for the 64%5 of people, was also significantly affected by the shocks. As such, the support of agricultural and livestock local production is notably among the top priorities identified for the possible restoration of livelihood in NWS. The proposed project aims at improving food security of conflict-affected communities in Northeast and Northwest Syria. This will be achieved through a combination of different complementary interventions at the individual household and community-level. At the household level, emergency responses assistance will be delivered through distribution of Ready-to-Eat Rations, cash and voucher assistance. In addition, Acted will enhance food production at HH level, through kitchen and home garden kit, enhancing individuals' food security. Acted will also target farmers and livestock holders with longer-term support, delivering agricultural inputs to redeem seeds, fertilizers, polytunnels and tools, and training on climate-smart agriculture and best practices. In addition, Acted will rehabilitate communal productive assets such as mills, bakeries, etc and provide flour assistance to one public bakery in Al-Hasakeh governorate, serving the overall targeted community. All project activities will be designed in a way that enables resilience to be built at a local level and that builds the capacity of men, women, boys, and girls to access basic items and meet basic needs themselves. Specifically, at the household and community levels, Acted will support men and women in building their own capacities to meet their basic needs so that they are more resilient and better able to access commodities and services in the future. This project is estimated to benefit 404,730 individuals overall. As part of its PSEAH strategy, Acted has also developed training packages on the matter which are delivered to all its staff members during the first month of employment and through regular refresher trainings. Training sessions will also be delivered to all the contractors under this intervention to ensure beneficiaries’ safety during the delivery of the services.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The proposed project aims at improving food security of conflict-affected communities in Syria. This will be achieved through a combination of different complementary interventions at the individual household and community-level. At the household level, emergency responses assistance will be delivered through distribution of Ready-to-Eat Rations, cash and voucher assistance. In addition, Acted will enhance food production at HH level, through kitchen and home garden kit, enhancing individuals' food security. Acted will also target farmers and livestock holders with longer-term support, delivering agricultural inputs to redeem seeds, fertilizers, polytunnels and tools, and training on climate-smart agriculture and best practices. In addition, Acted will rehabilitate communal productive assets such as mills, bakeries, etc and provide flour assistance to one public bakery in Al-Hasakeh governorate, serving the overall targeted community. All project activities will be designed in a way that enables resilience to be built at a local level and that builds the capacity of men, women, boys, and girls to access basic items and meet basic needs themselves. Specifically, at the household and community levels, Acted will support men and women in building their own capacities to meet their basic needs so that they are more resilient and better able to access commodities and services in the future. This project is estimated to benefit 404,730 individuals overall. As part of its PSEAH strategy, Acted has also developed training packages on the matter which are delivered to all its staff members during the first month of employment and through regular refresher trainings. Training sessions will also be delivered to all the contractors under this intervention to ensure beneficiaries’ safety during the delivery of the services. Women’s integration remains a challenge due to complex gender dynamics and traditional norms, Acted will continue promoting women's participation in all activities. Additionally, Acted will ensure that strong mitigation measures are put in place to prevent risks of GBV particularly during the distributions, which are increasing risks of sexual exploitation and abuse. This will include appropriate distribution times, minimizing distances between beneficiaries’ homes and the distribution sites, priority lines, gender-segregated queues for male and female, and presence of female staff and gender mixed teams. Finally, Acted is committed to establish and maintain a system that gives (non-)beneficiaries the possibility to formulate comments, suggestions, or complaints on Acted’s work. A range of communication modalities will be made available to targeted communities to ensure the mechanism is easily accessible (WhatsApp hotline, Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) channels including complaint boxes, a dedicated email address and direct engagement with Acted staff in the field or at Acted offices).
