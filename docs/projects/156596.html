Project Summary

The project aims to recover and expand the economic situation of the returnees, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host community business owners in the Aleppo governorate of Northern Syria, specifically targeting Ar-Ra'ee and Azaz sub-districts as hot spots for high number of returnees and IDPs.
These early recovery efforts will be achieved by, firstly, conducting a needs assessment on understanding the factors of production (Labor, Capital, Input and Technology) for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) which will lead to development of customized business development plans for local SMEs; and secondly, by providing a combination of business development services in the form of consultancy and seed grants based on the needs of the local businesses to revive and enhance the production capacities of local businesses in the targeted locations.
The overall aim of this intervention is to improve the local population’s access to the labour market by stabilizing and/or expanding local businesses in order to generate further jobs. In order to enhance social cohesion and reduce competition for resources, the project will target 360 businesses, supporting 30% IDPs and 70% host community enterprises, including 54 female business owners.
The factors of production needs assessment will be conducted in the form of a base and end-line multi-sectorial assessments on the local economy in the targeted locations, defining the gaps in factors of production as well assessing the causes of these gaps, their effect on the production capacity of local SMEs in the selected locations.
Upon completion of the needs assessment, WATAN will conduct a concentrated assessment among potential beneficiaries (business owners) to understand the operational status and size, their business production rates, financial situation, potential absorption and growth capacity. Assessed businesses will be filtered through a prioritization system according to their operational status, estimated capital size, number of employees and their market share. Integrating a tailored response model into early recovery efforts will ensure the restoration of productivity and expansion of economic activity at the local level.
Upon acceptance into the programme, WATAN will be responsible for providing business development services through a combination of consultancy and seed grants.
The support package can contribute towards the purchase of assets including consumable raw materials, initial premise rental payments, water and fuel infrastructure, electricity generation, repair of equipment and productive assets, the rehabilitation of work premises, and staffing costs. Business consultancy will continue to be offered after delivery of the support package, with WATAN coordinating referrals among IDPs and the host community to job opportunities that have been generated as a result of the project.

Implementation of activities

Activity 1 - Provide services for SMEs to enhance their production and business capacity1.1.1 Develop a framework and template for the NPM's local market production needs analysis1.1.2 Conduct local market production needs analysis through NPM which will identify priority sectors, analysis on factors of production, gaps and potential business target groups1.1.3 Develop seed grants mechanism in line with the outcomes/recommendations of the needs analysis1.1.4 Conduct a prioritization assessment to select 360 SMEs to be integrated into the programme/call for expression of interest. (This will include financial and management assessments, taking into consideration operational status, size, production rate, growth potential, finances and asset/technical needs)1.1.5 Deliver customized business development plans and consulting services to each SME, which will address the current operational/production issues, gaps and enhance overall productivity potential1.1.6 Procure essential services or materials/assets/equipment according to the business development plans and submitted response plan from the business owners through in-kind seeds grantsActivity 2 – Provide support for jobs development1.2.1 Monitor and follow up job vacancies, referring job seekers through combination of online and print media1.2.2 Conduct follow-up visits to SMEs to monitor the process1.2.3 Conduct final assessment of each beneficiary to measure impact of business development support1.2.4 Conduct regular monitoring and reporting including end multi-sectorial evaluation to assess the impact of the project on local economy of the targeted locations

Protection Risk Analysis

Although all Syrian population has been heavily affected by the current crisis, due to traditional local norms, women are exposed to additional protection related risks. All types of violence are cited as occurring in opposition areas of Syria, with domestic violence, sexual exploitation and child marriage as top three. While sexual exploitation is somehow new, child marriage and domestic violence are often normalized and ingrained in the culture. Women and girls are exposed to structural and societal risks as the lack of safe spaces and public transportation, limited freedom of movement, poverty, unemployment, displacement, stress associated to war, gender role change, lack of documents. All this poses enormous pressure, men feeling they failed in protecting and securing a good life for their families. With men perceiving women as a threat to their control due to gender roles change, domestic violence has increased in Syria. Whereas men actions are explained as a result of stress, women are often blamed for provoking the violence or allowing it to happen. Parental violence against children has also been reported as increasing. Displacement and living in camps are noted as being particularly unsafe environments for women/girls due to the lack of privacy (in tents or in latrines), overcrowding and mixing of people, poverty, financial desperation, and lawlessness.The WoS Protection analysis reveals that 17 sub-districts in Idleb and 23 In Aleppo are facing severe, critical and catastrophic situation regarding protection. While some services are available, GBV cases are less addressed and the assistance is focusing mainly on survival pathway support and less on community-based interventions, mandatory for mind-set change.Women and girls occupy a central role in WATAN’s strategy for 2018-2023. Part of the development approach, WATAN seeks to empower women to become more resilient in the conflict setting while securing their safety at all levels. When designing the project, WATAN has applied all its security protocols to ensure that interviewed GBV survivors and leaders of communities are not exposed to any risk for their involvement. For this purpose, WATAN and its partners have a set of policies and procedures to be respected when working with GBV cases.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:• Unforeseen changes in the conflict• Exposure of confidential data• Services are not producing the expected impactRisks Mitigations:• Considering the locations of the project, an immediate attack coming from GoS or fights between other armed groups is less probable.• WATAN has a system of collecting information that allows registration of beneficiaries data on KOBO and storing of this data directly in the headquarter from Turkey. In addition, WATAN staff is instructed not to share beneficiaries' names with third parties.• WATAN will prepare a suitable response plan for the businesses selected in the project. The plan will rely on the field assessment and projections prepared by the financial and technical specialists. Continuous monitoring of services, will allow adjustments if needed to reach the objective.
