Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to improve the well-being and health among the refugee and host communities in Cox’s Bazar. The project focuses on protection, equitable access to services without any discrimination specially targeting the most vulnerable groups (women, children, pregnant/lactating women, people with disabilities, the elderly, GBV and conflict-affected survivors).

Objectives

1. Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases. 2. Ensure the prevention and timely response to outbreaks of diseases with epidemic potential and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone. 3. Encourage healthy living, improve health seeking behaviour and utilisation of essential service package among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender considerations and vulnerable groups.

Needs

Despite the progress made so far, critical disparities exist in availability, access, quality and distribution of health services among sexes and age groups within as well as outside the camps. The needs and vulnerability vary likewise. There is a strong need for health programming that reflects these diversities. The crisis-affected population from FDMN and host communities is still facing combined vulnerabilities associated with natural hazards, congested settlements and public health risks due to insufficient and unequitable access to health services. Adolescent and disability inclusive friendly health services need to be positioned and mainstreamed. To that end measures are undertaken which are reflected in the project activties. Still gender-based violence incidents are not addressed properly and underreported. Although initially basic health needs were addressed, there is a need for comprehensive and sustainable health interventions and will be needed to address communicable and non-communicable diseases to save the FDMN and host populations with special focus on sexual reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psycho-social support.
