Project Summary

The project will take place in Idlib city-Salqin - Harim and Al-Dana districts – Idleb governate. Afrin and Azaz in Aleppo governate.
Cost per beneficiary: 133.9 $
Estimated # of disability beneficiary: 3 %
Estimated funding: 2,333,877$
Estimated number of beneficiaries: 17430 (12523 formal education – 4500 non-formal education - 407 schools’ staff)
This project will support the educational activities in Idlib city, Salqin, Harim and Al-Dana districts (camps) – Idleb governate and Afrin and Azaz in Aleppo governate by establishing three temporary Education Centers (TECs) and supporting 12 schools considering the access of children with special needs besides the cultural appropriateness of the context in order to ensure that girls have safe and equitable access providing children with the chance to continue their studies and providing access to quality formal and non-formal education is ensured by providing incentives and capacity building for teachers and education personnel.
The project contributes to the Education Sector’s HRB objectives through:
1- Project will scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education in Idleb and Aleppo governate age (6- 17) by establishing and operating three temporary Education Centers and supporting another 12 schools
2- Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 6-17 years) within a protective environment by providing Stipends/incentives for teachers and education personnel, PSS activities and remedial & catch up classes.
3- Strengthen the capacity of the education system and communities to deliver a timely, coordinated and evidence-based education response by raising the capacity of teachers and education personnel through in several needed training and coordination with education directorate in Idleb and Aleppo.
The project targeted the schools and centers through the following activities:
1- Establishing three temporary Education Centers (TECs) and supporting 12 schools and providing the schools and centers with running cost, supplies, equipment, staff salaries, and heating fuel
2- 12,523 students will receive formal education
3- 4,500 students will receive non-formal education (catch up & remedial classes)
4- 407 school staff will be provided with capacity building
The project will increase the boys’ and girls’ beneficiaries by providing the activities of formal and non-formal education and both will access these activities for children and youth (6 – 14)
The project will increase the boys’ and girls’ beneficiaries with disabilities by providing the centers and schools with the necessary tools and equipment’s to be appropriate with disabilities and training the staff on inclusion education
The project is not funded and we are looking for fund to cover all the activities in the project

4 - Needs

In its ninth year, the Syrian conflict has led to significant suffering in the civilian population and widespread displacements. High levels of civilian casualties continue to be reported amid regular and extensive violations of international humanitarian law (IHL) and international human rights law (IHRL). In this context, civilians face numerous and overlapping risks. Per the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 11.7 million people live in dire conditions and 5 million people face acute needs. The conflict has forced 2.1 million, over one-third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (including more than 100,000 Palestine refugee children) and about 245,000 education personnel are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 percent of whom are in acute and immediate need and 85% of assessed communities reported a high occurrence of child labor preventing school attendance (HNO 2019).In this context, barriers to accessing quality and relevant learning for children inside Syria are complex and include a wide range of protection and socioeconomic issues, extending beyond the education sector. The war in Syria has disrupted the nurturing care environment and has exposed children to repeated violence, displacement, loss, and separation from family. The consequences have been devastating as children experiencing harsh living conditions, lack of quality food, increased militarization, early and forced marriage, early motherhood, ended up with short- and long-term damage at physical, social and psychological levels. ِAt the end of 2019 in northern Syria revealed that 17% of the students do not feel safe in schools and 91% of schools do not have psychological specialists to work with children. Moreover, 18% of students have difficulties concentrating, 14% have very low marks at school, and 11% suffer from nervousness and agitation(ACU, Northern Syria schools report, 2019)

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

According to the Education Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) Matrix at the education guidance package, WATAN will mitigate the risks of violence and abuse and ensure child protection through mitigation procedures. All workers in schools (teaching, managerial and service staff) will sign in:1- Code of Conduct, that ensures enrolling vulnerable girls and disabilities. 2- Childe protection documents that ensure all rules of dealing with children and clarify all aspects of abuseOur MEAL Dept will:1- Pay a visit to schools and ensure the safety of children and staff.2- All beneficiaries will be able to contact WATAN through complaint and suggestion email and phone number to follow up on all complaints.3- All feedbacks from the community will be discussed in the monthly meeting

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

According to the Education Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) Matrix at the education guidance package, WATAN through conducting this project considers three main domains:1- Context: Project location in a relatively safe area because of its closeness to the Turkish border, so the probability of safety threatens is low. For emergency cases WATAN will provide all schools with:- Fire extinguishers - First aid kits - Evacuation plans To ensure the safety of schools and staff inside the project location.2- Coordination: Project will coordinate with two main entities:- Local authorities and other organizations: WATAN will sign an MoU with ED to clarify targeted schools, planned activities, and interventions and take the Authorities’ recommendation into account to achieve project target and community benefits. All working organization is related to ED and they organize a monthly meeting to update work in the area and discuss mutual challenges and opportunities of intervention, so WATAN can coordinate with any of field working organizations through these meetings.- Coordination with other sectors: WATAN is a multi-sector organization and conducted many intersectoral projects and has good experience if the project needs to coordinate with another sector.3- Accountability: Project staff will be organized tools and procedures to integrate three stakeholders in the project activities:- Direct beneficiaries: Any student will be able to share recommendations, complaints or opinions with the WATAN MEAL office by contacting through complaint box, email, phone or interview with field staff.- Families: Every school will establish a Parent & Teacher Association (PTA) that give feedback and recommendation on the educational process and improve work in these schools through monthly meetings.
