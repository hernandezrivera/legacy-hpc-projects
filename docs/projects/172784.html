Project Summary

The Overall Objective of the intervention is to support improved protection and resilience of refugees, migrants, asylum seekers, IDPs, and host communities in Libya. This action builds on the previous programming under EUTF-funded action “Strengthening Protection and Resilience of Displaced Populations in Libya - Increasing access to Primary Health Care for Mixed Migrants and Displaced Libyans” (No. T05-EUTF-NOA-LY-01-01 T05.62), as well as other activities that IMC is currently implementing to respond to the ongoing global emergency caused by COVID-19.
The Specific Objective of the intervention are to support health system in Tripoli to provide quality COVID-19 related services to mixed migrants and local populations.

1 - Needs

The Eastern Mediterranean region has also been severely affected by the global outbreak of COVID-19. Libya has not been spared from this ongoing global pandemic. As of November 27th, over 80,000 cases have been confirmed and 1,153 deaths of COVID-19 patients have been reported in Libya. . It is also worth noting that the impact of COVID-19 in Libya (much as in other countries) goes beyond purely health consequences.Containment measures, such as curfews and mass closures of businesses, have already had an effect on the fragile economic situation, and are expected to continue for at least the next 2 months. IDPs, migrants, refugees, and asylum seekers, as well as people with disabilities, elderly people, women, and children are especially vulnerable in this situation as they are often forced to stay in crowded homes or apartments and have seen their daily work disappear, leaving them without the means to support themselves and their families. This situation is also exacerbated by a constant increase of market prices that directly affects the population. Vulnerable groups such as those targeted by this action are much more at risk. A recent rapid assessment conducted by Mixed Migration Centre highlights that “most refugees and migrants surveyed in Tripoli are worried about catching COVID-19:• All surveyed refugees and migrants are taking extra precautions to protect themselves against COVID-19• Refugees and migrants report a wide variety of barriers to access to healthcare in Tripoli, including lack of information, discrimination, fear of being reported to authorities, or arrest, or deportation • Discrimination was perceived as one of the main barriers to health care and 7 respondents highlighted that heightened racism and xenophobia were impacting upon their daily lives.” Especially for women and children, COVID-19 related measures and limitations in accessing services exacerbates risks of GBV and domestic violence. This has been confirmed by UN Women, WHO, and UNFPA.With the introduction of COVID-19 into Libya, the lack of trained staff, equipment, and infrastructure can add to the difficulties in controlling an outbreak, resulting in high morbidity and mortality associated with the virus. The weakness of the health system and its functional capacity to manage high patient loads make Libya one of the most vulnerable countries to a sustained outbreak, which would have a devastating effect on the population.

2 - Activities

IMC plans to continue providing essential primary healthcare services to migrants and refugees through mobile medical team that visits 2 fixed primary healthcare centers in the Tripoli area. This activity ensures that migrants are able to access primary healthcare even during the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. IMC will also continue supporting Tripoli Central Hospital's COVID-19 Isolation Unit with trained doctors, equipment, and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).
