Project Summary

The proposed action will be implemented in 6 Regions of Somalia Galgaduud, Mudug, Hiran, Lower Shabelle, Banadir), where CESVI has a strong operational presence implementing a broad range of interventions running across different sectors namely, Health, Nutrition, WASH, Food Security and livelihoods. In Somalia, the combined effects of violent conflicts, widespread and severe flooding, severe drought, the desert locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, has resulted in massive challenges in the WASH sector. According t the WASH Cluster, around 6.6 1 million people are in need of emergency life-saving WASH assistance of which 4.2 million will need safe drinking water, 3.2 million sanitation services, and 5.2 million hygiene supplies. 16 per cent of these are females, 64% children and the elderly for 4 per cent. Lack of adequate WASH services to the population is a main cause of ADD (among which cholera), leading to malnutrition in the country.
The proposed action will be implemented in Galkayo, Hobyo, Cabudwaaq, Beletweyne, Afgoye and Banadir Districts, targeting vulnerable populations affected by different cyclic crises. The aim of the proposed intervention is to save lives, alleviate suffering, maintain human dignity. The intermediate outcome of the project is to reduce the vulnerability of crisis-affected people, especially PWDs, women, girls, boys: this will be done by increasing their access to safe water supply, adequate sanitation and hygiene promotion and at the same time contributing to health, nutrition, food security and education outcomes. To achieve this Cesvi will address the immediate needs of the crisis-affected host community and IDP households. The proposed intervention also aims at restoring access to safe and equitable basic water, sanitation and hygiene infrastructures where pre-existent infrastructures are in place and need to be rehabilitated/replaced. It is imperative for Cesvi to meet the needs of all beneficiaries: a disability inclusive approach and disability mainstreaming will be adopted. Gender mainstreaming will be ensured through all phases of the intervention.

1. - Projects activities

Activities Objective 1: • Emergency water trucking with temporary distribution systems for flood/drought affected communities in Galmudug, Hirshabelle (targeting 2800 people x avg. 2 months). Cesvi will use water vouchers where applicable.• Extension/rehabilitation of 5 existing water supply systems in Hiran, Mudug, Lower Shabelle (target:5000 persons).• Construction of 200 emergency HH shared latrines (2 units per latrine) for flood-affected IDPs in Beletweyne. Cesvi will consider using a mixed CfW + build approach (4000 people).• Distribution of HKs inclusive of HWTSS and MHM (2 months min.), for 1500 HHs all the targeted locations. Cesvi will consider supporting the local market through vouchers (involving local vendors)/cash assistance to purchase the WASH items. • Organization of 20 Hygiene Promotion and/or cleaning campaigns (solid waste removal/disposal) in the 4 targeted regions. CfW modalities will be considered (for 6090 people). Activities Objective 2: • Rehabilitation of 3 Shallow wells, as per cluster standards, in Afgoye District with also water tanks for water storage during periods of scarcity/contamination (targeting 1200 people). • Rehabilitation of 4 BHs with full compliance to cluster guidelines (1 in Cabudwaq, 2 in Afgooye, 1 in Beletweyne districts), with solar system, elevated water tanks, pipeline extensions, water kiosks (3000 people); • Improvement of 1 berkads for harvesting/storing storm water, repair of 1 water point (desalination system) in Beletweyne district (for 500 people). • Reinforcement of the existing water committees in the targeted locations to ensure proper maintenance of water services; In Beletweyne District, the DRR component in flood/drought prone areas will be reinforced. Women participation will be reinforced to guarantee decision-making and responsibility for water are equally shared by women and men (104 people). • Construction/Rehabilitation of gender sensitive latrines in 3 schools in Galkayo district, and 7 health care facilities (Banadir/Afgoye/Beletweyne, Cabudwaq districts)-target: 1410 people. • Construction of 200 HH shared latrines (2 units/latrine) IDP camps in Galkayo, Beletweyne, Banadir. Under Obj.1 and 2: all latrines/water points will be at a reasonable walking distance. All latrines will be built with solid walls, with locks at doors, will have light, will be separated by gender (or alternate options if relevant). HH will be consulted in the design, locations, delivery of water/sanitation services, considering ageing and disability as a vulnerability as well. Activities Obj. 1 and 2: • Organization of Hygiene promotion campaigns in the targeted locations, also entailing a component on Co-Vid prevention with the installation of handwashing facilities (+ water provision, soap distribution). Cesvi will conduct HP sessions also through the health/nutrition teams working in 3 static facilities and 12 mobile clinics in the targeted locations. Crosscutting: all categories/layers of the population will be involved in all phases of the intervention (needs assessments, consultations, design, implementation, evaluation). Consultation with women, men, PWDs, elders, marginalized groups will be organized. Boys/girls will also be encouraged to contribute, in accordance with their age and maturity. A gender sensitive, participatory approach will be adopted at all stages of the intervention to ensure that adequate and efficient support is provided by equally involving women, girls, boys, men of all ages in the project design. Disability will be mainstreamed through the entire intervention. Cesvi will consult with PWDs and will encourage their participation in all decision making processes. WASH related information will be in formats that PWD can understand/access. The WASH facilities will be designed and constructed after consulting with different groups, among which PWDs, and according to the principles of universal designed so they can be used by all.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In the proposed intervention, the voucher will not replace all traditional WASH activities but will be used to complement in-kind activities. The impacts of the different crisis have increased the demand of the populations in regards to WASH goods and services and at the same time affected the supply of the commodities in some locations. Therefore, using modalities such as Vouchers Assistance (CVA) to implement Market-based programming (MBP) approaches have the potential to re-build the WASH market systems and increase the purchasing power of the affected population.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

A market analysis alongside traditional WASH assessments will be performed to inform an intervention that promotes a people centered approach as well as economic recovery. Cesvi actively participated in the JMMI conducted in August 2020 and lead by REACH. The results of the initiative informed the proposed intervention. Moreover, Cesvi will continue monitoring and assessing market functionality in the proposed areas of intervention.Cesvi CVA’s component will be based on constant update of the market assessment and response analysis. The CVA component will be adopted in the assistance to the affected population. A mixed approach will be adopted: CVA + in-kind or CVA + direct building, according to the situation on the ground. Where possible, Cesvi intends to provide HHs with CVA, through e-cash or e-vouchers modalities (according to the situation), to purchase water and access sanitation materials. Where possible, Cesvi will give priority to CFW for latrines construction/rehabilitation and for HP activities such as the cleaning campaigns. All NFIs and/or hygiene services, where distributed in in-kind, will be procured from the local markets. More specifically and as mentioned in the Activity section Cesvi is planning to implement the following activities through a mix approach (: CVA + in-kind or CVA + direct building) : Emergency water trucking, construction of emergency HH shared latrines for flood-affected IDPs, distribution of hygiene kits inclusive of HWTSS, organization of 20 cleaning campaigns (Solid waste removal and disposal). Cesvi will coordinate and report to the Cash Working Group at National and local levels, and with the WASH Cluster through the Cash 3W matrix. Cesvi has an extensive expertise in cash based interventions in Somalia, having received funds, since 2013, from DFiD, UK- Aid, SHF, DEVCO, AICS and WFP dedicated to cash based activities (UCTs, vouchers, Cash for Work). Cesvi has assisted more than 100,000 vulnerable persons, both in rural and urban contexts, with Cash Transfers during the various emergencies that hit Somalia after the 2011 famine. Cesvi is part of the Building Resilient Communities in Somalia (BRCiS) consortium, a well-known actor in cash based interventions. Cesvi is also an active member of the Somalia Cash Working Group. Key staff have been trained on Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) at different levels (internal level, consortium level or by the Cash Learning Partnership (CaLP).

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1) Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: MM1: Cesvi will ensure the community engagement of all groups during all phases of the project, trough participation and inclusion of all categories and layers of the population . An inclusive community and authority consultations will be carried out in order to identify vulnerable, under-represented HHs, with specific attention to age, gender, minorities, disability, and clan representation. Moreover, marginalized groups, PWDs, women, boys and girls will be by default among the prioritized groups. MM2: a clear strategy for reaching marginalized communities will be drafted. It will be based on Cesvi’s Country Strategy and will be tailored on the proposed intervention. MM3: The complaint and feedback mechanisms will be strengthened in order to identify any condition of exclusion or any issue encountered by the most vulnerable groups. Awareness-raising sessions on Cesvi CFM will be organized in order to reach every group in the targeted areas. Information will be communicated through various means to reach the broader community. 2) Risks related to PSEA: Cesvi has established a formalized system with clear reporting channels (for staff and external stakeholders), including the presence of thematic Focal Point (fraud and corruption, safeguarding, code of conduct) both at HQ and at Country level. This system is presented to partners and shared with them all through dedicated training sessions. All formal agreements (normally MoUs) Cesvi stipulates with partners, include specific provisions regarding the full respect and application of internationally recognized standards and of the principle set in Cesvi policies, specifically for the partners who don’t have their own policies on the matters, which can directly apply Cesvi’s ones. Depending on the results of the Partner Assessment performed before entering into a formal partnership, Cesvi and the partner draft a capacity building path, which may include also tailor made trainings or –depending on the situation – also the development of internal tools/policies and procedures for the partner. As far as the safeguarding measures are concerned, Cesvi developed a full set of tools for accompanying partners in the development of their own safeguarding system based on the findings of a safeguarding risk assessment analyzing organizational strengths and vulnerabilities and activities implemented by the partner. Thanks to training and back-up by the senior specialist in the HQ, partner organizations are supported in developing needed policies and procedures and in integrating safeguarding measures in their systems. Specifically, on Children safeguarding, Cesvi developed a multilingual child friendly version of its Child Safeguarding policy. Complaints & Feedback Mechanism (CFM) plays a crucial role in the incorporation of anti-corruption, fraud and PSEA in the partnerships and throughout the entire project. Cesvi has a dedicated guideline for CFM, leading each project to design the most appropriate mechanism – context and project specific – while establishing minimum requirements. These are included in the MoU with each partner. Depending on the results of partner assessment, a different solution may be agreed, from the development of a joint system, to partner dedicated system.
