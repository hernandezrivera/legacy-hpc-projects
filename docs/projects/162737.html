Project Summary

In 2018 Simple Spatial Surveying Method (S3M) survey has shown high levels of stunting (46.59%), high levels of acute malnutrition (2.95% SAM and 16.13% GAM) among children 6 – 59 months of age, and poor IYCF practices with only 21.97% of children receive good IYCF in Rural El Fasher locality. Only 1.80% of pregnant women received Fefol for 6 months…….according to the S3M survey 2018.
ZDPO with support from UNICEF has implemented twelve months PCA in Korma Admin Unit covering12 village councils aiming at improving nutritional status and management of severe acute malnutrition of under five children and increase community awareness on breastfeeding, Infant and Young Child Feeding practices, hygiene and child care practices, the project was implemented with quality meeting target with 27,360under five children were screened for malnutrition, all SAM children (1,758) were admitted and received treatment in the OTPs and 1593 in TSFPs centers.
Moreover, the targeted locations in Rural El Fasher locality are characterized by high density of population, cluster of villages around each other; 21,745 persons, IDPs, 10,000 are returnees and 58289 are resident’s.
. Moreover the partner implemented Infant and Young Child feeding activities (breast feeding counseling, health and hygiene education awareness raising through setting up of 2 breast feeding corners where 12 women support groups were initiated and accordingly 313 session conducted with a total of 6704 beneficiaries. The fragile situation of these communities call for more need to continue this project.
This program will treat children suffering from acute malnutrition (severe with support from UNICEF and WHO will support the inpatient care treatment while the moderate malnutrition will be supported by WFP. In addition to the project will implement interventions to prevent malnutrition in the same populations.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition being underlying contributing factor of child deaths mortality. in 2013 S3M survey has shown high level of stunting (17.16%), highlevel of acute malnutrition (2.77%)SAM and (21.46%) GAM among children 6-59 months of age, and poor IYCF practices with only (25.1%) of children receive good IYCF, only (10.9%) of pregnant women received fefol during xim months of pregnancy, while sanitation facilities has been reported in (22.0%) of households and hand washing was practiced in average in 3 out of 5 critical times. Maternal malnutrition was high at (17.5%) according to the S3M survey Physiological needs of pregnant and lactating women make them more prone to under nutrition. If not prevented and treated, maternal under-nutrition lead to negative affect on fetal growth and development and low birth weight. Maternal under-nutrition contribute in an increased morbidity and mortality rate among Under 5 years. also this is give negative impact on nutritional during 1000 days. Pregnant and lactating women have additional nutrition needs due to increased demand from the unborn and breastfeeding child respectively.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The local community were involved from the start of the program on the initiation, planning, implementation and monitoring. The community selected the management committees in every village to ensure sustainability and close monitoring of their activities at village level. To ensure sustainability all the technical staff were employed by the government from the respective community, trained byZULFA/SMOH/UNCEF/WFP and currently they are under the government pay role. Moreover they are raising awareness among their community on IYCF activities, sensitization, mobilization and case finding and referral.Currently the local community are supervising the work implementation and monitoring the performance of the OTPs and managing the supplies.train to awareness purpose monitoring mechanism to reflect on the beneficiaries to project implementation benefit.The integration of Child protection in this project is crucial, as they are the primary beneficiary and most vulnerable to the negative impact of the inaccessibility and environmental contamination. The ZDOP programme will follow the Child Protection policies to ensure children are protected and treated equally irrespective of race, religion, gender, age or abilities. ZDPO would follow the Code of Conduct for the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement. ZDPO will during the first quarter of 2019 complete safety audits to assess gaps that might need to be addressed to ensure that children and Disable are protected and included in all aspects of decision-making affecting their well-being.ZDPO will strive to ensure safety, inclusiveness and protection of children in camps or voluntary returnees’ persons and host communities. ZDPO currently create awareness to staff, visitors and other stakeholders about child protection to make sure that the well being of children and disable is secure and their right to access basic services is understood. The ZDPO additionally strives to create child friendly spaces where children (including any unaccompanied children) can come together, play and talk comfortably.ZDPO will prevent and minimise as much as possible any unintended negative effects of intervention which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. ZDPO will arrange for people’s access to assistance and services - in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. ZDPO will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints> ZDPO will support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ZULFA’s core accountability is to affected population especially women and children who are systematically being engaged and benefiting from CERF -led humanitarian aid in Korma AU.They are part of all stages of the humanitarian response and management, distribution, ensuring they have a voice and a hand in the decisions that affect their lives. For humanitarian actors: CERF and ZULFA built a respected and transparent partnership with a great willingness to work with affected communities and especially malnourished children. In practice, this entails including beneficiaries in needs assessments, programme design, delivery, monitoring and evaluation; establishing open channels of communication for feedback and information sharing; and facilitating participatory processes for decision making and mutual learning. Doing so is not only fundamental to applying humanitarian minimum standards and principles, but also a practical means to improve the quality and effectiveness of humanitarian aid and ultimately the sustainability of aid assistance.Do No Harm: Implicit within the responsibility of humanitarian actors to be accountable to affected populations is the concept of “Do No Harm”; that is, ensuring that aid programmes do not in any way further harm or negatively affect the children and women. Doing so is a fundamental element of providing humanitarian aid with respect and in meaningful consideration of affected children and women.Commitment 1: Leadership/Governance: ZULFA commitments to accountability to affected children and women by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into response strategies, supplies distribution, monitoring and evaluations, and in reporting.Commitment 2: Transparency/ Information Sharing: ZULFA will provide accessible and timely information to affected children and women on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organization and its affected children and women over information provision.Commitment 3: Feedback and complaints: ZULFA and CERF Actively seek the views of affected children and women to improve response practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to, learn from) complaints.Commitment 4: Participation: ZULFA and UNICEF enable affected children and women to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence in humanitarian response.
