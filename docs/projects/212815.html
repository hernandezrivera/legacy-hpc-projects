Project Summary

The project aims to bolster economic opportunities and market systems in Tabqa and Markada by supporting local value chains and market actors. Through an Area-Based Approach, SI have already strengthened livelihoods and built relationships with local stakeholders. Now, they plan to deepen their support by enhancing market infrastructure, facilitating market linkages, and promoting entrepreneurship. Additionally, they will improve access to agriculture and livestock inputs, particularly for women and female-headed households, to increase production capacity and integrate markets. Overall, the project seeks to foster economic growth and resilience in Tabqa and Markada.
The needs assessment conducted by SI in November 2023 in Tabqa and Markada provided a comprehensive understanding of the challenges faced by vulnerable communities in these areas.
In Tabqa, a strategic economic location in NES due to its hydroelectric dam, the assessment highlighted significant infrastructure damage resulting from past conflict, including destruction of public services such as electricity, water, sewage networks, schools, health facilities, and roads. Despite some improvements following market rehabilitation efforts, Tabqa still faces critical gaps, particularly in basic services. Furthermore, the assessment identified key economic challenges, including low income levels, lack of employment opportunities, and barriers to accessing financial resources for livelihood activities. The need for support in various sectors, such as dairy and cheese processing, corn drying, and silo rehabilitation, was emphasized by local authorities and stakeholders.
Similarly, in Markada, located in the south of Al-Hassake Governorate, economically, the assessment revealed the decline of poultry farming, a previously common practice in the area, due to the economic crisis and violence. While egg production could be a viable investment to address food security, limited resources hinder households from engaging in poultry farming. Furthermore, the assessment revealed decreased access to economic opportunities.
Overall, the needs assessment underscored the urgent need for infrastructure development, improved access to basic services, and support for livelihood activities to address the challenges faced by vulnerable communities in Tabqa and Markada.
Protection risks analysis and mitigations:
-Abuse and Exploitation (Sexual or Economic) of participation by third parties (community leaders, partners, MTA staff, etc. who may ask for Favours in order to refer them to the services). : Communicate expectations, standards, and zero fraud and SEA policies. Ensure written agreements on engagement. Develop anti-fraud and PSEA IEC materials and messaging . Ensure developing a robust FCRM mechanism, verification exercises and PDMs to identify, investigate and respond to allegations
-Generation of individual and community level expectations when screening and assessing needs: Clearly and repeatedly explain the selection process and ensure proper community engagement in the earliest stages of the project. Participatory approach across the project. Training of staff on expectation management and transparency.
-Exclusion risks: Re-visit communities on different days and times to screen inaccessible households and ensuring adequate sex representation of surveyors when conducting assessments.
-Program staff misconduct and corruption: Regular staff anti-fraud training and refresher trainings. Conduct ongoing staff compliance monitoring and oversight. Realistic achievable targets. Ensure participants can access FCRM channels and staff can access integrity hotlines to report misconduct or corruption. Commit to swift investigation and responses to allegations.

Risk analysis & Mitigation measures:-Stigmatization and/or targeting of vulnerable households: Keep distribution events small, beneficiaries to receive the benefits on different days and times to avoid attracting attention. Request involved actors to avoid revealing wo the participants are.-Threats to staff after selection process: Ensure strong community engagement, and involvement trusted community leaders and authorities. Sensitization of the community about the selection criteria and limited resources prior to the assistance. Establishing a robust FCRM mechanism and when relevant establish complaint desks at community level to ensure providing proper feedback.-Accessibility challenges for children headed household, elderly, persons with chronic disease, women at risk, persons with disabilities: Consultations with the groups to understand challenges and risks, tailoring assitance methodology in different modalities case by case and ensure the timing, location are suitable for each group.-Violence, deprivation of assistance, GBV and other protection risks that may generate as a result of the assistance: Strengthen referral to protection service providers. Strengthen FCRM reporting mechanisms and ensuring adequate and timely response to complaints. gender considerations are crucial in both Tabqa and Markada. In Tabqa, the assessments revealed that women and persons with disabilities face particular challenges due to the lack of access to basic services such as water, sanitation, and electricity in the market. Women, who often play significant roles as vendors or workers in these settings, are disproportionately affected by these shortcomings, impacting their safety, health, and economic opportunities. Moreover, traditional gender roles may limit women's participation in decision-making processes regarding market activities and infrastructure development. Similarly, in Markada, gender disparities are evident in the economic sphere, with women often facing barriers to accessing resources and opportunities for livelihood activities like poultry farming. Addressing these gender inequalities requires not only improving infrastructure and livelihood support but also promoting gender-sensitive policies and interventions that empower women economically and socially, ensuring their meaningful participation and benefit from development initiatives.In this project, SI will mainstream protection throughout all its different activities, thanks to its trained staff, dedicated tools, and community engagement. Interventions through staff trained on Protection mainstreaming, as well as community consultation prior to setting up activities, will ensure that all beneficiaries, from all social and gender categories, have safe access to the services provided.The Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Specialist for Middle East will support the implementing teams in reviewing activities, community analysis and planning with a context appropriate gender lens.
