Project Summary

The targeted sub-districts in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor governorates are on a severity scale of 3-5. The estimated cost per beneficiary is around 24 USD. An estimated 2% of targeted beneficiaries may have a disability. Total funds received in 2020: around 1.1 m USD. The number of beneficiaries reached in 2020: around 44.900 individuals. The project is not funded at this stage. Considering the needs and in line with the first pillar of No Lost Generation Initiative, context analysis and demographic trends, the intervention aims at re-establishing basic education services in the targeted communities, which are return, underserved, newly accessible areas. The Action aims at increasing access to safe, inclusive, and quality education for children and adolescents, relying on WW-GVC expertise in NFE (Non-Formal Education), solid reputation, and knowledge of the areas. The total number of expected beneficiaries is 60,700 individuals.
Firstly, to increase access to education, WW-GVC will implement the following interventions:
-Rehabilitation/equipment of 14 schools and 12 ECD rooms (110 classrooms): rehabilitation works for 7 target schools in Rural Aleppo and 7 in rural Deir-Ez-Zor (14 in total) benefiting at least 7500 students; works include rehabilitation/equipment of 12 ECD rooms benefiting at least 360 children; in total, 7.860 children will receive school supplies/equipment;
-Rehabilitation of 54 WASH in-school facilities (including those in the 14 fully rehabilitated schools) to ensure access to safe water and sanitation to12,000 students; 54 School will also receive kits for cleaning and disinfection of spaces;
-2 Back To Learning (BTL) campaigns targeting 25,000 individuals, including door-to-door visits to identify out of school children (OOSC) and the main barriers to education access; interactive theatre events with OOSC; Focus Group Discussion with community members on the importance of inclusive education; distribution of awareness materials;
-Multi-purpose Cash assistance to families of 50 OOSC with disability for a 9 months period;
-Self Learning Program for 3,000 OOSC;
-Remedial Classes for 3,000 students (RCs);
-PSS events to encourage at least 6,000 children’s commitment to education.
To improve the quality of education, WW-GVC will:
-Train 700 teachers in Active Learning (AL), PSS, Hygiene Promotion (HP), Risk Education (RE);
-Distribute furniture/teaching and learning materials to 14 schools and NFE centers;
-Deliver school awareness campaigns in HP, RE, and inclusive school model for 35,000 students;
-Carry out life-skills development activities throughout SLP and RCs, supporting the wellbeing of 6,000 OOSC/students at risk of dropout;
-PTAs to be established in each rehabilitated school (tot.14).

4 - Needs

Intervention sub-districts are selected based on the Sector’s HNO, OCHA severity scale, assessments conducted by WW-GVC in December 2020 and January 2021 including infrastructure analysis and an evaluation of completed projects, and secondary data (e.g. DoE). Main problems identified: -Insufficient safe and protective education spaces. Deir-Ez-Zor: data show that at least 160 schools are in need of rehabilitation, including the rural sub-district of Ashara. Aleppo: at least 500 facilities are in need of rehabilitation, including the newly accessible areas in Zarbah, Hader and Haritan;-High rate of school dropout: caused by such as a limited number of functioning schools in the resident areas, child labor, presence of mines/IEDs, overcrowded classrooms, low quality of education provided, lack of teachers. Deir-Ez-Zor: the assessment conducted identified 1,570 OOSC in need of support in Deir-Ez-Zor city, while more than 2,000 are living in Ashara sub-district. Aleppo: the assessment conducted by WW-GVC identified 1,500 OOSC in need of support in the sub-districts of Al-Khafsa, Hader, Maskana, Manbij, Haritan and Zarbah;-Lack of gender/disability sensitive WASH facilities and awareness of safe hygiene practices among students, especially in rural villages;-Exclusion of children with disabilities (CWDs) from education, mainly due to social stigma and physical barriers;-Lack of teaching/learning materials and furniture;-Poor quality of education and lack of teachers: Formal and NFE services are weak. Quality of teaching dropped during the crisis as around 800,000 teachers fled or quit their job. Educators are receiving insufficient training to deal with the needs of children.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project is in line with Sector’s objectives 1 and 2; it is preliminarily discussed and approved by MoE, carried out in cooperation with the DoEs and SARC, and constantly monitored by WW-GVC in each governorate thanks to WW-GVC presence in the field with local offices in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor. The MEAL Department in Damascus creates monitoring and follow-up tools to ensure regular supervision and evaluation of activities’ impact, informing possible ongoing adjustments of modalities and contents. In particular, the MEAL team is composed of a MEAL Manager and a MEAL Officer based in Damascus, and two MEAL Officers based in Aleppo and Deir-Er-Zor respectively.Rehabilitation works are supervised by WW-GVC Engineers and assistant Engineers, ensuring that selected companies deliver quality and timely works; possibly, rehabilitations will be conducted out of school time to avoid affecting the regular and safe implementation of classes.NFE activities will be conducted by trained teachers, using contents/time schedules and modalities agreed at national level (by MoE and Education Sector). School awareness campaigns will rely on standard contents made available by national and international duty-bearers (e.g. Directorate of Health, DoH/SARC/UNICEF/UNMAS). Specifically, regarding the school awareness campaign on inclusive education, WW-GVC will finalize a package of contents in coordination with DoEs and UNICEF, based on existing materials –that will be integrated and expanded- approved by the MoE. Across all the Action, WW-GVC will collect data of beneficiaries in comprehensive databases for analysis, monitoring and follow-up purposes ensuring compliance with data protection standards.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Insufficient safe learning facilities:-Schools or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.); Likelihood: Medium Impact: medium/high Mitigation: WW-GVC will rehabilitate learning facilities to ensure that NFE activities take place in safe and protected locations. WW-GVC engineers will monitor the works and ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place. Students are not sufficiently protected:-Children are exposed to harm by school personnel, organization staff, or related entities. Likelihood: Medium Impact. High Mitigation: School campaigns and NFE activities are monitored by WW-GVC and School Counsellors. Pressure on teachers is reduced through measures like reasonable classroom sizes and reasonable working hours. Teachers are provided with training that includes CP contents. Opportunities for children to be alone with adults are avoided.-Children moving to NFE centers which are not close to their home face protection risks (mines, kidnapping etc.) Likelihood: Medium Impact: medium/high Mitigation: WW-GVC will activate NFE programs in school located in villages where children in need are living and that can easily and safely be reached; daily schedule of courses allows children moving only during day light. ensions among beneficiaries or between assisted and non-assisted population:-CASH Assistance might create tension between those who are selected and those who are not. 7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

WWGVC has a child safeguarding policy aimed at ensuring that children’s rights are recognized and respected and that children are protected from any form of violence. The policy contains a set of standards that all WWGVC staff must take into consideration, including:• placing children’s safety well-being above any other considerations• reporting any concerns cases of suspected child abuse or concerns about the welfare of a minor• planning and organizing project activities whilst minimizing risks for children• avoiding culturally inappropriate physical contact with children WWGVC is committed to create and maintain an environment where children’s well-being is promoted in every aspect of its operations. Preventions measures include:• AWARENESS RAISING: All new staff is introduced to the Child Safeguarding Policy.• TRAINING: WW-GVC commits itself to conduct periodical training in relation to this Policy to adequately inform the staff, raise their awareness and enhance their capacity in preventing, and dealing with possible events.• RISK ASSESSMENT: WWGVC endeavors to reduce these risks to a bare minimum. Regular risk assessments are carried out in order to identify, evaluate and mitigate risks throughout the organization’s operations.• SAFE PROGRAMMING: WWGVC takes proactive measures at every stage of project implementation to avoid causing harm inadvertently whilst mainstreaming best practices.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: -Security context is stable-Access to targeted areas is safe for staff/companies to conduct works/activities-If schools assessed are not accessible anymore because of security reasons, DoE will select new schools in need-Schools are accessible for SLP and RCs and beneficiaries can easily reach them-Relevant authorities approve activation of NFE -Local communities and other actors in the areas of intervention are positive with regard to the services provided-Market prices are not subject to huge variations than those already foreseen and materials are available on local market-Covid spread is under controlRISKS:1.Security situation may deteriorate Mitigation:Regular coordination with local partners/international agencies (UNDSS) to monitor the situation; WW-GVC adopts a security plan/SOP in case of accidents;2.Shrinking of humanitarian/operational space due to military operations. Mitigation:policy environment monitored by attendance to Clusters, WGs,coordination mechanisms 3.Demographic changes in the areas of intervention requires modification of strategy.Mitigation: WW-GVC monitors population movements and liaise with other agencies and with the Sector 4.Change in attitude of authorities/local partners toward the intervention.Mitigation:WW-GVC ensures good relationship with authorities,informing them of planned activities. It ensures a transparent and accountable way of working 5.Diversion of aid from staff/volunteers. Mitigation: WW-GVC ensures direct monitoring/evaluation of activities by ad hoc tools (e.g. complaint mechanisms) for each activity6.School closure imposed by MOE as Covid-19 prevention Mitigation:Distant learning strategies arranged with DoEs and ready to be adopted if schools close
