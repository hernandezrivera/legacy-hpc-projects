Project Summary

This project aims to build women and girls' resilience through GBV prevention and response. Specifically, it aims to establish safe spaces with GBV response services, support self-reliance skills, and strengthen social networks within and between host and refugee communities. The SSWG models will vary depending on the needs and opportunities within targeted operating areas in camps 1E, 1W 14, 15, 16 and host community Palong Khali. SSWG response services will include structured psychosocial support services and case management services coupled with GBV targeted community engagement with women, men, girls and boys for GBV prevention and mitigation. To enhance self-reliance skills and opportunities of women and girls, relevant, appropriate and adapted trainings and recreational activities will be conducted. Intervention design will be based on on-going feedback and consultation with women and girls, men and boys, as well as project learnings to date. To support solidarity and trust building within and among host and refugee communities to further build a protective environment, selected areas of host and refugee will be linked to each other. It will integrate adolescent sexual and reproductive health (ASRH) in the safe spaces through key services as per SSWG model (family planning and maternal health services, information and education on SRH and GBV, young mothers’ clubs). The project will be complimented with on-going projects in host communities’ safe spaces and CARE’s on-going programs (Shelter, Site Management, WASH and Host community program) through GBV mainstreaming and integration, with other sectors in the areas of operation.

Needs

Gendered cultural beliefs and norms deprive women and girls’ access to basic needs and services and opportunities for well-being and development. Men have the power and privilege in areas of life economic, political, reproductive and productive spheres at the household and community level; whilst women and girls struggle daily with household work, have restricted mobility, and so access to basic facilities and services for health and most especially SRH much more opportunities for household and community level participation in decision making, and more so, learning and self-development. (Oxfam GA and RCA) The culture of impunity and the risk for radicalization in camps are increasing protection and GBV risks especially to the most vulnerable (Protection JRP 2018 Aug Mid-term Review) Increased mobility then articulated by women and girls at the early months post-influx are threatened by targeted harassment of women and girls accessing safe spaces, by men and boys based on cultural beliefs on gender Centralized and continuous consolidation of power of male leaders in camps are barriers and add risk to ensuring equitable access of services, including to women and men, girls and boys. (CARE staff feedback & program experience).Shame and stigma remain as consequences for survivors who choose to seek help; and a growing fear of retaliation from perpetrators prevent survivors to safely access GBV support and care. In addition to the operational context which expose survivors and service providers at risk to backlash, there still remains a 70 per cent case management entry points gap (GBV SS, GBV case management entry points gap analysis, Sept 2018) which is attributed to the low absorptive capacity for GBV response services versus the scale of the response. Gains are being made in building local resource pools and in improving practice, external factors mentioned above continue to hamper quality and safe access to services. Social and cultural gender norms continue to harm women, girls and vulnerable groups in both host and refugee communities a year after the influx. Project experience shows that while there has been increased uptake of SRH and GBV services, the culture of impunity, insecurity and GBV risks women and girls are exposed to are exacerbating. Community dynamics within and among host and refugee communities have become more complex. Fear, stigma and shame prevent women and girls to access the urgent support and care that they need after experiences of violence. As a consequence, this inhibits them from full recovery, and in further gaining opportunities for self-reliance. Safe access to quality case management services, psychosocial support, life skills and access to capital are expressed needs by women and girls in safe spaces for self-development. Men and boys, local and religious leaders require a more structured engagement for GBV prevention. GBV programming that adapts to the fast-changing context, coupled with robust capacity building, and psychosocial support for frontline workers are essential to effectively respond to this protracted crisis. This project is heavily based on the 1 year project experience of CARE in implementing safe spaces which contains feedback from staff and volunteers, learning sessions and regular consultations with women and girls, men and boys engaged in and with the activities in th the safe spaces. CARE’s feedback and accountability processes across all its programs are likewise sources of key information.
