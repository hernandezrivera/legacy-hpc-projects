Project Summary

SAMS Nutrition program for the year 2020 aims to focus on strengthening lifesaving preventive and curative nutrition services for vulnerable population groups (Children U5 and PLWs), which include providing appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergencies (IYCF) and community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM).
SAMS will work through 2020 on the integration of nutrition services into SAMS supported health facilities, through implementing a holistic and multi-disciplinary program. In order to achieve this objective, SAMS will seek through its funding to strengthen CMAM/IYCF services and improve its quality, through:
- Hiring qualified (Nutritionists and CHWs) at SAMS supported health facilities, and build their capacities.
- Provision of outpatient treatment (OTP) at SAMS supported health facilities.
- Provision of Inpatient treatment (SC) in two of SAMS supported health facilities (including Idleb National Hospital).
- Provision of targeted Supplementary feeding program (TSFP).
- Provision of community outreach services through the CHWs.
- Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers by trained nutritionists.
- Disseminate messages on IYCF-E, by nutritionists and CHWs.
- Establish IYCF corners in SAMS supported Health Facilities.
To complement its health intervention, SAMS will support nutrition services (CMAM and IYCF practices) in its facilities: For the IYCF component, SAMS will contextually appropriate social and behavior change communication to prevent and manage malnutrition of infants, while via CMAM practices SAMS will strengthen the capacity of the health staff to integrate these nutrition services in the facilities. These include screening and treatment of acute malnutrition, multiple, micronutrients supplementation, the support will be through capacity building of health staff and provision of nutrition supplies for facilities in ldleb and Aleppo governorate where the targeted facilities will also be part of the nutrition surveillance system.
For the past two years, SAMS have been applying CMAM and IYCF-E in its supported facilities. In coordination with the Nutrition Cluster and nutrition actors, SAMS conducted training on both CMAM and IYCF-E for medical staff in the HFs (Paediatricians, midwives, and nurses) and CHWs. Within this program, the outpatient departments of the supported HFs and CHWs will provide the nutrition activities which include CMAM and IYCF components such as MUAC screening to children under 5 and PLW in all health facilities, reach the families with IEC materials, provide preventive and curative nutritional supplies to all health facilities, IYCF counselling, and group sessions.
Where possible, a dedicated space will be made available in the supported HFs for promoting IYCF practices, especially lactating exercises. Private space to conduct IYCF activities, particularly for teaching mothers how to breastfeed or manage re-lactating issues, is provided in some health facilities depending on the availability of space. In smaller health facilities that do not have space available, IYCF activities are conducted in reproductive health clinics or labor rooms.
SAMS will continue to coordinate with other health actors through the Nutrition Cluster, Health Cluster, and the NGO Forum where routine data is gathered and shared through the 4Ws and HeRAMS. and the DHIS2 system. In addition, SAMS is also coordinating with other humanitarian actors on field level to assess the needs, strengthen referral pathways, and respond to emergencies, among other ways. Moreover, SAMS will closely coordinate to share information about needs, ensure an effective and coordinated response to new emergencies, harmonize the salary scales and staffing structures across HFs.

4 - Needs

Now approaching its tenth year, the increase continues in both the scale and complexity of the humanitarian needs in Syria, both of its magnitude and severity, where from 1 December 2019 to 2 February 2020, over half a million people were displaced in northwest Syria. 200k of whom were displaced over one week late of January (OCHA Flash Update - 6 February 2020). On February 1, the Response Coordination Group documented the civilian facilities reportedly hit by GoS and/or GoR forces throughout the month of January 2020 which included 28 education facilities, 11 healthcare facilities, 9 markets, 7 IDP camps, and 5 civil defense centers. Most notably, chronic malnutrition remains evident in most sub-districts throughout the northwest of Syria while also on the rise with almost half a million children stunted (low height for age) in 2020 and in danger for losing their future due to hindered learning and physical developments, as well as early death. As per Nutrition cluster needs overview for 2020, at least one out of eight children in Syria is chronically malnourished with this ratio increasing in areas affected by displacements, returns, and over-burdened communities to (1 out of 5 children) whereas stunting in Syria is largely caused by poor infant feeding practices, access to quality diversified diet, and repeated illnesses. In addition, acute malnutrition remains relatively high among internally displaced children and mothers, as well as the population affected by conflict throughout Syria, about 240,000 children under the age of five years and over 50,000 pregnant and lactating mothers, are in need for urgent lifesaving curative nutrition services. At least 36,000 boys and 35,000 girls are acutely malnourished and are at increased risk of health complications, including death. Furthermore, it is the youngest children that are the most vulnerable and affected, with higher numbers of acute malnutrition amongst infants under 6 months of age.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Gender inequality and gender-based violence - Mitigation: Ensure IIYCF programmes have private spaces for breastfeeding mothers and help decrease the risk of harassment or violence against female participants. Train IYCF counsellors on how to supportively engage with GBV survivors and provide information in an ethical, safe and confidential manner about their rights and options to report risk and access care as applicable to their respective role, and ensure referral mechanisms to specialized GBV support, health care, MHPSS, and child protection are in place. Provide a supportive and confidential environment for women, girls and other at-risk groups seeking information about where to report risk or access care for exposure to GBV.Provide nutritional support to survivors, including those who may have specific nutritional requirements for supporting the healing process. Ensure locations, times and methods of nutrition services are safe and accessible for women and other at-risk groups. This includes distance, routes to be travelled, times that are convenient and safe for travel, treatment for malnourished women, adolescent girls and child mothers are offered at the same time as children, avoid long waiting periods for services, nutrition sites are designed to ensure accessibility for all persons, including those with disabilities. Risk: Children are not sufficiently protected. Mitigation: Have Child Safeguarding policies and systems in place and these should be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed. Child Safeguarding should cover all people who may interact with children. Risk: PSEA - Mitigation: Policies should be in place, practised and monitored. Polices should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Policies should address risks at the organizational level and the program level.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Attacks on facilities including airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a health or nutrition worker, a direct attack on a health or nutrition worker.Mitigation: Apply the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities. Continue reporting attacks against facilities through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM), as well as Health Cluster Flash Updates. Provide preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents. Facilities have safety protocols in place. Risk: Explosive hazards are not considered for safer nutrition programming- Mitigation: Select safe areas/sites/buildings/ infrastructures based on information collected, considering that there is currently limited or inexistent clearance capacity in your area of operation. If access allows, mine action organizations can survey and clear sites to prioritize humanitarian interventions. Integrate risk education and integrate a referral system for persons with disabilities, including survivors of explosive hazards to ensure access to required services and humanitarian assistance. Risk: Beneficiary data and other information are not sufficiently protected - Mitigation: Have a clear policy to protect information that may be sensitive or could put people at risk. This should include when equipment, paper and/or electronic data and documents should be relocated or destroyed. Have a clear contingency plan with closure procedures that includes the protection/destruction of data,...etc.Risk: Aid Diversion risks (Cash transfer). Mitigation: SAMS utilizes official channels, authorized by the Government of Turkey, to transfer money from Turkey to Syria. SAMS request all facilities managers to open a bank account in the PTT. Upon receiving confirmation of the opening the bank accounts SAMS transfer the amounts due to the designated PTT bank account.
