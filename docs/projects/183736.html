Project Summary

This project is in line and contributes to HRP Coordination and support objectives. It seeks to respond to the prolonged humanitarian crisis in the State of Palestine affecting the Palestinian by contributing to the protection of children and their families rights and dignity in accordance with IHL and IHRL, and with a cross-cutting focus with other clusters such as health and education. The project will support the implementation of the HRP and is aligned with the strategic objectives of the Protection Cluster as it:
i) seeks to increase the respect for the IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations as well as
ii) support protection response mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict-related violence.
Child Protection is an Area of Responsibility (CP-AOR) under the Protection Cluster, with UNICEF as the designated lead agency. The CP-AOR is an active working group of the Protection Cluster, maintains regular contacts with the Cluster, and contributes to overall needs identification, protection analysis, and coordination processes. The CP-AOR’s role is to ensure the timeliness, quality, and coverage of coordinated child protection humanitarian responses. Currently, the CP-AOR has 16 active members in the West Bank and 23 in Gaza.
This project ensures the effective and efficient coordination of CP humanitarian actors in the West Bank and Gaza, including a common situational awareness and timely, appropriate and effective humanitarian response. It will ensure the regular coordination of CP partners and support a strategic humanitarian response, including strengthening needs analysis, contingency planning, promotion of evidence-based programming, and inclusion of cross-cutting issues.
Gender considerations are an integral part of the coordination and response provided by partners in the field. The CP AoR will notably coordinate the interventions of Mental Health and Psychosocial service (MHPSS) providers in Gaza and the West Bank and support the provision of quality services and referrals with specialized services. This project will ensure the necessary support to CP actors in integrating CP Minimum Standards, including through training and technical support. This project will ensure Child Protection partners in both Gaza and the West Bank coordinate child protection interventions through a predictable, accountable, harmonized approach and understanding child protection needs. It also supports and ensures that there are functioning referral pathways for cases affecting children, notably in cases of violence (including gender-based violence), detention, injuries, but also demolitions, and settler violence response mechanisms. The final aim of this project is to ensure children affected by the conflict and their caregivers are provided with appropriate and quality services, including case management, MHPSS support, and legal aid.
The project also supports inter-sectoral collaboration, ensuring the Child Protection sector engages with other clusters so that CP concerns are mainstreamed in other areas of humanitarian intervention. This is notably the case with the Protection, Health, and Education sectors, via the MHPSS Technical Advisory Group (TAG) or similar arrangement, to ensure linkages and common standards between psychosocial and mental health actors providing services at different layers of the MHPSS pyramid. Child Protection will also work closely with the Education Cluster to ensure an integrated approach is provided, notably for cases requiring a multisectoral response and cases of violence affecting both areas.
UNICEF and the CP-AOR will promote compliance with PSEA principles and procedures. AoR members will be oriented on sexual exploitation and abuse. They will receive information on interagency channels, including SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms, and WhatsApp to report on PSEA.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian protection crisis in the State of Palestine, compounded by escalations in conflict-related violence and the COVID-19 pandemic, continues to have a significant impact on the protection and rights of children and their families. Between July 2020 and June 2021, Child Protection partners documented 1,465 violations, affecting 7,948 children across the State of Palestine (2022 HNO). During this period, amid renewed violence in the West Bank including East Jerusalem, and in Gaza, 81 Palestinian children (including 23 girls and 58 boys) were killed, and 1,248 (287 girls and 961 boys) were reported injured (OCHA). While conflict-related violence has an impact on both boys and girls, boys continue to be at a higher risk of being injured, killed, or arrested. Children in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, have been affected by a surge in conflict-related violence since April 2021, including more than 400 children (98% boys) arrested in East Jerusalem, and higher levels of settler violence recorded during 2021 compared to previous years. In Gaza, in May 2021, 67 children were killed and nearly 700 were reported injured during the most serious escalation of hostilities since 2014. The high number of deaths and injuries among adults also resulted in more female-headed households and created vulnerabilities for families who lost a breadwinner. The recent violence, coming on the heels of COVID-19, worsened the existing mental health and protection crisis for children, exacerbating the risk of psychological distress and mental disorders. 92% of boys and 88% of girls in the State of Palestine already face violent discipline, with 14% of children in West Bank subject to severe physical violence from caregivers (boys 23%, girls 17%). 10% of children (age 5-17) are engaged in child labour, with boys twice as likely as girls to be working and six times as likely to be working under hazardous conditions. The COVID-19 pandemic exacerbated violence against children, child labour, and other protection risks for the most vulnerable children (NIAF), with some 60% of adolescents across the state reporting their mental health had worsened during the pandemic and a reported quadrupling of suicide rates especially amongst adolescent girls. The CP-AOR estimates that 678,000 children (345,800 boys, 332,200 girls) across the State of Palestine are in need of protection and MHPSS services, including more than half of all children in Gaza and 12% of children in the West Bank. Of these, 122,000 children are in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services (2022 HNO). During 2021 consultations, AoR members highlighted the increased need for integrated and multi-sector approaches to strengthen children’s access to protection and MHPSS services, including safe access to services for girls, due to the inter-related and compounding factors driving the protection risks for the most vulnerable children. This situation requires increased protection interventions including child protection and structured psychosocial and psychological first aid services, case management, legal services, and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) awareness as identified during the humanitarian needs overview assessments. There is furthermore a need to coordinate the interventions of Child Protection sector partners, to: i) ensure a common understanding of child protection needs and gaps, including the specific needs of boys and girls informing needs-based access; ii) promote a harmonized approach to interventions for maximum impact, and avoid overlap, duplication, and inefficiency;iii) provide technical support to integrate common standards, thereby ensuring the provision of quality and sustainable services and reinforcing community-based approaches, resilience, and strengthening of local capacities.

2 - Activities

Gender will be mainstreamed through all activities to ensure boys and girls are considered with their specific vulnerabilities and needs and that the response provided integrates these considerations. . Additionally, CP AoR will support partners to strengthen AAP and Community Engagement for feedbacks on project design and implementation. The project covers the following activities: 1. The project will strengthen child protection coordination mechanisms and community-based approaches through: 1.1 Ensuring inclusion of key humanitarian Child Protection (notably MHPSS) partners and facilitate coordinated partnerships with civil society organizations to address the identified gaps; 1.2 Support the implementation of improved standards for child protection interventions. 1.3 Refer or support referral process for conflict-related incidents for child protection and MHPSS responses (West bank) 2. The child protection AoR’s needs analysis will be strengthened through improved trends analysis and reduced information gaps. This will be ensured through: 2.1 Support CP/MHPSS partners in uploading information about reached beneficiaries through the online 5W matrix (Who does What, Where, When, and With Whom) in the West Bank and Gaza, resulting in a quarterly updated dashboard articulating child protection and mental health needs and met response. Data included in the 5W is age and sex-disaggregated; 2.2. CP AoR will support partners to strengthen AAP and Community Engagement for feedbacks on different phases of their projects. This will be achieved by coordinating AAP training sessions for CP AoR partners with AAP focal points, sharing good practices on AAP with different actors, stories from the fields and lessons learns. 2.3. Complete needs assessment of areas with humanitarian needs and develop inter-cluster emergency contingency plans. These will integrate analysis on gender-specific vulnerabilities and gender-specific impact of ongoing concerns such as restriction of movement, GBV, and safe access to support services. 3. The CP AoR will contribute to the improvement of the provision of CP services by supporting the capacity building of child protection, MHPSS partners, notably local NGOs: 3.1 Support child protection partner programming and implementation maximizing intra- and intersectoral coordination based on the minimum standards for child protection in humanitarian action and other child protection instruments; 3.2 In partnership with the Protection Cluster Gender Focal Point, provide orientation sessions for CP and MHPSS responders on gender-sensitive approaches to child protection emergency response and mental health in emergencies. 4. The CP AoR will coordinate efforts in the CP field to ensure preparedness measures for humanitarian emergencies are in place: 4.1 Support operationalizing Gaza’s contingency planning and support the updating of West Bank’s contingency planning tools. 5. The CP AoR will support advocacy and resource mobilization efforts to ensure humanitarian needs are addressed and concomitantfunding raised for programming: 5.1 Coordinate joint advocacy efforts (including donor briefings) under relevant priorities of the Child Protection AoR Strategy; 5.2 Support preparation and distribution of information products on top Child Protection priorities, needs, and gaps; 5.3 Work with the other clusters and sectors to ensure Child Protection is included in other areas of intervention, notably education, health, and other areas of the Protection Cluster. This will be supported notably through intersectoral analysis, provision of guidance and guidelines and training to other sectors, active participation in the West Bank ICCG meetings and processes. Finally, the CP AoR will promote member organizations to adhere to PSEA principles, consult with beneficiaries on PSEA and provide information on available channels to report PSEA such as UNICEF emails, SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms, and WhatsApp.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Child Protection partners through the CP AoR in both Gaza and the West Bank coordinate child protection interventions and ensure there is a predictable, accountable, harmonized approach and understanding to child protection needs, as well as the integration of common standards in line with the principles of Do No Harm. Additionally, the Do No Harm approach will ensure protection and assistance remained principled, needs-based, and humanitarian in character. CP AoR will ensure the principles of inclusion of children with disabilities, gender and PSEA whenever partners design and implement the project. Partners will keep safe and protect the information gathered from beneficiaries and make sure it is accessible with the beneficiary’s permission. When there is a need to share an information, it should be shared on a need to know basis and limited only to the information necessary to enable the beneficiary to get services. Partners will develop or uses feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability to the affected population (AAP) and to mitigate any emerging risks identified by end-users including SEA. CP AoR through implementing partners will continue to receive, process and act upon help requests of feedbacks, complaints and allegation regarding SEA and will ensure to address, refer in accordance with the PSEA referral protocol and SOPs. CP AoR will conduct monitoring visits for safety and quality of services rendered to beneficiaries and will strengthen coordination with clusters and other AoR to share knowledge and practices. International legal standards such as the CRC, and guidelines including Minimum Standards for Humanitarian Action, Guidelines for integrating GBV in humanitarian action, IASC MHPSS Guidelines are followed whenever providing interventions.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The coordination platform supports transparency by engaging partners and holding them accountable for providing services for boys and girls of different ages and their caregivers within the HNO/HRP framework. Child Protection severity and needs for the 2022 HNO/HRP in line with the Joint Indicator Analysis Framework (intersectoral) and complimentary Needs Analysis Identification Framework (child protection), globally endorsed common approaches that consider groups with specific needs. Additionally, the Child Protection AoR consolidates information gaps on existing humanitarian needs based on inputs from protection members. Therefore, the CP AoR is well-positioned to effectively coordinate a humanitarian response to the ongoing protracted humanitarian protection crisis or unexpected escalation into conflict in the State of Palestine by ensuring that efforts of national and international humanitarian actors to protect children are well-coordinated and achieving maximum quality and impact. Considering COVID 19, CP AoR will ensure partners adhere to the ethical guidelines and procedures when providing face to face services and should be in line with the WHO COVID 19 guidelines. Consultations to beneficiaries will be conducted throughout the implementation period either by virtual or any other possible means. Community members including boys and girls, men and women (including people with disabilities) will be consulted using a different tool such as FGDs, field monitoring and visits, and assessments which should be in place through the project design and implementation period. Partners will ensure that complaints, responses, and feedbacks received from beneficiaries should be processed with UNICEF Operational Guidance on Accountability to Affected Population (AAP). The UNICEF Operational Guidance promotes the inclusion of beneficiaries into the decision-making processes and its important in promoting the participation of the project stakeholders and preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). CP AoR partners will ensure that children are protected from abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence by promoting and implementing children safeguarding policies. CP AoR will ensure capacity building is provided to the staff of the implementing partners so that they are able to protect children. Children with specific protection needs will be given a priority and CP AoR partners will facilitate their access to services, and identify the possible barriers that will need to be addressed. UNICEF’s child safeguarding policy and strong internal control system in place to combat the PSEA against children including other beneficiaries. In addition, CP AoR partners will ensure staff code of conduct which stipulated how their organizations should conduct themselves with interacting with children and beneficiaries in which each staff members has to sign the code of conduct and the child safeguarding policy when signing their contract, and each individuals staff member are aware of it consequences when not adherence to the policies. CP AoR partners will ensure to conduct awareness raising and community sensitization sessions during the project implementation period. Awareness raising will be carry out to enhance better understanding of child protection related risks. CP AoR partners will coordinate internally to improve the flow of information sharing. The project will identify and address the heightened risks faced by people with disabilities in humanitarian contexts with considerations for intersectionality of age gender, socio-economic factors and other diversities. Organizations of persons with disabilities and older persons where they exist will be engaged in identifying these barriers.
