Project Summary

COOPI has articulated the following objective for 2022: to provide equitable access to basic services and assistance for new and protracted refugees (with particular attention to children) displaced in North Darfur, Gedaref and Khartoum States, in WASH, Shelter/NFIs, Energy & Environment and Livelihoods & Resilience, strengthening self-reliance and promoting opportunities for resilience in line with humanitarian standards.
As a cross cutting issue, refugee protection but environmental protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle. Protecting the environment provides better protection for people – not only for those displaced but also for the communities so generously hosting them.
This multi-sector program will consider support to refugees but to hosting communities in order to promote acceptance and integration process through targeting the most disadvantage Host Community members by estimated percentage of 20%.
This project intends to provide Integrated Multi-Sectoral life-saving assistance in the sectors of FSL, WASH and Shelter/NFIs with particular attention to Energy and Environment actions. Particularly:
- to provide timely life-saving assistance and to improve vulnerable people’s access to livelihoods and life-sustaining basic services carrying out vocational training to people with special needs and supporting their families with cash and/or in-kind assistance;
- to provide access to basic water services (including quality monitoring) and to adequate, protected, and gender sensitive sanitation services always complemented with hygiene promotion;
- to promote healthy living conditions targeting their close environment through disposing solid waste and supporting its management, intervening to drain stagnant water and establishing vector control systems;
- to improve living conditions through the provision of timely ES/NFI assistance and supporting the transition to sustainable and appropriate shelter durable solutions;
- to mitigate the impact of future hazards through the prepositioning of life saving NFIs and supporting vulnerable households to build safer shelter, and community sensitization of the local risks and hazards explaining to vulnerable communities how they can manage these risks better to mitigate the impact of floods and fire.
- Enhance preparedness and contingency plans to adapt to different scenarios and shocks

1 - Needs

Sudan hosts one of the largest South Sudanese refugee populations, with 786,000 registered refugees, and a total of 1.1 million refugees including populations from Eritrea, Syria, Ethiopia and the CAR. In Khartoum alone, approximately 300,000 refugees are hosted. In Al Lait in North Darfur, almost 30,000 South Sudanese refugees continue to reside. Since October 2020, over 60,000 Ethiopian refugees reside in camps in Gedaref affected by the Tigray crisis.Data from the Basic Needs and Vulnerability Assessment for Refugees Hosted in Sudan (September 2021) shows the continued state of high vulnerability of refugees across the country.SHELTER: Appr. 75% of refugees in Khartoum, Gedaref and North Darfur experience high or extreme shelter and energy vulnerability. Refugees continue to reside in improvised/makeshift shelter in Khartoum (combination of plastic sheets, burlap, branches and bamboo), and in emergency shelter in the camps in Gedaref. Access to energy sources in poor, and with rising fuel prices making generators too expensive to operate, the need for solar solutions is high. The refugee crisis in Khartoum is a protracted one, with many living in camp settings in low-lying informal settings (Open Areas) in the periphery, which are extremely prone to flooding during the rainy season, leading to the annual destruction of thousands of shelters.WASH: In Khartoum, North Darfur and Gedaref, refugees experience high levels of WASH insecurity across the sub-sectors including latrine adequacy, access to clean water, handwashing facilities and access to waste disposal. In the communities of intervention, refugees do not have access to hand pumps or water-yards, as hand-washing facilities are non-existent or out-of-service. As a result, communities are forced to buy water, leading to negative coping mechanisms as a result of high prices. Communicable disease outbreaks are proliferating at the same time, with water and vector-borne diseases leading to high mortality and morbidity, particularly among new-borns.FOOD SECURITY / LIVELIHOODS: Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Sudan with increased and protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the high inflation rate. Household income levels among refugees are low, particularly primary educated, single-headed households often have a monthly income of below 45 USD. The need for further skills training and sustainable employment/business opportunities for refugees remains high. Overview of the project as a result of the needs:This project aims to respond to identified needs shared by sectoral clusters and several assessments conducted in the areas where COOPI is currently operating implementing funded projects. This proposal will assist 2,240 affected population in FSL solutions, 36,000 people living in communities targeted with WASH interventions and 4,100 people targeted with shelter assistance.Children and women (75% of the refugee population), particularly female-headed households and UASC/child-headed households, as well as people living with disabilities (15% of the population) continue to make up the most crisis-affected populations. Living mostly in in-camp/camp-like settings in the areas of intervention, there is a high need to improve water access and sanitation services and awareness (including latrine access, solid waste management, hygiene promotion) to control the spread of communicable diseases, upgrade emergency shelter solutions to ensure dignity and reduce the risks of flooding, as well as ensure skills training and livelihood opportunities to reduce overall levels of vulnerability in protracted crisis.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

COOPI promotes environmentally friendly and risk avert mechanisms in its WASH and shelter/NFIs response projects and livelihoods diversification interventions do have impact on the health of the public, environment and natural resources. Interventions to preserve and protect the environment will be included from the design phase, implementation and monitoring of each program intervention. The mitigation measures could include but not limited to: analysis of the potential impact of key interventions and discussion with community leaders and sector experts; regular joint water and sanitation facilities monitoring and supervision; emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmental awareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners; solid and liquid waste management; actions to avoid water pollution due to excavation, backfilling and concreating, hauling and damping of earth materials; controlling formation of stagnant water pool to avoid soil erosion and breeding mosquitoes. The Water User Committees shall be trained on water conservation to promote prudent use of water resources. Additionally, COOPI staff will work closely with the community to ensure that local materials used in construction are sources sustainable and where possible use of by-products of agriculture such as harvested crop stocks and other by products are reused in construction of latrine superstructures. Youth, women, men and others will be organized into water users’ committees, hygiene promoters, natural resource management groups, shelter committees where appropriate and tailored training will be provided to contribute positively to the management of natural resources and peaceful co-existence. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’s economic development and social cohesion; COOPI will focus on ﬁnding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, able to scaled, environmentally sustainable and climate smart.The project activities will take into account impact on environment and local resources by attempting to monitor the over-use of material (e.g., construction material, water, etc.) as well as the waste production after kits' distribution and other activities Furthermore, COOPI will continue monitoring markets and opt for purchase of locally available material wherever is possible.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

COOPI adheres to the Core Humanitarian Standards in all programmes and ensures Protection and the avoidance of negative impact (CHS 3) in integrated in project design and implementation. COOPI has in place and organizational safeguarding policy for all staff and local partners. In addition, COOPI mainstreams protection into all programs so that activities (i) prioritize safety, dignity and access, in addition to avoiding causing harm, (ii) ensure equality and meaningful access, (iii) include mechanisms for beneficiary accountability, and (iv) emphasize participation and empowerment to meet SHF minimum requirements. This is mainstreamed through awareness sessions, training, feedback mechanism, and follow up. For example, where COOPI will implement shelter assistance on provision of durable solutions, a minimum due diligence tools will be set in place in order to check land tenure or ownership and prevent further risk of eviction or conflict. In addition, participatory consultations and feedback systems are set up to ensure the intervention is appropriate and does not exacerbate underlying issues or conflict, nor causes this. In line with the SPHERE standards, the project will ensure that targeted beneficiaries have safe access to assistance and services based on need and without discrimination. Specific emphasize is put on protection from GBV and CP risks, through conducted gender- and age-specific assessments and including different age and gender groups in project design and implementation. Efforts will also be made to accommodate people with specific needs, ensuring access to services. The roll-out of Protection policies is the responsibility of COOPI Sudan's Protection Focal Person. All Project Management Units are expected to get trained and apply such policies in the day-to-day work. Local partners - which are mandatory for INGOs according to HAC regulations - will also be selected based on existing policies, signatory to COOPI’s safeguarding standards and trained on Safeguarding and Protection.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Community Participation: The project adapts a community-based approach where communities are involved in the design and implementation of activities. The project will be designed in a manner that humanitarian assistance will be provided in line and in proportion to the needs identified and documented in targeted areas, in adherence with SHF guidelines. COOPI will involve the beneficiaries and respond to their feedback when planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating its program. COOPI’ accountability includes four pillars i.e. risk management, capacity assessment and monitoring the performance of implementing partners, planning, monitoring and reporting, and project and partner auditing. COOPI will provide space for communities to shape their own recovery and for COOPI and partners to better deliver on commitments to stakeholders, including the people COOPI serves and the resource partners who make assistance possible. COOPI will ensure timely information sharing among stakeholders regarding targeted communities. COOPI will focus on various approaches to collect information for community-based indicators. Feedback and Complaint Mechanisms: COOPI will establish effective feedback mechanisms and a Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) so that COOPI and partners can incorporate feedback and address problems rapidly, including for sensitive issues like protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by humanitarian or development workers. COOPI and partner staff will be trained and oriented on SEA. Two community accountability focal persons will be selected from each target community (a woman and man) and trained to facilitate the process. In line with COOPI’ Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) policies and procedures, COOPI will consult communities in establishing an appropriate feedback and response mechanism to give each community the opportunity to identify and report issues on program quality and protection concerns. The system will address both sensitive and non-sensitive issues while upholding confidentially and non-retaliation principles. Specifically, COOPI will link project beneficiaries with existing feedback mechanisms in the area for formats like: (1) an existing Hotline establish by COOPI Sudan for all projects, where beneficiaries can make free calls to interact with MEAL Coordinator for any feedback/complaint and receive a response in immediate timeframe; and (2) Active outreach to project beneficiaries by the MEAL Officer, who will take findings from random beneficiary satisfaction visits to regular program management meetings, or (3) suggestion boxes to offer confidential feedback as appropriate.
