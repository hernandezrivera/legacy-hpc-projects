Project Summary

This project is designed to improve lifesaving and equitable access to nutrition information and services to address malnutrition for vulnerable children under 5 years, under 2 years, and PLW among IPDs, returnees, and host communities and build resilience among girl, boys and PLWs through multi sectoral preventive response. The total target beneficiaries are 240,000 individuals (47039 men, 48960 women, 73441 girls, 70560 boys). To reduce catastrophic levels of acute malnutrition, CARE will operationalize Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Supplementary Therapeutic Programs (STP) centers, mobile nutrition sites, and Stabilization Centers to offer improved lifesaving preventative and curative nutrition services to malnourished and acutely malnourished children. Through an improved and effective referral pathway, Children under 5 and children under 2 with acute malnutrition, and malnourished children with medical complications will be further referred and admitted receiving quality inpatient care in the stabilization centers. To strengthen the link between target communities and the integrated health and nutrition facilities, community outreach will be in engaged in delivery of this programme. Mother support groups (MSGs) and community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) will be trained to improve Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and provide consoling to caregivers addressing the rout causes of malnutrition and improving children and PLW nutritional status. Also, these groups will raise community awareness through home visits for screening and active case finding and deliver integrated health and nutrition social behavior change (SBCC) approach. Also, nutrition workers and community nutrition volunteers will be trained on (CMAM), PSEA, sensitization protocols and GBV risk mitigation through screening, referral, and treatment. Nutrition services will be coordinated with WFP, UNICEF, and SMOH to ensure provision of adequate food supplies, RUTF, RUSF, and MNP to prevent, treat and improve access to nutrition services especially during time of critical food insecurity all over the state.
Under current crisis, CARE will provide Nutrition services to additional 75, 259 individuals (14,470 Men, 15,373 women, 23,060 girls, 22,156 boys) through to provide nutrition lifesaving services to under 5 years children and PLW among IDPs, refugees, returnees and host communities in the impacted States by the conflicts in South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan, Kassala, Gedaref states, Khartoum State and Al Jazirah State through nutrition services.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan is impacted by the pervasive poverty and a structurally crippled economy, marked by chronic and increasing shortages of essential supplies, goods, and services. The economic crisis in Sudan continues to escalate with enormous inflation rates. While now the conflict that erupted on the 15th of April has significantly increase the vulnerability of the population and increased the need. An estimated 19 .9 M people in need for Food Security and Livelihoods assistances. The nutrition situation in Sudan has impacted drastically by the food insecurity developments in 2022 and moreover now with the April’ fighting. The updated HRP 2023, has estimated increase in people in need for nutrition assistance in Sudan total estimate is 4.6 Million people in need; among them 586159 SAM with no medical complication and 97690 SAM with Medical complications. Under 5 years old children with MAM in need for support is 2334809 and 1007752 PLW in need for nutrition assistance. The main drivers of malnutrition interlinked with current hunger crisis, economic depreciation, and funding constraints exemplified by the WFP suspension of TSFP programme which is directly affecting malnutrition cases, especially children under 5 years. Other factors might contribute into further deterioration of the nutrition situation including limited government technical and financial capacity to respond, beside low level of community awareness and capacity such as lacking trained community volunteer groups on issues related to CMAM, IYSCF and SC. Now the recent and ongoing Conflict-induced displacement of households and resulted in increased vulnerability to malnutrition, reduced access to food, lack of water and poor hygiene situation as well as increased risk of infections and communicable diseases such as measles. The deterioration of the nutrition situation has led to an estimated 30 per cent increase in the number of children with acute malnutrition in hotspot areas, a 15 per cent increase in IDP hosting states and a 10 per cent increase in other localities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

CARE is planning to implement through two pronged strategies, first is: prevention strategy aligned with sector objectives of '' Life saving preventive and protection intervention focusing among under-two” through which, CARE will sustain and expand access to quality integrated lifesaving nutrition preventive and protection minimum package services to contribute to preventing malnutrition, reducing morbidity and mortality among children under-two in crisis affected and vulnerable populations including social protection of malnutrition among children under 2 . The secdong strategy is thecurative strategy , which aligned with the sector objective’ of “ Treatment of Severe and moderate among under-fives and PLWs” the strategy includes: sustain and expand theCommunity based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) for children under 5 years, and pregnant and lactating women with treatment of sever acute and moderate malnutrition (SAM/MAM). CARE will provide a comprehensive package of nutrition services including outpatient therapeutic program (OTP); supplementary feeding program (TSFP); Stabilization Centers (SCs) and referral support to SCIn addition, CARE will scal up scale up maternal and young child nutrition (MYCN) and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices in Emergency in impacted areas and population. Moreover, activities will include Behavior Change Communication (BCC); malnutrition screening for early detection of malnourished cases; and Provision of fortified food and supplements to targeted groups. CARE’s referral pathways will serve Children under 5 years with acute malnutrition to be admitted in the Stabilization Centre (SC), malnourished children with medical complications will be further referred and admitted receiving quality inpatient care in the stabilization center (SC) supported by CARE. The proposed activities will contribute in building resilience of girls, boys and PLWs through preventive multi-sectoral response activities such as emergency blanket supplementary feeding for returnees, IDP and other crisis affected people. Community outreach groups will be engaged in delivery of this programme to facilitate the link between the community and this intervention. Mother support groups (MSGs) and community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) will be trained to improve Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and equip caregivers to address the rout causes of malnutrition and improve children and PLW nutritional status. Specific activities will be carried-out by the community outreach groups includes raise community awareness through home visits for screening and active case finding and deliver integrated health and nutrition social behavior change (SBCC) with awareness raising on Covid-19 prevention and mitigation measures. Also, nutrition workers and community nutrition volunteers will be trained on (CMAM), PSEA, sensitization protocols and GBV risk mitigation through screening, referral, and treatment.

CARE has an active sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) mechanism and policy in place for prevention, investigating and reporting. Staff member in direct contact with communities in the target nutrition sectors will be trained and mobilized around issues related to sexual exploitation and abuse, messaging through leaflets and banners using local languages will be made available in nutrition centers for ensuring communities are aware of their rights to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. Complain mechanism boxes will be available and accessible by the target communities in the nutrition facilities. Sexual exploitation and abuse cases reported and closed will be shared with the inter-agency AAP at national level for further analysis and providing collective oversight directives to reduce SEA in the operational areas.
