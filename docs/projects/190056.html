Project Summary

Historical resource-based conflicts - over water and grazing land - have been prevalent between communities across boundary areas between Oromia and Somali regions. Since 2017, ongoing inter-communal conflicts between Jarso/Oromo and Geri/Somali peoples along the Oromia/Somali regional border have aggravated the situation leading to recurrent and massive displacements and populations from both groups fled boundary areas into their respective regions. The conflicts in 2017 severely damaged critical infrastructure (water, health, education) and there have been few rehabilitation efforts since. A series of peace-building efforts in July 2019, involving traditional leaders and government authorities, resulted in the return of over 25,000 Jarso people to Tuliguled/Somali and around 42,000 Geri people to Chinaksen/Oromia. Since then, some improvements were observed but conflict restarted in July 2021 with over 8,370 new displacements (OCHA, flash update east and west hararge zones Oromia, as of 9 september 2021).
According to Multi agency WASH Assessment conducted between Feb.27- March 04/2021 in Oromia region including East Harrarghe zone, the number of IDPs in the region is increasing due to ongoing displacement due to security. Exiting and new IDPs and returnees are in need of WASH services. Moreover, over 2 million people including IDPs and returnees in Bale, East Bale, Borena and East Hararghe are facing critical water shortage due to extended drought.
East Haraghe zone wash cluster in collaboration with partners working in the zone conducted joint WASH need assessment from 8-13 June 2021, to know the depth of the impact in order to advise an evidence-based lifesaving humanitarian response. In all the visited woredas (Fedis, Kumbi, Gola Oda, Meyu, Chinaksen, Gursum, Deder , Goro Gutu, Midhega Tola, and Jarso), coverage for potable water is very low, below 50% at all level while coverage goes down to 35% in some woredas. Huge number of non-functional schemes is aggravating lack of access to water. Women and girls are forced to travel 6-8 hrs to fetch water and being exposed attacking by dangerous animals are reported.
According to the available need assessments, the proposed location is Fedis Woreda, Fedis is one of the woreda in the Oromia region of Ethiopia. Part of the East Hararghe Zone, Fedis is bordering on the southeast by the Erer River which separates it from the Somali Region.
Fedis district consist 19 kebeles including one kebele of Boko town. The woreda population is estimated nearly 133,382.
The project goal is to provide dignified support and save the lives of conflict affected communities in Fedis woreda through water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion and WASH NFIs interventions. The major outcome of the project will be to improve WASH needs of the target people through access to improved and standard WASH services. To achieve the expected project outcomes, ANE will closely coordinate with IRC and consult with the donor, target communities, local and regional authorities, WASH Cluster, UNOCHA, and all other concerned stakeholders during the project life cycles starting from project planning, implementing, supervision and monitoring phase.

1. - Projects activities

Detail project Activity 1.Water trucking for the critically affected IDPs, health center and school. ANE will provide water trucking based on the national WASH Cluster minimum standards of 7.5 liters per person per day. To facilitate the water trucking activities and to solve the water storage problems in the target kebeles, ANE will install 8 fiber glasses (2 at each damping site) with each storage capacity of 10,000 liters. 2. Rehabilitation of non-functional small-scale water schemes. ANE will fully rehabilitate 3 shallow wells at Balina Arba and Umer Kule kebeles in the target woreda. 3.Capacitate local water scheme WASHCO members to ensure local operation and long term (sustainable) use. ANE will facilitate 3 water management committee establishment and training for each water supply schemes (3 Shallow wells) to be rehabilitated. 4: Provision of WASH NFIs the displaced HHs have no WASH kits such as soaps, jerrycans, buckets and basins to practice improved hygiene practices. ANE will distribute basic WASH NFIs to selected most vulnerable 1900 affected IDP HHs at Bareda , Muleta, Dinika and Bidibora Kebeles 5. Intensify hygiene promotion activities to ensure improved hygiene and sanitation practices among conflict affected communities, ANE will recruit and train 31 hygiene promoters for three days and give them the capacity to mobilize women, men and children to take action to mitigate and adhere to safe hygiene practices to prevent the spread of diseases.
