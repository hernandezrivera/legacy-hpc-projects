Project Summary

Northern Bahr el Ghazal State faces severe humanitarian challenges, particularly in Aweil North, South, and West counties. Recent Initial Rapid Needs Assessments (IRNA) from September 2024 reveal extensive flooding and displacement impacts, compounded by Sudan conflict spillover. In Aweil North, 5,141 households (30,846 people) are flood-affected, while Aweil West reports 5,028 affected households (30,168 people). Infrastructure damage is severe: 57 schools in Aweil North are impacted (37 closed, 20 destroyed), affecting 19,837 students. Aweil West has 57 flooded and closed schools, disrupting education for 16,842 learners. Healthcare access is compromised, with 15 of 24 facilities inaccessible in both counties due to floods and damaged roads. Current challenges include overcrowded classrooms, insufficient materials, teacher shortages, inadequate WASH facilities, and increased dropouts due to food insecurity.
The project aims to provide emergency education services across three objectives. The first focuses on access to education, planning to enrol 9,067 (4,715 F, 4,352 M) children/youth (ages 3-17), construct/rehabilitate 9 gender-sensitive WASH facilities, establish temporary learning spaces, support inclusive education for disabled children, and implement outreach programs for out-of-school children. The second objective addresses quality teaching and learning through training 90 (45 F, 45 M) teachers in Education in Emergencies, distributing materials to 6,000 children (ages 6-17), providing ECD kits to 3,000 (1,500 F, 1,500 M) children (ages 3-5), implementing accelerated learning programs, and conducting teacher mentoring. The third objective strengthens coordination and crisis management by training school management committee members, establishing county-level coordination, developing disaster preparedness plans, and facilitating cross-sector coordination. The project is to integrate additional services including Mental Health/Psychosocial Support through teacher training and counseling services, Child Protection through safeguarding policies and reporting systems, and Gender-Based Violence Prevention through awareness sessions and reporting mechanisms. Expected outcomes include increased educational access, improved teaching quality, stronger community involvement, and enhanced protection for vulnerable children. Implementation will focus on school infrastructure rehabilitation, teacher capacity building, learning material distribution, community engagement, protection mechanisms, and cross-sector coordination. The initiative aligns with Education Cluster objectives 1, 2 & 3, emphasizing safe and inclusive education access, quality teaching/learning support, enhanced coordination capabilities, protection integration, and community resilience building. The project is to address immediate educational needs while building long-term resilience through comprehensive support and integration with broader humanitarian efforts working with other actors in the Heath, FSL, Protections(CP and GBV) and WASH Coordinations mechanisms. Key project interventions include infrastructure development, teacher training programs, resource distribution, protection services, coordination mechanisms, and community engagement initiatives. These efforts aim to establish sustainable education solutions in crisis-affected areas, recognizing education's critical role in fostering resilience and social cohesion while supporting long-term development and peace in the region. Through this integrated approach, the project seeks to create safe, inclusive learning environments that support children's holistic development while strengthening community capacity to respond to future crises. Through addressing both immediate needs and systemic challenges, the initiative will contribute to the broader humanitarian response in South Sudan, working to ensure that emergency-affected children and youth have access to quality education

1. Needs

The education sector in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State faces critical challenges requiring immediate intervention. The September 2024 IRNA reveals severe disruption across Aweil North, South, and West counties, with infrastructure damage particularly acute. In Aweil North, 57 schools have been impacted (37 closed, 20 destroyed), affecting 19,837 students, while Aweil West reports 57 flooded and closed schools disrupting education for 16,842 learners. This widespread closure has created an urgent need for alternative learning spaces and infrastructure rehabilitation. Physical infrastructure needs are paramount. Beyond the destroyed and damaged school buildings, there's a critical requirement for flood-resistant construction and rehabilitation. WASH facilities require particular attention, as many institutional latrines are submerged or damaged, creating health risks and barriers to attendance, especially for female students. The construction of elevated foundations, proper drainage systems, and flood-resistant structures is essential to prevent future disruptions during flood seasons. Learning material needs are extensive due to flood damage. Schools report widespread loss of textbooks, teaching aids, stationery, and other educational resources rendered unusable by water damage. This creates an immediate need for emergency education kits, including basic learning materials, teaching supplies, and recreational materials. Early Childhood Development centers require specific materials to support younger learners, while secondary schools need subject-specific resources to maintain educational continuity. Teacher support and capacity building emerge as critical needs. Many educators have been displaced alongside their communities, creating staffing gaps in operational schools. Those remaining face the challenge of managing overcrowded classrooms and teaching in compromised environments. There's an urgent need for teacher training in emergency education techniques, psychosocial support provision, and adapted teaching methodologies suitable for crisis situations. Professional development support and incentives are needed to retain qualified teachers in affected areas. Student support services require significant enhancement. The assessment identifies a pressing need for school feeding programs to address food insecurity and encourage attendance. Psychosocial support services are essential, as students cope with displacement trauma and ongoing stress. Special attention is needed for vulnerable groups, including girls, children with disabilities, and those from displaced families, who face additional barriers to education access.Community engagement and management systems need strengthening. Parent-Teacher Associations and School Management Committees require training and support to effectively manage education resources and coordinate community response to the crisis. There's a need for improved communication systems between schools, communities, and education authorities to ensure effective response coordination and resource allocation. The IRNA assessments also highlight the need for improved education sector coordination and emergency preparedness. This includes developing early warning systems, emergency response plans, and coordination mechanisms between education authorities, humanitarian partners, and other sectors. Better data collection and monitoring systems are needed to track student attendance, learning outcomes, and emerging needs in crisis-affected areas. Cross-sectoral integration needs attention, particularly in coordinating education interventions with health, protection, and food security initiatives. Schools need proper health and hygiene protocols, protection mechanisms for vulnerable students, and linkages to nutrition programs. The assessment emphasizes the importance of addressing these interconnected needs to create safe, supportive learning environments despite the ongoing crisis.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Enroll 9,067 (4,715 F, 4,352 M) (3-17 years) across three counties by conducting community mobilization campaigns, establishing mobile enrollment teams, and implementing simplified registration procedures for displaced students within the first three months of project implementation.Activity 2: Establish 21 temporary learning spaces equipped with basic furniture, blackboards, and elevated platforms in flood-prone areas, ensuring safe access for 7,500 (4500 F, 3,000 M) students by month 2 of project implementation.Activity 3: Rehabilitate 30 partially damaged school structures with flood-resistant improvements and proper drainage systems, accommodating 7,500 (4500 F, 3,000 M) students by month 4 of project implementation.Activity 4: Construct 12 new gender-segregated latrine blocks with hand-washing stations and rehabilitate 9 existing WASH facilities with flood-resistant improvements, meeting MoGEI/Education Cluster standards by month 3.Activity 5: Install water supply systems including rainwater harvesting, boreholes, and elevated hand pumps at 21 school locations, serving 9,067 (4,715 F, 4,352 M) students by month 4.Activity 6: Train 90 teachers(45F 45M) through 5-day workshops on EiE, subject-specific content, and pedagogical skills, with follow-up mentoring support completed by month 2.Activity 7: Distribute standardized learning materials packages to 6,000 (3000F 3000M) children (6-17 years), including core subject textbooks, supplementary materials, and recreational items by end of month 1.Activity 8: Provide ECD kits containing age-appropriate learning materials, play-based resources, and teacher support materials to 3,000 (1,500 F, 1,500 M) children (3-5 years) across all three counties by end of month 1. Activity 9: Establish teacher support networks and resource centers in each county, providing continuous professional development opportunities throughout project implementation.Activity 10: Implement bi-weekly teacher mentoring and classroom observation program reaching all 90 trained teachers throughout project duration.Activity 11: Train 21 SMC/PTA members and education authorities on school management, governance, and financial procedures through 3-day workshops completed by month 2.Activity 12: Conduct climate change and DRR training for 21 school committees, including disaster mapping, early warning systems, and emergency response planning by month 3.Activity 13: Establish monthly county-level education cluster coordination meetings with standardized reporting and information sharing mechanisms by month 1.Activity 14: Create and operationalize cross-sector coordination mechanisms with WASH, Health, and Protection clusters through bi-weekly meetings starting month 1.Activity 15: Train all 90 teachers(45F 45M) on psychological first aid and trauma-informed teaching methods through 2-day MHPSS workshops by month 2.Activity 16: Establish school-based counseling services and safe spaces in all participating schools, with trained focal points and referral pathways by month 2.Activity 17: Implement child protection mechanisms including reporting systems and response protocols in all participating schools by month 1.Activity 18: Conduct monthly GBV prevention awareness sessions for students, teachers, and community members throughout project implementation.Activity 19: Establish school-based hygiene clubs with trained student leaders in all participating schools by month 2.Activity 20: Create and implement monitoring and evaluation systems tracking all project indicators with monthly reporting mechanisms starting month 1
