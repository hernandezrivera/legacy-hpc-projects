1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The refugee response in Mozambique aims at strengthening the protection environment and enjoyment of rights by refugees and asylum-seekers; advocating for their inclusion into national systems and services; ensuring their meaningful engagement in decision making that affects their lives; providing life-saving humanitarian assistance where needed and supporting access to durable solutions. For 2024, over 25,000 refugees and asylum-seekers are projected to reside in Mozambique with the majority (46 per cent) living in the province of Nampula (including 31 per cent in Maratane, the country’s only refugee settlement), 21 per cent in Maputo city and 19 per cent in Maputo province. The remainder live in eight other provinces (three per cent each in Cabo Delgado, Tete and Zambezia, two per cent in Manica and Sofala, and one per cent in Gaza, Inhambane and Niassa). Among them, 39 per cent per cent are women, and 61 per cent per cent are men. 27 per cent per cent are 17 years of age or younger, 71 per cent are between 18 and 59, and two per cent are above 60. The majority originate from the Democratic Republic of the Congo (39%), Burundi (35%), Rwanda, (13%) and Somalia (8%). More than 1,500 persons with specific needs are registered in Mozambique, including single parents, unaccompanied and separated children, children at risk, women at risk, older persons at risk, persons with disabilities, and persons with serious medical conditions.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The 2024 refugee project will target the following areas: Maratane Refugee Settlement, Nampula, Maputo, Cabo Delgado, Tete, Zambezia, Manica, Sofala, Gaza, Inhambane and Niassa provinces The following summaraizes the key activities:- The Government will be supported to ensure the national legal and policy framework is aligned with international conventions and enhance inclusion into national systems, as well as in the implementation of the out-of-camp policy for Maratane.- INAR will continue to undertake biometric registration of refugees and asylum-seekers and the issuance of ID cards.- Refugee status determination (RSD) will be conducted.- Access to civil documentation and legal aid will be supported, including in support to naturalization.- Gender-based violence survivors and women at risk will have safe and adequate access to quality services to respond to their needs.- Risk of violence, exploitation and abuse of children and adolescents will be identified and addressed.- Lifesaving assistance will be made available to the most vulnerable refugees and asylum-seekers, including, nutrition, shelter and CRIs. - Design and implementation of context-specific livelihoods programmes, based on comprehensive assessments of skills, assets, and market opportunities, while strengthening agricultural sector and social and solidarity-based economies.- Access to basic services such as health, WASH, and education will be enhanced.- Disaster risk reduction and emergency preparedness will be strengthened through engagement with the government and communities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection is at the core of all of UNHCR’s work. Through its community-based and AAP approaches, which involve regular community engagement, UNHCR ensures people are at the center of all interventions, providing feedback before, during, and after, mitigating the risk of acting in a way that causes harm or is not in the best interest of the population. UNHCR will work with the refugee community to enhance their participation and inclusion at all levels. In this regard, UNHCR will strengthen the existing two-ways dialogue with all categories of the affected population, including women, girls, men and boys. The following mechanisms are in place and will be strengthened: individual protection counselling sessions, field monitoring visits, monthly and thematic meetings with community leadership structures, protection hotline, complaint boxes and participatory assessment using an AGD approach. UNHCR is also a member of Linha Verde 1458, the interagency humanitarian hotline managed by WFP and has increased its financial contribution to the hotline while sitting in its steering committee. UNHCR will continue to manage the protection referrals from the hotline. In 2023, UNHCR Mozambique piloted participatory data collection through KoBo to improve the systematization of the results. UNHCR will also continue to enhance participation through support to community structures and is planning a leadership free and fair elections, and also the initiation of a community volunteers initiative in Maratane refugee settlement that will help with timely outreach, enhanced two-way communication and a timely referrals of persons at heightened protection risks to appropriate services in a timely manner.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Through strengthened community-based protection, and comprehensive complaints and feedback mechanisms the identification of needs and protection incidents among refugees and asylum-seekers will be undertaken, contributing to a better understanding of protection trends and informing the response. UNHCR and partner agencies maintain a regular presence in Maratane and focus group discussions are conducted monthly. Community engagement with refugees and host communities is at the center of planning and decision-making to build protection strategies and strengthen social cohesion between communities and foster local integration. Through a rights- and community-based approach, community participation is ensured in decision making that affect their lives. For urban refugees and asylum-seekers, protection counselling sessions and monthly meetings are held to identify protection risks and strengthen participation and feedback. Partners engaged in the response are guided by the concept of ‘putting people first’ and drawing on the rich range of experiences, capacities and aspiration of refugees and asylum-seekers with recognition of age-gender diversity mainstreaming.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UNHCR engages and consults people with specific needs, including people with disabilities, through an age, gender, diversity (AGD) approach in order to identify the specific barriers they face and ensure disability inclusion in the provision of life-saving assistance, including in making referrals to specialized services as needed. By applying an AGD approach, UNHCR aims to ensure that all affected populations, including those with specific needs such as persons with disabilities, enjoy their rights on an equal footing and participate meaningfully in decisions that affect their lives, families and communities.People with disabilities are given a distinct voice in UNHCR’s participatory assessments and field monitoring, ensuring they have a say over decisions that affect their lives. During the countrywide verification exercise that was conducted jointly with the Government in 10 provinces from November 2022 to August 2023, UNHCR held separate meetings with refugee representatives to discuss the challenges and risks they faced, building their capacity to identify and refer people with specific needs in their respective communities, especially elderly and people with disabilities. Disaggregated data collected during such exercises includes data on people with specific needs, including those with disabilities, which contributes to informing project design and implementation. In addition, the protection hotline number will be made widely available to refugees and asylum-seekers to enable timely referrals or self-referrals by the affected people themselves. Furthermore, a volunteer programme was established in Maratane settlement in 2023, comprising of volunteers selected from the community with a mandate to strengthen community outreach efforts. Volunteers were trained on identifying and referring vulnerable cases, acting as a bridge between the community and protection staff and enhancing the visibility of vulnerable populations, contributing to ensuring their voices are heard. This programme will carry on in 2024.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Disasters linked to natural hazards, including the impacts of climate, are drivers of displacement. In Mozambique, refugees and asylum seekers reside in locations prone to flooding droughts and cyclones. Hence, UNHCR refugee response integrates environment considerations, including climate resilient shelter construction, latrines improvement plan to phase out pit-latrines and protect the aquifer from contamination by human waste, waste management and community hygiene and promotion and environment protection programmes.UNHCR is also actively involved in the contingency planning under the protection cluster to mitigate the impact of natural disasters, in line with INGD disaster preparedness and response plan as approved by the government, advocating for the inclusion of refugees and asylum-seekers in any government-led contingency planning.UNHCR is also involved in developing the capacity of the government authorities through legal advice and guidance at national and regional levels to enhance the protection of the rights of people displaced in the context of disasters and climate change.
