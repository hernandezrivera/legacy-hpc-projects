Project Summary

Given project is multisectoral and, apart from psychosocial component, includes hygiene assistance (cash and in-kind) as well as cash assistance for food and winterization. The protection component will be implemented by ASB’s local partner, Samaritan Union of Ukraine (SSU).
The PSS component of the project aims at reducing social isolation and loneliness of conflict-affected older people as well as increasing stress resilience of children and adolescents. Project will be implemented in the localities along the line of contact in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts (GCA).

Needs

Since the commencement of the conflict in Eastern Ukraine in 2014, hostilities have negatively affected psychological wellbeing of children, adolescents and older persons, particularly those living within the 5-kilometer zone of the contact line. Older PeopleThe conflict in Eastern Ukraine also led to massive displacement of local populations. Since older persons were not able or willing to flee the conflict-affected areas, many of them are now separated from their families and left in isolation. As a result, social seclusion and loneliness have negatively affected physical and mental health of older persons in Eastern Ukraine. Moreover, a significant number of older beneficiaries have problems with mobility or are bed-bound. According to the baseline report by Help Age International (July 2018), 96 % of older people are experiencing various conflict-related psychological issues, whereas 41,2 % of older people (86 % of them women) are living alone. Consequently, socialization for conflict-affected older people who live alone is essential for improvement of their physical and cognitive health condition. Social interactions with peers on a regular basis will have a positive impact on the older people’s well-being. Thus, designed activities will contribute to intellectual engagement of older people, helping to prevent general cognitive decline, including memory loss, Alzheimer’s disease and other forms of dementia. The older beneficiaries who receive in-kind assistance within the framework of the current ASB project also demonstrate need for psychosocial support. Thus, bed-bound beneficiaries will be provided with psychosocial support through the mobile groups.So, the project activities will be tailored to address psychosocial needs of vulnerable older persons, both mobile and home-bound ones. Children and Adolescents According to the UNICEF report, 54,000 children live within the 15 km of the contact line, whereas over 12.000 children out of more than 19,000 children living in the 5-km zone are exposed to shelling at least once a month. As a result, children living in the conflict zone suffer from symptoms consistent with post-traumatic stress disorder, anxiety, night terrors, bed wetting, aggression and depression, among other things (UNICEF’s report “The Children of the Contact Line in East Ukraine”, June 2017).Accessibility of psychological support is very irregular, especially in the 5-km zone. Even where it is provided by humanitarian actors, the lack of long-term consistent psychosocial support remains a major challenge. Since parents in the conflict-affected areas are also under significant stress because of the ongoing conflict and difficult socioeconomic situation, children’s psychological state is often complicated with neglect at home or even domestic violence (UNICEF’s report “The Children of the Contact Line in East Ukraine”, June 2017). The problem is aggravated by the fact that school teachers often lack techniques on how to communicate with children who suffer from different stress disorders.The above-mentioned gaps in addressing psychosocial needs of children in the target locations can be achieved through creation of the new child friendly spaces or support of the existing ones in the settlements within the 0-50-kilometre zone and conducting trainings on addressing specific psychosocial needs of conflict-affected children for school teachers in the target locations. The local communities of Zolote and Toshkivka (Popasnianskyi raion, GCA of Luhanska oblast), where ASB is active, has requested support in organizing leisure for conflict-affected children and adolescents in these settlements, which are located with the 5-km zone from the line of contact. Due to the lack of organized extracurricular activities, children and adolescents in these and other communities face the risk of overcoming stress through antisocial behavior (alcoholism, drug addiction, etc.).

Activities & outcomes

Based on the primary data from the project “Emergency Assistance to Conflict Affected Populations in Eastern Ukraine” (February 14, 2018 - June 13, 2019, which was funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany and implemented by ASB in consortium with ACTED and SSU, the population profile of 3900 beneficiaries residing in 25 localities within the 5-km zone along the contact line (GCA of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts) demonstrated significant vulnerabilities: • 38 % are older persons;• 63 % are women; • 18 % are children; • 16 % are people with disabilities. The project will be presented in selected localities to the target groups (children, adolescents, and older people), so that any person who meets the criteria (age, need for PSS) will have an opportunity to attend the social centre or (in case of home-bound people and persons with severe disability) be attended by a mobile psychosocial group.The visitors at the social centres will be residents of the localities within the 5-km zone from the ‘contact line.’ A mini-van will bring children, adolescents and older people from the localities in the 5-km zone to the nearest social centre (Druzhkivka, Zolote or Toshkivka). Three mobile psychosocial groups will travel by car to home-bound beneficiaries (persons with limited mobility and/or severe disabilities). Each group will consist of a trained psychologist and engaged volunteers from among youth and mobile older people. Activities (Senior beneficiaries):• Identification of the most relevant locations for the social centres within the 0-20 km zone along the contact line;• Establishment of psychosocial centres for older people in selected localities;• Provision of psychosocial support for mobile single older people visiting social centres;• Establishment of mobile psychosocial groups for home-bound older beneficiaries;• Individual visits to bed-bound beneficiaries (persons with limited mobility and/or severe disabilities). Outcomes for senior beneficiaries:• Improved social inclusion of older men and women;• Older beneficiaries’ well-being is improved.Activities (Children and adolescents):• Identification of the most relevant locations within the 0-5 km zone along the contact line;• Establishment of social centres in target localities (Zolote, Toshkivka and Druzhkivka);• Provision of group and individual psychosocial support for children and adolescents who will attend the centres;• Organization of outbound recreation (e.g. summer camps);• Conducting trainings on active listening, inspiring dialogue and anti-stress techniques for school teachers.Results for children and adolescents:• Children have developed resilience to stress;• The risk of negative coping strategies (e.g. alcoholism and drug addiction) among the conflict-affected children and adolescents has reduced;• Teachers are trained on the techniques of dealing with the consequences of post-traumatic stress disorder in schoolchildren. The project will contribute to the Protection Cluster Objective 1 “Strengthen protection for persons of concern, including prevention and mitigation of rights violations” and Objective 2 “People of concern benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to essential services and enjoyment of their rights, with particular attention to the most vulnerable” through the following activities: • Capacity building (60 persons trained);• Capacity building (6 trainings conducted);• Psychosocial Support through Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), Group Sessions, or Outreach;• Provision of Community spaces for older people and people with disabilities;• Individual psychosocial support for children and home-bound older people.

Indicators & targets

The first component of the project will specifically focus on single, socially isolated older beneficiaries, mostly women. • 85 % of beneficiaries are satisfied with the aid provided• 170 older people accessing community spaces• 100 home-bound older people reached by individual psychosocial support• 80 % are female• 100 % are older people (60+) Within the second component, project will focus on children and adolescents before the age of 18.• 85 % of beneficiaries are satisfied with the aid provided• 930 children reached by psychosocial support activities through Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), Group Sessions, or outreach• 900 children receive psychosocial support through group activities at the youth centers• 30 home-bound children with disabilities reached by individual psychosocial support90 children will attend summer camps• 6 trainings conducted • 60 teachers trainedLocal populations will be consulted on their preferred ways of submitting complaints. Project beneficiaries will be informed about the complaints procedure and provided with relevant contact details. All complainants will be treated respectfully and provided with information on how their feedback was addressed. Post Distribution Monitoring will be undertaken to measure the effectiveness of the programme. All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age and gender disaggregation.
