Project Summary

Context:
The Northeast region of Syria faces a dire humanitarian crisis due to conflict, displacement, and climate change. Over 14.6 million Syrians, including 2.6 million in Ar-Raqqa, Al-Hasaka, and Deir-Ez-Zor, require aid, with 12 million experiencing food insecurity. Recent escalations in conflict have worsened the situation, leading to civilian casualties and displacements, hindering aid access. Climate change exacerbates the crisis, with severe water shortages affecting agriculture, notably wheat production, which dropped from 2.8 million to 1 million tons due to drought conditions. Decades of conflict and displacement have further weakened infrastructure and community resilience, compounding the challenges faced by affected communities.
Needs:
The conflict in Northeast Syria has caused economic decline, unemployment, and protection risks, especially for women and children. Vulnerable families need livelihood support, including training and entrepreneurship opportunities. Women-headed households require economic empowerment to overcome financial challenges. Children face disrupted education and exploitation, with boys at risk of recruitment and girls vulnerable to child marriage and gender-based violence (GBV). Agriculture, vital for the community, has suffered due to drought and limited livestock vaccination.
Aid organizations stress the need for tailored livelihood and protection assistance. Proposed interventions aim to address these needs, focusing on women's and children's resilience, while complementing existing programs. By targeting unmet needs and maximizing collaboration, the intervention seeks to enhance impact and sustainability in Northeast Syria.
Intervention:
Efforts in Hajin target child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) issues comprehensively. This includes revising procedures, training case workers, mapping services, and providing tailored support. Psychosocial aid is extended through safe spaces with trained facilitators. An additional focus is placed on improving health staff and teachers' ability to identify and refer CP and GBV cases through training. Health workers learn Psychological First Aid (PFA), CP, and GBV case management, while educators focus on recognizing CP cases and enhancing communication. Follow-up sessions address any concerns, ensuring continued support. Overall, the aim is to provide holistic support to survivors and enhance community response to these issues. This comprehensive approach acknowledges the importance of both immediate aid and long-term prevention strategies in tackling CP and GBV effectively. By equipping frontline responders and creating safe spaces, the initiative aims to create a supportive environment where survivors can access the assistance they need while promoting awareness and prevention within the community.
In Hajin, women will gain economic strength through agriculture. Two groups will form, focusing on food processing and dairy production. Market analysis and workshops will identify needs and build skills. Training covers production, hygiene, teamwork, and business basics. Financial support aids initial production. Women will manage their centers post-project. Continuous guidance ensures sustainability. Market research will find buyers, and networking events will connect women with markets. Branding support will enhance product visibility. Overall, the aim is to empower women economically, enabling them to produce quality goods, access markets, and sustain their businesses independently.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Extended explanation:The project consists of an integrated approach that links the psychological wellbeing of the beneficiaries with their economic wellbeing which we strongly believe will contribute towards improving the socio-economic situation of our targeted beneficiaries.The project focuses primarily on improving the psychosocial situation of the targeted population through provision of PSS and Case management support. Furthermore, the creation of women groups and building their capacity technically and in terms of soft skills improvement, is a major contributor towards achieving the sustainability of the economic aspect of the project. Introducing the tools and principles of teamwork and group spirit will participate in obtaining better harmony within the group and thus productivity and achieve success and continuity. AFH will also ensure to continue the follow up and assistance of the targeted women group through regular monitoring visits to check the progress of the work and tackle challenges. The findings of the monitoring visits will be shared with AFH management and the organization’s technical team which is present in the area within other projects to provide technical assistance and orientation whenever needed. The AFH technical team will ensure to accompany the beneficiaries technically even after the end of the project’s life span. Risk Analysis:Maintain community acceptance through strong local connections and transparent communication channels.Address tensions between communities by implementing fair selection criteria and feedback mechanisms.Estimate prices based on market assessments and identify alternative fund transfer channels.Implement security measures and provide staff training to address escalated security situations.Readvertise positions and provide training until qualified staff are found.Establish clear governance structures and provide support to mitigate internal conflicts in women's groups. Protection Risks:AFH will ensure to keep the identity of all protection beneficiaries confidential, to avoid protection risks. The hospital and vocational training centers will act as entry points for GBV survivors and CP cases to seek protection support without the stigma that often surrounds these services. Monitoring and Accountability:AFH implements a robust Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) system for the ADH project. It includes data collection templates, third-party monitoring, and regular field visits. Monthly meetings and surveys gather feedback, ensuring project effectiveness. An accountability system facilitates transparency and responsiveness to beneficiary feedback and complaints. PSEAH:All project staff have been trained in Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH); in order to reduce the risk of, prevent and respond to SEA by the AFH staff, or other entities and persons involved in providing humanitarian or development assistance, including contractors.
