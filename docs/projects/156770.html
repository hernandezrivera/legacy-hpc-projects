Project Summary

The World Vision project will target directly households HH, collective centers (CCs) and camps in A'zaz district through providing the following activities:
Sector Objective # 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support
• Provision of core and essential NFIs including new arrival kits (1800 HHs), Kitchen kit (1800 HHs), and Comprehensive Temporary shelter kit (250 HHs) which will target newly displaced people in this area.
• Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (Winterization kits and heating fuel) to 7800 HHs. This plan will be implemented for the coming winter 2019.
Sector Objective # 2: Contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.
• Rehabilitation/upgrading of Life-saving and life-sustaining shelter spaces in unfinished buildings, spontaneous settlements, and/or other emergency spaces (650 HHs). WV will continue to build upon its HH rehabilitation activities into its third year by expanding towards A’zaz countryside and will target the most damaged houses.

Needs

The North-west of Syria is marked by ongoing conflict and witnessed hostilities throughout 2018 including between NSAGs. This has resulted in civilian casualties, damage and destruction of civilian infrastructure including attacks on humanitarian assets and personnel, and waves of mass displacement that continue to have a significant impact on needs and vulnerabilities. There are some 2.8 million people in need including some 1.9 million IDPs, many of whom have displaced multiple times based on SNFI cluster HNO 2019 Aleppo is the biggest government with people in need with estimated 1,130,815 individuals, additionally according to REACH assessments in northern of Aleppo 24% of interviewed persons reported shelter adequacy issues with huge need for support rehabilitation process as most of those IDPs don't have the proper income to cover those shelter needs, additionally most of the IDPs still in need for NFI specifically in winter as a lack of electricity and heating fuel. A Space of Hope (SoH) study conducted in November 2018, provides insight into the main protection concerns and issues in North Aleppo for child, adolescent and women. In the assessed villages, 73% of respondents believe that there is a community committee who provide the basic services (such as water, sanitation, waste removal…etc) and ensure these services are available and functioning sufficiently. However, 23% of respondents reported no presence of this committee in their villages, leaving a large gap to respond to basic needs. Received data from the study also showed that the percentage of IDPs to Hosts was high in areas such as Azaz and Souran, due the high waves of displacement in the last 8 months, adding extra strain to basic services. The increase in population coupled with the unstable situation has also led to increased safety and security concerns, particularly for women, such as sexual exploitation. In the target area, and more generally across Northern Syria, women can be understood to have a subordinated role in society with poorer access to services and resources; and are more commonly subject to physical and gender-based violence and sexual violence (HNO, 2018). Women’s decision-making power is low at all levels, making the exercising basic rights (e.g. access to life saving WASH, health and social services; registering for humanitarian assistance; or claiming their property to secure shelter) extremely difficult. Boys are also under threat of GBV and sexual exploitation and older boys and young men are at risk of being recruited by armed groups and criminal gangs. The children and youth of Syria have also become collateral damage in a situation beyond their control. A report by the Global Child Protection Working Group. the global level forum for coordination on child protection in humanitarian settings, indicated that 98 percent of Syrian children surveyed reported deterioration in their psychosocial well-being and humanitarian agencies are warning of a potential “lost generation”. Further to this, a War Child report writes that the provision of support for the psychological and social well-being and recovery of children – ‘psychosocial support’ – is being overlooked, despite confirmed evidence that psychosocial support during emergencies is critical. In February 2018, World Vision spoke to 1,254 Syrian girls and boys; their stories included war-induced violence, displacement, and missing, separated and deceased family members. Together, the children described material and social stressors that range from situations of violence to daily struggles for basic services: conflict-induced violence, violence from caregivers, unsafe and overcrowded living conditions, working to support their families, and finally, poverty and isolation that limit access to systems of protection, education, health care, and even water.

Implementation of activities

- 1.1.1 Provision of non-fuel core and essential NFIs (New arrival kit, Kitchen kit) which will target mainly the newly displaced people in this area.- 1.2.1 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including heating fuel (Winterization kits and heating fuel)- 1.3.3 Distribution/installation of emergency shelter materials/kits WV will distribute core and essential NFIs to the most vulnerable populations in target areas including camps and informal settlements. Selection and validation tools will identify target populations and their priority needs. Beneficiary lists will be coordinated with local councils and the field team will verify HHs via assessments. Responses will place emphasis on vulnerable households (persons with disabilities, elderly PLW etc). Registration will identify beneficiary’s needs and the response given will be appropriate. To support IDPs arriving prior to and during winter season, WV will provide winterization kits. Winterization kits will be to the SNFI sector agreed standards and will include blankets, carpets, plastic sheeting, mattresses, stoves, fuel and jerry cans. Additionally after assessment and selection, the most vulnerable HHs will be distributed heating fuel. Fuel will be provided to the selected HHs to the sector standard of 4L per day for 1 month. Targets groups will include informal settlements, camps and households who are already present.All projects will be directly implemented through WV field staff who have obtained experience from 3 years of undertaking shelter and NFI projects in Syria. Staff have been hired form target communities in Syria, this builds better local acceptance and increases WVs contextual understanding for essential needs in the communities it serves. The team will consist of engineers, assistants and distribution personnel. WV will have a full warehousing system to ensure all NFIs are stored to the correct standards and accurately monitored. - 2.1.1 Rehabilitation of damaged houses - upgrading of Life-saving and life-sustaining shelter spaces in unfinished buildings, spontaneous settlements, and/or other emergency spaces.WV will continue to build upon its previous work and implement HH shelter rehabilitation projects, expanding towards A'zaz countryside. Where possible data from the previous project will be utilized. World Vision will conduct house-by-house assessments and verification exercises for all target shelters. HHs identified by local councils for rehabilitation will be independently validated by WV’s engineering team. WV’s estimated costs to repair HHs range from $260 to $1500, with the average costing of $800. The demographics and needs of target areas have not changed significantly since the project inception therefore WV will continue to reach the most vulnerable HHs. For each HH rehabilitation the following will be produced; a narrative description of the scope of the proposed improvements in each shelter, beneficiary population census disaggregated by gender and age groups; bill of quantities (BoQ) and photos to illustrate the current status of the dwelling units.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and Assumptions:- WV is not able to mitigate risks around shelling, airstrikes, however WV will assess the capacity of partners and advise appropriately to protect their staff and ensure that people in acute humanitarian needs receive assistance.- Risk of bombardment or other forms of attacks may affect response. - Limited monitoring by WV’s 3PM. Restriction of enumerators access to areas based on political, social or ethnicity.- Changes in the areas of control between Turkey, GoS or OAGs.- Temporary closure of borders for shipments either Turkey or within Syria, may disrupt humanitarian response. - Potential diversion of supplies in areas under the control of armed groups. - Damage of supplies due to transport and handling.- In case there is a military operation and a resultant escalation of conflict, there may be large displacement of people in need of assistance.- Climate conditions may exasperate flooding or difficult road conditions. - Where local procurement is the only option, markets can become depleted - Mitigation Measures:- WV has a vast experience in delivering services and supplies in conflict situations especially areas under the control of armed groups. WV’s partners are equipped with warehouses in strategic locations and have strategic partnership with local councils. - Partners will provide monitoring plans specific operational context. Third party monitoring visits will monitor the progress. Implementing partners will provide situational feedback. Partners will provide monthly input to 4W matrix.- WV’s partners are advised to closely monitor utilization of supplies and to monitor what is out of stock in local markets. - WV and its partners carry out continuous security assessments drawing from multiple security sources including OCHA and has a dedicated security and context analysis team. - WV will advise its partners to train field staff in security awareness and management. - Partner staff “lock down”, restricted movements or suspension of activities can be required in times of conflict escalation. - Partners will liaise with local councils and obtain all necessary approvals prior to commencement of activities. Where possible WV will seek to have LC presence at certain activities for additional monitoring. - Establishing framework agreements with suppliers to secure supply chains.
