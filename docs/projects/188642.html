Project Summary

As reported in the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the humanitarian crisis in the north-eastern Nigeria states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, has persisted in 2021 as one of the largest and most severe in the world. At the start of the year, approximately 1.92 million people were displaced internally. The on-going conflict continues to severely affect millions of people, subjecting them to displacement, impoverishment and threat of violence, and impacts the ability to deliver assistance.
The major aim of the Shelter/NFI/CCCM Cluster is to improve living conditions of displaced persons living in camps, camp-like setting and host communities.
The urgent need for this project is as a result of increasing displacement of populations from other locations/LGAs into MMC and Jere, Fufore, Yola North and South LGAs couple with the destruction of shelter by wind-storm and fire outbreaks in the past few months.
HARAF proposed 12-month intervention aims to deliver life-saving CCCM and Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Item (NFI) assistance, directly benefitting Internally displaced persons in MMC, Jere, Yola North, Yola South and Fufore LGA’s.
Key activities will include Management of camp sites, site improvement and repairs, community leadership strengthening, site planning, Receiving and orientation of new arrivals, and referral for basic services, receiving and orientation of new arrivals, and referral for basic services, construction and repairs of Bama type shelters, Emergency shelter kits, distribution of NFI kits and prepositioning of emergency shelter and NFI Kits.

1 - Needs

The Borno State Government (BSG) resumed the closure of several IDP camps located across the metropolitan city of Maiduguri. In July the BSG relocated IDPs from Farm Center, Muna Garage and Custom House II camps in Maiduguri, the state capital, to Mafa LGA, approximately 40 kilometers to the north-east. As of 16 July, over 6,000 IDPs had been moved with many settling in Ajiri village, Mafa IDP camp and the host community. Over 60,000 IDPs across Dalori 2, Stadium and Gubio camps in Maiduguri have also been registered possible relocations.4 As stated in the HRP, the humanitarian community remains concerned about returns that may not be sustainable and aligned to the Borno State Returns Strategy.Records from assessment reports shows that targeted population for this project moved into the camps from Dikwa, Konduga, Mafa, Damboa, Bama, Marte, Guzamana, Abadam and Monguno and are likely to relocate to the host community in the event of likely closure of the informal camp like settlement in Jere and MMC, the IDP’s have been staying in congested shelters and the situation is getting worse by each day, as some of the emergency shelters are stressed and over stretched. Their NFIs gaps are of 1 year as such making it very more prone for destruction by rain and windstorms. While the recent fire outbreaks in some locations affected both shelters and NFI kits as well. The monthly SITREP shared by sector shows the increasing gaps and needs across these camps and the need for immediate response. In addition, HARAF team have conducted on-the-spot assessment and have confirm that the need for camp site facilitation, emergency shelters and NFIs are on the increase as a result of new arrivals, fire outbreaks in some camps and recently the rain/windstorms. In August 2020, 1,051,953 Households (HH) were in need of Shelter and Non-food items (NFIs) assistance across north-eastern Nigeria, especially Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states. Almost 75,104 HH remained in substandard shelter, including unfinished and abandoned buildings and makeshift, partially damaged or shared shelters and overcrowded camps. Shelter solutions, including reinforcement and rehabilitation, are expensive and therefore remain a challenge for most IDPs, vulnerable host communities and returnees. This continues to raise shelter and NFI needs across various locations. A specific needs assessment has been conducted to discover the need gaps present in Fufore, Yola North, Yola South LGA of Adamawa State and Jere, MMC LGA’s in Borno State. as well as other intended locations., humanitarian needs across all sectors are increasing as a result of the latest waves of displacement and the ongoing crisis, specifically in the CCCM, Shelter/ NFIs, WASH, Protection, Food Security, Health and Nutrition sectors. The fresh population movements and the high influx of IDPs are leading to camp overcrowding and congestion in some of the hosting sites, where capacity has been stretched from 5,000 to 28,000 people in one month. As a result, significant numbers of new arrivals are sleeping outside in the open, without shelter. The humanitarian situation remains critical in northeast Nigeria, with an estimated 7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, and more than 1.8 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs). Adamawa State alone hosts more than 1 million IDPs – approximately 67% of the total number of IDPs in the northeast. This displacement, coupled with, until recently, lack of access by humanitarian responders to areas controlled by Boko Haram, has resulted in critically low levels of essential, lifesaving services. Humanitarian assistance is increasingly and urgently needed as more areas are coming under Government of Nigeria (GON) control, and thus becoming accessible to relief actors. 2 - Activities

Activity 1.1.1: Deployment of Site Facilitators, setup of community-based workers and camp committees; Set-up and maintenance of leadership structures, and community representative groups; ensure that representation cuts across women, youth, Persons with Special Needs, elderly in governance structures, community groups. Activity 1.1.2: Regular intra-camp coordination meetings with different stakeholders and already setup leadership structures inside the camps and ensuring coordination with the Shelter/CCCM sector. This coordination mechanism will be led by HARAF and will involve representatives from service providers operating in the sites and out of camp, as well as local authorities such as SEMA/NEMA, which is the government agency in charge of coordinating humanitarian assistance. Activity 1.1.3: Regular site monitoring and reporting; Develop, update and circulate service mapping tool, the service mapping, or “4W”, is a tool elaborated by the CCCM agency at site-levelActivity 1.1.4: Conduct site improvement/flood mitigation assessment; CCCM team will start conducting an assessment in all targeted sites to identify the needs in terms of site improvement and flood mitigation. Based on these finding, HARAF will elaborate a strategy and plan for site improvement activities such as drainages, pathways, etcActivity 1.1.5: Site Maintenance and Mitigation; Selection and training of community focal points and formation of safety/site maintenance committees; The safety/site maintenance committees will be established by the HARAF CCCM team, and will be constituted by volunteers from the community on a community participation basis and according to specific terms of referencesActivity 1.1.6: Establishing proper referral pathways across all sites to ensure mitigation of risks of protection, GBV, health cases through engagements with Protection and Health actors present at sites of the project interventionActivity 1.1.7: Set up of information centres for all sites, ensuring that the site residents are informed on the assistance and services available in the camps/site and how to access them, including through establishment of information desks and through community outreach, and are aware of mechanisms to make complaints and receive feedback. Activity 1.1.8: Conduct of Complaints and Feedback Mechanism activities through the establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms Activity 1.1.9: Conduct training/capacity building sessions on community engagement, problem identification and solving. Other training topics could be proposed depending on the community structures needs and in coordination with relevant partners. Activity 1.1.10: Awareness raising sessions with IDPs on communicable diseases such as COVID-19 etc, Disseminate information through IEC material (flyers, leaflets, banners, information boards, sign posts). HARAF will print IEC materials harmonized and approved at DMS/CCCM sector level, such as fire safety sensitization tools.Activity 1.1.11: Ensure protection mainstreaming into all camp activities.Activity 1.1.12: Site-level coordination in managed sites to ensure that key services fill critical gap areas and advocates for targeted, immediate delivery of services based on sector standards and through community participation. It includes monitoring of services and service providers to advocate and ensure humanitarian standards are being met through multi-sectoral activities.Activity 1.1.3: Procurement of materials and servicesActivity 1.1.4: Carry out the reinforcement of damaged emergency shelters with technical supervision.Activity 1.1.5: Construction and technical supervisionActivity 1.1.6: Post-intervention monitoringActivity 1.1.7: Beneficiary identificationActivity 1.1.8: Procurement of 1200 standard NFI Kits, 400 for direct distribution, 800 for prepositioningActivity 1.1.9: Distribution of Standard NFI Kits to 1200 householdsActivity 1.1.10: Monitoring/evaluation reporting

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Concern was given to ensure that protection concerns were considered during planning as shown by the indicator that will guide the monitoring of the project.We intend to mainstream protection by incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid. The following elements must be taken into account in all humanitarian activities:1 – Prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of your intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks.2 – Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.3 – Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.4 – Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including –not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

HARAF will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, including through the camp Management committees. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the HARAF Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. For any distributed items, HARAF will ensure the community receives proper briefing on the selection methods and the date, time and place of distributions to take place ahead of time. During distributions, demonstrations and sensitization is done on the usage of the items and sampled post distribution visits. After the project has been completed, HARAF will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
