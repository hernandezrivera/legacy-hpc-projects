Project Summary

The major aim of the CCCM/Shelter/NFI Cluster is to improve living conditions of displaced persons in humanitarian crises. The sector facilitates assistance and strengthens protection and dignity of the displaced and works with beneficiaries to attain durable solutions.
BOAID will continue to expand its existing Shelter/CCCM/NFl sector services in Borno State, North-East which currently includes Camps in Kala-Balge and Konduga LGA. Currently managing 10 formal and informal sites IDP Camps spread across Rann and Dikwa in Borno State.
Specific activities will include:
(a) Camp coordination/management — coordinated improved provision of humanitarian assistance in IDP settlements and increase Camp's existing capacity;
(b) Communication with affected communities, including complaint and feedback mechanisms, community sensitization/mobilization, ensuring participation of vulnerable groups (women/youth) and ensuring that the woman committees are maintained and functional;
(c) Provision of NFls to new arrivals;
(d) Information Management, supporting BOAIDs CCCM activities, to areas where partners are not working, as well as additional surveys/assessments as necessary and agreed upon with the cluster. Variations in gender, ethnic origin, physical ability and age that affect vulnerability and coping strategies are identified and taken into account for planning to prevent their situation from deteriorating even further. Additionally, contingency plans are developed and put in place to ensure BOAIDs can quickly adapt to and respond to changing humanitarian needs on the ground.
BOAID will continue to address lifesaving needs of IDPs in North-East by expanding camp coverage. Responding to new displacements/ arrivals (through provision of camp management services), improving and upgrading basic services in sites, and finally, supporting the overall humanitarian response. Through this project BOAID will address the sector's overall objective of providing CCCM services and NFIs distribution improving the living conditions inside IDP camps and facilitating the immediate and targeted delivery of services to the displaced population. Furthermore, the project will address the needs of women, girls' men and boys throughout the planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Through the DTM and regular site monitoring, needs and gaps will be reported and shared with partners and all stakeholders. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to contend with GBV and protection concern within the camp settings. BOAID’s CCCM programming has expanded in response to new and protracted displacement throughout Rann and Konduga. Most notably, BOAID remains one of the CCCM partner in Maiduguri Borno State, where new attacks in ending 2020 resulted in the displacement of additional displaced households into Maiduguri, joining the hundreds of thousands already in camps. A majority of these sites are extremely vulnerable to the spread of communicable diseases, such as COVID-19. In 2021, Our CCCM teams will continue to increase CCCM capacity and presence across 10 IDP hosting sites in Borno state, with a focus on strengthening mobile and static CCCM teams in Rann, and potential expansion of services to host communities in Rann, based on displacement trends.

1 - Needs

Borno State has recently experienced a very high rate of movement since December, 2018. The rapid upsurge in new population movements and the IDP situation is causing overpopulation and stressing the existing shelter gaps, which has led to significant congestion in some of the hosting centers in Maiduguri, Jere, Dikwa and Rann. The large numbers of displaced people exacerbate the already poor living condition of displaced populations, thereby affecting the privacy and safety of households. Displaced families living in congested reception centers, informal Camps or in the open with no shelter are largely exposed to several protection concerns, as there is a lack of privacy and dignity, which especially places girls and women at risk of GBV, and other at-risk vulnerable groups including pregnant women, unaccompanied children and elderly persons who are more vulnerable to poor health. Without a meaningful and timely response, families may be forced to resort to negative coping mechanisms, such as forced labor, prostitution, engaging in unsafe activities when looking for access to essential household items. The Project is designed to provide mobile camp management and camp facilitation for Camp and camp like settlements with provision of CCCM assistance through sites facilitation and improvement in Rann and Konduga. BOAID's response will take into consideration the specific needs of women and young girls. The CCCM response will take into account the nature of the current context, which requires a fast and robust relief action to provide immediate life-saving assistance. This response is based on provision of improved CCCM services, to allow the targeted households to easily settle in dignity and safety. The humanitarian crisis in Kala-Balge and Dikwa is one of the worst in Borno state, with over 80 per cent of the population estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance. The protracted crisis has displaced thousands of the people in Rann and Dikwa placing pressure on host communities with limited capacity to support displaced populations. Rann is completely a camp without distinct host community. Kilagaru, boarding primary school, and General Hospital Camps were completely newly founded camps by the IDPs while Ajari, and Bulamari were originally host communities, but the original natives have all departed and fled during the crisis so the crisis occupants and dwellers are IDPs. The host communities constitute not more than 1-2% of the population. The camp has the least humanitarian activities, there wasn’t CCCM presence since when IOM withdrew its staffs in 2018 after an attack by NSAG. The Precious security environment in Rann, Kala Balge LGA continues to limit movement and hinder livelihood activities. Social and economic infrastructures are still devastated and will require considerable early recovery and development efforts if the security environment improves. Food security is of particular concern in Rann and Dikwa as rising insecurity, forced multiple displacements, lack of assured safety of PoCs and aid workers, and movement restrictions.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

BOAID is responsible for managing and coordinating assistance and services to displaced populations in communal settings in a manner that promotes their safety, dignity and integrity. By mainstreaming protection into our CCCM programs, BOAID can support displaced persons access and enjoy their rights; IDPs reside safely in camp settings and improve their quality of life during displacement. BOAID will Prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm 1. Ensurethelocationandaccessroutestocampsandcamp-likesettingsaresafe. • Avoid placing camps near borders, military installations and conflict frontlines. • Consider safety risks associated with landmines, explosive remnants of war (ERW) and busy roads. 2. Identify areas in and around the camp that could be potentially unsafe or a risk of violence, especially gender-based violence e.g. around showers, latrines, child-friendly spaces, schools and routes to schools, health facilities, water collection points, etc. • Monitor high-risk zones regularly and at different times of day. • Install adequate lighting, especially in public or communal areas, or provide alternatives such as solar lamps or torches for each household. 3. Ensure effective safety and security measures are in place: • Establish community patrol groups, where possible, to support prevention of forced recruitment, looting, GBV and other threats. • Secure the camp premises with fencing. • Design escape routes in the case of emergency evacuation and inform camp residents of fire safety and evacuation procedures. • Introduce a system to monitor the movement of persons and assets entering and leaving the camp. 4. Space arrangements, camps structures and distance between dwellings should respect privacy and cultural norms between men and women and minimize the risks of exploitation and abuse, whilst also allowing for family unity. • Install partitions or solid barriers between families in communal shelters and introduce door locks to better protect women and girls, particularly single women and female headed-households. 5. Be aware of existing land and property tenure arrangements, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land when selecting sites. Advocate with local authorities and/or property owners to obtain formal agreement to use the land, as a precaution to reduce the risk of eviction, rental costs or conflict erupting due to lack of clarity on these issues. 6. Be aware of existing friction between different groups inside the camps. Ensure camp lay-out adequately avoids fuelling tensions. 7. Regularly monitor and discuss with beneficiaries, especially the most vulnerable, if they feel pressured, directly or indirectly, to return to their place of origin or relocate, or if they feel prevented from leaving the camp. 8. Locate recreational spaces for children to play in areas where family members can watch children from shelters/community centers to avoid children playing in remote areas. BOAID will also Promote equitable and impartial access for all by; Putting in place a registration system with population records disaggregated by age, sex and diversity. Ensure the system enables women to be registered in their own capacity and provisions are made for child-headed households. The purpose of registration should be properly communicated to all groups. Ensure accountability to affected populations; Being transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for residency, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. Strengthen participation and empowerment; 1. We'll Ensure that both women and men are fully engaged in the management of camp facilities. 2. Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to camp issues. Different criteria may affect the power dynamics.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The Project will ensure transparency with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for residency, organizational procedures and processes that affect them. Our Project will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box in community centre or at site of facilities a. Set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff. b. RESPOND to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. c. Organize awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. d. Consider a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors to minimize confusion. e. Ensure two-way communication methods are in place. CCCM must be able to receive and respond to community concerns. This might include ensuring community mobilisers are able to answer questions, use of hotlines, monitoring of informal site social media to adapt key messages.
