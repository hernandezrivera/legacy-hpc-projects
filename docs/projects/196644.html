Project Summary

This project will support the monitoring of population movements across non-Government-controlled areas in northwest Syria to make available reliable and verified data for the humanitarian response in an efficient and coordinated manner. An online monitoring dashboard will provide humanitarian actors with the latest information per community level, regular/monthly overviews and trend analysis will be shared with cluster members, and ad-hoc emergency updates will be produced and shared when required. Technical guidance will be provided to cluster members to support data collection.

3 - Needs

Due to the lack of access to areas in northern Syria and the remote management of thecross-border operation, obtaining comprehensive and accurate data on populations anddisplacements is a challenge for humanitarian actors. In 2020, the CCCM Cluster trackedover 2 million individual displacements between January and December 2020, whichinclude second or third displacements, meaning that some people may have been forcedto move multiple times within the same period and that the reasons for movement mayvary. On average, the cluster tracked over 33,000 population movements on a monthlybasis, noting the huge increase earlier in the year (1 million in February). The CCCMCluster has developed an efficient inter-agency system to collect data in a coordinatedmanner, triangulate, and when possible, verify the data. UNHCR’s partner will use RapidDisplacement Monitoring (RDM) system to collect near real time snapshot about currentlocation displaced people in order to produce categorized (age, gender, vulnerability andshelter type) number of IDPs within 24 hours and identify their top three needs oncommunity levels. In addition, the Cluster also relied on specialized organizations toprovide data and information on population movements to ensure sufficient geographicalcoverage and technical capacity. Through its partner, UNHCR aims to continue providingsuch dedicated support to the CCCM Cluster under the IDP Situation Monitoring Initiative.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Physical and sexual exploitation and abuse. Mitigating measures: UNHCRensures that partners have a code of conduct (CoC) in place, supportsdeveloping/reviewing PSEA prevention materials, organizes internal and externalCoC/PSEA sessions, and follows-up on PSEA on a regular basis. PSEA focal points areidentified in the operation whom individuals can contact and/or report misconduct byhumanitarian staff. Risk 2: Assessment fatigue among beneficiaries and clustermembers. Mitigating measures: The purposes of monitoring and assessments areclarified to beneficiaries without raising expectations. The tools and questionnaires aredeveloped to be as light as possible.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely withother stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensurethere is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do noharm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to mandatorytrainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has becomemandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet the standard of thePSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the followingyear.
