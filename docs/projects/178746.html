Project Summary

The project will be implemented in partnership with Hand in Hand as AAR Japan doesn't have direct implementation in Syria. The Project aims to improve sanitary and hygienic conditions of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in northwest Syria, and reduce their risks to Covid-19 infection. 10,000 Hygiene kits will be distributed to approximately 34,638 IDPs (about 7,142 households) living in overcrowded camps and sites, overpopulated areas in Idleb Governorate in northwest Syria between 1st Apr and 31st Dec 2021. Although our partner (Hand in Hand) has other projects in the same sub-districts, but intervention will be in different camps and as result, there will be no overlap. Regarding other wash needs, 70 % of IDPs in camps in northwest Syria continue to rely on WASH partners to provide trucked water on a daily basis. Access to safe water through piped systems remains a challenge, with only 65% of systems in Idleb are currently functioning. Further support is needed for the rehabilitation of water stations and networks in order to connect the stations to electricity grids and improve service delivery. Dana and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts continue to be one of the most significant hotspots for waterborne diseases, and with the highest number of leishmaniasis cases.

4 - Needs

An estimated 3 million people in northwest Syria live at high risk of COVID-19 infections; yet, due to the vulnerabilities of their natures, IDPs are considered to be exposed to further high risk of COVID-19. In northwest Syria, 2.7 million people; more than half of total population is IDPs. Among them, 1.6 million people are still forced to live in over 1,300 camps. IDPs living in camps faced high vulnerabilities due to various restrictions resulted from COVID-19 outbreak. Moreover, high density and shared lives in camps make their infection risks higher. Positive rate in camps was around 30%. Despite pressing needs for preventing infections in overpopulated IDP camps, lack of fund for WASH cluster causes significant gaps. From past three months in northwest Syria, the number of people requiring WASH assistance has been increased from 0.77 million to 2.6 million; among them, 52 percent of people needs hygiene kits assistance. The highest needs exist in Dana Sub-district and Ma’arrat Tamsrin Sub-district. More than 0.7 million people requires hygiene kits assistance. For these reasons, AAR Japan aims to improve hygiene situation and prevent COVID-19 outbreak through distribution of hygiene kits in IDP camps in Dana Sub-district and Ma’arrat Tamsrin Sub-district.

5 - Implementation of activities

1st, selection of targeted areas: in coordination with the WASH Cluster, CCCM, and local councils, AAR Japan, with HIH, shall collect information on IDP camps in Dana and Ma’arrat Tamsrin Subdistricts; then, compile the list of potential target areas. HIH will conduct assessments of potential target areas using the questionnaire prepared by AAR Japan. Based on the results, target areas will be selected considering the ratio of new IDPs population, hygiene needs and gaps. To avoid overlaps, AAR Japan shall coordinate with the relevant clusters and organizations. 2nd, verification of beneficiaries: AAR Japan shall prepare the beneficiaries' list based on the information from local councils and camp managers. HIH shall verify the list via house-to-house visits. 3rd, procurement: the content of hygiene kit is determined based on Sphere Standards, WASH Cluster recommendations, and feedbacks from previous project. All items will be procured in Turkey by AAR Japan and shipped to HIH warehouse in Syria. 4th, distribution: Hygiene kits will be transported by small trucks from HIH warehouses to distribution points. If possible, HIH shall deliver the packages door-to-door. Distribution points setup and distributions will follow the guidelines by WASH cluster. Each household shall receive hygiene kit(s) once sufficient to serve its family members for 30 days. Hygiene promotion leaflets will also be distributed and staff will share messages to improve beneficiaries’ hygiene awareness and practices regarding covid-19. 5th, PDM will be approx. 20 days after distribution. AAR Japan shall create a list of samples for the survey considering location, family size, and gender. HIH M&E team shall then visit the selected households.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

AAR Japan and its partner uphold the nine commitments of the CHS throughout the project, putting the Code of Conduct in practice and fulfilling its essential responsibilities as humanitarian organizations. To avoid conflict with camps due to misinformation of the services, pre-coordination with camp managers shall take place to provide detailed information of the services to the beneficiaries, and to ensure that beneficiaries understand their rights and entitlements. The project will consider factors that hinder vulnerable groups, including women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female-headed households from reaching our service. They will have active participation in the assessment, design, and implementation phases. Tent-to-tent distribution will be considered to avoid inequality in distribution. Measures will be in place to ensure beneficiaries have safe and responsive access to handle their complaints and requests. Apart from monitoring, leaflets specifying the contact point for complaints will be handed to each household or individual who received the service. All complaints will be addressed by a responsible team from HIH appropriately. In case complaints and tip-offs regarding sexual abuse or exploitations are received, the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Officer shall intervene and respond to the case; an investigation team, under the direct chain of command of the Chief Executive Officer in HIH Turkey Office, shall report to the PSEA committee – the committee is held with the participation of other organizations –, and verify the facts on the case. HIH MEAL team shall report misconducts in the field in accordance with policies on preventing such acts.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

As a precondition for the project to be implemented, HIH staff members must be able to safely operate in the selected target areas; however, these risks are assumed: deterioration in security situation, intervention by armed groups, closure of border between Turkey and Syria, restriction of humanitarian activities within Syria by Turkish or Syrian governments, and the outbreak of COVID-19. Depending on the situation in the areas of operations, the project will be suspended until safe operations for HIH staff members can be ensured. In case no signs of recovery in security situation or improvement through coordination with relevant actors can be seen, AAR Japan shall consider changing access routes and target areas, or ceasing the project. Security assessment will take place before any field activity to check the security situation in the planned area of operation. To mitigate the risk of the spread of covid-19 in northwestern Syria (21.000 As of 21 January 2021), both AAR Japan and HIH have adopted their own policies to prevent Covid-19 infections among the staff members and beneficiaries. All staff members are required to abide by polices and restrictions imposed by the organizations and the local authorities, wear masks and gloves, wash hands on regular basis, measure body temperature, and take other precautious measures as necessary. In addition, HIH staff members have received a training recommended by WHO on Covid-19 prevention and control; field activities shall also incorporate safety guidance developed by the relevant clusters. Placing the safety of the staff members and beneficiaries at the utmost priority, field activities will be postponed and rescheduled in case any security concerns are confirmed.
