Project Summary

WFP is committed to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and is guided by a strategic, programmatic and governance instrument to operationalize its plan and achieve its project objectives to:
• End Hunger by protecting access to food and by improving dietary diversity; and
• Support the implementation of the SDGs by enhancing the capacities of national institutions to implement food security strategies and operations.
This project directly contributes to strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Response Plan by meeting the basic food needs of severely vulnerable Palestinians and by improving their dietary diversity.
WFP’s interventions focus on high food insecurity levels, including the malnutrition levels recorded through national surveys, and supporting vulnerable families in vulnerable communities with livelihood activities. By prioritizing assistance poor and severely food insecure Palestinian households in Gaza and Bedouin and herding communities in West Bank’s Area C, and by disseminating information on food and nutrition practices, WFP addresses the basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians and tackles one of the root causes of some of the food insecurity levels recorded by the government institutions. These interventions aim at improving the overall food security levels including nutrition and health status of the vulnerable Palestinians and therefore at increasing their coping abilities to sudden shocks in the market for access to food.
The project also contributes to the Food Security Sector’s objectives, the social development sector strategy 2021-2023 as well as to the National Policy Agenda 2017-2022 (policies on escaping poverty and strengthening social protection as well as meeting the basic needs of the communities) to ensure that the vulnerable Palestinians households basic food needs are met, by assisting households in enhancing their food security levels and by strengthening national institutions in implementing food security strategies and social security safety net programs to best identify and target food insecure and vulnerable Palestinians. Additionally, the project contributes to the operationalization of Humanitarian-Development-Peace (HDP) Nexus by facilitating programmatic interventions that reduce people’s needs, risk, and vulnerability and strengthens peoples’ resilience with the intent to achieve long-term economic development. In coordination with other actors and based on each and everyone’s respective comparative advantage, the project and related interventions minimizes risks leading to the empowerment of some of the most vulnerable population groups.

1 - Needs

According to the initial results from the inter-agency 2023 Palestine Humanitarian Needs Overview, 1.84 million Palestinians are food insecure, out of 1.54 million Palestinians are in need for food assistance (28% of the population), also according the 2020 Socio-Economic and Food Security Survey (SEFSec) findings 1.78 million Palestinians (35% of the population, and 31% of the households) are moderately to severely food insecure. Poverty and unemployment, the key drivers of vulnerability and food insecurity in Palestine, are at their peak: in Gaza, nearly six out of ten people are poor, and more half of the workforce is unemployed; brings the total poor people in Palestine to 1.4 million. Food insecurity is driven by several inter-linked factors including insufficient economic access to food, arising from restrictions on movement, trade and investment, high poverty resulting from widespread unemployment, erosion of agricultural infrastructure, land confiscation, limited access to land and water, and an overstretched government safety net. Rising inflation has further reduced the purchasing power of Palestinians. Food expenditure constitutes more than half of the total expenditure of households with a low standard of living; the current surge in staple food prices, resulting from the Ukraine conflict, has placed new financial strains on already vulnerable Palestinians. The combined impact of these drivers has resulted in exceptionally high numbers of people in need for food assistance, of which 53% are in the Gaza Strip and 11% in West Bank (of which 86% are in Area A & B, and about 23% are female headed households). Also, 48% of households are facing difficulties in obtaining enough money to cover basic needs (81% in the Gaza and 31% in West Bank). There continues to be widespread use of negative strategies, such as reducing the quality of food consumed, taking on debt, borrowing food from relatives or friends, and reducing the number of meals eaten in a day to cope with the deterioration of livelihoods and socio-economic conditions by food insecure households. The compounding impacts of multiple shocks have stretched national resources and capacities. Rising inflation, linked to the COVID- 19 pandemic, the disruption of supply chain and now also spurred by the Ukraine crisis, has contributed to the reduced purchasing power of Palestinians and increased food insecurity. Also, the government's ability to maintain the National Cash Transfer programme (NCTP) is at risk, the most vulnerable families registered under the NCTP have received only one payment since May 2021 but with a significant reduced value. Gender inequalities play a significant role in the food security and nutrition status of individuals; women are especially affected by food insecurity and poverty. The percentage of the people in need for food assistance among households headed by women is 3 percentage points higher than that among households headed by men – 31 versus 28%. In Gaza the percentage of women headed households that in need for assistance reaches 61% of the total women headed households. Furthermore, women’s economic empowerment is impeded by lower wages and domestic work evidenced by unemployment statistics: unemployment rate for the second quarter of 2022 was 24 percent (44% in the Gaza Strip and 14% percent in the West Bank. Unemployment among males in Gaza reached 38% and up to 65% among females. In the West Bank, the rate stood at 12% among males and 22% among females. Also, the people with disabilities (PwD) are more vulnerable to food insecurities where 47% (21% and 64%) of the PwD are in need for food assistance. Studies have shown around 20% of school children are anemic. Daily food consumption patterns show unhealthy trends with approximately 90% of adolescent consuming white bread daily full of “empty calories”, and only 35% of school aged children consumed at least one fresh fruit daily. Schoolchildren have higher nutritional needs and require more protein and energy than any other age group - approximately 2,420 kcal per day. Adolescents also have gender-specific nutrient needs; studies have shown the optimal diet to support adolescent girls’ healthy development is more expensive than the diet required by an adult man. The dual burden of malnutrition (i.e., the co-existence of under- and over-nutrition) is a growing problem in Palestine and poses a serious threat to the health of adults, children, and adolescents alike.

2 - Activities

WFP will carry out the following activities: Activity 1: Provision of unconditional food assistance - through cash-based transfers (electronic vouchers and cash assistance) and in-kind modalities. WFP will provide unconditional food assistance to targeted to the vulnerable and food insecure households in the Gaza Strip, in Area C and areas with poverty and high food insecurity levels in the West Bank. The target beneficiaries include 438,000 non-refugee poor and severely food insecure people including 38,000 individuals from the Bedouin and herding communities in Area C of the West Bank that will be assisted as part of the joint emergency food assistance programme in partnership with UNRWA. Around 30% of the targeted households are female-headed households. The primary assistance modality is a CBT value voucher that is redeemable through a network of local shops registered by WFP. An in-kind modality is maintained in Area C and the Gaza Strip, making up 17% of distribution activities. The provision of in-kind food for Bedouin and herder communities is based on protection risks and their remote locations, far from commercial centers. In the Gaza Strip, in-kind food assistance supply chains are maintained as a key component of WFP’s emergency response plan. During previous escalations in the Gaza Strip, shops have faced significant supply constraints, The supply chain bottleneck is attributed to the restrictions placed on trucks allowed to enter Gaza and distribution challenges. Maintaining a functional food pipeline and stocked supply system enables WFP to deliver in-kind assistance during such emergencies. WFP contracted retail shops offer voucher beneficiaries quality and diverse food at market prices. WFP works with partners to monitor food prices and issues monthly price monitoring reports, and carries out process monitoring at the shops, providing an extra layer of oversight and fiscal controls. To the greatest extent possible, shops are owned by women, offer local products produced by women-led micro, small and medium enterprises such as honey, olive oil, and produce, and promote women’s economic empowerment. The secondary impact of cash-based transfers on the local economy has shown increased sales turnover, increased number of clients, and creation of new jobs. Furthermore, the program adopts a NEXUS-approach by linking humanitarian, development, community resilience, and stabilization. WFP’s humanitarian assistance supports targeted households and the local economy which enhances stability by giving participating shopkeepers increased bargaining power ability to negotiate prices, discounts, and bonuses with suppliers, which have been passed to customers through price discounts and special offers. Disability is a key vulnerability factor taken into consideration in targeting poor and food insecure households and included in the data collection tool that is used in targeting. Operationally WFP considers disability a factor in the selection of distribution points and shops to ensure accessibility. WFP also links families and youth who are bypassed by mainstream economic opportunities -especially women, young people, people with disabilities, small holder farmers, and other vulnerable and food insecure persons - with livelihood and employment activities opportunities in agriculture- and non-agriculture value chains. The program aims to increase participants’ self-confidence, develop their technical and life skills, support entrepreneurial initiatives, and build opportunities to access income enabling them to escape household debt burdens and reliance on external assistance. WFP’s programming applies the “Do No Harm” framework to address risks so that humanitarian action does not exacerbate conflict. WFP’s commitment to protection ensures that programmes are safe, dignified and respect people’s other needs, rights, and capacities. WFP will focus not only on how it provides assistance in order to “do no harm” but also on how its decision making and related programming is strategically designed for, received by and perceived by affected populations and the impact that programmes have on addressing protection risks. The adoption of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) approach in Palestine focuses on three key areas: a) providing accurate, timely and accessible information about WFP assistance; b) consulting affected persons and inviting feedback throughout each stage of the project cycle; and, c) providing a complaints and feedback mechanism to affected people to voice complaints and provide feedback in a safe and dignified manner through secure boxes placed in retail shops for CBTs and distribution points, WFP ‘s dedicated hotline, and collecting feedback during monitoring visits. WFP has adopted a zero-tolerance stance toward SEA and is committed to the IASC Six Core Principles Relating to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. WFP has effective prevention and protection measures that safeguard the people WFP serves enabling safe access to WFP programmes and operations without being subject to sexual exploitation or sexual abuse by WFP employees and partners, and, if acts of exploitation and abuse are attempted or committed, WFP responds swiftly with a victim-centered approach. WFP empowers beneficiaries by addressing financial inclusion inequalities and aims to bridge digital divides by giving access to platforms for two-way communication as well as integrating technology in food assistance and livelihoods. WFP’s program design maximizes the benefits of different transfer modalities (MPCA, cash for food, electronic vouchers, in kind food parcels) to ensure protection risks are mitigated, particularly related to exacerbating inter-household and inter-group sensitivities. WFP household monitoring shows that its programs are implemented safely, appropriately, and respectfully without exposing them to protection or safety challenges.

WFP works closely with Food Security Sector stakeholders to continually improve the analysis and identification of the food insecure population in Palestine and to target food assistance. Targeting allows WFP to ensure that the most vulnerable, food insecure people are selected to receive WFP assistance. The targeting of WFP’s food assistance is based on a Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF) developed by the Ministry of Social Development, and in line with standard international practice, humanitarian, and development assistance providers. Food assistance will be provided in a manner that reduces the exposure to risk for beneficiaries (especially that of women) and at the same time reduces the burden associated with carrying food items, particularly for female headed households. WFP uses a robust monitoring system to ensure that all beneficiaries are verified and monitored after receiving assistance to ensure that the beneficiaries meet the criteria of falling below the PA’s threshold deep poverty line. The deep poverty line is calculated by taking into consideration household consumption levels of three categories of goods and services: food, clothing and housing. Prior to any activity implementation, WFP will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with line ministries offices, and with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Cash based transfers in the form of electronic food vouchers are provided to targeted beneficiaries who meet geographical and vulnerability criteria - they live in urban centers near one of approximately 320 participating shops in the voucher network and they are unable to meet their basic food and nutrition requirements. Beneficiaries receiving cash based transfers as part of a integrated program - aiming to support livelihoods, increase the household’s shock responsiveness, build economic empowerment, or support gender equality - are selected based not only on vulnerability but also taking into account program criteria including participant capacity, access to assets, education and interest. WFP`s Food Assistance through in-kind food parcels is selected for beneficiaries who do not have easy or secure access to urban centers where participating shops in the electronic voucher network are found. These beneficiaries often face movement restrictions and have significant protection concerns. While all WFP beneficiaries are verified using the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF) as its main targeting methodology by determining household income, expenditures and socio-economic vulnerability, beneficiaries of in-kind food parcel distribution modalities are also targeted based on their food consumption patterns which identify a need for increased consumption of food items provided by WFP, beneficiary preference for in-kind food parcels, and protection concerns. Beneficiaries targeted with Cash assistance - including cash for food and multi-purpose cash - are identified and targeted to reach those households who are most in need. WFP’s unconditional food assistance (in-kind and voucher) is targeted to non-refugee households falling below the Deep Poverty Line and with a poor or borderline FCS. Households already receiving other types of assistance from other sources are excluded from WFP’s beneficiary lists to avoid duplication of assistance.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

WFP has a functional complaints and feedback mechanism that supports accountability to affected populations by ensuring that affected populations’ input and feedback are taken into account to inform programme design and taking steps to understand the risks they face, prioritizing those in the most vulnerable situations and promoting inclusion. These control and risk management mechanisms are effective tools to mitigate the risk of abuse, protect the rights of beneficiaries and WFP’s accountability towards them. WFP Palestine has several channels for any feedback or complaint from their side. Through feedback and complaints system (Hotline). The hotline is open to all WFP’s beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. The service is free and for 12 hours/7 days. Through the complaint boxes that are available at the shop level and Food distribution points that are accessible for all beneficiaries. Beneficiaries also have the chance to raise whatever issues, concerns, or inquiries during the distribution of food at the distribution point for food/in-kind modality or shop for cash assistance during the presence of WFP’s monitoring assistants, programme assistant, and cooperating partners staff or during post distribution visits conducted by the WFP’s monitoring assistants. Beneficiaries who are part of the social safety net of the Ministry of Social Development have access to the Ministry’s complaint and feedback mechanism system. The Ministry provides WFP with feedback on a regular basis.
