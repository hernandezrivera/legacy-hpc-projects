Project Summary

Since April 2018, IOM has operated and managed a total of 9 humanitarian hubs composed of a base camp in Maiduguri (Red Roof) and 8 field hubs located in Gwoza, Bama, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Banki (2 hubs) and Damasak. The humanitarian hubs provide humanitarian actors the ability to work with safe and secure accommodation, office space and meeting/training facilities, so that they are able to stay and deliver interventions necessary to saving and improving the lives of hundreds of thousands of Nigerians affected by conflict. The field hubs also host the internet connectivity service of the Emergency Telecommunication Sector (ETS). These humanitarian hubs help sustain the access of humanitarian responders to the population affected by the conflict, enabling them to coordinate and provide better assistance to the affected population. The humanitarian base camp in Maiduguri has a total capacity of 142 beds while each of the field hubs except for the second hub in Banki (Banki Hub Extension), has a 15-bed capacity. The Banki Hub Extension has a capacity of 72 beds. All of the hubs have office space and training/meeting facilities available to humanitarian actors. Under the proposed project, IOM will aim to support the operation and maintenance of eight humanitarian field hubs and the base camp in Maiduguri (Red Roof).

1 - Needs

The North-East region of Nigeria experienced violence perpetrated by non-state armed groups (NSAG) since 2015 resulting in massive displacement of residents who fled from the areas affected by the violence. Several humanitarian agencies responded to the resulting humanitarian crisis but were met with several challenges. One of the major challenges faced by the humanitarian agencies was the availability of safe accommodation and office facilities for their teams. In response to the need, the International Humanitarian Partnership (IHP) through the Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency (MSB) partnered with IOM in setting up humanitarian hubs in Maiduguri and in newly accessible areas. On 30 April 2018, MSB ended their deployment in Borno State and handed over the completion of the building of the remaining hubs and operation of all the humanitarian hubs to IOM. Currently, IOM operates a main hub in Maiduguri (Red Roof) and a total of eight field hubs in Gwoza, Bama, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Banki (2 hubs) and Damasak in deep field locations. The base camp in Maiduguri (Red Roof) has a total bed capacity of 142 beds, while the field hubs each have a capacity of 15 beds, except for the second hub in Banki which has a capacity of 72 beds. In addition to providing accommodation, all hubs provide office spaces and meeting/training facilities for humanitarian agencies. In collaboration with the Emergency Telecommunication Sector (ETS), the field hubs also provide internet connectivity to humanitarian workers who are not residents of the field hubs. Despite the establishment of the eight deep field hubs, the continued insecurity in these areas still poses a major challenge to humanitarian presence. IOM put in place a cost-recovery mechanism which supports up to 30% of the total cost of operating the 9 hubs. Additional funding is needed to cover the budget gap in operating the 9 humanitarian hubs and keep their facilities in good running condition to ensure their continued operation. Additionally, since August 2019, the humanitarian field hubs were targeted by NSAG attackers whenever they attack the town where the field hubs are located. The hubs in Banki, Ngala and Monguno were directly targeted by NSAG attacker who managed to enter the hub in Ngala and burned the hub storage and laundry room, shot and damaged one of the hub’s generator and burned the hub’s armored vehicle. IOM worked with UNDSS in identifying the security facilities of the hubs that needed to be enhanced in order to improve the overall security facilities of the hubs. The needs were categorized by UNDSS and IOM between priority 1 and priority 2, depending on the criticality of the facility in the overall security set-up of the field hubs. With funding from various donors, IOM started to implement the priority 1 needs. However, some of the priority 2 needs are also critical and should not remain unmet. The installation of solar power systems in the hubs particularly is important not only for improvingto improve the security facility of the hubs but also to lessen the dependence of the hubs on diesel for power generation and to lower the operating costs of the hubs.

2 - Activities

• Complete the implementation of the priority 1 security facilities requirement of the hubs, IOM received funding to cover the implementation of all the priority 1 items in the list of security enhancements needed by the hubs. IOM shall continue the implementation of the security facilities enhancement of the hubs and complete it within the first quarter of 2021. • Install solar power systems in at least the following field hubs which are very heavily used by humanitarian workers: Bama, Gwoza, Ngala and Dikwa. Although the installation of solar power system was categorized by UNDSS and IOM as priority 2 items, having the hubs equipped with at least 30kw solar power system will allow the hubs to lower their operating cost. This will contribute to the sustainability of the operation of the hubs. • Keep the facilities of all the 9 hubs properly maintained and available for the use of humanitarian workers. The facilities and equipment of the hubs are at least 3 years old. They have been constantly used since the hubs started operating. Some of them will require replacement or at least some of their parts will be worn out and will require replacement to ensure that the hubs will be able to continue to sustain their operation despite the relatively old facilities and equipment.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM is committed to gender mainstreaming, promoting gender equality and prioritizing the needs andconcerns of vulnerable groups. IOM has worked to strengthen its institutional policies and commitments ongender and protection within its crisis operations worldwide by developing the Institutional Framework forAddressing Gender-Based Violence in Crises (GBViC Framework). Addressing GBV is an issue ofinstitutional accountability and at the core of IOM’s mandate. Central to the framework is the need toensure that the safety, dignity and well-being of all crisis-affected persons, especially women and girls, andtheir equitable access to services are prioritized, integrated and coordinated across all IOM crisisoperations. IOM is thereof committed to the (Nigeria) Call to Action on Protection from GBV.IOM will contribute to promoting gender equality by including both men and women in all components ofthe project. IOM also provides GBV/ Protection against sexual exploitation and abuses (PSEA) trainings across various locations (namely Monguno, Damasak, Ngala, Bama, Banki and Banki extension, Gwoza, and Dikwa) for all IOM staff hired locally. These trainings are scheduled and organized in 2-hour slot per location, depending on staff availability and workload. The humanitarian hubs shall arrange to have all humanitarian hubs staff undergo orientation/ workshop of PSEA to heighten their awareness on PSEA and the reporting mechanisms in place. In addition, IOM observes gender equality in recruiting staff. In the humanitarian hubs operations, accommodation rooms are assigned to guests taking into consideration the gender. Sanitary facilities in all the hubs shall be designated for female and male and shall be marked accordingly. The hubs assign sanitary facilities that are easily accessible to female guests and staff. All the hubs facilities including the bunkers are suitable for the use of both female and male. The PPEs that will be purchased for this project will likewise be suitable for the use of both female and male.
