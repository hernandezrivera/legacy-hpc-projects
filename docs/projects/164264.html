Project Summary

Information on the humanitarian situation in Northeast Syria is difficult to obtain and access to affected communities remains the main challenge. Humanitarian actors are reliant upon one-time and remote assessments like the SINA and the MSNA and ad hoc data from partners. Frequency and quality of current information flow are not consistent. There is general recognition among partners that Northeast Syria contains many sources of information, many are only accessible from the ground. This project will help OCHA (and relevant coordination stakeholders through OCHA) to tap into those sources of information/data, verification/fact-checking of information, aggregate and compile them into reports on the humanitarian situation in Northeast Syria. We propose to run a team of field officers and assistants in consultation with OCHA in Northeast Syria as Community/Service Observers. The organization will share the reports with OCHA on a periodical basis within detailed criteria about requested information and will be further shared by OCHA with senior leadership and relevant stakeholders and mechanisms including RHC, DRHC, clusters, ICCG, HLG, SSG, ISG, Access WG and other relevant stakeholders.
The organization is seeking to use the surplus in the fund for its ongoing project with the SCHF “Humanitarian Field Officers” to increase the planned activities and include additional activities that would help in providing information about the services provided to the IDPs, the local community and the humanitarian actor within the areas of displacement.

4 - Needs

Information on the humanitarian situation in northeast Syria is difficult to obtain. Currently,OCHA is reliant upon one-time and remote assessments, such as the SINA and the MSNA, as well as ad hoc data from partners. While useful, the frequency and quality of current information flow are not consistent. At the same time, there is general recognition among partners that northern Syria contains many sources of information, of which many are only accessible from the ground. Due to the displacement waves that took place in Northeast Syria. The need for information about the essential services became important as the IDPs are facing hard times to locate such services.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The HFO project aims to enable both male and female, “girls and boys” as indirect beneficiaries of the project in their right to receive principled humanitarian assistance. Cross-cutting issues and vulnerabilities (such as gender, age, disabilities etc,) will be focused on.The project will ensure gender balance among staff members to facilitate outreach and access to both male and female beneficiaries, taking into consideration existing traditions and cultural norms.Staff members will receive training on protection mainstreaming, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), Do No Harm and other related protection and GBV mainstreaming to ensure their awareness around their obligations and duties and to capacitate them in their community engagement. HFOs will also be bound to sign a Code of Conduct highlighting protection and GBV related issues. The organization will also ensure the establishment of accessible feedback/ complaint mechanisms. While supporting the cross-border humanitarian community in their community engagement with affected populations inside Syria, the HFOs will raise existing protection and GBV concerns and will contribute to raise awareness among local communities around their rights as recipients of humanitarian aid, taking into consideration cross-cutting issues and vulnerabilities (such as gender, age, disabilities etc,).

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:1- Military operations in the area and clashes based on scenarios anticipated by the organization that might limit freedom of movement and ability to deliver2- Limited access granted by authorities on the ground3- Kidnapping risks4- Casualty risks Assumptions1- Secure the field staff to fulfil the tasks within the project period2- Secure the information and equipment within the project period Mitigations1- The organization developed a contingency plan 2- The organization developed and secured duty of care policy (including health insurance)3- The organization revised and kept the risks scenarios updated The organization will monitor the safety of practices and locations on an on-going basis - and adjust project activities when threats to safety are identified in order to minimize risks. A safe and secure information exchange system will be established between the HFOS in the field and the organization's team.OCHA will support HFOs in their engagement with authorities on the ground to ensure their safety and access.
