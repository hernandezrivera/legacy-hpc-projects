Project Summary

Since 2016, political and social instability, exacerbated by sporadic violence, has had a negative impact on the civilian population of Cameroon’s South-West and North-West Regions, hosting four million inhabitants (16% of the total population of Cameroon). In November 2017, the socio-political crisis progressively translated into insecurity and armed violence. Since then, the escalation of tension and upsurge in hostilities between non-state armed groups and defence and security forces have triggered humanitarian needs across the two regions, linked to significant internal displacement.
The number of households forced to flee their villages in search of safer areas has rapidly and steadily increased since November 2017. Almost 680,000.people have been internally displaced in the two affected regions and an additional 58,000 people have sought refuge in neighboring Nigeria and are all in need humanitarian and protection assistance ( HRP needs Overview 2020)
80% of population in the two affected regions rely on agriculture and small trades for their livelihood. Threats and attacks continue to have negative impact on trade, markets, and access to land. Farmers, small-scale fishermen and their families find themselves in an extremely vulnerable position due to the disruption of their activities. IDPs have had to resort to negative food-related coping strategies, reducing the number of meals (to one meal a day) and adult consumption in order to feed the children. Additionally, restrictions on movements (curfews, security and administrative measures), poor infrastructures, and checkpoints continue to hinder access to land and markets.
Bamenda the head quaters of the North Wes Region is fairly calmer than the other towns and villages . About 60% of the schools are functional which has caused a good number of people to move to Bamenda for safety and for children to access education . More than 65% of the IDPs in Bamenda are living in extremely deplorable situation. They rely sometimes on the host who can barely provide a meal a day, some go begging from neighbors, others do menial jobs,
The level of food insecurity amongst other needs is getting to a critical state and requires emergency and mortality which is already at an increase with the crisis.
This project is aims to provide Livelihood support to 400 Female headed IDPs households living in Bamenda in Mezam Divsion through training and support on Market gardening and Poultry Farming in Bamenda.
SHUMAS has been quiet involved in providing livelihood assistance to IDPs in the NWR. 80% of the female IDP headed households who benefited from SHUMAS integrated poultry and market gardening project has had a remarkable positive impact in their livelihood. Given the continues pressure from other vulnerable IDPs for assistance, we intend to scale up this activity to benefit 400 additional vulnerable IDPs in Bamenda.
The 400 IDPS will be trained in 4 phases. Phase one will be held in February, Second in May , third in August and last installment in November.
After training, each participant will receive promotional materials such as
10 three weeks chicks, - 1bags of feed, - 3 sachets of vegetables seeds, 2 feeder,
2 drinkers, 1 bag of manure.
Vulnerability targeting will be done to select the most vulnerable . IDPs living with disability, Female headed households, GBV victims , pregnant and lactating mother and households headed by the elderly.
Each participant will participate by providing space for brooding and gardening.
We will Put in place strong outcome tracking system and backfilling when and where ever necessary.
A whatsapp forum will be created to enable beneficiaries share their experiences and reports.
A feedback mechanism will be established through suggestion boxes, sms, forms etc and provide timely response.
The project will run for 1 year starting from January 2021

1 - Besoins / Needs

The NW crisis has led to a great drop in agricultural and animal productivity, availability and access to products and foodstuffs. Humanitarian actors' access to such common areas has been affected, which also limits the possibility of implementing productive asset creation projects. All this, combined with the frequent and massive displacement of populations in the affected areas, leads to the abandonment of assets and huge losses of assets (productive and non-productive) and livelihoods - fields and cultivated agricultural areas, animals, pastures, housing, granaries, cereal stocks, etc. - and the loss of livelihoods. The Cadre Harmonisé, analysis in March 2020. In the North West 40% of IDP households adopt potentially irreversible emergency food-based coping strategies, compromising their productivity and future ability to cope with shocks. Methods of coping include relying on less preferred, less expensive food, borrowing food, reducing portion size or number of meals. 28% of IDP households in the NW resort to livelihood based coping strategies such as selling their land, begging or selling the last female animal.The project intends to improve the livelihood of 400 vulnerable women IDPs in Bamenda in the Northwest regions through training and support on poultry farming, and market gardeningBased on the market analysis conducted and experience, poultry farming will generate a monthly income of about 63000frs and vegetable gardening on 200 m2 land can provide a minimum income 35000 a month. . specific objectives a) Enhance the productive capacity of 400 vulnerable women IDPs on poultry farming, backyard gardening, basic management and marketing skills.b) Improve the mental health of the 400 vulnerable IDPs especially GBV survivors through group counseling and case by case counselling. c) Support them with promotional materials consisting of 10 three weeks chicks, - 1bags of feed, - 3 sachets of vegetables seeds, 2 feeder, 2 drinkers, 1 bag of manure. d) put in place a strong outcome tracking system and backfilling when and wherever necessary. Activities per objective will include;1) Capacity building for 400 affected women IDPs on poultry farming, backyard gardening, basic management and marketing skills.- Organize a preplanning meeting with all project stakeholders to ensure everyone is on the same page - Carry out vulnerability registration of IDPs- Purchasing of training materials- Organize and run two days training session in a safe an easy to access location especially for those with disabilities2) Support them with promotional materials consisting of 10 three weeks chicks, - 1bags of feed, - 3 sachets of vegetables seeds, 2 feeder, 2 drinkers, 1 bag of manure.- Purchase promotional materials - Distribute 10 , 3 weeks old chicks to each beneficiary, 1 bags feed, 01 bag of Manure 2 drinkers each, 2feeder each, 3 varieties of vegetable seeds each, and 1 bag manure.3 ) Provide psychosocial support to 400 affected IDPs especially GBV survivors for a period of 12 months in 4 phases - Engage a psychosocial specialist - carryout counseling sessions to the 400 affected women as a group and as individuals4) Put in place strong outcome tracking system and backfilling when and where necessary5) Carryout monthly follow-up trips to ensure all the beneficiaries are successfully implementing their activities - Write progress reports monthly and final reports We expect that this project will provide the following results;The vulnerable IDPs improve their level of economic sufficiency reducing their vulnerability and enabling them to meet their daily needsThe 400 beneficiaries become more confident and feel protected and safe to carry on with daily activities and integrate themselves in the society Improvement in health and nutrition.
