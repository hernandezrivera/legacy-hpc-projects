Project Summary

The specific objective of the project is to reduce the impact of explosive hazards for conflict-affected populations through Risk Education and land release in Suleymaniya, Kirkuk, Ninewa, Baghdad, Anbar and Diyala Governorates. Specifically, both risk education and land release activities will take place in Kirkuk, Suleymaniya, Diyala and Ninewa. Risk education activities will also take place in Baghdad and Anbar.
In direct relation with the priority needs identified, the project will provide an immediate humanitarian response and contribute to recovery efforts through integrated and comprehensive humanitarian mine action. The project will target IDPs, returnees and host communities living in and around contaminated areas in Iraq. The total target beneficiaries for this project are 137,500 including 120,000 for Risk Education activities and 17,500 for clearance and survey activities.
The project activities related to clearance and survey will be executed by direct implementation by Handicap International (HI). The Risk Education activities will be implemented both directly by HI and in partnership with Iraqi Health and Social Care Organization (IHSCO). The intervention will work in close collaboration with the National Mine Action authorities; Directorate of Mine Action (DMA) and the Iraqi Kurdistan Mine Action Agency (IKMAA)
Specifically, HI will work to facilitate safe return of populations and access to land by reducing the immediate threat posed by Explosive ordnance and increase awareness of the population in the target areas on the risks and threats posed by Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW).

In addition to the above, HI will contribute to national level advocacy, targeting the Government of Iraq and the international community. HI is indeed promoting a blended approach to Mine Action (MA) and is advocating for stakeholders to consider a fresh, community-based and cost-effective approach to the ongoing MA in the country.
Furthermore the intervention will follow a comprehensive approach as it will benefit from other HI interventions to ensure multiple yet integrated activities (including risk education, MHPSS, physical rehabilitation, and inclusive access to service) in order to maximize the intervention for beneficiaries.
The majority of the project is allocated to clearance and mine action which requires specific materials and human resources accordingly resulting in a higher budget required.

Needs

Iraq has experienced multiple conflicts over the last four decades and remains extensively impacted by the recent conflict with the Islamic State (IS) group. While Iraq was already one of the most contaminated countries in the world (global protection cluster March 2017), the recent proliferation of improvised explosive devices (IED), explosive remnants of war (ERW), and mines, has layered on to persisting legacy contamination from previous wars. Indeed, the IS group left behind the legacy of IEDs in the form of Improvised Barrier Minefields along the former front lines with the Iraqi Security Forces (ISF) and also in potential assault positions on the edges of towns and villages, and booby trapped civilian houses and other structures. This contamination has exacerbated the historical problem of legacy hazards from the various conflicts, mainly the Iran/Iraq war, which continue to be a threat. As a result of these conflicts, the area is highly contaminated but there have been limited clearance activities over the years. The new contamination in areas previously held by IS group is hindering the restoration of basic services and stabilisation efforts in addition to putting returnees at risk. As of beginning of 2018, more than 20 million square metres (equivalent of Slovenia surface area) of explosive hazard contaminated areas have been identified by Humanitarian MA NGOs across the Governorates of Diyala, Kirkuk, and Ninewa (Figures gathered by iMMAP, Grey areas IMSMA database). Whilst unprecedented levels of explosive hazard contamination pose a grave physical threat in retaken areas, and foster widespread fear amongst civilian populations, people at-risk are frequently not aware of how to identify hazards and of appropriate safe behaviour to adopt. In an HI study conducted with displaced populations from Hawija in July 2017, 40% of adult and 42% of child respondents could not identify IEDs among pictures shown to them. Additionally, the perception of the danger that explosive hazards could pose to daily activities fell short of the real danger posed.

Activities

To facilitate safe return of populations and access to land by reducing the immediate threat posed by Explosive ordnance the following activities are proposed by Handicap International:- Conduct Non-Technical Survey to identify suspected hazardous areas: The Non-Technical Survey (NTS) will be conducted by the Community Liaison Officers (CLO) to identify potential contamination in that location. NTS is the first basic step to identify Suspect Hazardous Areas without using Technical assets. The Community Liaison visits are also likely to identify other items of ERW which can be removed as a simple “Spot Task” by the clearance or Survey teams. - Conduct in-depth Surveys including detailed NTS & TS to mark off confirmed hazardous areas (CHA) and allow for safe release of confirmed non-contaminated areas: Technical survey (TS) methodology is used to identify precisely the actual scale and type of the threat in areas identified during the NTS. The TS will accurately define the limits of the contaminated area as well as identify contamination densities. - Clearance and hand-over of CHA priority to the DMA and IKMAA: Clearance of the ERW and IED’s will be conducted once the TS has been completed. The Manual Clearance team will remove any ERW and disrupt IED’s and then handing in the explosive components to the local military authorities or assisting in the destruction. Once completed, the sites will be handed back to the DMA and IKMAA which will then organize the handover to the local population. To increase awareness of the population in the target areas on the risks and threats posed by IED’s & ERW the following activities are proposed by Handicap International: - Provision of Emergency Risk Education to the population in the targeted area on the threats posed by IEDs & ERWs contamination: The community liaison officer (CLO) is trained to conduct Emergency Risk Education sessions. These are designed to be shorter presentations and provide the information to reduce the risk of injury and creating safer communities by raising awareness of men, women, and children in accordance with their different vulnerabilities, roles and needs, and by promoting behavioral change through. They also promote behavioral change through a more direct approach. As the CLO is closely tied to the Survey and Clearance teams the visits can be more frequent and productive due to the presence of the other activities and this can be a more visual form of information transfer. - HI and implementing partner, ISHCO will deploy RE teams to the allocated areas of intervention, to deliver safety messages amongst vulnerable populations. These will aim to raise awareness of the risks posed by ERW and IED contamination and promote safe behaviours to adopt in order to manage this threat. Integral to the RE activities carried out will be the provision of information on explosive hazard reporting channels and the recording of data on victims and survivors of ERW/IED incidents. - Deliver targeted RE sessions to CFPs: This training includes two days on RE; half a day on simulation and half a day on Psychological First Aid and other topics. This will also include follow-up QA sessions on CFPs' ability to transmit knowledge within their communities. CFPs are selected from among people that have access to civilian audiences such as mukhtars, sheikhs, teachers, or safety service providers. These key influencers are then able to promote safety messages and safe behaviour within their communities and alert RE teams about spikes of new arrivals that can benefit from Risk Education. In all its areas of operation, HI also supports Victim Assistance activities to ensure that survivors of explosive hazard related incidents, persons with disabilities and/or specific needs, and persons with psychological distress have access to rehabilitation and psychosocial support services. The budget for these is not included in this project sheet. Handicap International (HI), globally and in Iraq, is committed to complying, to the extent possible, with core commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations as agreed upon by the IASC. HI’s Mine Action teams have an embedded community liaison capacity to ensure that targeted communities are well informed about the activities and are able to provide their feedback. HI's Community Liaison Officers ensure that both men and women in communities are able to offer feedback on the prioritization of areas to be cleared and engage in the Non Technical Survey. Moreover, HI’s Risk Education sessions are tailored to audiences of all ages and genders and interactive, soliciting feedback at the end of sessions from beneficiaries.

Mitigation Measures

Protection policies in HIThe principles of equality, non-discrimination and protection are outlined in HI’s Gender Policy, HI’s Child Protection Policy and HI’s Protection of Beneficiaries from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy, which every HI employee reads and signs at the beginning of their assignment. In Iraq, HI designs a feedback mechanism for receiving complaints. HI also conducts awareness workshops and training sessions for all staff on PSEA in order to prevent incidents and to raise awareness on the complaint mechanisms. Finally, HI staffs received protection training on GBV, protection principles, humanitarian principles. Do No HarmHI’s activities are designed and implemented so as to not cause harm and to be consistent with core humanitarian principles. A do-no-harm approach seeks to mitigate any negative impact of external assistance, and ensure that the intervention does not reinforce or exacerbate the conflict and associated tensions. This is bound up with conflict sensitive programming, which entails close analysis and understanding of intergroup relationships within an operational context, as well as any potential interaction between the intervention and these dynamics, in order to avoid any negative impacts. Inclusion of Persons with DisabilityIn Iraq, persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected in situations of risk and humanitarian emergencies, and face multiple barriers in accessing protection and humanitarian assistance, including relief and recovery support. They are also particularly exposed to targeted violence, exploitation and abuse, including sexual and gender-based violence. HI has been working with LNGOs, INGOs and the government in order to address these barriers faced by different groups and also build the capacity of actors to promote an inclusive response for the affected communities at both displacement and return situations through awareness raising, training and coaching. Using the findings identified by HI in surveys, HI will aim at tackling existing barriers to increase level of access to those services. By doing so, HI expects to ensure meaningful access to all planned activities. Gender and Age MainstreamingHI is committed to equal access for people of all genders and a gender-tailored, conflict sensitive response that caters to the different needs and vulnerabilities of all people. The project design seeks to ensure access to services for women, men, boys, and girls of all ages through integrating gender mainstreaming at every stage of the project cycle. Among the steps that HI takes include, but are not limited to, the following:• Gender Sensitive Recruitment and Gender Balanced Teams: HI’s Human Resources seeks to recruit, to the extent possible, gender-balanced teams for Risk Education, Psychosocial Support, and Physical Rehabilitation support. Moreover, all HI staff are required to sign and comply with the PSEA policy. • Gender and Age Disaggregated Data Collection and Decision-Making: HI’s operational and monitoring teams collect data disaggregated by age and gender at every stage of the project, to ensure that the different needs of men, women, boys, and girls inform all decision-making. • Gender and age tailored programming: HI undertakes all measures, to the extent possible, to reach all beneficiaries through its project design and implementation process, based on its contextual experience. ERW/IED/Mine contaminationConsidering the level of contamination in HI areas of intervention, HI, as part of its comprehensive approach, combine to the extent possible its health programing with its protection programing related to Arms Risk Reduction and aiming at reducing the impact of the Conventional Weapon and Improvised Explosive Devices for conflict-affected populations by promoting safety messaging through Risk Education and land release. HI provides regular Mine Risk Education sessions to all HI staff and other service providers at request.

How is your project accountable to affected population

HI's monitoring evaluation accountability and learning (MEAL) practices are framed by HI's global quality policy and tools and HI Iraq MEAL strategy. HI Iraq understands accountability as giving HI beneficiaries, HI staff and partners the opportunity to understand and/or influence the key decisions which are made during HI actions. HI Iraq accountability commitments are the following;• Transparency - Information sharing: by making information available about HI activities• Participation - Listening to the people HI aim to assist, incorporating their views and analysis in program decisions is a key in accountability. It allows beneficiaries to share their feedback on HI activities. Attention is put on encouraging beneficiaries feedback during and following implementation of the activities, through field based feedback mechanisms. • Monitoring and evaluation - HI undertakes regular post intervention monitoring of beneficiaries currently receiving its assistance in order to gather feedback on the quantity and quality of services received, as well as on what beneficiaries found to be lacking or not useful. This feedback is reviewed and considered by HI's operations team and technical advisors, in order to inform project decision-making, as well as HI's long-term strategy. In addition, pre and post test results are collected and monitored to check the quality and understanding of the safety messages shared during Risk Education sessions. The technical advisor then provides recommendations, adaptation and training to teams based on assessed need• Requests-Complaints management - having a requests and complaints mechanism for the beneficiaries to report directly any issue to HI. In addition to coordination with the IDP call centre. All complaints, responses and follow up are documented. HI conducts regular needs assessments utilising inclusive methodologies to complete Surveys, FGD and analysis of the existing services, meeting with relevant government authorities, community leaders, and interviews with potential beneficiaries, in order to understand needs self-identified by the communities of concern. Findings from these surveys and consultations inform the design of proposals. This ensures that the different needs of men, women and children as well as age are taken into accounts when implementing interventions. In addition, for the clearance and survey activities, from the initial CLO meeting through to the surveys and the clearance to complete the Land Release process are all reliant on input and assistance by the local community. The relationship between the HI clearance and survey teams, the local authorities and the affected community is a key part of HI operations and the HI clearance and survey teams ensure that any additional input or feedback from the affected community is treated courteously and respectfully. The Survey and clearance are living activities in that they must respond to additional information. These inputs are recorded and where necessary documents like the Site implementation Plan or the Site Survey Report are amended accordingly.Likewise, complaints are dealt with expediently. If it is felt that the complaint originates from a lack of understanding of the HI activities then the CLO will be tasked to visit and redress the problem.
