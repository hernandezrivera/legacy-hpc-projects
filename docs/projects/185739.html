Project Summary

To achieve the impact, this project works across two objectives. Firstly general and sexual and reproductive (SRH) health services will be provided through health facilities in camp 12, 13 and 14. The inclusion of general health services is important in ensuring that everyone can easily access the health clinics. However, there will be a particular focus on SRH which is a particular gap in the camps and host communities due to stigma. The project will provide sexual and reproductive health services like ANC, PNC, family planning, STIs, menstrual health management etc. and undertake prevention and response to gender-based violence activities. CARE will also work to increase health-seeking attitudes and behavior by visiting house-to-house to inform people of available services, and provide counselling on family planning options. To address specific gender barriers for women and girls to access SRH services CARE will provide SRH services such as family planning, STI in WGSSs through medical personnels in camp 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16.
CARE will continue assessing how well the program is meeting quality and standards, accountability commitments as well as the adaptation of programs based on learning through a robust MEAL system which will be based on both qualitative and quantitative approaches. CARE will apply various routine and process monitoring mechanisms to monitor quality SRH and general health services. Outcome level changes especially around health seeking behavior of program participants will also be assessed periodically through outcome monitoring and assessments. A MEAL project plan will be developed including method, frequency, responsibility and timeline to guide the whole MEAL process throughout the intervention. All M&E and reporting system will ensure collection, analysis and reporting of sex, age and disability-disaggregated data, ensuring activities meet the differentiated needs of program participants.
Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) is central to all CARE programs and services and in connection to that, a responsive feedback and complaint mechanism will be functioned which will be safe and accessible for all members of the community including women, men, girls and boys. The target communities will be engaged and consulted throughout the intervention through door to door visits, court yard meetings, focus group discussions, different assessments and routine M&E activities.
CARE is participating in SRH WG, Health sector, District level and Upazila level health and family planing, Case Management working group meeting. CARE will ensure active participation in these coordination platform in 2022 and make sure the information shared and decisions made at sector level will be shared within the organization and reflected accordingly. CARE will address coordination gap created due to staff turn over and ensure active participation and coordination in 2022. CARE si coordinating with health partners and CHWG at ground. However, the identified areas to improve in terms of coordination will be taken seriously and CARE will improve coordination with CHWG for better coordination, better coverage and reduce overlapping and collaborative efforts in 2022.
CARE proposes an integrated approach to sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence that will reach 38485 people living in Camps 11,12, 13, 14,15 and 16 in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh.

1 - Identified Needs

In August 2017, violence against Rohingya in Myanmar provoked a massive population movement of Rohingya refugees to Bangladesh. There are now 896,879 refugees in Cox`s Bazar out of which 52% are women and girls who both need and have equal rights to comprehensive, essential, and lifesaving SRH services. Health service provision including sexual and reproductive health (SRH), and mental health cases is another critical gap which has exacerbated with the COVID-19 pandemic. However, within Cox’s Bazar there are complex challenges to ensuring the availability, accessibility, and acceptability of these services and information. Only 16% of male and 23% of female respondents are aware about the SRH services (CARE, March 2020). According to the studies, refugee camps are overcrowded, health facilities are more than 30 minutes away and mostly concentrated in similar areas in Ukhiya, and unavailability of medicine, treatment or service are key barriers to support the delivery of quality services. According to Gender and Intersectionality Analysis Report 2020, specific challenges women and girls faces in accessing health services includes fear of SGBV, lack of female health professionals and lack of mobile networks to call for an ambulance to take pregnant women to hospital in emergency situation particularly for refugee women-47% of maternal deaths in the camps are due to pregnancy-related issues and delays in accessing services. This coupled with service gap in the health sector, including family planning provision, particularly long-acting methods, availability and timely access to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape/intimate partner violence services and first-line support for GBV survivors worsens the crisis. According to Health Sector Bulletin # 15, the number of family planning uses is increasing including utilization of Long-Acting Reversible Contraceptive (LARC) pills. According to CARE’s March 2020 baseline assessment, 52% of females out of 83 sample size of reproductive age reported using contraception with only 12% of female respondents stating that they can make decisions about their own healthcare (CARE, March 2020) while male respondents confirming only 1% of their partners (wives) make decision on family planning. The most marginalized have specific needs relating to SRH, including adolescent girls and people with disabilities. SRHR needs of Rohingya and host community women and adolescent girls require urgent attention. Though reports show the improving trend of facility deliveries there is still significant number of deliveries takes place at home. According to the JRP (2021), the host community faces the same needs as the refugee community with reports of incurring debts to cover their medical expenses suggesting insufficient access to free health services. Additionally, the COVID-19 pandemic and its impact on movement restrictions further increased the challenge to access the SRH services. These limitations contribute to increasing the risk of maternal mortality, unintended pregnancies and other adverse sexual and reproductive health outcomes among women and girls including GBV incidents. Due to cultural factors limiting women’s mobility and placing a stigma on SRH and GBV, there is a need to access women and girls at their doorsteps. Integrated approaches, especially to protection, are prioritized in the JRP 2021. The GBV sub-sector is calling for SRH to be routinely integrated into GBV programming. The refugee community is known for its conservative practices, which implies that promoting the role of women in society may create tensions and that the safety of female staff and participants could be compromised. Prevailing social stigmas, wide acceptance of GBV and impunity against perpetrators might put those surviving further at risk when collecting information around their case.
