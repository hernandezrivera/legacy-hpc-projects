Project Summary

This project started in January 2021. As part of this intervention, AAH provides technical and organizational support to the health centers of the two health districts regularly affected by shocks in order to better equip them to respond to health / nutrition emergencies and to address psychological distress. Health workers and community volunteers participated in trainings on health / nutrition / mental health and care practices so they can better address the different needs and facilitate referral to activities provided by AAH and other actors in the region. AAH sets up mobile clinics to provide emergency assistance in nutrition / health / mental health for affected and isolated vulnerable populations in the Mayo Sava & Mayo Tsanaga divisions). The package of services and care is delivered free of charge. It targets children under five, pregnant and lactating women, but also disabled people and the elderly among the newly displaced. With regard to the MHCP (mental health and care practices) component, the activities targets children over six and adults with a specific focus on pregnant and lactating women to ensure they follow appropriate care practices and to limit the impact of traumatic stress on pregnancies and infants.
Project Targets: Adults and children Under 5 and above, PLW including IDPs / Refugee, CHWs: including IDPs / Refugee, Health staff and health committee's members, community leaders including IDPs / Refugees, Persons With disabilities including IDPs / Refugees, the elderly : 60 and > including IDPs / Refugee, Host populations.
The project will target 100 people at risk of GBV including 50 GBV survivors (women, men, girls, boys) who will received at least one form of assistance. This may include, but is not limited to, mental health support and case referral. Within this framework, the team will integrate the issue of GBV and psychological first aid training for 200 staff, community relay and local leaders.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The situation in the Far North region of Cameroon remains characterized by the persistence of non-state armed groups’ (NSAGs) activities in the departments of Mayo-Sava, Mayo-Tsanaga and Logone and Chari (SitRep OCHA August 2022). As a result of this recurrence of incidents, 1,281 new displaced households (10,607 people) were registered in August 2022, including 788 households (6,508 people) in the arrondissements of Koza and Mayo-Moskota, in Mayo-Tsanaga, and 493 households (4,099 people) in the arrondissement of Hilé-Alifa in Logone and Chari. In addition, since mid-August 2022, the region has been experiencing flooding. As of 20 September, more than 37,000 people were affected, including 9,000 in Logone et Chari and 1,300 in Mayo-Tsanaga. The priority needs are for food, shelter/AME, WASH and health. According to OIM (DTM 39), 3 219 households (11 930 individuals) were affected by floods in 20 localities in the Divisions of Mayo-Danay and Mayo-Kani from July to August 2022. According to our data:• 94% of SAM cases reported as cured (728 recovered out of 777 cases managed); 60 % of responses to shocks triggered autonomously by health structures supported in preparing for emergency responses. They were 2 alerts in June: population displacement and flooding (in Moskota and Koza 2); on May, one flood in Waza. 40% of villages covered with Nutrition activities could set up a functioning iCCM+ approach (16 out of 40); Community actors issued 8 alerts that were later confirmed by humanitarian actors. These alerts were about yellow fever, measles, displacements and floods; 13 680 people, i.e 7 509 female and 6 171 male (including 3 673 IDPs) were received and treated in HFs, MC and ICCM+ for primary health care.An MHPSS assessment (April 2021) done by AAH in two health districts (Koza and Mora) underlined that 83% of the persons show psychological distress and weak resiliency. Throughout this first year, AAH teams carried out psycho-education adapted to children and adults. Depending on the level of distress of the participants, a mental trauma management protocol is offered at the mobile clinic sites. In the case of responses to shocks in brief interventions, the teams propose emotional stabilization exercises to help individuals better manage stress.Output 1: The specific capacities of affected local populations are strengthened in terms of treatment of simple cases of childhood illnesses, prevention and health and nutritional surveillance.Activity 1: Implementation of the iCCM + approach in areas without access to health careActivity 2: Implementation of the MUAC / oedema strategy by mothers in areas without access to health careActivity 3 Identification of isolated communities and set up of health surveillance system in conjunction with the RRM teamsActivity 4 Training of CHWs on mental health and care practices and community leaders on psychological first aid / referral of GBV cases Output 2: The health facilities of the Mayo Sava & Mayo Tsanaga divisions, the most confronted with the influx of populations affected by shocks, have their response capacities in Nutrition / health / mental health improved, for the most vulnerable groups (PLWs, children 0-5 years)Activity 1: Support to affected health facilities for preparing responses to different degrees of potential shocksActivity 2- Provision of free care for U 5, antenatal and postnatal care for PLWs Output 3: Emergency assistance in nutrition / health / mental health is provided via mobile clinics for affected and isolated vulnerable populations in the Mayo Sava & Mayo Tsanaga divisions (Cameroon) Activity 1: Establishment of 2 mobile clinics offering free PHC including mental health and care practicesActivity 2: Free treatment of children under 5 for common illnesses in mobile clinicsActivity 3: Free antenatal and postnatal care in mobile clinicsActivity 4: Establishment of an emergency referral system
