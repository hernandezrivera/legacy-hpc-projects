Project Summary

The intervention aims to improve immediate access to food for vulnerable households and support resilience of vulnerable populations against shocks through unconditional and conditional food and cash based transfers with the following outcomes
- Food-insecure people in areas affected by natural or human-caused disasters have access to adequate and nutritious food that meet their basic food and nutrition needs during and in the aftermath of shocks.
- Food-insecure people in targeted areas are better able to withstand shocks and stresses throughout the year.

Needs

The food security situation for Somalia in 2018 indicates an estimated 1.5 million people are expected to be in Crisis or worse, which represents half of the needs in 2017 due to severe drought and three consecutive seasons of failed rains across the country. The improved food security situation is due to average and above average Gu rains received and reported to be the wettest season in two decades. (FSNAU). This has led to improved pasture and water availability leading to livestock productivity, herd size, milk availability and improved food access to pastoralists who are reliant on market purchases. Cereal production in Southern Somalia has increased to 17% higher than the long-term average. Sustained humanitarian assistance has also improved households’ access to food needs. Despite this improvement acute food insecurity remains prevalent among pastoral population who lost most of their livestock herds during the 2016/2017 drought leading to destitution, protracted displacement due to conflict and hazards such as the Cyclone Sagar. Households in the crop-dependent Northwest Agricultural livelihood zone and parts of the riverine Southern Somalia have little or no food stocks and limited farm labour opportunities. There are an estimated 2.6 million internally displaced persons with 13 of the primary IDP settlements in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 17,000 of IDPS reported to be in IPC 5. Nutritional survey results reflect an improvement from Critical (15-29.9% GAM) to Serious (between 10-14.9% GAM), however a high level of malnutrition prevalence exists due to underlying and structural causes including access to basic services, low education levels and poor feeding practices. Somalia continues to be affected by protracted food insecurity and natural shocks and given the limited capacity to withstand shocks and stressors, there is need to provide assistance to meet the food consumption gaps of the affected population.

Activities

WFP proposes to implement activities under integrated multi sectoral assistance and leverage resources with other partners, to sustain efforts and enable households meet immediate access to food, protect and restore livelihoods and support resilience against shocks for vulnerable women, men, boys and girls through unconditional food and cash based transfers in targeted IPC 3, 4, 5 areas to reach 1,539,050 people. 1. Beneficiaries will be targeted geographically through gender analysis-informed food security and nutrition needs assessments and targeted at the household level using vulnerability criteria that reflect the particular needs of men, women, boys and girls.2. Biometric registration of beneficiaries through SCOPE, socio-demographic profiling and assistance will be monitored through the SCOPE platform.3. Provision of specifically targeted relief rations providing up to 80 per cent of energy requirements for households as required though in kind or cash unconditional transfers (target – 615,620 beneficiaries)4. Provision of predictable urban safety net cash transfers (target – 100,000 beneficiaries)5. Rehabilitation of productive assets based on seasonality that will enhance resilience and skills training through food/cash transfers (target – 823,430 beneficiaries)6. Monitoring progress of implementation of unconditional activities, provision /distribution of food commodities/cash/vouchers to targeted beneficiaries (target 30% of Food Distribution Points monitored monthly)7. Calls to beneficiaries for feedback on targeting, entitlement and to understand if they face any challenges to receive entitlements (cash or food). Beneficiaries receive messages on programmes and entitlements as well as information on the hotlines and Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs).8. Monthly reporting to the food security cluster9. Development of early warning bulletins, participation in regular food security assessments, regular participation in FSC meetings.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Many vulnerable Somalis, particularly IDPs, struggle to meet their food and nutrition needs even after crises have abated. Others face chronic food insecurity as a result of poverty and exposure to shocks over many years. In response to this, WFP will implement broader safety net and resilience-strengthening activities, working across the humanitarian-development-peace nexus which are also aligned with the humanitarian response plan, chapters 7 and 9 of the NDP; and the livelihoods, employment, education, displacement, and safety nets recovery needs identified in the drought impact needs assessment which focus on resilience-building. This will include the following: 1. Food-insecure IDP and urban households receive safety-net assistance in the form of conditional or unconditional cash-based transfers and gender-informed social behaviour change communication that improve their food security and nutrition status and enhance self-reliance. 2. Food-insecure people receive safety-net assistance in the form of conditional in-kind or cash-based transfers for vocational training and the rehabilitation of community assets and gender-informed SBCC that reduce vulnerability to climate-related shocks and protect access to food.3. School-aged children in targeted areas receive safety-net assistance in the form of a nutritious meal every day they attend school. They also benefit from gender-informed SBCC and from the improved capacity of related ministries to meet their basic food and nutrition needs and increase school attendance and retention. WFP continues to be actively engaged with other stakeholders in the Durable Solutions discussions and meetings. We have also worked closely with other agencies such as UNHCR under the UN funded Peace-building Returnees programme in Baidoa.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

WFP Somalia receives regular feedback and complaints from the field during standard monitoring activities conducted by WFP and WFP-contracted monitors, and through third-party monitoring activities conducted by donors. To complement these essential sources of information and in line with the IASC principle of Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP), WFP Somalia developed a complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) that enables a direct channel and two-way communication between affected populations and WFP. It aims to handle feedback and complaints from all stakeholders, including beneficiaries, community members, CPs and NGOs.Beneficiaries have the ability to report exclusions during monitoring visits, through the hotline. Sensitization strategy defines the plan for beneficiary sensitization about WFP programmes and the WFP CFM. Sensitization is carried out systematically by all parties involved in WFP project implementation (CPs as well as WFP staff at area office and liaison office levels), and it is part of the WFP Somalia Country Office communication strategy. Sensitization is done by way of disseminating sensitization posters at food/cash-based distribution sites, by instructing beneficiaries during registration and enrolment activities. WFP plans to start releasing announcements through radio and other media. These sensitization materials inform beneficiaries and other project stakeholders about the beneficiary rights and their entitlements, about the WFP hotline numbers, and on how to report observed fraudulent conduct, possible corruption, abuse of power, sexual harassment or exploitation, discrimination or any other issues putting beneficiaries or WFP programmes at risk. The cases are followed electronically after being logged to ensure proper follow up.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk mitigation builds on WFP’s extensive experience in inclusive and transparent programme planning. Beneficiary targeting will be strengthened through greater community consultation and sensitization, drawing on the community based targeting guidance developed by the food security cluster and addressing gender inequalities in the targeting criteria. To address national partner capacity and presence, WFP has provided gender and protection training to staff and partners to strengthen their ability to deliver efficient and effective assistance in all locations. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

To mitigate the risk of being unable to reach people in need, WFP engages with national and regional actors to underscore the neutrality and impartiality of its assistance. In areas where WFP has no direct access, WFP uses third party monitoring and deploys innovative transfer measures such as establishing catchment areas in accessible locations. All programmes are implemented in a conflict-sensitive manner (in line with the do no harm approach), accounting for local dynamics, through community consultations and partnerships that seek to increase access and ensure that WFP activities do not exacerbate tensions in the country. This also ensures WFP programmes link local dialogue into programme design, and reduce food insecurity as a driver of conflict, ultimately contributing to local peacebuilding. Poor infrastructure, conflict and road closures pose challenges to the delivery of in-kind assistance. To mitigate this, WFP uses alternative routes where feasible and adopts cash-based transfers where markets are functional. Additionally, WFP measures the feeling of safety and security of beneficiaries going to/at/coming from distribution sites as part of post distribution monitoring. This way, WFP knows if beneficiaries do not feel safe when getting their assistance and WFP can adjust project implementation accordingly. In addition, beneficiaries can report of conflict issues through the CFM.
