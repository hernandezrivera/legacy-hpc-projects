1. Needs

South Sudan is going through the worst humanitarian crisis since independence in 2011, and children are the most affected. The findings of the latest (2023) IPC Analysis are concerning. Acute malnutrition situation is now (July – September) critical in 46 counties, an increase from 31 counties for the same period last year. The situation is projected to worsen between April and June 2024, with the number of counties where acute malnutrition is critical projected to rise to 49, if no drastic measures are taken. Age-appropriate nutrient-dense complementary feeding from 6-24 months remains very low with only 5 per cent of children with minimum acceptable diet and 21 per cent with minimum diet diversityThe situation worsened by from almost 400,000 individuals that have crossed the border from Sudan to South Sudan since the inception of crisis in Sudan that started on 15 April 2023. On top of close to 100,000 people that have crossed the border from Ethiopia to South Sudan since 1 August 2023 due to insecurity, in which, just over half are children. There are many sporadic catastrophes, alongside insecurities in the country. To respond to the worsened and unpredictable nutrition situation and be able to prevent the likely mortality and morbidity, UNICEF's strategy will be to strengthen government and implementing partner capacities to ensure field and last mile presence, guarantee community engagement, localization, feedback, and accountability mechanisms, and the protection of children and women from all forms of risks aimed to reduce morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs. In addition to ensuring increased people’s capacities to withstand or cope with the impact of new and protracted crises is heightened, and durable solutions to root causes.Amidst reduced funding, this will include promoting innovations to meet the needs of children, pregnant and lactating women in extremely complex humanitarian environment and in dire need. Preparedness actions will adopt a risk-informed and conflict-sensitive approach while supporting and championing the rationalization that comes with co-location of services, partners per county, nutrition sites per county, staffing – quantity and quality and alignment of incentives. For instance, as part of the the four frontrunner countries (others being Haiti, Kenya and Madagascar) for the 2023 revised guideline on the prevention and management of wasting and nutritional oedema, UNICEF will leverage the legacy leadership to champion change of the national nutrition strategy and support gradual implementation through strengthened health system blcoks. For this, UNICEF and WFP have agreed to pilot the revised WHO guideline in 22 of 80 counties while maintaining services in majority of critical counties and transition the more stable. The humanitarian strategy is aligned with the inter-agency humanitarian response plan and cluster priorities and complements development assistance that addresses the structural causes of fragility and vulnerability in the country. Supporting South Sudan Government through partners, UNICEF will lead procurement of the lifesaving and other critical nutrition commodities throughout the year 2024. 2. Project activities

Working with WFP, UNICEF will support agreed upon nutrition partners to ensure that there is integrated services for malnutrition prevention and treatment. Thus, ensuring credible partnerships for all service delivery points continue to increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative and curative nutrition services. Mandatorily, Nutrition sites will be used to provide integrated services to prevent and treat malnutrition. Promotion of optimal MIYCN will be strengthen including access to improved diet for young children. UNICEF will certain a people-centred approach and deliberately collaborate with the Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Health and Protection clusters to target and ensure that children and women access a safe, equitable and dignified comprehensive package of essential services through the nutrition sites to prevent malnutrition, including linking with Cash transfer programs to benefit nutritionally vulnerable children and women.Above all, support Government and working through partners, ensure timely forecasting, resource mobilisation, procurement and distribution of the the lifesaving and other critical nutrition commoditiesWhile supporting Government and partners to deliver urgent and life saving nutrition services where the need is dire, and transitioning the more stable service points into Government, UNICEF working with WHO and WFP, will also lead the country in accelerating the roadmap for the future of wasting to introduce the new WHO guidelines on prevention and management of wasting, ensuring:a. Supplemental operations guideline for agreed upon initial 22 of 80 counties b. Supplement operations data collection and reporting tools for the 22 countiesc. Develop operational materials and enhance health worker capacityd. Pilot while documenting learnings and develop recommendations required for existing CMAM guideline reviews that will allow nationwide rollout Specific interventions and activities will be to:1. Protect, support, and promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices2. Provide micronutrient supplements to young children including vitamin A supplementation twice a year for the 6-59 months as well as fortified food supplement that is intended to complement the diet of children aged 6-23 months.3. Screen and treat girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by severe acute malnutrition. Also include the high risk moderate acute malnutrition in children under five years.4. Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development services in the provision of care for children with acute malnutrition.5. Work with the WASH Cluster to ensure provision of WASH kits to children with acute malnutrition and sensitize communities with hygiene and sanitation messages.6. Work with FSL Cluster to promote chicken raring, establish kitchen gardens and promote seed multiplication for vegetable gardens and support fish preservation.7. Particularly for counties planned for the transition, through the anticipated Multi-Donor Trust Fund's Health Sector Transformation Project that replaces the Health Pool Fund, ensure prevention, screening and treatment of malaria and management of diarrhoea at the nutrition service delivery. This will result in an increased number of health and nutrition workers are trained on IYCF counselling and CMAM as per national standards, children who receive vitamin A supplementation and those that are severely wasted who are admitted into treatment as well as facilities reaching at least three SPHERE standards for the management of SAM (Recover rate, Death rate and Defaulter rate).8. Work with the Protection Cluster to ensure mainstreaming of services and referral of child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV) cases identified in the nutrition sites.9. Link with Cash and Voucher Assistance programs to benefit nutritionally vulnerable children and women.
