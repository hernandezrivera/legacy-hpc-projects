Objectives

S01 - To ensure regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access for all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities to sufficient and safe water for drinking and domestic needs. S02 - To ensure all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities have adequate, appropriate and acceptable sanitation facilities to allow rapid, safe and secure access at all times.S03 - To ensure the change of potentially dangerous behaviors through access and exposure to innovative hygiene promotion (including hygiene items) for all refugees living in camps and affected host communities and mainstreaming GBV in WASH delivery.

Needs

1) water supply Improved and maintained: Since the onset of the influx, a toal of 71% of refugee population still access water from the tubewells as compared to the piped water system. The tube wells are prone to contamination where a big percentage are shallow tube wells. In ACF camps and blocks of implementation, safe water provision will be ensured by installation, repair and maitenance of water points. Regular water samples will be tested for microbial contamination and analyis used to inform safe WASH programming. 2) Dignified and gender segregated sanitation facilites improved and maintained: Despite 99% of households reporting using pit latrines in the camps , functionality of the latrines remains low at 78%. 32% of refugee households are facing problems accessing or using latrines due to low coverage (too many people using latrines), while 14% face a problem of distance between the latrines and their shelters. There is still a significant need for further construction of sanitation and hygiene infrastructure to bring coverage up to minimum Sector standards. Challenges remain in achieving full coverage of faecal sludge management (FSM) which can ensure that all latrines are regularly emptied and safe to use. Access to latrines for people with disabled and the elderly remains a challenge. Overall 37% of households use communal/public facilities for bathing (of which 68% are functional ), with high numbers of refugees (especially females) using makeshift bathing spaces inside their shelters. Solid Waste Management (SWM) remains a challenge in the camps, with only 33% of the refugee households reporting using household bins or shared garbage bins and 39% reporting the presence of garbage near their houses. 3) Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: Appropriate hygiene practices for specific risk behaviours remain low with for example, rates of households reporting washing their hands after handling children’s faeces at 32% , and appropriate safe water handling at household level where contamination of stored water is reported at over 90% . Overall, 26% of households reported presence of human faeces in their areas , likely due to the lack of safe disposal of faeces of children under 5 years old, who are also reported as practicing open defecation regularly (46%). A total of 91% of households reported having soap, with 94% of men and 93% of women reporting washing their hands after using the latrine. In respect of Menstrual Hygiene Management, 24% of women and girls were reported as not receiving MHM materials within the last 3 months. In addition, 20% of women and girls were reported as practicing safe disposal of MHM materials. Only 26% of the females are aware of the existence of feedback and complaint mechanisms related to menstrual hygiene management.

Activities or outputs

1) Water Supply improved and maintained: Withing areas of coverage, ACF will install 20 tube wells and ensure operation and maintenance of 8000 water points with laundry blocks and drainage systems. Among the many un-fit/abandoned water points, ACF will decommission 600 and ensure water quality monitoring by collection, testing and analysis of water samples. To ensure sustainability, 50 water user committees will be formed and trained through community engagement. 2) Dignified and gender segregated sanitation facilites improved and maintained: Withing the areas of operation, ACF will construct sufficient safe and gender sentivie latrines, bathing cubicles to ensure adequate availability and access to the target populations.. Sufficient number of latrines will be constructed, while repair and maintenance of sanitation facilities will implemented. This includes decommissioning of ODPS and construction of appropriate FSMs, regualr desludging of full latries and installation of transfer stations for managemnet of faecal sludge. 220 colour coded solid waste management semi-permanent garbage bins and 7,600 rft of drainage systems will be constructed . To enhance protection and optimize WASH facilities utilization, ACF will install solar lighting systems in 2,100 latrines and bathing cubicles. 3) Targeted Improvement, promotion and maintainance of hhygiene: ACF will distribute 3 (three) rounds of 63,000 hygien kits, 29,000 MHM kits, 11,500 household sanitation kits and 564 handwashing kits respectively. 15,000 hygiene promotion sessions will be conducted, IEC/BCC materials will be developed as well as GBV in WASH mainstreaming will be implemented through sessions, trainings and gegregation.

Key project/performance indicators

1) Number of targeted people in settlements benefitting from safe water of agreed standards and meeting demand for domestic purpose2) Number of targeted women, men, children in settlements who are benefitting of functionnal latrines of agreed standards3) Number of population covered under fecal sludge managmenet safety net4) Percentage of targeted women, men, boy and girls who are satisfied with the hygiene related information shared5) Number of targeted households who have received a WASH Hygiene kit and/or a top up kit and/or a voucher in the last three months
