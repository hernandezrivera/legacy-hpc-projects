Project Summary

In Mayendit and Mayom Counties, Unity State, Samaritan’s Purse, South Sudan, provide life-saving primary health services using a mobile health approach in eight (8) separate hard to reach, crisis affected communities who are unable to reach existing health care facilities and who have are impacted by conflict and flooding, with many needing to temporarily relocate as well as host IDP’s from surrounding areas.
Objectives:
1. To provide and improve equitable access to basic, lifesaving primary health care in hard to reach, communities (who lack a health service provision), particularly prioritizing vulnerable community members, including women and children, the elderly, people with disabilities and those in need of MHPSS, survivors of SGBV and trauma in selected communities in Northern Mayendit.
2. To reduce morbidity and mortality through the provision of basic primary health care, infection prevention and control measures (IPC), rapid diagnostic testing (RDT), treatment, preventative health care strategies (malaria prophylaxis), routine & reactive immunizations, community health education and disease surveillance and reporting.
3. Provide consistent services, awareness and education on health the various services and advantages of preventative health measures to improve health seeking behaviour and positive behaviour change in the outreach communities.
4. To promote and enable increased resilience and build the capacity of outreach communities, through strong community engagement, representation of all community members in program development (such as developing shelters for services or fences), site development and engagement of local professionals and community health workers in each target site.
5. Improve coordination of humanitarian activities through collaboration with the CHD/MOH and other partners as well as multi/cross sectorial activities to improve overall health in Mayandit County.
6. SP will participate in single and multisectoral assessment activities to assess ongoing or developing needs and program impact.
Mayendit County Mobile Health Services
- SP Mobile Health Clinicians in Mayendit and Mayom include a Program manager who is Medical Doctor, with Mental Health and Paediatric training and experience, a Clinical Officer, Registered Nurse, Registered Midwife (female), a dispenser (all local professionals), vaccinators and locally engaged Community Health Workers (16F, 16M) provide Primary Health services; general outpatients for communicable, non-communicable diseases as well as initial first aid, screening and referral for SGBV/IPV, MHPSS screening, MHPSS first aid and referral, Reproductive health, antenatal, postnatal and neonatal care, nutrition screening and referral, routine vaccinations and community health awareness and education. On a daily basis the team triage and prioritize the most vulnerable, including women and children, the elderly, people with a disability, the very ill and those who have suffered trauma. The team link in with partners for specialized services such as for SGBV, disability (such as cleft lip & Fistula repair) and MHPSS services. The services are informed from learnings through regular community engagement activities & feedback mechanisms. All data is protected, disaggregated by gender and age and reported weekly to the CHD/MOH, the Health Cluster 5 W’s & WHO/EWARS.
The project seeks to serve 45,000 (22,500 male; 22,500 female) people. Of the targets, an estimated 8,700 are children U5; 16,000 children ages 5 to 18 years; 18,000 adults ages 18 to 59 years; and 3,200 adults over 60 years. Approximately 7,400 beneficiaries are expected to be IDPs.

1 - Needs

Mayendit is a very resource poor area and isolated County with a vulnerable population due to its lack of development and geographical remoteness as a result of the surrounding rivers and wetlands that impede free movement. There are few roads connecting Mayendit easily to other counties or internally among its different payams. The situation worsens during the rainy season – when flooding and swamp areas remain in place for between 7-9 months a year. Flooding has been a record levels and affecting more than 60,000 people, causing displacement (Inter-Agency Flood Assessment Report, 2021). The only referral locations include the PHCC in Rubkai (limited capacity) and for surgery is MSF in Leer, which is inaccessible to many locals for extended periods of time (Relief Web Health Service Functionality Monthly Bulletin, March 2020). Increased access to primary health services by means of a MMU in Mayendit North will improve health outcomes for treatable illness and decrease late presentation at the Leer health facility, which is 20 km (or approximately 40 minutes’ drive) away by 4WD.Conflict and resource scarcity have impacted the population. Mobile Medical Health services are at times necessary to provide essential primary health care to challenged populations in humanitarian crises. The mobile medical teams are positioned in difficult to reach areas with people in need identified by the CHD/MoH and/or the Health Cluster and SP. Mayom County experiences significant gaps in health care provision (WHO Health Service Functionality Monthly Bulletin, March 2020). The Implementing partner for the HPF also has significant gaps in human resource and supplies, Conflict and resource scarcity have affected the population. Mobile Medical Health services are at times necessary to provide essential primary health care to challenged populations in humanitarian crises. The people of Mayom also make up a proportion of the more than 200,000 people who have been severely affected by the protracted flooding since 2019 and into 2020 and 2021 (IOM DTM Mayom, September 2020). The mobile medical teams are positioned in difficult to reach areas with people in need identified by the CHD/MoH and/or the Health Cluster and SP. SP are targeting approximately 45,000 (22,500 male; 22,500 female) people (an estimated 8,700 children U5; 16,000 children ages 5 to 18 years; 18,000 adults ages 18 to 59 years; and 3,200 adults over 60 years) with life-saving primary health services.- Primary health consultations, Reproductive Health, Nutrition, SGBV/IPV and MHPSS screening & referrals (particularly with increased trauma and stress due to flooding), routine vaccinations and community health education and awareness; - Health care to remote communities (without functional facilities) - To provide Primary Health Care to the most vulnerable in a community – women and children, the elderly, people with a disability and those with severe illness or trauma – for whom reaching a static facility is impossible – particularly when cut off by floods. - To flood affected communities, - To IDP’s (from flooding and or conflict) Provide consistent weekly primary health care services despite flooding 2020 by adapting transport modalities; such as Quad bikes, walking and canoes as well as land cruiser when conditions are favourable which is not often.

2 - Project activities

- Regularly engage with community members and the CHD, RRC, Chiefs - meeting to listen and discuss needs and ensure the mobile services and health education are meeting the needs of the communities and reporting activities. - Travel to target remote communities (often by canoe) to provide life-saving primary health services, including; Outpatient (OPD) services; management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, early child health, MUAC assessments, maternal & newborn health & reproductive health (MNH & RH), routine vaccinations/EPI, wound care and community health education. - Screen, Identify, provide initial ‘first aid’ and referral for survivors of SGBV/IPV, as well as people identified with PHPSS needs (- with referral to specialist partners) - Provision of Snake bite venom to the local PHCC when a snake-bite victim is referred-as a response to an increase in snake bites in Mayendit due to the floods – with monitoring as needed.- Distribution of long lasting insecticide mosquito nets, birth kits and soap.- Active and ongoing triaging ensures the medical teams prioritize care for vulnerable groups, including women and children, people with a disability, the elderly and those seriously unwell or with trauma. - Care, referral and evacuation for people with Gunshot wounds in Mayandit. - Coordination with the SP WASH team to work with the community to install latrines near each of the sites and WASH hygiene promotion activities in the target communities. - Synchronize with the WASH and FSL teams to engage with and roll out activities in the Four target communities on the same days where possible. - Build the capacity of the team through ongoing trainings and CME. - Ensure responsible and appropriate dispensing practices through training and through the implementation of a solid pharmaceutical tracking system. - Collaborating with the Health IP and the CHD in providing/ donating essential medicines when there is an urgent need to the IP, CASS as well as logistical support for example to transport vaccines.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Following community consultation, the MMU team may adjust their target population and destination based on identified needs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Samaritan’s Purse is dedicated to giving programming with safety, dignity, and a Do No Harm policy. Health services are promoted so that all people have meaningful access. Triage is a priority of the clinic activities – prioritizing the most vulnerable – including women and girls. Daily Community health sessions enable engagement with the waiting patients – many of whom have been already prioritized to receive care before those who are less vulnerable. This includes the vulnerable such as persons with special needs, the elderly, pregnant mothers, and SGBV survivors. In order to ensure all are considered from the assessment to completion of the project, information will be timely and accessible. The team are well known to the community and communication is free flowing. Feedback mechanisms are communicated to the community as part of health education and CC. Feedback is free flowing – aside form CC - at times a community member may wish to remain anonymous and will feedback through the locally engaged CHW’s.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Each SP field office has a designated Safeguarding Officer, who actively promotes protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and supports creating an environment that protects and prevents harm as a result of all country activities. Beneficiaries are made aware of the Code of Conduct that SP staff and representatives have to abide by, the known consequences and how to report any concerns through the established in-country complaints and feedback mechanisms. In addition, SP employs an experienced Gender and Protection Program Development Officer (G&P PDO). The G&P PDO works closely with project staff to develop and promote cross-cutting social safeguards to be embedded in project activities. SP provides training on PSEA to all their staff. The team provide PSEA awareness as part of their community education sessions on clinic days. The MM team have also engaged recognized Protection partners on the ground to travel out with the team to the sites to provide awareness and community education as well as regular training for the team The SP MM team have weekly meetings where community and staff feedback is discussed and have endeavored to adjust priorities to meet community expectations and feedback. over the last 9 months of services - consultation and feedback has led to changes and improvements such as site location based on changing community needs and movement due to flooding, suggestions for health education topics by the community, improved triaging to ensure the most vulnerable are prioritized, cross sectoral collaboration such as the provision of latrines by the SP WASH team as part of Hygiene promotion, better community participation in protecting & maintaining of the mobile sites, an improved ANC set up (more privacy in some locations) and to fast track the roll out of EPI services at the request of the community and the CHD. The code of conduct is based on the IASC six core principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse, 2019.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Prior to the launching of the MM services, there was significant community consultation between SP, the CHD and local community members, including; regular community members, community leaders, religious & spiritual leaders, youth and women’s associations. Ongoing community consultation provides a platform for community members to express their priorities and particular health concerns. The team conducted sensitization and awareness about the scope of service provision by the MM teams - to manage expectations and ensure the target communities were informed regarding what the MM teams will be providing and when and where they will recommend referral. This is a dynamic process, while the MM modality may not meet all the team preferences and needs - consultations has enabled a free flow of communication.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

8.1 - The Team have a solid and well communicated routine – the team have left mats with the community for people to sit and wait. There is a strong established understanding by the community that the team will conduct their triage process and will not be intimidated or bullied into giving any service. There has never been an incident of violence in any of the sites for the duration of this project so far. By strong relationships that have been built the community know what to expect and the team and the community respect the processes. despite reducing the distance for people to access health care – some vulnerable community members may travel a long distance – the team hope by prioritizing women and children that this risk will be reduced. The mobile health team aim to complete their activities by 3:30 to ensure and enable people to return home safely during daylight hours. 8.7 - All patient information is recorded on consultation forms designed to capture essential patient/beneficiary related information. These forms are kept in a secure cupboard in a secure cupboard and office. Essential Patient information is entered into the Patient Database Workbook – The workbook collates and summarizes the overall patient/beneficiary clinical activities and is used to track clinical trends, common disease burden and generate reports such as the EWARS and DHIS 2 and Health facility functionality (HFF). Database workbooks are password protected with access limited to a few key staff. With significantly improved internet patient information could be entered into the workbook in real time reducing the need for paper forms. 8.8 - The information and recommendations from external stakeholders is measured up against – population data, assessment information/publications, distance from existing services, roads and access and impact form environmental disasters – In order to make balanced decisions. 8.10 - The risk may be there however the sites where the team set up are well traversed and so may not have a risk of explosion – there may be risks in bush land for people walking to the sites however the community may know the history and safe areas to walk. Include awareness of unexploded ordinances into the health education.
