Project Summary

The project will include training for jobs that are done physically (based on the situation) or online if the condition is suitable for physical work. The main training topics will be ( proposal writing Web-Design, E-Marketing, and Media Production), training programs that are required for the office jobs (Languages, MS. Office), business skills (Entrepreneurship and Introduction to NGOs), Maintenance training programs (Mobile maintenance and Computer maintenance),
The training will offer a "blending" of theoretical and hands-on teaching methods (upfront teaching, visuals, group discussions, practical exercises, learning tests, on-site visits, etc.) and combine different methods in order not to lose the attention of the audience.
Since most training modules will require both conventional classroom teaching and practical/hands-on exercises, IHR will seek the most appropriate training venue for each training subject that offers closed and open-air spaces (workplaces visits, labs, etc.) for the practical exercises.
After the Vocational Training end, three hundred (300) beneficiaries will prepare their proposals or integration proposals the best 200 project will be selected and each proposal will receive 700 $ for each participant to support their activities.

4 - Needs

The NW region is facing huge humanitarian and development needs. The unstable situation in the target area caused by the ongoing war in Syria, the migration crisis, and the additional effects of the COVID-19 pandemic has increasingly adverse effects on the economic, economic, and social situation. Both IDPs and HHs struggle to find a job, the inability to support their family, and difficulties in obtaining education for children and young people. The tenth year of the humanitarian crisis is marked by worries about the economy and its impact on the country's stability. Frustration is growing due to the prolonged stay of displaced persons and the limited number of returns, which results in long-term economic, social, and environmental problems in NW. At the same time, it is worth mentioning that most need assessment conducted in NW stated that the lack of lasting security in Syria, the lack of shelter, lack of access to services, and earning opportunities are the main barriers against the rotation of the lifecycle. Even before the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic, there were mass closures in response to the economic crisis that erupted in September 2019. This led to a catastrophic increase in unemployment and poverty. In Afrin district, 80 % of population numbers are IDPs the youth, women, and PWD are considered the most vulnerable groups in target areas, furthermore, due to the abovementioned bad economic situation most youths in Afrin are an easy to target for recruiting.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.1.1 Project announce and Orientation sessions with Key persons 1.1.2- Recruitment of the staff: According to IHR recruitment procedures we need two weeks to finalize recruitment all direct field staff 1.1.3- Rent and rehabilitate the Centre: during the first month IHR will complete (Rent-Rehabilitation and all furniture purchasing) The training center will be equipped in such a way as to meet the needs of all social groups. The center will create a safe space for youth , where they will be able to spend time under the supervision of qualified social workers. The center will have one large room, which will be equipped to accommodate young people and adults during training in life, business, and technology skills, and will have a separate space for children. Entrepreneurship education equips beneficiaries with additional knowledge in the context of setting up a new venture or company. Besides, the concept of creating a training center is also part of promoting the principle of social cohesion among all target groups. 200 young people aged 18-25 will be selected and invited to participate in training in business and entrepreneurship. These trainings will be divided into 2 cycles, with 100 participants per cycle. Each cycle will be divided into 4 groups, and there will be 25 participants in each group. They will have adequate space to maintain the right distance. Two cycles will start simultaneously in two separate and adapted rooms

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Examples of protection risks: Protection risk (1): Child labor in the established small businesses Mitigating measures: Ihsan RD will be sure that the staff working in this project are trained and fully aware of child safeguarding, Child protection and GBV principles. Protection risk (2): Lack/di􀂨culty of accessibility for PWDs in the proposed activities. Mitigating measures: 1- Availability of wheelchair for PWD at the reception point (IRD contribution), a trained staff to deal with PWDs, Priority for the PWDs, included in the criteria, House to house distributions Protection Risk (3): Sometimes, crowding during distribution of bread, may not provide the services at the desire quality, which might affect the BNFs dignity, especially women. Mitigating measures: :Set up separate waiting and entry lines at registrations Raise the awareness of community leaders on GBV and child protection principles and make sure that all BNFs can voice their concerns through the designated channels. Protection Risk (4): Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation for women during implementation of the project. Mitigating measures: Choosing appropriate time for distributions small business grants (cash vouchers), voucher exchanging in Hawala agent will taking place during daylight hours to allow women to return home safely, ensuring regular security and incidence reporting, conducting awareness sessions for the women about the issues surrounding with GBV to minimize and avoid such damage.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1- All protective tools materials needed to avoid COVID-19 infections are available during project cycle 2- Timely disbursement of funds 3- Stable security situation Risks and mitigation: 1- Corvid -19 Outbreak in the target location Mitigation: Ensuring that all protective measures had been carefully considered and applied to avoid COVID-19 infections all stage of project such as, social distancing at least two meters, providing of masks and gloves for all persons involved (field staff, vendors, drivers and ensuring the availability of sterilization materials at distribution points). Applying all updated preventive measures for COVID-19 shared by WHO, ERL and FSL cluster. 2- Delay in funds Mitigation: Senior level of coordination with SCHF team and responding to requirements to push them to release the requested payments at the optimal dates 3- Changing the security situation Mitigation: Monitoring and analysis security situation, temporary suspension of field works. When it is impossible to implement the project due to the bad security condition, IHR will develop a plan B for project implementation to change the project location to safer areas after advanced collaboration and coordination with SCHF team and ERL cluster.4- Cash not spent on intended needs. Mitigation: The CVA will be restricted to proposal components delivered by the beneficiary to start his business only
