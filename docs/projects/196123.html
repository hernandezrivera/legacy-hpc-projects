Project Summary

BRAC’s Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector has been working in Cox’s Bazar since 2017 for ensuring food security and self-reliance of the refugees and host community affected by the crisis. BRAC will implement project under the newly formed Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector (LSDS) to continue its work in attaining sector’s objectives along with BRAC’s strategic objectives of localization, humanitarian development nexus, resilience building of the affected Rohingya and host community and strengthening coordination among the humanitarian actors and stakeholders.
Through its Livelihoods and Skills Development programme, BRAC aims to assist refugees to acquire transferable and portable skills relevant to the labour market needs in Myanmar and the camp contexts, that would support sustainable return and integration (in Myanmar) when conditions permit; while enabling refugees to maintain hope for the future and have dignified and productive lives in the camps, including through engagement in multi-sectoral camp-based activities, minor livelihood activities, and community works, with the utilisation of acquired competencies. Accordingly, strategies will be geared towards enhancing skills development, economic and social empowerment, and self-reliance of the Rohingya refugees pending durable solutions. BRAC sees potential to provide support for skills and livelihood activities in Camp 2E, 7, 10, , 14, 15, 17, and Kutupalong RC (total 7 camps) and Haldia Palong, Jalia Palong and Ratna Palong union as host communities. There will be 1950 youths between 18-59 years with 50% women and 1% persons with disabilities.
The Livelihoods and Skills Development programme will target the youths and adults (both men and women) in providing them with portable and valuable skills training that are aligned with GoB-UN Framework on Skills Development for Rohingya Refugees/FDMNs and Host Communities. Based on the identified needs of skill trades, the programme would provide center-based vocational training on garments and sewing machine operator, community health worker, concreter (masonry), general mechanic, solar mechanic, plumbing, electrician, agricultural production, baking and midwifery. As a part of the vocational training, the programme will develop training modules on the identified trades aligned to international standards e.g., Myanmar National Qualification Framework (MNQF) and/or ASEAN Qualifications Reference Framework (AQRF) standards. Each of the vocational training courses would be 360 hours in duration and the project would arrange the provision of formal assessment of the training participants and successful candidates would be receiving level-1 completion certificates. The certification would help the project participants in pursuing their future education/training, as well as in negotiating higher wages both in Camp and in Myanmar as skilled manpower.
The programme interventions in the host community will integrate disaster resilient and green entrepreneurship and livelihood options for vocational skills development activities to improve the resilience of the beneficiaries from external shocks, minimise environmental degradation and increase food security-income.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Refugee influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 5, 2022) findings reveals that 95 percent of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance. In the Host Community (HC), 52% HHs have found to be moderately to highly vulnerable due to economic downturns, lacking adequate and expanded assistance, adequate livelihoods recovery initiatives, and skyrocketing price spikes of essential commodities, especially food. (REVA, 2022). Due to limited access to regular income & livelihood opportunities, the FDMN population are highly reliant on humanitarian assistance. According to JMSNA-2021, 72% HHs in refugee camps are living with food insecurity and livelihood gaps. 59% HHs reported food and 30% reported access to self-reliance activities as priority needs. 70% of HHs have adopted livelihood based coping strategies in the FDMN community. About 33 percent of Rohingya households and 4 percent of host community households had no work-based income sources other than assistance. No significant change was observed in the different types of economic activity in which men and women engaged among Rohingya households. Men continued participating in daily wage labour or running small businesses and shops, while women continued engaging in home-based activities or relatively more secure salaried jobs. In the FDMN community 30% income comes from work based income sources whereas in the HC 95% income comes from work based income sources. Gap between men and women in labor force participation is very high as 65% of men are participating in labor work whereas only 12% of women participated in labour force. In HC 71% men are engaged in labor force participation whereas the only 17% of women participated in labour force.BRAC will focus on supporting host communities’ existing skills and livelihood priorities and providing skills helpful to adapt to pandemics, disasters, or crisis and skills development and income generation to strengthen economic self-reliance through improving capacity and supporting local communities to integrate into labour markets, whether through employability, entrepreneurship, green jobs or support to affected markets. Meanwhile, for the Rohingya refugees, it will be through a tailored social and economic self-reliance support based on self-defined needs, starting with life skills and vocational skills development to assist refugees in gaining transferable skills that can be used upon their return to Myanmar, as well as they can utilise the gained transferable vocational skills within existing camps and humanitarian sector response needs/areas (e.g., shelter, wash, health, FSS).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Rationalisation will ensure that common strategies, systems, protocols, and tools are used across all the targeted areas. This will lead towards a standardized and cost-effective approach to providing equitable services to refugees and host communities which also ensure a minimum standard of service deliveries. BRAC will undertake a holistic humanitarian approach, with other partners and stakeholders, to enhance livelihoods and resilience of the vulnerable host community, promote self-reliance of Rohingya refugees and strengthen sustainable natural resource management for both communities. BRAC has a strong relationship with government stakeholders such as DC, UNO, Chairmen, RRRC and the CICs. In addition, BRAC’s LSD programme will establish effective coordination with other relevant actors and actively participate in sector and working group meetings for smooth implementation and avoiding duplication.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

BRAC focuses on non-discriminatory access to support and services, identifying the most vulnerable and their specific needs and strengthening positive community protection capacity. As a PSEA network member, BRAC has compliant policies prioritising protection and safeguarding, emphasising on 'Do No Harm' to prevent unintended negative effects of any programme. BRAC’s LSD programme will ensure meaningful access, participation and empowerment of its participants through programme design, planning and implementation with embed accountability at all levels. BRAC's protection and harassment policies will be strictly followed, and staff and volunteers will be trained on safeguarding, prevention and reporting on GBV/SGBV before deployment. Any protection issues including CP, GBV, SEA, protection of persons with disabilities as well as specific protection issues that arise in disasters and emergencies will be addressed through information-sharing, advocacy or referral to specialised actors.

3.2 - Gender

BRAC ensures that special needs of all gender groups are identified, taken into consideration and guided by the Gender Policy. Gender, and youth issues are important to consider for Skills Development among the refugees and host communities. BRAC's LSD programme will ensure at least 50% of its participants in the skill development training are girls and women. In addition, participation of persons with disabilities (minimum 1%) and LGBTQI would be prioritised. The programme will have a strong team of GESI and gender mainstreaming team to ensure the participation of women and persons with disabilities. BRAC will conduct periodical gender analysis to understand and tailor to potential trades for Skills Development of the participants. The gender mainstreaming team will also provide training gender mainstreaming and develop capacity of the project staff, and relevant volunteers engaged in the project.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Safety of the programme participants is a primary consideration of BRAC. The prevention of sexual harassment, child and adolescent abuse, child labour, intimidation, violence, bullying, humiliation and discrimination, neglect and exploitation is ensured at all levels of programmes from desgining to implementation. BRAC's LSD programme will train, supervise and monitor its staff and volunteers as well as establish multiple reporting channels and provision of investigation and punishment. In addition, there is a dedicated PSEA committee in BRAC with a focal person to deal with all cases including child safeguarding issues.

3.4 - Diversity

Diversity refers to the differences based on gender, age, disability, cultural background, sexual orientation, social and economic background. BRAC promotes equity and equality at every stage of project design and provides services to all according to the needs and insists on upholding the rights and dignity of the affected people. BRAC's LSD programme will be designed to address and respond to the needs of more vulnerable communities irrespective of gender, age, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, nationality, and disabilities. BRAC has a Gender Response Team (GRT) which will monitor and ensure the inclusion of gender-diversified people in the LSD programme. The programme will be respectful toward the local culture and practices. Therefore, all the modules and materials will be designed considering local and cultural context.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

While designing humanitarian projects, BRAC considers UNHCR’s AAP framework- i) participation and inclusion; ii) communication and transparency; iii) feedback and response; and iv) organisational learning and adaptation. In the designing phase, BRAC’ LSD team will ensure community participation through consultations. BRAC has a specific Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) to address specific needs of the Rohingya refugees. The LSD sector will collect and analyze gender, age, and disability-disaggregated data, and conduct participatory gender analysis to understand various needs related to livelihoods and skills, capacities, barriers and aspirations. LSD sector will regularly analyse the feedback/trends/unresolved issues and link with respective organisations/sectors for feedback referral to ensure AAP. Based on the community needs, interventions, planning and priority of the programme will be modified.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC already has an EOC (Emergency Operation Centre) and ERT (Emergency Response Team) structure with representatives from all the sectors to respond during emergency situations. BRAC's DRR sector provides basic Disaster Risk Management and Emergency Response training to project staff, including community volunteers, to respond effectively to an emergency crisis situation. They also provide first aid training to all staff and volunteers as a part of their skill development. Furthermore, BRAC has an Environmental and Social Safeguard Framework (ESSF), which applies a science-based precautionary approach to project design and implementation. BRAC’s all interventions anticipate and address any potential harm before it happens. Where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, circumstances of not having scientific certainty will not be used as a reason for avoiding cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation in BRAC’s project implementation mechanism.
