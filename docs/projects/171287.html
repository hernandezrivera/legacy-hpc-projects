Project Summary

UNRWA's proposed intervention aims at ensuring the provision of primary health care for vulnerable Palestine Refugees in the West Bank. Through the deployment of mobile heath teams, the project will provide essential health services to vulnerable and remote communities in Area C and East Jerusalem whose access to health services was significantly reduced due to access and movement restrictions exacerbated by the context of COVID-19 pandemic. Furthermore, the action will help preventing the spread of COVID-19 by distributing awareness raising material on best practices on how to prevent the transmission of the virus.

1 - Needs

The 2020 Covid-19 emergency put an increased pressure on an already overburdened health system. Before COVID-19, it was estimated that in the West Bank, approximately 408,000 people were in need of humanitarian health intervention, with limited access to basic primary healthcare services (HNO 2020). Many communities have faced restricted access to basic health care as a result of the presence and actions of Israeli checkpoints, as well as of the restrictive construction permit regime limiting the introduction of new medical service providers. The need to scale-up the response to acute health needs, while strengthening health preparedness at the facility and community level appeared very clearly, as the health system was already overstretched before the crisis, without capacity to absorb shocks. In March 2020, in the beginning of the COVID-19 outbreak, UNRWA Protection team has conducted an assessment of 22 communities living in remote areas (Area C) and in the seam zone to assess their situation in terms of access to services and their primary needs. The communities, with a total population of nearly 30,000 refugees, have identified access to health services as an increased priority and their most pressing need. These concerns were raised in light of the additional restrictions imposed following the COVID-19 outbreak which has added new challenges to the access and movement difficulties. The new restrictions significantly and further reduce regular and routine Palestinian refugee access to urban and village centers where health services are offered. Many Palestine refugees in Area C have ceased access to any health service. Another significant factor regards public transportation concerning especially those who live in remote areas, far away from urban or village centers where health services are located. These populations are not able to reach health centers by foot or by private vehicles and rely mostly on public transportation. However, due to the restrictions on public transportation, many people have no means to reach medical services. Through its work with the remote communities targeted by this project, UNRWA has identified a general need for primary health services due to the remote locations of these communities. The demographics of these communities also reflect the vulnerability categories identified in the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview and Response Plan for the Occupied Palestinian Territories (oPt); namely Bedouin and herders in Area C, refugees and people living below the poverty line. In addition, health services will be prioritized based on additional categories of vulnerability identified within the target communities. The activity targets six remote communities in Area C with a total population estimated at 30,000 people, namely: Um al-Kheir/Dkeieka, Arab Ar Rashyada, Nabi Samwil, Al Eizariya, Barta and Kfar Qadum. Five of these communities are agricultural / herder communities who identify as Bedouin and have been disproportionately affected by the economic impact of the pandemic, with limited means to obtain health services outside the village. Recent studies have also found that Bedouin communities have reported comparatively lower levels of knowledge about COVID-19 (UNICEF KAP preliminary findings, 2020). In addition to the identification and prioritization of the basic health needs for Bedouin and herders in Area C; additional vulnerability factors of gender, age and disability intersect to create additional and distinct health needs for sub-groups within these refugee communities. For example, multiple studies conducted in the oPt have highlighted additional barriers for elderly, pregnant and lactating women, members of female headed households, and persons with disabilities to access health care. An estimated 5.1 per cent of the West Bank population has a disability, with 6.8% per cent of households recently reporting living with at least one person with a diagnosed physical disability (PCBS data, 2017; UNICEF KAP, preliminary findings, 2020). This comprises approximately 1,500 persons within the target population of this project. UNFPA has also highlighted additional barriers for women and girls to access basic health services, particularly in rural/remote areas, with elderly women and women and girls with disabilities facing higher levels of exposure to violence, discrimination and access to basic services (oPt Gender Alert Analysis, 2020; UNFPA, Women and Girls with Disabilities, 2019; EducAid Italy, 2020; Al Marsad, 2020). Research also indicates that elderly persons are disproportionately represented in this group, with almost one-third of elderly Palestinians reporting more than one disability (BMC Public Health, 2019). The need for continued prenatal and postnatal health care during the COVID-19 pandemic has also been reported to the UNRWA Protection team as a priority for several of the target communities. Recent studies on the Palestinian population estimate that 12 per cent of households reported having a pregnant person in their household (UNICEF KAP preliminary findings, 2020). Moreover, the intermittent imposition of restrictions on movement during lockdowns has significantly impacted the capacity of women and girls experiencing GBV to receive essential services, including health services. As the provision of health care is often a primary entry point for GBV survivors, the offering of essential primary health care for these communities will offer at risk groups and survivors a vital means to obtain basic health services, information on available UNRWA GBV hotlines and support, and obtain appropriate referral services.

2 - Activities

UNRWA will introduce three Mobile Health Teams (MHT) to ensure that remote refugee communities have access to continuous primary health care services during the COVID-19 outbreak. The activity targets six remote communities in Area C with a total population estimated at 30,000 people, namely:Um al-Kheir/Dkeieka, Arab Ar Rashyada, Nabi Samwil, Al Eizariya, Barta and Kfar Qadum. The targeted communities are located in Area C and East Jerusalem and they experience humanitarian vulnerabilities in terms of isolation and have significantly reduced access to health services. Many are located several kilometres away from the nearest health clinic. Each UNRWA MHT comprises one doctor, one nurse, one pharmacists, one psychosocial counsellor, one clerk for registration of patients and a driver. The MHT’s will provide primary health care such as medical consultations, examinations, drug dispensary in a suitable space which will be identified and secured by the communities themselves. MHTs will strive to ensure readiness to interfere in case of need; capacity building (of the team and the community) how to deal in case of any emergency as first aid, life support, infection control; sufficient equipment, material and drug supply; stockpiling lab supplies, drugs, disposables, and IPC supplies (for a period of 2 months’ duration) ; enhanced coordination and communication (with all related health providers and the local community) and provision of health care services.In response to the distinct needs of the groups identified above, the provision of mobile health services will prioritize persons with disabilities, elderly, pregnant and lactating women, children and female headed households. These groups will be prioritized for health services in addition to the high-risk groups for COVID-19 (chronically ill and NCD patients) and SSNP status refugees who are routinely prioritized for health services (abject poor refugee families). Pending funding availability, hygiene kits will be distributed to vulnerable families according to criteria followed by UNRWA’s relief services, as complementary to the provision of health services. UNRWA’s hygiene kits have also been adapted to the distinct needs of the population based on gender, age and disability factors. Inclusive hygiene kits have been specifically tailored to meet the distinct hygiene needs of the elderly, persons with disabilities, women and girls and infants and will be provided as complementary to the provision of health services. Through the provision of essential hygiene materials, the intervention aims at improving the capacities and resilience of the people to reduce the spread of the COVID-19. In addition, awareness raising material about COVID-19 preventive measures will be distributed to beneficiaries when visited by the MHT.The addition of PSS Counsellors to the Health Team to better identify vulnerable individuals identified under this project, to make referrals for GBV survivors, and to conduct the community engagement aspects of the project. Health services will be provided to the remote communities in consultation with the communities in order to identify safe, confidential and accessible health services to all members of the community, including monitoring and responding to feedback. UNRWA’s protection team will also continue to monitor and communicate directly with community leaders and representatives regarding their satisfaction with the services and to support remote health teams to address the health needs of the most vulnerable groups within the community.All health officers working within the project have completed PSEA training and are obligated to report all suspected cases in accordance with UNRWA’s PSEA framework. The Protection teams’ communication and engagement directly with the population will provide additional safeguarding expertise and feeds into UNRWA’s existing PSEA reporting framework.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWAs Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly addressed as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneﬁciaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneﬁciaries, communities or staff. Further, UNRWAs Medium-Term Strategy 2016-2021 explicitly prioritises the "do-no-harm"-principles by stating that UNRWA will address protection challenges that arise from its programme service delivery in education, health, relief and social services, microﬁnance, and infrastructure and camp improvement. This will include focusing on instances when UNRWA, through its actions or policies, unintentionally undermines the realization of speciﬁc rights such as the socioeconomic rights to food, water, health and education. By addressing such concerns through ‘do-no-harm’ and other approaches, UNRWA service delivery promotes and respects the rights of beneﬁciaries and ensures the security and dignity of beneﬁciaries. Protection needs are addressed in all aspects of regular UNRWA programme and project design, policies, protocols and procedures, as well as in staff training. In all contexts, UNRWA will work to ensure that its assistance and service delivery is carried out in accordance with internationally agreed standards and its own minimum protection standards. UNRWA will regularly measure its level of compliance with these minimum standards and take necessary corrective action.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA as a service provider is dedicated to the application of the humanitarian principles in all of its service delivery, and takes it responsibility in this regard very seriously. Specific measures are taken to ensure that UNRWA's services are neutral and impartial in nature, including through regular monitoring throughout its operations. In the planning of its advocacy approach and strategy UNRWA engages with a range of community representatives, including protection committees, village councils, camp resident committees – as well as holding numerous focus group discussions including for women, children, youth and the elderly in communities that we are working with. In the context of the COVID19 response, additional steps taken to ensure UNRWA's activities prioritise the safety & dignity.
