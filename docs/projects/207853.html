Project Summary

The objective of the project is to build livelihoods and skills-building opportunities for both host communities/FDMNs population and promote economic inclusion through practicing climate-adaptive technologies, and better access to market. FDMNs will get better access to nutrition through homestead gardening activities. FAO will provide technical assistance and input support to the FDMNs households to improve their capacities for homestead gardening and accessibility to diversified nutritious food. Also, FAO will provide support to the host communities in practicing climate-smart agriculture technologies for better production and market accessibility through aggregation centres (ACs). To ensure market linkages, income generation and increased productivity, FAO will support farmer groups in practicing collective actions through 10 ACs in Cox’s Bazar. The project will provide organizational capacity-building support to the ACs to establish vertical integration of the smallholder farmers with the traders and retailers.
The project will support the resilience of farming communities through the diversification of livelihood opportunities and access to markets. In supporting farmers to diversify their production system, the intervention will reduce the risk of production failure. FAO will work to build the capacity of farmers' groups through the Farmer Field School (FSS) approach, while also enhancing institutional capacity and creating market inclusion, including through the provision of agricultural inputs to vulnerable farmers, based on their technical capacity and access to land and water. This will increase food production and market linkages, including through ACs, where farmers can bring their produce for sale in Cox’s Bazar district, ultimately improving food and nutrition security and income opportunities. Regular contact and economic activities are expected to improve social cohesion, both within/between communities.
Aligned with the Ecological Restoration Decade, FAO remains committed to restoring socio-ecological systems in Cox's Bazar. The program places host communities and authorities at its core, fostering consensus on sustainable natural resource use and management to safeguard ecosystems for current and future generations. The region faces challenges from climate change, environmental degradation, and high population density, which weaken ecosystems and make communities more vulnerable. For overall ecosystem health improvement and services, FAO will provide technical support and planting materials for reforestation, degraded land rehabilitation, streambank rehabilitation, and roadside plantation to minimize erosion. A landscape approach will be employed, treating watersheds as functional units for planning interventions like roadside and riparian plantations, all conducted through cash-for-work.
FAO will support partners in monitoring and implementing ecosystem restoration and biodiversity conservation, developing specifications and guidelines. Existing camp-specific guidelines will be used and shared, and a multi-sectoral joint monitoring platform (SAFE+2) will be developed for environmental/socio-ecological assessments, with training provided to relevant stakeholders in the camps.
Using conflict-sensitive approaches, FAO ensures that interventions align with community consensus, building trust with stakeholders for universally accepted solutions. Training sessions will employ natural resource management methods developed by FAO, ensuring that all plantations do-no-harm.
Within the FSS during 2024, FAO will aim at supporting a total of 22,250 FDMNs and 299,454 host community households. However, through FAO´s wider program 825,495 FDMNs and 420,285 host community members will benefit through the transformation to more efficient, inclusive, resilient, and sustainable agrifood systems for better production, nutrition, environment, and better life, leaving no one behind.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Cox’s Bazar is also unique for its high level of vulnerability to both social, environmental and natural hazards and disaster risks. Cyclones also hit coastal areas almost every year, usually accompanied by high winds and surge. Climate change is troublemaking in rain patterns, causing some areas to experience droughts. In addition the Rohingya Response marked its sixth year in 2023, food assistance and self-reliance activities remain a critical need. Presently, the Rohingya population is 965,467 individuals (UNHCR Population fact sheet September 2023). This additional Rohingya refugee in the eighth most populous country in the world resulted in a demographic imbalance, outnumbering the host community population. Food production and livelihood sector in the Cox’s Bazar district is threatened, leading to increased household expenditures on food and economic vulnerability. The presence of Rohingya in the district is not only contributing to a large-scale humanitarian crisis but also compounding the already existing strains on infrastructure, services, resources and the local environment, making the district even more vulnerable. The FAO/WFP conducted the Resilience Index Measurement Analysis (RIMA) study showed that about half of the Rohingya households were found with acceptable food consumption patterns. Among the remaining half, 44 percent have borderline consumption. The male-headed households are experiencing a better consumption pattern than the female-headed households in both the host and the refugee community (RIMA, 2022). The RIMA study also showed that marginal farmers (owning land ≤ 0.5 ha) achieved one-third of the desired resilience capacity. While smallholder farmers (owning land > 0.5 and ≤ 1.0 ha) are in half of the resilience capacity, the large farmers (owning land > 1.0 ha) achieved slightly higher than the smallholder. The underlying factors indicated that the lowest resilience capacity of the marginal farmers was the limited ownership of productive assets, non-productive assets, land ownership and inability to store crops for the lean period. FAO conducted study (FAO, 2021) observed that 38.3% of households’ income in two sub-districts is dependent on agricultural practices. Absence of efficient irrigation system, soil salinity, and inadequate investment capacities on improved farming practices limits achieving the desired farm productivity and household income. Agri-food systems in Cox’s Bazar are underdeveloped. To strengthen the agro food system need to increase farm productivity and profitability through strengthening the market system by engaging input and output actors aligning with the aggregation centers. The interventions will include input support and technical assistance to enhance food security and diversity of Rohingya refugees and host communities through aggregation center. The aggregation center will work as a service hub including digital financing system in collaboration with private sectors.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

FAO’s emergency agricultural assistance is a fundamental part of the humanitarian response to growing food crises, enabling the most vulnerable populations to secure own food production and meet immediate needs. From situation analysis, it becomes evident that the root causes of community vulnerability, and ecosystem degradation, are closely linked to land tenure and power dynamics. Reforestation effort will not be sustainable in a context where forest-dependent communities rely on forest resources and land for food and housing. In this context, FAO recognizes that no intervention is neutral and that any impact and its sustainability needs to acknowledge existing conflicts over resources, trade-offs between social, economic and environmental sustainability, power dynamics and land tenure issues. Investments in agriculture can make substantial contributions to local-level peace and stability by reducing conflict over natural resources and promoting sustainable livelihoods. The project intervention under the food security sector offers an ideal entry point for the nexus approach, as, besides being the main source of livelihood for the majority of those experiencing food insecurity. The key to this approach is building trust with communities and stakeholders, thus enabling sustainable solutions that are accepted by all. The approach takes into account that decision-making requires a clear understanding of trade-offs, and of the implications of various possible options, and synergies between ecosystem services, and on-farm livelihood practices in a sustainable manner. In achieving the significant results, in collaboration with humanitarian actors and development partners FAO is playing co-lead role in the FSS and EETWG. Government stakeholders (DAE and Forest Department) will appoint capable and relevant counterpart staff and will make it possible for staff to actively participate in the project implementation.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

FAO Cox’s Bazar sub-office has established a complaints hotline for the emergency response to the humanitarian Rohingya crisis. The hotline is attached to a database capturing the date of receipt, the details of the issue and the status of the response. Calls are anonymous unless specifically requested otherwise. The hotline number is disseminated in the community level at the distribution and training events as well as flyer/poster with AAP message and hotline number is attached at the fixed point. The project has captured several complaints and feedback related to the quality and usage of agricultural inputs distributed. All complaints are investigated through meetings and discussions with the targeted community and are followed up on until they are resolved. Thus, the complaints have all been addressed individually and programmatic changes implemented where required.

FAO has a well established PSEA mechanism which follows zero tolerance for any form sexual exploitation and abuse. Thus FAO is mainstreaming PSEA in it’s intervention.
