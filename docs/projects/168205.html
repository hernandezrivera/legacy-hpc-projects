Project Summary

Lock down restrictions due to COVID-19 have prevented farmers from accessing their fields, harvesting their produce and trading in the market. WV will provide support to farmers through distribution of seeds, agriculture tools, and goats, as well provide agricultural training. This project will support 2300 vulnerable farmers to recover their most necessary livelihoods from the impact of the COVID-19.

1 - Needs

Haiti is the poorest country in the Western hemisphere; about 65% of its population live under the national poverty line. Unfortunately over the past few years prolonged drought conditions, political instability, and worsening economic conditions have resulted in food insecurity across Haiti. These conditions continue to limit livelihood opportunities—notably agriculture and livestock activities—and exacerbate acute food insecurity. The COVID-19 outbreak is expected to plunge Haiti into a deeper food insecurity. According to the CNSA (National Food Security Coordination) report, as a country that relies on imports heavily for its food supply, Haiti has seen an average 4% increase in food basket costs within a span of one month and 25% increase compared to the previous year—some items increased as much as 28% in price within one month. The price increase is most significant in food staples such as rice, beans, vegetable oil and sugar. In March 2020, 4.1 million people in Haiti were identified to be in need of food assistance (IPC phase 3/crisis and 4/emergency), but within a span of one month (April) estimates have risen to 5.4 million people projected to be in moderate or severe food insecurity (CNSA Report).In the Nord department, the greatest sources of income and food are agriculture, livestock, petty trade and coal production. A post COVID-19 rapid assessment done by World Vision International (WVI) in Nord on April 2020 revealed that COVID-19 has had a negative an impact on all the above mentioned sectors due to DR/Haiti border closure, increased market prices, unavailability of seeds, and livestock loss. In addition, below-normal and erratic rains since the end of March have slowed the continuation of agricultural activities and are delaying the development of ongoing plantations in the Nord (CNSA). The Nord department according to CNSA remains at IPC phase 3/crisis, requiring urgent food security support as continued loss of livelihood, and increased food & market prices,. All these conditions are all likely to further deteriorate the food insecurity and nutrition conditions in the region. According to key informant interviews conducted by WVI in Nord, communities have not developed other strategies to deal with COVID-19 impact. They are simply counting on aid from either the GoH or NGOs, in the meantime they are resorting to severe food and livelihood-related coping mechanisms including limiting food intake, eating cheaper and less nutritious food, selling their livestock, etc. Interviewed key informants in Nord identified key needs including: opportunities for increases livelihood e.g. seed provision, livestock restoration, agricultural tools, food aid, and economic support. The food security cluster in Haiti has prioritized distribution of seeds, restoration of small livestock and vouchers to affected population on food relief and deterioration of the nutritional status of vulnerable groups, particularly for children under five, pregnant and lactating women.WVI has already reached over 67,363 people in the Nord through various humanitarian support activities in response to COVID-19, and of that 3,985 people have received food security assistance (seed distribution). However, the affected population is larger and in dire need for support, and with the requested funds, WVI will support the most vulnerable 2,300 households in communes of Cap haïtien, Limonade and Milot.To implement this project, WVI will work with, municipal authorities, MANDR and other local partners for a better integration of the interventions of different actors in the field. WVI will draw on its 30+ years’ experience in Nord and leverage its existing long-term structures and programs, for complementarity, impact and sustainability. WVI will also respect the guidelines established by the MSPP in response to COVID-19 to ensure the safety of its staff and members of the targeted communities.

2 - Activities

Re-establish the food security of 1,800 most vulnerable farmer households HHs) through the distribution of seeds for agriculture production, and goat distribution for increased livelihood. 1. WVI will provide improved seeds of staple crops to contribute to restore agricultural production capacity of 1,800 targeted households. Seeds of improved varieties of black beans, maize, Groundnuts, Congo peas, Pois inconnu, and melon will be provided to targeted farmers. WVI in collaboration with relevant Ministry of Agriculture representatives, community leaders and authorities, will organize seed distributions in the targeted communities. Each farming household will receive a seed package that will vary depending on each community’s local climatic and soil conditions. Prior to distributions, WVI will organize COVID-19 safe community mobilization to sensitize communities on the project, its goals, benefits and beneficiary selection procedures to ensure smooth project implementation. WVI will coordinate with community leaders in targeted areas of intervention and together will develop the selection criterion for households that should receive the seed and tool packages. 2. WVI will provide farming tools (1 hoe, 1 pick, and 1 machete) to the most vulnerable subset of the total seed beneficiaries. From WVI experience, farmers, especially the most vulnerable, always express concerns about the difficulties they face in preparing their lands during planting periods. The lack of appropriate farming tools represents one of the key challenges for farmers in agriculture production. This needs is most likely to be just as prominent due to the worsening economic conditions in part due to COVID-19. As a result, the project will support the most vulnerable farmers (30%, 540 HHs), especially the most marginalized groups (people living with disabilities, the elderly, women-headed households etc.) with farming tools. A selection criteria will be further developed with input for WVI vulnerability database, and community members. Agricultural training for improved farmers' knowledge and productivity 3. The project will provide agricultural technical training on key aspects of post-harvest management, hygiene and nutrition. Training will target key community leaders based on assessed proximity, experience, and capacity of replication of skills and knowledge transfer to other beneficiaries within their communities. The aim of the training will be to provide technical assistance to farmers and vulnerable groups of women to improve food diversification and diversify income sources through the production and marketing of farm products. During training, farmers will also be sensitized and provided with available IEC materials on household dietary diversity practices, and COVID-19 prevention and awareness, like hand washing at key times. Home gardens will also be promoted to offer families a means to supplement their diets. Distribution of goats to support the most vulnerable farmer households 4. With the support of representatives from MARNDR, community members, and locally elected officials, 500 HHs will receive one goat for petty trade, source of meat, as well as income generation. Selection of beneficiaries will be informed by agreed upon selection criteria; priority will be given to female-headed HHs. In order to increase chance of survival for this livestock before the distribution of goats, the health status of each goat was checked by the project.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

WVI affirms its commitment to receive and respect the views of its beneficiaries, and act upon them in a timely and relevant manner. This will be achieved by establishing community-based complaints and feedback mechanisms, in order to be able to collect feedback from different stakeholders throughout the life of the project. Moreover, WVI will work with communities to ensure that they are aware of their right to give feedback or make complaints about the behavior of project staff, activities and partners using the established mechanisms in place in the community. Each feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up in close collaboration with communities, after a focus group session to discuss their preferred type of feedback mechanisms. Feedback mechanisms will be set up, especially during distribution in the form of: face-to-face meetings; hot line; letter; follow-up meetings; suggestion boxes; Help desk; feedback boxes will be set up. Another key aspect is to ensure that partners and beneficiaries have a secure way to share their feedback with WV. Comments, which are simple cases collected using one of the mechanisms listed above, are entered by the MEAL officer, or Field DME, using an offline questionnaire that is sent to a server to be synchronized with a main web-based database. This data will be analyzed and sent as a report to the programme/project team leader. This response will be given either directly to the beneficiaries by telephone or through a community meeting organized by the field staff, as appropriate. More serious and complex feedback will be collected confidentially and processed by WV management. In addition, a series of activities will be carried out to ensure accountability to beneficiaries, including communication of the project with community/govt. stakeholders and beneficiaries, as well as other partner INGOs.
