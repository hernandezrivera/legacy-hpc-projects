Project Summary

The main of the project is to address pressing shelter and Non-food items NFIs needs and issues affecting IDPs, returnees, and host communities in ِAfrin district and NES, through the distribution of seasonal and essential NFI kits, winter clothing kits, kerosene heaters, and family tents for the most vulnerable groups.
Note that Bahar through this project will receive these items as In-Kind donation, and will be in charge of storing them in its warehouses and then distributing them in the targeted locations.
Project activities:
Distribution of:
(1) Core NFI kits: 2000 kits in Afrin district for 200 HHs (12000 individuals) in Afrin district.
(2) Seasonal NFI supplementary for 4050 HHs (24300 individuals) :
- 3000 full NFI kits and 18000 winter clothing kits for 3000 HHs (18000 individuals) in Afrin district.
Note that both NFIs and clothing kits will go to the same HHs.
- 750 Kerosene Heaters for 750 HHs (4500 individuals) in Afrin district.
- 300 winter NFI kits for 300 HHs (1800 individuals) in Northeast Syria NES.
(3) Shelter: 300 Family tents for 300 HHs (1800 individuals) in Northeast Syria NES.
The tents' specifications are as follows:
Semi-geodesic dome type, self-supporting, Lightweight, can be quickly assembled, can be erected inside buildings, fire resistant, durability: 6 months, packed: 36.2 KG, 70*42 * 34 CM\430 CM * 430 CM.

Needs

The main of the project is to address pressing needs and issues affecting IDPs, returnees, and host communities in besieged and hard-to-reach areas through the distribution of emergency kits including essential and comprehensive NFI kits, winter clothing kits and kerosene heaters. This all will be achieved through a process starts with the identification of the most vulnerable groups in the targeted location and supporting them with the needed aid in coordination with Shelter\NFI cluster and IOM.Moreover, the project is designed to respond to IDPs' needs for Shelter and NFIs in northeast Syria NES, particularly IDPs in NES camps and informal settlements.During the last 3 years, the NES areas witnessed several floods and harsh weather conditions, which caused significant destruction of IDPs tents and temporary shelters in camps and informal settlements where the most vulnerable people are concentrated. The NFI kits distribution in NES is based on the emergency situations in the area which are very changeable due to the ongoing displacements from areas under conflict to the more stable ones and change of control. Taking into consideration the aforementioned situation in addition to the significant deterioration of basic and social infrastructure and buildings, the shelter intervention of this project is considered to be critical, particularly in the highly conflict-affected locations such as Menbij, Ar-Raqqa, and Al-Hasaka camps.Generally, The project responds to all the age groups in need of Shelter and Non-Food items in Afrin, Jandairis, and NES areas, in particular IDPs and returnees, who did not receive any Shelter or NFI assistance in the last 6 months and match Bahar's vulnerability criteria.

Implementation of activities

Bahar usually conducts any distribution following a specific cycle that starts with Bahar’s M&E teams visit the targeted location (neighbourhood), prepare an initial beneficiaries lists aligned with IOM vulnerability criteria, and share it with Bahar program team to be endorsed. Then, Bahar distributes receipt vouchers, to the identified beneficiaries, which clarify the type of emergency kit\s that they will receive, the contents of each kit (materials and quantities), beneficiary’s ID number, and signature of the field project manager. Afterwards, Bahar hands over the emergency kit\s to the beneficiaries who have previously received receipt vouchers and asks them to sign the receipt vouchers which will be restored by Bahar to be documented later.Finally, and along with this process, Bahar uses also the beneficiaries’ distribution lists during the distribution which are used to collect beneficiaries HHS’ data (Age, ID number, family members according to ages and sex, vulnerability status, contents of the emergency kits, and signature of beneficiaries and field project manager).Putting protection mainstreaming in place, Bahar recruits female and male teams whenever it carries out any kind of distributions, where these teams distribute the kits for women and men separately in two locations in order to avoid exposing women and girls to any potential assault or harassment. Additionally, Bahar sets up appropriate beneficiary feedback mechanism through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across membersMitigation of risk1: 1. Use the Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content. 2. Use recommended minimum standards in quality and specification of items.3. If different to recommended standard, identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. Risk2: Tension between host communities, IDPs and returneesMitigation of risk2: 1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. 3. Make effort to target a % of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs4. Establish feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be5. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process Risk3: Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion)Mitigation of risk3:1. Cluster Members (and their implementing partners) ensure that their staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct.2. Cluster Members develop a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly.3. Cluster Members have a complaint mechanism in place. Risk4: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety) Mitigation of risk4:1. Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set-up, postponed or cancelled2. Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding3. Training to staffs on establishing and managing distributions and crowded management.4. Application of checklist at distribution sites5. Ensure that NFIs distribution are completed in time for beneficiariesto reach their homes during daylight6. Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion onhow to best avoid and mitigate risk7. Communicate with national and local authorities,stakeholders and community leaders8. Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women9. Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mitigation 1: Bahar has previous experience in implementing such activities and has good relationships with the targeted population. however, BO will inform the beneficiaries when delivering the receipt voucher about the distribution date and then will carry out the distributions on another day in a specific location to avoid any potential conflicts taking into consideration protection mainstreaming guidelines.Mitigation 2: Bahar will clarify the beneficiaries’ selection criteria among the targeted communities with to avoid any related-complaints or conflicts.Mitigation 3: Bahar is coordinating closely with stockholders on the ground and will inform IOM directly with any changes, challenges, difficulties related to access and security situation.
