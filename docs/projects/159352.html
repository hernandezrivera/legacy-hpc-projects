Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to provide emergency and sustainable WASH services to the crisis affected IDPs and host communities in Puntland, Hirshabelle and Southwest states of Somalia. The target population of the project is 40,000 crisis affected community who have not safe WASH services. At the inception of the project, AADSOM will hold a workshop to the prominent community stakeholders including women, disabilities and IDPs to familiarize them the modalities/objective and roles such as project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, feedback and complaint mechanisms, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement and roles. Side meetings with girls, boys, men and women will also be convened to ensure their different needs are addressed. AADSOM will present the project documents to cluster members to avoid overlaps, following the cluster discussions. The project will reduce people’s needs, risks and vulnerabilities and increase in their resilience, to be achieved over a period of 3-5 years. They are designed to strengthen coherence and complementarity between humanitarian and development efforts, and ultimately aim to reduce needs and vulnerabilities to a point where humanitarian action is no longer required. In this project,
Water:
AADSOM will provide lifesaving safe water trucking to 1,000 IDP households in Bari, Hiran and Bakool regions, benefiting 9,000 men, women, girls and boys who have no access to safe water for a period of three months. As an exit strategy, the project will rehabilitate 3 boreholes, 6 berkeds and 20 shallow wells. In order to keep the sustainability of the water source and increase the ownership of the rehabilitated water sources, AADSOM will conduct series training to 29 WASH committees and 46 well operators. As cross cutting, AADSOM will conduct community training on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms. AADSOM will conduct continuous water treatment, chlorination and routine water quality testing to reduce the AWD/cholera diseases
Sanitation and hygiene:
AADSOM will also construct 200 gender sensitive and flood proof IDP latrines, and 6 twin block latrines complete with hand washing basins in 3 schools and 3 health facilities with equal access of men, women, boys and girls. In order to reduce the risk of GBV against women/girls and discourage open defecation.
AADSOM will carry out hygiene promotion campaigns participating women, men, boys and girls and is planned to reach 40,000 people. The HP awareness campaigns will carry out by 300 CHPs who are trained through this project. Finally, to encourage men, women and children to wash their hands with soap during the use of latrines and to fetch and store the water safely, AADSOM will distribute 1,500 HP kits to 1,500 IDP HHs who are at risk of AWD and/or HHs with malnourished children and pregnant & lactating women. During the project implementation, AADSOM will encourage women to take part in the decision making of the community decisions. AADSOM will make continuous monitoring during the project implementation and will hand over the rehabilitated WASH services to the community leaders with the presence of the local authorities. The project is complementarity with the existing AADSOM food security and WASH projects in the target locations.

Project Activities

1. Conduct series workshops to 200 community stakeholders including women and people with special needs to familiarize them the project objectives, activities, feedback and complaint mechanisms for accountability & transparency; 2. The project will rehabilitate 3 boreholes equipped with hybrid system, 6 berkeds and 20 flood proof shallow wells (2 equipped with solar system and 18 with hand pumps); The rehabilitated WASH facilities will benefit equally men, women, children including people with special needs and marginalized communities;3. In order to keep the sustainability of the WASH facilities and increase the ownership of WASH facilities to the communities, AADSOM will conduct series of capacity building training to 29 WASH committees and 46 well operators shared equally men and women. As cross cutting, AADSOM will also conduct community training on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms. 4. Conduct continuous water treatment, chlorination and routine water quality testing to reduce the AWD/cholera diseases; 5. Construct 6 twin block flood proof and gender sensitive institutional latrines with lockable doors and hand washing points at 3 schools and 3 health facilities. 6. Construct 200 flood proof and gender sensitive IDP latrines with lockable doors and hand washing points;All WASH facilities will be constructed in a way to protect from destruction during floods as durable solution and will be accessible to women, children and people with disabilities and minorities. 7. Conduct hygiene promotion activities to reach 35,000 crisis affected women, men, boys and girls. 300 CHWs (150 women and 150 men) will carry out community sensitization and awareness hygiene promotion campaigns including hand washing with soap practices. KAP survey will be done at the inception and completion of the project to evaluate the knowledge of the community about hygiene and the impact of the awareness campaigns respectively; 8. Conduct training to 300 CHWs (150 women and 150 men) on hygiene promotion practices using WASH cluster manuals. The CHWs will develop action plans to disseminate gender and age segregated audiences; 9. Distribute 1,000 HP kits to 1,000 IDPs who are at risk of AWD/cholera diseases and malnutrition. Each HP kit contains: 200 aqua tabs, 5 bars of 800mg soaps, 1 Jerry can (20lt) and 1 Bucket (20lt). UNICEF will supply HP kits while AADSOM will provide to the beneficiaries.

Mitigation Measures

2b. AADSOM will include women, boys and girls in all the decision-making meetings during the project implementation. men and women will participate equally all the meetings, the WASH committees and community hygiene promotions. 3c. Bringing the water sources near the settlements significantly increases the security of women and girls fetching the water who otherwise needed to go outside their settlements. At the same time latrines will be lockable and the location of the new latrines is discussed with the community in order to increase security in regards to GBV.4d. AADSOM will establish a feedback and complaint mechanism to record and act on expression of satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the quality of the project services. The complaint mechanism contributes towards creating a safer environment for beneficiaries as well as increasing to listen their voice during the implementation of the project. AADSOM complaint mechanism will be accessible to everyone and beneficiaries are mobilized regularly. AADSOM will establish HOTLINE telephone contact, which everyone can report concerns without any any external stakeholders’ interference. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violenceIn Puntland: The security is sufficiently safe and stable and AADSOM has no encountered any security problem during the implementation of the previous projects WASH and Food security to the project locations. Currently AADSOM is implementing food security project with FAO with the involvement of state level line ministry, the community elders, women groups and youths. In Hiran Region: The security is relatively stable as the government with the support of AMISOM contingent from Djibouti, although sometimes the insurgents penetrate some parts of the district and do interceptions the security of the district, where innocent people lose their lives. AADSOM has good relation with the clan elders in the district since it has been working through out its establishment. AADSOM also has good understanding of the security context in the area. A security checklist is provided on monthly basis by the field security focal point at AADSOM’s office in Belet Weyne. AADSOM anticipates that the project can be implemented smoothly without any security threats and challenges. In the event the insecurity prevents staff movements, it is anticipated that it would be for short periods only and coordination can continue by phone until the situation calm down and travel can be resumed. All other assumptions can be addressed by negotiations and discussion with local authorities that may cause delays, but are normally successful in dealing with challenges that arise.
