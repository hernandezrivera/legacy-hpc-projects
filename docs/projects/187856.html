Project Summary

Due to the ongoing war in Syria, many families and children have lost their ability to access essential and vital services, especially after being forcibly displaced to reside in camps, which led children to drop out of school and created numerous child protection concerns related to exploitative child labour and child marriage, among other forms.
The critical lack of access to secondary education in many areas limits the ability of adolescents to continue their education; the lack of vocational education prevents adolescents who will not return to school from learning skills needs for economic opportunities.
With some families residing in camps with no nearby school their children can reach out to, the proposed program will give them access to education through supporting and empowering community members to offer education following the SLP program.
The ongoing crisis is affecting the children who have disabilities too, where they lack access to safe education, with the lack of families’ awareness on how to support their children with disabilities, and struggle to find the appropriate community services for them, with their rights to education just like the other children, the program aims to refer them to special education service that focuses on developing and promoting their academic and life skills.
The purpose of the proposed intervention is to reflect HRS 2020-2023 strategic plan to contribute to making a child safe, well, and educated through creating pathways for out-of-school children to have access to appropriate and safe learning opportunities, and improve the learning outcomes and resilience of children.
The proposed project targets out-of-school children who face difficulties in the learning process, through non-formal education after school hours, and these displaced and from nomad groups residing in hard-to-reach areas such as camps who are continuously moving between areas with lack of stability through back-to-school campaigns, in addition to reaching children who have disabilities with non-formal education.
So the project planned response is for:
1-Children of the primary key stage 2 (9-14 years old):
Since they are facing the challenge of not having qualified educators in their areas who acquire the required competencies to provide them with education suitable for their age group, therefore, their education outcomes are not satisfactory, leading them to drop out of school, so the intervention will target 5000 children with BTL campaign and support schools with running cost, teachers 'incentives and training to target 3000 children with formal and non-formal education ( as an after-school club) in Idleb and western Aleppo.
2-Children in camps ( 6-12 years old):
These children constantly face the challenge of lack of education services near them in their camps, therefore, HRS and in coordination with the local camp authorities, will work on activating supported schools in their areas, so the intervention would support establish 10 TLS for education in camps to reach 200 out of school children with a support to the teachers’ incentives and training to offer education in Idleb and western Aleppo.
3-Children with disabilities (6-12 years old):
These children are constantly facing challenges preventing them to gain the needed academic skills and essential life skills and enroll in basic PSS and education activities, and their lack of inclusion and provision of equitable opportunities for them among their peers, so the intervention would target 10 children with disabilities with non-formal education in the 2 HRS and 18 schools centers of special education in Idleb and western Aleppo.
With HRS experience in conducting the same activities for over 4 years, so all the M&E tools are already developed, and HRS is committed to reporting to the education cluster through on the 4Ws and other adopted tools, in addition to continually gaining feedback from the community.

4 - Needs

The protracted conflict in Syria has severely impacted children’s education, leaving one third of school-aged children out of school and one in three schools either completely or partially not functional; thus, disrupting their learning and jeopardizing their hopes of a better future, and education services continue to be fragmented across Syria and education needs will be further compounded by the Covid-19 pandemic, economic crisis, insecurity and displacement. According to HNO 2022, an estimated 18 per cent of school age children not attending any form of learning, where out of school children remain one of the most affected groups, where as per JENA report of 2021, the largest portion of children in camps go to receive education in the schools of neighboring cities and towns. Half of the children with a reported health condition, injury, or disability reported school attendance, compared to 84 percent of children without these issues, but exclusion of children with disabilities is multifaceted including accessibility to and within schools, qualified teachers, mobility/special devices, learning materials, and social stigma, teachers and other education personnel need support that is commensurate with the criticality of their role. The Limited availability of non-formal education services will continue to reduce the opportunities for children to return to learning and be brought to age-appropriate learning levels.

5 - Implementation of activities

1-Children of the primary key stage 2 (9-14 years old): The planned response is to support the teachers of this level with incentives and capacity building, in addition to supporting the schools with running costs and winterization, to target 3000 children with formal education in addition to non-formal education for 3,000 children out of school (as an after-school club) and reach other 5000 children with BTL campaigns.2-Children in camps ( 6-12 years old): The planned response for these children is to establish 10 tents as temporary learning spaces in their camps and work with 50 teachers who are working voluntarily in these camps to provide them with incentives and capacity building, in order to reach a number of 200 children in camps with non-formal education who constantly face the challenge of lack of education services near them in their camps in coordination with the local camp authorities.3-Children with disabilities (6-12 years old): The planned response for these children who get identified through the case management team and they lack the needed academic skills and essential life skills and enroll in basic PSS and education activities, and their lack of inclusion and provision of equitable opportunities for them among their peers, therefore it is planned to target 50 children with disabilities ( 10 boys- 10 girls) with non-formal education in HRS ’center and 12 schools.All activities proposed are in the subdistrict of Idleb and Daret Azza in Aleppo.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat: out of school children don’t get enrolled in schools Mitigating Measures: linking with case management team of HRS to ensure removing the protection related barriers preventing the child from going to school. - Protection Risk/Threat: children with disabilities are not able to reach the school due to their disability Mitigating Measures: -conducting an assessment with a safety checklist to the school to ensure no barriers against children with disabilities and if any to work with the school committee through micro-grant for any accommodation /reconstruction needed. -linking either with the case management team of HRS to ensure removing the barriers if related to the child. Protection Risk/Threat: Violence, physical punishment or bullying in schools Mitigating Measures: -Sessions are offered by HRS to the children in a child-friendly language to know what the expected behaviors are from the staff, and how they can report if any misbehavior occurs. -Following the safe staffing process in HRS, when everyone on board must attend the basic protection and safeguarding training and sign the code of conduct, in addition to effective communication with children-PFA-safe identification and referral-non-violent classroom management-safe photographing Protection Risk/Threat: Students are not sufficiently protected Mitigating Measures: -A safety checklist is applied. Having an e-system to receive complaints of PSEA, with management of the allegations.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Harassment/abuse by HRS or school’s staff: HRS has a well-established safeguarding allegation system that is accessible by children, families and community members and also trained SGOs in targeted locations and centers or schools where HRS planning to conduct the activities, distribute complaint boxes and set the system for the schools’ managers to follow up the cases and provide the proper intervention in the proposed community. Each education activity inside or outside the schools is assigned a safeguarding officer from school or from HRS, besides stakeholder “caregivers or children” are made aware of how to report incidents through multiple ways such as: online system, verbal communication with safeguarding staff and complaint boxes. HRS has a policy of PSEA which addresses the complaints of PSEA as part of child safeguarding complainants (through direct reporting to the child safeguarding focal point, then it is managed as investigation and allegation following the policy, in addition, HRS shares with the beneficiaries the brochures issued by the PSEA network which contains the contact of FP from the network to report to them any complaints, and shares the awareness materials ( part of the code of conduct) with children in a child-friendly language and identification of the complaint box installed in each school through the safeguarding officer.HRS adopts the survivors-based approach in all stages of allegation and investigation in all PSEA issues related response.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

● Risk to personal safety of field staff (Medium)HRS has a duty of care policy and all staff are trained on safety measures including emergency locators.● Security incidents (Medium to High)HRs has a strong security department with qualified staff that monitor the security situation and put in place prevention and response plans. All staff receive safety training and all facilities and schools in the program are screened and provided with first aid and fire extinguishers in addition to a selected safe room and evacuation plan.● Interference or influence by armed groups (Medium)HRs has strong relation with the community leaders and has been successful in the last 5 years in mitigating the risk and using such bodies as a buffer. In addition, HRS has a clear SOP on how to behave in such incidents and clear disengagement criteria in case the risk could not be mitigated. ● Potential loss of money due to theft or airstrikes (Medium)HRS’s finance team limits the cash on hand to a certain ceiling that minimizes the risk and relies on an established network of vetted Hawala offices. Each office is equipped with safety measures such as security cameras, guards. Etc.
