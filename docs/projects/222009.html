Project Summary

CSI project Summary of the Intervention.
This intervention aims to address the persistent food insecurity and livelihood challenges faced by vulnerable communities in Aweil North County, Northern Bahr -El Ghazal State due to prolonged conflict in Sudan, climate change, and economic instability. The project will focus on improving agricultural productivity, building resilience to shocks, and creating sustainable livelihood opportunities. It will empower individuals and communities with the tools, skills, and resources necessary to ensure reliable food access and income generation. Key components include providing agricultural inputs, promoting climate-smart farming practices, enhancing market linkages, and offering vocational training for alternative livelihoods. The intervention will also emphasize community engagement, gender equality, and support for marginalized groups.
Project Purpose
The primary purpose of the intervention is to reduce hunger, malnutrition, and poverty while fostering long-term resilience in Aweil North County vulnerable communities. The following objectives will support the purpose of the project.
1. Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock.
2.. Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households ability to absorb and adapts to shocks and contextual changes.
Project targets.
CSI project will response to 15,492 direct beneficiaries, and 92,952 indirect beneficiaries which include IDPs, Returnees and Host Community who are facing food insecurity, economic hardship, inadequate farming skills. In addition to the flooding, some parts of Malual North Payam are grappling with a millipede infestation, compounding the challenges faced by the local population. This infestation poses health risks and further threatens agricultural productivity, which is vital for the community's sustainability.

1. Needs

In according to Initial Rapid Needs Assessment Report led by Concerns worldwide in Aweil North County, Northern Bahr el Ghazal State in September 2024. The assessment team employed three primary methods for data collection in order to find the food security and livelihood needs in Aweil North as follows; Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), and direct observations and the finally summarized the FS/L needs in Aweil North County. Furthermore, the IPC second projection September 2024-July 2025, mitigating factor include the seasonal availability of livestock products and wild foods associated with the rainy season and availability of fish. Aggravating factors include the seasonal depletion of harvested food stocks by majority of the households and increased reliance on markets – resulting high food prices. The poor functionality of markets because of the degradation of road infrastructure during the rainy season is another factor. The ongoing economic crisis and currency depreciation will continue to drive high food prices, erode purchasing power of poor households, and limit income earning opportunities. An increase in human and animal water borne diseases as well as crop and animal pests and diseases also factor in. The effects of the conflict in Sudan are likely to continue in the form of a continued influx of returnees and refugees, disrupted supply chains for markets, and additional burden on already vulnerable host communities, especially in the northern parts of the country. Here are the Food security and livelihood Needs in Aweil North County.1. Lack of Agricultural inputs (tools and seeds) for alternative cropping.2. inadequate Livelihood Support Programs, by encourage income-generating activities for vulnerable households to enhance their purchasing power.3. lack of Capacity building opportunities, Particularly for climate-resilient agriculture to reduce dependency on seasonal harvests.4. High market pricing that affect the livelihoods of the vulnerable groups.5. lack of fishing kits for the affected households in areas with fishing potential, enabling them to capitalize on seasonal fishing opportunities. 6. No Early Warning Mechanisms established: Establish and strengthen early warning and early action systems in Aweil North County. 7. No disaster Risk Reduction Training opportunities, especially for community-based disaster risk reduction committees, focusing on planning and early action strategies. 8. Lack of Kitchen Garden programs to women and youth in the community.9. No Animals restocking programs to the vulnerable groups of elderly people, people living with Disability.

2. Project activities

Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) project activities on improving access to food and creating sustainable livelihood opportunities for vulnerable populations in Aweil North County. Below are the CSI project activities as per major components.1.under Agricultural Support.• Registration of kitchen gardens vegetable groups and farmers who are willing for vegetable gardens establishment near water sources • Procurement and distribution of Agricultural inputs (seeds, tools irrigation equipment for all alternative Agricultural cropping • Capacity building for vegetable farmers (e.g., training in climate-smart agriculture, pest management) and crop marketing • Establishment of demonstration farmers field schools.• Promotion of irrigation systems and water harvesting techniques.• Training on value chain and value addition to all farmers 3. Livestock Support• Provision of veterinary services (vaccinations, deworming) to livestock keepers • Distribution of small ruminants (e.g., goats and sheeps) to vulnerable households.• Training in livestock husbandry management and vaccines handling 4. Income-Generating Activities• Fishing beneficiaries’ identification and registration.• Procurement and Distribution of fishing kits for the identified vulnerable groups.• Support for small businesses or micro-enterprises with start-up capital.• Creation of Village savings and loan association VSLA programs.• Capacity building to fisheries group on fish preservation methods and marketing • Training of IGA group members on record keeping , saving and loan offering ( finance literacy training )5. Short-Maturing Crop Varieties: • Promotion of kitchen gardening or home-grown vegetables.• Nutrition education and awareness campaigns.• Support for the production and consumption of high-nutrition crops (e.g., millet, sorghum, or legumes).• Procure and distribution of hand irrigation materials to support kitchen gardening 6.Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)• Establishment of early warning system mechanism.• Organize workshop to integrate traditional and modern system of early warning system practices.• Training of early warning committee members on their roles.• Establishing a community managed disaster and Risk reduction Committee ( CMDRR )• TOT training of CMDRR Committee members on risk management and preparedness .• Introduction of drought-resistant crops and other climate adaptation techniques.7. Community Capacity Building activities • Training on sustainable natural resource management.• Establishment of farmer associations or women’s groups.• Awareness-raising on food security issues and rights.
