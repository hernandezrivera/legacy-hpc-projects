Project Summary

The situation in Sudan remains fragile with and associated deterioration of the health system in general and preventative programs specifically. This leads to a increase of oro- faecal route transmitted and vector borne diseases occurrence and continuing Covid-19 community transmissionhe public health threat of arboviruses is rapidly growing worldwide. The newly emergeing vector Anopheles stephensi has been confirmed in 8 states of Sudan and require a rapid scale up of vector control operations to prevent urban and semi urban outbreaks. Vector borne diseases and water borne disease outbreaks like cholera/AWD, are mostly triggered by the yearly heavy rains and related floods, which affected in 2021 more than 41, 000 families in 63 localities. By October 2021, outbreaks of Dengue fever were confirmed in 6 states. Malaria, the leading cause of morbidity in Sudan has crossed the epidemic threshold in sixteen states. The vector borne disease map has changed due to climate change (flooding), poverty, population movement, unplanned urbanization, vector resistance to insecticides and more areas are at considered at risk. WHO is the main contributor to vector control projects targeting vulnerable populations in Sudan. In 2021, WHO spent 3.4 M USD for vector control to protect the most vulnerable populations out of which 29% were covered by SHFand CERF and 71 % from other humanitarian funds. Water and sanitation indicator remain poor with only 37.42 % of the population having access to improved sanitation, widespread open defecation and about 85 % of the population not using basic hand washing facilities. In 2021, 91% of 2,410 analyzed did not meet the required standards. WASH in heath care facilities remains a major challenge with a lack of basic water services, sufficient numbers of functioning latrines and functional hand hygiene facility at the point of heath care. WHO as technical agency leading the overall coordination and implementation of vector control will continue it is technical support to other partners and advocate for engaging more partners to ensure the protection of crisis affected population from exposure to vector borne diseases through managing all risk factors by supporting entomological surveillance, multiple Integrated Vector Management Interventions, and community mobilization to apply effective measures for their own protection. Vigilant entomological surveillance will be done including 1. adult mosquito surveillance through different adult collection methods2. aquatic stages surveillance targeting open outdoor mosquito breeding sites and 3. indoor aedes mosquito breeding sites. Vector control measures will be applied including environmental management, chemical control of aquatic and adult vectors, training of the environmental health officers, CHW workers from MOH and partners on the integrated vector management and other WASH components. WHO will work closely with Health, WASH sectors to improve water quality at all levels.WHO will support water quality surveillance system in prioritized high-risk locations, operational cost for water quality surveillance and water quality monitoring equipment and supplies, and support existing water quality testing laboratories. Awareness raising sessions on best WASH practices and vector borne diseases prevention will be carried out in cholera hot spots and Aedes vector borne diseases high risk areas at household and community level. WHO will continue to support WASH &IPC services at Heath care facilities and COVID 19 isolation centers, assess WASH and IPC services at HCF, provide operational cost to improve environmental cleaning at HCF (including COVID-19 isolation centers, Cholera Treatment Centers, mobile clinic and fixed clinics for vulnerable populations including refugee, IDP and host communities). WHO will provide printing materials to promote best behavior for the staff and communities and fill the gap by supporting community based waste management.

1 - Needs

The situation in Sudan remains fragile with an increasing risks of an escalation of the health situation the deterioration of the health system in general and preventative programs specifically. This is leading to the continuity of covid 19 community transmission, and increase of oro- faecal route transmitted and vector borne diseases occurrence. Sudan continues to suffer from a high burden of of vector-borne diseases (VBDs). Viral hemorrhagic fevers such as Dengue and Chikungunya fever are transmitted by Aedes mosquitos and are emerging and reemerging globally. According to information shared by FMOH, the average mosquito density exceeded the alert threshold for vector density for emergence of vector borne diseases outbreaks. High indices of Aedes vector pointed to a potential risk of increased circulation of of different types of Viral Hemorrhagic Fevers. Transmission is particularly high during and after the rainy season and in 2021, 63 localities were affected by flood and heavy rains. A total of 41,878 households severely affected. A recent joint WHO/FMoH assessment mission to Darfur region and the Eastern states confirmed the magnitude of the burden of malaria, another vector borne disease The high burden of malaria detract attention from Aedes-borne infections, . ASAs of beginning of November 183 suspected cases of Dengue fever were reported and it is likely that the number of cases is highly underreported due to overlapping symptoms, lack of access to testing and care. The highest proportion of cases were reported from North Kordofan (32%), followed by North Darfur (33%), Kassala (25%) and West Darfur (8%). Cases are nearly equally split by gender, and 20 -39 year old are the most affected age groups. 48% of cases reported hemorrhagic symptoms and the CFR is 0.5% (WHO internal sitrep). Malaria remains the leading cause of morbidity , and has crossed the epidemic threshold in sixteen states. Vector borne disease map has changed due to climate change (flooding), poverty, population movement, unplanned urbanization, vector resistance to insecticides and invasion of new competent malaria vectors. Anopheles stephensi, an invasive malaria vector emerged in Sudan in 2019 and a 2021 entomological survey revealed invasion of An. stephensi in eight Sudanese States, some with open international borders. High disease transmission and the presence of new vectors require an urgent scale up of vector surveillance and vector control measures to prevent the further spread to non-invaded areas and large outbreaks among vulnerable population. Access to safe water and sanitation, as well as the ability to maintain good personal and hand hygiene, are critical to prevent the faecal oral transmission of infectious diseases and for an individual’s dignity, safety, and privacy. Based on the S3M I 2020; 83.03% of the population have access to improved drinking water sources, 37.42% have access to improved sanitation facilities while 33.34 % of the population are practicing open defecation and only 14.26 % are using basic hand washing facilities. During the last year , 91% of water samples, collected from different drinking water Sources in WN, BN, Sinnar, Gazera, Kassala, Red Sea and Gadarif did not meet the requirements when controlled for the water quality and safety, WASH at health care facilities is considered as a fundamental service to provide quality care, improve infection prevention and control and reduce morbidity and mortality. Primary data from the national assessment of WASH at heath care facilities revealed that out of 327 heath care facilities, 34% do not have access to basic water services, 20% do not have a functioning latrine, , 71% of HCF does not have a functional hand hygiene facility at the point of heath care, and 77% does not segregate the medical waste at the point of production.

2 - Activities/Outputs

WHO as a leading agency for coordination and implementation of vector control interventions will ensure the protection of crisis affected population from exposure to vector borne diseases through managing all risk factors by supporting entomological surveillance, multiple Integrated Vector Management Interventions, and community mobilization to apply effective measures for their own protection. Vigilant entomological surveillance sentinel sites will be done including Adult surveillance through different adult collection methods, and aquatic stages surveillance targeting open outdoor mosquito breeding sites and indoor aedes mosquito breeding sites or stegomyia indicators. A comprehensive package of vector control measures will be applied including environmental management, chemical control of aquatic and adult vectors, training of the environmental health officers, CHW workers from MOH and partners on the integrated vector management in selected 36 localities/locations. The community engagement as critical part of the control of the emerging Aedes borne disease outbreaks will be improved. WHO prequalified vector surveillance and control tools and equipment will be procured and distributed, In 2021 WHO procured different types of insecticides and spraying pumps for 8 critical states with high burden of vectors and diseases. In 2022, WHO will continue providing new spraying pumps,compatible with the new type of insecticide adapted to resistance patterns in the country. WHO will provide technical support and work closely with Health and WASH sectors to improve water quality activities at state and locality level to ensure the access to safe WASH services through strengthening water quality surveillance system and enabling WASH partners to conduct regular water testing in prioritized high-risk locations. These activities will include training of environmental health staff, community volunteers and hygiene promoters on water quality surveillance , provision of operational cost for water quality surveillance missions, provision of WASH supplies and equipment and support to water quality testing laboratories established by WHO. Enableearly detection and intervention for water related heath emergencies through timely and regular information sharing and consultations with health sector. Beneficiarieswill be involved in monitoring of water quality through implementation of community water safety plan Approach, awareness raising on best WASH practices, the dentification of vulnerable families in cholera hot spots and promotion of water quality and safety at household and community level. WHO will continue to support WASH &IPC services at Heath care facilities and COVID 19 isolation centers, support missions to assess WASH and IPC services in order to identify the operational and technical gaps,provide training/orientation sessions for health workforce ,and provides operational cost to improve environmental cleaning in health facilities , WHO will support development and implementation of incident/outbreak specific IPC training programs at all levels for staff, hand hygiene (HH), personal protective equipment (PPE), disinfection supplies, waste management built in environment. WHO will provide printing materials to increase the technical knowledge of staff, promote best behaviors, increase the awareness of the staff and communities in different setting and different heath related emergencies (addressing Water quality, Sanitation, hygiene promotion and IPC).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Distinct attention to the protection of the environment and natural resources is given by WHO during the implementation of the project with a medium impact on the environment. The main hazard is the waste generated by the vector control chemical activities. This hazard is reduced with several strategies; promote alternative methods to the chemical management and usage of the insecticide such as the habitat and environmental control, expanding the use of the gravitraps for adults which contribute to the control of gravid adult mosquito while searching for egg-lay positions and reducing contact with the incriminated vectors. However chemical control is still considered as the most important element in the integrated control of vector-borne diseases especially during the outbreaks where the vector density exceeded the reference threshold. During such situation WHO has already developed a plan for the proper disposal of the chemical generated during the process and accordingly WHO will ensure that the staff is trained and the necessary supplies are made available. Management of the potentially infectious wastes from health activities will be segregated and dealt with in a way that prevents environmental contamination and WHO will reduce reliance on open waste incineration and install high-temperature quality incinerators with treatment units to reduce risks of air pollution. For medical waste management; preventing air pollution will be managed through selecting high quality, environmentally friendly treatment methods, with fumes control and high-temperature production to minimize, control the potential hazards from the waste and reduce its volume to the amount that will be easily controlled.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHO will ensure the ease of access by the targeted population will be needs-based and without any constraints such as discrimination, or any other constraints. WHO will also enhance the ownership of the community for project activities raising the awareness of the communities during sessions and training to address the social-cultural barriers that affect GBV survivors from accessing services and assistance. Activities of risk reduction to ultimately increase the targeted communities’ level of resilience, awareness, and capacity when faced with humanitarian hardships and to improve all aspects of protection for children. The project will additionally contribute to achieving sustainable social development through, strengthening local partners on both the national and states level. WHO will also apply the standard measures for the containment of vector control remedies to ensure and prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm. Efforts will be made to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unanticipated or unintentional negative effects on the targeted population, which could potentially expose them to further physical and risks. By design, the project targeted vulnerable communities that requires protection intervention such as IDPs and the women population. The project also adheres to WHO PSEA standards.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will participate in inter-agency initiatives and measures to promote and implement approaches that foster accountability or feedback to the affected population. Vector control measures and community-based water safety plan activities are designed, implemented, and monitored with the participation of the beneficiary communities, Federal and State Ministries of health and partners to ensure WHO accountability and transparency of the program. Accountability to affected people at all levels is a core value of WHO work. Upon the commencement of this project and following the discussions during the planning phase; WHO will sensitize the community about the project objectives and activities and the importance of community involvement in implementation and feedback. A series of Focus Group Discussions (FGD) will take place during all project phases for different gender and age groups. Joint field visit and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. WHO through its capacity building program is delivering key messages on increasing community ownership and leadership to ensure efficiency and sustainability of the project, and how this will be achieved through the collaboration of different entities and areas where the lead will be taken by communities and the role of WHO and other partners. Because WHO efforts are made to prevent morbidity and mortalities resulting from Communicable diseases; immediate feedback is usually shared at the field level with the community on vector surveillance, vector control measures, and water quality/safety and sanitary inspection results. This timely feedback is aiming to inform the community on the deficiencies, their impact on health, and the responsible body for undertaking corrective measures which sometimes include the communities themselves. With the WHO identified focal person for PSEA at each field office with the country team will provide orientation on prevention, reporting and management of SEA. The team structure is well aligned and linked to community leaders, each team is having clear SOPs and clear roles, among the communication tree established for the work community will be shared with the contact of focal points at each level for complaints, with the intended sequence to the higher level ensuring the privacy.
