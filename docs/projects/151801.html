Project Summary

This project seeks to increase food security for the at risk communities in Garbahare host and IDPs by integrated interventions including screening, provision of food referrals, and awareness. The project aim is to reduce malnutrition, ameliorate severe effects, or fatalities associated with lack of adequate and nutritious food to the at risk population especially Pregnant and Lactating Women and their children between 6 - 59 months.
While at it the project will build resilience through provision of relevant skills to increases self-reliance, enhance resilience and provide cushionary livelihood activities against shocks. The targeted are the IDPs and those whose assets and sources of livelihood were destroyed by recent floods and most importantly those that demonstrate susceptibility to food deficiency diseases. Identification is done through standard screening processes and capacity building to PLW to undertake MUAC screening methodologies. Periodic screening, coordination with the local actors including the local administration.

Needs

The over two decades of conflict in Somalia has left in its tracks, deaths, family separation, destruction and vulnerabilities. Current conservative estimates, according to UNHCR Somalia Situation - 2017, indicate that over 1.5 million people live in IDP camps in Somalia with majority depending on humanitarian support for survival. The number has been significantly increased as a result of flooding and displacement. According to the recent Food Cluster Updated Despite above-average performance of the Gu rains, some 4.6 million people, including 2.5 million children, still require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 1.5 million are in crisis or emergency. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) are the most vulnerable and in dire need of immediate and long-term assistance. FSNAU report indicate that majority of people in Somalia live in dire conditions with majority in stressed IPC and with the natural disasters, dwindling humanitarian support and other factors come into play the food insecurity situation will only get worse in Somalia. Areas of greatest concern are IDP settlements, most of which will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) including Garbahare. Because many people still are food insecure the project seeks to support vulnerable families and households with blanket food provision and for those that are food insecure and rehabilitating cases where there is SAM case through SC programs. Garbahare is still under stressed IPC according to the latest FEWSNET report. The IDPs have swollen as new arrivals are registered. The common risk is associated with the volatility of the district especially the rural Garbahare. GEWDO has other long-term project with UNICEF & other partners and this has enabled it develop close working with the community to reduce risks. It also closely coordinates with stakeholders and local administration. In cash transfer planning strategies have been put in place starting with transparent selection process, identification and authentication.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Constant migration and movements of people in Somalia's complex geopolitical environment can not be avoided especially when the displacements are caused by factors beyond the control of GEWDO. However the organization undertakes routine documentation especially for the new arrivals in the IDP camps and host community in which it operates. Where appropriate it promptly includes the new arrivals in its programs provided they demonstrate conditions admissible under the standard screening and admissions criteria set out in the different programs. While at it the organization, cognizant of the complex clan-based anthropological nature of Somalia ensures that whenever its program includes communities displ;ace from other ares and trhe risk of inter-clan tension may be significantly higher, it works with the local community, local authority and stakeholders to thaw this tension.. These policy has helped GEWDO to operate in very complex areas including rural villages of Dollow, Rural Garbahare and the Somalia Elwak towns.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Somalia has very complex gender based exclusions that are embedded in the local culture. lately through its various programs GEWDO has been able to see possible progress which includes even women being accepted as part and parcel of decision making and peace processes. This is done through community wide collaborations and consultations, all the while working with stakeholders to promote women issues. This policy has worked in the past and GEWDO still intends to use it. At internal level the organization does not condone any exclusion based on gender and age and none of its employee or partners does the same. It is a policy that has guided the organization since its formation and is at the core of all its activities

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Somalia is still very dangerous for humanitarian work (and also to the local populace)and the risk of conflict in South Central Somalia can not be ignored. Working with the local administration, goodwill from the local community and the relevant security agencies, the organization has been able to avoid major security problems and none of its beneficiaries have been killed or injured unless in a very rare circumstances within the control of the organization.
