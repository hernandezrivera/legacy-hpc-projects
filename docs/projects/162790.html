Project Summary

The project is aimed at responding to the emergency WaSH needs of communities affected by the Lake Tchad crisis, especially those living in the Mémé, Zamaï, Kolofata, Tourou and Toufou IDP camps.

Besoins / Needs

Since 2014, recurrent attacks and threats have continued to cause the displacement of populations living in the Far North region, with 270,870 internally displaced and 110,574 returned according to IOM’s latest estimate (Round 19, August 2019). The departments of Logone-et-Chari (124,329 IDPs), Mayo-Tsanaga (77,310 IDPs) and Mayo-Sava (51,371 IDPs), host the majority of IDPs. According to the UNHCR, 108,678 refugees are hosted in the Far North region (October 2019), among these 46,845 refugees are living out-of-camps. Displacements have generated additional pressure on the resources and services of host communities that already were in a situation of significant vulnerability before the crisis. UNICEF survey results from 2017 show that the rate of access to safe drinking water is 40.5% in the Far North. According the last IOM survey, access to safe-drinking water is the first priority for 47% of those surveyed. The assessment carried out by SI in October 2019 in Mémé, Zamai, Kolofata, Tourou and Toufou IDPs sites has shown water supply shortage within IDPs sites, predominance of open defecation due to the absence of latrines and lack of solid waste management. IDPs and refugees have to walk to the surrounding bush to find privacy to defecate. Women have expressed concern about their safety because of these open defecation practices. The outputs of this assessment have also highlighted important needs regarding access to menstrual hygiene management items and WASH NFI, such as jerry cans, buckets and soaps.
