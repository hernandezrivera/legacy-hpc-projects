Project Summary

This project aligns with cluster objectives that aim to ensure crisis-affected populations access basic water services, adequate (basic or limited), protected and gender-sensitive sanitation services, and hygiene promotion and other environmental health interventions to reduce mortality and morbidity. Furthermore, it contributes to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic WASH services, by enhancing the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and responding to protection needs, strengthening community resilience, and enhance risk mitigation measures. The project will target 364,282 (91,034 boys, 87,464 girls, 91,034 women, and 94,750 men) individuals in need living in 46 localities within 10 states including Blue Nile, North Darfur, North Kordofan, South Kordofan, Central Darfur, West Darfur, Sennar, Khartoum, West Kordofan, and Red Sea states. The project will be focusing on provision of water supply at community and institutional level including construction, upgrading, improving, and rehabilitation of water sources, in addition to connection of water to schools, health facilities, and child friendly spaces. The project will contribute towards the elimination of open defecation within our operational areas through enhancing the capacity of local communities and mobilizing targeted communities to construct their own latrines. This in line with the construction of emergency latrines during emergencies for people affected by crises. The project will also contribute to solid and medical management efforts through provision of garbage and collection tools, support to the disposal of solid waste, construction of medical incinerators at health facilities to facilitate safe disposal of medical wastes. The project will focus on improving the hygiene situation and status within the targeted communities to mitigate and reduce the risk of diseases outbreaks as a result of poor WASH services through general hygiene campaigns, awareness sessions, home visits as well as strengthening the capacity of local community committees such as hygiene promotors, conduct training and establishing hygiene clubs at schools and child friendly spaces, this will be accompanied with provision of hygiene items such as soap to promote hand washing practices as mitigation measures to prevent disease outbreaks as well as supporting female with basic menstrual hygiene items to facilitate safe and dignified menstrual management for female at menstrual age. Moreover the project will support families who have malnourished children with hygiene items including soap, jerry-cans and aqua tabs to address underlying causes of malnutrition. The project will create synergies, complement, and integrate with Health, Nutrition, NFIs, Shelter and Food security interventions within the targeted areas and worked with other sectors to address root causes of malnutrition and factors contributing to diseases outbreaks. The project will focus and enhance gender, protection, disability mainstreaming and management of environmental risks. Furthermore, the way that services are provided will seek not only to meet immediate life-saving and life-sustaining needs, but to build community resilience, contribute towards durable solutions, and strengthen the capacity of local and national actors, including the appropriate government authorities, to lead future service delivery. The project will be implemented in 12 months with estimated budget at $ 10,199,895

1 - Needs

As per the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 (HNO), 14.6 million people will be in need for humanitarian assistance across Sudan in 2022. Needs are driven by an extended economic crisis characterized by sustained high inflation, weak public service provision and a volatile political context; with repeated shocks including intercommunal conflicts, mass displacement, flooding, and disease outbreaks including the COVID-19 pandemic.The Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) situation has deteriorated due to a worsening economic crisis, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, decreased/insufficient revenue collections, poor budget allocation, and increased operation maintenance costs attributed to an increase in fuel prices in 2021. IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable residents are the crisis-affected populations at significant risk of epidemics, food insecurity, malnutrition, COVID 19 pandemic, floods, and new resource-related conflict. Out of the 11.1 million people who fall under this category, WASH Sector plans to target 4.8 million people with the highest severity of needs across Sudan.Twenty-seven percent of the Sudan population (around 11 million people) don't have access to basic domestic water. Multi-Sector Need Assessment (MSNA) 2020 showed that about 25% of the water sources are not functioning, 25% percent of the population didn’t have access to sufficient quantities to meet their basic needs; 50% of the people take more than 50 minutes round trip to fetch water which also causes insecurity, especially for the girls and women. In addition, 54% of the schools and half of the health facilities do not have basic water services. 33 percent out of 28 million people needs for sanitation services practices open defecation, and 49% of the existing schools do not have improved sanitation facilities.The HNO2022 indicated that Centeral Darfur, East Darfur, Gedaref, South Darfur, South Kordofan, and West Darfur have very acute as well as chronic WASH needs, 71 localities are severe and acute WASH need.The SCI Households Economic Analysis (HEA) – June- July 2021 in North Darfur (Kalemendo and Tawila Localities) and Centeral Darfur (Azum Locality) indicated that Water scarcity and shortage is one of the most important chronic hazards and constrain affecting the livestock production, more water sources came as major demand and first priority for all households.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The planned WASH interventions under this project will include provision of safe drinkingwater for both households and institutional level during emergencies, early recovery and development stages through provision of emergency water supply, including trucking and provision of tanks / bladders and storage facilities, especially to displacement locations, Water quality monitoring and chlorination/treatment (at source, storage and household levels as appropriate) including provision of water purifications focusing on areas affected by floods and epidemics. Construction, repair, rehabilitation, upgrading and improvement of water sources (water yards, hand pumps, haffirs) including solarization at community and institutions. Water connection and installation of water tanks/containers and hand-washing facilities in schools and health facilities, nutrition centers and public areas to promote hand washing with soap as preventive measures to mitigate/reduces diseases outbreak and spread of COVID-19. The project will also focus on elimination of open defecation practices among communities through construction of emergency and permanent HHs and communal latrines, construction/rehabilitation of intuitions (Schools, health facilities, Nutrition centers, Child friendly spaces) latrines, support solid and medical waste management at community and health care facilities, construction of medical waste incinerators, and provision of solid waste management tools as well as raising the awareness initiatives, support sanitation activities in areas of displacement, including solid waste management, and cleaning campaigns, and drainage improvement. Hygiene promotion will be more focusing on improving hygiene status and prevention of diseases outbreaks including and provision of soap, water storage containers for households, family hygiene and dignity kits for women and adolescent girls, and supporting menstrual hygiene management for women and girls through ensuring female at menstrual age has access to appropriate menstrual items (e.g. sanitary pads) to restore their dignity. The project will contribute to capacity building of WASH community structures through training and strengthening existing committees, and /or establishing new communitycommittees such as hygiene promotion volunteers, water quality monitoring volunteers, WASH committees, and hygiene clubs at schools and child friendly spaces targeting children as an entry points to deliver key hygiene massages for their community and siblings. The project will also be contributing to capacity building of WASH staff at locality level through engaging them in capacity building programs, follow up and monitoring of the project activities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The climate crisis is an intergenerational child rights crisis, and the greatest threat to children's survival, learning and protection. Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programmes and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks.Save the Children will include environmental considerations into all interventions. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, especially those used for construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure, will be carefully considered to select the option with the least negative environmental impact, considering both local dynamics (e.g. deforestation, brick manufacturing processes) and contributions to global carbon emissions caused by freight and production processes. All construction works will be done to limit negative environmental impact on the soil and its surroundings. The project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and their communities to implement better practices which support and better environmental protection and a safe local environment. SC Sudan will also communicate publicly about the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation in Sudan. Environment will be considered as an integral component for this project, and a number of factors will be essential for improving the humanitarian response such as coordination with different actors at the communities, prioritization and awareness raising amongst practitioners, and effective policy frame work. The mitigation plans will include promoting innovative technologies; alternative energy sources such as solar panels for water pumps and lighting for safer use of latrines and showers; and ensuring high effectiveness of existing waste and medical disposal mechanisms as preventative measure against disease caused by insufficient or unsafe waste disposal through reducing packaging material and discouraging use of plastic bags, planning the locations of emergency waste disposal sites with local authorities to avoid contamination of water sources, disease vectors and odors, and using latrine construction materials which are more environmentally friendly such as using steel bars instead of bamboo wooden and sticks, and minimizing use of fired bricks in construction through encouraging use of cement blocks instead.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all programming, Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. Programme design and implementation is informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. Save the Children will work closely with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to avoid unintended negative effects on individuals or exacerbation of community tensions, and will seek programming which safeguards, protects, and capacitates vulnerable and marginalized individuals, and reduces tensions. SC Sudan Protection staff support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming including though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. A Gender Equality Specialist supports with improving the gender sensitivity of programmes, and all projects are assessed through gender equality marker. Through community engagement activities, training of community-based structures, and training of service providers, information will be shared on protection risks and appropriate prevention and reporting actions.The WASH project interventions will ensure protection mainstreaming during the design, implementation and monitoring phases of the project and will ensure maximum protection for beneficiaries specifically children, women, girls, people with disabilities and other marginalized entities. WASH interventions will priorities safety and dignity, and avoid doing harm through ensure the location of the facilities and the route is away from actual or potential threats such as violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence (GBV), and attacks from armed groups, installing lights near to latrines as appropriate, infrastructure adaptations such as installing ramps and railings to latrines, proper locks from inside, separate and label the latrines “male” and “female , also platforms and ramps at water points at appropriate height to ensure safe manual lifting to all. Because women and children are the most likely primary responsible for collecting HHs water, special consideration will be given to the design of WASH facilities such as providing shades during sun hours, schedule water collection times to limit overcrowding at the water points. Meaningful access will be ensured through discuss and consult with all representative samples of society (e.g. men, women, girls, boys, elderly, ethnic groups, persons with disabilities) that should have access to the service. Adapt the location to reduce the distance and to ensure that the most vulnerable/marginalized have access.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Stakeholders’ participation and accountability to affected people is part of SCI’s core values. Save the Children will ensure that girls and boys, women and men (of all ages, abilities and backgrounds) are consulted in a meaningful way in programming at all stages of the project lifecycle, and have regular opportunities to provide feedback and concerns. SCI will ensure that targeted communities have been provided with opportunities to express their views and influence decision-making through discussing together, deciding together and working together. Gender, age and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, including through adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all, and adopting child-friendly methodologies. Save the Children will provide timely and accessible information (in local languages and culturally appropriate mediums) to affected communities on proposed interventions and deliverables, expectations of staff behavior especially with respect to our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct, and how to raise concerns or provide feedback. SC Sudan has a well-established Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanism, including a telephone hotline, and multiple further modalities which provide affected populations opportunities to give feedback anonymously and/or privately, and options for those literate and illiterate. Field level MEAL staff are responsible for receiving complaints, managing responses, and reporting them to Country Office MEAL staff who report to Senior Management. Data is anonymized, analyzed and utilized for project management and planning. There are clear processes for responding to PSEA and other safeguarding concerns raised through any feedback channel. Save the Children has safeguarding specialist staff at Country Office level, who will provide additional support and conduct monitoring visits to project sites.
