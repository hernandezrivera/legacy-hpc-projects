4 - Needs

Health needs from previous years are expected to continue into 2020. Services in areas under governmental control mainly struggle to have functional services due to destroyed health facilities, a lack of essential staffs in rehabilitated units, a lack of functional equipment and a lack of medicines. At the same time essentially free services, are accessible at a costs driving vulnerable populations, especially female headed households or families with large number of children further into adopting negative coping strategies to get health needs met (Draft HNO, 2020). Since health services in cities remain generally available, but are often overcrowded vulnerable people are often required to drive far, which exacerbate the financial challenges of vulnerable households and causes significant challenges for people living with disability (Draft HNO, 2020). Conclusively one can state that healthcare is available, however to a varying degree, at high costs and also at a distance and at a low standard (Draft HNO, 2020). Also major gaps in service provision in health facilities are apparent. Among the different sub-districts where Dorcas plans to support health services it is evident that needs remain very high. People in need (PiN) and severity in Health in the locations where funding has been identified are: Dar'a: As-Sanamayn 84,122 PiN, Health severity scale 4, Ash-Shajara 11548 PiN, Health severity scale 5, Mzeireb 104,322 PiN, Health sector severity 4; Al-Quneitra: Khan Arnaba 37,215 PiN, Health severity scale 4, Al-Khashniyyeh, 33,806 PiN, Health severity scale 5, Quneitra 11,102 PiN, Health severity scale 5; Aleppo: Jebel Saman 1,052,505 PiN, Health severity scale 3; Homs: Homs 401,432 PiN Health severity scale 3; Rural Damascus: Jaramana, 477,918 PiN, Health severity scale 4 (HNO data, 2019). Dorcas aims at expanding its health work in additional locations into the health response in sub-districts indicated further below.

5 - Implementation of activities

DAI aims to continue to support health service provision using different approaches:1 - through supporting private health centers operated by local civil society actors, through providing trainings, through the provision of medicines or by covering needs of service providers to extend their services, these support activities mainly focus on strengthening services in Aleppo and Rural Damascus.2 - through directly working with the ministry of health, where DAI has signed an MoU. This approach primarily targets rehabilitation of six damaged health infrastructure, training of staffs, especially on MHPSS, GBV, NCDs, screening for malnutrition and provision of medical equipment, furniture and supplies3 - Finally DAI also works together with local partners to support health services through operating two mobile health facilities especially in Quneitra & Dar'a governorates, where primary health services, but also MHPSS, GBV and screening for malnutrition will be provided. The mobile services are expected to have around 11,500 outpatient consultations.Monitoring of health activities will be done through the monitoring officers that will on one hand follow up with the ministry of health to ensure they can provide us with reliable data, on the other hand it is interacting close with our local partners, through ensuring that the project is able to achieve its targets. Through reviewing data obtained from the health units, through on-site monitoring and through installing a beneficiary complaints mechanism the project will be able to ensure project performance from several angles. AAP is ensured through several options, where patients can submit claims either through a box that is installed at the unit, through listing a claim on a website or through contacting a trained officer by phone. The complaint or comment will then go through a specific process based on how serious or urgent it is classified. A response is provided to the individual patient.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Health workers or social workers exploit their position for personal advantage; Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Ensure thorough background checks during the recruitment process. 2. Detailed on-boarding of staff that is in direct contact with beneficiaries. 3. Ensure a climate of no tolerance against problematic behaviour within the organisation. 4. Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting. 5.Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/assistance, through regular and targeted sessions 6. Implement Dorcas' complaint mechanism in the mobile health units and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality; 7. Follow existing Guidelines and checklists already prepared by Protection Agencies and Code of Conducts; 8. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. Risk: Low quality of medicines available in the market; Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measure: 1. Advocate with WHO to address this risk sustainably with MoH; 2. Establish procurement channels for importing of quality controlled medicines into Syria with other international actors Risk: Diversion of aid Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: 1. Ensure thorough background checks during the recruitment process. 2. Thorough onboarding of staff. 3. Thorough andrepeated training of humanitarian principles and best practices. 4. Ensure a climate of no tolerance of problematic behaviours; 5. Regular monitoring, 6. Beneficiary feedback mechanisms in place and functional.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Resumption of conflict in some of the areas; Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High; Mitigation measures: Updated security plans, established security management procedures, staff trained in personal security, management staff trained in security management, Careful assessments done before starting interventions in areas close to areas prone to conflict. Risk: Delay in approvals for programming; Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium; Mitigation Measure: Establish a good relationship with MoH, Frequently update MoH on activities and achievements. Risk: High Inflation; Likelihood: High, Impact: High;Mitigation Measure: 1. Calculate budget for it accommodate for inflation; 2. Be realistic in the donor promise; 3. Advocate for solutions to the challenge with donors / coordination mechanisms. 4. Frequently transfer funds to the Syrian bank in the expectation of smaller differences between official and public exchange rates.
