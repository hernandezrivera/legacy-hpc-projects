Project Summary

In line with cluster guidance, the objective of this response is to save and sustain lives by providing timely, targeted, and appropriate shelter assistance and relief items. PIN plans to provide different shelter and NFI kits to meet the priorities and different needs in North-East Syria (NES), notably distributions of sealing-off (SoK) and emergency shelter kits, kitchen kits, and winterization kits/cash with preference of cash when it’s possible and feasible. In North-West Syria (NWS), PIN plans to provide in this cluster solely winterization grants (seasonal and supplementary NFIs). In NES, PIN will also contribute to the SNFI sector objectives of reinforcing and enabling a protective environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure through repairing the damaged housing and related community/public infrastructure. PIN’s response in both hubs will be grounded in needs assessment data, contextual understanding, and scenario planning. PIN aims to ensure safe, dignified housing to affected populations, protecting them from environmental and protection risks and allowing them to cover basic needs and restore livelihoods. PIN will continue activities started in previous years, where needs are still observed in host and IDP communities, and will remain flexible to the needs presented through 2022-23. PIN is moving towards a settlements-based approach, with the aim of ensuring affected communities have access to critical community infrastructure as well as shelter provision. At the same time, PIN retains the capacity to respond to sudden-onset displacements. In all sub-sectors, PIN will maintain flexible programming to respond to changes in operating context and support longer term more sustainable services; with multi-sectoral integration. PIN will target locations based on needs assessment data, highlighting the most critical needs, vulnerabilities, and gaps. In areas where security, access, and market functionality permit, and needs assessments indicate cash-based approaches would provide a more appropriate response, PIN may shift assistance to value vouchers to assist the targeted population. Value vouchers for winterization will only be provided in cases where needs assessments show that NFI/winterization items are priority needs to mitigate the risk of these being used for other purposes. PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. PIN aims to provide the most needed assistance to such groups through employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet the distinct needs, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritize the inclusion of those most vulnerable, and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programs are carried out in coordination with local authorities at all levels – from cross-border to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, 5.7M people need shelter and NFI assistance. 34% of the Syrian population is living in substandard and damaged shelters. In 2023, the deterioration in economic conditions, depreciating Syrian pound and volatile security environment has contributed to the increase of NFI PiNto 5.7M, 15% from 2022. Nearly 85% of the Syrian population reports deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs through the inaccessibility of NFI items, especially winter clothing, mattresses, and blankets.2.05M people are living in last resorts cites across Northern Syria in substandard, damaged or inadequate housing. In 2022, 54% of IDPs lived in poor shelter conditions with the average period of household displacement being 6 years. The districts that are most affected by SNFI needs due their large population of IDPs (more than the resident population) are Aleppo, Rural Damascus, and Idleb. 44% returnees and 27% of the host community households are living in poor shelter conditions with dire needs of repair and rehabilitation. In 2022, an average monthly shelter and NFI expenditure has risen to 26% and is expected to continue rising in 2023. So far, PIN provided shelter assistance including SOKs, emergency shelter kits, NAKs, and winterization cash to respond to these needs. PIN also assisted beneficiaries to repair their damaged home as part of its private shelter rehabilitation program, in addition to targeting the public institutional rehabilitations.

4 - Implementation of activities

PIN aims to support more sustainable housing solutions through improving housing and community infrastructure. PIN targets 72,340 BNFs in the NFI sector and 91,183 BNFs with Shelter. PIN plans to conduct the following activities in NES: Sealing-off kits cash modality grants (150 USD), emergency shelter kits, new arrival & kitchen kits, and core winterization NFI and heating kits (approx. 160 USD)/cash (180 USD grants); and in NWS winterization kits/cash (150 USD). In NES, selected beneficiaries will be supported with light, medium and limited target (about 5%) for high repairs to their homes in a continuation of private shelter repairs assistance, providing a durable solution to the chronic shelter needs of the population and reducing dependency on shelters of last resort. The work is done partly through local contractors and partly through cash-for-shelter, where the affected population is supported through conditional cash and technical assistance. PIN will distribute 8,000 winterization cash grants in NWS and 4,300 in NES to the most vulnerable HH; targeting winter-affected camps. The final cash value will be determined in coordination with CBR-TWG, SNFI cluster, and humanitarian organizations to cover HH winterization expenses for 3 months.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy carries out regular protection risk assessments, which feed the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1) MM: needs-based targeting criteria communicated with communities and LCs; use of Washington groups Questions and advocacy for inclusion PwDs; FGDs with community representatives, communication channels for CFRM, 2) R: inter-groups tensions over assistance; 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of packages across partners, assistance to host communities and IDPs.3) R:PSEA, 3)MM: Staff training; Gender balanced teams, PDM contains protection related questions, Clear process for PSEA cases, Signs posted in project sites stating free assistance. 4) R: Distribution sites are not accessible/safe,4)MM: distribution procedures inline with Sphere, collaboration with security teams for timing and locations, vulnerable people are fast-tracked; bypassing queues, distribution sites with sheltered areas, seating if possible; Different queues for men/women, PIN home deliveries assistance to beneficiaries with mobility difficulties; kits are grouped to lighter boxes for easy transportation. 5) R: Shelter infrastructure is not accessible to all/safe, 5) MM: Health facilities are rehabilitated to be safe for people with disabilities (platforms, ramps).


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA considerations are integral part of PIN protection mainstreaming strategy. In all programming, PIN takes seriously all complaints and concerns about sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (SEAH) and in line with its Investigation guidelines for Syria and Case Management-Survivor Centre Approach, it initiates an immediate rigorous investigation of complaints that indicates a possible violation of this policy, PIN Code of Conduct and other key policies, including Child Protection, Safeguarding Policy etc. These trainings are conducted to all new staff as part of the protection mainstreaming trainings. PSEA is also integral part of the Code of Conduct signed by staff, local authorities, daily workers, and other stakeholders involved in PIN projects. Protection risks and mitigations matrix is developed for all the activities implemented by PIN in order to ensure that PIN has well-thought identification of possible gaps and standardize the precautionary and preventive measures required. PIN has zero tolerance policy towards violations of its Code of Conduct.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation; (MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to other areas; and/or switch to remote management, through local partners. (A2/R2): Local markets remain functional across target areas; (MM2): border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted from transferring goods, PIN suspends activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; shift funds to Cash program. (A3/R3): There is a risk that payments or commodities are diverted or stolen by criminals, armed groups or terrorists; (MM3): Double control system of payment and money transfers, limiting large payments in Syria, limiting the handling of cash, subjecting procurement to anti-terrorist screening remote management guidelines, mapping local stakeholders. (A4/R4): Syrian pound may continue to depreciate; (MM4): anticipate risks, constant review and updating of FV value, larger payments in USD. (A5/R5): misappropriation of assets by staff; (MM5): Minimize cash operations, tight control mechanisms in, finance security guidelines, liability agreement, code of conduct, quality supervision, accounting controls, including separation of duties. (A6/R6): Local markets not able to meet increased demands for commodities; (MM6): Extensive market research, retain potential to revert to in-kind responses.
