Project Summary

ACF Started the project in October 2022, by rolling out a lifesaving cholera response to reduce transmission of the disease in affected areas and minimize the risk of introduction of the outbreak in other high-risk areas. ACF provides assistance to all populations regardless of status and intervenes in all types of settings (urban areas, ITS, CS, or rural areas) to tailor its response based on WASH needs. ACF covers the following geographical areas:
- Baalbeck (including Aarsal, Qaa, Deir el Ahmar and Labwe clusters),
- Hermel, and
- Saida (Clusters 6, 7, and 8).
Specifically, ACF implements:
1) Case-area targeted intervention approach (CATI) or case-cluster targeted intervention approach (CATI), to provide affected populations with enough water, sanitation services, and hygiene items for a minimum period of 2 weeks to face a cholera outbreak.
2) Preventive measures to ensure water safety in high-risk areas through different modalities, ACF works closely with public institutions to ensure water delivered through the public network is chlorinated and safe to drink.

As a result of the recently amended (26th April 2023) and extended (Until November 2023) cholera response under this grant and as part of preventive measures, ACF will :
1. Conduct cholera awareness sessions at community level.
ACF will work to raise awareness in high-risk areas in Baalbek-Hermel by providing door-to-door sessions to the community, and group awareness sessions on cholera prevention and treatment in: schools, health centers, and crowded areas (ATMs, supermarkets, restaurants, etc.) ACF will recruit community mobilizers (CMs) and focal points (FPs) in host communities and rely on existing volunteering networks in ISs in Baalbek-Hermel to raise awareness about cholera. Importantly, community mobilizers will support in improving referral system through the identification of symptomatic cases during the awareness sessions.
2. Support targeted schools through rehabilitation of WASH assets and / or distribution of hygiene kits.
ACF will expand its support to 15 additional schools based on a priority assessment conducted by its team. This assessment has prioritized schools based on several factors, including the availability of water, water filters, and the extent of dependency on water trucking. Additionally, ACF will also focus on the minor works in 31 schools by repairing handwashing points, wash basins, and soap dispensers. This will help to promote proper hygiene and sanitation practices among students, which is crucial for their health and well-being.
3. Support water establishments with spare parts, chlorination, and / or filter maintenance to mitigate water shortages.
ACF will provide water establishments with spare parts to improve the capacity of the establishment in maintaining the pumping stations, chlorination stations, and water networks. The list of spare parts is based on a request from the establishment considering the most frequently used accessories that are almost out of stock.
4: Solarize key water assets to ensure constant water supply to mitigate the spread of cholera
ACF will improve access to water in two villages in Baalbek-Hermel (Bajjaje and Zabboud) that are at 0.33 and 0.22 risk with respect to the cholera risk map. Zabboud and Bajjaje are connected to the same public borehole that is the only source of water for the community. The borehole is fully equipped and functional, while it relies on EDL (Electricité Du Liban) as the only source of power which is not available most of the time. Action Against Hunger is looking to solarize the main public water source that supplies Zabboud and Bajjaje cadasters with an initial approval from the Municipalities and the water establishment.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

ACF conducts water source quality testing in the areas with confirmed or suspected cases to ensure safe access to clean water to the affected population and ensures access to safe water for at least 14 days via Water Trucking (WT) services or point of use treatment systems, in accordance with the minimum Sphere standards of 35 litres/person/day.ACF provides also desludging services to cholera affected or suspected households and its surrounding areas and carries out Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities concerning Cholera. ACF has established a disinfection protocol for Lebanon, based on the ACF Cholera Toolkit protocol and supports water supply and wastewater disposal and treatment systems functionality, incl. chlorine. In addition, ACF has trained more than 70 field staff in Zahle, Tyre and Beirut offices about cholera prevention, awareness and control, and about 20 ACF staff have been trained on disinfection protocols as members of the RRT response teams. ACF will work to raise awareness in high-risk areas in Baalbek-Hermel and Saida by providing door-to-door sessions to the community and support targeted schools through WASH services. The amended WASH component will support water establishments with spare parts, chlorination, and / or filter maintenance to mitigate water shortages as well as with and intervention of solarization as key water assets to ensure constant water supply to mitigate the spread of cholera.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

ACF ensures the inclusion of gender and protection mainstreaming at every stage of the intervention. Through a rigorous compliance with ACF’s Gender Policy and Do No Harm approach, ACF is committed to promote participation, empowerment and equitable access to assistance for all right holders. Needs and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys and girls have been assessed through a participatory approach that ensured the development of meaningful activities in line with the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence. ACF’s field staff also engages with local stakeholders to identify specific risks through a Protection risks analysis (PRA), which highlights potential risks associated to ACF interventions that may cause harm to the right holders both directly and indirectly – and what are possible solutions, prevention and mitigation measures. The project also integrates findings from the Gender Analysis (GA) produced by ACF Lebanon in December 2020.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ACF’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team manages an internal FCM hotline during weekdays that provides a two-way dialogue with stakeholders, and offers timely information, and provides a means for communities to provide inputs for ACF activities. The helpline system, which includes calling, WhatsApp calling, and WhatsApp messaging, is appropriate and prevalent due to high phone ownership rates among vulnerable populations and a diffuse spread of vulnerable populations across a high number of (generally) small ITS, collective shelters, and urban / peri-urban settings. Besides, ACF field staff make regular visits to the field during which they solicit feedback and complaints in a safe and secure manner. Furthermore, ACF has an established grievance redress system (GRS) through an Ethic’s Committee at Mission and HQ level as a channel to receive more sensitive feedback. The staff will ensure that all beneficiaries and stakeholders are informed about the CFRM.
