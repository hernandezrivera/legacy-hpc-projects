Project Summary

Somalia’s informal economy, based on remittances, foreign imports and agriculture, has been heavily impacted by COVID-19. Lack of basic services including water and sanitation facilities has led to poor health outcomes particularly in flood prone areas. Insecurity and armed conflict continue to exacerbate the effects of periodic natural disasters and climate-driven shocks, such as droughts and flooding.
The REACH/JMCNA regional fact sheet shows the top three reported reasons for displaced families are drought at 25%, lack of livelihood opportunities at 24% and actual conflict or perceived conflict at 8%. While 40% of the IDPs sited having settled in the camps due to perceived safety at those locations. The report further reveals that 72% of households are without access to an improved water source, 10% of households have no access to sufficient quantity of drinking water, 40% of households have no access to soap at home and 31% of the households have no access to a functional and improved sanitation facility.
IDPs and Host communities in areas like Abudwak and Garbarey have a WASH living standard gap of 97% with most affected population segments being the PWDs, the old and minority clans. 20% of IDPs and 26% of non IDPS have not enough water for cooking, bathing, washing, and other domestic uses. 56% of IDPs and 67% of non IDPs lack enough water for personal hygiene. 29% of IDPs and 40% of non IDPs rely on less preferred (unimproved/untreated) water sources for drinking water; 32% of IDPs and 48% of non IDPs Sanitation facilities (latrines/toilets) are not functioning or full. 29% of IDPs’ and 24% of non IDPs’ Sanitation facilities (latrines/toilets) are unclean/unhygienic.
The most commonly reported barriers to accessing water are 25% with water points far from their dwellings, 25% have difficulty reaching water points due to disabilities and 21% having to wait long hours to get water at water points due to limited number of water points. HOD is targeting these segments of communities with this response due to their vulnerabilities and risks they face.
This project will ensure urgent lifesaving assistance for communities affected (3,233HHs) by drought and address the impacts of drought in the affected areas while also ensuring integration of protection elements and sustainable solutions in all drought response activities.
This will be done through the construction of new, safe, culturally, gender, disability and age-appropriate sanitation facilities in settlements groups hosting newly displaced population. hygiene promotion campaigns focusing on hand washing with soap (in strict adherence to Covid-19 guidelines from WHO and MoH). HOD will provide emergency access to clean water through water trucking and give sustainable access to water through rehabilitation of strategic water point and shallow wells. The default modality will be Market Based Programming (MBP) to strengthen local markets and this will involve cash for work for solid waste management (special consideration given to persons with disabilities to select those who will work on their behalf) including garbage collection and clean up campaigns. Consultations will give priority to persons with special needs such as PWDs, Women headed HHs, children and other persons with special protection needs such as minority clans.

1. - Projects activities

1. Provide emergency water trucking to 1000HHs in Garbaharey district 15% of whom are PWDs on a monthly basis for 3 months through water vouchers. This will provide urgent clean and safe water to the beneficiaries as the water points are being rehabilitated.2. Rehabilitate two Borehole with parts that need rehabilitation being rehabilitation of solar, construction of elevated tank, piping and water kiosk 1 in Abudwak and 1 in Garbaharey3. Rehabilitate 5 unprotected shallow wells in target locations affected by the drought4. Construct/rehabilitate 200 VIP gender-sensitive latrines including handwashing facilities in priority social settings in Abudwak districts. This will benefit a total 6000 people (30 persons per toilet as per the SHERE standards) vulnerable male and female IDP communities with equal access to sanitation facilities.5. Conduct hygiene promotion to 3,233HHs (10% of whom are PWDs). HOD will train hygiene promoters who will in turn train the rest of the community in groups on topics like hand-washing practices (adherence to WHO’s Covid-19 guidelines), Household water treatment and storage etc. 6. Conduct On-site monitoring during the distribution of hygiene kits to ensure the distribution are made as per the plan.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of cash is informed by the availability and accessibility of well-functioning market system. Safe distribution mechanism such as mobile money operators and other financial institutions. It also responds well to local needs during crisis. Also himilo Organization for Development (HOD) has extensive expertise on cash programing. Cash is cost effective compared to in kind. It is also easy to monitor. Beneficiaries data will be safe guarded and will only be used for the intended purpose only. The organization will continuously carry out risk mitigation exercise such as post distribution assessment to verify how much cash each received and the use they have put the cash to. A feedback mechanism/grievance mechanism will also be put in place for beneficiaries to reach out for any support. Frequent spot checks will be done to verify whether beneficiaries are receiving the right amount. Market data will be collected on regular basis to monitor cash impact on the market, as well as inflation & impact of seasonal changes

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The program prioritizes frequent market assessments and analysis to ascertain its functionality. key issues that will be constantly monitored include demand vs supply factors, commodity price trend, market inflation rates, cash circulation in the market. This will help in not over pumping cash that can jeopardize the market and has the potential of causing unnecessary market inflation

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Protection of civilians: i) Distribution points and routes shall be located in central areas that are known to be safe and have been cleared of any landmines, not near military installations or near conflict areas or in areas known to be infested with robbers. (ii) The distribution will be done door to door as much as reasonably possible. (ii) All distribution will be done during daylight between 10.00 AM to 3.00 PM. iv) Route guides shall be stationed at various locations to safely guide the beneficiaries to the distribution points. v) The shortest route to the distribution point shall be used. 2. Exclusion and marginalization risks: i) Data about beneficiaries shall be segregated by age, gender, physical ability/disability and group identity (minority or dominant) ii) During invitation to community sensitization meetings, messages will be sent through door to door, social media and posters to youth groups, parents, community elders to invite boys and girls to the meetings. ii) During meetings an opinion of women, boys and girls will be sought before conclusions are made on a matter. Iv) Beneficiary registration will be done according to categories such as women, people beyond 60 years old, youth, physically disabled, pregnant and lactating women and minority clan. v) Those who are most likely to be marginalized or excluded in distributions such as women and those who do not have documentation will be made part of the project implementation team to champion the rights of such groups. vi) All stakeholders will be involved in the selection of beneficiaries so that no vulnerable and deserving beneficiary is left out and all who can benefit are informed of the details of the project. 3. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: i) All project workers will sign a code of conduct which prohibits interference in the beneficiary selection process, involvement in gender-based violence and sexual exploitation. ii) A stakeholder engagement plan will be prepared and all stakeholders will be invited for community sensitization meetings for the project. Selection of beneficiaries will be conducted after an agreed-on criteria by all stakeholders iii) At distribution points, female staff shall be in charge of women and girls’ queues iv) A feedback and grievance mechanism shall be set up to pick out issues arising from stakeholders v) Distribution teams shall be rotating among the various groups of stakeholders. vi). 4. Protection mainstreaming risks: i) Beneficiary lists and personal data shall be kept confidential and safe from unintended use. ii) The location of distribution points and access routes shall have enough space for orderly distribution and will be guarded by security staff to ensure the safety of beneficiaries. iii) Distribution will be done twice in a month and during the day. To minimize safety risk and to ensure beneficiaries do not face theft, intimidation, sexual assault, and extortion whilst taking their rations home, undercover escorts shall be deployed during distribution days. Avoiding community tensions between displaced and host communities by providing the local/host community with access to services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of food, assets, and inputs
