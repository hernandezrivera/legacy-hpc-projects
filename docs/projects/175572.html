Project Summary

In line with CO1, 2, 3, and 4, this project will provide an integrated protection intervention to most vulnerable people affected by flood, COVID19, inter-communal conflicts and SGBV incidents in the targeted areas. 8,750 beneficiaries disaggregated as (Women 3,063, Men 1,313, Boys 2,187, Girls 2,187, PWD 15% of the total targeted beneficiaries) will be reached among host communities, IDPs and Returnees. An estimated 20,000more people disaggregated as (women 7,500, men 5,000, boys 2,500, girls 5,000) will indirectly benefit from this life saving protection assistance. The project will target Koch, Akobo, Leer, Pariang, Twic east, Kapoeta North, Maiwut and Longechuk counties of Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria states.
As per CO1, the project will ensure provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority protection needs of the targeted people in hard to reach areas. As part of Cluster strategic sectoral plan, Relief Agency will; a) Enhance protective environment through protection programming and ensure inclusive access to critical cross-sectoral services, b) Engage community stakeholders who have potential to contribute significantly to increase security and stability, c) Ensure regular protection monitoring and assessment to allow critical analysis and d) Ensuring protection mainstreaming and centrality of protection. Relief Agency will also provide protection interventions under this project through: 1) Mobile and static protection monitoring and assessment. RA will reach women, girls, boys and men through FGDs and KII sessions to collect information on protection relating risks to deeper understand protection concerns for advocacy and response, 2) Provision of individual and group Psychosocial Support (PSS) services to traumatized persons, 3) Protection awareness raising and prevention messages to mitigate protection risks and sensitize the community on existing protection risks. 4) Peacebuilding and conflict mitigation through capacity building trainings to local leaders to strengthen local norms that promote peace and advocate for the right of vulnerable groups such as women, girls, disables and children including sensitizing the community on pervasive cultural practices and integrate policies that mitigate protection risks and address hostile practices.
This project will mainstream protection component in our programming or other partners’ including WASH, SNFI, FSL, Health and Education to fully address cross cutting protection concerns for feasible intervention. The project will promote Do No Harm principle in order to provide holistic support to the targeting beneficiaries. Covid19 guidelines, AAP principle and complementarity or coordinated approach will be observed in this project.

1 - Needs

The COVID19 pandemic, floods, inter-communal violence and SGBV incidents has severely affected the vulnerable community in the targeting areas. Almost 90% of the targeted beneficiaries lack access to protection services due to insecurity and access constraint. The peace deal in the country has not fully stabilized as incidents related to cattle raiding, revenge killings, beating, harassments, robberies, domestic violent, communal conflict, among other incidents are rampant. The UNMISS released and recorded over 1,200 protection incidents related to human rights cases, arbitrary killings, abductions, injuries, conflict related to sexual violence, torture, forced military recruitment, looting and destruction of civilian properties over the period of last year. The peaceful coexistence among the community in the targeted areas has deteriorated as inter-communal violence and other crimes continuously emerging with no legal redress. The result of floods has displaced thousands of people in the targeting areas causing destruction of basic services such as shelters, NFI, Food and Livelihoods. Vulnerable children under 5 years and elderly are suffering from disease e.g. malaria, typhoid and chronic malnutrition. In the HNO 2021 findings, an estimated 8.3million people in dire humanitarian needs in the country of which 5 million of them in needs of protection services alone. 91,121people are targeted for protection services in 6 priorities 1 counties including Koch and Akobo counties in IPC 5 targeting by this project.To fully understand critical protection needs and other cross cutting issues in the targeting areas, Relief Agency had carried out multiple field Needs Assessments, deep-field protection analysis and outreaches in the following targeting areas: 1) From 12th to 20th Oct, joined Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) conducted with IRC, IMC, GAA, UNIDOR in Panyijiar county, 2) From 8th to 15th Dec, humanitarian situational analysis including deep protection analysis in Leer county, 3) From 25th to 30th Dec, assessment conducted in Ayod county of Jonglei state jointly with Development Aid and Relief Organization (DARO), and 4) From 8th to 19th Feb, joined Post Flood Assessment with CARE International in both Bor South and Twic east counties of Jonglei state. From these assessments, findings relating to cattle raiding, revenge killings, beating, harassments, robberies, domestic violent, communal conflict, child marriages, child labor among other incidents were confirmed and worrying in the targeting areas. The COVID19 pandemic is the major contributing factor that exacerbated protection situation in these areas. According to South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview, “The COVID-19 pandemic poses enormous risks to children, women, girls, families and communities including mental health and psychosocial distress, intimate partner violence, family separations, violence against children, physical and emotional maltreatment, and risk of exploitation including child labor among others”. Influx of returnees from both externally and internally of the country has raised tension as basic services have become limited.The decision by UNMISS to evacuate the protection responsibility of the displaced civilian in POCs raised tension at the protection point of view. Based on the previous traumatic events and experiences, there is need to scale up appropriate transition to rebuild trust between the government and conflict affected civilians living in the internal displacement sites. While the country is not stable especially in term of security, the affected persons are insecure and vulnerable to GBV and SEA with limited GBV and health support which increase death rate of GBV victims and complication on GBV survivors. Women and children lack friendly spaces for their recreation networks and empowerment. Children especially girls dropped out from school due to lack of hygiene kits while boys engaged in cattle keepings.

2 - Project activities

In line with CSO, Relief Agency South Sudan will provide the protection services and carry out the following activities; Activity 1.1.1: Mobile and static protection monitoring and assessment. RA will conduct this activity including in the hard to reach areas targeting women, boys, men, girls, community leaders including churches and schools and other private sectors and opinion leaders. The activity will be carried out using RA developed monitoring and assessment tools, FGD, KII and direct observations questionnaires. The purpose of this activity is to deeper understand protection concerns for advocacy and response. This activity will be done monthly to update the Cluster and other relevant protection partners for advocacies. Activity 1.1.2: Provision of individual and group Psychosocial Support (PSS) services to mentally affected persons. Through utilization of the constructed community protection centers, RA will provide recreational services, life-skills sessions and livelihoods services in mean of income generating activities (IGA). Vulnerable women, girls, men and boys will be targeted. This is a coping strategies and will support the vulnerable people affected by conflict, COVID19 stresses, communal violence, survivors of GBV and among other community problems. While the community protection centers shall remain open to receive those in need of PSS, community case workers will be recruited to attend to the concerns reported in each center. Activity 1.1.3: Protection awareness raising and prevention messages. RA will observe COVID19 principles and guidelines and will therefore execute this activity accordingly. Awareness creation will be carry out using IEC materials, community mobilizations and focus group discussions with at least 8 to12 persons with face masks sitting 2meters distance. This activity will be conducted monthly to address the protection concerns collected during the assessment and monitoring. Activity 1.1.4: Peacebuilding and conflict mitigation. RA will provide capacity building trainings to local leaders to strengthen the local norms that promote peace and advocate for the right of vulnerable groups such as women, girls, disables and children. The community will be sensitized on pervasive cultural practices and integrate policies that harmonize and mitigate protection risks. The capacity building sessions will be done twice a month with only 8 leaders participants. Activity 1.1.6: Develop referral pathways that facilitate further assistant to the community members. The referral will be strategically stationedActivity 1.1.7: conducting protection monitoring and assistance to identify protection concerns to inform humanitarian assistanceActivity 1.1.8: Conducting home to home visits to identify vulnerable persons with protection assistance to be supported with protection services as well as with other sector needs through linkage establishment and referral.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA information is a package of the key RA project information which is disseminated to the targeted beneficiaries during the awareness raising sessions and consultations during the implementation and feedback collection as well as during post distribution monitoring sessions. RA has PSEA policy that streamline issues related to SEA and. We provide training to our staff both field and the head to guide them on their relationship with the beneficiaries and among themselves. Community members especially leaders and other persons engaged in community activities are also trained on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will adopt to adhering to inclusivity principle during project implementation will ensure feedback is collected from social groups of all age, feedback collection shall only be limited to oral or written form but as well as phone calls and paying attention to body posture/language. Separate consultations will be with all gender groups to safeguard against exclusion of any vulnerable persons. RA has a policy that ensure inclusion of person living with disabilities or persons with special needs. We take into account Gender Age and Diversity Principle and equal participation of all. We provide wholistic services to all people in need.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project team will conduct wider consultation with the target beneficiaries through group discussions and key informant interviews with different groups of (women, girls, boys and men) to provide accurate project scope and collect their feelings about our services to help monitoring and shape next strategies of project delivery. Different FGDs for women, girls, boys and men will be conducted separately to maintain confidentiality because of information sensitivity. Inclusive mobilization will be carried out to explain the background of the exercise so that people will be free to speak and share their experiences without fear of being victimized. The project team will be flexible to give the beneficiaries choice of languages they speak with ease to provide feedback comfortably. RA has various developed tools such as FGDs, KII, complaint desks and suggestion boxes. Information are collected in those following ways and once processed and analyzed, we ensure FGD and KII tools are conducted to provide feedback to the community. community mobilizers also carry out messages needed to communicate using IEC materials to reach larger number of beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback collected during project activities will shape project implementation especially during the selection of distribution points, most vulnerable persons targeted for this support in addition to shaping next interventions the same location targeting the same beneficiaries or next follow up projects as lessons learnt. RA document information collected from the community members. The information is later on analyzed and use to access the progress of the project. Some of the information are use in adjusting project ways of implementation and approaches.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

During the registration and distributions, vulnerable women, girls and elderly persons are exposed to GBV and other protection risks or physical violence. For example, women and girls walking a long distance to receive food and non-food items at a distance expose them to GBV risks. Elderly and persons with disabilities are face with challenges at the crowded population or at the queue to receive services. However, for dignify implementation, we mainstream protection and ensure measures are put in place to avoid more harm. The vulnerable women, girls and boys vulnerable to SEA are protected. The authorities and the people in charge including humanitarian actors takes advantages of the situation and sexually manipulate these vulnerable groups in exchange of their needs. This project ensure PSEA is mainstream and taught both to the local authorities, beneficiaries and humanitarian actors. We ensure that their dignity is preserve for the wholistic support. We have child Safe Guarding and PSEA policies. These tools act as weapon to combat internal and external risks in project implementation process. Those with protection concerns are restricted access to life saving information. Therefore, education skills are necessary in mitigating such risks and for awareness purposes. This project aim at providing an inclusive support to all the vulnerable people in needs. The project ensures primary consultation through participation before launching of the project and risks are scale up to avoid harm among the beneficiaries. Complaint and feedback mechanism will be well position in accessible areas and meet point for reliable accessibility by all the vulnerable people. As part of our AAP practice, we consider highest confidentiality in managing personal issues of the beneficiaries. We protect the integrity and dignity of the beneficiaries to avoid further harm in their emotion and mental aspect. Equality is our main core value and provision of wholistic humanitarian support considering all the four principles such as Neutrality, Impartiality, independence and humanity in all our response approach. As part of our protection intervention, the HLP issue is part of the matter we consider and analyzed. We ensure that local laws are respected and community local authorities’ voices is elevated to tackle such issues. We will work in hand with the local stakeholders and local partners with understanding on the explosive hazard in the targeted areas. We consider highest precaution among our staff, children and beneficiaries as well in regard to such danger. Un exploded ordinances are serious when ignored and therefore associated with Mine Action groups and Cluster or partners for Mine Risks Education is importance
