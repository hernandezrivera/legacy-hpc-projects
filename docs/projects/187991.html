Project Summary

The project aims to provide broad support to WASH activities and lifesaving response aimed at increasing access to inclusive, accessible, conflict sensitive and gender sensitive WASH services for at least 344,053 people affected by the crisis. The project will contribute to the following objectives of the WASH cluster: Sector Objective 1: Support to water, sanitation/sewage, and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria, Sector Objective 2: Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services, and improve hygienic behaviors and practices of most vulnerable people, Sector Objective 3: WASH coordination structures facilitated and enhanced and Sector Objective 4: Improve WASH facilities and services in institutions to minimize sub-standard WASH conditions of the most vulnerable people in Syria.
Under 1st objective: Islamic Relief plan a number of activities including repair and rehabilitation of water systems, support to water quality assurance, operation and maintenance of water supply systems, repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems. Under the 2nd objective, IR plan to distribute essential WASH NFIs, hygiene promotion, and messaging on COVID-19. Under the 3rd objective, IR plan to strengthen prevention and mitigation of WASH-related protection risks, while under the 4th objective four, plan to support WASH facilities and services in schools and child-friendly spaces in close coordination with Education and Protection Clusters.
The action will target the groups identified in Islamic Relief needs assessment as most in need, including IDPs (displaced, in camps, and in communities), host communities, and particularly women and girls and persons with disabilities). Islamic Relief will work with gender-balanced local community volunteers which will include representatives from each community group, community leaders, and local councils as entry points for community consultation, mobilization, and as key implementation actors. The proposed response aims to meet critical, unmet needs as identified in the previous sections and the gender-based analysis around the lack of safe and accessible WASH services, compromising health and dignity. The proposed actions are developed on the basis of primary need assessment conducted by the IR team. The actions aim at reduced suffering, increasing and maintaining human dignity, and saved lives of conflict-affected IDP and Host communities, women, men, girls, and boys in NW Syria (Aleppo and Idlib Governorates), Syria.

4 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction, explosive ordnance contamination and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world. According to HNO 2022, the overall number of people in need of WASH assistance - 13.2 million, needs to be completed with figures presenting specific WASH needs related to water quality – 3.3 million, water insufficiency – 2.1 million, sanitation – 4.7 million, solid waste management – 5.6 million, and heavy financial burden linked with purchasing water – 5.5 million or hygiene supplies – 7.4 million. Further, 2.8 million people face barriers to effective handwashing. Over 6.2 million people are highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including over 2 million people living in IDPs last resort sites. WASH infrastructure requires significant support and substantial investments, WASH systems have suffered from damage owing to hostilities, strain from years of functioning at high capacity due to the growing demand, limited or no maintenance, the continuous drain of technical staff and poor water resource management, exaggerated by cascade effects of climate change, economic downturn, and electricity and fuel supply crisis. In addition, imposed coercive measures present a huge burden on ease of access to WASH consumables and equipment, with negative implications on the WASH humanitarian response.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. Repair and rehabilitation of water systems: Since the pipe network (for the distribution of water to the targeted communities) has limited capacity thereby only some of the population living in the targeted areas are able to receive sufficient quantities of water to meet their needs. Under this activity, IR will implement the installation of the solar power system to help in minimizing the negative environmental impacts and ensure a long-term supply of clean water. 2. Support water quality assurance, operation, and maintenance of water supply systems WSP plan will be used, chlorination, and water testing as the most effective means of consistently ensuring the safety of the drinking water supply. The WSP follows a comprehensive risk assessment and risk management approach that encompasses all steps in water supply, from catchment to the targeted beneficiaries. 3. Repair, rehabilitation, and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems IR will rehabilitate and expand the sewage network in the targeted locations. In addition, solid waste pump sites will be expanded. 4. Distribution of essential WASH non-food items (NFI), hygiene promotion, and messaging on COVID-19 Distribution of Hygiene Kits for Elderly People and People with Disabilities and to conduct a hygiene promotion campaign including awareness on COVID-19. 5. Strengthen prevention and mitigation of WASH-related protection risks IR will install solar lights in and around facilities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection and gender mainstreaming, IRW has an extensive understanding of the diverse vulnerabilities regarding gender, age, and other vulnerable groups, whose needs are taken into account at all stages of program design, implementation, and monitoring. Specific and disaggregated data and information regarding gender, age, and vulnerable groups will be collected during the needs assessment to support ongoing adaptation of the intervention. When conducting FGDs, IR will ensure that all gender, age, and vulnerable groups are consulted and represented and have the best understanding of the situation. Within implementation, the following measures will be taken: the facilities developed and/or rehabilitated throughout the project, will be set in locations that allow reducing the threats related to their access. Community consultations will be held to ensure appropriate choices for these locations and. Water and Sanitation facilities will be equipped with Lighting devices (solar-based solutions) in IDP camps, the design of emergency latrines should consider the diversity of the population (age, gender, disability). IR is strongly committed to accountability to crisis-affected communities and has a strong suit of policies, code of conduct, and operational processes at the global and country-level to ensure and safeguard beneficiaries, staff, and wider stakeholders. IR staff abides by a staff Code of Conduct informed by, amongst others, our ‘PSEA, ‘Dignity at Work Policy’.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Conflict settings place women and girls at acute risk of poor health outcomes and sexual and gender-based violence. IR’s CFM uses multiple channels to collect beneficiary complaints and feedback including a complaint box, hotlines, during-distribution and post-distribution monitoring, community committees, casual enumerators and program staff. In addition, during action activities, random interviews, spot checks and dialogue will allow beneficiaries to report concerns. The received complaints and feedbacks will be classified and handled as per IR policy for CFM and high-risk complaints, such as those relating to PSEA, child protection, or corruption, will be prioritized and promptly managed. Confidentiality and data protection are ensured as per Islamic Relief’s data protection policy. IR’s commitment towards Safeguarding against misconduct will be upheld, and options for safe access to IR’s feedback mechanism, especially to the most marginalized groups such as women, children, persons with disability, and those who cannot read and write, will be sought through WhatsApp, Telegram, Skype and SMS.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Escalation of hostilities in other areas leading to the new influx of IDPs in targeted areas. High Likelihood- High Effect. The action plans will be adapted in consultation with the donor to ensure emergency lifesaving WASH activities are implemented for the new arrivals. 2. Challenges in money transfer from Turkey to Syria. Very Low Likelihood- High Effect. All financial transfers, as of September 2020, are carried out through the Posta ve Telgraf Teşkilatı, PTT bank branches (Turkey’s national postal and bank services) located in Aleppo (Jenderes - Afrin - Azaz), which clearly reduces the risks of transferring money. 3. Covid-19 transmission among staff and/or beneficiaries- Medium Likelihood-High Effect. IR is working in coordination with the WASH cluster, Health cluster, and local water and health authorities. During the distribution of action items/supplies, all precautionary measures including the provision of handwashing soaps, IEC material, and other essential hygiene items as well as maintaining and ensuring social distancing by staff and beneficiaries, will be followed. 4. Children below 18 years old conduct works on behalf of Islamic Relief or contractors. Low Likelihood-High Effect. IR ensures a proper registration system for staff/contractors’ laborers and adds zero-tolerance disclosure on child labor in the contracts.
