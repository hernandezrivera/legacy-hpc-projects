Project Summary

Despite recent permanent ceasefire declaration and signing of the revitalized peace agreement by the different factions; the country has continued to witness increasing bureaucratic impediments, cyclical waves ceasefire violations especially in the Equatorias compounded by inter-communal violence, counter-insurgency further undermining efforts to create the previous status quo prior.
Subsequently, at least 5.2 million people are projected to be severely food insecure in the first quarter of 2019, likely to deteriorate further as we head into the lean season (IPC September 2018 Update; FSNMS Round 21).
Deteriorating food insecurity conditions have been induced by conflict, which broke out early 2018
between armed actors in the Equatorias and Jonglei, triggering displacement. This has been further
exacerbated by soaring inflation, deteriorating incomes and high food and commodity prices, livestock looting and diseases significantly eroding key livelihood assets and reduced harvests, shortage of seed and tools, and erratic rainfall patterns and reduced market functionality (IPC 2018; FEWSNET Food Security Update, 2018; REACH, Greater Akobo Rapid Displacement Brief, 2018).
Proposed actions which aim to establish a nexus between Humanitarian response and Resilience programming will target beneficiaries primarily made up 1,500 households (9,000 beneficiaries) in Western, Eastern Equatoria and Jonglei States, comprised of 3000 boys, 3000 girls, 1500 men, and 1500 women , these include elderly (> 60 years and those with disabilities/ special needs) amongst the most vulnerable emergency affected IDPs and host communities directly affected by the ongoing conflict. The action will respond to prioritized locations taking into account cost effective measures of prepositioning supplies through FAO pipeline hub.
Fishing support interventions will enable beneficiaries with access to riverine areas/ rivers/flood plains have immediate access to fish for food, income and livelihood rehabilitation. Vegetable and crop livelihood kits interventions will ensure medium to long term access to food production.
Livestock support animal health interventions will support improved access to milk, meat and incomes, in addition to protecting critical livestock assets.
These activities will provide a holistic framework of interventions to complement and create synergies into the project in addition to coordination with other actors in WASH,NFI, Nutrition, Health, Education and Protection. CARD will closely work with beneficiaries, line ministries including the RRC to foster ownership, in addition to mitigating potential risks via collaboration with counterpart agencies on ground, security focal points, UNMISS, UNDSS and involvement of UNHAS. CARD will infuse peace messaging during implementation to foster peaceful coexistence among communities even beyond the project life span in addition to advocacy on beneficiary protection, accountability to affected populations, quality programming, fostering inter-cluster synergies, advocating Gender Based Violence and liaison with protection partners, natural resourced conservation and HIV/ AIDS mainstreaming.

Needs

Despite recent permanent ceasefire declaration and signing of the revitalized peace agreement by the different factions; the country has continued to witness increasing bureaucratic impediments, cyclical waves ceasefire violations especially in the Equatorias compounded by inter-communal violence, counter-insurgency further undermining efforts to create the previous status quo prior.Subsequently, at least 5.2 million people are projected to be severely food insecure in the first quarter of 2019, likely to deteriorate further as we head into the lean season (IPC September 2018 Update; FSNMS Round 21). Deteriorating food insecurity conditions have been induced by conflict, compounded by inter-communal violence and cattle rustling especially in Jonglei and continued ceasefire violations in the Equatorias triggering multiple displacement. This has been further exacerbated by soaring inflation, deteriorating incomes and high food and commodity prices, flooding, livestock looting and diseases significantly eroding key livelihood assets and reduced harvests, fall army worm invasion, shortage of seed and tools, and erratic rainfall patterns and reduced market functionality (IPC September Update 2018; FEWSNET Food Security Update, 2018; REACH, Greater Akobo Rapid Displacement Brief, 2018). September 2018 IPC projections for January - March 2019, indicate Catastrophe is expected in Pibor and Canal/ Pigi (former Jonglei state) among other regions in the country. Despite recent harvests in the Equatorias which has seen marginal improvement in overall situation of acute malnutrition, the situation is projected to deteriorate further in the first quarter of 2019 due to an early lean season onset and depleted food stocks. Key market hubs such as Bor, Torit have witnessed limited trade flows and delivery of assistance (South Sudan Monthly Market Bulletins 2018), due to increasing killings and ambushes along Juba-Bor/Torit roads; in addition to other bureaucratic impediments (OCHA Humanitarian Briefings 2018). High food prices have sharply reduced household purchasing power, with 35% of households reported reduced incomes through their livelihood, hence seeking alternative sources of food/incomes, some of which including negative coping strategies such as overreliance on humanitarian aid. This is in addition to more than 51% of households reporting decreased incomes while 36% reporting complete loss of source of income in the last one year (FSNMS, Round 20; FEWSNET Food Security Outlook Oct 2017-May 2018). CARD proposes crop, vegetable and fishing livelihood kits interventions and animal health activities coupled with relevant trainings which will establish a nexus between Humanitarian response and Resilience programming. These activities will provide a holistic framework of interventions to complement and create synergies into the project in addition to coordination with other actors in WASH,NFI, Nutrition, Health, Education and Protection. CARD will closely work with beneficiaries, line ministries including the RRC to foster ownership, in addition to mitigating potential risks via collaboration with counterpart agencies on ground, security focal points, UNMISS, UNDSS and involvement of UNHAS. CARD will infuse peace messaging during implementation to foster peaceful coexistence among communities even beyond the project life span in addition to advocacy on beneficiary protection, accountability to affected populations, quality programming, fostering inter-cluster synergies, advocating Gender Based Violence and liaison with protection partners, natural resourced conservation and HIV/ AIDS mainstreaming.

Activities

ACTIVITIES1. Prepositioning through Cluster Pipeline and Distribution of agricultural livelihood kits - crop, vegetable and fishing kits as well as farming tools to 1,500 households to facilitate emergency food production and rehabilitation of livelihoods. 2. Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention and eradication3. support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality4. Support value chain addition e.g. through milk & fish preservation & marketing5. Provision of livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns targeting 80,000 livestock including training and equipping of 120 (70% male) community animal health workers to reduce livestock mortalities, morbidities and increase productivity and access to animal source foods6. Installation and maintenance of three cold chain facilities for livestock vaccines storage. INDICATORS1. Number of Households (female, child & male headed) receiving agricultural inputs/ vegetable & fishing kits2. Quantity & variety of seeds distributed3. Quantity & variety of crops produced (outcome)4. Women & girls at least 50% of targeted beneficiaries;5. Number of people trained (e.g. improved nutrition/ land conservation/ reducing post-harvest losses/ improved farming practices;6. Number of animals treated/ vaccinated7. Quantity of seed procured locally

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

As with all the other projects implemented by the organization, CARD will adopt a consultative approach in the implementation of this project; by including ensuring a two-way communication and dialogue throughout the project cycle. Beneficiaries (both male and female) will be dis-aggregated and involved -equal representation - taking into account the different roles and needs of boys, girls, women and men);local leaders among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring their full participation in implementation, monitoring and especially evaluating the project. All project activities will be aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the beneficiaries and communities.Implementation committees will be selected among IDP/mobile communities with linkages with RRC and local leadership to ensure targetsare met. Their roles will involve but not limited to beneficiary identification, selection and verification; including participation in trainings andcholera preparedness activities.
