Project Summary

This project will targets the most vulnerable household within the IDPs, Host and returnees community by following vulnerability selection criteria and considering gender equality and equity during the registration of beneficiaries. The project will provided beneficiaries with some techniques which will make them to cope with ongoing food security situation in the country and we will focus on priority which was listed by SSFSL to achieve the indicatives activities and met cluster indicators.
In line with the HRP objectives, priorities & indicative activities for 2019 Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) cluster’s strategy, this proposed FSL project aims to support the immediate and short-term lifesaving needs of the most vulnerable through the provision of livelihood protection support. The project will target 9,163 individuals (51% female) most vulnerable households in Malakal, Baliet and Fashoda counties of Upper Nile state. This project’s 40% IDPs, 30% Host communities and 30% Returnees which are disaggregate by gender proportion of 51% and 49% males each. In line with the cluster’s strategy to support displaced populations on the move from conflict, this project will support IDPs and Host communities as well as returnees. The key activities will be the distribution of main season crop kits, vegetable kits and fishing kits. War Child Canada has existing presence in this project’s targeted areas, including FSL programming expertise and experience, making it well-placed to address the identified needs.

Needs

South Sudan is facing a severe humanitarian crisis putting the lives of the most vulnerable at risk. War Child Canada's assessments and current presence in the area have found that there are urgent needs in Malakal, Fashoda and Baliet County of upper Nile State. War Child Canada's assessments in targeted areas found that the most vulnerable displaced and host community as well returnees individuals and households in conflict-affected areas are in urgent need of assistance in order to alleviate suffering and save lives.Sustained insecurity, restricted movement and extreme depletion of livelihoods in the targeted areas has prevented many from planting and harvesting, and levels of crop and vegetable production remain low (FAO, 2018). This has forced households to sell off assets and livestock to buy food, further deteriorating the nutrition status of vulnerable children. Even with these negative coping strategies, poor households are still facing significant food shortages. This is further exacerbated by the limited access for humanitarians to deliver services. War Child Canada has existing presence in this project's targeted areas, including FSL programming expertise and experience, making it well-placed to address the identified needs. The project design is based on War Child Canada's presence in the area, and the community needs identified and prioritized by the FSL Cluster and the Government of South Sudan jointly with other stakeholders, including War Child Canada.The proposed intervention will prioritize the distribution of main season crop kits, vegetable kits and fishing kits to 1,527 households. The most vulnerable households will be prioritized in order to protect livelihoods and improve food availability and provide support in time for the 2018 planting season in targeted areas.

Activities

Cluster Objective 2:• Train female and male extension workers• Train women, men and female and male youth in basic business skills using low literacy curriculum• Provide women and men with gender-transformative agricultural inputs • Train female and male farmers to use adaptive trials for seed multiplication • Host agricultural fairs to link female and male producers and traders• Facilitate visits and farmer field days with new and existing farmer groups • Establish agricultural training spaces, farmer schools • Provide training and support for farmer groups to engage in existing value chains (pineapple, fishing, honey, rice, etc.) • Form, strengthen and monitor savings groups• Provide women-headed households with small ruminants for income generation• Provide training and start up resources for female and male youth to start businesses to support agricultural efficiency (ploughing, transformation, etc.)• Purchase of agricultural inputs and fishing kits/gears• Purchase of business start-up and VSL kits

War Child Canada has a Gender Equality Policy, which includes efforts to mainstream gender equality throughout all programs to ensure the needs of male and female beneficiaries are met. War Child Canada mainstreams gender and child protection across all programming and within the organization itself, ensuring that interventions and policies consider equitable treatment of women and men, and that minimum standards for child protection are met. Yes Complaints mechanisms can increase accountability to aid beneficiaries by providing them with the opportunities to provide feedback on the quality and quantity of the services they receive as well as to complain about potential wrongdoing.WCC will have local context specific mechanisms, taking into account issues such as cultural norms and values, level of literacy, phone coverage, and social patterns, among others.
