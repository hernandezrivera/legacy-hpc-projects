Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to respond to critical education and well-being needs of vulnerable school aged children and adolescents in Area C, Hebron, East Jerusalem and the Gaza Strip to enable them to access safe education. The main drivers of education-related vulnerabilities include attacks on schools, movement restrictions, and poverty. These factors undermine the quality of education, generate protection concerns and contribute to early school dropout.
This project will contribute to the 2023 HRP Education Strategic Objective 1 “Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children and Strategic Objective 2 “Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to potential emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school-based psychosocial services.”
The project will ensure safe access to school for 4,500 students aged 6-16 and their teachers in H2, South Hebron Hills, Bethlehem, South Nablus, East Jerusalem and other selected areas in the West Bank. They will be supported with a holistic package of interventions including training of school counselors, volunteers and school teachers on child safeguarding, safe identification and referral, and Psychosocial Support interventions. Several assessments continue to show that the realization of the right to education remains severely compromised in these locations. In the first half of 2022, the Education Cluster recorded 115 education-related incidents affecting 7,906 students and 418 teachers and school staff. The lack of safe access to education has significant negative consequences for student’s schooling and results in a decrease of school hours, an increased drop-out rate of children, discouraging teaching staff and the displacement or separation of families as they seek solutions in getting their children in schools elsewhere. These negative factors affect children’s and adolescents’ well-being. The recurrent hostilities in the Gaza Strip compounded by the Covid Pandemic has resulted in learning loss for the majority of children due to school closures. According to the 2022 MSNA findings, 88 per cent of households with school aged children in Gaza, and 72 per cent in the West Bank, reported that their children were in need of catch-up programmes.
UNICEF in joint collaboration with child protection networks/services and in partnership with local NGOs will support 20,000 vulnerable children and adolescents (50% females, 1% CWD) with Mental Health and Psychosocial (MHPSS) school-based activities. The MHPSS interventions will be implemented after school and during the summer vacation with support from Ministry of Education and local implementing partners. The need for MHPSS has been exacerbated by COVID 19, the recent Gaza Strip escalation and the chronic protection challenges the children experience going to and from school as they cross Israeli military check points and closed military zones or pass by settlements where they may experience harassment and violence as reported by the education cluster. UNICEF with partners will support 5,000 children in the Gaza Strip to catch up on lost learning through implementation of an after school remedial programme

1 - Needs

The project will be implemented in the Gaza Strip, Area C, Hebron H2 and East Jerusalem. Children in these geographical locations remain the most vulnerable children in need of humanitarian assistance to access education in safe learning environments. The Education Cluster estimates 608,556 Palestinians (49 per cent females) across the State of Palestine, the vast majority (96 per cent) of whom are school-age children, including around 25,385 Children with Disabilities (CWDs); nearly 65 per cent live in the Gaza Strip, and over 35 per cent in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem will need humanitarian assistance to access education in 2023. The project will respond to children’s and adolescents’ most critical needs for safe access to learning and well-being. The following provides a summary of needs: 1. School based psycho-social support through recreational and learning activities to improve the well- being of vulnerable children.There is a general lack of school-based psychosocial support for children in the Gaza Strip, Area C, Hebron H2 and East Jerusalem who experience regular interactions with the Israeli military and harassment to and from school, and for children in the Gaza Strip continuing to deal with psychological distress as a consequence of the recurrent hostilities. The Sectoral People in Need (PiN) Aggregation Model for the Education Cluster estimates that around 84,000 households (HHs) with school-aged children in the Gaza Strip, Area C, Hebron H2 and East Jerusalem report that psychosocial support is not available at the school for children if needed; and approximately 57,000 HHs in the Gaza Strip reported that at least one child is showing signs of psychosocial distress. In addition, children studying in these schools lack opportunities to participate in extracurricular activities. The 2022 Multi-Sector Needs Analysis (MSNA) estimates that 170,680 children and adolescents (73,716 males) are in need of MHPSS. The main causes of MHPSS needs include attacks on schools, unsafe access to schools due to the education related incidents and escalations mainly for the children in the Gaza Strip, Area C and the Seam areas. The education related incidents included Israeli forces’ firing of tear gas canisters at schools, the detention of students commuting to or from schools, and their harassment and intimidation by Israeli settlers. In the Gaza Strip, the recurrent hostilities have exacerbated the already fragile circumstances surrounding their learning and well-being. Of particular concern are the nearly 600,000 school-age children in the Gaza Strip whose education was disrupted during the hostilities, compounding the learning loss and increased exposure to mental health risks. 2. Safe access to a safe school learning environment: Children from Area C, Hebron H2, and East Jerusalem, continue to witness a non-permissive environment, incidents of settler violence, increased threats of demolition and displacement. Children continue to face violence and challenges in accessing schools. The majority of these children cross checkpoints daily to attend school, and parents report that their major concern is military harassment and settler violence against their children on the way to school. Girls face a higher risk of school drop-out due to the fear of parents for their safety during the school commute. Protection risks on the way to school is a major reason for children dropping out of schools. According to the MSNA 55% of boys and 36% of girls in the West Bank who dropped out in the last 12 months was due to conflict related protection risks on the way to and from schools. School dropout rates are higher for boys than for girls, as adolescent boys are expected to contribute to household income in times of crisis. UNICEF in partnership with local NGOs will target 4,500 children (51% females) with a package of holistic protection interventions. 3. Remedial education to mitigate learning loss: The prolonged school closures due to COVID -19, escalations in the Gaza Strip and West Bank and teachers strikes, forcing closure of schools have caused a huge learning loss issue for students. The outbreak of COVID-19 in the past two years significantly compounded children’s vulnerabilities due to prolonged school closures as part of the measures adopted to contain the pandemic. The pandemic added burdens onto an already strained educational system to provide essential education services. All these factors resulted in students’ learning loss due to intermittent school closures. Girls and children from poor families were particularly affected as they did not possess the means to continue their education during school closures These children are in need of remedial education to enhance their literacy and numeracy skills and mitigate their risk of dropping out of schools. According to the 2022 MSNA around 70% of children are in need of catch-up programs (79% in Gaza). 4. Essential Education supplies to mitigate risks of dropping out of school: More families in Gaza have been pushed into poverty due to the prolonged COVID-19 pandemic and the recent Gaza Strip escalations. Parents are increasingly not able to afford basic school supplies and schools for their children, placing them at risk of dropping out. Households are unable to provide the basic supplies needed for their school-aged children, educational materials and supplies included, resulting in an increase in the dropout rate. According to the MSNA, 2022, 81% of the Gaza Strip households reported difficulties in obtaining enough money to cover basic needs. In the Gaza Strip 9% of households reported not planning to enroll at least one child for the 2022-2023 school year; being unable to afford to afford school related expenses was given as the primary reason for non-enrollment by 15% of households; and inability to afford the cost of schooling as a reason for school dropouts was cited by 56.5% of households surveyed.

2 - Activities

Based on the needs identified above and in line with Strategic Objectives 2 and 3 of the Education Cluster, the project and in close cooperation and coordination with MoE, will respond to the identified needs through the following activities:1. Provide school- based psycho-social support through recreational and learning activities to children and adolescents after school and/or summer activities to support their well-being and psycho-social support in close collaboration with child protection services/networks. 2. Support a holistic package of interventions to ensure that children have safe access to schools3. Provide remedial education services to mitigate learning loss to low performing children;4. Provide most vulnerable children with individual learning materials to mitigate the risk of them dropping out of school. 1. Provide school- based psycho-social support through recreational after school or summer interventions for children and adolescents which aim to provide access for vulnerable students in grades 1 to 6 to participate in Fun Days and stress release activities, and Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics (STEM) clubs for students in grades 7 to 11 through interventions in 200 schools in marginalized areas of the West Bank, the Gaza Strip and East Jerusalem. Service providers including teachers and school staff who will be trained through UNICEF will deliver quality, inclusive education through well-being and STEM activities. These activities will provide and reinforce opportunities to children and adolescents to develop and practice life-skills and citizenship education in humanitarian settings through access to age, gender, location and culturally appropriate child protection activities. 2. To ensure children’s safe access to quality education, UNICEF will support 4,500 children (40% girls) from Hebron H2, Area C and East Jerusalem through a holistic approach to a protective learning environment. The package of interventions will include training of school counselors, volunteers and school teachers on child safeguarding, safe identification and referrals utilizing Child Protection Referral Pathways, and Psychosocial Social Support interventions. Team Up kits will be procured and Team Up sessions will be conducted for the children in the communities to create safe environments for the children. In partnership with local NGO’s, UNICEF will work with community members to assess locality based protection issues in affecting children’s safe access to school, use their findings from the assessment to develop local solutions to address the challenges, implement their initiatives and monitoring. This activity will be complementary to the ECW interventions by targeting different schools and children3. 5,000 children (40% females) in the Gaza Strip will be supported with remedial education to mitigate the learning loss. More boys than girls will be targeted based on the most recent results from the standardized tests. Teachers in the targeted schools will be trained on how to manage lost learning. This will include training teachers on how to identify regressed knowledge and skills amongst their learners as well as underperforming students and on how to support the identified gaps with remedial education. A pre-test and post-test will be administered at the beginning and end of the project respectively to monitor progress of learning during the project period. 4. The project will support 60,000 poorest students in the Gaza Strip aged 6-17 (50% girls), who may be at risk of dropout due to poverty, to continue their education by providing basic, urgent supplies including stationary kits. The contents of the stationery kits are developed based on the feedback received from the beneficiaries and will be procured from the local market to boost the local economy. UNICEF and partners will ensure that the proposed action does not in any way further harm or negatively affect the children (girls' and boys' learning capabilities) and women and men (teachers, local community members and staff working in cluster partners). During implementation, all implementing partners will be accountable to ensure that a system for Accountability to Affected Populations is developed as part of the M&E plan to ensure that the voices of beneficiaries count. UNICEF will work with the Protection cluster to address issues related to protection issues, GBV and referral of cases to relevant services and ensure coordination with the PSEA focal point to timely address potential SEA concerns and make sure adequate reporting channels exist and are utilized. Staff will be trained on the Child Safeguard Policy upon signing their contract and will be supported to prevent harm to children they work with. IPs will also commit to support local organizations, and partners to be aware of the importance of a child safeguarding policy. The beneficiaries will be informed, at the start of the project, of what they can expect from the IP organization and staff, as well as being provided with clear information on details about how they can give feedback and complaints if they experience or witness misconduct by representatives
