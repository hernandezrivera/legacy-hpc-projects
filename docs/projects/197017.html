Project Summary

The project aims to provide essential, integrated health, nutrition, and child protection services, covering Qabasin and Al Bab communities in Al Bab subdistrict, Al Bab district, Aleppo governorate, in addition to the underserved IDP camps around them, the catchment population in the targeted area is 41.660.
The project duration will be 12 months, covering the mentioned locations throughout 2023, with CMAM, IYCF, and TSFP, in addition to awareness messages about handwashing practices, food processing, COVID-19 and cholera signs, symptoms, means of infection, and IPC measures.
Total project targets include 11,616 boys and 13,200 girls, among them, there are 3,721 children with disability, in addition to 13,544 PLWs, including 2,031 with a disability, as those groups are the most affected by the poor nutrition situation across Syria.
Projects activities, locations, targets, and budget are fully coordinated with the nutrition cluster, to ensure optimal planning, in addition, full coordination will be maintained during the implementation, to ensure the optimal coverage of the need, without any duplication or overlapping, and the optimal utilization of the available nutrition supplies and other resources.
Project activities will be implemented using two different modalities:
RRT modality: 2 RRT will be deployed, covering Qabasin, and Al Bab communities, and the surrounding camps. Provided services include screening for malnutrition and referral, SAM and MAM treatment, one-to-one IYCF-E counseling, IYCF-E messages, micronutrient supplementation, FMA, screening for external visual signs of corporal punishment and suspected abuse and violence cases and referral, screening for danger health signs and referral to healthcare centers, in addition to WaSH messages on handwashing practices, food processing, and dissemination of information on COVID-19 and cholera signs, symptoms, means of infection, and IPC measures.
Facility-based nutrition services: two nutritionists will be based in Maternity hospital/Al Bab/Al Bab/Aleppo, and Al Farabi hospital/ Al Bab/Al Bab/Aleppo, both hospitals are supported by SEMA, to ensure the integration between health and nutrition services, provided services include early identification and treatment of acutely malnourished boys and girls under five years and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), infant and young child feeding services, micro-nutrient supplementation. The center will also receive all the referred cases that need either nutrition or primary health care services from RRT teams or any other partner in the area.

3 - Needs

The poor nutrition situation is still a major challenge for public health in NWS, mainly affecting infants, U5, and PLW, causing different forms of malnutrition, including wasting, SAM, MAM, chronic malnutrition or stunting, and micronutrient deficiency, especially iron deficiency anemia. According to the nutrition smart survey that was conducted in NWS in July 2022, the nutrition trends were similar to the last SMART survey conducted in June 2021, in the same area, however, those trends reflect a clear deterioration in the nutrition situation in compared to previous years, this can be attributed to the negative coping mechanisms, as a result of the negative effect of the economic deterioration. The survey has shown that the prevalence of the GAM rate is 5.0% with a combined SAM rate of 1.7%, The GAM rate by WHZ increased to 3.3% and the SAM rate also increased to 0.9%, and the GAM rate by MUAC showed approximately the same rate in comparing with 2021 SMART survey, stunting rate did not show a significant improvement compared to 2021 results, 24.5% in June 2021 and 22.3% in July 2022, meaning that stunting in NWS is classified as a serious according to the global nutrition classification of severity ranking criteria. Anemia rates are also serious according to the global nutrition classification of severity ranking criteria, the prevalence of Anemia between children 6 – 59 months was found at 36.18%, *comments box

4 - Implementation of activities

The aim of this project is to improve the access to high-quality preventive and curative nutrition services for all vulnerable people in the implementation area, All the project activities will be implemented by SEMA team, including the recruitment of the staff for both the RRTs and the facility based services and deploy them, rent the vehicles for the RRT, procurement of the required materials and supplies, including the preventive and curative nutrition supplies, in addition to the MEAL activities.Throughout this project, SEMA will maintain a high level of coordination with the nutrition cluster team, and with all the nutrition partners in the area, to ensure optimal coverage without duplication or overlapping, and to organize the referral process between the nutrition partners, all the project activities will be reported to the nutrition cluster on monthly basis, throughout the 4W mechanism. Monitoring will be conducted at every stage of the program through the collection and analysis of routine data on a continuous basis throughout the life of the program. Data collection tools are appropriate and tailored to the program's needs. The methodology for monitoring will include using different tools and steps, such as beneficiaries’ disaggregated information, facilities status, pictures, and other relevant data to measure the achievement, observation checklist in the facilities to ensure that the staff complies with all SEMA’s policies and procedures. *comments box

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Exploitation by the service provider or other community actors Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigating: Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report. Conducting online sessions which include awareness key massages (using panners distributed by the PSEA network) with reporting channels available. SEMA as a member of the PSEA Network has a very robust PSEA policy. Furthermore, a standardized code of conduct will be signed by all employees. A strong reporting mechanism is in place, with the ability to conduct investigations through trained staff. 2- Tension between the host communities and IDPs Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigating: Services will be delivered for both host communities and IDPs following the vulnerability criteria after SEMA announces and explains it to them, with a special focus on new arrivals (also due to geographical targeting). 3- Difficulties in coordination with other sectors regarding the Referral pathway. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigating: SEMA will work on more coordination with other sectors by setting service mapping for all services that exist. An internal effective referral pathway will be used to ensure a comprehensive response. *comments box


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEMA ensures the privacy of the beneficiaries and vulnerable groups through adjusting and keeping the data of the beneficiaries secured, being responsible for the principle "Do-no-Harm” to the beneficiaries, and keeping their dignity. There is effective feedbackand complaint mechanism and beneficiaries are made aware of how to access it. This mechanism includes developed practices and channels for reporting Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The concept of PSEA is also considered in the awareness-raising topics offered for children and adults to raise their awareness on their rights and safety and the safety measure to report such misconduct. IEC materials, banners, and posters will be available in all facilities and distribution points. Staff will be oriented in the contracting stage on SEA issues and will sign the CoC. Refresher training for the staff will be conducted on a regular basis. All complaints will be taken into consideration and will be investigated following SEMA’s investigation policies, Disciplinary measures will be taken against any breach of CoC.
