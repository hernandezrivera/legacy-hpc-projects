Project Summary

According to the 2023 HNO, more than 12 million people in Syria are food insecure and 2.9 million are at risk of becoming so. This includes over 740,000 people in Latakia, more than half of the total population. The governorate also experienced the brunt of the February 2023 earthquake, which resulted in over 800 casualties as well as significant damage to buildings and infrastructure. Steadily increasing inflation – at an annual rate of more than 80% in 2022 – alongside both insufficient salary increases and the lag between them has reduced the ability to purchase enough food. Agricultural output remains constricted by low quality inputs as well as damaged irrigation systems, forcing the area to rely heavily on expensive imports to feed its population.
The VIP project will address these critical food security needs in three ways: providing vouchers to highly vulnerable households in earthquake affected areas redeemable for food commodities, providing agriculture and livestock in-kind grants and repairing damaged irrigation infrastructure. ADRA will target areas currently underserved by other humanitarian organizations – Jableh, Qrdaha, and Stamo. For the voucher program, focus will be given to households with many dependents, those with disabilities, those with young children, and families where women are the main provider and head of household. Manual verification in the form of house visits will be conducted for 100% of beneficiaries. For the irrigation repairs, locations will be chosen based on a needs assessment which will estimate the number of highly vulnerable people served by any given agricultural system.
There is a risk that beneficiaries could be exploited by vendors who recognize the limits placed on vouchers as opposed to cash. To mitigate this, vendors will be selected with price agreements to avoid gouging, and electronic vouchers will be redeemable for necessary goods at several vendors within reasonable distance from households, allowing for competition and thus greater beneficiary choice. There is likewise a risk of conflicts should people who were not beneficiaries of either program be upset about how beneficiaries were selected. This will be mitigated by transparently explaining criteria and communicating our actions proactively with local leaders, and providing information on how to contact our office for any complaints regarding program implementation or abuse.
In order to prevent sexual abuse and exploitation, vouchers will be distributed by staff which are trained in PSEA. Moreover, half of the staff members doing the distribution will be women themselves, and the location of distribution will be selected based on ease of access and security for women. The channels for reporting abuse will be well advertised and explained, and these will be monitored regularly to ensure that any problems which might arise are quickly dealt with.
There is likewise a risk of continuing inflation impacting how much food our vouchers can buy. To mitigate this, vouchers will be distributed on a monthly basis and converted from USD, and should the official exchange rate change, the amount of SYP provided by voucher will reflect the new number. The successful implementation of this project assumes that we will maintain access to all target areas, be able to procure all required equipment and materials, and obtain any necessary approvals from local authorities.
