Project Summary

The IRC proposes a sustainable food security response targeting 112,000 Individuals youths (boys and Girls) and women and men with access to farm land and/or previously engaged in farming in the three States, Northeastern Nigeria. The proposed project will target internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host community members affected by the Boko Haram insurgency, intermittent farmers/herds men conflict, and seasonal flooding for which 70% are women and 30% men.
Secondly, the IRC will pilot the delivery of multipurpose cash grant (MPG. The MPCG will enable vulnerable households affected by conflict to meet their basic needs in a manner that upholds their dignity. The MPCG enables the development of tools that can be used for other CBIs or MPCG implemented elsewhere. It also provides an entry point to identity options for linking the unrestricted and unconditional cash transfers with longer-term resilience and development assistance.
Also, the IRC will focus to rehabilitate community infrastructures through execution of Cash for Work schemes: The cash for work schemes are meant to support communities rehabilitate community’s infrastructures and well as increasing income of targeted individual or households. The focus will be for households having limited or no income opportunities and communities whose infrastructure were damaged during the crisis.
The IRC will initiate cash transfer approach (Unconditional Cash Transfer) through voucher system to meet food needs: The unconditional cash transfer part of the project will support communities through the provision of cash grants (Food Vouchers) to meet their food needs.
Lastly, Support to Small business enterprises making people self-employed will be initiated: In rural settings, the IRC will support women and men with existing business complementing their challenges in maximizing more profit. It will conduct a feasibility study to assess the most profitable IGA. Following that conditional grants will be given to the supported households followed by business and financial skills.
Protection Mainstreaming: The IRC has a protection sector that will conduct protection assessment to understand the dynamics of the typologies of the population (men, women, boys and girls with the IDPs, Host and returnees), finding from this assessment will help direct on how protection mainstreaming activities will be done. Also, the IRC has an established Feedback and accountability system that will allow project beneficiaries vent you their feeling about that project and report any abnormalities during the implementation of the activities listed above. The IRC will ensure that the markets are functioning well before initiating cash transfer programming, conduct thorough market assessment that informed on the security and challenges women face when dealing with money. The activities training and distribution will be used to pas awareness messages on how women should have physical, social and financial access to the market and should free from all forms of abuse. During PDM group discussion series will be held with women and men on separate group to discuss the use of resources. Banners and flyers will be developed with awareness massages and placed in project communities about sexual and human right abuses.
Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in the project design:
The IRC has a long-standing policy of ensuring a work environment that is free from all forms of harassment based upon an employee’s race, gender, religion, color, national or ethnic origin, citizenship, marital status, sexual orientation, age, disability, veteran status, or any other characteristic protected by federal, state or local U.S. law. Harassing conduct in violation of this policy, whether based on sex or otherwise, will not be tolerated. This policy applies to all employees of IRC, all Independent Contractors and Consultants, temporary agency workers and vendors or suppliers. Conduct by any of these individuals which violates this policy will also not be tolerated. As a result, the IRC will strengthen the awareness to the targeted beneficiaries of its Accountability and Feedback mechanism for any stakeholder of the project report such issues. In the event a determination is made that the complainant was subjected to harassment, effective remedial action will be undertaken to remedy the same. Any employees, including management, involved in harassment will be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including an unpaid suspension or termination from employment.
Monitoring and evaluation: The IRC understands that the context is fluid and continuous understanding need to be done. At the inception phase of the project a baseline will be conducted to understand the status of the targeted beneficiaries in each activity, after distribution of cash, agricultural inputs, and training, a PDM will be done to find out the appropriation and the impact of such distribution followed by and end line survey to measure the impact. Periodic field visit will be done by the project managers and coordinators to identify constraints and spontaneously find solutions to such problems.
At the end of each year, an evidence base learning event will be held to do a SWOT analysis that will inform project design for subsequent years.
Coordination: The IRC will continue to work with the FFS, Early recovery, and the Cash working groups to ensure project activities are aligned to their strategies. this will be ensured through consistently attending monthly meting and contribute to strategic documents of these groups. It will coordinate with other partners in the same project location to avoid beneficiaries overlapping and duplication of effort.

Needs

Around 7.7 million people in the BAY states are projected to be food insecure and considered to be facing crisis or emergency situations. This represents a significant improvement due to the delivery of food and nutrition assistance as well as livelihoods support; improved security conditions which has allowed for farming and market activities in some locations; and favorable climatic conditions for farming. Nevertheless, food security and nutrition situation remains extremely fragile particularly in Borno State, and IDPs state that food is their largest unmet need. Large populations in the northeast remain dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their food needs. In spite of some improvement compared to last year, access to farming/grazing land, labor market, productive assets and livelihood activities remains limited. Market and trade routes remain disrupted, restricting trade flows and activities. This is further exacerbated by route disruptions during the rainy season and sporadic hostilities. Agriculture accounts for 80 percent of the population food as well as income sources of the people in the northeast, the conflict continues to severely disrupt agriculture, livelihoods and market increasing food insecurity in the region. The food security in the northeast Nigeria remains extremely fragile, particularly in Borno State where many households still depend on assistance. As of end February 2018, over 2.3 million people have been supported with food security interventions, 82 per cent of whom were supported with food assistance and 18 per cent with agriculture and livelihoods assistance in the northeast of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. The 2018 Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis report on food insecurity—projected that nearly 3.7 million people in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe will likely face Crisis (Phase 3) or worse levels of acute food insecurity during the June–August, 2018 lean season if food assistance and livelihoods is not provided. The use of cash and vouchers for humanitarian assistance has grown steadily in the north-east since 2016. Between 2017 and 2018, the number of sectors with cash components grew from four to nine. - Education, Health, Shelter/NFIs, WASH, Nutrition, Early Recovery, Protection, GVB, Child Protection, and Food Security – while cash and vouchers programming accounted for 33% of the total financial requirements in 2018 HRP. In 2018, the CWG conducted various assessments including the Joint Cash Feasibility Assessment of February 2018 and the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment of August 2018 to confirm the potential for scaling up cash programming. The key findings from these assessments indicated that cash assistance will grow to approximately 45% and it will remain a significant component of the humanitarian response in 2019 across sectors. The aim of this cash assistance is to ensure that vulnerable households and returnees receive cash transfers and vouchers to cover a wide range of basic needs and services and linking them to early recovery efforts to support the rebuilding of resilience and livelihood.

Activities

Support to crop production through inputs (Seeds and Tools) through fairs with local traders and producers with a voucher system to 2, 000 farmers households (14,000 Individuals)• Establishment of Farmers Field Schools for farmers to adapt best farming practices through demonstration plots for the same 2,000 households (14,000 Individuals) in the agric activities • Strengthening of extension services • Agribusiness training for agro-dealers and farmers • Strengthen community savings through Village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLA) Increasing Safe Access to Fuel and Energy (SAFE) for 1000 Households (7000 individuals) : This part of the project will help address immediate food utilization needs, maximize nutrition intake, and minimize protection risks, health impacts and environmental hazards. The SAFE project has three main objectives- Reduced risk or incidence of gender-based violence- Reduced risk of house fires and burns- Environmental damage mitigation- Increase income for youthsThe project will first identify youths that will be training in making FES, to meet the first three objectives, the project will target 1000 households (7,000 Individuals) that will benefit from training, access to FES making kits. In order to increase the sustainability of the approach this service would be provided by local youth micro-enterprises set up and trained by a FES expert. The IRC will identify the most adapted technology FES models provide technical training to these youth farmers (male and female youths aged 18 – 35 years) on building of FES. The IRC will set up VSLA groups where women will save income and also have the access to borrow for business startup to start purchasing the stoves. • Support to Small business enterprises making people self-employed for 1000 individuals beneficiaries : In rural settings, the IRC will support women and men with existing business complementing their challenges in maximizing more profit. It will conduct a feasibility study to assess the most profitable IGA. Following that conditional grants will be given to the supported households followed by business and financial skills. Rehabilitate community infrastructures through execution of Cash for Work schemes: The cash for work schemes are meant to support communities rehabilitate community’s infrastructures and well as increasing income of targeted individual or households. The focus will be for 2000 Individuals(having limited or no income opportunities and communities whose infrastructure were damaged during the crisis. The IRC will facilitate the process by allowing communities identifying the most effective schemes. The schemes will be implemented in a four consecutive months with 10 days of work. Payment will be aligned with the government daily wage and will be paid at the end of each work period. Unconditional Cash Transfer through voucher system to meet food needs for 84,000 individuals ( 12,000 Housheolds): The unconditional cash transfer part of the project will support communities through the provision of cash grants (Food Vouchers) to meet their food needs. Targeted households will benefit from five consecutive months of food vouchers to enable them meet food needs. The Programme will allow people to improve their purchasing power, reduce negative coping strategy, improve food consumption and maintain/increase their productive assets. The amount to be distributed anticipates to meet 80% of the food basket value of the targeted households. Piloting MPCG for 2000 households (14000 Individuals beneficiaries) - MPCG will enable vulnerable households affected by conflict to meet their basic needs in a manner that upholds their dignity. The MPCG enables the development of tools that can be used for other CBIs or MPCG implemented elsewhere. It also provides an entry point to identity options for linking the unrestricted and unconditional cash transfers with longer-term resilience and development assistance.
