The hereby action aims at providing Education in Emergency assistance to vulnerable Syrian in Eastern Ghouta, rural Hama and Dara’a. All targeted governorates have been identified as relevant for intervention, with regards to the needs identified through TGH 2019 multisectoral assessments cross checked with the HRP severity summary and to TGH operational capacities.
As a response, this project includes activities aiming at covering Education immediate needs in line with Objective 1 “Scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth (aged 3-17 years)” and Objective 2 “Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 3-17 years) within a protective environment” of the Education sector 2020 HRP. The purpose of this project is to restore minimum levels of educational infrastructures and to enhance the quality access to safe and dignified learning environment for all, including those that are out-of-school, traumatized by the conflict, children with disabilities or who suffered from the absence of education services in the past years. Therefore, this project will contribute to the objective 1, 2 and 3 of the Education sector (access, quality, systems) and contribute to avoid the generation lost effect. The restoration of a proper learning environment starts with the light rehabilitation of schools, which lack doors and windows, desks and chairs, electricity and whose walls and roofs show numerous scars of war. This will guarantee the creation of a safe and dignified learning environment for all children and will ensure the protection of children, who will not be exposed anymore to intruders or to the harsh conditions of winter and summer.
WASH-in-Schools activities are also planned, responding to both WASH needs and WASH-related Protection issues, in particular in regard to gender and children with special needs. Another immediate assistance needed in all areas is the provision of kits (for schools and students) which most families cannot afford due to the national economic context. The provision of these items will also mitigate the risk of school dropout and will be a pull-in factor for children who are currently out-of-school.
The project also aims at strengthening the resilience of the targeted communities through the capacity building of all actors (training of school staff) and the inclusion of the communities in the activities (e.g. to identify out-of-school children and promote the importance of education) via Community Based Education and Committees (CBEC). NFE classes will also be ensured through an approach that ensures the sustainability of the service, by giving teachers the skills necessary to conduct these activities.
Noteworthy is that, although not fully developed under this sector specific project description, the locations have also been selected to be part of a more comprehensive response. They are thus targeted by WASH, Protection and Livelihood activities under other projects. These synergies will increase the impact of the Education activities described below.

4 - Needs

The dearth of Education services in Syria is significant due to the unprecedented damages or destructions of schools. Syria suffers from impoverished management capacities due to the massive departure of skilled personnel. As indicated in the 2019 HRP, 2.1 million children aged 5 to 17 do not have access to any education facility and a further 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out. Among the reasons behind this high number of OOS children, the lack of functioning schools has been commonly quoted and observed during field assessments (more than 10 localities newly assessed in November 2019 and more than 10 localities followed as per TGH ongoing projects), in particular in EG, Dara’a and Hama where the schools are overcrowded. Throughout 2019, TGH on-field national staff based in Hama and Damascus continuously monitored the needs in their localities and the newly accessible areas of Rural Dara’a and shared the same information. Moreover, the insufficient number of toilets at school has been reported as a common reason for dropping out, in particular for girls. Children work enrolment is another factor of dropping-out. Indeed, nearly 70% of the households interviewed by TGH in EG and Dara’a governorates stated that only one person is contributing to the household income, confirming that children early work is a tangible risk. Besides children labor, the lack of education services brings in other protection issues such as early marriages. Measures to prevent children to drop-out and to integrate OOS children into the education system seem therefore necessary. This is why TGH develops remedial classes, catch up classes and SLP activities. Teaching conditions are another key illustration of the current weakness of the education system. Teachers, of which up to 80% are non-formal, are often insufficiently trained and lack the required competencies to address the high diversity of pupils’ schooling history through tailored and conflict-sensitive approach.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A) Exploitation or abuse by TGH staff, service providers. Likelihood medium; Impact: high. Mitigation: At least 2 years of experience requested and recommendation letter from previous employers, organization awareness session on CP for staffs, volunteers and contractors before any activity at school . A child safety code of conduct will be signed by all actors. Close monitoring of the work done by contractors by TGH team. B) Long distance to go to school exposing students to GBV risk, UXO/ERW hazard. Likelihood: high; Impact: high. Mitigation: TGH will consult separately girls, boys, women and men in the assessment, design and implementation phases. TGH will add GBV and explosive hazard awareness. School rehabilitation will enable to organize only one shift which will reduce the school day duration and prevent the children to go back home too late.C) No gender sensitive toilets available in the school. Likelihood: high; Impact: high. Mitigation: Gender sensitive toilets rehabilitation is integrated in the project. D) Discrimination or perception of discrimination. Likelihood: low; Impact: high. Mitigation: Consultation and involvement of the community and neighboring. E) Gender inequality. Likelihood: Low & Impact: Medium. Mitigation: TGH will assure an equal access to the trainings for all. F) Delay or non-approvals of planned activities and processes. Likelihood: High; Impact: High Mitigation: TGH works closely with the local Education authorities of its areas of intervention DoE and national one MoE in Damascus, as well as with the education sector. G) Students, teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Likelihood: Medium; Impact High. Mitigation: TGH is implementing CP activities in all the locations targeted by Education and thus disseminate awareness messages about CP principles. In addition, through a community-based approach TGH Protection team is in charge of following children in need of PSS.

No major security incident targeting staff occurs. TGH authorization to operate in Syria and field acess remains granted. Syrian laws ruling humanitarian organizations remain favourable. Risks: (A) Suspension of TGH authorization to operate (B) access to area is suspended due to military operations or attacks (C) Access to beneficiaries is limited by local authorities (D) New population displacement in relation to conflict (E) Security incident targeting staff (F) Attempts from backlisted entities to bid for services, goods or work contracts (G) Sexual harassment from individuals in relation with action (H) Attempts to fraud and corruption by staff (I) Diversion of the provided assistance bringing in inequality towards the assistance (J) MoE confirms the restriction imposed to PSS activities in schools. B: Targeting of various regions to allow adjustments.
