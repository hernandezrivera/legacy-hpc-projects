Project Summary

The Emergency Food Security and Livelihood project focuses to provide continue supports to the Rohingya refugees and host communities for the live savings interventions (e.g. complementary food supports: Cooked food/ dry food ration distribution ; Cash grant supports -MPCG and enhancing adopted COVID-19 prevention and risk mitigation measures such as ensuring social distancing, limiting person to person contact, hygiene barriers, to cope the COVID 19 situation to the food security and livelihood) to meet the adequate diversified food access and basic needs. Considering current needs of food security & livelihood options as well as Government policy stands for restriction on direct cash injection in the camps, ACF will do the activities in collaboartion with Food Security sector and Transfer working Group.
Action Against Hunger has own gender policy which incorporates key gender equality measures of IASC Gender with Age Marker. The project will be ensured safe environment, upholding gender equality and protection-mainstreaming principles will be some of the measure taken to ensure gender responsive programming. The project will prioritizes service to COVID 19 affected most vulnerable populations including special group’s especially female-headed households, Pregnant and Lactating Women, under 5 children (vulnerable to malnutrition), People with Disability and Elderly.These interventions will contribute Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar District to live with good health, dignity and better safety.

Objectives

Expand support to improve food security and compensate for loss of livelihoods of the most vulnerable Bangladeshis and Rohingyas refugees in Cox’s Bazar District whose livelihoods will be impacted by the pandemic, through food and cash support according to need and situations.

Needs

From the first case of COVID-19 registered on March 8, Bangladesh has seen as of April 28 2020, 6462 peoples affected, and 155 deaths. Local transmission find 60 districts over the country. In order to mitigate the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic, the Government of Bangladesh has issued ‘Stay at Home’ directives shutting down productive, commercial and tourism activities from 26 March through May 5, 2020. Cox’s Bazar District is one of the country’s poorest and most vulnerable, with a total Bangladeshi population of 2,650,000 and refugee’s population. Additionally, the District Administration of Cox’s Bazar predicts that more than 700,000 people in Cox’s Bazar District will be immediately jobless due to the ‘Stay at Home’ directive. As consequence of all precautionary measures to prevent COVID-19 spreading the low-income people like all sectoral day labors, rickshaw/van pullers, auto and other three-wheeler drivers etc., became job less. This loss of livelihoods coupled with a decrease in access to the local market has disrupted activities and resulted in extra support needs for food security. On 19 April 2020, six patients tested positive for COVID-19 in Cox's Bazar district. Community transmission is alarming and day-to-day positive case is increasing. The situation reflect both at in the refugee camp as well as in the host community. A lot of the host community people and refugees were earning their income through involving in different intervention in the camp (i.e. day labor, supplier, or other daily basis jobs, small traders/ shop keepers) to sustain their daily livelihood. As most of the development implementation came to a halt, so the source of income are decreasing. According to the Refugee influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 2, May 2 2019) high vulnerability levels persist (over 88% of refugees and 39% of host community population), coping mechanisms such indebtedness to support expenditure on food and non-food needs. Almost 44% of refugees and 30% host community found to have unacceptable food consumption. Limited access to an income is major driver of poverty for refugees as well as host community population. So there is high requirements of food (both for cooed and food items) is gradually increasing and if this situation continues then a long time it would be a situation of as like disaster and then huge food distribution support would be required. ACF carried out the Rapid Need Assessment (RNA) in April 2020 and finding showed that only 6% of them received assistance either general food or cash after COVID-19 started and 96% are facing problems caused by the COVID-19 situation. Due to the COVID-19 situation, 91% of the sample respondent participants lost their work or job and 73% of HHs are facing food insecurity in their family. 98% primary livelihoods are affected due to COVID-19 effects and COVID-19 situation significantly effects on income of low-income peoples. If the situation become unchanged that case the next 30 day’s income will reduced 81%. 25% of the participants have no stock of rice and 65% of the respondents have less than one-week’s supply of rice. According to the recent emergency nutrition assessments done by ACF and Nutrition Sector in the refugee settlements, the Rohingya population constitute serious level of malnutrition and in need of nutritional support. Economic vulnerability because of lack of regular income source is the main driver of food insecurity, especially among the new and older refugees and host community of Cox’s Bazar district. Therefore, a food security intervention mainly focusing on delivering nutritious hot meals has become very essential to protect that population from food insecurity during crisis. Besides that, it is a high time to consider any outbreak of diseases and especially the pandemic of COVID-19

Activities or outputs

1. Food distribution (hot cooked meal): ACF/Action Against Hunger developed and operated Community Kitchen program to improve nutritional status of the targeted members of refugee households living in outreach makeshift settlements and surrounding villages in the host community as well as the disaster preparedness. As part of COVID 19 response and DRR preparedness, Food distribution (hot cooked meal) intervention will be implemented in 3 Upazila (Teknaf, Ukhiya and Cox’s Bazar Sadar) under Cox’s Bazar district and this will cover people from both camps and host community who are COVID affected/suspected and quarantined/isolated who are vulnerable. The food distribution (hot cooked meal) will be served pandemic of COVID-19 affected/suspected and quarantined and vulnerable populations included specially targeted groups such as PLW, malnourished children, elderly and chronically sick person etc. through community kitchen to ensure the food when they are not able to reach the cooked food at home because of COVID 19 caused. Action Aganist Hunger will provide the hot cooked meal in the quarantine people in the health facilities as when food request will be recieved from different agencies/stakeholders in the camps and host. Under this intervention, daily 6000 targeted persons/meals (30% persons/meals in camps and 70% targeted persons /meals in host) will get support for hot cooked meal once per day for 06 months within the project period. 2. Food distribution (Fresh food commudities): Food distribution (Fresh food commudities) intervention will be implemented in eight Upazila (Cox’s Sadar, Ukhiya, Teknaf, Chakaria, Ramu, Kutubdia, Pekua and Moheskhali) of Cox’s Bazar district. This action will be targeted the 8000 HHs in the host community in 8 upazila who are vulnerable people of COVID 19 in terms of capacity to provide cooked meal or people in shortage of food due to pandemic. Distribution of food ration package also will target to the specially targeted groups such as PLW, malnourished children, elderly and chronically sick person. Each targeted household will get dry food ration package equivalent to @ 3000 BDT for one time (8000 HHs * I time * package @ 3000 BDT/HHs) from this project to ensure the food security.3. Multi-Purpose Cash Grant (MPCG)distribution: The MPCG grant will be distributed among 10000 beneficiaries’ households. This will cover people from both camps and host community who are COVID affected/suspected and quarantined/isolated who are vulnerable especially intis pandemic situation. The project also focus on households having PLW, children under 5 years, women and child headed households, households with elderly and chronically sick person and person with disability from both Rohingya and host community people for this MPCG support. The project will distribute total BDT 9000 as MPCG for each targeted households to meet the need of food and non-foods items. Each of the targeted households will get BDT 9000 (2 months @ BDT 4500 per month). The Cash will be distributed through financial service providers (bank/ bKash). Therefore, 5% of extra fee or cost will be applied for cash transfers. The MPCG will allow the beneficiaries to spend the money as per their priority needs. Based on priority they will be able to buy foods and other non-food items from local markets according to their choice if it is priority for them to meet the need of basic requirements during this pandemic COVID 19 situation.
