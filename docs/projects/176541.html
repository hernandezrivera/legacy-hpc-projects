Project Summary

The project is aimed at addressing the most vulnerable community groups in the areas of IDP camps (Masna and Wau Naivasha camp) and Jur River (Nyin-akok and Maple) society that is suffering from WASH related basic necessitates. These location are identified to have low access to sufficient drinking water, sanitation facilities, sufficient materials for hand washing facilities, and presence of vector and water bore diseases. In Masna camp (which is localted in Wau County) Dorcas is currently reaching with various WASH intervention covering 728 people, which are in new settlement areas. However, in the older settlement location of Masna there is no partner working on WASH. Therefore, a total of 9,019 individuals are in a critical situation and need emergency support. Hence, Dorcas has designed this project to expand its intervention and reach out to this people to meet their needs. As Dorcas is currently operational in Wau Naivasha camp as WASH partner in collaboration with WASH Core Pipeline, UNICEF, and IOM the experience will be replicated in to Old Masna temporary settlement location. Dorcas by its Vocational Skills Development Project is also planning to train people with disability on business management and provide start up grant to enable them engage in to various activities and generate income. This intervention will further support the effort of inclusion and improvement of livelihood. Furthermore, the project will be implemented to address the most critical challenges of people in Jur River associated to WASH along with the FSL activities Dorcas is implementing in the area. The integration of different sectors is expected to bring tangible and sustainable outcome among the target households. In total the project will accomplish various activities to address the needs of about 14,592 community members in Jur River in a specific location of Nyin-akok and Maple. Major undertakings of the project will be new drilling of boreholes, Rehabilitation and repairs of boreholes, hygiene and sanitation promotion campaigns, training of water management committee (WMCs), training of Community hygiene promoters (CHVs), training of hand pump mechanics, supply of bicycle for hand pump mechanics, provision of repairs tool kits for boreholes, provision of dignity kits for adolescent girls, conduct water testing for chemical and bacteriological tests, setting up of hand washing stations in public markets and construction of emergency communal latrines in the IDP camps. Furthermore, the project will reach households with SAM chidden in the IDP sites by providing WASH NFI kit following the standard recommended by WASH cluster. The activity will be carried out together with a nutrition partner, Johanniter, operating both in Wau Naivasha and Masna camps. The organization has also a Stabilization Center at Wau Naivasha camp. The total duration of the project will be nine months. In general the project will endeavor to reach 24,971.

2 - Project activities

In the locations selected in Jur River various activities will be undertaken to achieve the intended objectives. The intervention will target those households that require access to basic WASH services. These includes both water and improved sanitation & Hygiene awareness. As per the national WASH cluster 2021 severity mapping Jur River county is at severity level five. This is mainly as a result of shortage of safe water as the majority of the people fetch water from unprotected sources. Moreover, there is a lack of knowledge on improved hygiene and sanitation practices. Therefore, the project will target those community members deprived of clean water access by bringing permanent solution by developing new water facilities and repair/rehabilitation of non-functional water sources. The interventions will strive to make sure the sustainable use of the facilities by establishing and capacitating management committees in collaboration with government experts. As Dorcas has been encouraging and training the community on sustainability, significant changes have been observed in different intervention locations where households began to contribute money on regular basis. The is being collected by selected water management committee members in the objective of maintaining boreholes that are broken or stopped functioning. Therefore, in the same approach the project will be undertaken in the objective of ensure long term use of the clear water sources. In connection to improving hygiene practices the same target groups will be reached through various approaches to enhance communities awareness and skills. This will be done through organizing and training of village Community Hygiene Promoters. The awareness creation will be conducted through facilitating campaigns and door to door education on sanitation and hygiene. 7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Project information is provided through kickoff meetings that will be carried out with community representatives. Besides, the project will establish village level project management committees (PMCs) which will involve in the project from the inception period. PMCs disseminate information related to the project to the beneficiaries in addition to staff. A PMC constitute 50% women so as to ensure sufficient representation and bring forward the needs of women and for the purpose of capacity building. PMCs will be selected by the project beneficiaries and attempt will be made to represent all sect of the community groups to achieve the intended objectives of the project. Accountability “is about using power responsibly. It involves taking account of the needs, concerns, capacities and disposition of affected parties, and explaining the meaning of, and reasons for, actions and decisions. Accountability is therefore also about the right to be heard and the duty to respond” according to Humanitarian Accountability Partnership International definition. To strengthen accountability for the affected population and to guarantee their full participation in project implementation, a feedback and complaint mechanism (FCM) will be formed. The feedback and complaints mechanism will provide safe environment conducive for project participants/community to present their feedbacks or complaints during the implementation period. The FCM investigation team will be form and mainly will receive and analyze the feedback or complaints and report the finding to both the participants or complainants and Programme Manager. Secured suggestion boxes, community help desk, phone feedback (hotline), and field monitoring visits shall be the main channels for gathering feedbacks or complaints from project participants/community. The choice for these FCM channels is based on their simplicity to use by the participants or community to provide feedback or complaints. The feedback and complaints procedures shall involve the reception of the feedback or complaints from the complainants, its registration by the receiver preferably MEAL officer, acknowledgment of the receipt of the feedback or complaints to complainants, investigation and finding, Reporting (communication of the findings to the complainants), and appeal if the complainant is not satisfied with the findings and resolution and appeal reporting.In a nutshell, the feedback and complaints mechanisms should be designed focusing on beneficiaries or communities’ participation to enable their full involvement during project implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiaries feedback will be collected periodically through various ways and the program team will discuss on each of the points forwarded from community members and provide timely response to the beneficiaries. In relation to feedback that require adaptation possible efforts will be done in consultation with stakeholders and the Donor to incorporate in the project undertakings.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

This will enable the beneficiaries not to come to the distribution site twice. Furthermore, based on the feedback obtained from the affected people partners are agreed to increase the one time distribution days from three to five. This will reduce the size of the crowed and waiting time. In both camps the distribution sites are very close to the shelters that IDPs live in. Hence, distance will not be a major problem. 8.2 Dorcas has protection policy that all the employees should sing before beginning working in the organization. Furthermore, during activity implementation close monitoring will be made in collaboration with community representatives through establishing feedback mechanize by creating awareness among target groups.
