Project Summary

The Norwegian Refugee Council through its Information Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) and General Protection programming seek to address needs of IDPs, host communities, returnees and other vulnerable individuals by increasing access to protection services, basic essential services, and securing Housing Land and Property (HLP) rights through improved security of tenure to the conflict-affected population in Northeast Nigeria, Nigeria.
NRC being the HLP AoR co-lead will coordinate and strengthen HLP services and structures for the target communities through a series of coordination meetings, community engagement, initiatives, and events. To achieve that, logistical support will be provided to collaborative dispute resolution centers/structures and the land ministry to facilitate their work. The project intends to enhance the capacity of local authorities and community leaders on HLP and collaborative dispute resolution so they can uphold the basic human rights of IDPs, returnees, and host communities. Furthermore, the absence of civil administration in hard-to-reach LGAs poses a challenge in terms of accessibility to basic essential services, especially for the physically disabled and other vulnerable groups. Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) in north-east Nigeria continue intensely across villages, communities, and towns and this has led to more displacement. Ongoing camp closure and return by the Borno State Government has exerted pressure on existing facilities and structures both at return locations as well as areas of integration which has led to reduced access to essential services especially for IDPs and returnees. Cases of evictions have been reported, and the trend will likely continue into 2024, with the most vulnerable households at risk of eviction and other related HLP rights violations.
NRC’s Protection from Violence (PfV) (the general protection component) team aims to contribute to increasing the rights and capacity of the conflict affected populations to prevent protection risks and threats. Specialized protection activities such as protection case management and individual protection assistance will be provided to improve the well-being and strengthen their resilience, protection, and dignity of the conflict-affected population in BAY States. The NRC protection project will target vulnerable individuals from displaced and host communities to benefit from the protection interventions that respond to their needs. This project will contribute to Protection Sector’s response objective to; a) to mitigate and respond to the harm caused to persons who have suffered violence, coercion, exploitation, serious neglect, or discrimination, and to restore their capacity to live safe and dignified lives, and b) to strengthen targeted protection services to address the needs and vulnerability of persons at heightened risks of violence, exploitation, serious neglect, or discrimination; enhance their capacities and reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms.

1 - Needs

Despite the ongoing military operations against NSAGs, the security situation in north-east Nigeria remains unpredictable. It is characterized by violent conflict, terrorism, and criminal activities that have resulted in multiple displacements, the killing and abduction of civilians, the destruction of property, and widespread human rights abuses. Adamawa state which is the second state across the BAY states presents 2.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023(HNO 2023).For the HLP-specific challenges, The HNO 2023 revealed that about 1,055,810 million displaced persons are not able to fully enjoy their housing-land-and-property (HLP) rights. This includes IDPs (27 percent), returnees (27.2 percent), and host community members (45.8 percent). The most affected areas are Bama, Chibok, Monguno, and Ngala LGAs in Borno State and Gombi, Madagali, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Yola North, and Yola South LGAs in Adamawa State. HLP issues include a lack of equitable access to basic HLP rights, limited access to land for both shelter and livelihoods, forced evictions, and access to justice, legal aid services, and other social services. Women are particularly affected by cultural and social norms and practices that limit their access to HLP rights, with many prohibited from owning and inheriting HLP or enjoying land tenure rights.Based on the few HLP needs identified above, there is a need to improve affected people’s access to legal documentation and identification to enhance free movement and reduce cases of harassment by security forces while going to their farms or other day-to-day livelihood activities. Additionally, there is an evident need for improved and unhindered access to housing, land, and property documentation by the affected people to strengthen tenure security. The protracted nature of the conflict, coupled with Borno State Government-led returns, flooding and high cost of living, has exacerbated the displacement of vulnerable conflict affected population in the BAY States. Secondary displacement, destruction of HLP assets, forced evictions, and disputes between original owners and secondary occupants which are the major drivers of HLP needs. These issues expose the most vulnerable to significant protection risks that threaten their dignity, physical well-being, health and living standards. Approximately 1.6 million people are projected to need Housing, Land & Property assistance. It is also believed that these number may increase as the Government push for more camp closure, relocation, and returns. (HNO 2024)There is a need to ensure that HLP concerns such as issues of Lack of tenure insecurity, HLP disputes, Damaged and destruction of HLP, Legal pluralism are addressed to prevent escalations of these concerns after people are returned..

2 - Activities

NRC's ICLA will establish local coordination points through local partners most especially in the areas of return/relocation in Borno state, and garrison towns of Michika and Madagali LGAs of Adamawa state. The HLP AoR, will strengthen the capacity of the existing dispute resolution mechanism (both formal & informal) in material and technical capacity to be able to respond to HLP disputes arising in the places of return and relocation. It will carry out the provision of group information/awareness on HLP rights and legal identity, and the provision of counselling services on HLP and legal identity. It will support project participants with processing of HLP documentation including certificates of occupancy for returnees and IDPs with land allocation to secure their land title and prevent future disputes on those land. Special consideration shall be given to those benefiting from permanent shelter construction by Shelter partners as this will assist in the achievement of a durable solution. Also, through the sub-sector, it will continue close monitoring of informal settlements and any likely additional to mitigate against surprising eviction incidents. ICLA team will strengthen the capacity of line ministries and legal identity structures through the provision of material and logistical support to enhance its capacity to be able to respond to issues and it will propose to establish and donate a dispute resolution center to the line ministries. Through the general protection activities, the conflict-affected communities in Nigeria are aimed to be protected from violence, abuse, coercion, and deprivation and are physically and psychologically safer. Strong advocacy and coordination will also be NRC’s commitment in North East Nigeria. NRC’s protection objective to contribute to the improvement of safety, dignity, and protection of rights of conflict-affected populations will be achieved through standalone and integrated protection responses such as a community-based protection approach, direct service provision, establishing protection information management systems for evidence-based advocacy use and development of self-protection strategies aiming to prevent the violence in conflict-affected communities. In 2024, NRC PfV will provide case management services, individual protection assistance, internal and external referrals, and capacity building of key stakeholders through training on protection topics. It will also carry out protection monitoring and establish or strengthen the existing community-based protection structures. NRC will provide case management services. Based on individual assessment, NRC may provide the following basic psychological first aid, tailored ad-hoc assistance to meet the specific needs or referral to other NRC internal core competencies or to external partners providing services in the target area. NRC will also provide individual protection assistance (IPA). IPA support is a one-time off assistance and should be directly related to a specifically assessed protection needs of the individual, with a clear protection outcome. NRC will establish or strengthen community-based protection networks who will be provided with various capacity building training to strengthen their structures or committees and mainstream protection especially on understanding protection risk, vulnerability and identification of persons with specific needs. Other relevant stakeholders in the community will also be trained about protection. NRC will carry out protection monitoring and assess the situation and respond to immediate needs of the affected population. NRC will adopt the harmonized protection monitoring developed by the Protection Sector Technical Working Group on Protection Monitoring which NRC is an active member.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NRC will adhere to the core guiding protection mainstreaming standards throughout the project. NRC will ensure that the proposed intervention ensures the safety and dignity of target populations, as well as avoids causing harm. NRC will ensure that all demographic groups can be part of the community engagement and governance process, to ensure a better representation. The participation of women, youth, older persons, and persons with disabilities will be highly encouraged and supported. NRC will thereafter work in close coordination with community representatives to identify needs and conduct referrals to appropriate services. NRC will also mainstream protection and GBV in the site improvement and maintenance activities, by ensuring that specific groups of the population have an appropriate access to communal services. Infrastructures and services will be improved to integrate accessibility and inclusion facilities. NRC has developed a Safe and Inclusive Programming (SIP) Action Plan and will deliver training to project staff, to increase its staff capacity to ensure SIP in all the phases of its programming. NRC will ensure that any community-based structures that it work with are trained on safeguarding and basic protection principles, as part of the induction process.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NRC takes guidance from the Core Humanitarian Standards definition of accountability and understands AAP as a process of using power responsibly, taking account of, and being held accountable by the people we seek to assist. As with other NRC projects, a Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) will ensure that beneficiaries and communities have the opportunity to register feedback and complaints. This mechanism includes complaints and suggestion boxes at project sites. NRC will ensure that these sites and CFRM are accessible for people with disabilities. NRC’s CFRM mechanism will also include complaint and feedback hotlines, email, and face-to-face sessions between staff and beneficiaries. NRC uses this feedback to resolve any issues that may arise, and adjust programming plans to better suit the local context. A dedicated team, which is responsible for ensuring multiple channels, delivers this CFRM and tools for complaints and feedback are available for the affected population. The M&E team, as well as technical program staff will manage complaints related to NRC services. CFRM staff reporting directly to senior management provides additional accountability toward targeted communities and ensure feedback and complaints are discussed on a senior level within NRC.NRC encourages face-to-face meetings with senior managers for all site visits, as well as ensuring a practical ratio of female field programming staff. Beneficiaries will therefore have the opportunity to express their grievances, feedback and report challenges with accessing the assistance directly. NRC’s M&E department will carry out post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the progress of activities towards achieving the project objectives.
