Project Summary

Project Summary: Sudan plunged into violence on 15 April 2023 after an armed conflict erupted all over the country, but mainly in the capital, Khartoum, and the Darfur region, The clashes are the result of a power struggle between the Sudanese army and the paramilitary group known as the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and it has been marked by brutal violence against civilians and destruction of basic services. Insecurity and active hostilities constrain civilians’ freedom of movement. The multidimensional crisis for conflict-affected populations is characterized by continued violence, widespread displacement due to the violence, high rates of death, disease outbreak, and injuries, coupled with severe food insecurity and disrupted livelihoods, a major malnutrition crisis, climate induced hazards and deterioration of basic service provision, overall affecting children’s lives, and wellbeing. In parallel to the increasing insecurity, the economic situation has severely deteriorated, with a dramatic drop in the value of the Sudanese pound and inflation estimated at close to 300 per cent. The rising costs of goods is exacerbating food insecurity which have added a new dimension to the complex security situation in East Darfur State. According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), there was an estimated 606,700 IDPs, returnees, refugees, and asylum seekers in the state needing humanitarian assistance – a situation that was only aggravated by the April conflict. A WASH sector primary needs assessment conducted in May 2023, highlighted how women, girls, and children are among the most vulnerable groups in the target areas because of lack of access to water, poor hygiene and sanitation practices, while the number of people in need (PiN) of humanitarian assistance has increased from 15.8 million, estimated in November 2022, to 24.7 million in May 2023, representing a 57 per cent increase (Revised HRP, 2023). WASH needs for families arriving from conflict are visibly increasing, with the vast majority being internally displaced persons (IDPs), return movement and spontaneous relocations, especially in high vulnerability areas such as informal sites or host villages. Such population movement in these areas has led to a higher demand of basic WASH services already under sustained pressure. 2 Implementation Strategy & Technical Guidance: Saving Lives: Reduction of morbidity and mortality rates to below emergency thresholds and improvement in wellbeing, by ensuring that the most vulnerable households, groups, and communities are better able to mitigate risk and withstand shocks and stresses through increased access to WASH services. The lack of safe drinking water, in combination with poor hygiene practices leaves, a large proportion of East Darfur population at persistent risk ,good hygiene practices will be done to reduce health risks ,during emergencies such as response to floods and disease promotion of activities will emphasize prevention of diarrhea, hand washing, menstrual hygiene, infant and young child feeding (IYCF)), water treatment, food storage and waste disposal. This project is aimed at mitigating the challenges related to safe water access, Sanitation, and basic hygiene in the Eldaein Besides, it will endeavor to enhance knowledge and technical skills of the target households on improved practices of sanitation and hygiene. A total of 28,200 people will be reached with various types of WASH interventions activities that AAO are implementing in the area. The integration of different sectors is expected to bring tangible and sustainable outcome among the target households. Major undertakings of the project will be Rehabilitation and repairs of boreholes, hygiene and sanitation promotion campaigns, training of water management committee (WMCs), training of Community hygiene promoters (CHVs), training of hand pump mechanics, provision of repairs tool kits for boreholes, WASH committees will be composed of men, women

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

AAO will prioritize safety and dignity to ensure no harm is caused and will seek to identify, prevent and mitigate any unintended negative effects or risks during the intervention to girls, boys, children, young people, and project participants. AAO will target the most vulnerable without discrimination and ensure that all participants are aware of and has access to necessary and relevant services. AAO implements ‘Do no Harm’ measures to ensure the safety and dignity of children and young people that interact with its staff, associates or volunteers. It has zero tolerance against all forms of abuse and exploitation as articulated in its policy on safeguarding for children and young people. AAO will continue to engage with the local authorities to ensure that activities are context appropriate and will orient all third parties working with and for AAO and keep signed acknowledgements of the safeguarding policy thereafter. Activities will be underpinned by human rights principles and will strictly adhere to the core humanitarian standards and maintain the principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. AAO maintains high standards of safeguarding and preventing sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse PSEA, especially in emergency situations. AAO s Safeguarding Policy and procedures are implemented during the design and implementation of project interventions with vigilance. Safeguarding and PSHEA risk analyses and assessments of project interventions will be conducted with mitigation measures/contingency plans identified for each risk. AAO will work to ensure staff, partners, project participants and communities are aware of the safeguarding and PSEA policies, its minimum codes of behavior, and requirements to uphold high standards of professional behavior. Project participants, especially children and young people will be respected, and their safety and protection will not be compromised when obtaining and using information on and images of them. AAO will identify groups at risk of unsafe WASH practices and priorities provision of alternatives. AAO will identify the power dynamics (who can access assets and inputs and who can influence decisions) and will monitor and respond to potential exploitative practices or abuses. Information will be communicated through various means to reach the wider community and account for different literacy levels. All layers of the affected population, especially those with intersecting vulnerabilities, will be consulted in identifying and responding to WASH needs to collect accurate information about specific requirements and preferences. AAO will encourage communities to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes to promote a sense of ownership, build self-esteem and improve the sustainability of the response. Collective WASH projects will be promoted contribute to community protection and resilience.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

AAO has consistent and strong capacities in gender mainstreaming, and a strong focus on protection for vulnerable women, girls, boys, and men. Gender has been a main consideration in the design and delivery of the project. Due to a very strong religious and traditional culture in these provinces, women's movement is restricted and their participation in the public sphere is limited. AAO has managed to reach the IDP women through the trained hygiene volunteers from the internal displaced persons , together with an experienced female national emergency coordinator engaged to directly talk to women and record their concerns. AAO makes a deliberate effort to identify the specific WASH needs of women and girls while including equal number of women enumerators in needs assessment teams and ensuring the collection of data from 50 % of women. AAO and partners gathered dis¬aggregated data that included safety and security concerns of women and girls .The dis¬aggregated data from the needs assessment were also split into age and gender categories. Special attention is paid to the needs of vulnerable groups of women and girls, such as female headed households, pregnant and lactating women, women with disabilities, elderly women, adolescent girls, and children. AAO has carried out consulting meetings with women and girls on water and sanitation needs to ensure that program strategies mitigate the exposure to risk. During the implementation, AAO and partners will maintain a protective environment at the community level, so that girls and women are not exposed to harm or gender-based violence while undertaking their daily WASH activities. Further, gender equity will be observed while implementing those activities that are not specifically targeted at women and girls. Women, men, boys, and girls are treated equally, with their differing needs being targeted and supported accordingly. Participation of all age and gender groups will be emphasized so that all beneficiaries can voice their concerns and express their preferences during implementation. Women and girls will be consulted to ensure they feel safe when using the established WASH services at their preferred places. The project will consider several approaches to address the issue of women's involvement by mobilizing elderly women, which is more accepted, identifying trained women from the community already engaged in the previous emergency response project and in the needs, assessments carried out by AAO. The project will work with the men of the target communities to get acceptance for women's participation in WASH committees and trainings. Women and men will be equally and meaningfully involved in decision ¬making related to emergency WASH program design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. AAO have already established complaint mechanism to enable women, youth, elders, disabled and minorities to raise their concern
