Project Summary

As per the Centrality of Protection in Humanitarian Action statement by the IASC Principals of 17 December 2013 protection in a humanitarian setting entails upholding human rights and respect for international humanitarian law (IHL).
1 The project “Human rights in humanitarian action” seeks to increase the presence of the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine in the conflict affected areas, both in the areas controlled by armed groups and areas controlled by the Government of Ukraine, and to establish a stable and continuous monitoring mission with geographical coverage throughout the Donetsk and Luhansk regions, including the ability to cover the grey zones.
2 An important aspect is to ensure that analysis stemming out of the human rights monitoring is utilized by key humanitarian actors, and thus contribute to shape their humanitarian response to the protection needs of the conflict affected population. A human rights approach including gender analysis to the humanitarian response is vital where the population is suffering due to lack of protection and failure of accountability mechanisms.
3 The project also aims at providing direct support to victims and their families, and strengthening the protection of the conflict affected population as the physical presence of human rights monitors in conflict affected areas may deter further human rights violations and serve in itself as a protection mechanism. By engaging directly with victims and witnesses of human rights violations, this will allow to refer victims to life saving humanitarian services, promote an environment that is conducive to the promotion and respect for human rights and contribute to protect the humanitarian space. HRMMU will also place a specific focus on accountability for the affected population, as its monitoring and reporting work will culminate in documentation of the information that would lay a foundation for future accountability through international mechanisms, and as an early collection of accounts that may bolster transitional justice and reconciliation efforts. In addition, HRMMU will help to create conditions for increased accountability for human rights violations.

Needs

Human rights situation has deteriorated in Ukraine as the hostilities evolved into an armed conflict in April 2014. There has been a breakdown of the rule of law with serious human rights violations including illegal detentions, hostage taking, torture and ill treatment, sexual and gender based violence in the territory under the control of the armed groups. OHCHR has also documented numerous human rights violations in the areas under control of the Government of Ukraine. Coupled with repeated indiscriminate shelling of residential areas and civilian infrastructure and reports of international humanitarian law violations, this has led to displacement and protection needs for a large group of people. As a result, access to socio-economic rights as well as to justice and accountability has deteriorated. In that context, human rights monitoring is critical to document human rights abuses, to identify critical human rights violations and subsequent need of protection services to design protection/humanitarian responses. There is a need for a stable presence in the field. Field offices will make it logistically easier for the population to access the monitoring mission as it can reach out to the most remote or marginalized parts of society. It can help to build confidence, which in turn is likely to increase cooperation with the community, which is essential for human rights monitoring and fact finding to be effective. Confidence building measures are crucial to reverse the entrenchment of the divide between the population living on both sides of the contact line. A field office will benefit from a direct and regular contact with local authorities and greater access to a larger number of communities, which can enable prompt local responses. Experience has shown that the presence of field offices considerably bolsters protection at the local level. Field offices help to “regionalize” the analysis, thus reflecting more accurately local conditions and leading to strategies tailored to the local peculiarities. It enables the establishment of more systematic communication with both the humanitarian community and the authorities to be used for advocacy for change. 1 Civil and Political rights: Women and men having been held in conflict related detention by armed groups and government of Ukraine entities; women and men subjected to conflict related ill treatment, torture including conflict related sexual violence by the armed groups and the government forces; women and men who are missing.2 Social and Economic rights: vulnerable individuals including older men and women, boys and girls whose rights have been impacted by the conflict, including right to health, right to food, right to water and right to freedom of movement. 3 Individuals at particular risk such as women living in proximity of military personnel who may be at risk of sexual exploitation; civilians near the contact line at risk of shelling as well as explosive Remnants of War (ERW), where men and boys often at higher risk; human rights defenders and journalists in areas with lack of rule of law and limited space for civil society and freedom of expression. A risk analysis will be conducted ensuring that the individuals interviewed are both men and women, and that the interviews will be conducted by mixed teams (men and women). Men and women are at times at risk of the same human rights violations, but there are also certain violations where one gender is predominantly targeted, e.g. based on current findings men have been more at risk of conflict related detention, while older women have been of higher risk of violations related to social and economic rights. The project is designed to target mainly adults, but HRMMU will also pay particular attention when children are at risk, for instance in the casualty recording, and is coordinating with UNICEF on Children in Armed Conflict and the 6 grave violations.

Activities & outcomes

Activities:1) Daily interviews, field visits, monitoring and reporting contributing to protection by presence. Identifying particular risks of men and women will be part of the monitoring work.2) To utilize the findings from the monitoring to track trends; inform early warning and provide referrals to the humanitarian community for follow up. This includes strengthening protection referral mechanisms with humanitarian actors in Government controlled areas and territory controlled by the armed groups. 3) To utilize the findings to raise awareness of human rights violations among Ukrainian stakeholders and parties to the conflict, the UN system and broader international community. To support a human rights approach in humanitarian coordination mechanisms, particularly Protection meetings, Child protection, Gender Based Violence, Mine Action and Housing Land and Property. Work to inform humanitarian actors of the human rights situation allowing humanitarian actors to respond to needs of persons whose rights have been violated (as per Rights Up Front).4) Address relevant authorities when urgent interventions are required such as torture in detention, enforced disappearance and support related to civil documentation. 5) Civilian Casualty monitoring; continue and further strengthen the casualty recording (deaths and injuries with gender and age disaggregated data) to advocate and inform parties to the conflict on measures to be taken and their obligations as per International Humanitarian Law (IHL), and to inform humanitarian actors of where the needs are greatest, e.g. need for Mine Risk Education in areas with high number of casualties due to ERWs. 6) Through regular interaction and consultations, develop capacity of local stakeholders to identify and address human rights violations and vulnerabilities and how to address this. 7) Pay particular attention to possible conflict related sexual violence: document and report on cases of sexual gender based violence with a view to ensure referrals to relevant GBV actors and conduct advocacy with relevant authorities on this issue. Outcomes:1) Government of Ukraine, armed groups of the self-proclaimed ‘Donetsk people’s republic’ and ‘Luhansk people’s republic’, civil society counterparts, the UN and wider international community will be better informed of the human rights situation in the eastern Ukraine, in particular in the Donetsk and Luhansk regions.2) Increased understanding and capacity of the civil society counterparts and authorities to address human rights violations; Government of Ukraine, armed groups of the self-proclaimed ‘Donetsk people’s republic’ and ‘Luhansk people’s republic’ and civil society counterparts to have better understanding of accountability mechanisms and the conduct of effective investigations of most egregious human rights violations, including conflict related GBV and sexual exploitation.3) The Government of Ukraine to have an effective national human rights action plan informed by the findings from the human rights monitoring mission, including key priorities on women, peace and security issues.4) Humanitarian organizations use human rights analysis as a basis for designing a response with a human rights based approach to the protection needs of the conflict affected population.5) A reduction in human rights violations due to the overall work of the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine and its partners, e.g. individual follow up on cases leading to release of persons illegally detained; advocacy based on civilian casualty recording may lead to decrease of targeting of civilians and procedural changes such as amendments to the Temporary Order on Freedom of Movement.
