Project Summary

The overall purpose of the project is to improve the protection environment and to improve the access to quality of protection services for displaced, conflict affected persons as well as returnees. The delivery priority will be to populations located along the 'contact line' and in non-Government controlled areas of Ukraine.

Needs

During the 2017 participatory assessment, internally displaced persons in eastern Ukraine mentioned that they face challenges related to accommodation, access to employment, high cost of living, freedom of movement, registration/verification, and access to services in particular health-care delivery. UNHCR participatory assessment findings match those undertaken by other partners including, for example, the World Bank’s 2017 Assessment of the Social-Economic Impacts of Internal Displacement in Ukraine.Protection risks vary greatly by location. Along the contact line, new emergency humanitarian needs are a priority. There are growing humanitarian needs in non-government controlled areas (NGCA). Basic humanitarian needs must be met, in particular for older persons at risk, for whom basic food and medicine are a challenge. The fact that the Ukrainian authorities have chosen to suspend the payment of earned pension benefits and other social entitlements for thousands of persons living in NGCA has created great strain, particularly on the elderly. At locations along the line of contact, continual shelling of civilian locations has led to both displacement and hardship for persons remaining behind. The psychological trauma they experienced became evident during the 2017 participatory assessment, expressed by people of all ages, with several breaking down when discussing the protection risks they face. During focus group discussions, persons living along the line of contact also reported protection problems relating to the military presence in civilian areas. They noted problems with harassment towards civilians, as well as military occupation of civilian housing. Incidents of domestic abuse are noted in reports received by partner monitors, and UNHCR’s response is being provided in cooperation with UNFPA.Lack of government control over areas of Ukraine has also created numerous legal challenges for persons living in these areas. They face difficulties in obtaining identity and civil documentation, including birth certificates, as well as accessing pensions and social benefits. IDPs living away from the line of contact have also faced discrimination, including towards persons of Roma ethnicity, and by host community members, some of whom blame the current conflict in Ukraine on persons from the Donbass region. Survey data suggests that some 10% of IDPs experience discrimination, mainly in the areas of housing and employment. Employment is another priority issue for IDPs. Only 46% of IDPs are employed with unemployment disproportionately affecting women; however, employment rates and income levels have risen gradually over time. Further, caustic statements made towards IDPs, such as those linking IDPs with benefit fraud, have also created a negative social sentiment. During focus group discussions, internally displaced persons additionally stated a confusion regarding local procedures, and cited gaps in their ability to access information. Also of concern, particularly to men and women of working age, was the need for compensation for lost or damaged housing, for which the authorities have not yet provided a mechanism.

Activities & outcomes

1.Objective: Services for persons with specific needs strengthened; Outcomes: cash grants or in-kind assistance provided; support to persons with specific needs provided.UNHCR will target assistance to persons with specific needs, using a cash modality where appropriate. UNHCR will also work with communities to further develop referral mechanisms.2.Objective: Peaceful co-existence with local communities promoted; Outcomes: Projects benefiting host and displaced communities implementedUNHCR will support small-scale local infrastructure projects designed to meet the needs of IDP and hosting communities, as well as to promote social cohesion. UNHCR will work with affected communities and local authorities to identify, implement and contribute to projects.3.Community mobilization strengthened and expanded; Outcomes: Community groups supportedUNHCR will create community support initiatives (CSI) that utilize and build on the experience, skills, and capacities of affected populations to protect themselves and promote the inclusion of IDPs in host communities. UNHCR has feedback/complaint mechanisms, but will strengthen them and ensure a proper AAP system is in place.4.Objective: Reintegration made more sustainable; Outcomes: Land, housing, property and documentation rights of persons of concern are upheld; Outcomes: Partnerships with development actors establishedUNHCR is planning to conduct a joint Area Based Initiative to tackle the priorities for IDPs and host communities. Within this initiative, other UN Agencies will join strengthening health access and provide partial economic recovery based agricultural business and employment in two areas along the line of conflict as pilot projects. UNHCR will contribute its protection.5.Objective: Comprehensive Solutions strategy developed, strengthened or updated; Outcomes: advocacy conductedUNHCR’s strategic objective is to advocate for legal and policy measures that facilitate integration of IDPs and establish conditions for voluntary return. UNHCR intends to conduct advocacy through training, exchange of experience with other countries, and expert policy advice. 6.Objective: Access to legal assistance and legal remedies improved; Outcomes: Capacity development supported for the FLAC; Outcomes: Legal assistance providedUNHCR will support the state-run Free Legal Aid Centers (FLAC) to develop their capacity in order to reduce legal assistance provided by NGOs with a gradual handover of IDP-related cases to FLACs in 2019.7.Objective: Law and policy developed or strengthened; Outcomes: Advocacy conductedUNHCR will analyze legislative and policy developments and implement advocacy actions (e.g., participation in legislative working groups and parliamentary committees, high level meetings, roundtables, public information). 8.Objective: Administrative institutions and practice developed or strengthened; Outcomes: Capacity development supportedAs a result of complexity in accessing administrative services, there is a high demand for legal services. UNHCR will support individuals in overcoming these barriers, but also works with the authorities in terms of improving the accessibility of administrative services, especially MinTOT.9.Objective: Coordination and partnerships strengthened; Outcomes: Co-ordination mechanisms establishedUNHCR will support the coordination of the Protection Cluster. UNHCR will also engage international development actors and financial institutions. 10.Objective: Protection from effects of armed conflict strengthened; Outcomes: Protection by presence provided; Outcomes: Situation of persons of concern monitoredUNHCR and partners will continue protection monitoring across Ukraine with emphasis on the areas along the line of contact, as well as areas hosting large numbers of IDPs. Protection Monitoring will be conducted at community level through regular interaction with local institutions, community groups and affected populations.
