Needs

Somalia continues to face huge internal displacement crisis. According to a recent UNHCR bulletin, there was an estimated 2.6 million Internally Displaced Persons in Somalia by end of August 2018. The number continues to grow due to conflict, forced evictions and natural disasters. In August 2018 alone, up to 53,000 people were newly displaced, over half of whom came to Banadir from Lower Shabelle. The displaced are often forced to leave their belongings behind and begin to live in settlements with no adequate access to basic necessities. Data from the JMCNA that was collected from 54 districts in Somalia in 2018 reveal that 80% of the IDPs live in makeshift shelters, temporary emergency shelters, tents, or under trees while as many as 70% reported that the shelters they live in have no internal separation. 80% also reported to be lacking access to NFIs. Out of the 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia, 2.3 million can be concluded to be in need of humanitarian shelter assistance.Lack of adequate shelter conditions leave IDPs exposed to inclement weather conditions, health hazards and gender-based violence (GBV). The latter is particularly happening at alarming rate whereby 76% of recorded GBV cases countrywide have happened in IDP settlements (Protection Cluster report). AVORD field assessments reveal similar trends that link poor shelter with increase in GBV.Besides IDPs, large numbers of refugee returnees and vulnerable host communities continue to live in deplorable shelter conditions further complicating humanitarian conditions on the ground. Prospects for the reintegration of 121,108 refugee returnees is negatively impacted by the chronic lack of sustainable shelters that could have enabled the returnees to settle and focus on establishing livelihoods. With the risk of continued violence, natural shocks and acute food insecurity, IDP influx to urban centers is expected to continue in 2019 which warrants utmost preparedness to mitigate against widespread suffering.

Activities

Following need based and transparent selection of beneficiaries, AVORD will provide emergency and sustainable shelters, Emergency Assistance Packages and Non-Food Items to 8,000 most vulnerable households from the IDPs, refugee returnees and host communities in specific regions of Somalia where these groups are concentrated. 21,000 newly displaced and protracted IDP households will receive appropriate core relief items suitable to cover the specific needs of women, girls, men and boys while 9,000 evictees and newly displaced households will be supported with emergency shelters and assistance packages. Additional 1,000 refugee returnees and protracted IDPs will receive sustainable shelters with appropriate land tenure security. The design of activities will always be informed by context-specific need and protection analysis and will be completed in close coordination with target communities to ensure their priority needs are met. Where market conditions allow, AVORD will employ a cash-based modality for implementation. AVORD will also adapt a systematic approach to beneficiary selection with a clear criterion to prioritize the most vulnerable in target communities including unaccompanied children, older Persons, persons with disability, single women, pregnant and lactating mothers, single headed-households, persons with chronic illness or psychological condition, large families and/or families with babies, toddlers or children attending school and persons at risk as identified by protection officers. Data collected to inform beneficiary selection will be desegregated by gender to ensure that no group is left-out. The effectiveness of the aid package will always be evaluated through Post Distribution Monitoring exercises, field visits, and other evaluation methods thus ensuring that beneficiaries access their services safely and in a dignified manner. Elaborate feedback and complain mechanisms will be setup to ensure accountability to the effected population.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This proposal is humanitarian in nature focusing on the provision of life-saving shelter assistance to people in humanitarian crisis. However, it also contributes to building the resilience of targeted communities in a number of ways. On the one hand, provision of shelter and NFIs in an environment of humanitarian crisis protects beneficiaries from harsh climatic conditions, health hazards and suffering which leads to improvement in their living conditions and chances to access livelihoods. Complementary multi-sectoral interventions such as food aid, WASH and protection are often more effective when combined with shelter, leaving IDPs more resilient to future shocks and able to cope with the grim reality of displacement. On the other hand, the project involves construction of permanent shelters for protracted IDPs and refugee returnees thus providing durable solutions to the protracted IDP crisis in Somalia and promoting reintegration. The process will take into account land tenure arrangements to avoid eviction or disproportional rising in rent and will be facilitated in close coordination with local authorities and other key stakeholders such as land owners. To maximize impact and sustain project outcome, AVORD will seek integration with other clusters providing response in areas where proposed activities will be implemented. Details pertaining to project activities will be shared through relevant clusters and 4W reporting matrix to avoid duplication of effort. Local communities will play a key role in different phases of the project to create sense of ownership which will contribute to sustaining of project outcomes. AVORD will strive to build local capacities thus ensuring that various community leaders and committees are able to sustainably maintain resources following project completion and handover.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

1) Risk: Gatekeepers may try to hamper the equal access of IDPs to the assistance provided by the project.Likelihood: High - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• AVORD will undertake direct identification and registration of beneficiaries without use of gate keepers.• Develop an elaborate criterion to ensure that only the most vulnerable IDPs are registered for assistance.2) Risk: IDPs from marginalized communities excluded from receiving the aid package by IDPs from major clans.Likelihood: High - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• Collect and use data dis-aggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming • Work with local elders to prioritize selection of marginalized IDPs• Apply systematic beneficiary identification and collect adequate household profile before the intervention.3) Risk: Forced eviction.Likelihood: High - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• Establish emergency and permanent shelters only in areas where land tenure is secured for a minimum of 10 years and obtain relevant legal documents e.g. land titles.• Work closely with local authorities to address the impact of eviction on IDPs• Identify which group could be more prone to eviction during need assessments and design accordingly.4) Risk: IDP and host community tensions.Likelihood: Medium - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• Register host community households as beneficiaries• Establish project steering committees involving members from both IDPs and host communities.• Avoid use of land in the construction of shelters without obtaining prior endorsement from respective owners • Consult both displaced and host communities about shelter and NFI needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension or conflict between the two communities.• Be transparent with the target communities by providing them with timely information on the selection criteria, organisational procedures and processes that affect them.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

1) Risk: Exclusion of women and girls from beneficiary selection processes.Likelihood: High - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• Develop selection criterion to ensure that women and girl are registered for assistance.• Collect and use data dis-aggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform beneficiary identification and selection processes. • Consider the gender balance of distribution teams and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community.• Establish mechanisms to give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women.2) Risk: Women and girls could be exposed to gender-based violence trying to access assistance.Likelihood: High - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• Ensure distribution sites and access routes are safe with enough space for orderly and safe distribution.• Separate male and female queuing areas and ensure female staff presence at distributions so that they are accessible to women and girls. • Secure distribution sites and prohibit entry of armed men• Ensure that distributions take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. 3) Risk: The unique needs of women, men, boys and girls are not addressed by the project.Likelihood: High - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• Ensure that the content of aid packages is gender-sensitized by including gender-friendly items such as female sanitary pads. 4) Risk: People with special needs such as the disabled face difficulties in accessing project services.Likelihood: High - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• Introduce special arrangements for persons who have difficulty accessing the distribution point or unable to carry the kits.• Ensure the ration size is appropriate for beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risk: Armed conflicts may hinder access.Likelihood: High - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• Pre-position relief items in strategic locations where feasible to respond to humanitarian emergencies in the event of restricted access• Continuously asses local dynamics and plan accordingly. Risk: Target communities exposed to risk trying to access assistance in situations of armed conflicts.Likelihood: High - Impact: High.Mitigation measures:• Undertake contextual analysis before program implementations to assess the likelihood of project activities leading to unintended negative consequences and plan accordingly. • Ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly and safe distribution.
