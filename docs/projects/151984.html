Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to increase the capacity of vulnerable conflict affected populations residing in areas within 5km of the contact line of Donetsk and Luhansk GCA to cover the most burdensome critical household expenses, through a flexible and versatile multipurpose cash scheme that sustains protection exigencies and preferences of beneficiaries while offering an appropriate value for money solution. The settlement will be selected based but not limited to the following criteria: proximity to the line of contact, isolation as a result of ongoing conflict, access to services and commodities, recent developments in the conflict area.
In line with the Cash Working Group (CWG) guidelines, over a period of six months each individual will receive the equivalent of a total of USD 215. The transfer will be carried out over two rounds and the total family amount will be calculated based on the total number of household members. The cash transfer amount might however be revised in coordination with the CWG, taking into account the inflation rate and market price fluctuation, which might affect the overall number of targeted beneficiaries.
All multipurpose cash beneficiaries will undergo a preliminary standard verification through phone interviews, followed by the cross-checking with other cash partners to avoid duplications. IOM will directly monitor the disbursement process and the documents gathering process. A final PDM will be conducted after the second round of transfers in order to assess the achieved results against project indicators and the action’s impact. A separate block of questions within PDM will be devoted to assessing coping mechanisms to which the beneficiaries resort as the result of insufficient household income.

Needs

According to the results of the post distribution monitoring (PDM) of multipurpose cash assistance in Donetsk and Luhansk (GCA) carried out in June 2018, the average income per person in the targeted sample is lower than the minimum subsistence level in Ukraine set at 1,700 UAH per person per month for April to June 2018. Out of the total sample of beneficiaries interviewed, only 6% stated that their income is sufficient to cover their basic household needs, while the rest (94%) stated that for covering basic household needs they lack on average 1,383 UAH per person per month. As a result of insufficient household income, most beneficiaries resorted to unhealthy coping mechanisms, the most common of which are choosing cheaper food and avoiding going to the doctor or buying medicines, even if very much needed;Eighty seven (87%) per cent of households rely on governmental social support, pensions and benefits. Employment is among the most pressing issues for the beneficiaries – fifteen (15%) percent of households have one, two or three unemployed household members that are looking for a job;According to Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) report on multi-purpose cash assistance, conducted by IOM in June 2018, 100% of the cash assistance provided to vulnerable conflict affected populations was employed to cover the most critical household needs. The majority of the money received within provided support was spent on healthcare, savings in advance of winter, winterization needs and child care;According to Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) report on multi-purpose cash assistance, conducted by IOM in June 2018, 98% percent of respondents expressed satisfaction with the modality of assistance provided with only two percent preferring to receive assistance in-kind. With markets operating in the areas of project implementation, multipurpose cash assistance allows vulnerable individuals the flexibility to choose which goods or services they would like to purchase based on their own time-specific needs and household priorities.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome: Vulnerable conflict-affected persons residing within 5km along the contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA) are able to access essential commodities and services Output 1: Vulnerable households benefit from cash transfers to cover their essential and urgent needs Activities to output 1: 1) Establish cash assistance hotline operations to conduct verification, identification and PDM (12 months); 2) Select, verify and notify multipurpose cash beneficiaries (3 months);3) Carry out cash distribution for target beneficiaries (6 months);4) Conduct PDM of the cash transfers to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of assistance provided (1 month). The project will ensure that both men and women benefit from the project through their representation in the target groups as elderly, disabled and families with 3+ children. The project aims to target not only female single headed households, but also male single headed households where possible. During the beneficiary selection phase of the project, IOM will collect age and sex including specific needs as single parenthood, numerous families, physical or psychological needs of the population of concern. The project will prioritize groups such as elderly, people with disabilities, single headed households and households with many children adequately even for priority of distribution or priority of eligibility if entire needs could not be satisfied.

Indicators & targets

Outcome: Vulnerable conflict-affected persons residing within 5km along the contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA) are able to access essential commodities and services: 1) % of beneficiaries that reported that the transferred cash increased their access to essential commodities (Target: 75%);2) % of beneficiaries with reduced use of existing negative coping mechanisms due to the assistance provision (Target: 50%); 3) % beneficiaries satisfied with the procedure and the modality of the cash transfers (Target: 90%). Output 1: Vulnerable households benefit from cash transfers to cover their essential and urgent needs 1) # of disbursement rounds in target areas throughout the project implementation period (Target: 2 rounds);2) # of households benefiting from multipurpose cash assistance (Target: 2,500 households (5,250 beneficiaries, including 3,150 female and 2,100 male; as well as out of them 525 children, 1,575 adults and 3,150 elderly). In line with the current recommendations that were discussed and endorsed by the partners during the meeting held on the 11th of October 2018, MPC transfer value that will be used under the current project activities is UAH 970 per person per month. The grant amount will be calculated individually based on the household size multiplied by the amount of assistance (based on CWG recommendations) and number of months. The cash transfer amount might however be revised in coordination with the CWG, taking into account the inflation rate and market price fluctuation, which might affect the overall number of targeted beneficiaries. The beneficiary households will be selected according to the following vulnerability criteria: presence in the household of persons with disabilities (1st and 2nd category) or chronic diseases, disabled children, elderly, single parents and families with three or more children. All project activities will be monitored and evaluated throughout the project’s duration. In addition to the regular monitoring of project actions, targeted Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be carried out by IOM staff after cash assistance disbursement through administering at least 10% phone surveys and 5% individual household visits to ensure the collection of sex and age disaggregated quantitative and qualitative data. This will enable IOM to extrapolate information on level of satisfaction and on how beneficiaries – particularly women, girls and other vulnerable categories – are impacted by project activities.
