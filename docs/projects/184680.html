Project Summary

According to HNO 2022, Conflict, drought, COVID-19, and flooding are some of the main shocks that drive displacement and largely affect at least 2.9 million internally displaced persons in Somalia. As per the CCCM Cluster and partners, the IDPs have settled in 2,400 sub-standard and unplanned IDP sites in urban, semi-urban and rural areas across the country. The IDPs are in need of multi-sectoral support services including protection from harsh climatic conditions, improvements to their living conditions and improvement to access livelihoods and durable solutions.
Somalia continues to experience effects of protracted crisis, with displacement of population due to recurrent conflict, drought, COVID-19 pandemic and flooding, leading to concurrent shocks across the country stretching the already limited resources available and exacerbate sectoral needs in the IDP settlements. The IDPs in the settlements face challenges ranging from poor living conditions, overcrowding, inadequate settlement design, disproportionate access to humanitarian aid, inaccessible sites, difficulty in leaving the sites at night especially for women and girls, and the shocks from COVID-19 effects and new displacements. Vulnerable groups namely women, elderly, refugee returnees, minority groups, youth and people living with disability (PLWD) are the most acutely affected, with PLWD facing barriers in access to services in the settlements. The youth face inclusion challenges, with boys and girls facing disenfranchisement in accessing essential services, including inadequate ground for playing and child-friendly spaces in the settlements, further creating deteriorating and worsening situation for IDPs, with their protection compromised and human rights violated. Poor coordination of the camps by the village relief committees or the camp leaders with inadequate capacity to manage the population in the camp, affecting quality of service delivery. The returns of IDPs to their home areas of origin are extremely limited due to lost livelihoods and reliant on humanitarian services.
Through this project, HIWA aims to address immediate needs and improve the living conditions of the newly displaced and the protracted IDPs living in a deplorable conditions in Beledweyne, Bula Burte, Kahda and Deynile districts of Hiraan and Banaadir Regions by strengthening protection referral pathways, support and participate coordination mechanism that advocate for provision of essential social services to IDPs, and contribute to community-driven site maintenance activities including infrastructure rehabilitation and safe spaces, build existing governance structures on CCCM principles and participate safety audits assessments in the target sites while working towards transitioning to durable solutions.
HIWA will train 40 camp management committees and Key local authorities representatives on HLP (Housing, Land and Property) to improve their knowledge and understanding of HLP issues as well flood and fire prevention techniques. HIWA will also train its CCCM staff on site planning and layout to ensure that all site maintenance projects implemented in the target district meet the required standards. Assistance will be provided in accordance with minimum humanitarian standards of service delivery and protection while ensuring every member of the IDP community participate in the activities directly or through inclusive representation including persons with disability. HIWA will conduct regular monitoring to track population changes and communal facility inspections to ensure that the camp is properly maintained and/ or upgraded to meet Sphere standards and the changing needs of the IDPs. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to content with GBV and protection concerns within camp settings. Referrals will be established with GBV AOR.HIWA will establishe responsive and reliable CFM.

1. - Projects activities

The following activities will be undertaken, in line with the objectives of the Somalia CCCM Cluster Strategy:Activity 1.1: set up in the targeted settlement mobile site management teams for identification, selection, implementation, coordination and for monitoring purposes to ensure that there are no gaps. Activity 1.2: Improve community participation, living conditions and safe access to services and assistance in selected sites. Activity 1.3: Support formation of Camp Self-Management Committees (CMCs) in the targeted IDP camps. Provide training on CCCM principles to community governance structures. Establishment and training on CCCM principles and community engagement for site committees and community governance structures.Activity 1.4: Conduct quarterly site verification-assessments including population numbers with gender and age break down. Activity 1.5: Facilitation and support to community meetings, in particular for women groups. Conduct advocacy and mobilization for the active participation of women in IDP leadership. Activity 1.6: Maintaining coordination through regular meetings, updates and communication flows with different partners working in IDP Site. Provide technical assistance and guidance in establishing or reinforcing existing community representative/committee structures as necessary (including women’s representation) Support and help facilitate IDP governance meetings. Activity 1.7: Conduct safety audits on regular basis in coordination with relevant partners. Activity 1.8: Conducting site planning, verification and maintenance in collaboration with the CCCM committees. Activity 1.9: Support the protection and restoration of livelihoods to strengthen resilience of communities HIWA Organization will support the Cluster with Detailed site assessments. Support IDP communities to access land rights. Produce regular report (per site per month) on site management and conditions are provided and disseminated to relevant stakeholders
