Needs

Displacement in Somalia is complex due to its causes that include incidents of violence and disaster. The impact of the global climate change on Somalia is real. Somalia experienced successive droughts beyond normal trends. The new phenomenon caught communities off guard and it was worsened by insecurity that is affecting the region. The long civil war damaged community preparedness and resilience to withstand drought effects. The impact of 2011 devastating droughts/famine is evident in the displaced people who overwhelmed Mogadishu and other Somali cities. The memory of the lives and livelihoods lost is still vivid. It is difficult to tell the actual number of IDPs in Somalia but there is no doubt that it increased due to the persistent factors that cause it. However, according to OCHA, Internal displacement in the country stands at 2.6 million.The lives of the displaced population have changed substantially as they were often displaced multiple times. The living conditions in IDP settlements could only be described not just substandard but inhumane. They live in a state of homelessness, poverty and destitution and have gone through multiple evictions or are threatened by it. Not to mention health hazards for lack of sanitation and basic health services. IDPs are subjected to all kinds of Human Rights violations, hence, protection is on top of their priorities especially women and children. What makes matters worse is a complicated and horrendous gatekeeper system that are hard to comprehend let alone to resolve. Mogadishu and other Somali cities are not able to accommodate the increasing volume of IDPs, therefore, their living conditions will not probably improve. These conditions need to be resolved and the only feasible solution is returning people to the lifestyle they are accustomed to in the rural areas where they originally lived or anywhere else they choose that will allow them to lead to a meaningful productive live.

Activities

1.1 Camp Management: community mobilization and awareness raising to help establish informed decision making target: 750 IDPs headed households1.2 Peace Building meetings at return areas for 750 IDPs Household representatives.1.3 Distribution of conditional/unconditional cash assistance: Unconditional cash will support returnees’ livelihood for 6 months. 1.4 cash for work will be used to rehabilitate identified 5 market places, 8 health centers. Required tools and equipments will be provided. Fairness and equity among returnees and host community will be observed to prevent hostility.1.5 Construction/ rehabilitation of productive 5 shallow wells, 2 water catchments constructed and 3 country roads to be rehabilitated in Lower and Middle Shabelle.1.6 Shelter and NFI Support: 750 local traditional shelter will be offered to returnee households.1.7 Community Economic Development: 60 women and 30 youth receive finances through micro financing and revolving loans for small projects including grain mills, sesame oil mills, shops.1.8 construction of seven schools in Jannaale, Marka, Afgoye, Jowhar, Qoryooley, Furuqleey and Mahaday in Lower and Middle Shabelle regions and fully equipped with operational cost for one year including staff salaries and cost of teaching materials. The Schools will be connected with piped water. The project will cover costs of school operation and running expenses including teachers salaries for one year. Target: 665 children of boys and girls.1.9 Distribution of agriculture inputs to 500 returnees and host community farmers, fertilizers, seeds, tools etc and land preparation of 1 hecter for cultivation

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

To avoid exclusion of certain people from benefiting the project, strong cooperation with community elders and religious leaders and village councils and local authorities and with beneficiary committee, in place that detects any deviation in the process. In addition, OIC maintains lists of target beneficiaries verified externally to make sure that people are selected fairly.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The risk being faced by beneficiaries based on gender and age might happen at 2 points; one at the registration of beneficiaries and while distribution of food. People might tempt to rob food being distributed to elder women and a lonely women or girls. To prevent from occurring that we cooperate with security institutions to tighten security of the area and prevent robber to approach from the site.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

To mitigate the impact of armed conflict and violence:1. Cooperation with security institutions to guarding the site.2. Setup a committee from beneficiaries, elders and religious leaders to endure secutiy of the site using local means and tactics where there is no security institutions.3. Distribution of food and nutritious dates through commercial stores and big shops whereby grouped beneficiaries do get food with vouchers.
