Project Summary

Fifteen years after independence, the South Sudan’s population and the economy continue to struggle, witnessing another deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Reports document that the situation has been worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges. The humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that 7.1 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023, an overwhelming 76 per cent of South Sudan's population, increased by 500,000 people from 2022. At least 148,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. The inter-sectoral analysis conducted using cluster people in need (PiN) revealed that there are needs in every county of the country. Of the 7.1 million people in need, an estimated 56,325 people will experience catastrophic need in Panyikang county. Moreover, there will be 7.6 million people experiencing extreme need in 66 out of 78 counties and 1.7 million people in severe need in 10 out of 78 counties. Upper Nile and Western Equatoria States will contain the highest number of counties in critical need. This situation has further been exacerbated by the conflict in Sudan that has displaced thousands to the neighboring countries. As of 25th/10/2023, the UNHCR/IOM Dashboard for arrivals from Sudan indicated that 335,078 have been recorded to have crossed borders from Sudan into South Sudan at the various points of entry (PoE). 77,539 Households (289,447 Individuals), 86.38% being South Sudanese. Of all the total new arrivals from Sudan, almost half of them, 162,335(48%) intend to stay in Upper Nile State. A recent WFP assessment indicates that 90 per cent of returnee families are experiencing moderate to severe food insecurity. Screening data from the points of entry (PoEs) revealed that almost 20 per cent of children under age 5 and more than a quarter of pregnant and lactating women are malnourished. The updated Emergency Response Plan for the Sudan Crisis anticipates a total of 600,000 individuals to cross to South Sudan from Sudan by December, 2023. As more returnees arrive in South Sudan en route to their final destinations, the Humanitarian body, UNOCHA advise partners to scale up their response under the Humanitarian Response Plan to support integration in their areas of return. Also, Upper Nile has continued to experience intercommunal violence and climatic shocks, flooding being the main disaster causing displacements and loss to livelihoods. Panyikang, Fashoda and Manyo have all been affected by the December, 2023 intercommunal violence that caused significant loss of lives and looting of livelihood assets such as goats and cattle. IPC acute food insecurity and malnutrition analysis for South Sudan has shown deteriorating situation. In the peak of the lean season (April - July 2023), the number of people projected to face high levels of acute food insecurity have continued to deteriorate from 7.24 million (60% of the population) in 2021 (April - July 2021) to 7.7 million for the same period in 2022 (April - July 2022) and 7.8 million projected for the lean season in 2023. In the Greater Upper Nile region, the food security situation remains critical across all the analysis periods, with a similar number of people facing acute food insecurity as in the 2021-2022 period. In the second projection period of April to July 2023, the food security situation was projected to deteriorate, and the number of people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity is anticipated rise to 3.4 million (73.4% of the population in Greater Upper Nile region). The peoples living standards have continued to decline. August 2023 Joint Market and Monitoring Initiatives price compilations presented a continued rise in the Multi-Sectoral Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSSMEB). It has recorded a general increase of 5 percent from Jul – August, 2023 at a unit of 136,944 SSP, 138.33 USD.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face high humanitarian needs. The Humanitarian Needs overview for 2023 document that, 7.1 million people, including 5 million children are in need of humanitarian assistance, higher than the 8.9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. The inter-sectoral analysis conducted using cluster people in need (PiN) revealed that there are needs in every county of the country. Of the 7.1 million people in need, an estimated 56,325 people will experience catastrophic need in Panyikang county of Upper Nile State. Moreover, there will be 7.6 million people experiencing extreme need in 66 out of 78 counties and 1.7 million people in severe need in 10 out of 78 counties. Upper Nile and Western Equatoria States will contain the highest number of counties in critical need. The projected 9.4 million people in need for 2023 include 1.9 million IDPs, 1.4 million returnees, 5.8 million host community/non-displaced people and 337,000 refugees. Among them, there are 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children, including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys. Nearly 15 percent of the total people in need are People with Disabilities (PWD). Of all the sectors, the food security and livelihoods remain the highest with up to 8 million people who will be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023. Conflict, persistent and unprecedented flooding, disease outbreaks, and food insecurity continue to compound the complex, chronic needs of the most vulnerable, especially children. Increasing costs of fuel and staple foods - driven by the depreciation of the South Sudanese pound and global supply chain disruptions, continue to exacerbate vulnerabilities. Considerable internal and cross-border displacement has further strained scarce resources, livelihoods, and basic services and has increased protection risks and intercommunal tensions. The HNO for 2023 present that, Upper Nile Sate has a total of 1,241,252 people in need; 741,081 Host Community, 191,738 Internally displaced persons and 308,433 Returnees. These statistics will be higher added to the Sudan Conflict that started in April, 15th, 2023 which has led to thousands of new arrivals entering South Sudan. As of 25th/10/2023, the UNHCR/IOM Dashboard for arrivals from Sudan indicated that 335,078 have been recorded to have crossed borders from Sudan into South Sudan at the various points of entry (PoE). 77,539 Households (289,447 Individuals), 86.38% being South Sudanese. Of all the total new arrivals from Sudan, almost half of them, 162,335(48%) intend to stay in Upper Nile State. A recent WFP assessment indicates that 90 per cent of the returnee families are experiencing moderate to severe food insecurity. The Humanitarian team coordination team body, the UNOCHA, in its updates for the response of South Sudan to Sudan crises advised for a scaled-up response at the intended areas of stay for the returnees to include the new arrivals from the Sudan Crises in the programming. IPC acute food insecurity and malnutrition analysis for South Sudan has shown deteriorating situation. In the peak of the lean season (April - July 2023), the number of people projected to face high levels of acute food insecurity have continued to deteriorate from 7.24 million (60% of the population) in 2021 (April - July 2021) to 7.7 million for the same period in 2022 (April - July 2022) and 7.8 million projected for the lean season in 2023. 2. Project activities

2.1. Conduct six (9) Consultation meetings with the stakeholders for project implementation in the three Counties as well as participate in national, state and local coordination meetings; 2.2. Selection and formation of 9 Project Management Committees (PMC), 3 in each County, composed of 60 members; 2.3. Orientation and Training of 60 PMC members on Accountability to the Affected Population and other pertinent aspects like sensitivity to gender, disability, people with special needs, children U5, HIV & chronic sick, the aged... 2.4. Beneficiaries identification, selection and registration for livelihood inputs; 2.5. Establish/Re-establish of 15 kitchen Gardens sites at Nutrition Facility 2.6. Distribution of tools, crop and quick-maturing vegetable kits to 23,377 farmers Panyikang, Fashoda and Manyo Counties;2.7. Distribution of fishing kits to 18,000 fisher-folks in Panyikang, Fashoda, Manyo and Panyikang Counties; 2.8. Conduct Post-distribution monitoring and document as necessary; 2.9. Training of 550 farmers and fisherfolks on nutrition, hygiene and primary health care, 2.10. Run a conditional cash-based transfer scheme targeting 22,376 beneficiaries in the 3 Counties and premised on investments in assets, training, communal work groups...;2.11. Identification and selection of 80 traders to be supported to re-supply markets;2.12. Support and stimulate 9 local markets functionality through small grants to petty traders to aid in re-supplying markets and facilitate the formation;2.13. Train 620 fisher-folks, including from the returnee community, on improved fishing methods as well as preservation and marketing of fish;2.14. Training and capacity building of 540 Good Agronomic practices (farmer field schools, Demo plots, post-harvest management, income generation especially business skills...);2.15. Training of 750 ToTs (380 men & 370 women) on post harvesting, improved agronomic practices, vegetable production and development of nursery sites for vegetable production;2.16. Mobilize targeted IDPs & hosting communities for preparation & clearance of 4,400 Feddans of land in time for gardening season in the designated counties;2.17. Promote sustainable management of natural resources, in particular promote use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves and conduct training in natural resource management among 9 local groups (including women and youth groups) in the 3 Counties;2.18. Advocacy, project monitoring in consultation with stakeholders to ensure sustainability beyond the project life. The beneficiaries through their representatives (for example, IDPs representatives, disabled representatives....) shall also be central to these activities;2.19. Training of 30 community-based extension service providers (CBESP)2.20. Provision of water pumps, Irrigation training/support to 470 farmers in the three counties2.21. Training 500 farmers on making PHL Storage granaries (using locally available resources as much as possible)2.22. Provision of Canoe, Fishing, Boating materials/tools to 300 fishermen2.23. Training and formation of 15 Village Savings/Loans Associations groups (each group composed of 25 – 35 members)2.24. Procurement and distribution of processing Equipment for value addition (fish, meat, vegetables, fruit, milk preservation)2.25. Training of 780 farmers and fishermen on value addition (fish, meat, vegetables, fruit, milk preservation, honey)2.26. Provision of Vocational & other enterprise/business related support2.27. Install or strengthen community based EWS by providing require equipment (rain gauge, disaster prediction information tools, etc.)2.28. Support downscaling of climate information-flood early warning information through radio programmes (Radio show, solar radios etc.)2.29. Provide flood adaptive seeds and approaches (rice, floating gardens), tools and fishing inputs to farmers.2.30. Conduct assessment and review existing early action protocol (EAP)
