Project Summary

This project will deliver a strategic set of interventions under the SNFI sector at 10 communities located in WoS’s high severity communities. These communities characterize severity scale of 4 and the highest number of PIN according to the WoS’ list
The project will benefit these high priority areas by strengthening and enhancing the dignified living conditions in both in/ and off-camp locations. TAS will consider Do No harm principles, Cluster guidelines, matching with the context and ensuring the adhering to HLP rights to prevent any harm.
As the beginning of the project coordination communications with the SNFI cluster will be done in order to ensure that no overlapping occurs.
TAS will seek to achieve the following deliverables:
1. Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support. This will be implemented in in-formal settlements regions, where comprehensive intervention will be undertaken to upgrade the living conditions and providing dignified shelters for the IDPs instead of tents. This will include, but not limited to,
• Provision of core NFIs
• Installation and/ or construction of temporary transitional shelters
• Implementation of site level improvements/ infrastructure (Infrastructure in IDP sites) including HH-tents leveling, drainage systems, graveling , ..etc
The informal settlements located in the following communities will be benefiting from this outcome: Qah/ Dana (Dana)/ Sarmada which has valid HLP documentations and the shelter type is tents. The targeted sites having 17500 IDPs and 3500 HHs. 150 Transitional shelters will be fabricated locally and provided for the most vulnerable HHs in the camps.
2. Reinforce and enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure. This will be implemented in off-camp locations, where shelter rehabilitation and upgrading activity will be undertaken to provide more dignified shelters for both IDPs and HC. This service will be delivered in the communities targeting 80% IDPs living in camps and 20% the HC.
As per SHELTER SITUATION 2021 IDP REPORT SERIES that 89% of renting IDP HHs report facing some sort of difficulty when finding a place to rent, 27% of IDP households in NS and NWS live in tents, 12% of IDP households live in damaged shelters, 43% of IDP households have been unable to afford shelter repairs and 42% of IDP households reported cold damp conditions in their shelter as the primary issue, therefore the need for house repair activity is high in order to achieve two objectives:
- move the most vulnerable HHs affected by harsh weather from informal sites to repaired houses
- upgrading the living conditions of HHs living in un-finished houses
300 shelter units (med-damaged) will be repaired in Qah, Dana and Sarmada.
The total estimate number of BNFs is 19000, out of them there are 3820 men, 4045 women, 5130 boys and 6005 are girls.
Before starting the implementation phase the HLP documentations will be collected as well as in designing phase as per each camps utilizing the CCCM data about the existence and validity of HLP documentation in each targeted site.

4 - Needs

Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute. A significant portion of the population continues to experience protracted displacement. Especially in North-east and North-west Syria, resorting to living in unsustainable settlements with poor access to basic services. Continuous support remains crucial to maintain the dignity, health and safety of those displaced. Infrastructure upgrades, NFIs and winterization of existing shelters remain vital support [HNO, 2021].According to Whole of Syria report in Shelter and NFI sector 2022, almost all households surveyed live in finished undamaged or slightly damaged apartments or houses. Shelter PiN in 2022 is found to be 5,916,585 while NFI PiN 2022 is 4,909,468. TAS implemented Shelter Repairing NA in 2021 at the governorate of Aleppo and Idleb in the sub-district of Al-Bab, Ariha, Atareb, Azaz, Dana, Daret Azza, Idleb, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Maaret Tamsrin, and Qabasin. It was found that the most requested maintenance for unfinished houses were doors, windows, lighting network, and rehabilitation of bathrooms. As for the partially damaged houses, the most requested repaired were found to be doors, bathrooms, and windows.Dana; IDPs 916273 -Res 173432 - Total pop 1089705 – PwD 34.6% - (G 23.6%, B 29.4%, F 22.8%, 21.9%)Sarmada; IDPs 99736 -Res 21155 - Total pop 120891 – PwD 34.6% - (G 23.6%, B 29.9%, F 22.9%, 21.9%)Qah; IDPs 123669 -Res 6039 - Total pop 129708 – PwD 34.6% - (G 23.6%, B 30%, F 22.9%, 21.9%) [Pop 2021].

5 - Implementation of activities

The Activities will be implemented as follow:1- As for the in-camp interventions, an assessment will be conducted to the targeted camps in order to select the flood-prone, highest population density and the worst infrastructure status in order to be targeted with the proposed transitional shelter solution for 150 HHs. In addition to the shelter solution itself, the infrastructure will be improved as well including graveling works, HH-tent leveling works, road rehabilitation, establishing lighting poles and a net of drainage systems in order to apply a settlement-based approach and promote the living conditions in the selected site.As for the other IDP sites, typical shelter activities to improve the infrastructure will be undertaken including:- site leveling and graveling - road graveling for no less than 15 km of roads- tent ground insulation As for the shelter repair activity, 300 shelters located in Qah/Dana and Sarmada communities, will be repaired. Those shelters will be basically unfinished houses. The BNFs will be most vulnerable IDPs living in flood-prone areas and characterize bas living conditions during winter. HHs consisting of elderly and PwDs will be prioritized. A MoU will be signed with the house owner committing to give the house free for the IDP for one year long. The renting IDP will be provided with Core NFI Kit. The modality used in house repair will be through service provider. The house repair activity will target 80% IDPs and 20% HC.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk 1: The vulnerability of children and women in conflict circumstances highly increases and places them in higher risk of being exposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violations.Mitigation strategy: 1- Staff personnel will sign code of conduct and child protection policy.2- Most of the management and field staff had attended workshops to increase the awareness of PSEA cases.3- the compliance mechanism will be defined and shared with beneficiaries.Risk 2: There are not enough humanitarian staff (both male and female) at the distribution site monitoring the distribution process Mitigation strategy: 1- Ensure oversight of distributions by aid workers who are trained on PSEA, Codes of Conduct and how/where to report misconduct.2- Ensure field level PSEA Focal Points in distribution teams and/or that staff involved in distributions know how to report or refer any PSEA allegations received.3- Deploy gender balanced teams to support projects for disaster-affected communities.4- Consider wearing clearly marked organization logos on shirts/jackets/caps for ease of identity by beneficiaries.Risk 3 : when challenges and issues arose during distribution, they are not handled well, and there is no female employee if the beneficiary was a female1. Train field workers on PSEA2. Ensure female aid workers are present and involved in the resolution of the challenges3. Sensitize affected communities on PSEA and who/where to report
