Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to provide urgent protection assistance to an estimated 2 000 vulnerable conflict-affected civilians of Eastern Ukraine, on both sides of the contact line. In addition, the project aims at raising awareness of population in NGCA on Gender-based violence (GBV) through GBV awareness raining activities.
The overall objective of the proposed project is to mitigate the negative effects of the protracted conflict by reinforcing the protection system along the contact line for the benefit of the affected people.
The specific project objective of the project is to reduce vulnerability of conflict-affected population through enhanced local capacities, awareness raising and improved access to protection.
The defined project objectives will be achieved through the following expected outcomes:
Under Outcome 1 “Vulnerable affected population in GCA benefit from improved access to protection assistance and home-based care”, 110 community members will be trained on social work, protection mainstreaming and basic PSS concepts to provide home-based care and basic PSS to vulnerable 500 conflict-affected women and men above 60 and persons with disabilities not targeted by the government social protection scheme. The action will establish an informal network of community-based social workers with the aim of ensuring access to protection and basic community-based PSS to vulnerable individuals residing along the contact line. TGH will employ peer-to-peer methodology that proved to be effective in former TGH projects where vulnerable community members were engaged to support other community members with specific needs. The selected approach will also contribute to strengthening community cohesion and solidarity.
Under Outcome 2 “Protection concerns of at-risk women and girls, survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV) in Donetsk Oblast, NGCA” are addressed and risk factors reduced through provision of tailored protection assistance and awareness-raising: capacity of 20 medical staff will be built in GBV identification and prevention as well as in provision of psychological support to victims of GBV. GBV awareness activities will be conducted in cooperation with health care facilities with which Variant, TGH local partner based in Donetsk, has long-term standing partnership and cooperation. Focus group sessions with men practicing aggressive behavior including among ex-combatant staff and persons in detention facilities displaying high levels of aggressiveness will be conducted by Variant psychologists and social workers. Assistance will be provided by the qualified project team comprised of psychologists and social workers for GBV survivors and children affected by family-based violence.
Under Outcome 3: “Access to protection services of the most vulnerable conflict-affected women and men, boys and girls in Donetsk Oblast NGCA is ensured through targeted protection assistance including PSS and documentation support”, PSS support will be provided by social workers and/or psychologists depending on individual beneficiaries’ needs. Furthermore, support will be provided to beneficiaries in need of legal documentation to confirm their disability or vulnerability status or identity documents to ensure access to social benefits and services. Support will be provided in the form of individual consultations and accompaniment to relevant institutions as required.
Activities under Outcomes 2 and 3 will be implemented jointly with TGH partner, Variant, based in Donetsk, who has relevant registration and permission to operate in NGCA.
Outcome 4. Accountability to affected populations is enhanced.
AAP is an integral part of the project. Representatives of the affected population and beneficiaries will be included in all stages of the project cycle. A project Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) will be established as part of the overall TGH CRM and will have multiple feedback channels to ensure better access and inclusion.

1 - Needs

GCA: The conflict in Ukraine has a disproportionate impact on theelderly where 30% of the people in need of humanitarian assistance are above the age of60 (Ukraine HRP 2019-2020). Often neglected or separated from their families, the elderlyare highly prone to economic insecurity and face severe hardships when accessingessential services due to high rates of disability and immobility. The results of TGHvulnerability assessments and PDM demonstrate increased vulnerability of isolatedelderly individuals and persons with disabilities and high needs in protection services,notably in home-based care, PSS and basic needs. The conflict also has graveconsequences on social fragmentation, which worsens the situation of isolatedpopulation who cannot benefit from traditional solidarity mechanisms. The disruption ofthe traditional solidarity network mostly affects vulnerable and isolated individuals, suchas the elderly or people suffering from disabilities, and in strong lack of mobility. NGCA: The protracted crisis and linked with it posttraumatic stress disorders force many to resortto negative coping mechanisms. Use of psychoactive substances and alcohol hasdrastically increased in NGCA, predominantly by men leading to aggressive behaviours,gender-based and sexual violence. According to data from Variant, TGH local NGO partner,83% of men interviewed by the organization practice aggressive behaviour, of which 65%cases were caused by use of psychoactive substances and alcohol. 87% of femalerespondents and 42% of teenagers surveyed including boys and girls reported havingsuffered from GBV or family violence. GBV awareness is low and GBV cases are notproperly detected and addressed therefore enhancing capacity of service providers inidentification of GBV cases is required. Furthermore, the situation analysis andassessments conducted by Variant demonstrate increasing number of individuals withmultiple vulnerabilities such as mental health and PSS needs; demonstrated high level ofpsychological distress caused by psycho-traumatic events as a result of protractedconflict in Ukraine; long-term depression caused by lack of income and means to sustainthemselves and their families. Among the key protection concerns for the affectedpopulation in NGCA also remain challenges related to the receipt and restoration ofidentity and civil documents and access to social benefits.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1 “Vulnerable affected population in GCA benefit from improved access to protection assistance and home-based care”.Activities: 1. Identification of community-based volunteers. 2. Capacity building workshops for 110 community-based volunteers on general principles of social support, PSS, protection mainstreaming, individual case management, specific accompaniment of elderly and persons with disabilities; types of home-based services; referrals to specialised services, Do No Harm, and conflict sensitivity. 3. Identification of 500 project beneficiaries affected by the conflict, not included in the government social protection schemes and their needs assessment. 4. Provision of home-based care and PSS. 5. Monitoring and post-distribution monitoring activities. Outcome 2 “Protection concerns of at-risk women and girls, survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV) in Donetsk Oblast, NGCA”Activities: 1.1 Capacity building of medical/social protection staff in identification of GBV cases and provision of psychological support through series of training. 1.2. Development and distribution of GBV awareness information materials in 20 medical institutions and social protection departments. Referrals to multidisciplinary project support and/or relevant service providers. 1.3. Multidisciplinary care for GBV survivors and children affected by family-based violence provided by qualified project team comprised of medical specialists, psychologists, and social workers. 1.4. Individual and group PSS counselling. 1.5 Focus group sessions and individual consultations for men practicing aggressive behaviour. 4. PDM and project monitoring visits based on the Project Monitoring Plan. Outcome 3: “Access to protection services of the most vulnerable conflict-affected women and men, boys and girls in Donetsk Oblast NGCA is ensured through targeted protection assistance including PSS and documentation support”Activities: 1. Identification of beneficiaries. 2. Forming individual cases for each beneficiary and development of action plans. 3. PSS consultations by Variant social workers and/or psychologists depending on individual case management plans corresponding to the PSS and protection needs, psychological reactions of beneficiaries in accordance with the pyramid of mental health and psychosocial needs. 4. Weekly PSS individual and group sessions. 5. Individual consultations on documentation support and accompaniment to relevant institutions. 6. PDM and project monitoring visits based on the Project Monitoring Plan. Outcome 4: Outcome 4. Accountability to affected populations is enhanced. Activity 1: Project monitoring and learningActivity 2. Establishing a project CRM to ensure feedback from project beneficiaries and other stakeholders as part of the overall TGH CRM.
