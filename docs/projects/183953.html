Project Summary

Mercy Corps plans to deliver a multi-sectoral response for refugees in Sudan during 2022. The proposed project covers Gedaref and White Nile state where Mercy Corps is already delivering a range of interventions to assist refugees from Ethiopia and South Sudan. The proposed activities cover the following sectors - FSL, WASH, Protection, Health and Nutrition.

1 - Needs

Sudan has a long history of hosting refugees and asylum seekers with 991,787 individuals, 51 per cent female and 53 per cent children, expected to live in Sudan by the end of 2020. In 2021 it is estimated that the country will continue to host refugees from the Central African Republic (CAR), Chad, the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), Eritrea, Ethiopia, Somalia, South Sudan, Syria and Yemen who are looking for safety from violence, persecution and other hazards in their countries of origin.Sudan is host to one of the largest South Sudanese refugee populations in the region, with a total of 736,685 persons living in the country in December 2020. In East Sudan, a new influx of over 50,000 Ethiopian refugees began in November 2020. This new wave joins more than 130,000 Eritrean and Ethiopian refugees and asylum seekers living in protracted situations in camps and urban areas across Aj Jazirah, Gedaref, Kassala, Red Sea and Sennar states. Moreover, over 110,000 refugees of various nationalities (Ethiopian, Eritrean, Congolese, Somali, Yemeni and Syrian) are living in Khartoum together with over 190,000 South Sudanese refugees, all in dire need of assistance.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will provide vital humanitarian assistance to Ethiopia refugees in Gedaref state and South Sudanese Refugees in White Nile state. The proposed interventions will meet the needs of these groups in terms of WASH, FSL, Protection, ESNFI, Health and Nutrition.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program, Mercy Corps will abide by Do No Harm principles. MC will also strive for gender balance in the recruitment of staff at all levels and will train officers, responsible for delivering services and inputs to participants, on gender issues and gender-sensitive approaches. Mercy Corps’ in-house gender expertise will provide a solid foundation for the program. Recognizing the key role of women in agriculture and household nutrition, a minimum of 40 percent of participants will be women. Specifically under WASH interventions, in the target locations, water collection is typically the responsibility of women and girls. Limited access to water sources forces women and girls to walk long distances in search of water, often exposing them to additional protection risks. This intervention will help to reduce these risks through rehabilitating infrastructure which will increase the availability of water and reduce the distance women and girls are forced to walk to collect water. In addition, Mercy Corps will ensure that women and girls are represented in the community-level WASH committees – minimum 50 percent of WASH committee members. Mercy Corps plans to encourage the involvement of women and girls in all elements of the community-based operation and maintenance of the water sources. Ongoing discussions with some of the targeted communities have clearly shown that women are more interested than men in taking key roles in the implementation and management of the proposed user fee. As part of the user fee implementation strategy, Mercy Corps will form an advisory board with women taking key decision-making positions regarding rates, exemptions and managing collected money.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Mercy Corps places the issue of accountability at the core of this project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation processes. Ensuring relevant, quality and timely programming and interventions reflects this commitment. MC considers the target communities as leading the solutions for the humanitarian issues they face, and thus are regarded as key stakeholders in the design, implementation andmonitoring and evaluation of the project. MC will work through existing community structures comprising of a representative of women, youth and community leaders to represent communities during project implementation. Due to the limitation of gatherings as result of COVID-19, the information will mainly be shared through public media, posters, call centers, community microphone information broadcasts, and the use of a public address system in the target areas. Information dissemination will be adapted to the context of COVID-19 to ensure actions contribute to containment. Rather than gathering large groups, some activities will be done through a variety of approaches, including voice messaging, to cater to the vast majority who are illiterate. The project will use participatory, gender- and age-sensitive monitoring approaches to ensure girls, boys, women, and men, as well as CBOs and services providers, are involved centrally in the process of project follow-up, provide their feedback and concerns on the project performance and are informed about decisions made in community meetings and discussions. MC will provide timely, relevant, and precise information about the organization including feedback and complaints mechanisms.Communities and beneficiaries will be kept up to date in changes in feedback mechanisms and will be provided with information on how they can feedback remotely, such as via hotline, WhatsApp etc. Information sharing will be via different formats to ensure information is available promptly while taking into account language barriers and literacy status – such as flyers and posters left in communities, health facilities, hospitals etc. with simple and clear messages/standards and accountability assigned a number to all project locations. Mercy Corps’ functional Complaints and Response Mechanism will collect feedback where appropriate via FGDs, interviews, and a dedicated call-in number – any meetings held (where still possible) will be in open air locations, in spaces large enough for distancing; and otherwise can be conducted remotely. Complaints/feedback will be captured, categorized, and shared with relevant staff for resolution and logged into the action plan trackers. Depending on the type of feedback and complaint, some will immediately be closed through sharing the required information (different methods dependent on scale of response – face to face using distancing, telephone etc.). At the same time, for some, an investigation will be undertaken to verify the facts before a concrete decision is made.
