Project Summary

The proposed project seeks out to enhance the food security of highly vulnerable populations, aligning with SO1 Food Sector objectives for 2024, by providing essential food assistance. This project prioritizes support for female-headed households, individuals with disabilities, the elderly, and unemployed daily workers, addressing the critical issue in Syria, which ranks as the country with the sixth-highest level of food insecurity globally. According to the World Food Programme (WFP), an alarming 12.1 million Syrians, over half of the nation's populace, are currently facing severe hunger.
A key component of the project involves distributing food vouchers to 10,000 households identified as the most vulnerable in targeted areas (HNO 2023 severity scale 4), Maliha , Harasta , Nashabiyeh , Deir-ez-Zor, Astamo ,Hama, Jebel Saman . These households will receive unconditional, restricted vouchers, enabling them access to a diverse range of food items and essentials. The voucher value is calibrated to match the WFP's Minimum Expenditure Basket for eight cycles/months for the year, in accordance with the Food Security Sector's minimum standards and the Cash Working Group. This approach aims to lower the Coping Strategy Index for the targeted communities, while also bolstering local economies by increasing demand for food, enhancing community economic opportunities, and integrating local shops into the voucher program.
The selection process for food voucher beneficiaries will involve nominations from community committees, followed by verification through home visits by TDH volunteers. This step ensures the avoidance of duplications with other humanitarian efforts in the area and guarantees that recipients meet the pre-determined vulnerability criteria. Furthermore, the project is committed to enhancing localization among humanitarian actors by developing the capacities of project staff and volunteers in various technical areas, including disability inclusion, GBV, CP, data and needs assessment, market analysis, and PDM. Terre des Hommes Italy will maintain coordination with other FSA stakeholders to prevent service overlap and will actively participate in cash working groups, sharing 5Ws reports to optimize response coordination and effectiveness. Supervision by TdH staff, in collaboration with local partners, ensures ongoing partnership and effectiveness, with MEAL team conducting regular field assessments. Additionally, a MEAL officer will engage a random sample of beneficiaries to gather feedback through PDM processes. Coordination with the FSA Working Group and adherence to COVID-19 preventative measures will be ensured throughout the project, ultimately aiming to enhance community resilience and sustain food security. TDH will use E- Voucher System and the list of items available will be printed and distributed during the distribution and inside the local markets where possible, also TDH will include items recommended by Food Security Sector for meeting a minimum of 60% daily kcal intake.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In response to the critical food security challenges in Harasta, Maliha, Nashabia, and Deir ez-Zor—regions with a severity rating of 4—TDH Italy has undertaken a rapid need assessment, revealing a dire need for food assistance due to income loss and weakened agricultural assets. The primary coping strategies identified involve borrowing food, reducing meal sizes and frequency, and opting for less preferred food types. Our project aims to address these issues by enhancing food security and resilience among the most vulnerable communities through strategic interventions focused on improving access to food through dignified form of assistance and supporting local markets.TDH Italy's approach is multi-faceted, employing household and trader surveys to assess food security improvements and market capacities, respectively. These surveys will inform our selection of local shops for participation, ensuring they meet stringent criteria for quality, hygiene, and supply chain reliability. A critical component of our strategy is the implementation of the FSA indicators, including household demographics, vulnerability status, income access, and more, to monitor and evaluate the project's impact effectively.AAP is a cornerstone of the project design. TDH It are committed to engaging affected communities in decision-making processes, emphasizing the inclusion of vulnerable groups such as women and individuals with disabilities. TDH-It AAP framework includes a transparent feedback and complaints mechanism, facilitated by collaborations with local partners like SARC and direct engagement with beneficiaries during key project phases.the project also prioritizes the prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, enforcing strict protocols and training for staff and beneficiaries to ensure a safe and respectful environment for all participants. Gender equality and disability inclusion are integral to the project as per the international guidelines (IASC), with targeted strategies to address the unique needs and barriers faced by these groups, promoting equal access to resources and participation in project activities.Risk management is an essential aspect of TDH It operation, with specific strategies in place to address potential incidents during distribution.R1: incidents during distribution are managed by TDH IT's Security Officer, who implements appropriate security measures. R2: risk of ineligible beneficiaries receiving vouchers, TDH IT conducts assessments of beneficiary needs and eligibility criteria, prioritizing vulnerable groups. R3: The risk of vouchers being exchanged for cash or goods is mitigated through beneficiary guidance on voucher usage and goods purchases. M&E activities ensure appropriate voucher usage and prevent exploitation incidents
