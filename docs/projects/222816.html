Project Summary

In 2025, Action Against Hunger (ACF) will continue its WASH interventions for the Rohingya refugees in Camp 11 and adjacent host communities in Ukhiya, which have been impacted by the prolonged presence of refugees. In Camp 11, ACF will continue with IOM funding for 100% beneficiaries coverage ensuring comprehensive coverage while maintaining flexibility to address gaps in staffing, facility upgradation, new construction and emergency response as needed and alignment with the WASH sector strategy. The funding will give us the scope to fill up the gap of other camp/s based on the discussion with WASH sector, AFA and operational partner/s. The project will focus on new construction and upgrading deteriorated WASH facilities and maintaining existing services based on gaps and needs through cost-effective measures, aiming to provide reliable water supply, functional sanitation systems and efficient solid waste management. Key intervention will be community engagement, particularly around solid waste management, to ensure sustainable practices and enhanced community ownership. In addition, ACF aims to support new arrivals through emergency water supply and other essential WASH services. ACF’s approach will integrate the promotion of adequate hygiene behaviors, employing a multi-sectoral approach to ensure consistency and efficiency across WASH, health, and livelihood support programs in both camp and host communities. Throng the collaboration with WASH focal of the targeted host communities, ACF will work to expand equitable access to quality WASH services, aligning these efforts with national development plans and WASH vulnerability criteria, such as lack of improved water sources and sanitation facilities, to reach both directly and indirectly affected populations.
The distribution of essential WASH items, including soap and menstrual kits, will be coordinated with the area’s focal agencies, the WASH sector, and the host community to avoid duplication and ensure efficient resource use. ACF will leverage monitoring systems to assess the quality and equity of WASH services and will support both internal and external monitoring to sustain the integrity and accountability of these services across all intervention areas.
The project will also focus on environmental sustainability and inclusivity, emphasizing gender-diverse individuals, age, and disability considerations. Disaster preparedness and response measures concerning climate change will be incorporated to mitigate public health risks, such as disease outbreaks, especially given the vulnerability of both refugees and host communities to natural disasters and climate change. Community-based approaches, youth engagement in mass awareness, strengthening WASH committees, and increasing community involvement in facility maintenance and awareness efforts will be key to fostering long-term sustainability and local ownership. ACF WASH response will consider environmental issues, gender, age, disability inclusion, and accountability, with emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters and public health risks, including disease outbreaks. This project will also ensure community ownership over the facilities and the project’s sustainability.
Aligned with the WASH Sector’s strategic objectives, ACF’s intervention will incorporate practices to improve environmental sustainability, including alternatives to plastic materials and measures for sustainable groundwater management. ACF will work in line with WASH sector Technical priorities especially for managing solid sludge, operationalizing the new water strategy, and facilitating community ownership of WASH services through capacity-building for hand-pump maintenance and community-led solutions.
In addition, ACF will explore collaboration opportunities with governmental and institutional projects, to ensure alignment with broader development goals.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Inter Sector Need Assessment (ISNA) 2024 reports that 85% of Rohingya refugees have sufficient water for household needs, this is joint efforts results by the government and WASH partners. 68% of households use the water network, with an average supply of 23 liters per person per day.In Camp 11, although 96% report adequate water access, only 71% rely on the network, and water collection averages 16 liters per person per day, below both the camp-wide average of 23 liters and the emergency standard of 20 liters. This indicates a need for targeted interventions to improve water access. Ongoing challenges include gaps in water network coverage, requiring consistent operation, maintenance, and infrastructure expansion to ensure reliable and safe water access throughout the camp.As per WASH sector Overview and Monitoring October 2024, latrine and bathing facilities are reported as functional, but ongoing operation and maintenance are required, though there is still gap in terms of WASH standard, currently access to the latrine following 1: 27ratio, this is lower than the standard(1:20), Challenges persist in FSM management, with only 17% of FSTPs in this camp meeting the acceptable COD standards (WASH sector Overview and Monitoring October 2024). In solid waste management, 79% of households practice source segregation, yet challenges persist in covering the remaining gaps. The ISNA highlights gaps in MHM kits (18%) and soap (24%), necessitating collaboration with area focal agencies and the WASH sector. As per ISNA 2024 report, 15% of female family members feel unsafe using communal latrines at night, requiring ongoing advocacy efforts and improved lighting near latrines and access roads. According to gender analysis report, the Rohingya refugee camps in Bangladesh face a deficiency in proper WASH infrastructure, primarily due to space limitations (Gender in Humanitarian Action Working Group,2022), This report remains relevant for Camp 11 due to its geographic constraints and congestion, leading to poorly placed of the facilities, Still gap in gender-segregated and disability-friendly facilities, and insufficient privacy and security. These conditions increase the risk of SGBV, especially for women and girls, limiting their access to essential WASH facilities. The ISNA reports that while 50% of people with disabilities (from the 12% identified as having disabilities) have adequate latrine access, the other 50% still face challenges, underscoring the need for continued improvements in safe, accessible facilities.The Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (JMSNA 2023) identified significant WASH gaps in the host community. Access to safe and functional latrines ranked three as a priority, with 9% in Palong khali, 12% in Jalia Palong, and Ranked 2 priority 14% in Raja palong.Union-specific assessments from the Cox's District WASH situation report 2024 lead by DPHE and UNICEF indicate significant gaps in latrine access, with 11.81% in Jalia Palong, 5.88% in Palong khali, and 24.2% in Raja palong lacking improved latrines..For safe drinking water access, only 45% of households in Palong khali, 43% in Raja palong, and 24% in Jalia Palong use improved sources, leaving 55% reliant on unimproved sources (JMSNA 2023)Hygiene promotion is inadequate, there is gap in ensuring handwashing space/facilities and Unsafe water handling 82.81% in Raja palong, 83.11% in Jalia Palong, and 94.22% in Palong khali, while unsafe latrine management impacts 95.87% in Raja palong and 92.39% in Jalia Palong underscoring the need for increased awareness and intervention.Menstrual hygiene remains a concern, as 60.4% in Jalia Palong, 59.96% in Palong khali, and 59.72% in Raja palong use old cloths, highlighting the need for increased awareness and safe practices.Overall, these findings point to urgent needs for improved sanitation, safe water access, and enhanced hygiene intervention among the Rohingya refugee and Host community.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

To enhance protection in WASH, ACF will ensure all facilities are equipped with safety measures for secure and dignified use by women and girls. Accessibility will be improved through ramps, chairs in latrines, and distribution of assistive devices based on needs. Gender-segregated signs will designate areas for females, and women’s participation in user groups and the model mother initiative will be encouraged to mitigate GBV risks.Hygiene kits will be distributed safe and easy process to minimize GBV risks from large gatherings. Awareness sessions will address personal safety, disease prevention, and GBV, with input from ACF’s mental health, protection, and gender teams.Staff and volunteers will receive training on preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), GBV, and child protection. ACF will advocate for improved lighting near WASH facilities to ensure safe nighttime use of latrines. Privacy, safety, accessibility, and user comfort will be prioritized in facility location planning.

2.3 - Age & Disability

ACF will ensure inclusive support for all individuals, regardless of gender diverse, age, gender, or disability, by maintaining functional, accessible, and climate-resilient WASH facilities. Specific needs of older adults and persons with disabilities will be identified through assessments. Based on these needs, ACF will enhance facility accessibility and provide assistive items, such as disability-friendly chairs and buckets for those with mobility issues. Older adults and persons with disabilities will be involved in the planning and design of WASH interventions. Additionally, WASH staff will receive training on accommodating the needs of these vulnerable groups.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure community involvement, ACF will consult with members throughout the program’s implementation. Meetings with beneficiaries will gather input and address their needs, fostering active participation.Community members can engage through an established complaint feedback mechanism, including direct communication, complaint boxes, hotlines, and other channels. ACF will respond promptly to all complaints and provide feedback to the complainant.Project information will be shared at camp, host community, and sector-level meetings. ACF will hold regular discussions with community leaders and user committees to prioritize needs and plan interventions. Additionally, user groups and WASH facilities management committees will be activated to empower affected populations in decision-making, with joint visits conducted regularly to enhance accountability.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

ACF believes that everyone, regardless of age, gender identity, disability, sexual orientation, or ethnic background, deserves protection from harm, abuse, neglect, or exploitation. ACF enforces a zero-tolerance policy against bullying, harassment, abuse, and exploitation by its staff and partners.ACF integrates women into all aspects of program implementation to reduce their vulnerabilities, ensuring that projects are designed to minimize risks of exploitation and abuse.WASH facilities will be made safe and accessible, strategically located in secure areas, and designed to accommodate the needs of people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups.ACF will maintain strict oversight of its staff’s work environment to prevent any instances of sexual exploitation and abuse, implementing its PSEA policy effectively for proper reporting and response.To enhance gender sensitivity, ACF will conduct PSEA training for volunteers, staff and relevant stakeholders increasing their awareness and capacity to identify, report, and respond to incidents appropriately.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project adopts a comprehensive approach to climate change adaptation and mitigation, integrating disaster risk reduction (DRR) and management (DRM) strategies. Action Against Hunger (ACF) has incorporated preparedness and response measures aligned with the WASH sector strategy for natural disasters and disease outbreaks. A Rapid Response Team (RRT) ensures swift action in emergencies. Environmental and vulnerability assessments using NEAT+ guidelines will map specific risks like landslides, floods, and cyclones. The project focuses on controlling WASH-related diseases, including acute watery diarrhea (AWD), scabies, and dengue, particularly in vulnerable areas. This multifaceted approach addresses immediate and long-term climate challenges, balancing current needs with future resilience and environmental sustainability. The project's comprehensive strategy builds resilient communities prepared for future climate-related risks.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

ACF will implement comprehensive EPR measures to ensure effective response during emergencies. The organization maintains a detailed business continuity plan that delineates procedures for sustaining critical operations during disruptions. An Emergency Response Team has been established and is regularly updated. Standard operating procedures for emergency response have been developed and are periodically revised. These SOPs encompass various scenarios and provide step-by-step guidelines for personnel to follow during emergencies. Furthermore, ACF has established a dedicated contingency stock to facilitate rapid mobilization and resource allocation in crisis situations.Continuous coordination with stakeholders will support effective emergency actions. Additionally, ACF will focus on constructing resilient infrastructure and securing facilities to withstand vulnerable conditions in accordance with sector and national standards, thereby mitigating disaster impacts.This multi-faceted approach, integrating planning, procedures, and financial preparedness, enables ACF to respond expeditiously and effectively to emergencies while minimizing operational disruptions.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The WASH project aims to ensure access to water, sanitation, and hygiene services for individuals across all age demographics, including youth aged 15-24. Although the project's primary objective is to achieve universal access. It also includes specific youth engagement activities. Young people will participate in extensive awareness campaigns (covering topics such as Dengue, AWD, camp cleanliness, and special day observances). Additionally, female youth will be involved in menstrual hygiene management activities. A youth-driven support group will be created to enhance awareness and education about menstrual hygiene. ACF will conduct training sessions to build capacity before involving youth in program implementation. These efforts are part of the broader hygiene promotion activities, which are a crucial aspect of the program's community engagement strategy.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees in Camp 11 through several key initiatives. It emphasizes community engagement and ownership, particularly in solid waste management, to ensure sustainable practices. The project strengthens WASH committees and enhances community involvement in maintaining facilities, fostering long-term sustainability. Capacity-building initiatives, such as training for community-led solutions, are incorporated. It integrates disaster preparedness and response measures, addressing climate change and potential disease outbreaks, thereby enhancing refugee resilience. By involving community members in decision-making processes through user groups and WASH facilities management committees, the project empowers refugees to take an active role in their well-being. It focus on environmental sustainability and inclusivity, considering gender, age, and disability, contributes to building resilient community capable of addressing future challenges.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project activities in the host community align with development priorities in Cox's Bazar district. The project enhances equitable access to quality WASH services in host communities, ensuring these efforts are congruent with national development plans and WASH vulnerability criteria. It addresses significant WASH deficiencies identified in the JMSNA 2023 and the Cox's District WASH situation report 2024 led by DPHE and UNICEF, which serve as district-level planning instruments. The project focuses on enhancing access to safe water, functional sanitation, and hygiene promotion, aligning with broader governmental initiatives and development goals. Emphasizing environmental sustainability, including plastic reduction and groundwater management, it aims to boost public health, protect the environment, and improve living conditions in host communities, supporting district development priorities.
