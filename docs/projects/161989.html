Project Summary

To improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive sexual reproductive health services for refugees and host communities, UNFPA will continue to strengthen sexual reproductive health and rights of women and girls. UNFPA will ensure continuous availability of sexual and reproductive health commodities and equipment through improved supply chain management, including family planning methods to meet contraceptive needs of refugees and host communities. Capacity building will continue for healthcare providers on sexual and reproductive health, gender-based violence, adolescent health, obstetric fistula screening, newborn care and cervical cancer screening. Minimizing loss of life cannot be achieved without sustaining funding for emergency and specialized health care referrals. A robust referral mechanism will be implemented for both the camps and host community through trainings and knowledge exchange among the health care providers/midwives and implementation of a functional hospital rotation plan. Maternal and neonatal death surveillance will be expanded to help identify and address avoidable mortality factors.
The interventions planned under this project will ensure timely access to essential live saving and comprehensive sexual and reproductive health services to Rohingya refugees and host community in Ukhiya and Teknaf Sub districts of Cox’s bazar district. The project aims at addressing the priority sexual and reproductive Health including ensuring the minimum initial service package (MISP) for reproductive health in crisis and now transitioning to comprehensive SRH for the affected population
Of the 1.3 million people targeted in the JRP, this project will focus on those 406,108 refugees and host communities which includes women of reproductive age that are facing critical life threatening situations in the Teknaf and Ukhiya sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar. These women of reproductive age are at constant and immediate-risk of pregnancy related complications and will need urgent and immediate care.
The project also seeks to improve and maintain the quality of SRH facilities, strengthened referral pathway and safe access to 24-hour rapid transfer of all life-threatening emergencies, maintaining continuous supply of life saving SRH commodities including family planning services, scale up quality midwifery led care across the camps by providing mentorship support to the service providers through capacity building program, strengthening community based SRH services and behavioral change approaches by ensuring that quality SRH information and services are available near the communities.
Bangladesh as a country being prone to natural disasters and ranked 13th in the world in the risk for natural disasters with flooding and cyclones ranking the highest. The project will also take into consideration the contingency planning and needs to respond to sexual reproductive health and rights during natural disasters. Less than two year ago, this same area of Bangladesh, including Cox’s Bazar and Chittagong, was ravaged by Cyclone Mora bringing widespread flooding and damaging winds affecting over 3 million people.
The project will be implemented under the auspices of SRH Working Group under the Health Sector, which provides technical guidance and support to partners to ensure that the projects are implemented in an integrated manner as per the international standards and at par with nationals.

Needs

The priority sexual and reproductive health includes ensuring the minimum initial service package (MISP) for reproductive health in crisis and now expanding to comprehensive SRH for the affected population. Although some partners are providing the minimum initial service package of sexual and reproductive health, access to essential comprehensive reproductive, maternal and new-born health services remains a major concern. There are critical gaps in comprehensive emergency obstetric care, newborn care, family planning provision (particularly long-acting methods), availability and timely access to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape/intimate partner violence services and first-line support for gender-based violence survivors. In 2019, 70% of Primary Health Care facilities had no systems in place to inform community and stakeholders of the GBV health services available in the facilities and were not adequately trained to identify signs and symptoms of sexual/intimate partner violence (GBV Service Quality assessment 2019). The proportions of facility-based deliveries have improved from 32% at the start of 2019 to 47% by end of September 2019 but remain below target with geographic variations. There is a need to better understand the reasons/social beliefs around decision making and engage men in health education as only 13% reported that pregnant women themselves are the decision-maker on where to deliver their baby (MSNA 2019). Mobility restrictions among women may also impact their health-seeking behaviors with 20% of married women and 28% of unmarried women reportedly not able to move in the camps unaccompanied (MSNA 2019). Adolescent health has been identified as a priority need for 2020. It is critical to ensure 24/7 lifesaving services and strengthen referral pathways to ensure timely access. More support is required to strengthen community-based SRH services and behavioral change approaches by ensuring that quality SRH information and services are available near the communities.The SRH needs for high-risk and special needs populations (e.g. LGBTQI, sex workers, Adolescent Sexual Reproductive Health and disabled) are yet to be addressed systematically and need to be integrated into the overall reproductive health care response. Given the high home deliveries and low skilled birth attendance, issues such as obstetric fistula need to be addressed urgently. There is also an urgent need to scale up quality midwifery-led care across the camps by providing mentorship support and capacity building to the service providers. The temporary nature of facility infrastructure poses a challenge to longer-term continuity of services, as the infrastructures degrade. Additional support is needed to strengthen host community facilities and a mechanism is needed to cover referral costs to avoid burden on Government facilities and more support is required to strengthen the referral facilities to provide 24/7 management of emergency obstetric care and gynecological complications. Care provision for pregnant mothers with other complications, which may increase the risk of obstetric emergencies, need to be strengthened. Continued support to the SRH Sub Sector coordination will be required to provide high-quality technical assistance to ensure comprehensive SRH information and services are of international standards and on par with nationals.

1. Expand the services from MISP to comprehensive SRH by strengthening existing health facilities to provide 24/7 emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC)- • Continue to support deployment of midwives and other health care providers at health facilities/tent clinics in affected areas for pregnant women and provide essential reproductive health services, including Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), EmONC • Provide high quality technical assistance to ensure comprehensive SRH information and service provision are of international standards and on par with nationals • Support upazila health complexes of Ukhia, Teknaf, Cox's Bazar Sadar Hospital to provide 24/7 CEmONC services • Support a field hospital to provide CEmONC service in camps by ensuring midwifery led care and proper referrals coordination • Support referral cost for specialized obstetric emergencies e.g. obstetric fistula and cervical cancer, pre-cancerous lesions cases; establish 24/7 SRH facilities/birth units at the sites where no SRH services are provided based on joint health partners’ mapping • Procure and distribute Emergency Reproductive Health kits/commodities and ensure orientations to health care providers on utilization • Implement conditional cash voucher and non-cash incentive program to improve service uptake, focusing on ANC visits and facility-based delivery in all camps. • Provide comprehensive SRH services in all facilities including family planning, management and prevention of STI, HIV/AIDS. • Provide comprehensive adolescent sexual and reproductive health services (A-SRH) • Provide screening services, specialized treatment for reproductive health morbidities i.e cervical cancer, obstetric fistula • Contingency planning and preparedness to respond to lifesaving MISP during natural disasters i.e cyclone and monsoon • Provide SRH services for special groups i.e. LGBTQI, sex workers and TG by establishing service access points (Dropping in Centers) 2. Strengthen the referral pathway- • Procure three-wheel ambulances, stretchers to ensure safe transport of pregnant women to health facilities during labor/complications • Reinforce, repair, and maintain vital SRH structures • Establishing and supporting the emergency referral hubs in the camps • Support referral facilities (CEmONC) services and referral costs for patient 3. Ensure GBV-SRH integration i.e ensuring basic sexual reproductive health services provision in all UNFPA supported safe spaces for women and girls (deployment of midwives in WFS) 4. Continue to strengthen SRH coordination and ensure that SRH and GBV services are provided in an integrated manner and provide technical assistance to partners through and SRH resource centre, i.e. workshops, training, capacity building activities, providing technical information • Contribute to Health information system strengthening and ensure timely distribution of SRH Information and communication products e.g. bulletins. • Improve data management systems and assessments to better understand the SRH needs and gaps 5. Strengthen community based SRH services to improve service uptake. -• Expand Community Health Volunteers Network to increase community awareness on SRH issues and services • Engaging the religious leaders, community leaders and Mahjis as well as involvement of men as SRHR partners to raise awareness on key SRHR Topics,i.e right-base form of FP • Ensure AAP through maintaining and establishing communication, community participation, feedback support mechanism and strengthening the PSEA network 6. Quality improvement in facilities including infrastructure, infection prevention and control 7. Capacity building on SRH services - Strengthening local capacity through continous training, master trainers development and TOT on key SRHR Topics 8. Strengthen SRH commodity security- Expand the E-stock register system to support in forecasting, quantification of commodities and transitioning from bulk kits procurement to a sustainable supply chain
