Project Summary

In the NWSW, a socio-political crisis erupted in 2017. What started as corporatist peaceful protests against a perceived encroachment of the regions’ cultural practices and autonomy turned into an armed conflict between the government defense forces and Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs), triggering very dynamic and massive internal displacements and related humanitarian needs. Since December 2019, a significant spike in military operations is forcing more individuals to flee their homes as property destructions as well as human rights violations are being reported on a regular basis. According to the HNO for 2021 , violent conflict in the south-west and north-west regions has displaced more than 700,000 people. Since the beginning of the year, IOM’s Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) has identified more than 90,000 new forcibly displaced persons and around 30,000 returnees, mainly linked to violence and school closures related to COVID-19 governmental measures. IOM’s latest ETT dashboards have highlighted the increasing trends of recurring short-term displacements characterized by individuals seeking refuge in makeshift shelters or agricultural structures in the surrounding bushes of their villages. Priority needs, as indicated by displaced populations through the ETT assessments include protection, access to food as well as shelter and non-food items. Access in NWSW for humanitarian actors continues to be an issue, with frequent ad-hoc road blocks and Monday ‘Ghost-towns’ having a considerable impact on the maintenance of reliable and effective humanitarian assistance.
IOM, as the only actor present in these regions with the capacity, expertise and access to certain areas to conduct comprehensive assessments on displacement trends and statistics, was asked by OCHA and the Humanitarian Coordinator to carry out multi-sectoral needs assessments (MSNA) in NWSW. The data from MSNA is shared with more than 30 operational humanitarian actors and is used as the primary data source for the update of the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Data that is produced and disseminated through the IOM DTM methodology has become the key source of information on internal displacement across both crisis-affected regions and is used by humanitarian partners for vital planning purposes. The last partner survey conducted in late 2019 stated that 95% were either ‘very satisfied’ or ‘satisfied’ with the data supplied, with 95% also stating that they either want more DTM data or the same amount for coordinating their activities. Comprehensive assessments provide humanitarian actors with reliable data on the number and location of displaced populations (IDPs and returnees), which contribute to a better understanding of the displacement trends and most pressing needs.
In light of the above, IOM seeks to continue providing the humanitarian community with information on displacement trends and most pressing sectoral needs of conflict-affected populations in these two regions. IOM will continue its data collection activities with additional rounds of displacement tracking, Emergency Tracking Tools, Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments as well as the general informal support to the humanitarian community in North-West, South-West with ad-hoc information requests and situational analysis. A range of information products will be distributed throughout 2021 depending on partners’ needs, from the Emergency Tracking Tool to help coordinate partners’ emergency response to sudden displacements, to larger scale Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNA) that will fit into wider Humanitarian Country Team planning.
All these activities will contribute to better availability of data to allow for better evidence based programming with more informed and appropriate crisis response based on accurate and up to date information made available.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In order to support evidence-based and accountable service provision by humanitarian partners in Cameroon, IOM plans to continue collecting, processing and disseminating information on displacement trends and most pressing sectoral needs of conflict-affected populations in the NWSW through its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). The DTM methodology, tools used, as well as dissemination strategy will be adapted to the context and will be closely coordinated with key stakeholders (OCHA, cluster leads, humanitarian actors) in order to ensure that the data collected will be used by partners for a more informed response. A variety of DTM products will be made to fit humanitarian data gaps, such as displacement reports and dashboards, Emergency Tracking Tool dashboards, datasets, reports on specific thematic sectors, data from Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNA) that feed directly into Humanitarian Response Plans (HRP) and Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), as well as a wider stock-taking evaluation of DTM activities led so far to address partner’s needs. Work will be made throughout the action also to ensure that IOM’s DTM operations are contributing towards further aims to transition protection cluster programming to recovery and more sustainable development, with the continued roll out of the stability index and human security index in partnership with other United Nations agencies.
