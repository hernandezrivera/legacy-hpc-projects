Project Summary

AHA works to strengthen the capacity of the education facilities in Northern Syrian and enhance the possibility of delivering better spaces for learining in schools to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children aged 5-17 years
This designed project is to enhance the overall status of schools through repairing to return to their schools or enhancing the current condition of schools.
AHA will rehabilitate 20 damaged schools in Afrin and Dana.
The rehabilitation activities will include repairing damaged classrooms and WASH facilities; sanitation systems, bathrooms, solid waste disposals facilities, drainage facilities and adequate water for personal hygiene and to clean toilets rely on INEE standards.
After rehabilitation the schools, the project will provide furniture; such as desks, chairs, blackboards
AHA has extensive experience in rehab works for damaged building and schools, more than 3000 Houses was rehabilitated through teams of AHA funded by many donors, where SCHF is one of them. in addition, AHA rehabilitated about 9 schools in Syrian crises were damaged in Southern Syria.
AHA will implement the rehabilitation work for those 20 schools in Afrin and Idleb in order to provide better place for the students to have proper, safer, and more adequate education. Those schools are meant to be run by Syrian NGOs that are providing education services while they can not provide rehab work. In this context, AHA will provide needed services as rehab work starting from construction works and WASH facilities, not to forget providing proper furniture for those schools.
special attention will be given to children with disabilities through providing needed facilities for children with disabilities regarding ramps that facilitate their entrance to the schools and classes or using bathrooms or any other services in schools.

4 - Needs

As per OCHA reports 2.1 million children are reported to be out-of-school and 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Most of out of school children are at primary school age and/or in hard-to-reach and besieged locations. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor. The escalation of violence has led to increasing war-related psychological trauma for children, their families and education personnel. The high proportion of crisis-affected children and adolescents out of the public education systems is of high concern since they are at bigger protection risks situations, many children were forced to stay out of school or if managed to access schools, they were often exposed to unsafe and overcrowded classrooms. The destruction and damages of schools have led to an acute need for additional learning spaces for both host community children and internally displaced children residing in these areas. Teaching capacity remains overstretched with more than 150,000 teachers and education staff who have left their positions since the start of the crisis, the capacity and capability of available teachers are being severely over-stretched. These shortages have limited the quality and provision of education.

5 - Implementation of activities

AHA will rehabilitate 20 damaged schools. we will target the schools in (Aleppo / Afrin district - Idlib Dana) where its severity scale 5 (based on HNO2019). Schools will be repaired to the targeted areas that promote equity and the physical safety of learners, teachers and other education personnel. Schools will be designed giving careful thought to who uses the learning space. Spaces will be appropriate to the sex, age, physical ability and cultural considerations of all users. Entrances and exits will be repaired to allow students, teachers and other education personnel to exit safely in an emergency. The rehabilitation activities will include repairing damaged classrooms, roofs, floors and WASH facilities; such as sanitation systems, bathrooms (Girls, boys and disabilities), solid waste disposals facilities, drainage facilities and providing adequate water for personal hygiene and to clean toilets rely on INEE standards. Sanitation facilities will be accessible for persons with disabilities and will maintain privacy, dignity and safety. Toilet doors will lock from the inside. To prevent sexual harassment and abuse, separate toilets for boys/men and girls/women will be located in safe, convenient and easily accessible places. After rehabilitation the schools, the project will provide furniture; such as desks, chairs, blackboards and laboratory equipment. Rehabilitation needs and related costs will be assessed and on the basis of which a targeted rehabilitation plan is designed and then implemented. During implementation the project we will take into consideration gender sensitive and disability friendly that would ensure the protection needs of boys and girls as well as the needs of children with disabilities allowing adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighbouring community not served/cannot access or if information offered is not consistent between communities. Mitigating Measures: Continuously ensure service mapping, consultation with community on program interventions, regular communication with different communities on availability of services including host communities and returnee populations. Expansion of services to non-camp locations where needs are present, to avoid concentration of services in camps. Monitoring Mechanism: Regular consultations with communities, FGDs, feedback boxes Risk: Resistance from local authorities to accessing and implementing programming. Mitigating measures: AHA has established relationships with relevant local authorities and stakeholders, such as local council, education directorate, ministries, community gatekeepers and religious leaders. Monitoring Mechanism: Regular meetings with relevant authorities. Risk: Affected populations and AHA staff, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment. Mitigating measures: Training will be provided to AHA staff PSEA Policy and Code of Conduct.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: Access to targeted areas remains possible and safe. Risk1: Access to one (or more) of targeted areas becomes hindered or insecure. Likelihood: medium. Effect: medium. Mitigation: AHA will advise staff on alternative routes to mitigate the risk. If the security situation changes in a certain area, we can wait or postpone activities for a few days or change targeted areas to another accessible area in need of support. Assumption 2: Skilled and unskilled workers can be hired from the local market to complete rehabilitation works. Risk: It might be difficult to find enough qualified technicians and engineers to implement the rehabilitation works, Likelihood: low. Effect: low. Mitigation: contract and recruit high-quality workers from AHA roster or hire from a wider geographic range if needed. Assumption3: Rehabilitation items and materials are available to be procured locally. Risk 3: It might become difficult to locally secure one (or more) of the needed rehabilitation items/services of good quality or qualified workshops to implement the rehabilitation works. Likelihood: medium. Effect: high. Mitigation3: through previous experience was able to directly purchase the materials from larger markets within Syria and transport them to the project sites. Moreover, we will seek to contract high-capacity trusted vendors who can deliver the required rehabilitation works in Syria effectively.
