Project Summary

Organization for welfare and Development in Action (OWDA) is proposing a Lifesaving Emergency Nutrition Response Project to drought-affected IDPs and Host communities of Dhagahmadow and Dhagahbur districts of Jarar zone, and Denan districts of Shabelle zone, Somali Region. The objective of the project is to provide life-saving assistance and improve nutrition status and reduce morbidity and mortality rates with top priority given to children under the age of five, PLW, the elderly, people with physical injuries, and disabilities. Similarly, the project will help to focus on the early warning of Nutrition surveillance case finding and linkage between health facilities which is mainstreamed with COVID-19 pandemic prevention and control measures.
The project will provide support to 35,605 beneficiaries with an estimated budget of $410,000. The proposed districts will be hotspot classification priority 1 woreda for drought-induced IDPs and host community response prioritized sectoral approach assistance.
The proposed districts has an increasing number of people in need due to drought-affected communities, Limited health services, overwhelming nutrition needs due to the Prolonged severe drought conditions, shortage of Nutrition supplies, low capacity of the health workforce, high staff turnover, Low number of health staff trained on Nutrition care service provider which has limited capacity to undertake the routine nutrition activities. The impact of drought and COVID-19 cases amongst internally displaced people and host communities devastated their nutrition service delivery and stretched the weak health systems to provide essential nutrition service delivery to the needed communities.
The project will provide Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) including Therapeutic Feeding Program (TFP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding (TSFP) for moderately malnourished children of 6 to 59 months old and malnourished PLW, Support the optimal Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) practices for infants and young children under 24 months and support the nutritional needs of pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and continuum of care. Mainstreaming of Gender and Protection activities will be also included.
The project will support the nutrition screening of all children 6-59 months, Provision of CMAM training in a short session using prevention and control measures of COVID19 for service providers for the new guideline, Support the treatment of SAM with/ without medical complications in children between 6-59 months in the OTP and SC sites, Support OTP and SC sites for referral cases, support TSFP and OTP commodities to end-users, provision of food for mothers/caretakers of children in stabilization centers (SCs). Gender, Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be mainstreamed for the overall project activities.
In line with the 2022 Ethiopian Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), the proposed project will deliver a full spectrum of emergency nutrition interventions including prevention, treatment, management, community mobilization, and case identification to achieve and sustain the project impact

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

OWDA is committed to uphold the Core Humanitarian Standards on Accountability and Quality Management, including appropriateness, relevance, and timeliness of response, strengthening of local capacity, participation, and feedback mechanisms, and incorporate learning across all activities. To this end, OWDA strives to ensure transparent and participatory processes throughout the project cycle, during project development, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. Feedback from community members, including disability people, people with special need and children, on the quality and accessibility of project resources and services, is collected regularly through participatory monitoring mechanisms including community feedback and response meetings, focus group discussions, and suggestion boxes. Appropriate and confidential feedback/complaint mechanisms will be strengthened in consultation with beneficiaries to enhance OWDA’s ability to protect beneficiaries from various forms of exploitation and abuse. Existing project/context-specific accountability systems will be established, and the project will have a detailed accountability plan. All responses will be delivered verbally, in writing, and in recorded form. This mechanism improves the quality of program implementation, strengthens relationships, increases acceptance by communities, reduces the risk of fraud and inefficient use of funds, and reduces the risk of harm to beneficiaries.
