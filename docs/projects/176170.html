Project Summary

The burden of disease is caused by unsafe water, sanitation, and hygiene. Therefore, enabling access to safe, clean drinking-water and basic sanitation and hygiene is essential for the basic survival of any population and can reduce nearly 90% of all deaths from diarrhoea(https://www.who.int/elena/titles/bbc/wsh_diarrhoea/en/)
Through this response CIDO targets 43,688 persons with much needed WASH services in line with the following WASH specific objectives for the 2021:
- Reduce morbidity and mortality, as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents, of the most vulnerable populations in severity levels 4 and 5.
- Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5.
- Enable vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, support returns and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses through targeted programming to support coping capacities and livelihoods in prioritized areas
This response will ultimately provide lifesaving WASH emergency interventions in the Lakes, Jonglei and Central Equatoria states. The WASH emergency interventions will improve access to safe water, secured and gender appropriate sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion, which will be integrated in other sectors, among them health, nutrition, Food security and livelihood, and child protection/ GBV.
CIDO, being a national NGO working in the locations of Jonglei, Lakes and Central Equatoria and with over 7 years’ experience in humanitarian response and currently implementing SSHF SA1 Multisector project with WASH in Akobo is potentially capable to ensure that these response is well coordinated integrated within FSL, Nutrition, Health and protection to meet cluster and HRP objectives for 2021

1 - Needs

Protracted conflict in South Sudan has contributed to an extended humanitarian crisis, which has been exacerbated by unusually high levels of flooding and the COVID-19 pandemic. Although conflict has reduced between parties to the Revitalized-Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS), violence has continued in many pockets of the country, threatening to reverse gains made at the national level since the peace deal was signed in September 2018. Conflict with non-signatory groups in the Equatoria Region has continued, with sub-national and localized violence in Lakes and Jonglei States persisting. In Jonglei 43 per cent of the proportion of households rely on surface water, Central Equatorial 41 per cent, while 17 per cent of the people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39 per cent of the people reporting insufficient access to water for handwashing. WASH remains a major contributor to public health burden, including malnutrition, food insecurity as well as WASH related protection issues. The cases of AWD for 2020 were noted as140,856 cases including 26 deaths across the Country, a 60 per cent increase compared to 2019, In early 2020, the Global Acute malnutrition prevalence (GAM) was high (16.2%) above the WHO recommended threshold of 15% as an estimated 1.4 million children are currently facing malnutrition, 313,319 of them are severely malnourished. Major causes of malnutrition in the population range from high prevalence of diseases (up to 36%), poor quality and diversity of food, deficiency of safe and quality drinking water, sanitation and hygiene services, poor access to health and nutrition services, and COVID-19 pandemic. Furthermore, the assessments and Monitoring reports by CIDO in Akobo, Morobo and Yirol East (November 2020) Reports revealed that out of 40 Boreholes within Mapordit and Aluakluak payams (Yirol East - Lakes State) only 20 were functional while 21 were nonfunctional, 16 boreholes in Akobo were completely damaged and not working whereas one (1) functional hand pump in the Morobo Headquarter serving over 300 households was identified since most of the boreholes were abandoned and destroyed during the conflict and are no longer functional while In the rural areas where some of the returnees settled, especially in Bura, Aworo, Isebi, Panyana, Kendre, Nyei and Wudabi, the water sources such as boreholes are destroyed and the returnees and host communities get their water direct from rivers and streams,These responses therefore, will attract and demand concerted efforts in coordination and joint programming with other clusters (Health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection) to be enhanced to address not only WASH related issues but also capitalize on the interventions that will ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services, which will further decrease the prevalence of acute malnutrition, and, ultimately, reduce morbidity and mortality

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CIDO has a functional policy on PSEA. All staffs will be obligated to sign against the policy in respect to the 6 core principles of PSEA. The respective project staff leading this project will be guided by the standard operating Procedures (SOP) of PSEA and where applicable will use the community based complaint Mechanism to address cases of PSEA. CIDO will ensure that PSEA IEC materials are used and are visible in all designated areas of service delivery. In collaboration with other partners and recognition that CIDO is an active protection partner, efforts will be consolidated to conduct capacity building of front line workers on PSEA and extend applicability of SOP to other NGOs at grassroots level

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

These project activities are designed to give priority to safety and dignity of all men, women boys and girls and prevent negative coping strategies and mitigate risks including issues of discrimination in regards to accessing services. Through regular coordination meetings, CIDO will work with other partners to train the frontline workers on disability mainstreaming and also report and share protection concerns for persons with specific needs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

An integral part of this project delivery will be participatory Learning and Continuous Improvement. CIDO will organize regular program performance and quality review meetings with staff, beneficiaries and partners to assess progress towards objectives and work plans, monitor project performance and quality based on monitoring and beneficiary feedback, identified risks and risk management strategies during implementation. At the end of the project CIDO will carry out a project evaluation and document the process used to achieve project outputs and outcomes. The report will also be shared with the beneficiaries

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In consultation with the beneficiaries an appropriate, safe and inclusive community based feedback and complaints handling mechanism will be established and implemented to provide platforms for beneficiary’s active involvement in project and avenues to voice any issues or concerns about the project. This will also optimize existing community structures and ensure or establish committees where they do not exist. Focus Group Discussion and individual conversations as part of getting feedback from the beneficiaries and suggestion boxes stationed in the strategic places within the community and CIDO offices so that the beneficiaries who are able to read write use this means to share their feedback will be explored

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CIDO will put necessary measures to ensure that beneficiary not only suffer from the service acquisition but also in data protection. This will involve ensuring that the available data does not trickle in the wrong audience by keeping confidential information well and educating Nutation health workers on importance of confidentiality. Referral pathways will be created for survivors of SGBV and CIDO will ensure that they access health services without impediments in to the health facilities. To ensure that beneficiaries access feedback and complaint mechanisms, CIDO will optimize existing Community structures to address accountability issues and identify the possible threats that could negatively impact the service provision. Suggestion boxes will be placed in the strategic places within the community to ensure that the beneficiaries can easily access feedback and complaint mechanisms. This will also include having a dedicated line for receiving feedback
