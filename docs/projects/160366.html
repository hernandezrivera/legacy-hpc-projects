Project Summary

African Humanitarian Corps is planning a one year emergency food security and livelihood response targeting Nassir, Maiwut, Longichuck and Ulang of Upper Nile State composing main and dry season campaign. At least three fourths (77%) of South Sudanese are facing unprecedented levels of food insecurity across the country (South Sudan IPC update September 2019). This has been compounded by a soaring inflation, reduced purchasing power and increased household expenditure on food, with monthly expenditure on food at 64%; and reduced/ same incomes among 90% of households for the past one year (FSNMS Round 24). These have been attributed to a combination of a protracted and escalating conflict in Greater Maiwut( Nassir, Longichuck, Maiwut and Ulang Counties) and increasing armed movements across the Sobat corridor (South Sudan Humanitarian Bulletins September 2019) and below average harvests due to recent Fall Army Worm invasion.
Poor agricultural production capacities, dysfunctional markets, poor terms of trade and soaring inflation have led to worsening food and nutrition security situation, with populations resorting to negative coping strategies.
The project aims at enabling the most vulnerable men, women, girls, boys and youth (male and female) among IDPs and host communities (including elderly and disabled) in Nassir, Maiwut, Ulang and Longichuck Counties of Upper Nile State to access life-saving services and income opportunities so as to improve immediate and medium term household food and nutrition security and enhance their resilience, preparedness and self-reliance. This will also reduce their reliance on negative coping strategies such as distressed sale of high value assets and unsustainable exploitation of forest resources that lead to environmental degradation.
The proposed activities will involve support to emergency distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits for the main season and vegetable and fishing kits for the dry season production targeting 21733 Households - 130,398 beneficiaries (20000 girls and 20000 boys) and adults (30000 men and 60,398 women). Light training will also be included in the project implementation. The project will mainstream cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including GBV/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

1 - Needs

At least three fourths (77%) of South Sudanese are facing unprecedented levels of food insecurity across the country (IPC Update September 2019). This has been compounded by a soaring inflation, reduced purchasing power and increased household expenditure on food, with monthly expenditure on food at 65%; and reduced/ same incomes among 90% of households for the past one year (FSNMS Round 24). These have been attributed to a combination of a protracted and escalating conflict and cattle rustling in the four counties of Longichuck, Nassir, Maiwut and Ulang. Seasonal flooding and fall army worm invasion has resulted to below average harvest.Ongoing migrant Ethiopian and Arabs pastoralists traversing into former Upper Nile as they head into the lowland grazing areas (Touches) has already sparked tensions compounded by localized clashes between host communities, due to destruction of livelihood assets such as livestock due to raiding and crops (Humanitarian Bulletins August -September 2019)These events continue to negatively impact food security and livelihood options among the most vulnerable populations in the four targeted Counties with a significant of populations at risk of of sliding into IPC Phase 5 (September 2019-October 2019 FEWSNET Food Security Outlook; FSNMS Round 24; IPC update September 2019). This has subsequent led to and a significant deterioration in GAM in the four Counties (23.8%), with a national average of 19.1% (FSNMS Round 24). Markets, main source (76%) of cereals and tubers in the four Counties (4%), markets have been disrupted by current conflict. Hyper-inflation and stagnant incomes have significantly impacted purchasing power, with nine out of ten households unable to buy food. Many vulnerable host and displaced families, especially women, the elderly, boys and girls and children solely depend on crop farming, fishing among other coping strategies like vegetable farming along rivers and flood plains. Fisheries resources (projected to deliver immediate/short term benefits) and crop and vegetable production (medium term benefits) are projected to still play a very key role in securing household food and nutrition security in 2018 especially among children (boys and girls) under 5 years and pregnant women (FSL Cluster Strategy, August 2019).The project will mainstream cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including GBV/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project aims to positively impact the lives of the most vulnerable conflict affected persons to meet their immediate needs for reduced vulnerabilities as well as to enhance resilience and coping strategies mainly the IDPs and host communities. The provision of seed, tools, fishing gears and training to the most vulnerable will not only address their immediate food in-security by improving quality and quantity of yield but will have longer term impact on reducing vulnerabilities through the enhanced skills that will continue benefiting the local population beyond the project life. To build sustainability, AHC will ensure appropriate tools and seeds materials are accessed, replicated and allow for continued use in the next seasons. The light training imparted to local farmers will continue benefiting the local communities through increase in their yields beyond the project life. The AHC’s implementation and transition strategies are designed to support gradual or sudden changes in the environment, including the potential migration of communities or the transition from early recovery to resilience activities. Close engagement and consultation of communities and local authority will allow for active participation and acceptance of the project by beneficiaries whose expectations will be leveled throughout the project and at final project closure meetings

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

AHC seeks feedback both negative and positive from the community. This is important because we are then able to align our interventions to the dynamics of society. We always seek to close the loop by getting back to the community with the solution ad where it is not in our mandate, we refer to the responsible partners and actually follow up to make sure that the community gets the issues resolved. on the same note, AHC's approaches and programming across all sectors are designed in line with humanitarian and accountability principles (HAP) and good practices. AHC adheres to the ‘Do No Harm’ principle and ensures that beneficiaries are consulted in accordance with their gender and vulnerability groups. AHC has global commitment to gender equity and ensures all programs are designed with strong gender sensitivity taking into account the needs and views of women, men, girls, boys and elderly through gender sensitive consultation and inclusion. All AHC staff are trained in these core principles. Additionally, AHC has a designated Gender Equity Technical Advisor who provides direct guidance in ensuring programs are gender sensitive during the design, planning and implementation of activities. Staff are routinely sensitized and reminded of the core humanitarian principles to ensure beneficiaries are not abused or affected by our interventions. AHC will put critical focus on ensuring the inclusive and participatory involvement of beneficiaries in making decisions during beneficiary selection , scheduling of distributions, trainings, meetings and engaging them in field activities such as farming by consulting them (beneficiaries) in selecting the appropriate time and location for conducting those activities. Feedback desk will establish in each distribution point for the beneficiaries to give their feedback.
