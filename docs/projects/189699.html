Project Summary

SAADO's proposed project will provide comprehensive, lifesaving SGBV prevention and response interventions to the floods and conflict affected communities in Terekeka, Ayod and Duk Counties in Central Equatoria and Jonglei states respectively. The proposal will target 4,000 beneficiaries (1000 boys;1000 men;1000 women;1000 girls) in Terekeka County and 10,672 beneficiaries (1974 boys, 2436 girls,2852 men and 3410 women) in Ayod and Duk Counties of Jonglei, South Sudan. SAADO proposes to implement comprehensive, lifesaving SGBV prevention and response activities by providing quality and timely, age appropriate, case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors.
This project is designed to improve responses to cases of GBV and enhance the ability of survivors to recover from GBV incidences. SAADO will provide individual psychosocial support for women and girls in 4 established women and girls' safe spaces(4-WGFS). The project's proposed activities will also include Strengthening Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group, GBV awareness and risk mitigation measures, Strengthening the implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions, Strengthening coverage of dignity kit distribution and programming as part of risk mitigation, GBV preparedness and contingency planning and provision of livelihood support, through the provision of need based capacity building trainings to GBV actors and frontline service providers and strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways. The activities in the established WGFS will be conducted using the guidelines from the ministry of health and World Health Organization (WHO) on COVID-19 prevention and will include maintaining social distance, wearing of masks and washing hands with soap. SAADO also intends to strengthen the gender-based violence prevention through EMAP and community care programs to reduce GBV risks on men, women, boys and girls.
The targeted locations have been selected because of the prevalent needs stated in the HNO as well as SAADO's long standing presence in Terekeka, Duk and Ayod Counties through implementation of Girls Education South Sudan (GESS) II; Food for Assets (FFA); Agricultural Markets, Value Addition and Trade Development Project (AMVAT); Fisher Community Resilience Enhancement Project (FICREP);Emergency Livelihoods Response Project (ELRP)in Terekeka County since 2014 as well as running two (2) WGFS in Duk Pajut and Duk Pagak established through the South Sudan Humanitarian Fund and four (4) WGFS established in Duk Poktap and Padiet. In Ayod County, SAADO is managing two (2) WGFS in Jiech and Mogok payams supported by UNFPA through CERF funds.
The proposed project will build upon and complement SAADO's currently ongoing GBV prevention and response projects in the targeted locations. SAADO will coordinate this proposed response with the GBV sub cluster to ensure that the proposed activities are not duplicated and there is no overlap of activities and will work with health partners such as John Dau Foundation to refer survivors for Post Rape services.

1 - Needs

The people of South Sudan continue to be impacted by violence in 2022 as the UNMISS Human Rights Division (HRD) documented more than 982 violent incidents affecting at least 3,414 civilians, subjected to one of the four major types of individual harm (killing, injury, abduction and conflict-related sexual violence)in the second half of 2021 according to the humanitarian Needs Overview 2022. This analysis is corroborated by world Bank’s Harmonized list of Fragile Situations report which cites that sub-national violence continues to obstruct the realization of a durable and sustainable peace in South Sudan and is manifested by armed clashes, killings, sexual violence, abductions and looting/destruction of property and resources. To buttress this further, the Global Peace Index 2021 report South Sudan is ranking at 160th position out of a total of 163 countries listed in terms of protection concerns. In the proposed targeted locations of Terekeka in Central Equatoria, Ayod and Duk Counties in Jonglei state, violence, abuse and exploitation is still the greatest protection risks for women and girls reflecting on the continued gender inequalities and discrimination across the agro-pastoralists communities. The humanitarian needs overview (HNO) 2022 indicates that armed conflict, sub-national organized violence, COVID-19 and flooding have additionally exacerbated the already extreme levels of GBV with over 6,000 GBV incidents recorded between January and September 2021. Jonglei state especially has been experiencing intensified sub- national violence which has displaced thousands of people and resulted in hundreds of casualties as violent clashes and reprisals have played out between Murle, Dinka Bor and Lou Nuer communities. This desperate situation has increased GBV needs in Duk and Ayod Counties which remain severe as violence and violations persist as a reality of daily life for girls, women and children. The increased presence of mixed populations such as returnees and IDPs has exerted pressure on the few services available in these locations thereby exacerbating protection risks and increasing the need for GBV prevention and response interventions. The GBViMS statistics (2021) further indicate that 97% of the reported GBV incidents survivors were females (women and girls) with intimate partners accounting for more than half of the alleged perpetrators. Access to GBV services for the targeted locations of Terekeka, Ayod and Duk Counties remain a major challenge owing to the limited availability of the services. The global COVID-19 pandemic has further caused devastating socio-economic impact on people, including severe economic contractions, spikes in prices of basic commodities, loss of livelihoods, increased protection risks, and disrupted access to basic services. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 have also contributed to a rise in GBV incidents. A multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted by SAADO in October 2021 in Duk and Ayod Counties indicated that GBV is more prevalent within the households and mostly perpetrated by intimate partners. In addition, majority of the community members who participated in the key informant interviews and FGD acknowledged that the prevalence of GBV was high in their communities as they mentioned to have witnessed GBV related incidents perpetrated almost on a daily basis. This is largely attributed to the deep-rooted cultural beliefs, attitudes and perception about women and girls that contribute significantly to unequal power dynamic among men and women, hence resulting to normalizing violence against women within the agro-pastoral communities. In addition, fear of retaliation through revenge killings was mentioned to be a major barrier in accessing the available services by survivors. Across the three counties the GBV situation has further been exacerbated by the insecurity situation, flooding and COVID-19 pandemic.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To adapt beneficiaries feedback into the project assistance, SAADO's proposed project will encourage communities to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members through having tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions made by the project; In addition, consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard),as well as consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) which will be used.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure that beneficiaries are provided with information about PSEA through tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; In addition, consultations with women and men(separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), will be undertaken as well as consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and will include confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) which will be used.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

A core component of SAADO’s beneficiary accountability and communication in this proposed intervention will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will ensure there is adequate security using triangulated information from the UN,RRC and NGO forum at the distribution points. The project will also ensure that security protocols are in place when conducting activities bringing beneficiaries together. The project will ensure there is proper awareness creation with beneficiaries aware of their project entitlements and project staff trained on how to recognize PSEA and how to handle it using confidential means. The project will utilize technology to ensure that beneficiary data is protected and project staff will have limits of authorization with passwords provided. Risk of interference from external stakeholders: The project will utilize a highly transparent approach of beneficiary selection that will involve the local authorities, traditional structures and community structures to ensure the most vulnerable members of the community are selected as beneficiaries.
