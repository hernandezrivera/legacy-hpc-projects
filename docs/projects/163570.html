The project will be implemented under the Food Security Sector in liaison and coordination with the protection sector, especially when identifying the most vulnerable households for proper targeting to avoid doing harm to beneficiaries (such as Gender Based Violence). It will also be implemented in close collaboration with the coordination and common services sector for proper targeting, coverage of needs and transportation of agricultural inputs to target locations.

1 - Needs

Continuing insecurity and conflict across Libya has caused damage to the entire infrastructure especially to the agricultural fields. The result has been the socio-economic destabilization and serious risk of displacement of farming communities with a long history of cultivation as traditional livelihoods and ancestral lands are abandoned in search of alternative means of survival. Moreover, there has also been loss of livelihoods and increasing food insecurity, and the ensuing social destabilization and displacement. Food shortages have been reported mostly in the south and east where basic food items including wheat, bread, flour, pasta, oil, milk and fortified blended foods for children are in short supply. The majority of farmers want to grow wheat, being the most important cereal directly related to food security, but unfortunately, the seeds of improved varieties of wheat are not available in the country. Therefore, the area planted with wheat and other cereal crops has been reduced drastically. As a result the wheat production in the country has reduced substantially during the 2020-21 growing season. The wheat production in the country will be negatively affected if the improved varieties of wheat seeds are not provided to the farmers. It will also exacerbate the food insecurity and livelihood of vulnerable farming communities and their families. The United Nations (UN) Humanitarian Country Team in Libya estimates that three million people, out of a total population of 6.3 million, have been affected by high-intensity and frequent conflicts in Libya (Libya Multi-Sector Needs Assessment [LMSNA]). The overall project objective is to enhance the resilience of the most affected wheat-growing households by supporting them to restore their livelihood base. The project will eventually contribute to food and nutrition security at the community and targeted governorate level, as wheat is the staple food security crop and major ingredient of daily food intake both in the rural and urban communities in Libya. The current project will support 1 500 wheat producing households in Bangazi, A-Abiar, Al-Marj, South Marj, Al-Sahel, Al-Baida, Shahat, Al-Abraq, Al-Qoba, Derna, Toubrek, Sabha, Obari, Marzaq, Gat, and Al-Jofra governorates, to restore their livelihood for the cropping season 2020/2021, by providing them with quality wheat seeds of improved varieties and a training programme on improved wheat crop production. The project will contribute to the overall production of approximately 4 500 tonnes of wheat in the targeted area and support the most affected farmers to restore their wheat production capabilities both for their families as well as for most parts of the country in the November 2020 planting season through seed exchange with neighbouring communities or sale of extra seed. While FAO will be responsible for the overall implementation of the project, it will work closely with the Ministry of Agriculture to ensure project goals are achieved through proper technical expertise and monitoring capacity. All the project activities are in line with the UN Strategic Framework (2020/21). Moreover, this proposed project contributes to the implementation of activities to support food security stated under the “HRP 2020” launched in December 2020 by UN Agencies, which was prepared by the Humanitarian Country Team, and aims, among other objectives, to address the immediate food and nutrition security through the protection of productive assets of crisis affected people in Libya

4 - Protection risk analysis

The political and security situation in Libya continues to be deeply divided, as threeauthorities including the Tripoli based UN-backed Government of National Accord, vie forpolitical legitimacy, control of territory, resources and infrastructure. Armed conflict andpolitical instability have impacted the lives of more than three million people across thecountry, displacing hundreds of thousands and disrupting people’s access to basic services,including fuel and electrical power, as well as severely constraining life-saving protection andhumanitarian assistance.The protection crisis in Libya is characterised by violations of international humanitarian andhuman rights law, restricted access to safety and freedom of movement, forceddisplacement, severity and pervasiveness of gender-based violence. All parties to the conflicthave committed breaches of international law including torture, unlawful killings,indiscriminate attacks, abductions, kidnapping, disappearances and the forceful displacementof people. The number of people with mental health and psychosocial problems increasedsubstantially as a result of the conflict, including severe psychological distress for the conflictaffected population, as well as the contamination from explosive hazards affecting humansecurity and access.
