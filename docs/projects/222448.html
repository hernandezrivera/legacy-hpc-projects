Project Summary

South Sudan is still going through some of the worst humanitarian crises driven by a number of factors, which includes, extreme weather conditions fueled by climate change, a deepening economic crisis, as well as sub-national violence among others. Considerable internal and cross-border displacement has further strained scarce resources, livelihoods, and basic services, increased protection risks, and inter-communal tensions, with the biggest impacts felt by children and women.
An estimated 2.1 million children aged 6 – 59 months are suffering or expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and will be in urgent need of nutrition services from July 2024 to June 2025. This shows a 20.4 percent deterioration of acute malnutrition compared to 1.66 million registered in 2023. The total number of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases are estimated at 650,000 and 1.43 million, respectively. The analysis was conducted using data from the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) survey, Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) surveys, and program admission trends. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. Additionally, over 1.1 million PBW are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in the same period (July 2024-June 2025).
During the lean season (April to June 2025), the severity of acute malnutrition is likely to deteriorate in 62 counties including Twic East and Uror and remain similar in 16 counties – only improving in two (Pochalla and Ezo).
For the period covering July to September 2024, 39 counties are in Phase 4, 14 are in Phase 3, 22 are in Phase 2 and 5 are in Phase 1. The 39 counties in Phase 4 are from Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity states i.e., 10 counties are from Jonglei State (Akobo, Ayod, Bor, Canal/Pigi, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Pibor including Twic East and Uror.
During the post-harvest period of October 2024 to March 2025, the acute malnutrition situation is expected to improve in 24 counties, remain similar in 43, and deteriorate in 13 (Ikotos, Kapoeta North, Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Duk, Nyirol, Pibor, Twic East, Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, Tonj East, and the Abyei Administrative Area).
Contributing factors of acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases with 49.4 percent of children reported to have been ill two weeks prior to the FSNMS Round 30 assessment and 44 out of 80 counties reported illness among 50 percent or more children. Diarrhea, fever and cough were the leading symptoms reported at 18.3, 38.0 and 20.3 percent, respectively. Poor access to sanitation and drinking water compromises hygiene practices and increases the risk of high incidences of illness leading to acute malnutrition. Only 9.9 percent of the population reported having access to improved sanitation facilities and 74.3 percent reported using open defecation. Overall, 63 percent of the population reported having access to improved sources of water leaving 37 percent of the population depending on unsafe sources. Inadequate feeding practices of infants and young children are other high-risk contributing factors to acute malnutrition. All Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) indicators remain suboptimal at national level, making them another high-risk factor for acute malnutrition. Only two children out of 100 are getting the recommended quality and frequency of food required for optimal growth. 16.5 percent achieved the Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) while only 7.5 percent achieved the Minimum Meal Frequency. Funding gap observed in 2024 nutrition programs that is 56 percent funded (as of Sept 2024) affects all the counties. 2. Project activities

Tearfund will continue its nutrition interventions primarily focusing on provision of quality preventative nutrition-sensitive services to children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women and adolescents in Twic East, Pochalla and Uror Counties. Activities will include: Community mobilization, training of CNVs, active case finding, mass screening campaigns, TSFP services, and provision of deworming tablets and vitamin A supplements to under five years old children and PLWs OTP Treatment: All children with a MUAC of<11.5 cm will be admitted into the OTP and treated with Ready to Use therapeutic food (RUTF) and routine medicines according to the South Sudan CMAM guideline. Community Nutrition Workers (CNVs) will deliver health and nutrition related messages at the community level through individual counselling, group discussion and encouraging other women to share their experiences with the group. SC Treatment: Medical and nutritional treatment for severely malnourished children and infants in the stabilization Centre will be conducted according to the standard inpatient treatment protocols. Admissions to the SC will be children who are severely malnourished and who have bilateral pitting oedema grade +++ or any grade with medical complication. Children will be discharged when the medical condition is stabilized and they are able to eat well or have passed the appetite test. All discharges will be through the OTP where they will continue with treatment and follow-up. All children discharged from the SC will be given a priority for outreach follow-up during their rst week in the OTP. Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP): Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months, children meeting the criteria (MUAC >=11.5 cm - <12.5cm) will be admitted in TSFP and treated with Ready to Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) and pregnant and lactating mothers who meet the criteria (MUAC <23 cm) will be admitted and treated with super cereal plus (CSB++). Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN): provision of quality MIYCN and micronutrient supplementation (VitA and deworming) services in nutrition sites and community level. PLWs attending the feeding centres and within the community will be targeted for individual and group counselling (mother-to-mother support groups) on IYCF practices, following the UNICEF recommended approach Multi-sectoral intervention. Specific multi-sectoral activities are to increase access to safe water and sanitation, promote critical WASH messages, and integrate early childhood development strategies in nutrition treatment sites. Kitchen gardening, screening and treating of malaria, managing diarrhoea, and mainstreaming child protection and gender-based violence risk mitigation will also be promoted at the nutrition sites. In addition, advocacy efforts will be undertaken to integrate inpatient care of severe acute malnutrition in the health system. The use of community platforms through the community nutrition volunteers will be supported as a main vehicle for delivering multi-sectoral services at community and household levels. Tearfund is currently implementing UNICEF, WFP and BHA funded Integrated Lifesaving Support Project (ILSP) to IDPs and Returnees in South Sudan supporting nutrition, WASH and FSL projects in Pochalla, Uror and Twic East Counties of Jonglei state. Each county has well equipped storage facilities and full time. Nutrition staff as well as a network of IYCF groups, Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and community-based staff. It has been supporting respective County Health Departments (CHD) in identifying and treating acutely malnourished under ve years old children, and pregnant and lactating women as an immediate life saving measure while at the same time tackling barriers that cause poor utilization of health and nutrition services.
