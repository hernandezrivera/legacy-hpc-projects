Project Summary

This project aims to reduce morbidity and mortality resulting from waterborne diseases by increasing access to safe water, sanitation and better hygiene practices among displaced and other vulnerable communities in states most affected by disease outbreaks such as cholera, locality with high levels of malnutrition and food insecurity.
In BN and WN State, HA-UK will continue to provide access to safe water in communities suffering from malnutrition above emergency thresholds for IDPs as well as the vulnerable host communities impacted by the presence of IDPs through context specific technologies such as surface water treatment systems and sustainable Point of Use water treatment solutions appropriate. HA-UK will provide means for safe excreta disposal through the installation of gender appropriate sanitation facilities and will conduct hygiene promotion activities to support effective behavior change.

1 - Needs

Blue Nile and White Nile states remains one of the affected states in Sudan. the lives of the people in the state have been disrupted severely. Among the states, the incidences of poverty and need for humanitarian assistance is one of the highest, and at times greatest, in Blue Nile. Mass displacement is a general feature of humanitarian crisis in BN. The 2021 HNO estimates that 13.540 persons are currently IDPs Kurmuk and Bau localities in Blue Nile state sand the trend of displacement continues. Safe drinking water and sanitation in Blue Nile remains a huge humanitarian issue which has gone through difficult turn that affected it negatively especially in places where people need it most, such as Russeris locality. This situation has become more worsen and difficult after the eruption of the Blue Nile conflict in September 2011, where the majority of people in rural areas fled from homes leaving their water sources and sanitation facilities in schools, clinics and other institutions exposed to damage and miss use. Recently a Multi sectoral Joint Assessment Mission including State Ministry of Planning and public utilities (WES), NNGOs, INGOs and UN-agencies were tasked to verify the humanitarian situation, including Water , Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), where the assessment covered five localities; namely Kurmuk, Bau, Geissan, Russeris and Attadamon localities. The joint assessment has focused on verifying the water sources facilities including hand pumps, hand dug wells and hafeers (artificial bonds) and as well the sanitation facilities and hygiene situation. The JAM revealed that there is extreme huge gaps in term of accessibility to safe drinking water where only 29% have access to safe water in these localities especially Kurmuk and Bau localities which targeted by this project, on the other hand only 8% have access to safe sanitation, where as hygiene practices were found obsolete. This situation poisoned Blue Nile State as the worst among other states in term of WASH services. The assessment also indicated that the poor sanitation environment has resulted in increasing different water and hygiene inter-related diseases specially among children and women, where the assessment showed that in many of the surveyed villages, many water reservoirs (traditional hafeers) are without fences but used by people and at the same time for animal watering. In summary the assessment has showed a high need for safe drinking water and sanitation facilities in schools, clinics as well as households.

HA-UK seeks to provide public information to the beneficiaries about the programmes provided through local government, community outreach and facility based awareness and hygiene promotion activities. HA-UK consults with local authorities, community leaders and staff regarding decisions to commence, adapt or complete programmes. Emergency Response Team assessments include key informant interviews and group discussions within communities and HA-UK uses household surveys to assess programme coverage. Every staff member working with HA-UK in Sudan receives an orientation on the Code of Conduct and has to sign it, together with a “Summary of Minimum Standards for the Protection of Women and Children Against Sexual Abuse and Exploitation” which form part of the National and International Staff Guidelines. HA-UK also has Fraud and Misconduct Notification Guidelines in place.
