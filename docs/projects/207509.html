Project Summary

In 2024, WVB will continue the implementation to address the gaps in education, especially for the children, and adolescents aged between 3 to 18 years living in the Camps and Host Communities in Ukhiya, and Teknaf at Camps 13, and 19 and extend to 27 and Host Communities where the number of targeted beneficiaries will be 1990. WVB will continue the ECD intervention and start the Myanmar Curriculum, Adolescent and Youth Learning, and Skill activities at the same locations. WVB will sustain its Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLF) modality and establish, use, or renovate ECD centers in the project locations. WVB concentrates on implementing the Myanmar Curriculum, and Adolescent and Youth learning facilities. Community Support Group/Learning Center Management Committee supports identifying the learners and potential Facilitators in the camps and host communities. This project includes an evident disability inclusion focus, aiming to identify and assess the disability status of learners attending learning facilities and cater individualized support for those most vulnerable to reduce learners dropping out. In 2024, WVB will ensure the quality of learning opportunities following the Education Sector and WVB NO’s guidance and will emphasize updating and upgrading teaching and learning materials ensuring inclusion, and gender sensitivity across the learning facilities. The project focuses on community engagement to reduce gender inequality to ensure quality education in all aspects of the project implementation.
WVB will build capacities of facilitators of ECD and Myanmar Curriculum including Pre-vocational/Vocational skill training, especially soft skills. WVB will involve and support the development of gender-responsive pedagogy, inclusive education, safeguarding and PSEA theme-based training, crisis-sensitive education, and planning to improve learning outcomes of refugee and host communities’ learners. WVB will identify and build the capacities of Rohingya women to become Myanmar Curriculum teachers and create a pool of female facilitators. The project team and MEAL team of WVB will conduct regular assessments to monitor and track the enrolled learners' progress. The project will invite the Education Sector's colleagues to share learning.
WVB will continue to emphasize community ownership by engaging them and strengthening their capacity enhancing participation in all the education activities for children, adolescents, and youths. Parents, Parents Support Group, and faith leaders will receive training on gender, girls' education, disability inclusion, child protection, and safeguarding. WVB will support the Community Support Group in preparing emergency response plans related to Learning. By implementing aforesaid interventions, the proposed WVB project will reach a total of 1990 children, categorized into 1140 children aged 36-59 months participating in the ECD program and 850 children aged 5-18 engaged in the Myanmar Curriculum.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The J-MSNA report 2023 (REACH_BGD2301_ MSNA) shows that the age-disaggregated enrolment rates of the host community boys and girls are 91% (5-11 years), and 72% (12-18 yr). This rate in the Rohingya camp community for boys & girls (5-11 years) is 85% & 90 % simultaneously and for the age group 12-18 years is 51% boys & 21% girls. 72% (12-18 yrs). This scenario says that the boys’ enrolment rate in the host community is significantly higher than camp community. In the case of girls’ enrolment rate aged 5-11 years the camp community is significantly higher than the host community. The enrolment rate of girls aged group 12-18 years in both communities is the same. In the age group of 5-11, both boys and girls from the host community show higher attendance rates compared to their Rohingya counterparts (the difference is 6% boys & 1% girls). It also revealed that the attendance rate of boys aged 12-18 from the Rohingya community is 51% compared to the host community's 72%, while girls from the Rohingya community aged 12-18 have a strikingly lower 21% attendance rate compared to 72% from the host community. Gaps also remain in access to education, in particular among adolescents, with the vast majority of individuals aged 15 and older reportedly not having access to learning opportunities. This gap is even more pronounced for female learners (bangladesh_2023_jrp_rhc_appeal_en.pdf). The limited range of educational and economic opportunities exposes them to exploitation, and increased exposure to protection risks, and can lead to negative coping mechanisms. The % of early education-aged children (referred to as aged 3-4 for refugee and 4 for host community) not attending early education programme during the 2022-2023 school year- In Host Community: Attending-22% & Not attending-78% in education. In Refugees community: Attending-54 & Not attending-46% in education. The host community reflects that 78% of these children are not engaging in early education, while in the refugee community, the number stands at 46%. These figures underscore an urgent need for increased access to and participation in early education programs for both communities. WVB addresses the main challenges and needs of Rohingya refugees and host community children, especially girls and children with disabilities, to access equitable and gender-responsive quality learning/education projects. J-MSNA report findings and needs would be the precondition of the project implementation. It is found that the enrolment and attendance rate of Children with Disabilities are lower than their peers of all age groups. Parent awareness, negative attitudes, transportation, toilets, sitting arrangements, and social stigma are the major barriers to ensuring enrolment in the learning center. In the Camps, the majority of female teachers are from the host community and are Bangladeshi. Female Rohingya teachers only comprise 25% of the total. According to the needs, the Mayanmar curriculum needs to be developed and functional in coordination with the Education Sector.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The education projects of WVB will follow the rationalization principles and procedures guided by the Education Sector. WV will continue its project implementation at Camps 13 and 19 with ECD interventions which have the maximum coverage with a reputation for working with both the CiC and community-level stakeholders. WVB will ensure the implementation of services in specific blocks (Block A & G of Camp 13 and Block A of Camp 19. WVB will work on the Myanmar Curriculum of Block A & G at Camp 13 following the integrated approach. This project targets 3-18-year-old learners to implement Education interventions like ECD, Myanmar curriculum, Adolescent and youth programming focusing on Functional Literacy and Numeracy; Life skills including; Capacity building for both the project staff and teachers; and community engagement. WVB will participate in camp-level coordination and maintain a good relationship with Government officials. The education project will carry on the education activities and maintain the quality of implementation that will be finalized by the Education Sector through a rationalization process.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

World Vision has the Humanitarian Program Accountability Framework (PAF) and Accountability Operating Guideline (AOG) aligning with global standards covering CHS, IASC, etc. Following organizational policy each year WVB conducts an annual multi-sector need assessment (MSNA) and accountability & safeguarding assessment where AAP needs and recommended actions are captured. Projected people's needs will be given priority without any discrimination or bias. A detailed AAP plan and progress tracker will be followed over the project duration. Standard Operating Procedures and tools on AAP will be developed and agreed with all key stakeholders. Key activities will be 1. Strengthening the capacity of all staff, volunteers, and CMC members on AAP, 2. Selecting learners inclusively following agreed criteria through engaging community peoples, 3. Providing project & AAP related and asked information among community peoples, 4. Consultation meetings with learners, parents, CMC, facilitators, and others will be conducted, 5. Promoting community peoples’ participation in implementation, monitoring & maintenance through volunteerism, community platforms like children's forums, CMC, parent groups, etc. 6. Establishing feedback & complaint channels (feedback box, help desk, face-to-face interviews in the community, hotline, etc.) 7. Conducting learning workshops with all relevant stakeholders, 8. Conduct joint monitoring, 9. Providing community-friendly IEC materials focused on beneficiaries’ entitlement, project activities, beneficiary criteria, free assistance facility, feedback & complaint mechanism, frequently asked & answer tool, etc. 10. Disseminating AAP-related messages, 11. Continuous monitoring and improvement of AAP activities.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

WVB has a disaster preparedness and response plan for the Rohingya Response Program. WVB has also three cyclone shelters in Cox’s Bazar. Already WVB has implemented DRR projected activities in both Teknaf & Ukhiya upazillas covering both Camp & host communities partnering with WFP. The key activity under this education sector will be orientation for projected community peoples including Facilitators & CMC members on Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) issues. The project will conduct a brief environmental examination (BEE) for each intervention before execution. The project will arrange awareness and campaign programs, and day observation to make the community aware of disaster response and reduction and prepare the mitigation strategies. IEC materials and messaging will be also ensured aiming to increase awareness of DRR.
