Project Summary

HRSS has been working with vulnerable conflict affected communities across South Sudan to realize their full potential and build resilience to create environments in which local population can thrive and overcome situations that adversely affect their livelihood by providing humanitarian aid and support.
This project will be implemented in Panyijiar and Guit Counties of Unity State targeting 15,000 beneficiaries (10% in cash based intervention) IDPs, returnees and host community (women, men and youth), due to continued conflict, population has been disrupted and trapped in villages of Panyijiar and Guit not able to access basic facilities including market where they can buy food or replenish farm tools and equipment, while community fully depends on fishing, there is currently no available fishing gear to carry out this activity, community struggling to make their needs fit within little available resources has resulted in to destitution and malnutrition among young children and elderly persons among these communities, every efforts being made to stabilise situation has never been successful since the community lack basic tools and seeds leading to a very severe food insecurity, this project contributes to cluster objective no.2 which is set to increase vulnerable household food production to protect the erosion of livelihoods through agricultural seeds and fishing inputs.
The project aims at improving food security by increasing capacities of the affected people through training on best modern farming practices besides, increase kitchen gardens distribution of improved quick maturing vegetable crops alongside hand tools in both Panyijiar and Guit counties. During the project 15,000 beneficiaries (9,000 female and 6,000 male) will be provided with assorted vegetable kits out of which 650 (380 women and 270 men) will be trained on kitchen gardening and expected to act as farmer promoters in kitchen gardening practices, the training will as well take care of on nutritious value of vegetables. During distribution HRSS will ensure that complain and feedback desk is made available for the beneficiaries, and a post distribution monitoring will be done after. A cash based programming will be necessary for improving resilience in both Panyijiar and Guit. The project implementation strategies will include: capacity development for sustainability; protection programming; area-based development approach; Do no harm, conflict sensitivity planning, and gender and youth mainstreaming and empowerment. This project will also be integrated with other cluster projects such as, CCCM, WASH and Shelter/NFIs, child protection for joint missions and other projects that HRSS are undertaking in the same location such as Protection and Returnee Monitoring in Leer and Panyijiar supported by UNHCR, CCCM in Panyijiar and Leer supported by SSHF and UNHCR respectively, Police Community Relations and Oil for Development as part of protection supported by UNDP and NPA respectively in Guit among others. The project will be guided by the following principles and in relation to AAP: leadership and empowerment of women and girls; women and girls engaged in all aspects of the space; safety and accessibility; integrated in community and contextually relevant; inclusivity - all women and girls consulted – especially marginalized; coordination for multi-sectorial support and services – and referral; outreach to communities to encourage women and girls to participate – and activities organized in communities; feedback mechanisms in place for women and girls – and for community; safety and ethically data collection and management; sustainability of FFS – including transition and exit strategy.

1 - Needs

ccording to an Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) update, released jointly by the Government of the Republic of South Sudan, the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), the United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF) and the World Food Programme (WFP), 54 percent of people in South Sudan are still severely food insecure. The report estimates that 10 000 people are currently in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and facing an extreme lack of food, while about 1.7 million are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and another 4.6 million people are experiencing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) levels of acute food insecurity. According to IPC report released on 11th September 2019, the food security situation slightly improved during the 2019 lean season compared to the 2018 lean season. This improvement was driven by reduced conflict and improved access to livelihoods, markets and humanitarian assistance. However, In August 2019, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). In the former Unity State, the food security situation has slightly improved compared to same time last year, and an estimated 660,000 people, representing 62.3% of the State population are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity due to improved security that has permitted greater household access to fish, wild foods, markets and humanitarian assistance. Despite these slight improvements, the food security situation remains a concern in the four counties of Mayendit, Guit, Koch and Panyijiar in former Unity State, where Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes have persisted due to pockets of insecurity, cattle-raiding, high food prices, flood-related destruction of crops and disruptions to market access. In Guit and Panyijiar, food security situation is expected to deteriorate due to limited access to livestock products, early cereals depletion resulting from flood-related damages to crops and Fall Army Worms infestations. Furthermore, expected insecurity and cattle-raiding is likely to disrupt regular access to assistance and markets. Overall, an estimated 685,000 people, representing 64.9% of the State population are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) during the January to April 2020 projection period. UNIMISS reports (23rd October 2019) that as of 17 Oct. 2019, a total of number of civilians seeking safety in six Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites located on UNMISS bases is 194,954 including 115,960 in Bentiu, 32,608 in Malakal, 29,745 in Juba UN House, 1,935 in Bor, and 14,706 in the area adjacent to UNMISS in Wau. Bentiu PoC still has high population of 115,960 people. Inter-communal tensions continue to result in displacement particularly in Rubkona, Guit and Koch. Humanitarian Situation Report Issue No. 10 released between 4th and 10th MARCH, 2019 reports that according to an integrated UNMISS field monitoring mission to Bill in Guit County on 1 March, an estimated 6,000 people needed humanitarian assistance, including food, shelter, health services and clean water. Reportedly, 1,800 individuals returned from Bentiu PoC site, but went back due to the lack of services in the area. DRC’s Rapid Site Assessment report of August 2019 reveals that according to the local authorities and triangulated with service providers in the area, Guit town has seen a small but regular number of spontaneous IDP and refugee returnees in 2019. 975 individuals are reported to have returned between January and June 2019, from Sudan, Uganda, Juba and Bentiu PoC. Some people have been able to rebuild their homes, and others are IDPs staying in family/other tukuls but are unable to cope up with their present life. They need livelihood opportunities to improve their resilience.

2 - Activities

HRSS will intervene in cluster objectives 1, 2 and three with the following activities: Community mobilization and sensitization- HRSS will inform and sensitize community of the forth-coming distribution of livelihood kits ahead of distribution.Beneficiary identification and Registration- HRSS will carry out beneficiary selection in close collaboration with the main stakeholders including FSL Cluster, local authorities, rural communities and any other relevant authority as per cluster beneficiary identification criteria targeting the most vulnerable households from IDPs and host communities in Guit and Panyijiar.Preposition of seeds and tools – HRSS will receive seeds and tools from FSL cluster and preposition the supplies for distribution to the affected most vulnerable communities in Guit and Panyijiar.Distribution of livelihood kits, seeds and tools – The kits will be distributed to the affected community considering needs of the most vulnerable and people with special needs first to avoid keeping them in long queuesTraining on Kitchen gardening – HRSS will give kitchen gardening orientation to beneficiaries both in Guit and Panyijiar, the trained persons will act as lead farmers and train other community members on handling new variety of vegetable seeds including carrots, kales and onion.Post Distribution monitoring – HRSS will work with 40% of total beneficiary to carry out PDM to determine views of the beneficiaries and intention of items distributed.Protection and rehabilitation of livelihoods: Potential livelihoods options specifically targeting women will be restored to increase food availability and improve household diet diversification. 6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

All interventions to assist IDPs towards a durable solutions will uphold humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality, independence, and non-discrimination and will ensure that interventions are based on the voluntary and informed decisions and undertaken in a manner that upholds the safety and dignity of the population of concern. All assessment, analysis, and any intervention will be cognizant of the specific needs and vulnerabilities of particular individuals and communities.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will employed area -based approach to restore the rights of affected populations and place them at the center of the process where by the IDPs are in a position to make voluntary and informed decisions and IDPs are engaged and involved in processes that directly impact their well-being and participate in the planning and management of such processes and IDP's, have access to humanitarian and development actors and IDP's have effective monitoring mechanisms and as well as establish community led initiative like (livelihood and income generation club).

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project does not to any degree detract from duty that the Government has as the primary responsible party to protect IDPs and enable them to find solutions for displacement. All activities undertaken by this project will be coupled with robust advocacy to the Government to uphold their responsibilities under international law and Chapter 3 of the Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (ARCS ) does not supersede existing guidelines and established procedures for protection-mandated organizations to facilitate returns and relocation to address specific protection risks at the individual or household level.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The length of displacement in South Sudan continue to increase. The protection environment in areas where IDPs have fled remains fluid but has generally declined and there are no locations that provide absolute security. Throughout the conflict, some IDP have spontaneously returned or relocated to find better and safer solutions to their displacement. The capacity of IDPs to act on their intentions is varied with some requiring more assistance than others. IDPs have been able to pursue better solutions when they have the resources and conditions necessary to do so. Many IDPs find themselves in areas where freedom of movement is severely compromised and their resources and coping mechanisms depleted, both of which limit their ability to decide where they will reside and to act on their intentions. In general the project is aligned with national objectives.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Protection assessments will be done for areas of concentrated movement, with active participation from the host community and including analysis of housing, land and property concerns, service mapping, and identification of conditions that would compromise progress toward achieving more sustainable solutions. Assessment will also include locations that are currently not conducive for solutions to monitor the situation, identify potential risks, and to collect information to share with IDPs. Protection risks along the potential routes of travel will also be taken into account.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Transitional durable solutions that enable IDPs to improve their protection environment and take steps that will lead to more sustainable solutions need to be taken into consideration and supported. Protection driven support to individuals, households, and communities could enable IDPs exercise their right to choose the best of available solutions for their displacement. Area based approach to displacement seeks to restore the rights of affected populations and place them at the center of the process whereby, IDPs are in a position to make voluntary and informed decisions, IDPs are engaged and involved through participation, empowerment and training that directly impact their well-being and participate in the planning and management of such processes, IDPs have access to humanitarian and development actors, IDPs have effective monitoring mechanisms. This project can therefore continue beyond this support period.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. HRSS is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. HRSS is a lead national NGO on GBV in Unity State and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. HRSS with other partners have developed a referral pathway in Panyijiar, Mayendit, Guit and Rubkona, and will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools HRSS will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in-charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. HRSS staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. HRSS is also a very active member of Protection Cluster.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

HRSS will participatory implement this project in a conflict sensitive manner, ensuring that inclusive participation of all stakeholders and in particular the beneficiaries at all stages of this project cycle. Participation will be enhanced through conducting participatory needs assessment in an inclusive and participatory manner and frequent consultations of key beneficiary groups in decision making particularly women and girls. HRSS will spend at least two weeks in the field to engage and work closely with beneficiaries to set up a project implementation committee that will last throughout the project, nominated by the target beneficiaries and are composed of the beneficiaries. This committee will share with all the beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, the elderly, those who are illiterate, women and girls issues agreed upon during their meetings such as types of assistance needed, safety audits, meeting and distribution locations and sites. HRSS will also set up a distribution committee composed of protection specialists, beneficiaries and local leadership to monitor any distribution process, and to provide feedback to HRSS on issues concerning protection such as SEA, HLP issues, livelihoods, the distribution process and the whole intervention process from the beginning of the project. HRSS will also use the presence of beneficiaries to pass messages such as health, livelihoods, SGBV, HLP among others through messaging and awareness creation. The feedback team will be composed of the project implementation committee representatives, representatives of various constituencies of beneficiaries and HRSS’s protection advisor. Conducting both pre and post distribution awareness and monitoring respectively will provide feedback from the various groups of beneficiaries. Reports developed by HRSS will be available for community, partners and even government authorities to access for references. This action will address key lifesaving threats identified.
