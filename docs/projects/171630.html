Project Summary

Access to healthcare in Somalia continues to worsen due to widespread violence, and the health system remains fragmented, under-resourced, and ill-equipped to provide lifesaving and preventative services. The triple threat of floods, locust infestation, and the COVID-19 pandemic compounds the situation. In addition, Somalia has less than 40% of its target of two health facilities per 10,000 populations and has only 19% of its target skilled health workforce. Maternity services are lacking in 74% of IDP settlements. In particular, displaced and marginalized groups face barriers to healthcare and higher exposure to diseases. Somalia has a high mortality rate for children under 5 years old (U5MR) at 27.2 per 1000 live births; the leading causes are reported to be pneumonia (24%), diarrhea (19%) and measles (12%), as well as neonatal disorders (17%), which are predisposed by malnutrition.
The proposed action will be implemented through the already existing Government-MoH systems to enhance sustainability (durable solutions) through the strengthening of the health system to offer quality health services to the target populations during humanitarian crises.
The proposed intervention is aimed at contributing to reducing morbidity and mortality among host and displaced conflict-affected people in Somalia and target to reach 158,707 beneficiaries. Implementation will be at community and health facility levels.
At the community level: a large network of CHVs - currently at over 1,300 - will link beneficiary populations with catchment health facilities using the behavior change model of Care Groups. The support includes a heavy emphasis on community health care, working with community health volunteers in Care Groups, or - in areas far from primary health care facilities - with integrated community case management (iCCM) models. These volunteers increase active case finding, encourage appropriate care-seeking for vaccines and early management of the common childhood illnesses, increase utilization of reproductive healthcare services, and empower communities to adapt health social and behavioral norms.
The implementation modality will be at 12 PHC and 22 outreach sites. At the PHC facilities, Medair will continue to work with trusted local NGO partners to ensure that outpatient services, including; reproductive health services; ANC, delivery, PNC, and FP; consultations for communicable disease; basic management and referrals for NCDs, mental health, and trauma; support to EPI; pediatric care; participation in the early warning system; and outbreak response.
The interventions will aim to increase access, utilization, and coverage of essential maternal, new-born, and child health services during a crisis through a strengthened health system for better service delivery. This will be achieved through capacity building of health care workers on IDSR in order to improve their capacity to deliver quality health services; provision of essential supplies and medicines, avoiding stock-outs and ensuring availability and accessibility of quality supplies; strengthening emergency obstetric and new-born care through adequate staffing to provide BEmONC services 24/7; scale-up of immunization services (both routine immunization and campaigns during disease outbreaks). In addition, through this action, mitigation of the impact and response to shocks to the affected populations as a result of conflict and public health emergencies will be enhanced through supporting the MoH to prepare emergency response plans at regional health authority level and establishing rapid response teams (RRTs), who are trained and can promptly investigate rumors, confirm outbreaks, notify WHO and MoH, mount an aggressive response, and report promptly. It is envisaged that through these interventions, target populations will have improved access to quality healthcare services during humanitarian crisis and therefore reduced excess mortal attributable to the crisis.

Mitigation Measures

1. Medair will provide refresher training to all staff involved in project implementation on preventive measures against sexual exploitation and abuse/ protection mainstreaming and ensure they sign the code of conduct on physical sexual exploitation and abuse as a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. Medair has nominated a safeguarding officer to ensure continued sensitization, awareness and follow up on sexual exploitation and abuse of the beneficiaries. Care Group volunteers and health/nutrition workers will strive to identify passive cases in the community and report using the existing reporting mechanism and also continue dissemination of behavior change communication key messages on sexual violence and exploitation. Trauma care, counseling and first aid will be provided to the survivors (victims) and staff trained on clear referral pathways in coordination with the gender-based violence actors in the target communities. Medair recognizes women and children are among the most vulnerable groups to sexual violence during conflicts and ensure they are aware of their rights to access treatment and support due to sexual violence. 2. Medair has an efficient and effective feedback mechanism in place for suggestions and complaints that is accessible, confidential and well understood by beneficiaries. The system has been established with a view to obtaining feedback to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Beneficiaries are encouraged to report any incidents through the established reporting mechanisms: either through their community leaders, assigned household volunteers or through calling the established toll-free hotline #3377. Timely responses are provided to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. A dedicated feedback and complaint focal point immediately communicates to the senior management team for prompt follow-up action including investigations and further decisions making. Ongoing awareness and sensitization sessions are held at the health facilities through the monitoring and evaluation officer/nutrition facility workers and in the community through the Care Group volunteers and community health workers where beneficiaries are informed on the mechanism in place and how it works. All Medair and partners’ staff/nutrition staff, and community volunteers will be trained on Medair’s Code of Conduct and required to confirm their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients.Exit interviews will be conducted amongst the targeted beneficiaries to understand their needs and concerns in relation to the quality of services they received and to address these whenever possible.3. Medair will ensure that members of minorities, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), have systematic participation and representation in consultation meetings with local leaders during assessments to understand their needs. Consultations will involve both host communities and the IDPs. Nutrition facilities will be open six days a week for 8 hours and will ensure access for both the IDPs and the host communities. Contacts will be established with the local leaders from the marginalized communities. A hot-line number will be provided where the community can provide feedback and share concerns. Community leaders will be informed of the importance of all groups being represented in the village health committees. Consultation with the minority groups (women, boys, girls and people with disabilities) will be carried out to identify their specific needs. Community leaders are encouraged to advocate for the minority groups in their locations and awareness-raising and sensitization will be carried out amongst health staff to prevent discrimination.
