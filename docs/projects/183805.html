Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen the resilience and coping capacities of vulnerable mobile populations, including Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees and their host community to address food insecurity and enhance livelihood security. This will be achieved through delivering agricultural and livestock related income generating activities with special attention to small-scale farmers, IDPs, returnees and other vulnerable groups such as youths and women. IOM will apply a community centered approach to promote community initiatives to assist durable solutions and ensure that planned interventions are met, based on needs and the rights of the vulnerable migrants and community, to access improved economic opportunities.
The project will focus on 14 localities: Wad Al Mahi locality (Blue Nile), Yassin locality (East Darfur), Al Fao and Galabat Ash-Shargiah localities (Gedaref), Reifi Khashm Elgirba (Kassala), El fasher and Tawilla localities (North Darfur), Buram, Rehaid Albirdi, Tulus and Um Dafoug localities (South Darfur), Delami locality (South Kordogan), Jebel Moon and Sirba localities (West Darfur). Returnees, IDPs and local communities in these localities are vulnerable to food insecurity and limited livelihood opportunities due to limited purchasing power resulting from inflation and high staple food prices. According to HNO data in 2021, the selected localities have been suffering from the high prevalence in Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 3 (Crisis) and/or phase 4 (Emergency) further highlighting the need for timely and adequate livelihood interventions to address food insecurity, strengthen community resilience to protect lives, and move towards economic self-sufficiency.
Under the HRP 2022, The response plans under the project are in line with FSL Cluster Objective 2: Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives; and objective 3: Improve communities' capacity to sustain households’ livelihoods through the rehabilitation and building of productive infrastructure as well as supporting services. The planned interventions will include: (1) provision of agricultural support to the most vulnerable farmers with capacity training for local production cooperatives and inputs on agriculture employing climate adaptation techniques; (2) support sustainable livestock husbandry practice and veterinary services to assist household asset building; (3) small business support for youth and women on agriculture and other livelihood skills such as food processing, brick making, welding activities to enhance community resilience and reinvigorate local economies through the creation of small businesses.
IOM has already established a good access to most of target localities, and seeks to continue implementing community stabilization and durable solution initiative activities under UNDAF outcomes.
The planned interventions will be delivered in a way to complement past and on-going activities implemented, thereby strengthening links between the HRP Outcomes and UNDAF Outcomes.

1 - Needs

The combination of the economic crisis, natural shocks such as floods, inter-communal conflicts and the negative impacts of COVID-19 are impacting households and communities’ ability to engage in livelihood activities in Sudan. The high price of agricultural inputs and food has undermined food security and livelihood situations. The economic crisis has led to a drop in people’s purchasing power and reduced their ability to purchase enough food and essential basic services. This is particularly relevant for small scale farmers, IDPs, returnees and host communities affected by human displacement and inter-tribal conflicts. Growing constraints on access to sustainable income generating activities have added to the high level of vulnerability in the targeted localities. Approximately 7.3 million Sudanese were estimated to be facing crisis levels of food insecurity (the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) phase 3 and above) during between April and May, 2021 (22 percent increase from 2020). These figures are the highest ever recorded in the history of IPC analysis in Sudan. This includes about 1.8 million people facing emergency acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4). Furthermore, despite the efforts being invested in peace, the escalation of communal clashes/conflicts in Darfur, and in areas bordering Darfur as well as Kassala, Red Sea States was reported and are leading to new displacement. According to IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) report, close to 198,000 people have fled their homes in the three states of West, North and South Darfur in 2021. The humanitarian situation is notably worse for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and local communities, especially among women and youth for whom a lack of access to livelihood opportunities and basic services continue to remain a main concern. The influx of returnee and movements of IDPs and seasonal migrants combined with deterioration of economic are causing a loss of lives and livelihoods, and increasing humanitarian needs. This is adding further pressure on the usage of local natural resources of host communities. Since 2016, IOM began working in return and displacement areas of the project target locations such as Wad Al Mahi, Yassin, Al Fao and Galabat Ash-Shargiah, Tawilla and EL Fasher, Buram and Um Dafoug and Delami localities. These locations are facing crisis and emergency levels of food insecurity and align with the IPC projection 1 priority (June and September 2021)- 5-10 % of population of each locality fall under IPC Phase 4 and 15-20% are classified in Phase 3 and higher. IOM’s need assessments further highlighted that community members in these areas noted that most of the communities rely heavily on agriculture and livestock as a source of food and income while many of them are selling local agricultural and livestock products for a short-term income to acquire enough foods. Despite its' self-identified importance, local production of crops and livestock remains a low and limited source of self-subsistence due to fluctuating weather patterns, deteriorating grazing lands and poor farming and livestock practices that do not meet the local market demands. Post-harvest losses are also a major, constraint on farmers’ profitability. Furthermore, many of female headed households in the target localities still rely on unstable income sources such as firewood collection and charcoal making which have a potential of environmental damage, with limited ownership of common and productive assets. They also adapt food based negative coping strategy- such as selling the last remaining female animals to acquire enough food, which is leading to irreversible loss of livelihoods. Hence, there is a need for timely and adequate livelihood interventions for the most vulnerable group of small-scale farmers, IDPs, returnees and other non-agricultural livelihood supports for those who currently rely on volatile and unsustainable income generating activities and food sources.

2 - Activities/Outputs

All proposed activities will be coordinated with the relevant government authorities and the sector leading agencies and other livelihood partners working in the same areas. IOM will support each locality utilizing a needs-based approach. Activities I.1. Set-up new or re-activate and train local livelihood management committee for each locality (ten committees: 10-20 members for each committee). 2. Provision of agricultural support to the most vulnerable farmer with capacity training for local production cooperatives and inputs on agriculture employing climate adaptation techniques: This will be made through: • Distribution of improved seeds and farming tools for a total of 200 households (HHs) per locality and this includes agricultural training (5-7 days) for the selected beneficiaries by the trained cooperatives members. • Establishment of drip irrigation vegetable farm using locally available materials for a total of 50 female headed HHs per locality. The interventions will include the distribution of vegetable seeds, training on establishment and management of drip irrigation farms.• Other agricultural inputs such as small water pumps for irrigation farm, motorcycles (tuktuk) and or/donkey with donkey carts to support transportation of agricultural products to local markets, as needed. This will be donated to the local livelihood management committees and they will be responsible for maintaining the donated items. 3. Support sustainable livestock husbandry practice and veterinary services to assist household asset building and this will be done through: • Provide upgraded veterinary care equipment and refresher training targeting a total 60 Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) (5-10 CAHWs from each locality). • Provide vaccinated healthy local livestock (goats) with training on better husbandry practice and livestock health for female headed households (HHs)- At least 30 households per locality. • Establishment of household chicken farm with business training for 30 female headed HHs per locality 4. Small business support for youth and women on agriculture and other livelihood skills to enhance community resilience and reinvigorate local economies through the creation of small businesses. Such activities may include but not be limited to: • Provide food processing and conservation based on local agricultural productions and addressing key components of nutritional care for women (PLWs) and children for 25 female headed HHs per locality.• Establishment of welding workshop for a total of 25 youths per locality.• Establishment of traditional brick making workshop for a total of 25 youths per locality.• Other income generating activities identified through community consultation process. The proposed project will maintain a level of flexibility to determine and design livelihood interventions according to community priority and results of feasibility and local market analysis. 6. Monitoring and evaluation (M&E)• Enhancing monitoring and continuous supervision for the trained beneficiaries per livelihood activities. • After the end of the project (three months period), conduct impact assessment on facilitating a sustainable income generating opportunity including cost-benefit analysis. The evaluation should also highlight lessons learnt and good practices. A general cost benefit analysis for the delivered livelihoods interventions demonstrating that the activities can successfully generate income in a reasonable period of time after the investments in inputs and materials. The cost benefit analysis should be at the household level and show that income from the investments exceeds the cost of the investments in equipment and input themselves.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Interventions mainly targeted towards supporting agricultural inputs, seeds and tools to farmers seek to mitigate the impact of climate change and support livelihood. For instance, agricultural trainings were implemented through a resilience-oriented approach in an effort to strengthen coping capacities to tackle future climate and environmental change. The establishment of vegetable farms with a locally made drip irrigation system was completed with the goal of introducing the community to alternative solutions/“water-smart” innovations. This can ensure better yields and increase water use efficiency while ensuring water productivity. Furthermore, this is best suited for small scale farmers due to its simplicity and ease of maintenance and operation. Local agricultural cooperatives will also be capacitated to be able to face shocks in supply value chains in the face of the ongoing economic crisis and assist in the provision of animal treatments, feed and supplements, specifically targeting herders whose movements are restricted, and support livelihood diversification to vulnerable communities.

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will employ a right based approach to promote the protection and human rights of migrants. The project will pay special attention to returnees, IDPs in vulnerable situations including, youths and women as well as local/hosting communities who do not have adequate access to basic services and vulnerable to food and livelihood security. The project will empower these groups by involving themin the decision-making processes through a people-centered approach. The project will focus on community consultation process during the project design and implementation and this will ensure that the proposed/delivered livelihood activities are met with the needs of target beneficiaries. A broad range of mobility issues will be discussed during the community consultative process, ranging from protection threats faced by vulnerable returnees, IDPs, women and youths, for example what causes their displacement and vulnerabilities and economic disparities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase -immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants/returnees to the implementation of the interventions. The proposed project is built around a community-centred and participatory approach to ensure that the communities themselves are enablers and drivers of their own resilience, recovery and development. Accountability will also be promoted through capacity development activities for relevant government stakeholders, community leaders and leaders from women and youth groups, etc. As part of capacity development, the project will establish the community management committees or re-activate the existing committees and the key responsibility of committee will be to coordinate and monitor overall project implementation as well as obtain feedback from the project beneficiaries. Women’s representation will be assured within the committees, and female committee members will be encouraged to report to IOM’s focal point if protection threats such as harassment sexual exploitation and abuse are reported by target beneficiaries. If needed, IOM will provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counselling and medical assistance to such victimsand this will be done in coordination with other partners in protection sector.
