Project Summary

VERSION 2:
This is to highlight the arrangements to resume critical life-saving activities in Sudan, as and where security allows. When the resumption of operations becomes possible, WFP will engage in three key pillars of work:
A. Resume life-saving assistance to some or all of WFP’s pre-existing 4.9 million relief caseload including 700K Refugees. Targeted beneficiaries include food-insecure IDPs, refugees and resident population.
B. Meet new food security and nutrition needs, where feasible.
C. Provide critical common services to humanitarian partners (Emergency Telecommunications Cluster, Food Security Cluster, Logistics Cluster, UNHAS).
WFP resumed critical food assistance on 2 May 2023 to a proportion of the existing relief and nutrition caseload, where WFP has assured security, capacity, access, stocks and partners. The focus is:
Gradual provision of general food assistance (GFA) with the aim to reach 4.9 million food-insecure IDPs, refugees and resident population, over the next 2-3 months.
Given the highly volatile context, a gradual approach will be applied. As a first relief response, a two-month in-kind food ration covering May and June will be provided to refugees (100% ration) in White Nile, Kassala and Gedaref States.
There is a possibility that food commodities in warehouses may be looted if not distributed urgently. Accordingly, only in-kind food will be distributed in this preliminary response (no CBT at this stage with a particular concern on liquidity). While in-kind food relief is being prioritized for the initial response, CBT preparedness options are being concurrently developed.

VERSION 2:Since the outbreak of armed conflict, more than 1,042,114 individuals have been newly internally displaced in seventeen states, including nearly 249,000 in West Darfur, 205,000 in White Nile, and 163,000 in River Nile, per the International Organization for Migration’s Displacement Tracking Matrix. At nearly 720,000 people displaced, Khartoum has been the largest state of origin. BUT NO CHANGES HAVE BEEN NOTICED WITH REGARD TO THE CASELOAD OF REFUGEES HENCE THE NEEDS AND PLANS ARE THE SAME AND NO CHANGES TILL FURTHER NOTICE.WFP Sudan plans to initially reach nearly 1,015,056 IDPs, refugees, and those from vulnerable host communities immediately with two months of life-saving food assistance, using immediately available resources —security, capacity and access-related considerations permitting— across Blue Nile, Central Darfur, East Darfur, Gezira, Gedaref, Kassala, North Darfur, Northern, Red Sea, South Darfur, South Kordofan and White Nile. Additionally, WFP will increase its caseload for the nutritional support to meet the anticipated increase in prevention- and treatment needs for children under-five and pregnant and breastfeeding women experiencing moderate acute malnutrition. This marks the first time for WFP Sudan to provide unconditional emergency food assistance in Gezira and Northern, where many have been displaced to from other neighboring states, including Khartoum, in the aftermath of current events in Sudan. While activities are being implemented through NGO implementing partners across other states, the interventions in Gezira are hosted directly by staff from the World Food Programme.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The programme will involve unconditional food assistance to the refugee community in various locations across Sudan. WFP will use community-based nutrition centers as platforms for treating moderate acute malnutrition using a targeted supplementary feeding programme and food-based prevention of acute and chronic malnutrition. The unconditional food distribution will result in waste generation requiring a simplified waste management plan. The environmental significance is however very low, and the impacts can be mitigated/eliminated with the application of best waste management practices. And the partners implementing following WFP Environmental Policy and Guideline. In cooperation with other agencies, WFP contributed to the climate vulnerability assessment that examined climate vulnerability with a focus on the implications of vulnerability to climate change on the environment, energy and food security situation of targeted communities. The methodology for the assessment was based mainly on remote sensing techniques. The types of data and information used included historical records on precipitation, temperature, vegetation, land-use/land-cover (LULC), flood events and population. The idea is to use the long-term mean and coefficient of variation of the inter and intra-annual (e.g., monthly and annual) assigned climate parameters to detect the potential impact of climate risks and vulnerability. The assessment identifies the climate risks based on observed climatic trends and variability that have contributed to the vulnerability of the target areas. The analysis established that some states are prone to high rainfall variability. Vegetation productivity proxies showed a variable trend. Understanding of the vegetation dynamics is expected to help for better planning of livelihood activities to a wide array of beneficiaries in regions where the majority of the population depends on natural vegetation and agriculture to secure their livelihood. The general pattern of the LUL changes that took place is rapid increase in bare land and a reduction in natural vegetation. The increasing trend of bare land is expected to increase the risk of erosion of fertile soil from farmlands and might create hotspots of desertification. The concentration of the humanitarian population in the most populated parts of the states is expected to create high pressure on the natural resources and the surrounding environment. Based on the findings, a set of recommendations was made to be taken in project design. Under the current variable climate conditions careful management of WASH intervention is needed that take into account the amount and quality of water supply that might diminish due to droughts. Local population should play an active role in managing and maintaining water facilities.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In alignment with the WFP Humanitarian Protection Policy, WFP remains committed to ensure that the assistance does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of the people it serves. In the design phase, WFP conducts in-depth consultations to key stakeholders for needs collection and inform beneficiaries and other relevant individuals of their entitlements (e.g. what to be served at what time for how long) to ensure that everyone is aware of the WFP’s assistance. In the implementation phase, regular visits to and monitoring at project sites will take place throughout the project period by dedicated staff including senior gender and protection associates who are based in Area Offices with support from four officers in gender and protection team based in Khartoum.
