1 - Needs

Conflict affected persons in NE Nigeria face severe living conditions across the BAY states. Yet, the epidemic and the responding government restrictions has further exacerbated the living conditions of IDP and vulnerable demographic groups. For example, the reduction in the physical presence of most humanitarian actors on the ground is translating to decreased humanitarian service delivery to the affected population. Thus, as conditions worsen DRC anticipates that those most impacted by the situation will be forced to ignore public health measures in order to access basic services for survival. Such risky behavior will further expose individuals and communities to COVID19, putting further strain on, especially public health, services. WASH services are a critical component to limit the spread of the disease. Yet, across NE Nigeria WASH coverage continues to be below SPHERE standards in densely populated hard-to-reach areas, such as Dikwa (14,877) and Gwoza (90,019) . Although spatial variation in coverage exists, the data provided to the WASH Sector is limited to areas covered by humanitarian actors and is based on an average figure by camp. This methodology masks spatial disparities in access to WASH services and does not reflect the real situation on the ground, especially in terms of equal access to services. For example, according to the latest WASH services gap analysis (Jan 2020), in Dikwa LGA the water supply average per capita was listed as 32 litres/person/day (lpd) in the camps. Yet, several IDPs in that camp were reported to have had access to less than 15 lpd. Even for the camps with above average WASH coverage, there was evidence of internal unequitable WASH coverage. While negative health impacts resulting from spatial variations of WASH services are hard to quantify at the moment, it is certain that insufficient and unequal access to WASH services is an important vulnerability factor during the COVID19 outbreak, especially in high proximity settings. Hard-to-reach camps and settlements are mostly crowded beyond SPHERE standards due to limited space for expansion. Even in cases where expansion through land agreements has been made, the governing authorities seldom allow for expansion citing security concerns. Frequent data on population density inside camps is limited, however, many are visibly overcrowded in camps, like Dikwa where makeshift shelters in close proximity to one another are rampant. The government proposal to mitigate COVID19 through social distancing cannot be observed in camps where overcrowding is prevalent. It is impractical to enforce in most camp settings due to existing dynamics resulting from settlement characteristics and shelter typology. With a poorly functioning public health system, and weak existing structures to cater to basic social services of the population, a spike in COVID19 cases in Borno and Adamawa States will further exacerbate the vulnerability of the population. The state governments are ill prepared to deal with such a situation, which is why the attention of the State health authorities have been focused on prevention. Containment would prove a very difficult task for regions already facing numerous humanitarian challenges. DRC has consulted various health actors on the ground in Borno and Adamawa states and can conclude that necessary infection prevention and control (IPC) measures are lacking to address the threat posed by the coronavirus. The lack of personal protective equipment (PPE) and general preparedness amongst various health facilities is frightening; leaving health facility staff and patients at a high risk of infection.

2 - Activities

Considering the diverse needs for WASH interventions to prepare and mitigate against the risk of infection of COVID19, DRC will employ a flexible approach for the proposed WASH activities. At the inception, rapid assessments will be conducted to assess the existing needs in collaboration with existing health and WASH actors in Dikwa (FHI 360, UNICEF, ICRC, and State actors), Gwoza (IRC, MSF, UNICEF, ICRC, and State actors) as well as those in the proposed locations in Adamawa State. Changes in the needs of the population will be monitored and appropriate action taken to address them. Particular attention will be given to vulnerable populations and those who will be potentially discriminated against regarding people’s real or perceived COVID19 status. As the threat of this new coronavirus evolves, people’s knowledge and beliefs are expected to change, and so ongoing assessments and monitoring will ensure that interventions remain relevant to people at risk, while being as much flexible and adaptable to prevailing situations DRC intends to rehabilitate (72) water points as opposed to construction of new ones while ensuring continued operation and maintenance of 100 other water points. Rehabilitation measures for broken water facilities will include replacing broken riser pipes and worn out valves. Rehabilitation of existing water points will result in higher per capita water supply as well as reducing distance travelled and queuing time spent at water points by women and girls. DRC, through the WASH committees, will sensitize communities about how to access water points being mindful of risks associated to COVID19. These communications will include promoting accessing the water point throughout the day rather than the traditional hours in the morning and evenings. This should reduce crowding at the water points. This measure is expected to ensure reduction in the number of people queuing and subsequently minimize the risks of COVID19 infections. 530 emergency latrine units as well as 530 emergency shower units will be rehabilitated. Rehabilitation works will include desludging of filled up latrines which will then allow for DRC to undertake repairs to latrine and shower units. Hygiene promotion and sensitization campaigns will focus on promotion and distribution of clear, consistent and accurate messages pertaining to COVID19 in coordination with the WASH and Health sectors. Effective community engagement will assist with identifying and addressing rumors or misinformation. Hygiene promotion and sensitization communication will be contextualized based on rapid assessment findings. In addition, communication materials will be in relevant language(s) to enhance understanding by the local communities. Community based hygiene promoters will also distribute soap to vulnerable community members. During distribution activities, DRC will conduct temperature checks using digital non-contact thermometers. Individuals identified as having above average body temperatures will be referred to relevant health providers for further medical attention.
