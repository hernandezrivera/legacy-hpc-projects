Project Summary

The proposed interventions are centrally located within all of the 2019 protection GBV, and CP cluster objectives. For 2019, WVI will build on existing protection, child protection, GBV responses that are already in place Ezaz. Projects will directly target IDPs, host community, and returnees at a household, collective centres, informal settlements, collective centres and camps in A'zaz under the following objectives:
Objective 1: The protection of population affected by the crisis is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and through advocacy with duty bearers. WVI provide quality and integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness raising, psychosocial assistance, targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives through community centers and outreach mechanisms.
Under Objective 3: Survivors have access to quality specialized GBV services and measures are in place to prevent and reduce risks of GBV. The intervention focuses on providing GBV specialized services to survivors including case management and PSS support, provide activities empowering women and adolescent’s girls and GBV prevention and mitigation activities.
Under Objective 4: Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action. The child protection teams will provide psychosocial and recreational support through structured and age appropriate group and individual activities, including recreational, skill building, and basic learning activities. These activities will also provide children with a protective and conducive environment to play, socialize, learn, express themselves and receive tailored support as they and their caregivers rebuild their lives. The child protection team will also provide psychosocial support services and activities that will reach children, youth and caregivers thereby strengthening the continuum of care for vulnerable children both inside and outside of the home. The parenting skills training will be based on a WVI parenting curriculum.

Needs

In 2018, protection issues in Syria remained widespread. Despite a steady increase of protection interventions, the magnitude of protection needs still outstrip response capacities. Life for children and women in Syria is unpredictable and extremely dangerous with indiscriminate attacks on densely populated areas becoming a defining feature of this conflict. A Space of Hope (SoH) study conducted in November 2018, provides insight into the main protection concerns and issues in North Aleppo for children, adolescents and women. In the assessed villages, 73% of respondents believe that there is a community committee who provide the basic services (such as water, sanitation, waste removal etc) and ensure these services are available and functioning sufficiently. However, 23% of respondents reported no presence of this committee in their villages, leaving a large gap to respond to basic needs. Data from the study also shows that the percentage of IDPs in relation to host communities was high in areas such as Azaz and Souran, due the high waves of displacement in the last 8 months, adding extra strain on basic services. The increase in population coupled with the unstable situation has also led to increased safety and security concerns, particularly for women, such as sexual exploitation. In the target area, and more generally across Northern Syria, women can be understood to have a subordinated role in society with poorer access to services and resources; and are more commonly subject to physical and gender-based violence and sexual violence (HNO, 2018). Women’s decision-making power is low at all levels, making the exercising basic rights (e.g. access to life saving WASH, health and social services; registering for humanitarian assistance; or claiming their property to secure shelter) extremely difficult. Boys are also under threat of GBV and sexual exploitation and older boys and young men are at risk of being recruited by armed groups and criminal gangs. The children and youth of Syria have become collateral damage in a situation beyond their control. Negative Coping mechanisms for children continue to be increasingly affected by the protracted crisis, results from HNO 2019, recently shared by the cluster, report a high occurrence of child labor preventing school attendance and early marriage, both identified as coping mechanisms (in 80% and in 45% of assessed communities respectively). Qualitative sources indicate that these negative coping mechanisms are major sources of concern for children. The results highlight how multiple and complex child protection risks continue to shape the lives of girls and boys throughout Syria. Continuous displacement, exposure to violence, deepening poverty and the persistent challenges in access and availability to services are taking a huge toll on children, and grave child rights violations remain a critical concern. In February 2018, World Vision conducted a coordinated survey which aimed to highlight the psychological impact of the Syrian conflict. Their stories included war-induced violence, displacement, and missing, separated and deceased family members. Together, the children described material and social stressors that range from situations of violence to daily struggles for basic services: conflict-induced violence, violence from caregivers, unsafe and overcrowded living conditions, working to support their families, and finally, poverty and isolation that limit access to systems of protection, education, health care, and even water. Children caught up in the Syria crisis urgently need protection, care and opportunities for learning. Helping children and their families find ways to meet these needs requires action in schools, homes, camps and communities. According to HRP 2019 PROTECTION SECTOR (v1 28 January), the protracted effects of the crisis have disrupted social services and weakened institutions; have put strain on family coping mechanisms and community safety nets

Implementation of activities

All project components will be carried out by the WVI direct staff. WVI has recently updated its global safeguarding policy and all staff will be trained and sensitized on new policy and procedures. The current staffing structure is able to support the accommodate the current and new interventions. Programming implementation is led by the Operations unit with close support and coordination with the program development along with specific technical advisors per sector. The technical advisor is responsible for ensuring and maintaining overall program quality through supervising technical staff, work with monitoring and evaluation teams to apply a protection lens to their tools and activities, liaising with partners, clusters and donors, and supporting compliant, timely, efficient and effective project implementation. To ensure that the interventions implemented are in accordance with the global standards project teams will follow standards and procedures provided by coordinators and technical advisers. As an additional layer, WVI will continue to work closely with relevant partners to strengthen the effectiveness of GBV, protection and child protection responses, harmonize coordination, and protection is integrated across responses. In all locations, WVI will identify community staff and volunteers to be trained and support programming. Where possible, project teams will work closely with local and community based organizations to build their capacity on GBV, and CP prevention and response. WVI will use a participatory approach in the development, planning and implementation of the proposed oriented toward responding to community needs. Under the overall objective of Enhancing and improving the protection of population affected by the crisis through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions, criteria will be developed in conjunction with vulnerable communities and be orientated to their priorities and contexts. In WVI’s experience, protection, GBV and CP interventions play a vital role in preventing adapting negative coping mechanisms and building resilience as well as helping communities to recover when displaced populations return home or move to new locations. To support the development of evidence¬ based programming, WVI will strengthen M&E systems for all objectives, indicators and activities. Monitoring of activities will be aligned with agreed work plans, the log frame, and performance indicators. Monthly progress reports on implementation will be submitted to the grant managers, and technical coordinators for review, including implementation and financial status, problems encountered and reparative measures, and any lessons learned. Program progress will be measured against indicators listed for sectors and sub sectors and quality assurance of services will be assessed through M&E site visits, review of records, and beneficiary feedback. The protection child protection and gender based violence teams will compile regular activity reports, which will feed into an indicator tracking system to monitor the progress of implementation on a weekly, monthly, quarterly, and semi -annually basis. Protection teams will focus on providing quality and integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness raising and psychosocial assistance, targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives through community centers and outreach mechanisms, awareness raising will touch on PSS, availability of services, and mine risk education, while the GBV intervention will focus on staff capacity building, case management/referrals, specialized services, psychosocial support activities both mobile and static. Staff will be trained on the following, humanitarian principles, protection standards, GBV core concepts, CP minimum standards, Psychological first aid, disclosure, and referrals.
