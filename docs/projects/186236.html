Project Summary

Project Summary
For the past 5 years, Cameroon has seen a socio-political crisis emerge in the North West and South West Regions of the country known as the Anglophone Regions. The unrest which started in 2016 has progressively escalated into an armed conflict between secessionists and government security forces, disruption of basic services and public infrastructure, and subsequent large displacement of population which has led to increased food insecure homes. 15 % of IDPs are living remote areas, often in farm huts, bushes and the forests (MSNA August 2021).The onset of covid19 pandemic in March 2020, introduced new threats to children and vulnerable communities worsening the situation in the NW Region.
Morbidity and poor sanitary conditions remained accelerated in 2021 especially for the following diseases, malaria (92%), cough (57%), diarrhea (46%) and skin diseases (31%) (MSNA August 2021). Access to health care still remains a challenge to IDPs and host population due to insecurity and persistent attacks on healthcare system. 25% of the facilities are not functional with majority of IDPs (58%) not able to access health care due to distance (40%), financial constraints (17%) and insecurity (5%) (MSNA August 2021). Furthermore, weak disease surveillance system has put the population at risk of epidemics with late detection and limited response capacity with repeated outbreaks of infectious diseases and new strains of Covid.
SHUMAS intervention will be designed to reduce morbidity and mortality of children under 5 years, adolescent boys and girls and pregnant women affected by the crisis in 9 health districts in the NW and for 10 months through consultations and treatment of 4 common diseases in children (Malaria, Diarrhea, Pneumonia and Skin disease), provision of Vitamins and deworming medications, vaccination coverage, provision of baby and delivery kits and psychosocial support.
The aim of this project is to contribute towards the reduction of morbidity and mortality rates among children under 5 years, adolescent boys and girls and pregnant women in 9 health districts in the NW (Benakuma, Wum, Kumbo East, Kumbo West, Ako, Nwa, Nkambe, Batibo and Mbengwi) region of Cameroon. This will be by improving access to basic curative and preventive health care services for vulnerable IDPs and host population
The specific objectives are;
• To improve timely access to basic curative and preventive health care services in children less than 5 years (12960 M, 14040 F), pregnant women (1800) affected by crisis in 9 health districts in NW for 10 months through consultation and treatment for 4 common diseases, distribution of LLINs, distribution of clean delivery kits, deworming, distribution of Vitamins supplements and sensitization on Essential Family Practices.
• To strengthen epidemiological surveillance/ Vaccination coverage from below 50% to 80% in children 6-59months, adolescent boys and girls, pregnant women affected by crisis in 9 health districts for 10 months through measles vaccination, routine immunization and search for loss to follow up children.
• To improve access to Sexual Reproductive Health services (Assisted deliveries by skilled personnel/ management of pregnancy related complications) in adolescent girls (900) and pregnant women (1800) in 9 health through provision of baby and delivery kits, ANC cards.
• To provide mental health and psychosocial support to trauma victims affected by crisis in 9 health districts through referral and counter-referral of GBV survivors
