Project Summary

The project seeks to minimize children’s exposure to increased protection risks as a result of the ongoing humanitarian crisis as well as the COVID-19 pandemic. Zimbabwe’s socio-economic challenges exacerbated by the pandemic has resulted in heavily strained livelihoods, disruption in household incomes and loss of employment for many households. This has posed new risks and challenges, thus increasing vulnerability to trafficking. The prolonged closure of schools has worsened the case for children exposing them especially girls to multiple forms of abuse, especially sexual abuse and exploitation, child labour, early child marriage and new forms such as online child abuse. The project will focus on strengthening the existing child protection structures through capacity building workshops, awareness raising and direct assistance to child victims of trafficking. The will strengthen the capacity of targeted communities to reduce the risks of child trafficking in Harare, Matabeleland South, Midlands, and Mashonaland Central provinces. Total target for the project is 9,322.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Key child protection stakeholders were consulted during the design of the project. In addition views of communities gathered during other activities being implemented by both IOM and its partners were taken into consideration during the design of the project. Key community leaders would be capacitated to raise awareness within their communities. Communities will also identify and agree upon strategies to prevent child trafficking and protect vulnerable and at risk members of the community. Additionally, IOM will utilize established toll-free hotlines to facilitate submission of beneficiary complaints and provision of feedback.
