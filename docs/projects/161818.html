Project Summary

Since the beginning of the humanitarian crisis in northeast Nigeria, affected populations have been suffering from a continues deterioration of living conditions, a disruption of pre-existing protective mechanisms and a lack of access to essential services. They have accumulated protracted stress and experienced extreme violence, and loss of family members, social connections, and property. Displaced populations face grave protection risks and abuse such as exploitation, family separation, restrictions on freedom of movement and insecurity. The before mentioned vulnerabilities are seen exacerbated by pre-existing and emergency induced problems faced by the affected population. This is particularly true in terms of the most vulnerable sub-groups of the population, including the elderly and chronically ill, persons with disabilities, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and/or separated children, adolescent boys and girls, pregnant and/or lactating women and people returning from captivity.
The proposed project aims to strengthen the existing protection and MHPSS response in the North-East, through: A) deployment of psychosocial support mobile teams, who will provide informal education as well as focused psychosocial support services through lay counseling and support groups B) capacity building and deployment of governmental mental health care workers to support referrals and treatment. C) Provision of assistance to victims and capacity building of relevant partners D) provision of training on MHPSS approach and protection mainstreaming for relevant stakeholders and partners E) Outreach activities dissemination of protection focused messages.

1 - Needs

Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities. The conflict affected population in the three BAY states experienced extreme levels of violence, including killings, attacks, injuries, abduction, forced marriage, arbitrary detention, forced recruitment. The crisis exacerbates negative coping mechanisms and gender-based violence. For instance, women, adolescent and girls are particularly vulnerable to forced early marriage, forced prostitution and survival sex. Displaced populations face grave protection risks and abuse such as exploitation, family separation, restrictions on freedom of movement and insecurity (2019 -2021 Nigeria HRP). The before mentioned vulnerabilities are seen exacerbated by pre-existing and emergency induced problems faced by the affected population. This is particularly true in terms of the most vulnerable sub-groups of the population, including the elderly and chronically ill, persons with disabilities, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and/or separated children, adolescent boys and girls, pregnant and/or lactating women and people returning from captivity. The population that was displaced due to the conflict is facing additional stressors created by the displacement itself. Stress factors include family separation, difficulties and sometimes denial of access to services, limited opportunities to livelihood, breakdown of family and community support mechanisms, potential stigmatization, secondary and repeated displacement. All of the above factors can severely impact the conflict affected population’s mental health and psychosocial wellbeing. Positive coping mechanisms and endurance can be compromised by the duration of the ongoing conflict, eroding the already limited resources. According to the UNHCR Protection Monitoring Report 2019; north east Nigeria, women and girls are the face of abduction. The abductions involve both mass abduction and individual/small scale cases. 100% of all the cases identified were women and girls, 70% are below 18 years of age; 93% forced to marry NSAG and 55% became pregnant while in captivity.

2 - Activities

Activity 1. Deploy 120 psychosocial support mobile team members, including to newly accessible areas and areas of return in Yobe, Borno and Adamawa state. Activity 2. Provide culturally appropriate structured psychosocial activities for adults, teenagers and children as well as informal education for adults and youths through psychosocial support mobile teams. Activities will be tailored according the age and gender categories. Activity 3. Provide focused psychosocial support services through lay counseling and support groups provided by trained psychosocial support mobile team members. The focused services will be tailored on an individual or problem based basis, considering age and gender categories. Activity 4. Support affected populations with increased access to specialized services and follow up for individuals suffering from mental disorders (through supporting referrals and treatment, through the deployment of governmental mental health care workers and capacity building and resources for governmental facilitiesActivity 5. Provide monthly training and weekly supervision for the psychosocial support mobile teams.Activity 6. Support vulnerable persons facilitate referral to other service providers to address their immediate needs. Activity 7. Provision of an integrated Counter Trafficking in Crisis response through provision of direct victim assistance through National Agency for the Prosecution of Trafficking in Persons, capacity building of relevant actors. Activity 8. Provide training on MHPSS approach and protection mainstreaming for relevant stakeholders and partners involved in the field of mental health and psychosocial support.Activity 9. Conduct outreach activities dissemination of protection focused messages including how to report risk, trafficking in person and access to care targeting conflict affected population
