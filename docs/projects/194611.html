Project Summary

Malaria Consortium will continue to improve surveillance and response towards epidemic prone diseases and access challenges for integrated health services by communities which are currently not covered by other partners in Aweil South; HPF and Concern World Wide; Communities that are located at least 10km from the nearest health facility in Aweil South County of Northern Bahrel Ghazal state in order to decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates, such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine preventable illnesses such as among 6,981 measles cases. This will be achieved through expanding reach of essential primary health care services through both static and mobile outreach points targeting 34,976 beneficiaries (19,752F and 15,223M) of which 11409 (6,443F and 4,966M) are children under 5 years.
Key project interventions will include: provision of life-saving essential health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (PHCU) and mobile healthcare services; Immunization of children under the age of five against vaccine preventable diseases; coordination with UNICEF and State Ministry of Health Expanded programme of Immunisation department (SMOH EPI) for the supply of vaccines and last mile distribution to the supported health facilities; procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities; procure, pre-position and provide essential medicines and supplies to health supported health facilities; provision of BEmONC by skilled birth attendants at supported health facilities to reach 1,736 SBAs. At Community level the project through BHWs will empower communities to demand quality health services and actively participate in health decisions that affect their communities and to build positive health seeking behaviours; work with Boma Health workers (BHWs) mobilise care givers of children under the age of one for the vaccination of children at health facility and outreach points. BHWs will also follow up on vaccination defaulters and through this network of BHWs, the project seeks to improve surveillance and referral for epidemic prone diseases. BHWs will also deliver Integrated Community Case Management of childhood illness(iCCM) services in communities cut off by floods and provide health promotion activities including screening and referral to health facilities and Outpatient Therapeutic (OTP)centres of children identified with malnutrition.
To ensure provision of quality health services, health workers will receive training/refresher training on the following packages: 1) Clinical Management of Rape, reproductive health services including adolescent reproductive health and FP. The project will provide Job Aids and tools, capacitating the County Health Department to do supportive supervision and mentorship of Health workers to develop clinical competencies and skills. To raise awareness, promote identification of people with mental health problems and create demand among 66 for mental health services, the project will focus on psychoeducation, community messaging and awareness using community health structures to Identify and refer people with mental health problems. The project will also seek to improve the screening and referral of people with disabilities using the Washington group of questions at every point of contact with health service provider.
In view of the COVID19 Pandemic and other diseases that are prone to outbreaks, the project will work with both facility and community-based health workers to improve on surveillance (prevention and control of epidemic prone diseases including outbreak detection, investigation, response) and reporting of suspected cases. Community Health workers will receive refresher training on IDRS and risk communication messages about COVID19. Facility based Health workers will be mentored on triaging, passive and timely surveillance.

1 - Needs

Recurrent devastating floods since July 2020 with consequent destruction of food have contiued to cause internal displacement of populations in 8 Payams and (Ayai, Tarweng, Nyieth, Gakrol, Wathmock, Panthou, Nyocawany and Tiar-alet) and high malaria disease burden have continued to drive the current humanitarian needs in Aweil South County (NBEG Flood Assessment report). Depleted food stocks, poor household sanitation and hygiene, poor access to functional health facilities and other essential basic needs predisposed children <5years, pregnant and lactating women, the elderly and PLWDs to malnutrition and preventable communicable diseases.The recurrent floods that destroyed both field crops and stored grains-maintained Aweil South County in Phase 5 Catastrophe/Famine of the Integrated Food Security Phase and Classification (IPC)for the period 2021and early 2022 a situation that predispose community members to malnutrition consequently making them more susceptible to infection, and infection also contributes to malnutrition. Aweil South County has a total of 18 health facilities: 20 PHCUs and 2 only PHCCs. Of the 20 health facilities in Aweil South, five are not functional and therefore populations around these facilities are largely reached through Mobile outreach clinics supported South Sudan Humanitarian Fund (SSHF); 9 health facilities are supported by HPF, 2 by SSHF and 4 by Concern Worldwide. During the rainy season June to October 2021 5 health facilities in Aweil South were physically inaccessible by motor vehicles or motorcycles. During this period, prepositioning of drugs and other health commodities before the peak rain season, for both the health facility services and the BHW services, remote supervision and engagement of Boma Health committees as monitors proved to be effective in improving access and utilisation as well as prevention of drug leakages in those health facilities and communities.The access to health services trends in Aweil South for outpatient consultations for children and adults in the last 2 years have also increased by 10.6% from 117,260 in the calendar year 2020 to 129,680 in 2021 (HMIS Data) and with trends of children below 5 years sharply influencing these increased morbidity trends with a rapid rise by 28.5% from 39,865 in 2020 to 51,238 in 2021. In the month of January 2022 alone among the 9 HPF supported facilities, up to 5,188 treatments were provided for Diarrhoea, Malaria and Pneumonia out of which 79% (4,081) were for Malaria, 12% (606) were RTI/Pneumonia cases while 10% (501) represented Acute Diarrhoea cases (Facility registers/records).. According to the NBeG 2021 annual EPI performance bulletin, NBeG state annual Penta 1 coverage was 82%, however the Penta 3 coverage was 68% and the Drop Out Rate (DOR) was 18%, an indication of low use of EPI services.Other problems include poor structures, Nyieth and Ayai PHCUs are grass thatched; storm damaged structures at Tiaraliet, Panthou posing a risk to female patients and pregnant mothers; reduced HPF funding for the county creating a big gap in terms of supply of essential drugs, support to the disease surveillance officer and referral of obstetric cases to the next level of care; inadequately skilled and a lack of qualified Health Workers(HW) countywide (10 of the 16 existing qualified HWs are in Panthou), contributing to poor diagnosis and management especially for children under five (U5), survivors of Gender Based Violence (GBV), pregnant women and women in labour. Poor staff attendance, lack of integration-clinical officers and CHWs not screening and referring children for immunization also leads to missed opportunities for quality health service provision. The county has only one fully supported PHCC (out of two) making access to services difficult and increasing referrals to Aweil State Hospital. Further, long distances coupled with COVID-19, myths and sociocultural beliefs affect uptake of services at community level

2 - Project activities

This project will increase access to health services to 34,976 beneficiaries (19,752F and 15,223M) by continuing mobile clinics to 2 HFs and selected 17 outreach points in hard-to-reach locations, in addition to already supported HFs and also work with BHWs to deliver iCCM services to 11409 (6,443F and 4,966M) children under 5 years. HWs will be supported to develop outreach micro plans and schedules, engage Boma Health Workers (BHWs) and community leaders on defaulter tracking, promoting uptake of routine outreach, mobile and campaign immunization. Static and Mobile health service delivery model will be used to deliver life-saving essential healthcare services and vaccination of children under 5 years against vaccine preventable diseases. Other activities include servicing and fueling of the CHD diseases surveillance officer to strengthen the routine surveillance systems and active response to disease outbreaks at both facility and community; recruitment of additional staff to provide both static and mobile health clinics; scheduled Clinical officer visits to selected PHCUs to increase use of health services by 1,010 (505F & 505M) PLWDs, mentally challenged; clinical management and referral of 62 survivors of SGBV. In the Communities cut off by floods, the project will focus on community health service provision through increasing the BHWs and ensuring that they have enough health commodities to support child health interventions such as ICCM and are linked to facility-based services. In addition, Boma Health Committees, Health facility Committees and community leaders will be engaged regularly during community consultative meetings to ensure that the services offered by BHWs in the community and by HW at the health facility are of quality and address their needs. Community Consultative meetings will be used as forums to give and get feedback about the project performance. HF infrastructure improvements include renovation/ construction of ramps at some health facilities to ensure accessibility by people with disabilities; construction of incinerator and placenta disposal pit to ensure proper waste segregation and disposal of sharps, infectious and non-infectious waste; fencing the waste disposal area to prevent entry of animals and unauthorized persons; supportive supervision, on-the-job training and mentorship to develop HWs’ clinical competencies to provide integrated quality health care; classroom training for community and facility-based health workers on selected technical areas which have not been supported by SSHF and HPF in 2021; provision of the current treatment guidelines for all staff. This will address the technical capacities of the clinical staff and reduce unnecessary referrals to Aweil State Hospital. The project will procure other essential medical commodities to complement supplies from partners; conduct inventory management and drug stock reporting; preposition supplies during the rainy season. MC will coordinate with the state EPI office & UNICEF to support CHD to maintain the EPI cold chain. The project will engage community structures-BHWs, HFCs, community surveillance systems, to raise awareness on key health promotion messages, distribute IEC materials, link and refer identified people in need of basic services to health facilities. To strengthen prevention of COVID -19 we will train health workers on triaging and BHWs on Home management of Flue like symptoms. Management of human resources for health: We will work with CHDs to ensure that all CHD team members and health workers have their job descriptions reviewed and updated based on national guidelines and recruit critical staff at HFs and for the mobile health clinics in agreement with CHDs. We will Support CHD to develop Micro Plans for EPI and hold programme progress review meetings and quarterly planning with partners. Support the CHDs to ensure timely reporting of data in the DHIS and other reporting mechanisms/channels

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project on inception will educate communities on channels available to launching complains and grievance redress mechanisms. To ensure that individuals with disabilities and those not literate can give feedback on the services they receive, Boma Health Committees at communities will be empowered through training to engage with community members to get their views and perceptions. The project will incorporate service satisfaction surveys through focus group discussions with people with disabilities) and anonymous suggestion boxes at health facilities, Boma Health Workers will continue to educate communities about their role in improving service provision through making suggestions and recommendations. Deliberate identification of marginalized groups such as women, people leaving with disabilities, people with mental disorders and their care givers and adolescents in the community to be members of various project committees will be done to ensure that their voices and feedback is taken note of

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The majority of the staff will be recruited locally based on merit. All staff and community-based structures (Boma Health Committees (BHCs) comprising of a Boma Chief, head teachers, traditional leaders, religious, women’s group members; youth group members, adolescents, people with disabilities and other community members) involved in the project implementation will be oriented on the existing complaints and feedback mechanism and procedures for using it. The community will be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints and provide feedback on the project implementation, staff behaviour and quality of services provided. The affected population can use this platform to request for information and assistance, report any violation of the Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and/safeguarding policy or code of conduct by staff or representatives of the organization (contractors, suppliers), express minor/major dissatisfactions with project implementation as well as provide suggestions. Individual or group feedback throughout the project cycle will be collected from a multi feedback channel, i.e frontline project staff and volunteers; Boma Health Workers (BHWs) who provide basic health services at household level; ‘champion’ women selected by the community; focal points at facility level and community meetings through focus group discussions. Community information sharing sessions targeting local authorities and the community members; men, women, girls and boys as well as people living with disabilities will be held on the start of project. These sessions are meant to inform the affected population about the organizations, principles both organizations adhere to, how staff are expected to behave and project activities to be undertaken clearly stating their involvement in the project to ensure its success and effectiveness besides their entitlement. Bi-monthly meetings, annual community dialogues will be conducted to discuss feedback and complaints reported. A walk-in help desk, suggestion box and a hotline will be established to receive real time feedback. Feedback posters providing information on 5 Ws (who, where, when, how, to whom) to launch complaints or give feedback will also be visibly displayed at service points. The posters will display reporting hotline and emails. All complaints received will be addressed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner with relevant local government authorities and organization. Sensitive complaints will be urgently prioritized while non-sensitive complaints will be addressed within a span of two weeks. Confidentiality of the complainant will be upheld to ensure safety. Feedback and complaints logged will be documented in the existing reporting templates and uploaded in the feedback database. Details on how they are responded to and timeframes will be made available.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

General protection needs of the affected population will be considered during project implementation at all service delivery points and on contact with all persons receiving assistance. We will ensure the affected people receive all services irrespective of their gender; encourage gender-balanced participation in activities. Marginalized-female headed households, GBV survivors, PLWD and the elderly will be prioritised. Protection and GBV will be mainstreamed through reference checks of all recruited staff; contractors and consultants so that no people with a record of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) is recruited into the project. All direct project implementation staff will be trained on the PSEA policy and reminded of the staff code of policy and sign an acknowledgment to uphold these policies. We will strive to recruit 50 female (technical and support) staff on the FSL project implementation. Both organizations commit to ensure every service provided at contact points do not expose affected populations particularly children, women, girls, PLWD and the elderly to further risks. Health workers will be trained on first aid and clinical management of survivors of GBV including SGBV and the referral pathway.At community level, BHWs will be trained on how to form psychosocial support groups focusing on demand creation for use of health services by survivors of SGBV, their identification and community support. We will sensitise and raise awareness on stigma, its effects on access to health and how to support its reduction. We will ensure men and boys are included in awareness-raising on sexual and reproductive health, and HFs deliver counselling for girls and boys on pregnancy prevention and options of culturally accepted contraceptive methods. Beneficiaries including PLWDs will be sensitized on their rights and entitlement and distributions scheduled to take place will be concluded early in the day to ensure beneficiaries’ safety. During county planning, we will work with local authorities to take the lead in identifying communities, groups or individuals vulnerable to exclusion from services, their locations, the types of exclusion they experience, and how this exclusion is perpetuated or reinforced. We will develop actions with local authorities and in consultation with communities, on how to organise services in a way that includes all vulnerable groups. We will work with CHDs to regularly document actions that CHDs and HFs can take (e.g. outreach services) to improve equity in service delivery. Local Authorities will lead the mapping of available social and community support mechanisms and services for people with mental illness and disabilities. Following this, we will work with CHDs to set up committees at county (with participation of women and disabled people in the committee), Boma, and village levels, linked to HFs, to support the review of GESI issues, and propose strategies and actions to improve gender equity, prevent exclusion, and mitigate violence against women and girls. We will support improvements to HF physical infrastructure to protect the privacy and dignity of all, and support access by those with disabilities. We will also work with WASH partners to support accessible design of latrines and hygiene facilities for disabled persons at HFs. All data collection and analysis will also be disaggregated by gender. The existing feedback mechanism will be applied in reporting any safeguarding or SEA concerns. Identified beneficiaries with protection concerns mainly GBV will be referred to existing service points for further support based on their needs. Posters with hotlines and emails will be posted on all health facilities and the mobile team will have a banner with safeguarding reporting contacts.The same information will be disseminated through radio talk shows; conduct receive training and refresher training on PSEA and safeguarding to ensure that staff are compliant

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During bimonthly review meeting, the feedback from communities about project activities is used to realign the project assistance to address the issues raised by community members. The same feedback will be used to reach out to other partners to leverage resources for improved service delivery . The feedback will be shared with CHD and local authorities for their address and inclusion in county future plans if the needed resources are not available for immediate response.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

We will maintain a risk register that is updated quarterly and reviewed by Malaria Consortium’s Global Management Group (GMG) during monthly operational calls. The risk register will detail: programme, financial, contextual (political and economic) and safeguarding risks; their impact, likelihood, acceptable risk level and existing controls; and the mitigating actions, timelines for these actions, and the person responsible for implementing them. These risks will be factored into our quarterly work plans by identifying any potential causes of delay and ensuring we have actions in place to mitigate against them, to ensure delivery of the contract on time and within budget. Mom Health Teams (BHTs) will work with local leaders and administrators to mobilise communities to support people with mental illness and disabilities. BHTs will engage men’s groups on issues of GBV, to make them aware of the special health needs of women and the importance of their involvement in family health affairs. BHT training will cover non-discriminatory practices, interpersonal communication, and their role in sensitising communities about the negative effects of stigma
