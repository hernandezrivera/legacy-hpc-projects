Project Summary

UNICEF will prevent and respond to all forms of violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect of children, including GBV. Will reach 182,965 beneficiaries of whom 101,515 children and adolescents, and 81,450 adults. Women and girls will constitute 60 % of beneficiaries and persons with disabilities 11 %. While 70% of the project target will be of Rohingya refugee camps and 30 % host communities. UNICEF will cover 16 camps (4, 4 Ext, 8 E, 8W, 9,11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 19, 20, 20 Ext, 22, 24) following a 50% coverage approach in line with rationalization. Will engage gender transformative programming to prevent exposure to violence and support children affected by or are at risk of violence. Department of Social Services as lead government department will deliver case management services in 31 camps. DSS presence across the camps is significant towards localizing child protection efforts and fostering greater sustainability across the humanitarian-development nexus.
UNICEF will follow a public health approach to child protection that prioritizes prevention and ensures interventions are rights-based and value for money. Interventions will Do No Harm and be in the Best Interests of the Child and based on robust needs analysis. Will strengthen child protection systems as illustrated in the socio-ecological model . Support government to lead and scale-up presence in the Rohingya response. DSS is responsible for implementing overarching social welfare programmes in Bangladesh to enhance the quality of life. DSS is one of the first government agencies present in the camp and providing services for the children. Partnership with DSS contributing to establish community-based child protection mechanisms, strengthen capacities of social workers and deliver case management. UNICEF will support DSS with more social workers for case management and on government leadership within the Child Protection sub-sector for better coordination in the response .
UNICEF will invest in multi-sector coordination of judges, police, probation officers, and community-based structures for a comprehensive continuum of care for children. Continue working with the Office of the Deputy Commissioner to accelerate Birth Registration through BDRIS .
Key elements of this endeavor along with strengthening social service workforce, reinforcing case management, will be provide comprehensive package of services through MPCs with outreach services. Through a blend of approaches along with Child Protection Facilities i.e. 76 Multipurpose centers essential lifesaving child protection services will be continued for children, parents and caregivers in an integrated manner.
UNICEF will ensure child survivors and those at-risk of violence have access to case management and CPIMS+, care plans and referrals. Family-based alternative care will be promoted. The social service workforce will collaborate with CBCPCs to ensure a robust continuum of care for children. Access to structured and unstructured MHPSS for recovery and resilience will be extended for children. Parents and caregivers will be supported with knowledge and skills emphasizing positive parenting and nurturing environments. Children aged 10-18 will have access to Life Skills sessions and engage as agents of changes in their communities. UNICEF will strengthen the community-based child protection mechanisms through CBCPCs to identify child protection risks and work together to find appropriate solutions in the best interests of the child. Also, will be empowered through training, mentoring and access to resources.
UNICEF will embrace its role as a lead agency in the Child Protection Sub-Sector Strategic Advisory Group, Protection Working Group, Case Management Technical Working Group, and other coordination forums for close collaboration with Government, NGOs, Civil Society, and private sector. UNICEF will also support organizational development of government counterparts and implementing partners.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

It has been six years into the Rohingya crisis, and the country is hosting 931,574 refugees in Cox’s Bazar, with 480,947 children of the total population heavily rely on international aid and humanitarian assistance. The complex conditions and limited services in the camps makes children and women, especially Unaccompanied and Separated Children, female-headed households, person with disabilities, those with alternative gender orientations be at risk to various forms of violence, including trafficking, child marriage, child labor, and GBV. The JMSNA report 2023, reports among the refugee community 66.5% of households indicate their children's absence from community-based child protection and psychosocial programs. Moreover, the percentage of households with children experiencing psychological distress is concerning, underscoring the urgency of mental health interventions. While 32 % households with at least one child (<18) not residing in the household due to seeking employment. And about 38% of households in the refugee community reported signs of distress in the last two weeks.The JMSNA report 2023 also echoes the situation in the Host community highlighting 81.6 % children never participated in community-based child protection programme. While 23 % households with at least one child (<18) not residing in the household due to seeking employment; and 32.7 % reported signs of distress in the last two weeks.Reductions in food rations due to funding cuts continue to contribute in heighten the occurrence of all forms of violence against children and women, including GBV incidents. The periodic daily incident reports of CCM sector in 2023, indicates the ongoing community conflict/tensions at the camp level remain concerning. Case management trend analysis from CPIMS+ as of October 2023 has shown 35.91% children are neglected, 11.73% children affected by physical distress, 9.88% children experienced Physical abuse, 8.07% engaged in child labor, 7.94% children affected by physical and emotional abuse, and 5.60% children with disabilities. These highlights the importance of continuum of child protection responses and care to address the increasing trend of child protection concerns both in the refugee camps and host community. Child protection systems in Cox's Bazar is under strain. The justice system requires to be child-friendly with investments to bring it in line with the Children’s Act 2013. Capacity building to operationalize diversion and strengthen the monitoring of children as they move through the justice system is pivotal. Support in strengthening Children’s Courts, Child Welfare Boards, Child Affairs Desks at police stations for strengthened coordination in dealing with children coming in contact with law is vital. DSS needs technical support in strengthen its role as the government’s lead agency in dealing with the protection concerns including responses for children in the refugee camps and the host community. Social Service workforce needs capacity building on case management to support children access to continuum of care. The Birth and Death Registration Information System relaunched in district, but there is need for advocacy with and technical support to the Officer of the Registrar General and District Administration in accelerate the registrations. Capacity building for local government units on upgraded BDRIS including inter departmental collaboration much need. The district is vulnerable to natural hazards and heavily impacted with climate changes facing monsoon, cyclone, and landslides affecting safety, livelihoods, and infrastructure. The landscape in camp due to continuous natural disasters presence the vulnerabilities to increased risk and incidences.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The Project will ensure that Rohingya refugees have equitable access to child protection services in a predictable, efficient, timely, transparent, and accountable manner. UNICEF will work in 16 camps (4, 4 Ext, 8 E, 8W, 9,11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 19, 20, 20 Ext, 22, 24) following the rationalization architecture avoiding any geographic overlaps and g aps in implementing harmonized programme strategies, systems, protocols, and tools in prevention of and protection from abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence for the children and women. . Department of Social Services as lead government department will deliver case management services in 31 camps. UNICEF and implementing partners will provide a consolidated package of services in the camps and host communities thus ensuring equity in service provision. The Project will work with the Government, UN, NGOs, and donor community together with the relevant sectors and working groups to further avoid gaps and overlaps.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNICEF have incorporated protection principles of safety and dignity, avoiding causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, upholding accountability, promoting participation, and fostering empowerment in planning and implementation of the programme for the children, adolescents, families, and members of the community in the Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. Through service delivery nodes (Multipurpose Centres, Adolescent Clubs, etc.) and ensuring robust outreach to the most vulnerable following do no harm and inclusion of diversity, the Project will ensure access to assistance and services without discrimination or barrier. Embedded within project design is an information and feedback mechanism through which the children, adolescents, parents, caregivers including the community people can demand accountability for the adequacy of interventions, and for UNICEF and implementing partners to address concerns and complaints. Children and adolescents’ participation are promoted through creating child friendly environment and participatory approaches to identify safe and unsafe places, along with the underlying reasons for these designations. Their views and recommendations in developing strategies for mitigating risks, contributing to a safer environment are acknowledged as pivotal.

3.3 - Age & Disability

UNICEF aims to prevent and respond to all forms of abuse, exploitation, violence and neglect of all girls and boys of all age groups. Age-specific interventions for children and adolescents will be provided throughout the programme responses, including Psychosocial support activities and adolescent life skills-based learning. Parents and caregivers will be supported with knowledge and skills emphasizing positive parenting and nurturing environments for the children. UNICEF will ensure all child protection facilities having accessibility measures and services for all targeted groups including person with disabilities. Will exercise the Washington Set questionnaires in accumulating information for programme design and planning ensuring the rights and needs are documented in programme design to promote and upholds the inclusiveness of person with disabilities. Acknowledging, the age specific needs and rights of the children and adolescents, the project will promote and build the capacities of staffs and volunteers on tools and skills on disability and inclusion. The programme will plan and support community outreach services through mobile services as well establishing outreach service points, adolescents clubs, parents groups within the community to encourage and promote participation and access to activities and services for children and adolescents, especially girls and women. All interventions are tailored to ensure that all beneficiaries, particularly children and persons with disabilities, can actively engage with our services and interventions and derive maximum benefit from them.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNICEF will ensure that AAP feedback and response mechanism is established following the AAP principles of participation, inclusion, communication, and transparency that child protection is made central in all stages of the programme cycle. UNICEF will ensure all staff, volunteers including children, adolescents, caregivers, and targeted community are accessible, reliable and timely information about organizational procedures, structures and processes on affected population capturing age, gender and specific groups at risk of discrimination and vulnerable. UNICEF and partners will take measures to reduce barriers so that children of all age groups and those who are at risk of discrimination are participating in planning, implementation and monitoring process while implementing the projects by the partners. UNICEF will support partners to strengthen organizational child safeguarding policy through conduct risk assessment and increase staff capacity to take appropriate measures. Also engage the community and target groups to participate throughout needs assessment, programme planning and implementation using standard assessment tools while inclusion of Children with disabilities and their caregivers. UNICEF will ensure all partners will have multiple feedback and reporting mechanisms are in place to introduce feedback and response mechanism into their activities, with follow up actions and assign AAP focal person where needed. Embedded within project design is an information and feedback mechanism through which the affected population can demand accountability for the adequacy of interventions, and for UNICEF and implementing partners to address concerns and complaints.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNICEF upholds stringent standards and protocols related to Child Safeguarding and the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Implementing partners are assessed to ensure a high level of adherence to the standards and principles, with the requirement to achieve a low-risk rating. UNICEF is key member of the of the PSEA Network. UNICEF is ensuring information and communication materials including capacity building of the partners organization are at the core of the programme planning to ensure the partners including staffs, volunteers and other service providers are informed, orientated, and trained on PSEA & Child Safeguarding. UNICEF will continue developing IEC materials on PSEA and child safeguarding and its dissemination for raising awareness among the children and women, including the community. UNICEF will support partners in wider sensitization and introduce among the community on the PSEA Complaint and Feedback Mechanism along with reporting mechanism through e.g. hotline numbers, feedback, and complaint boxes, assign a PSEA and child safeguarding focal person. UNICEF will ensure partners are supported in adopting child friendly approaches during sensitization and introducing Complaint and Feedback Mechanism among children and adolescents. UNICEF will support partners on their mainstreaming Safeguarding practices in all areas of work, and developing partner PSEA action plans to ensure a robust reporting, response and accountability framework.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

A central goal of the Project is to ensure sustainability and resilience in child protection programming. This objective is primarily achieved through a systems-strengthening approach, combined with community-based child protection, both of which complement and mutually reinforce each other. The aim is to reduce reliance on humanitarian service provision over time. This approach enables the Project to enhance the resilience of children, caregivers, and communities to various climate related shocks and contributes to Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) with a child protection focus. Furthermore, the Project is committed to ensuring the presence of adequate preparedness and response plans to address the impacts of natural disasters, including monsoons, cyclones, and other emergencies. The Project will continue to mainstream the programme interventions in its emergency preparedness and response plans through specialized capacity building for the community, contributory stocks of emergency recreational kits, dignity kits, awareness-raising activities, enhanced case management and follow-up services during the emergency. UNICEF will ensure partners having plans for child protection prevention and response for the emergency preparedness and responses. Identify and build capacity of Child protection focal points (CPFP) and volunteers on e.g., child protection in emergencies, preparedness and response, rapid child protection assessment, PFA. Undertake CP facility risk assessments to inform development of mitigation plans and response pre-disaster and during disasters. Ensure, partners will follow the CMTFWG referral pathways for family tracing and rapid reunification. Ensure UASCs are prioritized and receive expedited access to emergency services including PSS support.
