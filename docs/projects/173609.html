Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring that unaccompanied and separated children and other children with protection concerns are identified and receive holistic support through case management, psychosocial support and socio-economic reintegration. The project target areas are Bolori II in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC), Kaga LGAs (Borno State) and Damaturu, Gujba, Geidam, Potiskum, Bade and Yunusari LGAs (Yobe State).
The project includes an explicit focus on building capacity within communities to deliver child protection services, including prevention and response to abuse and violence, and calls for investment in capacity building of local partners to strengthen case management and referral systems. At the same time, the project will focus on socio-economic reintegration services based on the needs expressed by children during COOPI orientation and assessment process.
Gender equality measures will be mainstreamed starting from the needs assessment phase and
integrated into the design of the project in order to uplift the status of girls and boys and to ensure that attention to girls’ and boys’ protection issues will be equitably balanced. The proposed project is aligned with the 2020 Child Protection Sector Strategic Objective 1: "girls and boys facing protection risks have access to comprehensive case management services" and Objective 2: "conflict- affected children, adolescents and caregivers' well-being is enhanced through provision of quality mental health and psychosocial support services and information on child care and protection".
The impact of the project will ensure that the communities are better able to receive children previously associated with non-state armed groups, and that their leaders have the tools and capacity to facilitate their acceptance in the future. This will allow the project to continue impacting these communities beyond the scope of the project and to benefit the recipients of future reintegration efforts. The project will be carried out in an inclusive and gender-positive manner by prioritizing full and equal participation of girls and young women in all project activities. No discrimination will be based on gender. Specific attention will be put on gender-balance to ensure that girls and boys work together for greater girls’ empowerment and reintegration. COOPI's team will identify gender dynamics of the conflict and the context of the communities to adapt female participation strategies to ensure that girls are fully represented and substantively engaged. COOPI will also enforce gender-balance in its hiring of project staff, including key leadership positions, and the project’s monitoring and evaluation (M&E) will include gender-specific analysis through the collection of sex-disaggregated data. The intervention is designed to ensure essential protection support and services to children at risk and survivors that include: • case management services: direct support to unaccompanied and separated children and children with other protection concerns. Referral and liaison with specialized services and care in coordination with the CP partners and according to the established CPSSWG referral pathways and follow-up; • support to foster parents (and vulnerable caregivers), training on parental skills and follow-up, including psychosocial support and integrated assistance (Food/Nutrition/NFI); • provision of psychosocial support (individual counselling and group counselling); • empowering local counterparts’ capacity to identify and respond to children protection needs (State Ministry of Women Affairs – SMWA and State Ministry of Youth, Sport, Social & Community Development – SMYSSCD). The project will also consider engaging local CSOs to be trained and supported to carry out CP Network and monitoring activities.

1 - Needs

North-East Nigeria still faces immense humanitarian and protection challenges. An estimated 1.7 million people (1.6 million children and 100,000 caregivers) need childprotection services; this includes an estimated 21,000 children and caregivers living with disabilities (Guidance on the Child Protection 2021 Humanitarian ResponsePlan in North-East Nigeria). The Child Protection Guidance tool for HRP 2021 also estimates population of children aged 6-17 years in need of protection services in North-East Nigeria is 987,636 (517,249 girls and 470,388 boys).These children are lacking the fundamental protection of a caregiver, whether parents, extended family members or care professionals and are exposed to heightened risk of sexual violence, physical abuse and exploitation. Moreover, they have been subjected to grave violations with resulting psychosocial distress, including from abduction, exposure to sexual and gender-based violence, separation from or loss of family members, destruction of property, looting of food and livestock and forced displacement. As a result, the project’s community strengthening initiatives will directly engage community leaders and stakeholders to create a conducive environment for children reintegration. The analysis of data showed that women and children under 18 continued to bear the brunt of this crisis. Child and forced marriage, physical assault, rape, survival sex, sexual assault and denial of resources were the major incidents (UNHCR 2019 Aannual Report on Sexual and Gender-Based Violence North-East Nigeria). The Protection Monitoring Report released by UNHCR (May-June 2020) - as result of the vulnerability screening of 32,519 individuals conducted in 11 LGAs of Borno State - revealed various child protection related concerns: child marriage (44%), child headed household (21%), UASC (16%), adolescent parents (11%) and orphans (8%). Forced and child marriage, where the community and parents are harassed into marrying girls off to enable them support their families, have been reported in Monguno. In Yobe State, forced and child marriage (44%) continue to thrive in part due to pre-existing community norms of marrying out girls at earlier age. The schools have remained closed for long time as a preventive measure to avert the spread of COVID 19. Children, as a result, have adopted negative coping behaviours such as idly roaming in the camps, engaging in rough plays and being engaged in child labour and hawking. Due to their proximity to the periphery of danger, children become easy targets for radicalization and for recruitment to extremism in the absence of any meaningful engagement activities for them. Persistent conflict, floods and windstorm in camps and host communities have led to the destruction of shelters, WASH facilities, properties, and Non-food items (NFIs). This particular set of circumstances has also led to hardship and loss of livelihoods in most households posing risks and protection concerns.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Sensitization sessions with beneficiaries about the process of children reintegration within the communities of origin/host communities;Activity 2: Sensitization sessions with Imams and religious leaders about Child Protection;Activity 3: Awareness-raising campaigns on psychosocial support, promotion of human rights, empowerment of women and family tasks-sharing within the communities;Activity 4: Training sessions for foster parents and caregivers on Child Protection; Activity 5: Training sessions for Case Workers, LGA Departments and Ministries (SMWA and SMYSSCD), Community Support Workers and Social Workers on Child Protection and case management;Activity 6: Training sessions for teachers on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and case management;Activity 7: Capacity-building sessions/field coaching on Child Protection and case management for Community-based Child Protection Committees (CBCPC) and other existing community groups;Activity 8: Provision of case management service through 4 components: (1) identification and assessment of vulnerable children, including UASC and other children at risk/with protection concerns, within the community (opening of a case and start of documentation); (2) individual support planning (planning of response and care); (3) referral and liaison with support services (where required); (4) monitoring and review (including case closure);Activity 9: Establishment of Emergency Case Management Fund;Activity 10: Employment of health staff with skills and experience in working with vulnerable children affected directly by COVID19 or indirectly through a family member;Activity 11: Identification of alternative care arrangements for children affected by COVID19 linked to a family tracing system. Existing traditional and religious structures in the community will be used to support this effort;Activity 12: Construction/Rehabilitation of 6 Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS) in the targeted areas;Activity 13: Reintegration of former CAAG into schools and provision of school kits;Activity 14: Provision of psychosocial counselling to children;Activity 15: Conduction of recreational activities with children and youth (5-17) within the CFS; Activity 16: Production and distribution of IEC/training materials; Activity 17: Provision of socio-economic reintegration services for youth (ex. training on life-skills/financial savings/business-skills to enhance future employment opportunities); Activity 18: Support to vulnerable caregivers through psychosocial assistance, regular follow-up and integrated assistance (Food/Nutrition/NFI);Activity 19: Supply of Reunification and Reintegration packages, including housing sustenance-related ones.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

COOPI staff will be trained and sensitized on COOPI Code of Ethics and COOPI Nigeria Child Protection Code of Conduct. All trainees will be asked to sign COOPI’s Code of Conduct documents to ensure accountability and reporting after the completion of the training sessions. CSOs, caregivers and community leaders will be sensitized on PSEA and on COOPI internal policies and Code of Conduct in addition to reporting channels such as the internal COOPI whistleblower policy via email or helpline phone number. COOPI Protection Officers, PSEA Focal Person and PM will coordinate with each other in order to ensure that protection is mainstreamed at the programming level. COOPI will put into place a protection system to guarantee children’ safety and security at the community level and at reunification sites. This includes: extensive sensitization which should favor community acceptance; family’s preparation ahead of reunification; community safety plans developed jointly with the communities of origin; providing children with skills and job placement opportunities to divert them from wanting to re-join armed groups. Generally, the close collaboration with Government authorities should enable the program to better manage external risks and to design more informed solutions to protect children.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI will build on its previous experience of monitoring and evaluation of protection, emergency and early recovery programs to ensure that the proposed project's strategy is appropriate to respond to context-specific needs as well as to demonstrate changes in outcomes to which the programme contributes. The monitoring system will include regular site visits, supervisory visits by the management team and/or technical staff, and community feedback and accountability mechanisms. It will be used to inform periodic management meetings in which the team will review data, identify trends and propose changes to interventions when necessary.COOPI will establish a community feedback mechanism and project management committees in alignment with the guidance provided by the Protection Sector (including Child Protection sub-sector), UNICEF and other donors. COOPI will ensure regular and representative consultation of affected populations before, during and after the implementation of activities to gather their preferences, respective priority needs and to document barriers they face in accessing to safe and meaningful assistance and information. COOPI will recruit dedicate protection and AAP officers who speak the local languages and are gender-balanced to facilitate the communication with the communities. In addition, COOPI will also hire dedicated Community Feedback Assistants for the help and feedback desks, equipping them with computers/tablets to reduce the risk of data mismanagement/breaches in data protection.
