Project Summary

To strengthen the leadership of the GBV Sub-Cluster to tackle pervasive high rates of GBV in the occupied Palestinian territories, through effective coordination of GBV prevention, mitigation, and response interventions, identification and response to GBV gaps, trends, and emerging risks, capacity building and technical support for both GBV and non-GBV specialists, GBV data collection and management and GBV mainstreaming in the humanitarian response overall.
The project responds to the prolonged humanitarian crisis in the occupied Palestinian territory by contributing to the protection of rights and dignity of the Palestinian population in accordance with IHL and IHRL, and particularly GBV survivors with a cross-cutting focus in other sectors such as Protection, Health, and Child Protection. The project is aligned with the strategic objectives of the Protection Cluster as it 1) seeks to increase the respect for the IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations as well as 2) to put in place protection mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence.

1 - Needs

A key protection concern in the oPt, rates of gender-based violence (GBV) are significant according to the 2019 Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistic (PCBS) survey on violence, which was updated in July 2022, 59.3 per cent of (married or previously married) women between the ages of 15 and 64 experienced violence in the 12 months preceding the survey – 70.4 per cent in Gaza and 52.3 per cent in the West Bank. Of those, 57.2 per cent experienced psychological violence, 18.5 per cent physical violence, and 9.4 per cent sexual violence, while 9.6 per cent experienced the emerging issue of cyber violence. The recurring and frequently increased risk of violent, military conflict exacerbates pre-existing risks of GBV, including early/forced marriage, intimate partner/family violence, sexual harassment, rape, incest, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. Based on the MSNA and HNO GBV people in need, 1.7 million women, girls, men, and boys are in need of GBV interventions in oPt. In particular, women and girls are vulnerable to GBV, with 12 per cent of households across oPt reporting that women and girls avoid certain areas because they feel unsafe, an increase from 10 per cent in 2022. Women and girls feel most unsafe on their way to school, markets, and on public transportation, and near settlements, checkpoints or Access Restricted Areas. Women and girls most fear being subjected to verbal harassment, sexual and physical harrassment and violence. (oPt MSNA, 2023) These disproportionate impacts on women and girls are underpinned by socio-cultural patriarchal norms, which continue to undermine women and girl’s rights.The COVID-19 pandemic resulted in increased reporting of severe and complex GBV incidents, where women more commonly reported severe physical injuries requiring professional health care. Women reported more threats to their lives during the lockdown period, mainly from intimate partners and other relatives. An increase in occupation-related violations, such as home demolitions, home raids and violence involving Israeli forces or settlers, can be connected to a general increase of risk for exposure to GBV. Women with husbands and families exposed to various occupation violations report higher levels of exposure across all types of GBV in the household setting than women whose husbands and families are not exposed to occupation violations. (GBV SDR, 2022) The context in oPt has a detrimental impact on employment, income, and educational opportunities. MSNA results indicate that 50 per cent of households across oPt are reporting that increased competition for jobs/not enough jobs are the main barriers to employment. Limited economic opportunities and availability of livelihood activities increase the use of negative coping mechanisms that increase the risk of GBV, like forced early marriage for girls and forced engagement in transactional sex. GBV risks in this regard are higher in Gaza, as MSNA indicates that 60.3 per cent of households in Gaza reported having at least one member of their household unable to find work, in comparison to 21.2 per cent in the West Bank. (oPt MSNA, 2023) As such, the GBV SC will prioritise improving high quality, multi-sectoral services for GBV survivors, including people with disability, highly sensitive cases (incest, rape, and sexual abuse within the family) and cyber violence against women and girls. Ensuring the availability of, and access to, life-saving GBV services is also a priority, including GBV referral and case management, health services, MHPSS (survivors and service providers), safe shelter, and legal aid and representation. The GBV Sub-Cluster will also focus on advocacy and coordination for effective delivery of essential GBV services and a strengthened national referral system. This project will support the response to and mitigation of the following key GBV risks: Verbal, physical, and sexual harassment;Domestic and intimate partner violence;Lack of access to high quality multi-sectoral services (including safe shelter, and adequate clinical management of rape (CMR) services). Analysis shows that certain groups have a much higher risk of GBV. These include female-headed households, women and girls living in refugee camps, Bedouin women, women and girls with disabilities, women and girls in Hebron H2 and Area C and female farmers and heads of households. Against this background, GBV coordination is needed to keep identifying and meeting GBV priority needs in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt), including by ensuring GBV services align with global GBV minimum standards and survivor-centered, address gaps and reduce duplication in humanitarian response. It ensures that each aspect of the humanitarian programme cycle is applied as part of a joint effort that uses available resources and capacities. This role is performed by the GBVSC, which is chaired by UNFPA and brings together more than 110 national NGOs, INGOs, UN agencies, and government line ministries. The GBV SC meets on a bimonthly basis in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, and ad hoc based on emerging needs. The Sub-Cluster uses the ‘Inter-Agency GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies’ as the guiding document for provision of services in the humanitarian setting.

2 - Activities

Given the large membership (approximately 110 NGOs, INGOs and UN agencies) of the GBV Sub-Cluster and the multi-sectoral service provision for GBV survivors coordinated under the Sub-Cluster, human resources are needed to ensure leadership, information management and coordination. In addition to this, a key objective of the GBV Sub-Cluster is to build the capacity of all GBV Sub-Cluster partners, and national organisations (women-led and women-focused) to improve their ability to provide services in alignment with GBV minimum standards, to access humanitarian funding, and to track and monitor humanitarian interventions. The GBV Sub-Cluster targets their partners with capacity development interventions to enable them to engage more comprehensively in humanitarian situations, as per the localization agenda. Working with the Protection Cluster, other Areas of Responsibility (AoRs), with the members of the GBV Sub-Cluster, and in close coordination with Al Muntada Forum to Combat Violence Against Women, WESAL Network in Gaza and the Hemaya Network in Jerusalem, the GBV Sub-Cluster coordination team will work on 5 main components:- COORDINATION AND SUPPORT TO GBV SERVICES: Provide leadership to the GBV Sub-Cluster and strengthen GBV services by undertaking day to day coordination of the platform, including convening regular meetings to identify and address GBV gaps, trends and concerns among UN, Government and non-government entities, by updating GBV service directories on a regular basis, by identifying and proposing actions to respond to priority GBV concerns and gaps, by supporting partners with resource mobilisation capacity and protection mainstreaming through their interventions, including on access for people with disabilities and accountability to affected populations, by providing quality GBV inputs to humanitarian processes (e.g. HPC), by establishing a tracking tool to follow on the progress of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) implementation throughout the HRP GBV projects that receive funding and by ensuring coordination with national gender and GBV platforms and the work of Protection Cluster AoRs, especially the Child Protection and MHPSS Working Group, in order to conduct joint initiatives, fill response gaps jointly when relevant and ensure equal access to and gender sensitive protection responses.- ADVOCACY: Coordinate joint advocacy efforts, including by developing factsheets, policy papers and analytical reports on top priority GBV needs, gaps and trends, ensure the participation and leadership of the GBV Sub-Cluster in key advocacy processes such as the annual 16 days campaign against GBV, and provide GBV updates at donor briefings and protection briefings at HCT+ meetings. - CAPACITY BUILDING: Continue working with other Clusters to mainstream GBV in the overall humanitarian efforts and providing trainings to non-GBV specialists on safe GBV referral. Upon the request of GBV Sub-Cluster partners, to keep improving the quality of GBV services by organising capacity building sessions on the safe delivery of remote GBV services, on culturally appropriate self-care/stress management solutions, as well as the GBV mainstreaming within the humanitarian response in oPt based on the GBV IASC guideline and the Inter-Agency GBV Minimum Standards for prevention and response in emergencies. The GBV Sub-Cluster will also undertake a GBV Case Management Capacity Building Initiative training of trainers, with the aim to provide GBV service providers in humanitarian settings with a harmonised approach to building capacity on GBV Case Management.- GBV INFORMATION MANAGEMENT: Strengthen the GBV information management system of the Sub-Cluster by increasing the number of GBV organisations sharing data in a safe and ethical manner, by updating the system in partnership with OCHA based on GBV Sub-Cluster partners’ feedback and HRP monitoring needs and by analysing its content to inform the Humanitarian Needs Overview as well as develop GBV reports. In 2023, the GBV Sub-Cluster in oPt will continue the roll out of the GBVIMS+/Primero platform to improve programming and advocacy for the benefit of GBV survivors through the safe and ethical collection, management, sharing and analysis of service provision data. To that end, further capacity building of GBV Sub-Cluster partners is necessary. - PREVENTION OF SEXUAL EXPLOITATION AND ABUSE: Building on the work done by the GBVSC and the PSEA Coordination Network on awareness raising on PSEA principles and protocols and integrating PSEA in the GBV national referral systems in 2021, the GBVSC will continue to offer capacity building sessions and trainings on the PSEA principles and protocols in 2023.

The GBV Sub-Cluster is working in close collaboration with the PSEA Network Coordinator to ensure that GBV partners’ interventions, as well as the GBV referral system is safe and accessible to all i.e. women, girls, men, and boys and other at risk groups both for GBV and SEA; to monitor GBV risks; to maintain a safe and ethical feedback mechanism for the affected population in line with IASC GBV guidelines and global minimum standards, providing an entry point for SEA reporting by the community; and to provide survivors of SEA the necessary assistance and response services, in line with GBV high quality services, that are safe, accessible and appropriate for the special needs of GBV and SEA survivors.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

All GBV Sub-Cluster partners are required to follow the GBV Survivor Centered Approach and the ‘GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies’ thereby prioritizing safety, respect, confidentially and do no harm, providing meaningful access to services, ensuring accountability mechanisms are in place as well as participation and empowerment of GBV survivors. The GBV Sub-Cluster will continue to provide capacity building to partners on these principles including on GBV Minimum Standards and safe GBV remote service delivery.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Protection mainstreaming has been prioritised as a critical issue to be addressed by the GBV Sub-Cluster as early as 2019. The second key principle of Protection mainstreaming, “ensuring meaningful access”, was identified as a starting point with special focus on people with disabilities (PwD). This came as a result of a discussion among GBV Sub-Cluster partners on the findings of a study conducted by UNFPA in 2019 on GBV and women and girls with disabilities, which highlighted the importance to scale up support for them as they are more exposed to violence compared to other groups and the existing services offered to them are inadequate. In 2020, the GBV Sub-Cluster partnered with Stars of Hope society in the joint review of their manual on Disability Mainstreaming in VAW programming. Efforts in mainstreaming and ensuring meaningful inclusion of PWD will continue in 2023 through the UNFPA coordination role in the GBV Sub-Cluster.
