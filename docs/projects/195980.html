Project Summary

This project aims to build the resilience of Rohingya and Host Community women, adolescent girls, women with a disability, and transgender (Hijra) through increased livelihood skills and access to livelihood opportunities, including start-up support and support to establishing market linkages.
UN Women has five Multi-Purpose Women's Centers (MPWCs) in camps 3, 4, 4-Extension, 5, and 18; and three MPWCs in host communities in Ukhia, Teknaf, and Cox's Bazar Sadar. Through these centers, women and girls including gender diverse population can access tailored multi-sectoral services that consider their specific needs and challenges, with a focus on women’s empowerment to build resilience and strengthen women’s role and leadership in their communities. The project has a separate programme for the transgender (hijra) community in the camp.
Activities provided through these centers encompass 1) those that promote the empowerment of women and girls, building their resilience through livelihood and skills development activities; and 2) activities that respond to and mitigate risks of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), inclusive of psychosocial support, case management and referral services for GBV survivors, alongside 1st aid medical assistance, SRH services and awareness-raising sessions on women’s rights issues. The current project is focused on the former (1) activities, with specific attention placed on ensuring the inclusion of the most marginalized women and girls in the project’s activities, including women and girls from female-headed households, women with disabilities, and gender-diverse persons in the MPWCs.
Through the project, skills development training ranging in length from one to six months will be provided in the MPWCs to Rohingya and host community women. The training will focus on handicrafts, beauty parlors, tailoring, production of masks and reusable sanitary napkins, as well as eco-friendly jute products. At the end of the training, the skills of the participants will be tested. Upon the successful completion of the test, the participants will receive certificates. If the participant is found unsuccessful, they will be provided the opportunity to continue attending the training. UN Women will also be liaising with the sector on the standard training modules and provision of the certificates.
Following the training, beneficiaries will be supported to continue their own businesses through the provision of in-kind grants comprising specific materials from the training and other start-up kits, and support to set up shop and access the market through the Women's Market in Camp 5 as well as three dedicated women’s café and display corners set up in camp 4, 4-Ext. and in the Women's Market in camp 5, facilitating private sector engagement and contributing to social cohesion, as the women vendors will be from both Rohingya and the host community. UN Women will provide the shopkeepers with a monthly incentive in kind/cash for coming to the market and running their business, alongside which the women can collect orders from the community and do home delivery. Project activities will include basic entrepreneurship skills development training for the women for setting up their businesses.
In Camp 5, the Women's Market will continue to serve as a hub for women producers supported by also other partners such as AAB, DCA, FAO, HIS, PRANTIC, SNKSS, UNDP, and WFP.
Linkages to the local and national market and private sector will be strengthened by the Women Entrepreneur Association of Bangladesh (WEAB), a Women Peace and Humanitarian Fund grantee. In addition, the implementing partners (Oxfam, ActionAid Bangladesh, and BRAC) will sell the products through their national-level outlets. Also, UN Women will explore opportunities for conducting a market assessment and will engage with the ISCG for the planned market survey.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As demonstrated by the Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment 5 (REVA5), vulnerability levels among the Rohingya households remains alarmingly high, at 95 percent of all Rohingya households moderately to highly vulnerable and entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance, with households headed by a person with a disability, high crowding index households, and households with aged members significantly most vulnerable – while in the host community where 52 percent of the population remain moderately to highly vulnerable, women-led or women-headed households are among the most vulnerable. Within the Rohingya community, this reflects the lack of income sources and livelihood opportunities, which is especially acutely felt among Rohingya women and girls. Barriers faced by women and girls in the Rohingya community relate to traditional gender norms which limit their mobility and therefore access to information, assistance and services, and lead to low levels of women’s participation to decision making, livelihood activities, and high levels of GBV.The gap between men and women in the labor force remains high in both communities. According to REVA5, 65% of Rohingya men participate in the labor force compared with just 12% of Rohingya women, while it was 71% of men and 17% of women in the host communities. Restricted by harmful gender norms, the vast majority of Rohingya women, unskilled and uneducated, are left to stay at home, take care of children and the elderly, and do household chores. This undermines their self-sufficiency and leads to negative coping mechanisms such as begging, survival sex and selling off of remaining assets to mitigate economic and food insecurity and increases the risk of early marriage and human trafficking of Rohingya women and girls. As livelihood and cash-based activities have become increasingly limited, the situation of Rohingya women and girls, especially the roughly 16% that comprise the women heads of households, has become increasingly dire. Scaling up sustainable livelihoods and skills development opportunities, especially among women and girls with less education and lower skills than their male counterparts, is critical to enhancing economic empowerment and resilience of the Rohingya and host communities overall. This is also the main objective of the current UN Women project, which pays special attention to ensuring women and girls in most need of self-reliance and livelihoods activities benefit from the project, skilling them up to participate in income-generating activities and facilitating their access to market opportunities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UN Women has been implementing its refugee response programme, including its livelihoods and skills development activities, through Multi-Purpose Women’s Centers (MPWCs) since 2017, with the location of the MPWCs agreed with RRRC and CiCs following consultations, and building on inputs from stakeholders. The 5 Gender Field Officers (GFO) have been working with CiCs to strengthen gender equality in the camps. They regularly participated in the CiCs coordination meeting. The 8 MPWCs are run by UN Women’s implementing partners, ActionAid Bangladesh, BRAC, and OXFAM, which are all members of the PSEA Network and have PSEA and Child Safeguarding Focal Points in place. All UN Women implementing partners closely coordinate their livelihoods activities with relevant stakeholders, seeking approval from the RRRC and CiCs, with the latter periodically visiting MPWCs to observe activities. MPWCs also benefit from UNFPA SRHR counselling and support. UN Women looks forward to contributing to the development of a strategy for the new Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector and will ensure that all project activities are closely coordinated with the Sector Working Group and with relevant authorities. With its implementing partners, UN Women will ensure active participation in the sector meetings and regular information sharing. In addition, UN Women has been co-charing the Gender in Humanitarian Action Working Group (GiHA WG) and will work towards strengthening the coordination and collaboration among GiHA WG and LSDS. UN Women will continue to actively contribute to HoSO and sector coordination meetings, and will aim to further contribute the information sharing, coordination and synergies among the coordination mechanisms it is participating.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The programme will ensure the safety, dignity, and rights of the targeted women, girls and gender-diverse population. The programme will implement do-no-harm principles. The programme activities include psychosocial counselling and referral pathways, awareness-raising sessions on reducing GBV, child marriage, polygamy, and women's economic empowerment and leadership activities. This will also reduce the impact of physical and psychological harm that arises from violence, coercion, deliberate deprivation, and other threats. The programme will assist participants in claiming their rights and accessing appropriate services. The MPWC is restricted to male participants.

3.2 - Gender

The programme participants are women, girls, a gender-diverse persons, and other marginalized groups in the Rohingya and host communities. The programme will address the gender norms in the community and provide sex, age, and disability disaggregated data (SAAD) in the sector. The programme has different activities/training to address gender norms to support improving women's empowerment and leadership. Furthermore, it will increase women's mobility and participation in decision-making.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The programme will ensure “do no harm” to children. The participants will allow in the MPWC with their children and have facilities of breastfeeding corners and indoor games. The participants can make food for their children and clean facilities inside the MPWC. The programme will strictly maintain a child safeguarding policy. The training participants also receive the key messages on prevention of child marriage and PSEA at the MPWCs.

3.4 - Diversity

The programme will equally consider the participant regardless of social and ethnic backgrounds, genders, sexual orientations, and abilities etc. Women and girls are the targeted beneficiaries of the programme. In addition, project activities will provide tailored activities for gender-diverse persons, including transgender (hijra). All participants have equal access to the livelihoods and skills development services provided through MPWCs. UN Women will improve its disability inclusion during the project implementation. UN Women will organize disability inclusion and livelihood development training to its 60 Rohingya volunteers. Following the training, UN Women will develop an Action Plan for Disability Inclusion, to increase the inclusiveness of its MPWCs and community outreach.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will follow the core humanitarian standards and AAP guideline. UN Women will ensure effective participation of the community, including the most excluded, in the planning, implementing and monitoring of share the project. Project related information will be shared with the community in transparency. There is an existing complaint mechanism system in the MPWCs, which includes complaint box and hotline number. Through this mechanism, participant can share their complaints and feedback both verbally and in written. UN Women is also piloting advancing its complaint and feedback mechanism, by engaging women leaders for collecting the feedback, to ensure greater reach and among women. The received complaints and feedback are addressed within a specific timeframe and the feedback provider is provided an update on the steps taken related to their feedback, to ensure full accountability.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The MPWC infrastructure is environmentally friendly. The participants will receive training on eco-friendly jute products and homestead gardening. Further, they will engage in production and will receive materials. The centre will be considered an emergency shelter during flash floods or other natural disasters. In addition, DRR awareness and preparedness sessions will be provided for the participants.
