Project Summary

This project will contribute to preventing and treating malnutrition by providing infant and young child feeding interventions, Micro-nutrient deficiency prevention, control, and improve equitable access to high-quality, lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW.
The project will be implemented by nutrition Rapid Response Teams (RRTs) and nutrition facilities.
These distinctive RRTs will conduct HH visits and orientation sessions in areas populated with IDPs and affected people in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates. These RRTs will screen, refer and follow up children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished and provide emergency IYCF counselling for mothers whose children are moderately malnourished. Moreover, RRTs and nutrition facilities will distribute nutrition supplements and micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children, pregnant and lactating women. Also, they will screen the children regarding general danger signs and educate their mothers about them.
They will do hygiene promotion through their activities and will distribute IEC materials for targeted PLW, girls, and boys. There will be 23 nutrition RRTS teams and nutrition facilities distributed in various locations in Idleb and Aleppo. Each team will provide basic nutrition services (MUAC screening, distribution micro-nutrient, high-energy biscuits, and case referral for treatment). The project duration is 24 months and the target beneficiaries are 115000 including boys and girls <5 years, pregnant and lactating women from IDPs and Host communities,
On the other hand, this project is an important foundation for integrated primary health and nutrition services and community-based outreach activities, it will include a network of RRT, to provide integrated health and nutrition services focusing on IYCF counseling and screening of children under 5 and referral for treatment of malnutrition at a community level. These RRT will be linked with 10 health facilities. This project will also contribute to treating malnutrition cases in 10 facilities in Idlib and Aleppo.
The project will also focus on building the capacity of the health and nutrition staff on IYCF counseling to provide integrated health and nutrition services. The health facility staff and RRT will be trained in aspects related to child health care and nutritional screening and referral, they will also be provided with sufficient education to help to raise the awareness of the related communities. RRT will cover a big gap resulting from poor coverage of nutritional services in health facilities. QC will provide training on IYCF principles for all staff in RRT and health facilities, and this training will be conducted for 6times, each time will include 25 people and it will be organized in Syria.

4 - Needs

After 10 years of conflict, the humanitarian situation in Syria remains alarming as violence and displacement continue to have a devastating impact on millions of people's lives. Immense humanitarian needs persist, including but not limited to WASH, food, health, and nutrition. According to HNO 2021, an estimated 4.3 million people (1.7 million pregnant and lactating women and 3.3 million children under 59 months of age) required nutrition assistance. More than 1.8 million people have 'critical' needs whilst almost 1.9 million have 'severe' nutrition needs.According to the SMART survey conducted in June 2021, the prevalence of chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months increased from 19 % to 24.5 % over the same periodThe prevalence of GAM increased from 0.9 % to 4.7 % between May 2019 and June 2021 in Northwest Syria in Aleppo and Idlib governorates. Among PLW, the prevalence of acute malnutrition across northwest Syria is 12.4 percent. There are also significant differences across northwest Syria. Maternal Malnutrition continues to be a major public health problem in NWS especially in areas affected by displacements where 10.8% of Women of Reproductive Age (WTA) are malnourished, while this rate was higher between PLWs 12.4% According to the nutrition, surveillance report had conducted in September 2021 that included 9 districts, 35 Sub-district, and 50 spatial points in the northwest Syria surveillance team had reported.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by supporting 23 nutrition Rapid Response Teams "RRTs" in high severity communities and IDPs hosting areas. Each RRT will consist of " a nutritionist, 2 CHWs, Data entry". The teams will provide basic services such as screening of children, pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition signs, and referring them to the mobile clinic or SC, in addition to PSS cases. Moreover, the RRTs will disseminate IYCF-E messages among mothers of children under five through individual counseling and group sessions, facilitating IYCF-E campaigns and integrating them into the Child Health Days, registering all screened cases, and reporting to the nutritionists and data entry team members. In addition, the RRTs will distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant and lactating women, monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes, and distribute nutrition supplements. The RRTs will disseminate messages (IEC/BCC)on IYCF-E feeding practices, support hygiene promotion through raising WASH awareness by providing key messages such as Hand Washing, Food Processing, etc., identifying the abused cases among boys and girls 6-59months, pregnant and lactating women and refer them to child protection and GBV partners for appropriate action. QC ensures programs and projects are monitored, controlled, and evaluated with a recommendation to improve the services delivered in the targeted areas.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Gender-Based Violence (GBV) affects accessing nutrition services. Mitigating Measures: Provide nutrition services inaccessible easy to reach areas. Training of nutrition RRTs on Prevention and management of sexual violence. The presence of female staff in the nutrition teams increases access of women and girls to the nutrition services. Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) of women: Mitigating measures: Establish effective community-based complaints mechanisms, awareness-raising on SEA amongst nutrition team members, with continuous training on the topic. Performance management and monitoring of all team members. Risk: Lack of Duty of care for Nutrition RRTs. Mitigating measures: DOC policy in place and applied for all staff, measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights. Risk: Inequitable access to nutrition services by Vulnerable Groups. Mitigating measures: Implementation of standard-essential services package across the sector interventions, including improved quality reporting and robust monitoring through standardized SOPs. Addressing the specific needs of vulnerable groups through supporting dedicated nutrition subgroups groups(IYCF, CMAM, Capacity Building...etc.)

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Through all project locations, environmental risks may affect the activities' implementation in case a major disaster happens. The likelihood of this risk happening is low while the impact on project activities will be very high. QC will have a preparedness plan and mitigation mechanisms in all the activities and this is to be shared with all staff and schools’ principals. In this intervention, QC assumes the implementation of the activities will go smoothly in all targeted locations. However, some risks might be beyond QC's control. The reinforcement of health facilities does not provide sufficient protection for patients, exposing them to physical harm (some nutrition activities are provided within health facilities. These can be mitigated by putting in place task-shifting guidelines as part of care protocols - providing Training and adequate follow-up of competency - decentralizing services, clear messaging to communities around risks associated with health care facilities and underground care, Increased malnutrition rate due to increasing the opportunity of diseases and medical conditions that needs professional intervention. Mitigating measure: - Hire qualified medical staff - Distribute nutritional supplies and rations, QC assumes northwest Syria areas will be stable for the next year. The risk of increasing pressure on health services influx of IDPs is very high. Program activities will be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs in some areas suddenly increases.
