Project Summary

Based on community participation, the project is designed based on the following: reduce hunger by providing access to food to the population affected by the crisis especially people living inside camps to have access to life-saving food to meet their basic food needs, all year round in Idlib and Aleppo Governorates.
Key interventions will include:
- 5000 food baskets in Harim district and Jarablus District for 12 months.
- 3500 food vouchers in Dana, Al-Bab, and Maaretmasrin districts for 8 months in line with FSL cluster recommendation.
- Moreover, there will be 14000 RTEs that will be distributed in emergency situations.
- Free bread for the families and supporting the bakeries.
ATAA will identify, verify, and profile target beneficiaries through local councils according to the vulnerability selection criteria:
• Women headed households (widows, divorced, GBV survivors).
• Large families (more than 5).
• Families with disabled members.
• Families with low income (less than 50 USD)
• Families with elderly (men-women).
The total number of beneficiaries will be 202,500.
ATAA will remain AAP through open dialogue, that accurate and timely information (including goals, outcomes, and any vulnerability criteria to participate) will share with them.

4 - Needs

Entering the tenth year of Syria crisis, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across remain overwhelming. Based on results from a countrywide household assessment, an estimated 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity, an estimated increase of 8 percent from last year’s PIN. Due to food insecurity, protracted displacement, and lack of livelihoods. 2021 HRP There is significant overcrowding in several areas in northwest Syria. Food needs in Aleppo were soaring with no additional capacity to provide displaced families. In addition, HCs face significant pressures in terms of food supply and adequacy. Based on OCHA situation report N26, in NW Syria there are 2.7M IDPs; 1.5 M out of them living in IDP sites, moreover 80% of the overall population are women and children. The humanitarian situation for people living in northwest Syria remains dire. The impact of displacement, military operations, and security hazards are exacerbated by the rapid devaluation of the Syrian Pound and the outbreak of COVID-19, Humanitarian groups fear catastrophic consequences if the coronavirus numbers increase in overcrowded areas for the displaced in opposition-held areas in north Syria, as in Dana, Jarablous, and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts, there are a large number of IDPs still live in either camps or temporary housing, they lack care, health, and hygiene services. Many people have serious underlying conditions such as diabetes or chronic heart disease that constitute a conducive environment for the spread of the virus. Moreover, in 2021 1st Standard Allocation these sub-districts were ranked as very in need locations to be targeted with humanitarian assistance

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: Female beneficiaries/Project disabled beneficiaries experience sexual exploitation or abuse by the project trainers/other staff, which endangers lives of the perpetrator and victims. ATAA will lose the project funding accordingly. ATAA Office, assets and other field staff will get under risk, as well. Mitigation: ATAA have developed PSEA, Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure every effort is made to protect all Project BNFs. ATAA PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising (for staff regarding power dynamics, risk and vulnerabilities etc.), Prevention (awareness regarding the existing controls which are in place and how PSEA is prevented), Reporting (how participants can report and what are the specific steps which need to be taken regarding SEA cases) and Response (how and what ATAA will do when cases of SEA are reported). A similar mechanism is available for CSG where the staff are familiar with how to approach and deal with CSG cases of child abuse, exploitation or violence. All Project staff will sign ATAA CSG and PSEA Policies to ensure a safe environment for all Project beneficiaries. A dedicated channel for CSG and SEA related complaints will be available through dedicated WA number to ensure such complaints go to PSEA and CSG Focal Point in Turkey office directly.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Relative Stability in Jarabulus, Maaret Tamsrin and Dana sub-districts and access to people in need. No disruption in the supply chain, transport and distribution of commodities. Adequate humanitarian access is secured. There is social acceptance for the project and the people are prepared to interact with it. An analysis of all risks related to the project was conducted where risk has been classified into protection, environmental and security risks as well as COVID-10 risk. Protection and GBV risk: When selecting the beneficiaries, some people and communities or dominant groups in the project area do not accept the idea of participation of women in project activities. This risk will be mitigated by external awareness-raising sessions in the community on women's rights and the importance of their participation in the project and signing MOUs with local councils which include the participation of women in the project. The inability to meet the needs of all women in the region that we work in. This risk will be mitigated by transparency with local authorities and the community and by informing them very clearly of the selection criteria regarding women. Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected community members by anyone associated with the provision of aid. This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the field staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff relating to sexual exploitation and abuse.
