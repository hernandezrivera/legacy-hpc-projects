Project Summary

On 26th August 2018, IPCA and National Disarmament and Demobilization Commission (NDDRC) had carried out a needs assessment in Yambio, Mayendit and Leer Counties and has proposed a child protection project to rehabilitate and reintegrate the some released Children Associated with Armed Forces And Groups (CAAFAGs), into civilian lives through life skills and basic vocational skills training on i) Driving ii) Hair dressing iii) Tailoring, iv) Building &construction. Other mainstreamed activities will include, provision of counseling, mentorship services, GBV sensitization and conducting of public information campaigns on child rights. IPCA is convinced that the successful implementation of this project in Yambio and Leer Counties will wean the released CAAFAGs away from a culture of violence to one of peace and development. The long-term objective is to progress to other self reliance and employability skills development. This proposed project objectives are:
1) To provide Vocational skills to 20 girls and 25 boys released CAAfAGs in 2018.
2) To create awareness to communities, security organs and community leaders on the dangers of recruiting child soldiers.
3) To provide psychosocial support and trauma healing services to 45 CAAFAGs
The project activities will include provision of basic vocational self-reliance skills to Children Associated with Armed Forces And Groups (CAAFAGs), provision of psychosocial support services to Children Associated with Armed Forces And Groups (CAAFAGs), public information campaign on child rights and provision of Startup kits to the trained Children Associated with Armed Forces And Groups (CAAFAGs). This project will partner with National Disarmament and Demobilization Commission, Gbudue State, State Ministry of Education, Gbudue state Ministry of Labor, Yambio Vocational Training Center, CAAFAG working group and UNICEF, in Yambio.
This project targets to benefit 800 released Children Associated with Armed Forces and Groups (CAAFAGs) and 600 vulnerable children in Yambio and Leer Counties. The project period is 12 months with possibility of extension based on demand and availability of resources. IPCA will collaborate with government institutions, NGOs, CBOs, women and Youth groups in the project implementation process for sustainability of the project.

Needs

In December 2013, different factions of the Ruling Sudan Peoples’ Liberation Movement (SPLM) government split up, and the country degenerated into violent conflict having severe consequences for the state-building process of the young Nation (South Sudan). With recurrence of violence in South Sudan, July 2016, after just a short and fragile peace agreement signed, for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (ARCSS). As a result, the effects of the conflict continued to torment lives; many children are abducted, raided in numbers from their homes, schools and forcefully recruited into the armed forces. From many researches and assessment reports by UNICEF and the Ministry of General Education in South Sudan, these young generations have missed out an opportunity of formal education due to the long protracted conflicts in the country.In South Sudan today, at least 17,000 children are estimated to have been recruited by armed forces/groups in the country as at July 2017 with continued reports of forced recruitments in Yambio, Leer, Mayendit, Pibor, and Southern Koch among other locations (Issue 11 South Sudan Humanitarian Bulletin). Boys and girls have dropped out of school to support parents, including girls engaging in transactional sex further exposing them to negative reproductive consequences.With the recent releases carried out by the National Disarmament Demobilization Commission (NDDRC) in collaboration with UNICEF, 935 CAAFAG (257 girls and 678 boys) of Children were released in yambio and Pibor respectively in 2018 alone and more release are expected to be conducted in Leer County of Unity State.Initiative for Peace Communication Association (IPCA) in recognition of the apparent plight of these former child soldiers has proposed this project to build on its Child Protection Programme that aims among others, to rehabilitate and reintegrate the demobilized former child soldiers into civilian lives. IPCA with utmost exigency seeks to initiate programmes to retool these former child soldiers and engage them in meaningful projects such as Alternative Education Programme (ALP), Vocational skills training (brick laying and concrete, carpentry, driving and auto mechanics, Hair dressing, Tailoring etc), Income Generation Activities (IGAs), establishment of an interim placement care centres, provision of counselling services and sensitization and conduct of public information campaigns on child right. We are convinced that the successful implementation of any one or two such projects in objectively identified key projects locations will wean former child soldiers away from a culture of war to one of peace and development.

Provision of marketable vocational training courses and start-up kits to 400 released CAFAAGs, 150 vulnerable children and youth in Yambio and Leer in Southern Unity state, (250 girls and 300 boys).2. Provision of psychosocial support services to help children and youth deal with trauma and social and emotional pressures, (1200 girls and 14500 boys) in Yambio and Leer Counties.1. Advocate for release of more CAFAAGs from government forces, SPLA-IO, and other militia ranks in Yambio and Leer County in Southern Unity state.2. Support 10 community based safe spaces for children in Yambio, and Leer in Southern Unity State.3. Establish of FTR teams to conduct static and mobile reunifications services to missing children in Yambio and Leer County in Southern Unity state, targeting 150 children.4. To support and strengthen the CP referral pathways in Yambio and Leer in Southern Unity.5. To conduction public information on impact of child soldier, child rights and responsibility of the community, targeting 15,000 children, 12000, women and 10000 men.6. Conduct training to 60 community actors on comprehensive case management, structured PSS and referral (40 women and 20 men).7. To conduct quarterly needs assessments to inform children protection response and advocacy.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

This project supports regular stakeholders’ consultative meetings to brief all stakeholders on the objectives, activities and expected results of the project, to collect their insights to shape the outcome. This project supports beneficiaries’ focus group discussion on the content of the project to buy community ownership of the project for sustainability. IPCA will establish project feedback desk to enable beneficiaries seek information on the project and report their concerns including sexual exploitation and abuse cases.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project has taken into account cultural and religious norms of the communities in the project sites, thus all project IEC materials are design to address specific concerns of women, adolescent girls/boys, youth and men. IPCA will work together with the Ministry of gender, child and social welfare, ministry of health, community leaders, women groups, youth groups, faith groups and cultural leaders to collect their views on the development of all project IEC materials to enable that project information do-no harm to the targeted beneficiaries. IPCA will continuously review messages to incorporate the community perspective for smooth project implementation.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IPCA as a NGOs has enacted SEA code of conduct in 2017 and has established reporting lines for SEA cases across IPCA Board of Directors, staff, partners, stakeholders and beneficiaries. This project will strengthen the already established structures to effective SEA reporting, IPCA SEA focal person will inform all project staff, stakeholders and beneficiaries on SEA reporting procedures. IPCA establish village project management committees at the village levels to strengthen communication with beneficiaries. All SEA cases will be investigated and dealt by IPCA SEA focal person, IPCA SEA code of conduct and Laws of South Sudan.

The assessment team interacted with 92 CAFAAGs (50 boys and 42 girls) in Yambio County. Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

This project will operate quarterly forums to collect stakeholders’ perception on its activities and impact, this project will establish a feedback desk including email and phone lines to collect additional concerns from all stakeholders with focus on beneficiaries, and information collected from stakeholders will used to inform the project direction and other relevant interventions.
