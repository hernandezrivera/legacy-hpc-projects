Project Summary

NGO is intending to improve the livelihoods status of vulnerable host communities through integrated livelihoods supports. The project will address the needs of the effected community to access to market and promote income generating opportunity and protect the productive assets in Hassaka, Deir ez Zour and Raqqa governorate. 1400 households are protected and/or restored income generating through appropriate market skills/inputs/services, as well with appropriate agriculture inputs (seeds, fertilizers, tractor ploughing hours) and farming tools needed to increase production both for domestic consumption and for commercial to increase household income. Appropriate engagement will be made with smallholder farmers accompanied by market assessments to understand inputs needed, availability and market prices to best design appropriate input distribution modalities. In addition, poor livestock breeders will get cash assistance for fodder to protect productive assets and improve food consumption in household level and community.
In addition, 500 households will be engaged in gainful temporary employment through cash for work involving rehabilitation of prioritized community infrastructures. Cash for work activities are intended to provide households with much-needed cash injections to meet their basic need while rehabilitating community assets and infrastructure that are vital for sustaining and restoring the livelihoods of the communities.
The NGO will mainstream gender equality and promote gender inclusiveness, allowing the most vulnerable and disadvantaged women, girls, boys, and men to have equal participation in the program and to realize benefits for their own wellbeing. A participatory beneficiary identification and selection including agreeing on affirmative action to register at least 50% female beneficiaries. Community-led project feedback and complaint mechanism will be established to foster equal chances for female and male to voice their complaints and feedback. Benefits and progress will be tracked through regular monitoring and data will be collected through program reports.

4 - Needs

As a result of the deteriorating economic situation paired with the effects of COVID-19 on the community, the population across Syria is facing an extreme decrease in purchasing power. 88% of people in NESE are estimated to have insufficient income to support their basic needs and reported a lack of employment opportunities hindered this further. The skillset available does not tend to match any employment opportunities that are available. NES is the ‘food basket’ of Syria as the largest agricultural region; any impact on agriculture in Hassaka affects the whole country in access to food. However, ongoing barriers due to COVID-19 as well as damage to the irrigation infrastructure and limited earning opportunities – not to mention devastating wildfires and other climate-induced shocks – have all contributed to the worsening situation.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities will focus on market-based activities and agricultural livelihoods support. In both rural and urban areas, NGO will employ its bundled services approach, integrating provision of capital and inputs with market-relevant and climate-smart vocational skills training. In urban and rural areas in AL-Raqqa and AL Hasska governorate, NGO will provide cash transfers designated for livestock and agricultural inputs, and mobile agro-vocational training in climate-smart agricultural and livestock production. Due to low supply and quality of inputs in the markets, the trainings will provide information about input quality and will focus on breeding and fodder production for livestock, and seed saving, quality testing, and bio-fertilizer production for agricultural value chains. In Raqqa City, the NGO through Livelihood Center (opened in February 2019) will provide vocational training, business and life skills training, and business grants, with a focus on market-relevant skills like agriculture and livestock as well as non-agricultural livelihoods based on market demand and opportunities. NGO will equip conflict-affected individuals and their families with practical, transferable skills to increase their resilience to environmental, political, and conflict shocks. With training, farmers will be better prepared for the next growing season even given unpredictable weather and shifting political and conflict-driven context. Training recipients will have greater opportunities to earn income and support themselves in the future. In addition, a cash-for-work program will improve household’s access to temporary income to support their basic needs. This will be delivered through a rehabilitation community project service.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Project staff and volunteers including the Cash and Livelihoods team, with support from the protection mainstreaming officer, will sensitize on the do no harm principles and implementation of PSEA. By working with community leaders, community representatives, and elders, NGO undertakes through sensitization and awareness-raising of stakeholders on the rights and responsibilities of all project parties. Beneficiaries are made aware of their entitlements, freedom of choice, and how support is provided. Stakeholders and project staff are sensitized to exercise and abide by the core humanitarian principles of impartiality, neutrality, humanity, and independence while providing and accessing support.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The NGO will exercise program integration with the protection sector to sensitize and provide protection assistance to survivors and ensure referral pathways including during beneficiary selection through referrals, data triangulation and sharing, reporting and follow-up of beneficiaries receiving integrated support.Mitigation: The project will be inclusive in targeting the most vulnerable groups. During project launch, NGO will ensure the representation of marginalized groups in reviewing the selection criteria. The NGO will conduct FGD’s to increase access to beneficiaries who may not have access to the complaint feedback mechanism committee. NGO will use ONA mobile application or CommCare/Kobo for registration. NGO will ensure non-disclosure and protection of beneficiary’s personal data. During the project launch, the NGO will be engaged with stakeholders; Humanitarian Affairs Office, local council, municipality, agriculture council, women and youth representatives to explain the project target, services, and expectations from community leaders and beneficiaries. Beneficiary selection criteria will be shared and agreed upon registration to mitigate the risk of impartiality or inequality. NGO Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) will embed an inclusive Gender-Sensitive Feedback and Complaint Committees. Risk: local authority request to share part or all assistance amount. Threats from community who don't receive cash. mitigation: inform beneficiaries that this assistance is free and no one can take in any way a part or all the assistance amount. Engage support from local authorities. Advise beneficiaries to keep assistance in safe place. Explain the project and criteria before any activity, engage HAO and DCC.
