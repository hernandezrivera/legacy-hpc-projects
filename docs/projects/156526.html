Project Summary

The project will support 10 ambulance cars with staff, salaries, running costs and medical supplies necessary to provide referral services between primary and secondary healthcare facilities. The system will be integrated with the referral network in the area and will work and coordinate with all health facilities working within the service area to provide the referral service for patients. Patients will be provided with the necessary medicines during their transportation.

Needs

Entering the eighth year of the crisis, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. Some 13.1 million people in Syria requires humanitarian assistance. 1 Of these, 5.6 million people are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and services. 2 Conflict continues to be the principal driver of humanitarian needs, with the civilian population in many parts of the country exposed to significant protection risks which threaten life, dignity and wellbeing on a daily basis.Within the overall 13.1 million people in need, and notwithstanding individual vulnerabilities related to age, gender, disability and socioeconomic status, there are 5.6 million facing particularly acute needs.Access to health care remains poor, especially in UN-declared besieged and hard to reach areas due to a lack of functioning facilities; shortages of health staff, drugs and medical supplies; and persistent clashes between various armed groups. Referral systems for trauma, emergency obstetric care and surgical services are insufficient, while there remains limited capacity for mobile clinics to reach out to fluid temporary settlements and host communities. The maintenance of medical equipment is also a core challenge, compounded by restrictions related to international sanctions on Syria, which prevent partners from importing critical spare parts.11.3 Million people in need for healthcare services in Syria, high percentage of them are in the NW of Syria, (5.5 M Male, 5.8 M Female – 1.3 M Children 0-4 years, 3.4 M Children 5-17 years, 6.1 M Adults 18-59 years, and 0.5 M Elderly >59 years).(HNO 2018)This situation and the lack of working referral system had led to huge need for a referral system to transport patients to the health facilities that can provide the healthcare services they need.

Implementation of activities

The project aims to strengthen the referral system and emergency services in Aleppo Governorate and Euphrates Shield Area of Syria via: 1- Operating 10 ambulances in Aleppo Governorate and Euphrates Shield Area and supporting them with the required staff and running cost. The referral system in Aleppo Governorate will covers 11 villages which are (Berqoom, Meznaz, Rasm Aleis, Zmar, Tel hidyeh, Kamari, Talafeh, Alothmaniyeh, Tel Alnabariz, Sheikh Ali, Kanater).2- In this project, 3 new ambulances will be purchased along with 7 cars already existed.The first two months of the project will include purchasing of the new cars, transferring them to the implementing area, recruiting the staff required for the project, and do all the preparation work required to start delivering the service for people in need within the next ten months. 8 cars will work in Aleppo Governorate and two will be working in Euphrates Shield Area. The new cars will be working in Aleppo Governorate.The project provides its services to all patients of all ages; children, women and men of productive age and the elderly, with, to PWDs, or IDPs. And it aims to coordinate with the local councils and health authorities to strengthen its emergency referral system by arranging for timely transfer of the patients with illnesses and injuries to the next point of definitive care. Patients are provided with the basic medications while transported to the next health facility as possible and needed.Part of the implementation activities is to collect feedback data from the beneficiaries throughout simple and clear complaint mechanisms, the data collected through these mechanisms are analyzed and reviewed by the quality team, and the necessary actions are undertaken to solve any issues faced by the beneficiaries regarding the provision of the service.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Increase risks of Sexual Exploitation from staff while receiving healthcare services.Likelihood: low. Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:1. Providing simple and clear compliant mechanisms2. All staff commits to sign a code of conduct3. Providing PSEA training for all staff4. Services are provided to all people regardless of gender male of female Risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to providing healthcare services to specific members of the community.Likelihood: low. Impact: Medium.Mitigating Measures:Providing healthcare service to all people in need regardless of any other criteria Risk: Difficulty of reaching health facilities/services for PWDs.Likelihood: low. Impact: Medium. Mitigating Measures:All ambulances are equipped with the required tools to help provide the referral service to all PWDs. Risk: Possible inconsistency with local traditions and culture.Likelihood: low. Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:The nature of the project excludes this risk as the referral services are provided urgently asap for people in need, in addition to coordinate the service provision with the local health authorities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:• There are safe storages where medical supplies and medicines can be safely stored.• The closing is often temporary.• The project basically depends on the ambulances which are already transferred to the implementing area during the first two months of the project Risk: The providers may not commit to their contractsLikelihood: low. Impact: High.Mitigating Measures:We deal with many dealers to avoid this risk. Risk: Military conflict between local armed entities.Likelihood: low.
