Project Summary

DRC through the Mobile and static team based in the field area will improve access to provision of appropriate shelters and Non-Food Items (SNFIs) to the most vulnerable conflict and disaster affected people is South Sudan. The project targets provision of cash and voucher assistance for shelter and NFIs, provision of in-kind Non-Food Items assistance using the DRC static team based in Malakal POC and emergency SNFI support and emergency shelters to crisis-affected populations in hard to reach locations across South Sudan. The DRC mobile response team is composed of Protection, S/NFI, WASH and CCCM sectors. DRC as a protection focused agency will mainstream Protection, Gender Based Violence (GBV) mitigation and prevention and AAP to enhance the overall quality of S-NFI response in 2021. According to shelter-NFI cluster strategy, DRC will participate in joint missions among others to avoid duplication and better coordination among the Shelter-NFI cluster partners and other clusters. DRC will draw on both its own funds to procure distribution items and the SNFI cluster pipeline as most appropriate to ensure an efficient and timely response. In accordance with SNFI cluster priorities, there will be an ongoing focus on monitoring, evaluation, assessment, and learning (MEAL) activities through post-distribution monitoring activities and integrating quick monitoring exercises conducted for all distributions done by mobile teams.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continue to face extreme level of humanitarian needs. According to the 2021 HNO SNFI report 2021 , the need of emergency shelter and non-food items will affect the living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 2.1 million people. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers are also identified with shelter and NFI needs. The number of people in need increased by 100,000 in 2021, compared to 2 million in 2020, due to an increase in the number of returnees It is estimated that more than 70 per cent of newly displaced people outside PoC sites and collective centers are unable to meet their basic household needs and will require a humanitarian intervention. More than 50 per cent of returnees also need emergency shelter and NFI support. Insecurity in areas affected by sub-national and localized violence have led to people being newly displaced and have created new vulnerabilities. The situation has further been escalated by lack of access to naturally grown shelter materials, the seasonal flooding, particularly in Jonglei, Unity and Upper Niles states People in need are found in three different contexts: in camps, including PoC sites and other collective centers, outside of camps, including host communities who share scarce resources, and in relatively stable locations where people are returning to their places of origin. There is approximately 1.65 million women, men, girls, boys and older persons living with inadequate shelter and NFIs. Beyond survival, the shelter provides people with protection and security. Shelter protects a person’s wellbeing, dignity, and health. Shelter is the foundation and basis for engagement in livelihoods and economic activities. The shelter is essential to have a feeling of security, identity, and belonging and fundamentally underpins peace and stability. Without shelter and the basic NFIs or having a strong social support network, women, children, the elderly, and people living with disabilities are especially vulnerable due to their roles in society. Their health, wellbeing, safety are compromised, and security is at risk. Emergency shelter and NFI needs are greatest in Upper Nile, Unity, Jonglei, Lakes, Warrap, Western Bahr Ghazel, Central and Western Equatorial.(HNO-2021 Lifesaving access to emergency shelter and NFIs needs to be provided immediately to displaced people to prevent further loss of life and increased vulnerability. Access to naturally grown shelter materials has continued to be a challenge due to recurrent sub-national and localized violence, compounded by seasonal flooding, particularly in Jonglei, Unity and Upper Niles states. In 2021, needs will continue to exist for vulnerable displaced IDPs and returnees due to the lack of access to services; flooding; sub-national violence in areas of displacement and return; and challenges in accessing homes or property after prolonged displacements in PoC sites/IDP camps. There will be a need for better shelter options in IDP camps instead of the widely used plastic sheets. The transition offers an opportunity to decongest PoCs and takes incremental steps towards meeting minimum shelter standards in terms of living spaces (3.5 square meters per person) since the government will be responsible for the camp administration. Through its SNFI focal point, DRC will continue advocating for intercluster assessments in the areas of need to improve the speed and quality of response to the affected population. Further ensures that all multisectoral needs are covered and responded to. DRC will continue to observe Covid 19 measures to curb the spread, disseminate Covid-19 awareness messages to the community, target distribution of masks to the community's most vulnerable members. Promote and advocate for virtual meetings to minimize contact. To conduct community awareness during FGD and community meetings on how communities would control the floods' impact within the areas as a coping mechanism.

2 - Project activities

DRC will conduct the following activities;Activity 1: Provision of cash and voucher assistance for shelter and NFIs: DRC program based in Malakal, will implement a shelter program targeting the most vulnerable households among returnees, IDPs, and host community. DRC will conduct the shelter and NFIs activities jointly with the protection team in the proﬁling of the most vulnerable populations with acute shelter and NFIs needs and exposure to risk due to their current shelter conditions. Based on the approved designs and BoQ by the cluster the Cash intervention rates will be determined by a thorough market analysis and protection risk analysis that determines the cash is a safe/appropriate modality for beneﬁciaries. The project targets 1500 beneficiaries (300HH) with cash for shelterActivity 2: Provision of in-kind Non Food Items assistance Distribution of life- sustaining NFI items to most vulnerable IDP and returnee individuals/households in frontline areas based on the cluster analysis report identifying the needs and the protection and do no harm considerations. The S/NFI response missions will be conducted over a period of one to two weeks, and the same team who conducted the analysis would be deployed to the location. Response interventions include distribution of emergency NFI kit based on the needs assessment findings, the cluster standard and recommendation. The project targets 21,500 beneficiaries 4,250HHs with NFIs, DRC will use pipeline supplies from the cluster for its interventions.Activity 3: Conduct six assessments and response missions in hard to reach and conflict/disaster affected areas mostly targeting IPC 4 and 5 locations across the country. DRC mobile team based in Juba will be deployed to the high priority locations triggered by SNFI cluster, NAWG or from other cluster priorities for two to three weeks assessment where for assessments/analysis will use SNFI cluster tools, and will aim to identify the S/NFI needs and best mechanisms of delivering S/NFI assistance considering the protection risks associated with such assistance and will use the community engagement/community structure in all stages of assessment and response and a key consideration of DO NO HARM. Based on the findings, a response mission will be planned and where appropriate for emergency shelter and non-food items to the most vulnerable population in response mission. Close coordination with state focal person (SFP) from SNFI cluster will be maintained throughout from trigger to the assessment where the assessment report will be share and approved from the SFP including Core pipeline request where need to response and throughout the response period the coordination will remain in place with national and sub national level and the response report will be shared in the end. During Mobile Response Team (MRT) project period aims to conduct 06 assessment/analysis that guide the response. Through a mobile response modality, DRC will distribute life-saving shelter materials to IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable members of host communities affected by sudden shocks/stresses and/or crisis. The S/NFI response missions will be conducted over a period of one to three weeks, and DRC will aim for the same team who conducted the analysis would be deployed to the location. Response interventions include distribution of emergency shelter kit, usually plastic sheet and rope, based on the cluster standard and recommendation. DRC will use pipeline supplies from the cluster for its interventions as well as its own prepositioned stock. Rapid response monitoring will be conducted in the days following distributions and will be factored into mission planning and daily activities. DRC will meet SNFI cluster standards by ensuring that a PDM is done for at least one in three distributions (four total). DRC will deploy members of the mobile response team to Malakal to increase the response speed, thus reducing the time for mobilizing human resources.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Through the protection teams, DRC conducts regular awareness-raising with beneficiaries on PSEA, including reporting mechanisms. DRC further operates complaints desks to enable community members to ﬂag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously. Through its activities, DRC will ensure conﬁdentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC has several methods for lodging complaints which are accessible to most vulnerable members of the community including those that are not literate. These include community visits to receive complaints (oral and written), FCRM desks which are staffed by both male and female staff, having gender disaggregated FGD during PDM including youth participants, and individual interviews during PDMs. DRC also receives complaints through community representatives who are more accessible to beneficiaries. Other methods include suggestion boxes and in person office visits.Having several mechanisms allows for beneficiaries to choose a method that is most comfortable and practical for them and ensures there is at least one mechanism in place in all locations. Apart from the aforementioned channels, DRC will also gather feedback from the vulnerable groups through community meetings and surveys. DRC Staff have been trained to handle personal complaints and inquiries in a confidential manner, and protection issues are referred to DRC protection team or to protection partners to ensure sensitive issues are handled by qualified staff only.DRC will continue to engage the beneficiaries in a consultative manner, engaging them together with their local leader's representation and jointly working together of the protection and shelter NFIs sector to ensure that all sensitive protection matters are addressed and handled.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

DRC has a protection presence within the implementation field location with functional Feedback, Complaints, Response Mechanism (FCRM). They have further strengthened activity implementation through a community-based approach where the beneficiaries and the local leader's participation are involved throughout the project implementation. DRC further implements activities following the cluster APP tool kit's guidelines, thus improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC has a working CCCM help desk at the POC and malakal county in the upper Nile, where beneficiaries raise their complaints and receive feedback. CCCM and the protection sector will conduct community awareness on the importance of using CFMs desk for improvement of their service delivery.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

DRC always employs feedback mechanisms, from the start of assessment to the end of PDMs, to ensure all beneficiaries, including marginalized groups and vulnerable, have an active role in their project contribution and on ways they would prefer DRC carry out their assistance and improve response appropriateness. The project will factor revisions in upcoming activities and on situations where the feedback would not permit the revision due either their specific standards the beneficiaries will be enlightened in the explanation on the deliverables and their minimum requirements. DRC uses and will continue with a multisectoral approach to ensure that all sensitive protection matters within the community are approached from a protection perspective. DRC engages the beneficiaries in a consultative manner through their involvement with their local leader's representation. Dissemination of project information through meeting with the community and their local leaders and adopting a cultural design approach where locally available resources are considered in the shelter design. Ensuring the beneficiary feedback is considered in the project design and where improvement is required, like the size to meet the minimum standards community are involved and information and reason shared with the community.
