Project Summary

An estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) 1 or worse through December going into 2019, according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018. Forecast average to above-average Deyr (October-December 2018) rains, expected average to above-average off-season harvests, and favorable market conditions are expected to further improve food security conditions through December 2018. However, acute food insecurity remains prevalent among pastoral populations who lost most of their livestock and became destitute during the 2017 drought and Cyclone Sagar; riverine populations affected by flooding in April and May 2018; and others experiencing large-scale, protracted displacement due to conflict and natural hazards.
The forecasted El Nino phenomena and associated abnormal rainfall in last quarter of 2018 is expected contribute to an increase in production while at the same time risks of shocks, including loss of production are real. Indeed, the expected above normal rains may cause the deterioration of the situation of IDPs who are already in a precarious situation. Under these circumstances Somali household’s recent food security improvements risk deterioration if the humanitarian assistance is not sustained particularly among vulnerable population groups. Assistance should be sustained including livelihood support to make lasting improvements to the Somalis food security situation.
In Galmudug, an inter-agency assessment report established that an estimated 28,000 people were displaced from Xarardheere district between 16 April and the third week of July 2018. Of these, 26,000 were in the assessed areas of Cadaado and Hobyo districts, whilst another 2,000 people had reportedly settled in the villages in Hobyo district. Amongst the IDPs there were 430 unaccompanied and separated children. Alarming levels of food insecurity were observed among new IDPs, with respondents reporting consuming only one meal per day.
An estimated 6,966 people (around 1,061 HHs) have fled their homes in lower and middle Shabella, Bakol and Bay regions in search of safety and security due to natural disasters, armed conflict and escalating violence since mid-2011 and settled in five IDP camps in Banadir. An assessment by Human Appeal in Banadir in September established that IDP groups most susceptible to food shortages were reported to be the newly arrived IDPs. Child-headed and female-headed households, as well as households headed by older persons, also face major constraints in accessing food due to high prices of food commodities if they are available. People with low mobility or those in remote locations far from the main street of KM 13 had no access to humanitarian assistance since they arrived in the assessed locations.
CPD proposes to intervene by providing immediate access to food to the vulnerable communities and IDP’s through unconditional cash transfers and conditional cash (through cash for work in water structures rehabilitation. The water structures will be used by the community to enhance their resilience towards drought and conserve the environment they live in. CPD will also strive to restore livelihoods of the communities by providing farm and agricultural inputs and support like seeds, tools and training.

Activities

1. The unconditional cash transfers will be provided for 1,500 IDPs, urban and rural destitute households in Banadir, Lower Shabelle and Galmudug State regions prioritizing IDP women and child headed households. 2. The project will also target the tract land preparation support for 5,000 River Shabelle riverine farmers whose farmland land lies abandoned in Marka, and Afgooye Districts. The farmers will be supported with 250 tonnes of certified seeds.3. The project will also support training on modern crop husbandry to help increase production of crops. 4. The project will support the rehabilitation of 16 strategic water points used by livestock, specifically waterpans in Galgadud and Mudug regions through CfW targeting 1,936 labourers of skilled/unskilled with women being prioritized. 5. The 650 farmers shall also be supported to keep beehives using locally proved beehives and to have backyard poultry with Women headed households being prioritized. 6. Fodder cultivation support for 550 riverine farmers and agro pastoralists in Lower Shabelle and Galmudug State regions. 7. Support 380 fishing households in Hobyo (Mudug region) and Marka (Lower Shabelle Region), with fishing inputs and refrigeration units to support the fishery.8. The project will support the adoption of modern Hermetic Storage Technology (HST) to 5,000 riverine farmers to cut down on post harvest loses in Lower Shabelle region through training and provision of bio pesticides to control armyworm and other crop pests to eliminate them from farmer’s fields.9. The project will facilitate the formation of gendered Community Resource Committees (CRCs) in all the areas the project targets take up proactive roles in the management of communally owned resources. 10. Carry out restocking of goats, sheep and pack animals for 800 most vulnerable agro pastoralists and pastoralists in Galmudug State regions with special focus to women headed households (7 shoats per HH and 1 Pack Animal per HH.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

These projects are going to involve the community together with their leaders and the local authorities. The rehabilitation of water structures and training and support of beneficiaries on farming will ensure that food security is increase for long term gains. The beneficiaries will also be trained and supported on alternative livelihoods that will ensure they have food on the table even during droughts. Committees elected to manage the water structures will be given the mandate to run the assets on behalf of the community after training through a proper hand-over of the assets. The committees will be registered with government/local authority agencies to give them legal basis to manage the assets on behalf of the communities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The beneficiaries risk losing their source of livelihoods every time there is a drought, which is a cycle. The measures CPD will put in place will ensure that first those who have lost their livelihoods are saved from hunger by being provided with food. They will then be helped to restore their sources of livelihoods through restocking and rehabilitation of water structures to maintain their animals and enhance resilience. They will also be trained on alternative sources of livelihood like fishing along the coastal communities. CPD will collaborate with community Child right monitors, community stakeholders (leaders, women, religious leaders, elders, IDPs gate keepers, teachers, local authority to ensure that food support given to beneficiaries takes into consideration the most vulnerable first and is given in a dignified and impartial manner. Support will be sought from security forces and justice institutions in these communities to enhance the child protection activities, monitoring mechanisms, documentation and reporting.CPD will liaise with the protection cluster for guidance, and other clusters to ensure the other essential services like WASH, health and nutrition, and Shelter are also extended to the beneficiaries in their locality.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and girls face the risk of violence of different nature in less secure environments. The projects are going to be designed after consultation with the different categories of community members so that activities are done in safe environments. Activities will be carried out in set times during the day with security officers from the local authorities providing security. This will enable vulnerable women and girls to go back to their residences before darkness when incidents of sexual violence occurs. CPD will ensure the project design and delivery process will be sensitive to the needs of the most vulnerable segment of the target groups in terms of gender inclusion and safety and security of the most vulnerable groups.The beneficiaries will be registered and from the registration data, vulnerable categories will be identified. Women-headed and child-headed households will be given priority. Community committees will be selected on a criteria that gives equal opportunity to both gender. Decisions will be made taking both genders' concerns into consideration.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

CPD will incorporate issues that cause conflict due to scarce resources in the designing of projects for displaced communities. For communities that have been displaced because of water resources conflicts, CPD is going to identify strategic water sources and improve them to serve both communities sufficiently. The project will also take a Do No Harm and participatory approach, ensuring that interventions and interactions are context specific and involve the community in all aspects of the project, before, during and after implementation thereby reducing risk of conflict over resources.
