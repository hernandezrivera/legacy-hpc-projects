Project Summary

Malnutrition continues to be the leading cause of mortality and morbidity globally. A child with severe acute malnutrition has 12 times chances of dying than a health child. Given the challenges in South Sudan, such as floods, conflict, and food insecurity, the situation is even worse because those have continued to contribute to the high levels of malnutrition. The 2022 Nutrition Programme data shows no improvement compared to 2021 meaning the situation is getting worse. The admission rates in 2022 increased by 24% and that is alarming. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. The other main challenges include flooding, insecurity, climatic changes and lack of access to WASH services which increases the likelihood of water borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout.
In 2023, World Vision will join the humanitarian community to address the needs by implementing the nutrition programs which aligns with the Nutrition Cluster objectives which include: Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2023, Objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2023, Objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2023 and Objective 4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2023
The lifesaving nutrition interventions will be implemented in Warrap, Central Equatoria, Western Equatoria and Upper Nile. The proposed nutrition project will be integrated with health, WASH, protection and FSL activities. World Vision will prioritize life-saving treatment services targeting children under five years of age, PLW, and chronically ill persons as the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs. Prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition will be enhanced. Additionally, the response will expand on nutrition with other clusters to address key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, sub-optional childcare and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation. It will prioritise areas within World Vision program areas where high prevalence of acute malnutrition, conflict and displacement co-exist.

1 - Needs

As of December 2021, an estimated 5.3 million people were reached with some form of humanitarian assistance. However, to enable the response to people impacted by flooding, humanitarian organisations were compelled to the re-program in-country support, which resulted in a reduction of food assistance to internally displaced people, refugees and communities in acute food and livelihood crises. These unmet needs will increase vulnerability for populations in 2022 as more people are likely to experience severe food insecurity. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). Of those, an estimated 2 million people including 1.3 million children under the age 5 and another 675,548 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be acutely malnourished. Basing on the FSNMS Round 28 and the 2022 SMART survey results, it is estimated that a total of 2,175,546 people (1,469,919 in the host community, 315,503 returnees, and 390,125 internally displaced people -IDPs), will suffer from acute malnutrition (AM) in SS in 2023. These include a total of 1,437,735 million children under five years (733,245 girls, 704,490 boys) of which 353,606 will have SAM (180,339 girls and 173,267 boys), 1,084,129 (552,906 girls and 531,223 boys) will have MAM, and 737,812 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will have acute malnutrition (AM-PLW). Sadly, more children and pregnant and lactating women in SS will suffer from acute malnutrition in 2023 compared to 2022 (increased by 158, 201). Furthermore, 26,555 (7.9%) out of a total of 337,120 refugees residing in 11 counties in SS will need treatment for acute malnutrition, including 5,139 SAM children, 14,347 MAM children, 4,337 AM-PLW, and 2,731 patients with HIV or TB. Two of the World Vision’s three targeted states (Warrap and Upper Nile) have Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 percent (very high acute malnutrition level- WHO classification). From the 2022 Integrated Phase Classification for Acute Malnutrition analysis (IPC- AM), all the 10 target counties are classified in phase 3(Serious) or phase 4 (critical). The target counties have experienced multiple challenges namely flooding and intercommunal violence. In Warrap State, a conflict erupted between the Twic and Abyei communities causing displacement of over 20,466 persons in February 2022 (Joint IRNA assessment). From August to November 2022 the same community has experienced widespread floods which destroyed crops, hampered access and left 38, 130 IDPs and the host communities in a dire need of humanitarian assistance (Coordinated assessment IRNA report August 2022). In August, 2022 fighting erupted in Tonga, in Panyikang county displacing communities from Tonga, Adididiang, Obia to Malakal POC site. An estimated 4,606 households were displaced and their belongings completely destroyed. The same conflict extended to Fashoda county displacing 1683 households and 10,613 (Male: 4,882 and Female: 5,731) individuals. An assessment conducted between August 22nd to 23rd, 2022 revealed that the entire Upper Nile has had significant flooding in all the target counties of Melut, Panyikang, Fashoda and Manyo. According to 2022 IPC analysis, an estimated 6.6 million people (53% of the population) face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 2.2 million people face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and 61000 in catastrophic (phase 5). These figures are projected to increase in 2023 to an estimated 7.76 million people (62.7 % of the total population). In addition, too few children 6-23 months (4.6%) have access to minimum acceptable diet diversity (MADD) for them to grow and develop well. This is compounded by reduced funding to the blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP), restricting support to areas with extreme needs only.

2 - Project activities

In 2023, World Vision will continue to support: i) The outpatient therapeutic program (OTPs) which focuses on treating children identified as having severe acute malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications ii) The supplementary Therapeutic program (TSFPs) that focuses on treating children aged 6-59months with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) without medical complications iii) Stabilisation Centre (SC) which provides inpatient care for CU5 having SAM with medical complications iv) Community Mobilisation which focuses on awareness, mobilisation and involvement of the community to participate in prevention of acute malnutrition v) The Family MUAC program targets all House Holds in the catchment areas with children aged 6-59 months. It involves training mothers, family members or caregivers of CU5 to conduct screening of their children for malnutrition using the MUAC tape at home. This approach was adopted to prevent the spread of COVID-19 while continuing to identity and provide treatment to malnourished children. The activities will be conducted in line with each of the four outputs outlined by the Nutrition Clusters: Sectoral Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2023, World Vision will Prevent malnutrition through provision of blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP), conducting behavior change communication and through provision of micronutrient supplementation to prevent malnutrition. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The Nutrition project management will involve persons with special needs including the impaired and individuals who are not literate. The tools used during needs assessment, and distribution will specifically target these group of people, in order to gain their inputs. Data that is gathered will also be dis-segregated including during reporting. Community mobilisation for activities will also be done in a way that encourage them to actively participate in the project. Various local workers including interpreters will be recruited to disseminate information in local languages.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

World Vision will ensure that all staff are involved in the implementation of this project will involve and inform the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders at all stages of the project management cycle starting from the initial assessment, design, implementation and close out stages. The project participants will also be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process, quantity and quality of services they are getting from the project. Communities are asked which complaints and feedback mechanisms they prefer, with efforts to ensure that unique needs of vulnerable populations are met (for example, considering child- and gender-friendly methods of collecting feedback, accounting for literacy or individuals’ safety, protection, and access to mechanisms, etc.) Project participant feedback is compiled and shared with the project teams on a weekly basis. The feedback is then verified and responded to by the relevant teams. To accurately respond to feedback and complaints, an investigation is conducted on each feedback item submitted. Thus, after the team verifies the concerns raised, corrective measures are taken and incorporated into project implementation.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Complaints regarding any protection-related issue are handled by a Protection Officer to ensure confidentiality and to be able to determine an appropriate response to the feedback received, particularly in the event that such responses require CP or GBV intervention from the case management team. Any complaint involving sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be immediately escalated to the human resources team in Juba. All WVI staff are trained on SEA and all have signed WVI ’s code of conduct. Cases of SEA will be investigated by a team led by WVI South Sudan’s human resources staff in collaboration with the Safeguarding Manager in a safe and confidential manner. Strengthening the capacity of the stakeholders and staff.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In line with the Core Humanitarian Standards, WVI will maintain its commitment to engage with affected communities at all phases of the project cycle, through focus group discussions with women, men, boys and girls on issues concerning their education, WASH, and food security concerns. Establishing/strengthening local structures such as Project Management Committee is one example of how WVI engages the community in a sustainable and accountable manner to determine and support appropriate need-based responses. WVI's Quality Assurance framework ensures that each project implemented is carried out effectively and continually reviewed in line with community needs and in line with other humanitarian frameworks.
