Project Summary

This project aims at increasing access to quality lifesaving and life-sustaining essential health services for vulnerable and needy population during humanitarian situations in Sudan, 2024.
The project is targeting Khartoum, Red Sea, Gedaref, Sennar, Blue Nile, White Nile, Gezira, North Darfur, West Darfur, Central Darfur, River Nile, South Kordofan. Humnaitarian access will be an issue in some conflict active zones. SCI will be jointly assessing the situations prior the implementation. SCI with new innovative mode of service delivery were able to collaborate and coordinate successfully with Community Medical Response Teams in Active conflict zone of Khartoum state. Such teams the have been incentivized by SCI. Indeed, they utilize these incentives to buy medicines and provide mobile services in areas where they live and can access. These CMRTs are well-trained and having medical specialists among them where supervision and monitoring might be of low concern to SCI due to their high commitment towards serving needy and hard-to-reach population. This delivery modalities could be replicated in similar settings where humanitarian access would be an issue.
This project will strengthen health sector capacity to prevent, prepare for and deliver timely response to population affected by conflict, natural emergencies, disease outbreaks and pandemics. This will include a) procurement, prepositioning and rational use of essential medicines, basic medical equipment, laboratory equipment and supplies, PPE and strengthening capacity on rational use of drugs and supply chain management; b) building the capacity of healthcare providers and community health workers including volunteers on provision of maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health services, communicable and non-communicable diseases, referral healthcare services and vaccination services; c) integrating, expanding and maintaining effective disease surveillance system with early warning component including community-based surveillance for communicable and notifiable diseases while strengthening related response (IDSR) and health information system; d) strengthening the rapid response capacities to emergencies including setting up emergency health teams, supporting and training RRTs and case management capacity; e) improving health facility set-up to provide an acceptable quality of health services including the supply of water and power and basic needs of quality of care; f) supporting system for early referral of complicated cases; g) supporting community mobilization and participation, health education and preventive messages aiming at changing communities towards healthy behaviors.
SCI will be prioritizing and collaboratively working with other partners such as WHO to respond to sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment. SCI Health sector in integration with protection sector will be able to provide counseling, awareness raising, and tailored services to victims including identified referral pathways within supported health facilities in addition to community-based services. Supported health facilities will be equipped with trained personnel to serve as focal points for SEAH services with joint supportive supervision to monitor the implementation and assess capacities.
The project implementation plan will be detailed, implemented and monitored jointly with stakeholders including targeted communities at national and state levels and local health authorities. The project will be implemented over 12 months with estimated budget at$16,234,200.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Save the Children aims at increasing safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. Program design and implementation is informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. The project will implement purposeful awareness raising for targeted beneficiaries and communities on the community level protection measures, availability of GBV services and importance of timely notification and access to service. GBV survivors will have safe access to health facilities and health providers will be quipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services. Health staff will be trained on first line GBV management and CMR will be conducted in coordination with the protection and GBV sector. Further, information sharing of violence, IDP movements, and GBV incidents will be shared among sectors to ensure timely response and referral pathways.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Stakeholders’ participation and accountability to affected people is part of SCI’s core values. In practice, our participation and accountability approaches cover the following: a) Regular, timely and accessible information sharing, b) Participation –discussing together, deciding together and working together, c) Proactively seeking feedback from children and communities and handling their complaints, d) Generate data and evidence for continuous learning. SCI is signatory to Core Humanitarian Standard and considering this SCI Sudan has already established strong accountability mechanisms which include three critical components of information sharing, participation, and complaints and feedback for the targeted communities and children. SCI will provide regular, timely and accessible information to targeted communities about: a) Who we are and the principles we adhere to; b) Project interventions and what SCI intend to deliver (e.g., what people are entitled to receive and when); c) Our staff and how we expect our staff to behave (including our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct); d) How communities can be involved. This information will be communicated in the local language and medium that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the targeted communities, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups. To ensure accountability to the beneficiaries during the consultation phase, SCI has will consult and engage the existing community structures/bodies (traditional leaders, representatives from IDPs, returnees, refugees, host communities, youth groups, women groups, and local government authorities). During the implementation phase, SCI will ensure that targeted communities have been provided with opportunities to express their views and influence decision-making through discussing together, deciding together and working together. Particular attention will be given to the vulnerable groups to enable for active participation in the implementation of the project activities.
