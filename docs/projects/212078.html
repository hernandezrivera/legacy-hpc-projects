Project Summary

In 2024/2025, UNHCR will continue to support areas of returns, strengthening early recovery for returnees' families by small to medium WASH infrastructure repair, in line with the Sector Objective Indicator 4 Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria.
Needs
According to HNO 2024, the crisis affected dramatically the WASH infrastructures functionality, requiring significant support and investments, with potential particular focus on sustainable solutions. The water supply network and sewage systems are not functional or in need of repair or maintenance, increasing public health risks for the population.
Intervention, Implementation of activities
To sustain our shelter program, UNHCR project aims to assist 395,800 individual / 79, 160 households mainly in the areas where shelter assistance is provided by UNHCR. This will be done through the rehabilitation of WASH infrastructures: repair of boreholes, and water system network in the area of the returnee, maintenance of 18 projects Sewage systems in Aleppo,rural Damascus, Lattakia, Damascus , Dara, Deir Azzor, Hama, Homs, and other locations; in doing this, UNHCR will ensure that an integrated approach is followed to provide basic and essential services to People of Concern in areas of returns.
An important consideration will be given to ensure that WASH facilities are equally accessible to both returnees and affected host communities. Consultation with local authorities will ensure the specific needs of every community is tacked. A community engagement and consultations mechanism will be implemented to help in selection of targeted projects, with various participatory methods including FGDs, and community/household visits and use of UNHCR community centers.
Since UNHCR is working through partnership modality to implement most of WASH interventions, as usual, also in 2024 UNHCR and partners will provide regular and timely inputs to the WASH sector 4Ws.
Protection Risk Analysis
Through provided UNHCR support to target populations, the number of persons exposed to the risks of displacement, GBV and PSEA risks, and risks of health diseases deriving for not having clean water or safe swage system in place will be reduced.
Potential risks:
Potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/rehabilitation of wastewater infrastructure (WWTP, sewer networks)/rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure (water stations, towers, distribution networks). Likelihood: M, Impact: H MM: Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared; Conduct safety training with stuff and contractors; Add explosive hazard awareness material to the program.
Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a WASH partner's service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others - Likelihood: M, Impact: M MM: Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed - Ensure consultation with communities related to location of WASH services to understand relevant community perspectives - Reflect on the inclusivity of the target and analyze how each target group has safe access to WASH services/infrastructures provided - Add feedback and complain mechanisms and remedial action - consultation mechanisms in place, considering ethnic, gender, cultural, political related sensitiveness to avoid exposure to harm and risk.
PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy
UNHCR always raises awareness among UNHCR staff members and Partners on PSEA, through basic PSEA trainings organized by UNHCR periodically. PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet the PSEA criteria will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigation measuresRisk 1: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply. Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow for more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning and implementation in response to market changes. Risk 2: Delay in providing WASH services for people in need due to the lengthy approval processes. Mitigating measures: close coordination with relevant governmental authorities, follow up with UNHCR partners for early submission of projects’ approvals, consider approvals’ potential delay within projects’ design, clear selection criteria focusing on the most vulnerable groups and areas with high WASH severity scale. Risk 3: Escalation of cholera cases due to poor sanitation. MMs: Close coordination with WASH sector partners and Health actors, focusing on sewage system rehabilitation in areas of lower sewer connection rate (Rural Damascus, Aleppo). Risk 4: Current power supply crisis which effect the functionality of rehabilitated water supply systems. MMs: Solarization of water boreholes where possible.
