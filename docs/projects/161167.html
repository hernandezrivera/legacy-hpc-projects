DCA’s humanitarian mine action programme has been operative in South Sudan since 2013. In former Eastern Equatoria State, DCA has since 2013 carried out emergency EORE, needs assessments and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) clearance activities mostly in Magwi, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North Counties. More recently in 2018 and 2019, DCA deployed EORE teams to Magwi, Torit and Kapoeta to provide risk awareness to local communities and to train Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) to do the same. Furthermore, in the second half of 2019, DCA initiated SSHF-funded clearance activities in Kajo Keji in the former Central Equatoria State.
DCA proposes in this action to scale up its current Mine Action activities in 2020 in terms of clearance and EORE risk awareness raising activities in the former Central and Eastern Equatoria State. Activities will include innovative risk awareness raising sessions and the disposal of ERW, which will open access and movement for both local communities and humanitarian actors. The intervention will target the most mine and ERW-affected communities in the former Eastern and Central Equatoria State, including Magwi town, Pageri, Palotaka, Pajok, Lao, Opari, Farajok and Ulako in Magwi county, and Liwolo, Kangapo I, Kangapo II, Nyepo, and Lire communities in Kajo Keji county. Below is an overview of the proposed activities:
1. Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE): The EORE component of the action will target approximately 20,000 beneficiaries, including 4,000 males (out of which 200 are male Community Focal Points (CFPs), 4,000 females (out of which 200 are female CFPs), 6,000 boys (out of which 200 are Peer-Risk Educators (PREs), 6,000 girls (out of which 200 are PREs). The total cluster target for EORE is 250,000 people, thus DCA’s project is covering 8% of the total target.
2. Hazardous Areas (HAs) to be surveyed/cleared: The action will survey and clear at least 50 HAs and handover at least 160,000 m2 of land released to local communities in both Kajo Keji and Magwi counties. Actual number of HAs will depend upon UNMAS tasking and prioritization. The total cluster target is 450 HA to be cleared, thus DCA’s project is covering approximately 11% of the total target.
The project will contribute to the Cluster Objective 2 to “Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience”, by surveying and clearing at least 50 HAs, corresponding to at least 160,000 m2 of land released from mines and ERW. The project will also contribute to Cluster Objective 2 by providing sustainable EORE awareness sessions benefitting a total 20,000 beneficiaries (4,000 men, 4,000 women, 6,000 boys, and 6,000 girls).
Due to extensive experience in the areas of operations, DCA already has sound logistical and administrative setups that will immediately enable the teams to deliver significant outputs. Finally, field operations will be pending security clearance, as to minimize risks for DCA staff, beneficiaries and other stakeholders.

1 - Needs

Decades of conflict has littered vast areas of land in South Sudan with mines, ERW, and cluster munitions. In addition to the risk posed to their safety, ERWs prevent people’s safe movement, including those fleeing from violence or trying to return to their homes; preclude the delivery of humanitarian aid; and inhibit socio-economic development by injuring community members and making inaccessible local land, resources and infrastructures. Central and Eastern Equatoria States continues to be to be among the most contaminated areas in South Sudan, with 5,309,825 m2 of HAs affected by ERW as a result of recent fighting and decades of conflict (UNMAS, August 2019). Furthermore, the protracted rainy season in South Sudan has ERW exposed due to floods during the rainy season and small arms proliferation also contribute to spread death and injuries across the local communities. Currently, more than 300 known HAs cases are open in Central and Eastern Equatoria States, with Kajo Keji and Magwi counties being reported to be among the most affected by mines and ERW. Although the country still remains deeply affected by armed conflict and instability drivers, continuous negotiations throughout 2019 between the conflicting political parties on the Peace Agreement might spark renewed hopes for sustainable ceasefire and peace. In case the Peace Agreement holds, large numbers of displaced people are expected to return to the areas of previous fighting in the coming years. According to UNMAS, surveys of internally displaced persons have identified a fear of ERW and landmines as among the most significant reasons for their inability to return home. Hence, the humanitarian impact of conducting the EOD spot tasks and the clearance activities will be significant as many displaced people are expected to start returning to the areas as a result of the Peace Agreement.

2 - Activities

In South Sudan, DCA is well-positioned to carry out clearance and EORE. In line with the overall goal to “increase civilian security by protecting lives and property through disposal of explosive remnants of war and sustainable Explosive Ordnance Risk Education”, the action shall include two main components, namely land handover to communities through release and clearance of suspected hazardous areas, and EORE awareness sessions and trainings. The proposed project activities include: One Multi-Task Team is already operational in Nimule town (funded by the Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Danida). With this proposed action, DCA will increase the capacity to three EOD and four operational EORE teams. Activity 1: Refresher Training: The teams will deploy from DCA’s logistics base in Nimule as soon as the refresher training is completed and UNMAS accreditation granted if applicable. Activity 2: Community Liaison with Local Authorities: the local authorities at the County and Payam level will be contacted in order to Re-introduce DCA and explain DCA’s intended activities in the area. Activity 3: EOD: The teams will respond to all reports of ERW encountered by displaced persons, refugees, humanitarian actors or host communities in its area of operations. Activity 4: Explosive Ordnance Risk Education: In each community, the EORE teams will focus on supporting the work of the EOD teams as they move throughout the identified locations. The team will be providing EORE activities to affected populations, including via direct RE sessions, training of CFPs and PREs, and by generating Hazardous Areas. Trusted members of selected villages will be trained as CFPs. CFPs are selected on voluntary basis, receiving information materials to support their outreach efforts in targeting their families, peers, and communities. Similarly, selected among volunteers and children representatives identified in collaboration with school teachers and principals, children in local schools will be trained to be PREs. DCA will provide PREs with knowledge and materials to engage their peers in spreading safety information to avoid the dangers of ERWs in their surroundings. CFPs and PREs are paired per location, to reinforce the outreach of the action, and the CFPs will provide support and supervision to the trained children in case of reporting. The CFPs and PREs will acquire the capacity to disseminate ERWs safety messages to other community members, while identifying and reporting to Mine Action stakeholders and local authorities the presence of Suspected Hazardous Areas. DCA will ensure the active participation of women and girls in all aspects of the intervention by conducting comprehensive sensitisation sessions throughout the project period and by conducting regular monitoring visits to the areas of intervention. Activity 5: Follow-up: DCA will follow up on trained beneficiaries with monitoring activities, tracking eventual reporting of dangerous items and sharing relevant information with MA stakeholders for further investigation and eventual clearance. All activities will be conducted in line with DCA SOPs, international standards and national guidelines, ensuring progress is regularly documented and reported to the relevant mine action stakeholders. Activity 6: Reporting: The teams will submit the following IMSMA reports to UNMAS detailing their work: Hazard Reports, Hazard Survey Reports. Internal statistics will be logged in DCA’s Project Output Tracker or database. Activity 7: Impact Monitoring: DCA will carry out an Impact Assessment towards the end of the project but also a Pre and Post KAPB survey on EORE. All activities will be conducted in line with international standards, NTSG guidelines, and DCA SOPs ensuring progress is regularly documented and reported to the relevant mine action stakeholders.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

In order to mitigate the risk mentioned in the above sections, DCA seeks to do the following. First, the risk of explosive hazards in the project areas will be mitigated through DCA's comprehensive mine action intervention. The main objective of this proposed action is exactly to handover to affected communities at least 160,000 m2 of land released from mines and ERW threat. Together with the EORE activities, this will significantly mitigate the risks of ERW posed to the local communities and communities will be able to move freely to the areas following the clearance operations. Second, in order to mitigate local disputes around the land ownership following clearance operations, DCA will liaise with local authorities, traditional and religious leaders even before the teams start operating. A handover certiﬁcate will be issued to local leaders after the land being cleared and a community meeting will be called to ensure transparency and accountability. Additionally, DCA will closely monitor the socio-economic impact of its work by utilizing its own methodologies and dedicated Monitoring & Evaluation mechanism to ensure that targeted local communities remain at their locations and use the cleared land for agricultural or hunting purposes in an eﬃcient way. This will help building their resilience and pave the way for self-suﬃciency at community and household levels. It will also reduce the level of vulnerability towards humanitarian aid. Finally, DCA will ensure the active participation of women and girls in all aspects of the intervention by conducting comprehensive sensitisation sessions throughout the project period and by conducting regular monitoring visits to the areas of intervention. This will significantly reduce the risk of women and girls being excluded from consultations and decision-making processes.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure accountability towards the communities, DCA will engage beneficiaries during Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning activities, such as pre and post Knowledge, Attitude, Behaviour and Practice (KABP) analysis, interviews and post impact assessments. Beneficiaries and local community representatives will be given the opportunity to provide feedback regarding activity performance and overall impact of the action, achieving two objectives: first, collect data to improve the team’s targeting and performance; secondly, ensure that accountability of the implemented activities is registered and shared with relevant counterparts and the donor.
