Project Summary

Since 25 August 2017, extreme violence in Rakhine State, Myanmar, has driven an estimated 730,000 refugees across the border into Cox's Bazar. The majority of the population are children (55 per cent) and women (52 per cent) They live in the overcrowded settlement in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila and in the poor condition, prone to infectious disease outbreak (due to low immunity and poor health and nutritional status).
UNICEF proposes the project “Provision of equitable quality lifesaving and essential health services for Rohingya refugees and host communities’ children and women in Cox's Bazar” providing services to Rohingya Refugees and Host population. UNICEF project will serve 167,103 beneficiaries through this project. UNICEF will implement this project in partnership with Cox's Bazar Civil Surgeon, DD Family Planning, Partners in Health and Development (PHD), Relief International (RI), Research, Training, Management International (RTMI), Food for the Hungry/Medical Team International (FH/MTI), icddr,b. UNICEF will need 15, 608,320 USD in order to implement this project.
UNICEF’s project activities are in line with health sector’s following four objectives:
Objective #1 Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psycho-social support; and non-communicable diseases.
Objective #3 Encourage healthy living, improve health seeking behavior and utilization of essential service package among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender considerations and vulnerable groups.
Objective #4 Strengthen health sector coordination and information management with a focus on rational and accountable health service delivery systems, including monitoring of minimum standards and the quality of the services provided.

Needs

Rohingya Refugees have no record of immunization before the migration and remain vulnerable and prone to disease outbreak. Despite 9 massive vaccination campaigns since October 2017 to May 2018, routine immunization remains a challenge. UNICEF, WHO and other key partners support routine immunization through government to reach 51,022 children under 1 year of age. 130 vaccinators work in 780 outreach sites in the camps and in 46 fixed sites (through NGOs). Incidence of vaccine preventable diseases in children under five need to be determined. and actions need to be taken based on thee finding. Health facility utilization is within the normal range of 2-4 consultations but access to 24/7 MNCAH services remain a challenge. Adherence to minimum standard of services and staffing is a concern and quality of maternal, newborn, child, and adolescent health services need to be improved. Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission (PMTCT) is now rolling out in the targeted health facilities in the camps, in upazila health complex and district hospital. Newborn care is not up to standard, especially in community level. Referral linkages needs to be improved and more support needed for secondary care in field hospital and upazila health complexes to reduce burden in District Hospital. Community outreach activities need to be harmonized and monitored so it can be improved. Health information and management systems does exist and follow government systems but it needs to be strengthen and improve adherence from NGOs partners. Acute watery diarrhoea and other infectious diseases are need to be prevented and treated, and consider the risk in the overcrowded makeshift settlement and host communities, it needs robust surveillance systems. Health sector coordination led by government and co-chair by WHO and it needs to be strengthened.

Activities or outputs

Activity 1.1: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners in implementing routine immunization for children under 2 year of age and pregnant women including booster doses for extending age groups, and Td for child bearing age women, and special Immunization Activities (SIAs), when needed, for Rohingya and host population in Cox's Bazar. Activities include planning, implementation, monitoring, procurement and supply of vaccines, cold chain and logistics, defaulter tracking, data management, monitoring and implementation with strong emphasis on community mobilization (in collaboration with communication for development sector). Activity 1.2: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners to provide quality and equitable maternal, newborn, child health, adolescent health services and GBV health services. Activities include establishment and management of 06 Primary Health Care Centers and 13 Health posts; strenghtening Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EmONC); Strengthening Special Care Newborn Units (SCANU) and 2 Newborn Stabilization Units (NSUs) in Ukhiya and Teknaf, and Newborn Care Corners in health facilities in the camps linking through referral system with community based new born care (includes acquisition of medicines, equipment, and supplies, capacity building, management and operational support); strengthen newborn and child health services (Integrated Management of Childhood Illness and Nutrition-IMCI&N) both at health facility and community levels as well as improving quality of care. Maternal Perinatal Deaths Sureveillance and Response (MPDSR) will be extended to cover Rohingya communities in camps in addition to host population. Establishment and functioning of adolescent friendly health services (AFHS corners in upazila health complexes).These activities will be implemented in partnership with Partners in Health and Development (PHD), Research, Training and Management (RTM) International, Relief International (RI) and Food for Hungry/Medical Team International (FH/MTI) Activity 1.3: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners establishment of PMTCT services for Rohingya and host communities (including consensus building, awareness and education on HIV/STI/PMTCT, ANC HIV testing, initiate ART for identified positive pregnant women, ensure safe delivery, prophylaxis and ART for infants, ART adherence follow up mother and children through outreach workers, and referral for Early Diagnosis of infants). Activity 1.4: Support Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners in preventing and managing Acute Watery Diarhoea (AWD) outbreak including management of 5 Diarrhea Treatment Centers (activities include capacity building, procurement of medicines and supplies, surveillance, hygiene promotion and Oral Cholera Vaccine efffectiveness study ). Strong collaboration with WASH and Communication for Development sectors will be maintained. Activity will be implemented by International Centre for Diarrheal Disease Research, Bangladesh (icddr,b) in collaboration with MoH&FW and health sector partners. Activity 3.1: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners in strengthening community health mechanisms to enhance community based Maternal, Neonatal, Child and Adolescent Health services including health promotion and community outreach services. Community Health Workers are part of primary health care services in order to improve health seaking behavior and linking communities with health care system abd timely referral to nearby health facilities. Capacity of community health volunteers for continuing dialogues with community for health with mobilizing mothers and children for ANC, PNC, ENC, Immunization, including defaulter tracking, and under five children will be improved.
