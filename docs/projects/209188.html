Project Summary

Internally Displaced Persons (IDP), host communities, as well as other most vulnerable women and girls at risk (e.g., pregnant women, breastfeeding women, women and girls with disabilities, and female-headed households), will receive lifesaving specialized gender-based violence prevention and response services through the IRC's Integrated Protection program in Gedaref. In order to ensure that survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) have access to timely, secure, and confidential clinical care for the detrimental physical and emotional health effects, the initiative will facilitate the integration of reproductive health and GBV services. Promoting family-based care in the child's best interest to ensure children are safe in their homes and receive support when they experience harm, the project will additionally concentrate on delivering lifesaving Child Protection (CP) services, such as specialized case management and PSS, prevention and response to child separation from family, and PSS. By integrating/establishing temporary SHLS within the health facility. The IRC will further this intervention through its existing Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) in the Tunaydbah refugee camp, where it also implements its Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) curriculum. Additionally, community members will be engaged to further mitigate potential risks for children and adolescents, and the IRC will encourage community participation throughout all phases of the project.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The objective of this project is to guarantee that protection makes every effort to deliver and tackle protection mainstreaming in the technical design. The project incorporates foundational protection mainstreaming principles into every stage, including design, implementation, and monitoring. These principles include guaranteeing meaningful access, safety, and dignity, as well as participation and empowerment of the intended beneficiaries and stakeholders. The 'Do No Harm' approach is also prioritized. Information dissemination regarding child protection risks and the prevention and mitigation of gender-based violence will be a component of community mobilization and outreach efforts. Training will be provided to all personnel involved in the project regarding fundamental protection principles, gender mainstreaming, and referral systems.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The IRC adopts a client-centered strategy that prioritizes the vulnerable groups and communities impacted by the crisis as the focal point of the intervention. Consistent feedback sessions involving multiple sectors, including caregivers, women, and girls, will be organized within secure areas to verify that services and activities are effectively attending to the requirements of the population impacted by the crisis. The involvement of women, girls, parents, and caregivers in the location and activity decision-making process will be encouraged. On a voluntary basis, client satisfaction surveys will also be administered to survivors of GBV. The IRC team intends to actively involve women, girls, children, and caregivers residing in crisis-affected communities in the process of devising and executing strategies to avert, mitigate, and address the repercussions of CP risks and GBV. IRC will ensure that its Meal and Evaluation (M&E) and feedback systems facilitate the complete involvement of the community in the project's design and redesign. To ensure that beneficiaries are cognizant of the integrated protection services at their disposal, the IRC will implement community outreach and awareness campaigns. These initiatives will aim to raise awareness regarding the IRC Code of Conduct, policies regarding the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, and safeguarding children, the procedure for offering feedback, and the guiding principles of beneficiary feedback, which encompass non-retaliation, accountability, and confidentiality. The IRC intends to furnish beneficiaries with various feedback channels (e.g., focus group discussions (FGDs), key informant interviews (KIIs), feedback receptacles, and a hotline) across all sectors. This feedback will serve to guide the development and execution of projects. Additionally, the IRC has an impartial division known as the Ethics & Compliance Unit (ECU), which handles all significant grievances, such as those pertaining to sexual abuse and SEA.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The IRC shall consistently address clients' concerns and feedback, utilizing this data to modify and enhance service provision in accordance with the IRC Accountability to Affected Populations [AAP] framework. Already in place, the IRC will fortify feedback mechanisms. The IRC will administer an annual standard client satisfaction survey (CSS) in accordance with the IRC Core Feedback themes, which reflect our international commitments and standards regarding AAP and participation. While evaluating client contentment with the rendered services, the CSS will additionally afford clients the chance to designate preferable feedback channels or assess the efficacy of current channels.
