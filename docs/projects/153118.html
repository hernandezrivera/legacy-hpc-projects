Project Summary

This project is designed to effectively contribute to the building of resilience and increasing own production through livelihood support and livestock intervention in Maiwut, Longechuk and Nassir of Upper Nile State and Mayendit of Unity State. It is a twelve month project targeting 12000 HH of vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host communities. AHC intends to make use of the window of opportunity for the targeted population who missed out the cropping season either due to conflict, or lack of access to humanitarian assistance. Those who planted also suffered from dry spells and FAW attack thus less harvest. It is in line with the FSL allocation both in terms of the Priority, Geographical coverage, and the seasonality of the proposed activities; crop, vegetable, and fishing production, training and livestock treatment and vaccination. This project corresponds to strategic objective 3 as well as cluster objectives 2 and 4.
A total of 72000 vulnerable individuals will be targeted and selected from the targeted areas. These will include 21600 men, 36000 women, 8000 boys and 6400 girls selected from the 36,000 IDP, 14400returnees and 21600 host population. The targeting will be done with gender consideration as well as the invaluable incorporation of Accountability to affected population and mainstreaming of protection issues. The Do No Harm principle will be adhered to as we endeavour to give priority to the community to have a say over their own situation.
AHC is committed mitigate any risks associated with this project bearing in mind the added advantage we have as a local NGO based in the target project sites with extensive integration with the community. The presence and good rapport we have with the community is an added advantage to the success of this project.
FAO will be the core pipeline and technical backstop partner. The requested funding from SSHF is going to be used to provide front line services. A transparent direct distribution system based on the FAO guidelines will be used to implement this project. There is a huge influx of returnees and the number of the IDPs and most vulnerable host community members is also high.

Needs

Humanitarian and Food security situation is again likely to deteriorate for the sixth consecutive year with 6.1 million people severely food insecure (IPC 3, 4 & 5) in the January – March 2019 projection period. This is the highest number of severely food insecure people at this time of year, since the start of the crisis, and this is expected to increase during the lean season and may even surpass the 6 million in the July/ August 2018period.Upper Nile state is one of the most affected states by the ongoing Conflict which has lasted for about years non-stop but becoming more and more complex. Nassir, Longechuk, and Maiwut of Upper Nile State and Mayendit in Unity State are considered to be the most affected counties. Despite the signing of the peace deal, the key driving factor contributing to the dire Food insecurity situation is the result of multiple hazards which include continuous and sporadic fighting between pro and anti-government forces which has been going on since the beginning of the crisis in December 2013. In the middle of 2018, in Nassir, a serious non-lineal spread of insecurity impacted Maiwut. High food prices, depleted household assets and irregular rainfall were characteristic and present across Upper Nile State driving the decline in food security. 69% of the population in Upper Nile is classified as crisis, emergency and catastrophe for 2019 with 2000 people in phase 5 (IPC Sept2018). On the other hand, Unity states has 71% of its population under phases 3, 4 and 5 with 19,000 people classified as will face catastrophe. (IPC Sept2018). Worsening terms of trade (sale of livestock and labour wages against the price of sorghum) has continued to take a downward trend across the two states .According to several evidences IRNA assessment conducted by different agencies in different areas of the four counties, markets are destroyed and only very limited informal trade is in place. Prices in the functioning markets are extremely high due to lack of goods and high transport cost and the current inflationThe recent peace agreement will see a lot of population returning and this means returning to nothing in terms of livelihoods. It is important to therefore be ready to cushion them and prepare them to own production, so that they get to normal life. And not continue depending on food distribution. Reliable sources from recent IRNA assessments and FSNMS done in Sept 2018 shows huge consumption gap with some household reducing food consumption to as little a one meal per day consisting of only fish and water lilies. Massive displacement of people also resulted to movement of livestock to crisis free area. This movement and lack of animal health services resulted in increase of spread of livestock disease and parasites. There is an opportunity to take action in the beginning of 2019 to support and save the livelihood activities in order to prevent further worsening of food security and livelihood. Lifesaving services should be prioritized to save lives of the vulnerable community and their livelihoods. With the good rapport from the local authority , experience and the field presences in the two locations AHC is proposing a one year resilience Response Project to address the worsening humanitarian and Food insecurity need through the provision of fishing kits, vegetable kits ,essential crops seeds and livestock support through provision of animal health services(Treatment and vaccination ). They will target extremely vulnerable households in the two counties Nassir, Longechuk, and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile Counties and Mayendit in Unity State. We will intergrate dthis project with nutrition and education to be implemented by other colleagues from world vision and UNICEF partners.

Activities

AHC is out to Protect livelihoods through the main and lean season . seeds and also appropriate tool distribution . We will ensure that the farmers get of seed protection rations as we work on seed multiplication sites wherever possible in our project sites. Reduction of pest and crop disease e.g. Fall Army Warm surveillance, detection, prevention and eradication. Livestock vaccination; disease prevention and treatment especially zoonotic like RVF. livestock vaccination treatment will be carried out in all our project sites. AHC will Support value chain addition in our project areas through milk and fish preservation as well as marketing through postharvest management.Training and capacity building is another activity that AHC will undertake in this project sites. We aim at doing farmer field schools and demonstration plots. We will also engage our beneficiaries to a level they will be having Income generating Activities by the closure of the project for sustainability purposes.

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes. AHC endeavors to to inform the beneficiaries about all that pertains the project. We will also be training them on different aspects of the project like in crop, vegetable, fish production and livestock intervention. Focus group discussions, brainstorming and village meetings on market days as well as religious gatherings are avenues that AHC will utilize in making sure that information about services provided by this project reach the people.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. AHC knows the importance of contextualization in communicating with the different categories of beneficiaries based on their gender , age and aspect of response they fall under. For example the training on vegetable production will be specifically to the beneficiaries receiving the vegetable kits. Peace building is an important aspect that will be integrated in our intervention. So in our engagement with the community, we will ensure that we inform them of the importance of living together peacefully to attain not only food security but also other essential developments.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. AHC is aware of the many needs arising from sexual exploitation and abuse. This is why we have mainstreamed protection in our interventions. we encourage the community to report to our protection officers when there is even suspicion of this ill. We also have our staff trained on PSEA so that they are able to efficiently and effectively deal with these issues in case they arise in our line of duty. Reporting of all cases and dealing with the perpetrators is key to the fight against sexual exploitation and abuse. AHC is an ardent advocate for the rights of the victims of PSEA by pushing for action against the perpetrators and counselling as well as psychological support for the victims.
