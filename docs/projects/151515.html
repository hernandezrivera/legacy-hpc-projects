Project Summary

The overall health situation in Somalia continues to deteriorate, especially in Northern and Central parts due to the persistent drought, continued insecurity and other underlying factors. This project will strive to address emergency health and nutrition needs of marginalized and underserved communities in Deynille and Darkenley districts of Benadir region and Gaalkayo district of Galgaduud region.

Needs

The IRC conducted an integrated rapid needs assessment between 18 and 30 January 2018 in Wadajir district. The main health concerns identified amongst these populations were pregnancy related conditions, mental health issues, lack of family planning (FP) services, coupled with limited knowledge on modern family planning methods, increased cases of non-communicable diseases (NCDs), and sexual violence. In Wadajir district, the critical nutrition situation among the IDPs is linked to high morbidity associated with measles outbreak; low immunization coverage; increased AWD/cholera cases enhanced by on-going private and government evictions of the Mogadishu IDPs; and limited access to humanitarian interventions. Approximately, 24,000 IDPs reside in K8 and K12 catchment population. 18,000 urban households live nearby and depend on the healthcare services that IRC provides. Existing poor water, sanitation and hygiene practices continue to increase cases of sporadic, endemic or outbreak diarrheal diseases. An estimated 95% of children in the IDP areas use open defecation, and the host communities reported 100% use of shared family toilets.

Activities

Health Interventions: The IRC will provide life-saving health interventions which through existing health structures as a way of bolstering the existing health system. The IRC will implement these services through K8 and K12 health centers in Daynille and Dherkeynley districts of Benadir region respectively, and Gaalkayo South Hospital in Gaalkayo district of Mudug region. The services that will be provided will include general outpatient consultations, the dispensation of pharmaceuticals, laboratory tests, and expanded the program on immunization (EPI) for children less than five years as well as pregnant and lactating women. Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) services in K8, K12, and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (CEMONC) services in Gaalkayo South Hospital will also form a key component of IRC’s health interventions. In order to augment health facility-based interventions, IRC will also provide community outreach services focused on hygiene promotion and health education. Nutrition interventions: IRC’s health staff in K8 and K12 clinics will be trained in comprehensive Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM) with a component of anthropometric assessment in order to be able to screen all children who will visit both health facilities and provide them with therapeutic and supplementary food commodities. Besides providing routine health services in K8, children identified with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications will be enrolled

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

IRC through DFID funding constructed community resilience centers for community meetings and engagement. Through multisectoral approach, IRC is able to address more complex needs or by modifying awarded funding during a major shock to immediately fund an intervention while greater emergency funding is secured. The local committees will also allow the IRC to monitor and respond to localized or wide-scale emergencies more decisively should they occur. Multiple purpose cash transfers and scalable safety nets of predictable cash transfers allows beneficiaries to avoid negative copying strategies, protects households from selling crucial livelihood assets and allows them the opportunity for early recovery. Interventions will be implemented through existing government structures to ensure sustainability and bolstering the capacity of the local government as an effective exit strategy.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Internally displaced persons stand the risk of not affording high quality health care because of their poor socioeconomic status, long travel distances, lack of proper public transport system, insecurity and marginalization as a result of local clan dynamics. IRC will ensure services are provided to IDPs impartially and at no cost.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and children remain the most vulnerable to health and nutrition shocks because of their multifaceted physiological needs. IRC’s interventions are tailored in such a way that they will address specific needs of women and children. Reproductive health services will be tailored specifically for women of reproductive age while EPI services will be for both children as well as pregnant and lactating women.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Internally displaced people strand the risk of further displacement because of continuous inter-clan conflicts.
