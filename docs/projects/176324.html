Project Summary

The project aims to provide critical life serving GBV response and prevention services through the provision of comprehensive case management including psychosocial support services, legal services, medical care, safety and security within UNFPA established One-Stop Centers (OSC), other UNFPA supported GBV service provision centres and Health facilities as well as enhance referrals where GBV services are not available.
The project will strengthen existing GBV referral systems and establish new ones where there is none to ensure survivors receive the recommended care.
UNFPA will implement Women and Girls Friendly Space programming as an entry point for women and girls to receive more psychosocial support and other empowerment activities. Cram activities, such as knitting, beadwork, crocheting and embroidering bed sheets, and tea discussions, will be introduced as the women often enjoy these activities, so they are a good way to bring them together encourage conversation and relationship-building with each other. On the other hand, some of these activities will be introduced as income-generating activities to allow women and girls to earn some income to meet their basic needs and referrals for livelihood support with other programmes supporting women and girls.
Response services will complement Dignity Programming, which will be used as an entry point to bring women and girls together to discuss important issues such as GBV risks, receive information on available GBV and SRH services and targeted assistance and referrals for livelihood projects available in the project areas.
The project will also support existing GBV prevention activities by engaging communities through awareness-raising and focusing on transforming harmful social norms that contribute to gender inequality and conducting GBV assessments and coordinated GBV safety audit.
Prevention and response strategies will also include men to increase the understanding of the value of gender equity and promote long-lasting changes in gender relations. Thus engaging men and boys in GBV prevention activities will also be supported by the project.
Also, the project will provide capacity building to frontline service providers on GBV guiding principles. This project will also support the training of non-GBV actors on minimum standards of GBV in emergencies in locations where GBV actors are not in the area.
All awareness-raising will consider the COVID 19 prevention measures, including crucial prevention messages on the same. The numbers of beneficiaries per session will be reduced to ensure social distancing is possible. The project will ensure that all beneficiaries are reached by increasing the numbers of sessions and megaphones in the community. Staff will be trained to refer beneficiaries who show signs and symptoms of COVID 19 to the nearest health facility for support.
It is important to note that as a result of the degradation of community protection systems and the inadequate GBV lifesaving services, women and girls have increased exposure to various forms of violence, including rape, sexual assault, psychological abuse, physical violence, child and forced marriage, denial of opportunities, resources and services where some basic services exist. Also, livelihood options are increasingly limited due to the ongoing conflict, exacerbated with COVID 19, disproportionately affecting adolescent girls and young women, resorting to harmful coping mechanisms that include child marriage, survival sex and sexual exploitation and abuse.
Survivors urgently need to access specialized services, including case management and psychosocial support services. A clear referral pathway needs to be established to facilitate the survivors' access to critical life-saving services.
In the areas with critical humanitarian needs, the health situation of women and girls is made worse by limited access to services or non-existence.

Implementing Partners

UNFPA will work with Implementing Partners through existing Implementing Partner Agreements with some of the GBV partners/ NGOs. They are delivering Gender-Based violence prevention and response services in the prioritized crisis-affected areas. These include the International Medical Corps (IMC), International Rescue Committee (IRC), International Medical Assistance (IMA) and Smile Again Africa Development organisation (SAADO).
Also, UNFPA will engage other GBV Sub-Cluster-approved NGOs not directly contracted by UNFPA through a specific Program, Supplies Distribution Agreements (PSDA), specifically to implement only Dignity Kit Programming. The organisations who will be selected in coordination with the GBV Sub Cluster will be authorized to receive, hold, administer, manage, handle, store, transport and use DK supplies following the terms of and solely for the purposes permitted by UNFPA.

1 - Needs

Although the protracted humanitarian crisis impacts people across the country, states such as Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Lakes and Warrap continue to face multiple shocks, further deepening extreme levels of vulnerabilities. Humanitarian needs are mainly driven by the impacts of years of conflict and exacerbated by climate change. According to the HNO 2021, People's physical and mental well-being, living standards, and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2021. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2021. As a result of the degradation of community protection systems in the project areas affected by the ongoing conflict, floods and with a high influx of IDP's, women and girls have increased exposure to various forms of violence, including rape and sexual violence, physical violence as well as Child/forced marriage, besides, livelihood options are increasingly limited exacerbated with the effects of COVID 19 restrictions on women and girls. Adolescent girls and young women are particularly at risk of resorting to harmful coping mechanisms that include child marriage, survival sex and sexual exploitation.Gender-based violence is a serious and pervasive societal, public health and human rights issue globally. In South Sudan, GBV has become a persistent reality for women and girls. They have continued to bear the brunt of gender-based violence in a society where entrenched norms and practices that violate their rights are widely promoted, condoned and deeply rooted in communities. Through service provision, a snapshot of GBV incidence is known - albeit representing just a fraction of the aggressions faced by women and girls, in an environment where undertaking daily survival tasks, such as collecting firewood and water, place them under real threat. Many forms of gender-based violence are pervasive but go largely unreported due to the high levels of stigma and shame, a lack of awareness amongst women about their rights, and limited services for survivors.It is a harsh reality that most women and girls suffer from some GBV, be it rape, sexual assault, physical assault, forced/child marriage, or psychological/emotional abuse. Family violence is common across the country and has flourished in a conflict-affected society where violence has been a regular feature of life for years. Men and women alike have been socialized to tolerate domestic violence and view it as acceptable within families. The GBV Information Management System consistently documents intimate partner violence as most of all reported GBV cases, followed by sexual violence (all forms, including rape).Survivors of GBV have the right to access timely, quality and compassionate care to help them heal and recover. Life-saving intervention such as case management, including Clinical management of Rape, is critical. Through this project, the UNFPA will build the capacity of frontline workers as one of the key strategic goals in its three-year south Sudan Strategy for prevention and response to GBV in Emergency. The project will be organized into the capacity building to ensure quality service provision and support the GBV response through service delivery and procurement and provision of dignity kits to reduce vulnerability and connect women and girls to information and services.To ensure the service provided especially medical care, reaches a higher quality, this project will benefit from tthtUNFPA RH project offering specific training on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) for health service providers in addition to the GBV case management/PSS support for social workers/caseworkers in the targeted areas. Coaching sessions will follow this second training during monitoring missions with the final aim of building pools of trained psychosocial staff able to provide services up to minimum standards. The capacity building program will also include two additional training for non-.

2 - Project activities

Through its programming, UNFPA will continue to provide specialized and integrated GBV services through the static and mobile approach, including comprehensive case management, referrals, enhance access for women and girls in WGFS, awareness-raising and Dignity kit programing.UNFPA will regularly update risk assessments and service mapping of the areas while advocating with the different Clusters like WASH, Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), Nutrition, Education clusters, among others at state and national levels, for a prompt and more comprehensive response to the identified needs. The services provided within the WGFSs will focus on initial healing and the ability to identify and strengthen their own coping mechanism. This service can be accessed by women and girls at particular risk of sexual violence, exploitation, abuse, denial of resources or forced and early marriage. UNFPA will challenge any deliberate attempt to exclude parts of the affected population and will provide assistance based on need and guard against any form of direct or indirect discrimination. Interventions will focus on the following activities, 1. Train 45 social workers in Psychosocial support and GBV case Management 2 training are planned to take place for 4 days each. UNFPA specialists will train social workers from partner organizations and other organizations providing case management in the target areas.2. Train 50 frontline responders in the GBV Minimum Standards safe identification and referral pathway in Upper Nile, Bentiu, Jonglei and Wau. UNFPA staff and GBV field coordinators will train frontline workers on GBV Minimum Standards, safe referral and the referral pathway (two trainings planned for each day).3. Train at least 30 non-GBV actors on how to support GBV survivors if the GBV actors are not in the area4. Procure 8,000 dignity kits for women and girls of reproductive age in targeted areas 5. Distribute 8,000 Dignity Kits according to the distribution plan and requests from GBV actors. GBV organisations will distribute the kits in the field, prioritizing the most vulnerable women and girls among the displaced ones. During the distribution, information on services available will be disseminated and GBV messages to make sure survivors of violence know where to seek help.6. Establish 5 WGFS providing both static and mobile GBV services and information to survivors.7. Provide quality and confidential survivor-centred case management services to GBV survivors8. Provide PSS, skills building, non-formal education, information sessions, and other initiatives for women and girls at the 2 women's centres and the 3 mobile WGFs.9. Establish/Update referral pathways in the targeted areas (Balliet county in Upper Nile, Mayom County in Unity State) and Pibor in Jonglei and Wau.). UNFPA, in collaboration with IPs and other GBV actors, will develop or update the referral pathway. The partners will disseminate the referral pathway in the field with GBV Working Groups' support.10. Conduct safety audits and community discussions to identify risks faced by women and girls and develop strategies to address these in collaboration with local communities and humanitarian actors. 11. Conduct community outreach targeting community members to raise awareness on service availability, COVID 19 and facilitate confidential referrals to response services.12. Engage with women groups for the sustainability of GBV services.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

UNFPA will ensure that girls and boys with disability are identified. The appropriate ways are designed for them to get registered and receive services to reduce the risk of SEA, and this approach of reaching them other than letting them look for services will greatly reduce SEA risks. UNFPA will ensure access to information on available protection assistance or referral services through media accessing the different category of people. UNFPA will work with communities to explore context-specific complaint mechanisms to ensure identified access constraints are addressed.The risk of unsafe and unethical data management, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries, will be mitigated through training and orientation of service providers on GBV principles, including confidentiality.
