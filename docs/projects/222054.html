Project Summary

Misinformation on health topics has fueled attacks on health services and health staff throughout Northern Mozambique. This project focuses on addressing health misinformation, particularly on issues like cholera and other stigmatized topics, in Northern Mozambique (Cabo Delgado, Nampula, and Niassa provinces). The aim is to reduce hostility toward healthcare professionals and facilities by promoting accurate health information and fostering community trust. Key target groups include under served rural and semi-urban communities, where reliance on traditional medicine and mistrust of modern healthcare are prevalent. In addition, specific population target groups will be community leaders, actors involved with traditional medicine and mothers.
Activities will include community talks led by Community Health Workers (CHWs), media campaigns through social media, radio, and advertisements in urban areas, and visual materials like posters in public spaces and health facilities. These efforts aim to improve health awareness, reduce reliance on traditional practices, and prevent violence against healthcare workers and infrastructure.
Partnering with teachers, CHWs and other community leaders will allow to transmit the message throughout the community.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project may utilize a wide range of approaches to promote community engagement across the 3 target provinces of Cabo Delgado, Niassa and Nampula: - Community Talks with Key Influencers: Engage community leaders, mothers, and traditional medicine practitioners through interactive sessions led by CHWs. These talks will address misinformation on health topics like cholera and build trust in modern healthcare.- Poster Campaigns: Distribute culturally tailored visual materials in public spaces, health facilities, and community hubs to reinforce messaging.- Radio Programs in Local Languages: Develop and broadcast health awareness messages targeting rural and semi-urban communities, where radio is a widely accessible medium.- Workshops for Traditional Healers: Organize sensitization workshops to integrate traditional medicine actors into the healthcare ecosystem and promote collaboration with modern health practices.- Community Engagement through Mothers’ Groups: Leverage existing women’s groups to deliver targeted messages on child health, sanitation, and the role of health professionals.- Community Engagement through schools: partnering with schools on information sharing, delivering targeted messages on prevention for main transmissible diseases. - Social Media Campaigns: Utilize Facebook and WhatsApp to reach urban and semi-urban audiences with engaging, fact-based health content, tailored to the province's tech-accessible population.Working closely with the MISAU of each province and with the support at central level, teams for improvement of the communication and community engagement will be defined.Further cross-cutting activities might be implemented to ensure the good quality of activities implemented- Feedback Mechanisms: Implement community feedback channels across all provinces to gather insights and improve approaches.- Training for CHWs: Conduct province-wide capacity-building sessions to equip community health workers with culturally sensitive communication skills.These geographically tailored activities aim to enhance health awareness, foster community trust, and mitigate violence against healthcare workers and facilities in under served regions.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Addressing misinformation on health issues and engaging communities in Northern Mozambique comes with inherent risks due to the complexity of the social, cultural, and security dynamics in the region. The following risks have been identified, along with measures to mitigate them: Potential Risks1. Physical and Security Risks:Trainers as trainees, may face risks of violence or hostility, particularly in areas with high mistrust of modern healthcare.2. Cultural Sensitivities:Addressing stigmatized health issues or challenging traditional practices could provoke resistance or exacerbate community tensions.3. Misinformation and Backlash:Miscommunication or poorly delivered messages could inadvertently reinforce distrust or lead to backlash against healthcare workers.4. Dependency on External Support:Over-reliance on project activities could weaken local community-driven solutions and undermine sustainable practices. Measures to Avoid Harm1. Upholding Humanitarian Principles:The project will strictly adhere to principles of humanity, impartiality, and neutrality. All activities will be culturally sensitive and respectful of local traditions, fostering trust and minimizing conflicts.2. Inclusive and Participatory Needs Assessment:Use participatory approaches to identify community needs and priorities, ensuring representation from all key groups, including women, community leaders, and traditional healers.3. Ethical and Quality Engagement:Train CHWs and project staff on ethical standards, including respect for beneficiary rights, cultural norms, and informed consent. Regular monitoring will ensure high-quality, respectful interactions with communities.4. Culturally Sensitive Messaging:Co-develop communication materials with local leaders and stakeholders to ensure alignment with cultural values while addressing misinformation effectively.5. Establishing Feedback Mechanisms:Set up confidential and accessible channels (e.g., suggestion boxes, hotlines) for communities to voice concerns, grievances, or suggestions. These will help course-correct and address unintended harm promptly.6. Building Local Ownership:Partner with community leaders and local organizations to build capacity and ensure sustainability beyond the project's duration. By carefully addressing these risks, the project aims to foster trust, ensure equitable engagement, and promote a long-term, positive impact on health and community resilience.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

By focusing on key members of the communities, including community leaders, mothers, and traditional medicine practitioners, the project directly addresses the root causes of stigma and misinformation surrounding certain diseases, including HIV, TB, and cholera. This strategy ensures that trusted voices within the community deliver accurate and culturally sensitive information, fostering acceptance and reducing barriers to accessing healthcare.Specific Measures:1. Targeted Outreach:o Community talks led by CHWs will include tailored sessions for women and girls, emphasizing the importance of regular check-ups, adherence to chronic disease treatments, and maternal and child health.o Special efforts will be made to reach children under five and their caregivers with messages on immunization and early disease detection.2. Empowering Vulnerable Groups:o Women and girls will be empowered through focused engagement to advocate for their families’ health and become active participants in promoting healthcare utilization.o People living with HIV or TB will be supported through stigma-reduction campaigns and information about treatment adherence, delivered through community leaders and radio programs.3. Breaking Stigmatization:o By fostering open dialogue on stigmatized health issues, the project will encourage individuals to seek help without fear of judgment, particularly for chronic or highly stigmatized conditions.4. Inclusive Participation:o Feedback mechanisms and participatory approaches will ensure that the voices of vulnerable groups are heard, allowing for the co-creation of solutions that reflect their needs and perspectives.5. Sustained Behavior Change:o The project will utilize culturally appropriate communication channels, such as radio, posters, and interpersonal discussions, to drive sustained behavior change, ensuring that vulnerable groups understand the value of accessing healthcare services and adhering to treatments.By reducing stigmatization, promoting equitable access, and engaging trusted community figures, the project seeks to empower vulnerable groups to actively participate in improving health outcomes for themselves and their communities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Access to health services for the most vulnerable is first affected when there is misinformation and mistrust in the health system. This project will identify the most vulnerable sub-groups and will prepare messages tailored for these sub-groups, as messages to remove barriers from the general population.The importance of inclusiveness and gender considerations should be considered during the design and implementation phase. When addressing the elderly consideration should be considered as their cultural beliefs may vary a lot from the general population. This believes could also be part the origin of misinformation, and precaution when addressing or challenging deep rooted believes should be taken.
