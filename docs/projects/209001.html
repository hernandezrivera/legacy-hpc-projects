Project Summary

20.3 million People in Sudan are now experiencing high levels on food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+), this accounts for 42% of the total population. The implications of the ongoing war including displacement and disruption to agricultural production are likely to exacerbate the situation. The target localities in this project demonstrate high severity of need and number of people in need (Blue Nile: Damazine 3 and 123,943 PiN, Roseries 3 and 103,677 PiN, Tadamon 3 and 50,990 PiN, Gissan 4 and 69,743 PiN and Wad-Almahi 5 and 55,038 PiN; and White Nile: Kosti 3 and 146,620 PiN, Rabak 3 and 121,723 PiN, Alsalam 4 and 385,775 PiN, and Aljabalein 4 and 342,245 PiN). All the targeted localities are at IPC 3 (crisis) except for Alsalam which is at IPC 4 (emergency). This project aims to respond to this crisis through provision with emergency FSL support focused on lifesaving and life-sustenance of people in need. Lifesaving agricultural support will be through provision of farming inputs and protection of livestock assets of affected populations. In addition, livelihood restoration interventions e.g. goat restocking and income generating activities especially targeting women and youth. Further, unconditional cash transfer will target the most vulnerable food insecure population as essential life-sustaining support.
The project objective is to improve the food and livelihood security and wellbeing of 149,400 conflict-affected person, including IDPs (85,800), host communities and vulnerable residents (50,400 and 13,200) in Blue Nile’s Damazine, Roseries, Tadamon, Gissan and Wad-Almahi localities (Blue Nile total: 75,000) and White Nile’s Rabak, Kosti, Alsalam and Aljabalein localities (White Nile total: 74,400). The project will achieve its objective through implementing emergency and lifesaving interventions to improve agricultural production, protect livelihood assets, diversify income sources, and provide immediate support in the form of cash to vulnerable people. The project interventions include the following:
1) Provision with farming inputs such as seed (food and cash crops), tools, irrigation equipment and extension packages to 8,200 HHs (49,200 individuals)
2) Support pastoral, agro-pastoral and fishing HHs (450) with restocking programs and fishery equipment (2700 individuals), focusing on women and youth
3) Livestock voucher treatment program and vaccination campaigns to reach 15,000 pastoral and agro pastoral HHs (90,000 individuals), targeting 750,000 heads of animals
4) Training and provision of start-up kits to 700 HHs (4200 individuals) focusing on female-headed families to provide alternative income generating activities such as milk and leather processing, soap making, sewing and perfume making
5) Unconditional cash transfer to 600 HHs (3,600 persons) to meet immediate needs of vulnerable individuals included persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women and malnourished children

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF G will ensure to integrate the protection mainstreaming in this project focused on improving food and livelihood security in Blue Nile and White Nile states through incorporating measures to identify, prevent, and respond to protection risks and concerns. The project will ensure that vulnerable populations are safeguarded, and that the interventions promote the dignity, safety, and rights of all individuals involved. VSF G will conduct comprehensive conflict and vulnerability assessments to understand the context and potential sources of tension in the project areas, and to identify groups or individuals at higher risk, such as women, children, elderly, or marginalized communities, especially among IDPs. VSF G will also analyze potential protection risks, including gender-based violence, discrimination, or any other threats that may arise during the project implementation and develop mitigation and management strategies for each challenge that may occur. Along with that VSF G will encourage community engagement and participation throughout the project life cycle and involve them in decision-making to ensure their needs and concerns are well considered. VSF G monitoring, evaluation and feedback and response mechanisms are in place to allow community members to voice their opinions and report protection concerns. Conflict and gender-sensitive approaches are well integrated in VSF G programming, recognizing and addressing the specific needs and vulnerabilities of different community groups as well as women and men in the target communities. VSF G in coordination with partners provide capacity building training for project team on protection principles, including the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. VSF G will continue to coordinate with the protection sector to ensure complementarity to create a safe and supportive environment for vulnerable populations in Blue Nile and White Nile states.
