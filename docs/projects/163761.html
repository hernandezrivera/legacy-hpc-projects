1 - Needs

In terms of humanitarian project planning and implementation regardless of sectors in Libya, some partners carry out conflict or context analysis and demonstrate a sound approach to responding to issues raised in project design. Other projects do not specifically conduct conflict analysis, but directly attempt to respond to conflict causes. Others do not explicitly seek to address conflict but use staff experience and in-depth understandings of local conflict and political dynamics, to succeed in achieving a conflict-sensitive approach to planning. On the other hand, there are other projects for which conflict sensitivity should have been taken into account better in initial assessments, with analysis of the context clearly articulate in written form. It is then possible that projects may actual exacerbate local tensions, rather than contributing in a broadly positive way. By ensuring no harm is done, understanding the interaction between projects and their contexts, and considering whether projects can contribute to peace, assistance can be more effective, efficient, and appropriate. The project of "inter-sectoral assessments linked to conflict sensitivity" need to be implemented independently from specific sectors but under the Coordination and Common Service Sector.

2 - Activities

In coordination with the Humanitarian Country Team and the Inter-Sector Coordination Group to ensure conflict sensitivity, the project of "inter-sectoral assessments linked to conflict sensitivity in Libya" aimed to assess the conflict sensitivity of the overall humanitarian programme in Libya, identifying best practices as well as areas in need of improvement, providing practical recommendations for follow up actions. The Assessments also evaluate the conflict-sensitivity of key Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 projects across a variety of geographical and sectoral areas. This proposed project will provide a brief and practical set of steps for incorporating conflict sensitive principles in all steps of the program design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. This funding will allow for the training of relevant project staff, a forum in which updated conflict analysis can be shared, and support for organisations in integrating conflict sensitive analyses in their own projects. There will also be scope to analyse and design projects that contribute to peace in some way, strengthening assistance that spans the humanitarian-development-peacebuilding nexus. All organisations and agencies involved with then be capacitated to implement their own conflict sensitivity assessments of their own projects; a snowball effect from this initial conflict sensitivity assessment support. In 2019, WFP piloted conflict sensitivity analysis for inter-UN agency projects in Sabha and Sirte and its own resilience-oriented projects in Azzawiya, Murzuq and Zwara.

4 - Protection risk analysis

SAFETY risk component - key informants are exposed to hostile or unpleasant treatments from others due to their participation in the primary data collection process of the assessments; DIGNITY risk component - key informants do not want to share their voices and observations to impact on their privacy; INTEGRITY risk component - some groups are excluded from the coverage of the assessments.

5 - Mitigation measures

To mitigate the identified protection risks and other risks and to enhance the reliability of the data, WFP will utilize its established call centre services and/or web-based surveys. All the key informants will be briefed on the privacy protection measures, the purposes of the assessments and the common feedback mechanism before answering the assessment questions. Only under their informed consents, the primary data collection from key informants will be conducted. The protection and gender lens will be applied to the data collection process.
