Project Summary

Under this project, IOM will manage and maintain a common humanitarian pipeline system in order to improve localization, coverage, effectiveness, and efficiency of distribution of relief items in hard-to-reach areas. IOM has established the online portal where partners will continue to access material, technical, logistic, and operational support for the distribution of relief items. The database for the active partners will be available to relevant stakeholders including cluster leads, working groups, and donors for further information, coordination, and funding purposes. In addition, IOM will continue to work in coordination with the government and the clusters in prepositioning, maintaining and managing the common pipeline. IOM plans to supply partners with the following items through the pipeline: (1) 60,000 non-food items (NFI) kits to reach approximately 300,000 individuals; (2) 20,000 emergency shelter kits (ESKs) to reach approximately 100,000 individuals, and (3) 120,000 family hygiene kits (FHKs) to reach approximately 720,000 individuals; as well as logistics and operational support. The support provided through the pipeline will act as a preparedness and response measure for addressing the needs of newly displaced and protracted populations as well as other and vulnerable people with special needs in different states. The distributions will be conducted in close coordination with the cluster leads, partners, and other relevant stakeholders to ensure consistency in provision of assistance and to reduce possible duplication of assistance. This will strengthen the role of local partners in delivering timely and effective emergency response to the affected populations through the system, fostering transparency, accountability, and integrity, and more generally building the promoting the capacity and promoting the role of local partners in responding to humanitarian crises.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM will adhere to its gender mainstreaming, PSEA and accountability to affected populations (AAP) guidelines, as they are aligned with the “do no harm” principle and IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Crises. These frameworks and principles will be discussed with common pipeline partners to ensure that distributions are being managed in a consistent way across the humanitarian response and adhere to the humanitarian principles. IOM will approve partners with proven capacity to deliver relief items and assistance especially if they have access to hard-to reach areas and require partners to assess and document the needs of individuals in extremely vulnerable situations to inform programme design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation in these areas. IOM and the relevant clusters and partners will coordinate to ensure the following elements are considered: (1) safety, security, and dignity for staff and beneficiaries; (2) equitable access to assistance based on fair eligibility criteria; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms. To ensure appropriate support is provided, partners will need to assess members of the community including people with additional vulnerabilities as well as women, girls, persons with disabilities who be separately consulted and encouraged to be fully involved in the consultation, distribution, and post-distribution processes; e.g., identifying the locations of distributions points, and identifying appropriate risk mitigation measures that ensure adherence to “do no harm”.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

IOM will conduct quarterly coordination meetings with partners, where records of requests for items and subsequent distributions will be presented and used as an evidence base to discuss the recorded gaps and needs and ensure the smooth flow of the common humanitarian pipeline towards providing timely assistance to vulnerable populations. This will improve coordination within the common pipeline system, ensuring that partners' concerns are considered. IOM will take the opportunity to provide regular updates on displacement trends and potential gaps in response, possible increase in needs, and ensure availability of relief items based on consultations with partners. This activity will be conducted targeting the partners utilizing the common pipeline system to (1) obtain information on their satisfaction with pipeline management, (2) obtain feedback from partners on the quality and quantity of the items distributed and (3) to get an update on the distribution of items/kits to affected populations. Quarterly online surveys will be shared with partners via email to obtain feedback on the performance of the pipeline management system and the quality of items. In addition, the survey will also assess how partners will be conducting distributions of the items in compliance with IOM guidelines, including conducting post distribution monitoring (PDMs). IOM will request the partners to share the distribution and PDM reports related to assistance provided through the common pipeline that will highlight the impact of the assistance on the affected populations and show any gaps and challenges. A report will be produced showing the level of satisfaction from the partners and their performance, and this can also be shared with relevant stakeholders. In addition, IOM will create a mailing system to include all partners, I/NGOs for the purpose of sharing information regarding the shipments of relief items, templates, pipeline SOPs, and IEC materials as well as to receive feedback/complaints or any other requests from the partners, in addition to sharing other relevant information and updates. IOM will also seek to scale up the pipeline which will increase predictability of response, reduce vulnerabilities, and provide dignified assistance to affected communities; this will include establishing the pipeline and identifying suppliers in Chad, South Sudan, and Egypt to procure, store, and transport items from the neighbouring countries which are more stable. IOM will request partners to conduct community meetings where feasible with local authorities, community leaders and members or individuals (with participation of representatives from all segments of the community including women, youth and people with disability) to share information pertaining to the project and obtain feedback. In addition, IOM will also request partners to promote the participation of affected communities/ individuals during the assessments that will inform the selection of beneficiaries. Help desks will be established by partners during distributions and inform beneficiaries to create sense of ownership. Partners’ commitment to the requests will be monitored through the distribution reports and post distribution monitoring exercises. Where possible, IOM staff present at the distribution sites will monitor the use of these help desks, taking note of any complaints raised.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA. Staff and partners will be trained on protection topics including how to treat GBV disclosures ethically and appropriately, as well as using the referral pathways and provide essential support to community members to access these pathways. IOM will work with staff, suppliers, partners to strengthen the implementation of the PSEA through awareness raising and delivery of PSEA face to face or virtual trainings in line with IOM PSEA guidelines.
