Project Summary

The FAO is planning to target under the revised HRP 2023 project to provide emergency agriculture and livestock-based livelihoods interventions to support vulnerable farmers, agro- pastoral and pastoral people from IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable resident host communities in Sudan. The vulnerability of the target populations stems from the protracted crisis, the ongoing economic hardships at the macro and micro-levels, negative impact of long dry spells and floods and recently the ongoing war.
This project covers all states in Sudan except Khartoum. Targeting 15,000,000 people among the most vulnerable communities including 8,250,000 vulnerable hosting communities’ people, 5,250,000 IDPs and 1,500,000 returnees. The planned interventions will benefit 4,590,000 women, 3,000,000 girls (less than 18 year of age), 4,410,000 men and 3,000,000 boys (less than 18 year of age). The proposed interventions are based on the basic and specific needs of the beneficiaries taking into account the factors of age and gender. The project will target about 1,500,000 venerable people (elderly, chronically ill and people with different types of disabilities.
The project interventions include as a top priority; supporting food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining through provision of support for agricultural production for 1,700,000 household (8,500,,000 individual), livestock and veterinary inputs and services targeting 1.300,000 household (6,500,000individual). The agricultural production will be improved through provision of climate-smart crop production, livestock and veterinary inputs and support to services. Similarly, small scale farmers will be targeted for reducing food losses and strengthened post-harvest techniques through improved storage of food and crop products and processing facilities.

FAO will support coordination with FSL sector partners and harmonises the response to food chain threats including animal and plant pests specially locust and birds; in addition, communities will be involved in protecting and maintaining their productive assets through awareness raising. Accountability to affected populations and environmental concerns will be mainstreamed into the proposed activities, also risk to GBV will be taken into consideration and application of the PSEA measures will be applied in collaboration with sister UN agencies.

1 - Needs

The food security of the Sudanese people in all Sudan affected severely due to the conflict. It was a critical food security situation even before the conflict. Now most of the people struggle to provide their families the daily meals. The conflict, causing large-scale displacements and livelihood losses, has severely constrained food availability and access and resulted, after less than one and half month, in the deterioration of an already difficult food security situation. According to the recently released 2023 Revised Humanitarian Response Plan, 19.9 million people expected to require emergency food and livelihood assistance in the June-September lean season, if the conflict continues. This figure is 70 percent higher than the pre-conflict estimate of 11.7 million people as reported in the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan that released at the end of 2022. The Food Security Sector (FSL) pins 19 Million people as experiencing crisis or worse levels of acute food insecurity. Food insecurity remains frighteningly high, accompanied by increased and prolonged displacement, economic collapse, with significant increases in food and other commodity prices. The FAO CFSAM 2022/2023 reported that the overall cereal production at national level is impressively better than the previous season, but food insecurity at the household level remains a serious challenge. Communities are facing differing scales of vulnerabilities driven by soaring prices of staple crops, the combined effects of economic downturn, high inflation, climate-induced hazards and conflict, and continued conflict, and high unemployment. Extreme gaps in food consumption or substantial losses of assets used to support the household's livelihood needs, are characteristics of households with severe food insecurity. For food insecure groups of people under IPC levels 3 and 4, will be supported by FAO to diversify their food consumption in 2023, through complementing vegetables and field crop production with support for livestock production, and enhanced access to essential services and social safety nets. According to WFP, the price of a local food basket, already 28 percent higher on a yearly basis, it is expected to increase by a further 25 percent in the next three to six months, if the conflict continues. The prices the prices of the agricultural inputs, the increase of the fuel prices will lead to increased cost of production and prices of crops’ produce. The FAO under the revised HRP 2023 project is targeting 15 million people in seventeen states. The main targeted beneficiaries of the project are people facing multiple shocks and those who are extremely vulnerable people (under IPC phase 3 and above). Who are in urgent need for lifesaving support from IDPs, returnees and vulnerable residents population groups, who are mainly engaged in agriculture and livestock activities and living in rural areas in Darfur, Kordofan, Blue Nile, White Nile, Sinnar, Kassala, Gedarif, River Nile, the Northern, Al Gazeera and Red Sea states. They are continuously affected by varying levels of conflict, drought, flood and adverse economic conditions over the last decade. The recent conflict eroded the reserves of the venerable small farmers all over Sudan, agro pastoralists and pastoralists, and increased the need for support in the prioritized areas. The findings of the FAO Country Gender Assessment (CGA) of Agriculture and the Rural Sector showed that, despite the common issues facing Sudanese men and women in rural areas, the lack of gender equality in rural areas, and in the agricultural sector places women at a clear disadvantage. The project will support mainstreaming FAO gender and Women Empowerment policy and women-friendly livelihood interventions to improve community and household resilience. The collection of sex-disaggregated data, develop gender-sensitive success stories training of government officials and sector partners will contribute to gender equality in Sudan.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Project objective is to provide lifesaving support for small-scale farmers, agro pastoralists and pastoralists’ food insecure people in Sudan. Outcome: 3,000,000 households (15,000,000 people) in 14 states in Sudan (5 Darfur states, 3 Kordofan states, White Nile State, Sinnar State, Blue Nile State, Gadaref state, Kassala State and Red Sea state) produced their own food to meet their family needs and sell the surplus in the markets.Proposed interventions:Output 1: 1,700,000 vulnerable food insecure households (8,500,000 people), supported with emergency agricultural inputs and on job training.Activities:• Procurement of agricultural inputs; seeds, tools and equipment • Provide emergency Staple food crops’ seeds (Sorghum and millet) and on job training to 1,700,000 households (8,500,000 people) for 2023 planting season;• Provide emergency vegetables, grain legumes seeds and donkey ploughs plus on job training to 700,000 vulnerable food insecure households (3,500,000 people) for winter/dry season planting;Output 2: 1,300,000 vulnerable pastoralist households (6,500,000 people) supported with livestock services (vaccination and treatment).Activities:• Procurement of livestock inputs; seeds, tools and equipment; • Provide livestock vaccines (PPR, Sheep Pox, HS and BQ) to support vaccination of 40,000,000 head of animals (Shoats, cattle) belong to 1,300,000 pastoralists’ households (6,500,000 people);• Provide assorted veterinary drugs for deworming/treatment of 4,000,000 heads of animals.Output 3: FSL Assessments conducted to inform FAO and FSL sector’s members’ planning and decision-making.Activities:- Development of the Terms of Reference (ToRs) for the assessment;- Selection of suitable sector partner to conduct the assessment;- Conduct the assessment data collection, data analysis and reporting- Sharing of information with partners and stakeholders.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

According to Sudan's Third National Communication (TNC) under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), which has been approved by the government, trends of lower levels and more unpredictability in rainfall, progressively rising temperatures, and more frequent extreme events, such as flooding, desertification, and drought, are seen throughout the nation. One of the nations acknowledged to be impacted by climate change is Sudan. This has had negative and significant effects on the country's natural resources, economic growth, and human vulnerability. The recent conflict will have short term and long-term impacts. The environmental impact of wars begins long before they do. Building and sustaining military forces consumes vast quantities of resources. These might be common metals or rare earth elements, water or hydrocarbons. Maintaining military readiness means training, and training consumes resources. Military vehicles, aircraft, vessels, buildings and infrastructure all require energy, and more often than not that energy is oil, and energy efficiency is low. This project has a medium impact on the environment since it focuses on agriculture,Livestock activities in 14 states out of the 18 states in Sudan. The project by its nature has some negative implications on the natural resources particularly soil due to agricultural and livestock activities. Soil erosion related to deforestation andInappropriate agricultural practices are the key challenge to sustainable natural resources exploitation in the targeted areas. The project will promote practices that favor soil protection and regeneration and don’t harm the local ecosystems to favor their progressive recovery. It will promote the cultivation of multipurpose legume crops that enrich and protect the soils, and strongly contribute to improving the nutrition of humans and their livestock. It will ensure that wastes from livestock vaccination and treatment are disposed in a proper way. Awareness rising in better natural resource management will be part of training. The project will engage in a number of mitigation activities that include tree plantation, training of households in effective and durable caretaking of the planted seedlings, and on agro‐forestry practices and use of LPG and fuel‐efficient stoves for domestic uses. The project has an environmental impact in general and in Darfur, Kordofan and Eastern Sudan in particular, as those areas are experiencing more droughts that are frequent, floods and storms causing damage to the environment and people's livelihoods.Poorly managed livestock has much more implications in GHG emissions and its intensity varies between regions causing climate change phenomena. The project will contribute effectively to the efforts that can build resilience, prepare for, and adapt to climate change through some mitigation measures that are explained below:1- Increasing livestock productivity through supplementary feeding.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

TThe FAO project contributes to the protection of people’s livelihoods and food security by mitigating and addressing food chain threats. Food security, livelihoods, and national and international markets are all impacted by threats to the food chain. Food security and food safety may be significantly impacted by risks, which include illnesses and pests of animals and plants as well as dangers to food safety. FAO depends on Emergency Prevention System (EMPRES) to capture those threats on time by obtaining many assessments. FAO strengthened the process of helping transform its agri-food systems to achieve better production, better nutrition, a better environment and a better life, by supporting farmers and rural communities with seeds and livestock to strengthen their agricultural livelihoods and produce healthy, nutritious food and hence protect people from hunger.The Organization’s overarching goal is to improve the food security and nutrition of communities affected by conflicts, natural and manmade shocks and disasters, while enhancing families’ self-reliance and resilience to threats and crises in line with national priorities and FAO’s corporate Strategic objectives. The project will consider identifying who is at risk and how to support at the very outset of a crisis and thereafter, taking into account the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities. The project will provide suitable interventions that improve capacities of women, men, youth and people with disabilities to produce and meet their needs. The project will give equal opportunities to all categories of the population including women, men, girls, boys, elders and people with disabilities to access the provided livelihood support, addressing the rights of all population categories to decent work to restore their food security and livelihood situation. The targeted communities have been involved in dialogue through meetings, interviews and group discussions throughout steps of this project pre-design and they will be directly involved during the implementation process including beneficiaries’ selection, registration and implementation of the activities, monitoring the implementation at the field and beneficiaries levels. As explained in the above paragraphs, FAO provides the necessary guidance for promoting the four pillars of decent work for the beneficiaries in the targeted rural areas. Under the present circumstances, where the fighting is still going on and many cases GBV were not reported because of absence of law enforcement bodies and weak access to hospitals besides the social stigma; FAO will mainstream awareness on GBV and PESA to protect women, girls and boy’s sexual abuse and violence, especially during the agriculture and livestock activities. The sector Partners will provide the training and input distribution.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

AAccountability to affected population integrated into all aspects of the way that FAO works. It is incorporated into the definition of results in the Strategic Framework and Medium-term Plan, and how FAO plans, implements, monitors and reports on its programme of work. FAO accountability towards affected people starts from selection of interventions, varieties and quantities of livestock and agricultural inputs. The inputs selection defined in a participatory manner during the consultation phase. FAO encourages the local communities not only to participate in the implementation of the activities but also to ensure ownership of the project. As a result, FAO assists beneficiaries to form a local committee that represent all people in the community, train them on their roles and responsibilities. This training facilitates the fair selection of beneficiaries and distribution of livelihoods support to its members. Either Beneficiaries” complaints about types, varieties and quantities of inputs or any other question received directly by FAO staff, or they can call the Service Provider nominated person to respond or in case the reply is not convincing, he/she can raise the issue to the higher managerial level in FAO Sudan. Comments and recommendation usually collected during post distribution missions.FAO shows continues interest to hear the voices of the beneficiaries about its standard packages of inputs, which have been subject to change every year taking into account the views of the targeted people. FAO urged all its implementing partners to open hot lines with the beneficiaries to allow them to express their opinions about the intervention and give them the chance to bring out any grievances they may have about the interventions. The project will promote understanding between the different livelihood groups (pastoralists and farmers) through group meetings, discussions and other community platforms to reduce conflict and encourage mutual support. Special focus will be on enhancing the knowledge and skills of men, women, adolescent girls and other at risk-groups on natural resources management and leadership to ensure sustainable livelihood interventions; this will help in avoiding competition over natural resources. The economic devastation of the pandemic is leaving women and girls from affected communities, already in vulnerable situations, a greater risk of SEA. SEA complaint mechanisms have become even harder to access due to the rise in self-isolation measures, such as physical distancing policies, curfews and quarantines. School closures have not only interrupted education, but also affected school-based services for children at risk and placed additional childcare responsibilities on women, further restricting women’s access to livelihoods and economic security. As the crisis continues, children expected to face separation from their families due to illness, and death.
