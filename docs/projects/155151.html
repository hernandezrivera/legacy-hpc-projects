Project Summary

This project aims to improve access to quality survivor centered services responding to individual needs, prevention and mitigating of GBV risks, and empowerment of vulnerable refugee and host community women, girls and boys survivors of GBV. UNICEF will work in partnership with ActionAid Bangladesh, DanChurchAid, BRAC, CARE Bangladesh, World Vision Bangladesh and IRC to reach 134,137 beneficiaries (31,388 women, 28,973 men; 38,364 girls and 335,411 boys) by supporting GBV awareness, strengthening services at existing Safe Spaces, providing GBV trainings to service providers, supporting adolescent with critical lifesaving services including distribution of dignity kits, supporting watch-groups and strengthening the roll-out of GBVIMS

Needs

One year has passed from the influx of September 2017, when the humanitarian situation was declared a Level 3 Emergency – the highest level of disaster in the global humanitarian system. Different Humanitarian agencies have put the best effort to improve the situation but still according to the Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) Situational Report Data Summary of the Rohingya’s refugee situation (November 2018), there are about 923,000 estimated refugees in Cox Bazar seeking for international protection and relying on humanitarian assistance for survival and dignity. The GBV Sub-sector has projected that 610,117 people need assistance in case of GBV Prevention and response programming and among them 455,241 people are from refugee community and 154,875 are from host community Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a high risk and reality for Rohingya women and girls displaced in Cox’s Bazar district, Bangladesh with the current displacement and congestions putting them at further risk. Women and girls face additional challenges in securing their safety, accessing information and services and negotiating decision-making. Restrictions on movement, cultural and social norms, low literacy and numeracy and reduced community and legal protections increase women and girls’ exposure to GBV. Women and girls face an on-going protection risks, including sexual exploitation and abuse. The current camp situation in combination with the trauma experienced in Myanmar contributes to increased vulnerability to the forms and patterns of GBV that are still being highlighted in this context which includes Rape; sexual violence; Intimate partner violence; Sex trafficking; Early, child and forced marriage; Sexual exploitation etc. Adolescent girls face unique and specific risks as they enter a life-changing phase in their development including trafficking which has been an ongoing issue in the Cox’s Bazar area predating the current crisis. According to the Mid-term review of JRP 2018 only 43 % of minimum service coverage has been achieved for urgently required GBV case management and psychosocial support for children and adults. At least 115 additional GBV case management service entry points are required to achieve essential coverage for life-saving care for the total population in need (1.3 M). The report has highlighted the requirement of expanded GBV service provision for the host community with a clear mention of the limited access in approximately 85% sites within Bangladeshi communities hosting refugees. In the Joint Agency Research Report called "Gender Analysis" by ACF, Save the Children and Oxfam it is very clear that the prevalence of traditional harmful practices is very common in the community like child marriage, polygamy, domestic violence etc. The report has several recommendations for preventing GBV that focus on dissemination of information on GBV referral systems, engaging men and boys, women and girls and community leaders in behavior change activities, addressing GBV with the aim of changing harmful social and traditional norms through awareness raising.

Activities or outputs

UNICEF Child Protection will carry out the following key activities to contribute to the overall GBV JRP objectives through the following key activities: 1. Support awareness activities on Gender Based Violence Prevention and response2. Strengthen existing and create new Safe Spaces for Women and Girls as a place for GBV Service provision for survivors of GBV and women in general. Referral services and case management will be the key priority for those spaces. UNICEF has already established 1 Safe space in Camp 9 that has the access of both host & refugee communities and planned to establish 3 more spaces strategically located for access of host and refugee communities in camps 11 and 16. So, in this way UNICEF will attempt to build the coordination and social cohesion with the host and refugee communities.3. Provide training to GBV service providers and other humanitarian actors minimum GBV Minimum Standards (GBVIMS, Caring for Child Survivors, GBV IASC Guidelines) and GBV mainstreaming. 4. Ensure women and adolescent girls access to critical lifesaving and Gender appropriate services including dignity kits and Structured Psychosocial Support. Dignity kits will be distributed after a certain period considering the camps in need and after consultation with the women and girls from the safe spaces. UNICEF will also store 10,000 DK for responding the emergency. 5. Support women and adolescent girls with structured empowerment programmes including Cash based assistance and skills building. UNICEF is planning to have an assessment to find out how cash assistance can be an influencing factor for reducing GBV. After the Assessment, UNICEF will choose the modality of cash assistance. 6. Community Watch Groups and Women Leadership Groups will be formed and functional to strengthen the Community Based Protection System. There will be Interactive Theatre groups for adolescent girls and boys to mobilize the community and raise awareness. 7. All the Implementing Partners will be reporting in GBVIMS for the UNICEF funded project. UNICEF has already signed ISP and the active member of GBV Sub-sector, GBVIMS Taskforce, GBV Case Management Task Force, Needs Assessment Task Force and PSEA Network. The inter-agency coordination will be maintained to prevent and respond GBV in the community.8. UNICEF Protection section will be maintaining close coordination with other sector like WASH, Health, Education, etc. to provide the best services to the women and girls attending the safe spaces.
