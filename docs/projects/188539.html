Project Summary

Solidarités International (SI) and its implementing partner Concern Worldwide (CWW) will build on the experience and results accomplished during Phase I and create safe learning environments for school children, and at the same time, increase the employment opportunities of vulnerable youth. During Phase I which ended November 30th, 2021, fourteen schools were rehabilitated and provide now a safe learning environment for students in Raqqa and Hasakeh, and 358 boys and girls were enrolled in non-formal education services. SI and Concern conducted assessments to fine-tune the response strategy, and developed productive relationships with relevant education departments. The Partners can now determine the needs for Phase II phase together with local authorities, and particularly with the Education Committee and the Education Working Group in Northeast Syria.
In Phase II, SI and Concern will continue to increase access to education in a safe learning environment for Syrian children affected by conflict in Raqqa, Hasakeh, Aleppo and Deir ez-Zor governorates, according to a list of schools prioritized by the local education authorities in consultation with the NES Education Working Group and in addition to technical assessments. The project will prioritise schools with the highest number of out of school children.

4 - Needs

School Rehabilitation, including WASH in SchoolsWhile specific needs differ in the proposed target areas, many schools were destroyed or heavily damaged by coalition forces. According to UNICEF, one out of three schools in Syria cannot be used, as those schools either have been destroyed, damaged, or are used for military purposes . Furthermore, WASH facilities, health and hygiene education in schools are in need of urgent improvement. According to the Humanitarian Needs Assessment for Whole of Syria on WASH, the majority of students had multiple complaints ranging from no toilets and no sinks to nearly all complaining about cleanliness and two-thirds of students noting no soap to wash hands .

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection mainstreaming is provided through meaningful access to impartial services to beneficiaries, while paying attention to the context and socio-cultural norms, to limit the risks created or heightened by aid. SI and Concern work to minimise any unintended negative effects of the intervention (do-no-harm principle) and work towards equality, impartiality, accountability, participation and empowerment. This promotes safety and dignity of the beneficiaries. Furthermore, Concern’s education work is complemented by its protection programme, providing child and adolescent protection services in 15 locations across NES, as well as referrals, case management and emergency cash for protection support.The proposed intervention seeks to minimize secondary risks and deter the increase of existing risks, including physical harm and injuries, protection risks and financial losses. Targeted do-no-harm strategies are integrated in program design, procurement policies and activity selection. The gender mainstreaming approach is one such strategy, the success of which is recorded through its MEAL activities and the Feedback and Complaint Mechanism.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The Partners’ Protection of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse Policy (PSEA) guidelines specifically address risks of sexual abuse within the organisations. 11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• Access to vulnerable population and vendors is not further reduced by additional border-crossings restrictions • Operations are not further limited by regulatory restrictions inside Syria • Safety and security of SI and partner staff is not compromised • SI is able to appropriately manage the risks of aid diversion to designated terrorist groups or through corruption and bribery • Exchange rates and material/service costs remain reasonably stable throughout implementation period of the project • Sufficient materials and skills required for the output are available in the targeted geographic areas• Construction companies with sufficient capacity are available inside Syria• Labour with sufficient technical skills is available inside Syria• Security situation allows regular sharing of information between stakeholders• Communication channels remain open and enable relevant monitoring methods• Payment mechanisms remain open for the reimbursement of suppliers• Inter-agency coordination mechanisms minimize the risk of duplication and variations in levels of assistance. Risks: Non-materialization of the assumptions mentioned aboveMitigation: • Use money transfer system (hawala) for operations in North Syria including for the payment of its staff and contractors • Cooperation with Turkish authorities and capacity to work Cross-Border from Turkey. • SI maintains a representation in Gaziantep in Turkey with its main coordination office in Amman, Jordan.
