Project Summary

The proposed project aims at addressing emergency and more sustainable shelter and NFI needs of vulnerable households with acute needs (both IDPs and host community members) in Northeast and Northwest Syria.
The intervention will target a total of 123,625 beneficiaries under shelter activities, and 153,004 beneficiaries under NFI activities across Northwest and Northeast Syria for a total budget of 5,472,963$ including 4,209,680$ of program budget. To note, some beneficiaries will receive more than one type of assistance. As a result, some of them if will double-counted under the activities in section 4.
In Northwest Syria, ACTED will provide fuel for heaters to better equip the residents for winter in A’zaz and Idleb sub-districts for 111,095 individuals for 1,148,474$ (program budget). Under this intervention, the same number of camp residents will also receive emergency NFI kits -composed of, for example, tarpaulin, jerry can, mattresses/sleeping mats, blankets, solar lamps, floor mat, stove, cooking pot, bowl for food, and cutlery - as well as winterization kits, to guarantee shelter and dignified living conditions to newly displaced household, for 196,296$ (program budget). Through contractors, ACTED will rehabilitate private off-camp shelter units to 600 individuals in A’zaz sub-district for 131,046$ (program budget). In addition, ACTED will rehabilitate shelters in-camp to 500 individuals in Jarablus sub-district for 135,061$ (program budget). In addition, 750 individuals out-of-camp will benefit from shelter repairs for 255,409$ (program budget) and ACTED will provide multi-purpose cash Assistance for winterization to 15,000 individuals in Aleppo governorate, for a total amount of 266,051$ (program budget).
In Northeast Syria, ACTED will provide fuel and heaters to 14,433 camp residents across Areesheh camp, Al-Hasake governorate, to better equip the residents for winter for a total amount of 829,868$ (program budget). In Serekaniye camp, Al-Hasake governorate, ACTED will distribute fuel and heaters to 12,476 camp residents for a total of 291,840$ (program budget).
These camp residents will also benefit from installation and maintenance of fire safety points, for 11,333$ (program budget). In Ar-Raqqa governorate, ACTED will rehabilitate household shelters for dignified living conditions to 10,680 beneficiaries in out-of-camp areas for a total of 1,365,650$ (program budget).
ACTED will take into account the different risks and concerns affecting women, men, girls and boys and people with disabilities when designing this intervention. With regards to the latter, ACTED will ensure that the distributions are conducted respectfully of the needs of the most vulnerable, with specific distribution lines dedicated to women and elderlies. ACTED will also conduct the distributions directly at the HH level when needed. As for the shelter rehabilitation activity, ACTED will ensure to select the HHs also based on vulnerability, with consideration for income and/or demographics. ACTED will develop BoQs for each shelter based on technical shelter assessments. The assessment of the shelter will take into consideration privacy needs, as well as specific needs for people with disabilities when relevant and particular works will be incorporated as necessary. This will ensure safe and dignified living conditions to the targeted HHs in the area of intervention.

4 - Needs

Due to limited access to livelihoods, camp residents, IDPs and conflict-affected people are highly dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs, particularly with regards to non-food items (NFI) and winterization, as limited purchasing powers means households must prioritize food and water at the expenses of NFI and shelter. In addition, access to NFI markets has become increasingly challenging due to the Covid-19 crisis and depreciation of the SYP, with displaced populations among the most impacted groups. Shelter destruction and limited livelihoods to conduct necessary repairs put already vulnerable populations at risk, especially in winter. IDPs in camp settings in NWS endured an exceptionally harsh 2021-22 winter. In NES, in January 2021, nearly half of assessed IDP communities reported winterization assistance to constitute their third priority need, according to REACH. Living in unsafe, overcrowded, damaged shelters pushes out-of-camp residents to resort to relocate to camp settings. In turn, extensive shelter damage significantly discourages in-camp residents from returning to their location of origin. As formal camps face critical capacity issues, newly displaced households turn toward informal settlements, where access to NFI items is the most limited. This is compounded by continued displacement trends, particularly across NES. This highlights needs for in-camp and out-of-camp SNFI support.

5 - Implementation of activities

ACTED will implement a multi-faceted intervention. On the first hand, ACTED will focus on providing winterization NFI items as well as heaters to conflict-affected IDPs and camp residents across Northwest and Northeast Syria to better equip them for winter to individuals: ACTED will distribute heating fuel to 14,433 and 12,476 camp residents respectively across Areesheh and Serekaniye camps in NES, as well as in A’zaz camps and Idleb camp clusters in NWS for 111,095 camp residents. The same number of camp residents in Idleb and Aleppo governorates will also receive emergency shelter kits and winterization NFI kits.On the other hand, ACTED will provide shelter rehabilitation support out of camp in order to provide IDPs and vulnerable host communities with safe and dignified places to live: ACTED will provide off-camp private shelter repair works across Ar-Raqqa governorate for 10,680 individuals in NES and Aleppo governorate in NWS for 1,850 individuals. ACTED will consider to include the provision of solar energy systems as part of the rehabilitation interventions should budget allow. ACTED will increase individuals’ capacity to undertake sector interventions through multi-purpose Cash Assistance for winterization for 15,000 individuals.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups’ needs during project design/implementation. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach. ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct covering Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV),and ACTED implements Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects. Risk 3: Eviction of beneficiaries from the household upon the completion of the rehabilitation works. Likelihood: Medium – Impact: High. Mitigation Measure: A strong HLP due diligence process and MoUs with the rehabilitated buildings' owners will mitigate eviction risks and related conflicts. Where possible, in order to reinforce sustainability of the agreement. ACTED will also ensure that a Shelter Beneficiary Agreement (SBA) is signed between the involved stakeholders (owner of the house/apartment, ACTED and the contractor). These contracts will contribute to securing tenure for the displaced families for at least twelve months after the SBA signature.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behaviour of the contractor employees towards camp residents and beneficiaries in targeted villages. Information on the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) will be communicated to camp manager, community leaders, camp residents and targeted individuals in out of camp areas. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. ACTED will ensure that out-of-camp population are particularly aware of the CRM and tools to provide complaints (WhatsApp group, letterbox, desks).Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff in the teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Theft: SNFI kits procured for the project could be stolen in transit to or from warehouses. Ground-level storage spaces below residential properties are used as and when possible, warehouses are protected by a security guard 24 hours a day, and keys to warehouses are solely in the possession of the Storekeeper who is responsible for all stock movements. Warehouse locations are carefully considered and when possible located outside of the town perimeter and near checkpoints for additional security.Diversion of assistance: ACTED has a robust and established Standard of Procedures for its activities to track the assistance to ensure it reaches the targeted beneficiaries. ACTED and partners’ security teams will conduct thorough security assessments in each target area to identify secure distribution sites. Distribution schedules will be set up to ensure that there is only a small number of beneficiaries per day and the location of the distribution site is close to the area where beneficiaries are. Monitoring and evaluation units also conduct 100% verification and extensive Post-Distribution Monitoring to track the assistance provided. Access and security to the sites, for in-camp and out-of-camp contexts: Security and Political, Expansion to new areas: in-depth access mission from the ACTED Security team, formal approval required for daily movements and daily sharing of security updates.
