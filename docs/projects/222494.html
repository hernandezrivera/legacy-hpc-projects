Project Summary

The Medair planned health activities are proposed to be implemented in Pibor and Leer counties, inclusive of scaling up emergency response activities through an emergency response team (ERT) to locations with public health emergencies which are accessible and feasible for program activities. According to the Health Cluster’s severity scale for 2025, Leer and Pibor are priority 1. The project is designed aligned to the Core Humanitarian and Sphere Minimum Standards to ensure that affected beneficiaries receive timely, coordinated, adequate, resilient, and impartial basic quality healthcare services inclusive of reproductive health, mental health, and psychosocial services (MHPSS). The proposed health project will seek to reduce morbidity and mortality by improving access to quality essential primary healthcare, including reproductive health, services to survivors of SGBV, and mental health services, to girls, boys, men, and women in emergency situations, with a specific focus on accessibility for persons living with disability and the elderly, as well as responding to public health emergencies. The project aims to build the resilience of communities and the MoH to prevent, detect, and respond to disease outbreaks as well as other public health emergencies such as floods and large population displacements such as from conflict that disrupt critical health services.
Methodology and target: Medair's project focuses on the provision of primary healthcare in areas of high need with attendant service delivery gaps, including Leer and Pibor. Supported activities include the provision of curative consultations in primary health facilities; community-based treatment of common illnesses in children under five; routine vaccination; reproductive and sexual health including the provision of ANC, safe delivery, PNC, and clinical management of SGBV; management of at-risk infants through MAMI; and mental health treatment. Medair conducts ongoing training and supportive supervision for capacity-building staff of Medair, CHD, and other partners to improve the quality-of-service provision. Medair primarily targets children under five, women, and people with disabilities and mental health conditions, due to their unique vulnerability, but not exclusively. Through the ERT, Medair can respond in any area of South Sudan in response to disease outbreaks, acute displacement, reports of increased morbidity and mortality, nutritional emergencies, flooding, and other natural disasters. Medair ERT aims to reduce morbidity and mortality of vulnerable populations through outbreak response such as vaccination campaigns or case management, and provision of primary healthcare services where these are inadequate. Through training and community engagement. Medair aims to increase the capacity of long-term health partners and the local community to be prepared for future shocks.
Medair's health activities are integrated with WASH, Nutrition, and Protection activities to enhance the effectiveness and address underlying causes of morbidity and mortality.

1. Needs

It is estimated that there are 6.3 million people in need of healthcare, an increase from 6.1 million people in 2023 with 80% of the population continuing to report barriers to accessing health services. Beyond the health crisis that is made more complex by factors such as insecurity, conflict, cattle raids, flooding, and disease outbreaks, factors like long distances of travel to facilities only compounds the situation. Adding in the dynamic of internally displaced persons, and unregistered returnees or refugees, the burden on an already overwhelmed system only worsens. In addition, only 8% of the 1869 available facilities provide the full basic package of health and nutrition services. Service delivery gaps continue to be more noticeable because of the combination of challenges including a lack of funding, inadequate staffing, medicine, and equipment to support quality service provision. The health sector transformation project currently provides support to approximately 43% of the health facilities in the country and with only 2% of the national budget directed towards the health sector, there remain many gaps in healthcare access. Malaria continues to be a leading cause of morbidity and mortality (50.2% of cases and 39.4% of deaths attributed to malaria), and the number of AWD cases is increasing across the country. There was an official cholera outbreak declared in Renk County in September 2024 and as of November 2024, the country was also dealing with multiple outbreaks of cholera and measles across the country, as well as Hepatits E outbreaks across multiple counties and states. Outbreaks of vaccine preventable diseases such as measles have been attributed to the low levels of routine immunisation with an average of approximately 6 out of 10 children being vaccinated. The continuance of these outbreaks is highly influenced by the congested living conditions in many of the IDP and refugee camps with cholera in particular being affected by factors such as limited access to clean water and sanitation, ongoing flooding and limited access to hygiene products.Medair’s static sites are in Pibor and Leer counties while the ERT conducts responses in the event of acute emergencies across the country. Pibor and Leer are classified as priority 1 with the highest level of health needs. In Pibor, ongoing insecurity in the area continues to impact people’s access to health care, due to looting of health facilities and facility closures due to population displacement. Geographical impediments also impact services, with areas cut-off for significant periods of time. In previous programming, some capacity has been built in the area of staff training in reproductive health and PFA however, inadequate staffing numbers and poor referral mechanisms on account of absence of referral facilities have led to many psychosocial support needs and RH needs remaining largely unmet. In Leer, increasing insecurity and catastrophic flooding has not only led to massive displacements of the population but has also led to looting and destruction of health and nutrition facilities within the county. The existing health services are insufficient in meeting the high level of needs in the community and an emergency response is needed across all sectors to bridge the large gap in access to appropriate health, nutrition and WASH services. Mental health services continue to be a significant need in Leer with acute and chronic insecurity increasing the vulnerability of the population to poor mental health and reduced psychosocial well-being, impacting families and communities.The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will be deployed for a period of up to 3 months to respond to health emergencies by providing facility and community-level basic life-saving primary healthcare integrated with nutrition services.

2. Project activities

Medair’s aim will be to support lifesaving health programming with a long-term view of strengthening local capacity for future service delivery. The Medair project will consist of both acute emergency response capacity and longer-term primary healthcare interventions. All activities will be conducted through multisectoral responses to maximize impact on acute and chronic public health emergencies.The emergency response team (ERT) will assess and respond to acute public health emergencies in all areas of South Sudan, including disease outbreaks, large-scale population displacement, emergency GAM rates, significant increases in morbidity and mortality, and natural disasters. The ERT health team will conduct multiple interventions simultaneously with short-term responses aimed at managing the initial shock of acute emergencies with interventions of up to three months duration. Exit strategy will be a core part of pre-response planning and on completion of a response, handover will be conducted either to other international or local NGOs or to the MoH/ County Health Department (CHD) where there is no long-term partner present or capable of taking up activities. In the event that the intervention is no longer necessary-return of displaced persons home, cessation of new cases in an outbreak, etc- the response will be closed without handover as the services will no longer be needed. The ERT will use different modalities including temporary or mobile clinics, integrated community case management (iCCM), mass vaccination campaigns, health facility technical and or operational support for MoH and or existing health partners. Medair will focus on capacity building of local health staff and increasing resilience of community members through training, education and community engagement.In Pibor, Medair will continue to work to improve access to essential primary health care in a community that has experienced multiple compounded shocks in recent years, including ongoing insecurity and geographical access challenges. Medair will continue to support two PHCUs in Gumuruk and Lekuangole with OPD, EPI, and maternal health services, with a focus on scaling up maternal health care to provide the basic package of maternal and neonatal care. ICCM services will continue to be provided in the two payams, as well as Cascade groups which will aim to improve health, nutrition, and WASH knowledge and practices in the community. Medair will provide supportive supervision, technical quality assurance and operational support to all activities and work towards providing all services according to local and international standards. Medair will also introduce MHPSS programming in Pibor, with community-based psychosocial support and MH treatment following mhGAP-HIG protocols in 3 health facilities. Medair will also provide mobile outreach services to communities with limited access to existing health facilities. In Leer, Medair will continue to implement community-based programming through iCCM, treating common illnesses in children under 5 and working closely with other partners for referral. Medair will also continue to provide mental health services in three primary healthcare facilities following mhGAP-HIG protocols, services for survivors of SGBV, and psychosocial support services through community cascade groups. Medair will support primary health care services through ongoing support to Dindin PHCU, including OPD, EPI, and RH services.
