Project Summary

Through this project, Action Against Hunger is aiming to target 111073 (58338 males and 52735 females) of vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host community.
Our areas of intervention will primarily be Fangak, and Pibor Counties of Jonglei State, Tonj East and Gogrial West counties of Warrap state and Aweil East county of Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Our response activities will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, and protection. All the under 5 children, pregnant and lactating women will be screen for malnutrition at the health facilities and enrolled to our nutrition programming. All our health facilities will have minimum package of WASH services. We will consider protection aspect and our medical team will be train on SGMV counselling and management. We will take into consideration the protection area by providing the first aid care to the SGBV victims and MHPSS as well as identifying and strengthening the referral pathway for clinical management of rape and mental health cases. Our response will focuses on returnees, IDPs and host communities. The response plan will be prioritized based on epidemiological prole of disease outbreaks, such as cholera hot spot locations, measles outbreaks and malaria seasonality. IDSR, EWARS, attacks on health care and disability tracking tools will be used to determine the feasibility of the response. Our operational plans will always involve the community members, women girls youths and PLWs (people living with disability) County Health department (CHD) in planning and organization of health services. Meetings with communities and CHDs are some of the indicators used to monitor the accountability for affected populations. We will facilitate meetings with all stakeholders like partners, beneficiaries, women groups and local communities for their involvement in planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects in all stages of the program cycle.
To improve access to essential life-saving health care services, and to address inaccessibility and long distance to access health services, the organization will use outreach clinics through community health volunteers/ICCM to provide health services at the community as well as static health facilities (PHCCs and PHCCs) to provide Basic Package of health and nutrition services as per South Sudan guideline to achieve the following objectives:
1. Specific Objective 1.3: Decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses, such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine preventable illnesses, such as measles, through ICCM and EPI program by targeting the following:
- ICCM: 5921 (3083 Boys and 2838 Girls )
- Outreach and health facilities EPI program: 8201 ( 4199 Boys and 4002 Girls)

Sectoral Objective 1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations through the following activities:
- ANC services to 1389 women
- Assisting 347 women in deliveries by a skilled birth attendant
- Provision of OPD consultations to 95215 (49485 male and 45730 female)
- Out of the total beneficiaries, will consider 15% as people living with disability.

1 - Needs

As per 2022 South Sudan humanitarian needs overview, the majority of population in the country continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation has worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have affected people’s livelihoods, access to sanitation and hygiene and health services. In this year, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 compare to last year. Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing repeatedly. At least 90,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. In 2021, there were 2 million IDPs in the country (55 per cent of whom are women and girls), as compared to 1.7 million in 2020. Above normal rainfall for the third consecutive year in 2021 led to prolonged flooding, which affected areas that had not flooded in previous years. Many flood-displaced people, including those who were displaced by the 2020 flood were unable to return to their homes until early 2022, if at all. Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei states were the most affected in terms of crop and livestock production. The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage of the water facilities, leading to increase in number of water borne diseases, limited access to basic health services and high prevalence of disease outbreaks. Humanitarian access to essential health care services remains a challenge in an already complex context. Between January and December 2021, 591 reported humanitarian access incidents were recorded. Humanitarian warehouses and facilities were targeted during the violence, and the supplies were looted in some locations, significantly affecting response operations in conflict-affected areas. Only 59 per cent of the population can access health facilities in one hour’s time while 80 per cent reported barriers in accessing health services.Extremely low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3 coverage) has made children more vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases. Malaria is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 52.6 per cent of all morbidities and 58.2 per cent of all mortalities in 2021. While no new cholera outbreak has been declared since the end of 2017, the number of reported AWD cases is increasing and 20 counties are now classified as cholera hotspots with a high risk of new outbreaks. Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with 789 per 100,000 live births139, Only 39 per cent women are availing recommended four Antenatal Care Services during pregnancy and institutionalized delivery as well as skilled birth attendance are low at 12.3 per cent and 14.7 per cent respectively. Access to BEmONC is very low and contributes to pregnancy related and new-born morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable populations. Based on the July 2021 Gogrial west nutrition SMART survey, childhood ilnness was reported by 50.5% of children and only half of the children could access medical care. EPI service is below the SPHER standard of 80%. Children received measles vaccination was 52.9%. Children received vitamin A supplementation and deworming was 38.6% and 33.6% respectively. 59% of Household with poor food consumption score.

2 - Project activities

This project will focus on the following Activities:1- ICCM services: To address the issues of inaccessibility and health service coverage, the organization will use ICCM approach to diagnose and treat common childhood illnesses such as mild malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhoea at the community level. The CBDs will be train and equip with raped diagnostic test for malaria/mRDT, Respiratory timer, Thermometers, oral anti-malarial drugs, oral Amoxicillin, Zinc tablets and ORS for diagnose and treatment of Malaria, Pneumonia and Diarrhoea. ICCM will be used as the first referral point, whereby the CBDs will be referring the complicated cases to the nearby health facility for further management.2- EPI services: To increase the coverage and to respond to the outbreaks such as measles and Vitamin A supplement, the organization will use mobile vaccination in the community and static vaccination at the health facilities to ensure the wider coverage. We will be engaging UNICEF to provide Artech, refrigerator for hard to reach areas and referral of women of childbearing ages to receive routine immunization in static and outreach EPI services.3- OPD consultations/services: The OPD will provide consultation and treatment for common illnesses such simple malaria, Acute water diarrhoea (AWD), acute respiratory infections (ARI), UTIs, skin and eye infections, minor wounds/injuries, active case finding for TB/HIV defaulters, Visceral Leishmaniosis (Kala-Azar) and timely referral of such cases to the nearest TB-HIV or Kala-Azar treatment centres. 4- Antenatal care and maternity services: Through ANC services, blood pressure and haemoglobin level will be monitor in each visits, and all the pregnant women will be provided with ferrous + folic acid, Albendazole for deworming, screening for Syphilis, HIV and other STIs, rapid test for malaria (RDTs), malaria prophylaxis, support of normal deliveries in health facilities and referral of complicated pregnancies and deliveries to the nearest CEMoNC facilities. 5- Detect, investigate, report and respond to outbreaks example of cholera, measles, COVID-19 in the areas of target through strengthening EWARS/IDSR system in training and coordination with stakeholders (Health Cluster-WHO, MoH and CHD). 6- Identification, provision of psychological support and first aid treatment to the SGBV victims and Clinical Management of Rape/CMR as well as referral of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) through protection partners in the areas of our operation. We will use our existing projects across the proposed locations to boost up the 2022-HRP project activities.7- Screening for acute malnutrition: To ensure the integration of the services, all the health workers (outreach teams and health workers in health facilities) will be train on identification of acute malnutrition in both children under-5 and pregnant and lactating women/PLWs using MUAC and link the malnourished one to our nutrition programming. 8- Identification of PLWDs (People Living with Disabilities) and mentally ill persons among the community and refer them to specialized centres after giving basic health care at our health facilities. Action Against Hunger is capable to provide humanitarian response to emerging crisis that are anticipated in 2022. We will use our presence in 3 states to contribute to the strategic plan of health cluster, WHO and MOH in the year 2022. These activities will tailor to achieve the health cluster strategic objectives. Reporting tools will be ERWARS weekly report, IDSR, monthly HMIS2 and 5Ws to Health Cluster, WHO, CHD and MoH. All these forms will be availed to the field staff in all locations and make sure all indicators are captured and any outbreak investigation will be recorded and reported during the implementation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our integrated program (Health, Nutrition, WaSH and FSL programs. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specifically asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. In each of our project sites, we avail five FCM modalities as “minimum standards”: 1. Monthly staff meetings with first-line implementers, 2. Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries, 3. Individual interview form, 4. Comment Box at Field Office Gate and nutrition facilities, 5. Anonymous Hotline Number. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. We have standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites and on all project structures (e.g. equipment used by beneficiaries). IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers during the monthly meetings. Specific responses will also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the national level. Urgent issues will be addressed immediately by the Head of Base and Program Managers through appropriate channels.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Action Against Hunger implements a comprehensive, gender-sensitive feedback mechanism. This feedback mechanism uses a range of modalities to solicit feedback from frontline program implementers (including volunteers and field staff), local communities in which programs are operating, and direct program beneficiaries. While the feedback mechanism is firmly institutionalized, the organization continues to examine how to best track and respond to the feedback received, and how to integrate this process into its M&E strategy.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Action Against Hunger trains all of its staff and volunteers on the organization’s PSEA Policy. During our mobilization meetings, we shares all information about PSEA from the organization’s PSEA Policy. During project implementation, the organization regularly shares PSEA information with beneficiaries as reminder/refresher to emphasize the organization’s Zero Tolerance for PSEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Action Against Hunger considers the feedback from beneficiaries as an instrument to improve the organization’s acceptance, and improvement of our interventions in the community. Hence, all feedback from beneficiaries are analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project as we place emphasis on the organization being a learning entity.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Given the context of South Sudan, the limited functional health facilities and long distance to access health services, increase the risk of beneficiaries to insecurity while accessing humanitarian services due to several reasons like inter-communal violence, cattle raiding, revenge killing etc. Action Against Hunger teams on ground have built strong relationship with local community leaders and authorities to ensure safety and security of our operations, as well as, beneficiaries. The Field Coordinators at each of our bases are the security focal points and they are in close collaboration with local authorities and other relevant stakeholders to exchange information.COVID-19 has affected the entire population in South Sudan. As of 10 March 2022, there have been 17,044 confirmed cases of COVID-19 with 138 deaths, reported to WHO. Action Against Hunger is ensuring enough PPE supplies to all our staff, awareness sessions on prevention of COVID-19 at community level on regular basis and supporting the government on vaccine enrolment campaigns. All our program activities at field level are implemented in line with the revised COVID-19 guidelines shared by MOH.The flooding in South Sudan has affected 854,000 people and displaced over 365,000, adding to the already high levels of internal displacement. Women and children are feeling the greatest impact of widespread flooding, sub-national violence, fragile health systems and food insecurity, exposing them to protection risks, malnutrition and life-threatening diseases. Action Against Hunger mitigation measures involve the management and control of flood water movement, such as redirecting flood run-off through the use of floodwalls and flood gates (Dykes), rather than trying to prevent floods altogether. In far-off areas where beneficiaries are not able to access services at our health facilities, our teams provide outreach services/ICCM in the community using local canoes.To prevent instigation of SEA and other forms of GBV by leading gender-targeted interventions, Action Against Hunger will aim to sensitize all gender and age groups on project activities before their implementation. All our project activities are designed to safely challenge harmful gender dynamics in a context-appropriate manner. Results of gender analyses will directly inform project design and implementation strategy. All staff implementing GBV referral pathway awareness, Men Group Discussion facilitation, Psychological First Aid, and any other sensitive discussion with target community members will be instructed on our mandatory policy for maintaining the identity of project participants strictly confidential. Data related to GBV and other sensitive topics will be anonymized in organizational databases to preserve the privacy of survivors. Moreover, survivors or reporters will not be solicited for further participation/discussion. Any GBV case concerning staff or beneficiaries which proceed to investigation will only involve the Country Program Ethics Committee, consisting of the Country Director, Head of Logistics and Head of Operations. All information will be confidential with access restricted to those personnel. We will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements. A diverse set of FCM modalities ensures that differing gender, age and ability groups have access.
