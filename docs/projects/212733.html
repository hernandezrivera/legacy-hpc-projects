Project Summary

According to HNO 2024, There has been a 4% increase in PiN, compared to the previous year. This emphasizes the urgent need for emergency education services to support up to 7.2 million school aged children and teachers, including CWDs.
The PiN comprises 6.9 million children 5 – 17 years (46% girls, 54% boys) and includes 230,000 education personnel, with (48% female, 52%). 10% are CwD.
Also, there are more than 2.45 million Out of School Children OoSC (46% girls, 54% boys) and over a million who are at risk of dropping out.
Also, learning environments are unconducive due to overcrowding, lack of school furniture/school supplies, as schools lack heating, cooling, and lighting to operate in multiple shifts.
Regarding the staff, there is a decrease in the availability of qualified teachers, affecting the access and retention of children in educational services.
Rapid assessment was conducted in March 2024 by MASRRAT’s team in cooperation with schools' managers and teachers. Data collection involved questionnaires and KIIs across 12 villages.
The primary aim was to evaluate the pressing needs of students, schools, and educators within the specified communities. We conducted 21 KIIs involving school administrators, teachers, community organization members, concentrating on both educational and infrastructural requirements.
Most contributors highlighted three urgent educational needs in the targeted communities at present, notably resources for teacher salaries, school maintenance (such as painting and repairing windows and doors), as well as the provision of teaching materials and school equipment.
Key findings include:
• A significant majority of targeted schools face shortages in teaching and learning materials, including textbooks, hiteboards, and stationery.
• Most targeted schools lack facilities for students with special needs, while approximately 19% reported the presence of specialized bathrooms and pathways in four schools.
• Most respondents revealed that students in targeted schools do not receive psychological support sessions, although a small percentage reported some participation, emphasizing the positive impact of such sessions on children's behavior and mental well-being.
• The majority of respondents observed a lack of incentives or rewards for teachers, with a significant portion indicating insufficient training opportunities for educational staff.
The primary objective of this project is to support and facilitate access to formal education by supporting schools in Aleppo and Idlib governorates, specifically in the administrative areas under the Aleppo Directorate of Education, including Atarib and its countryside, Daret Azza and its countryside, and Termanin.
The project targets 40 schools in the mentioned area for a duration of 6 months. These schools comprise educational and administrative staff totaling 791 teachers (373 males, 418 females) and target students aged between 6 and 17, numbering 13,010 students (6,643 females, 6,376 males), including 50 individuals with disabilities. The schools have 413 classrooms and an administrative room, and this project will directly support 13,801 beneficiaries.
The intervention includes:
A. Repair and maintenance of classrooms.
B. Providing schools with educational materials and furniture.
C. Providing incentives and scholarships and teaching materials and resources For 791 teachers and staff, amounting to $150 per month for 6 consecutive months.
D. Providing awareness sessions for the community and camps for Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns and provide children with school-based psychosocial support programs.
E. Provide children with winter support including clothes for children and winter package for schools.
These interventions are directly related to the objectives of the education sector within the humanitarian aid plan, aiming to increase access to formal education for adults and girls of all ages (6-17 years) who are required to pursue formal education.
