Project Summary

Young Prodigy Humanitarian Organization (YPHO) plan to provide humanitarian intervention to people who's standard of living have been badly disrupted by insurgency in Borno and Yobe State through the following Objectives;
1. Enhance resilience and improved ability of communities and local partners to cope with displacement and ensure local ownership and self-governance through inclusive participation, gender mainstreaming and engagement of displaced persons.
2. To expand CCCM activities to new and unreached populations in Kaga LGA, Borno State and Fune LGA, Yobe State.
3. Ensure equal access and needs based assistance to improve quality of lives and livelihood services for affected populations, displaced persons and returnees.
4. Improved engagement with host communities and returnees (Reintegration) and be responsible to vulnerable populations with priorities for addressing protection gaps.

1 - Needs

1.0 GENERAL BACKGROUND MAINOK is a town in KAGA Local government, located along Maiduguri-Damaturu Road in Borno State, which consist of host community and large number of IDPs who were displaced from Magumeri and Konduga LGA currently living in informal settlements. It is located at 58 km from the city of Maiduguri. Sequel to the alert raised by the Chairman Kaga Local Government Area, for the need to support existing and newly displaced IDPs (over 2000 individuals displaced in last four to five months) in the town, Young Prodigy Humanitarian Organization (YPHO) had to mobilized to conduct the rapid need assessment. The purpose of this assessment is to observe the overall situation of the town and advocate for service provision especially as regards to CCCM and Shelter/NFI. 2.0 PURPOSE OF MISSIONThe overall purpose of this Mission/Assessment is to understand the nature of the general needs, varying degrees of damages experienced by different houses, local construction practices, availability of WASH and other facilities, access to basic hygiene kits & local hygiene practices, market condition, access to construction materials so that assistance can be targeted and tailored. The specific objective of the study were to understand: • The nature of shelter needs; • The types of accommodation/housing used traditionally in the town; • The varying level of damage sustained by the houses of the affected population; • The condition of the markets and access to construction materials; • Availability of skilled labor; • Availability of WASH, Health, Education and other facilities; • Access to basic hygiene kits; • Local hygiene practice;• Access to road network;• Access to CCCM and• Status of New arrivals. 3.0 COVERAGE The assessment covered the through (FGD and KII) with the persons of concern in the camps. 4.0 METHODOLOGYThe methodology used was based on qualitative data collection method where a Focus Group Discussion (FGD) with community/market leaders and stakeholders was conducted with gender consideration taking into account to respect to culture, belief and norms, and KII (key informant interview) and physical structure/facilities observation within the town. Five (5) KIIs were administered to stakeholders and two (2) FGDs (men 1, women 1) comprises of 6-8 participants, involving separate groups for women and men of the displaced population. The result of the findings from FGD, KII and Direct observation were triangulated, harmonised and consolidated into one report. 5.0 FINDINGS 5.1 Humanitarian Assistance Received:During the cause of the Assessment, some of the respondents reported having never received shelter, NFI, food or Cash interventions either from the government or from INGOs after the settlement. The only assistance received are as follows (based on FGDs & key informants): • GFD by NEMA • IOM 5.2 Current Security Situation:The CJTF and the Acting C.O of the Nigerian Army Major Ade explained the current security situation, threat, and residents in person said it is a calm situation so far. Although, there are AOGs along the road to the town, which is, like 20km.5.3 Shelter and NFI: The gap in shelter intervention is huge in the community according to the field observations, almost all the 2000 new arrivals from Magumeri and Konduga are either living in damaged makeshift shelters or in an open space. The new arrivals as well as the existing IDPS equally lack NFIs for their day-to-day living chores. 5.4 Market Condition and Availability of Labor: There is a nearby village market in the community where some basic needs can be purchase. When asked about the availability of skilled laborers, the participants reported that many skilled laborer’s (carpenters & masons) are available with their Association & leaders. However, the assessment team were not able to meet with associations leaders during the trip due to the limited time we had on ground.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

1. Information ManagementAs a core component of the work of CCCM, a strong evidence base and excellent information management systems underpin work at both site and state level to ensure that the cluster response is efficient and appropriate for the needs of the population. The cluster will enrich information management at site/ host community level by enhancing or implementing: i. Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM): IDP populations utilize CFMs in POC and displacement sites, which will guide service providers in integrating systems that ensure effective and timely response. ii. Population Registration: New arrivals into POC sites/host communities (returnees) will be registered with priority activities aligning with demographic tracking systems and improving the quality of data collected. This information will be also be utilized to advocate to local authorities and the humanitarian community on needs and population flows in the sites. iii. Site Profiling: Will be regularly updated and information will be used to advocate to donors, the wider humanitarian community and allow for identification of gaps in a coordinated and rationalized manner. iv. Intentions/Perceptions Surveys: CCCM Cluster through its partners and in coordination with other clusters / stakeholders to ensure intentions of IDPs are established to guide programming at POC sites, collective centers and spontaneous settlements, as well as inform dialogue on protection led returns and solutions. v. Movement Trend Tracking/Flow Monitoring: Will be maintained in sites and where possible, tied in with camp management registration systems. vi. Contingency Planning: The cluster in consultation with its service providers and NEMA/SEMA will ensure quarterly update of the Joint Contingency plans for the PoC sites. CCCM partners including Integrating Civil Society Organizations will map out sites in Kaga LGA where populations are likely to seek refuge in the event of new or reoccurring conflict. Focal points for each site will be identified, and an analysis of available services and provision of life saving assistance measured. B. Service MonitoringThe monitoring and quality assessment of services, using Camp Management Agency monitoring processes in PoC sites and community based monitoring mechanisms in collective centres /other camp like settings, will identify needs and gaps, thereby ensuring clear communications on service provision between all stakeholders. i. Indicator monitoring: In working toward meeting internationally recognized standards such as SPHERE, there will be ongoing monitoring based on HRP and specific CCCM cluster indicators for camps and camp-like settings. ii. House to House Service Monitoring: POC site management agencies will establish an address system with strict confidentiality protocols to support service monitoring at the household level. iii. Rationalization of service providers: The cluster will work with both the CCCM and Protection sector and other actors to ensure rationalization of partners delivering services inside the POC and displacement sites to ensure cost effective and efficient humanitarian service delivery. A key mechanism of this will be regular service monitoring with consequential strategic mapping and advocacy to relevant stakeholders.
