Project Summary

Needs;According to Child protection cluster 2020,up to 66 per cent of the population in need in Somalia are children who continue to be exposed to threats of recruitment, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation and violence. Hence, 1.88 million children including 10% of children with disabilities need immediate child protection services.Boys and girls of all ages in Somalia continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights including family separation, sexual and physical violence, killing and maiming, psychosocial distress, worst forms of child labor, widespread recruitment and use of children, forced and protracted displacements, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/or discriminatory practices and exploitation. Despite important measures taken by the Government of Somalia to reduce recruitment of children, Somalia has the highest total number of grave violations in the world. From January to June 2020, the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) verified 1,774 grave violations affecting 1,613 children (303 girls) of which 34% (545,) and 30 girls were cases of recruitment/use, 34% (555) cases of abduction. The protracted conflict has left most girls and boys exposed to profound mental health and psychosocial risks and some of the children end up experiencing profound distress. According to a perception survey conduct by the Child Protection Area of Responsibility in April/May 2020; 71% of child protection partners reported that were children experiencing psychological distress. Child protection partners have also witnessed increased fear and anxiety amongst children due to disruption of their daily routines. Whilst significant progress has been made in delivering stabilizing and generalized psychosocial support to significant numbers of children in need, the need to scale up more structured psychosocial support for those most severely affected is urgently required.
The objective of the project is to Reduce loss of life for 3.1 million of the most severely vulnerable people, including 1 million children under 5, by decreasing the prevalence of hunger, acute malnutrition, public health threats and outbreaks, and abuse and violence by the end of 2021.
The project will provide 2000 children including those living with disabilities with psychosocial support,case management and referrals.The project will provide 2000 caregivers with psychosocial support ,parenting messages, positive parenting and other kind support leading to improved relationships with the children under their care. WOCCA will also help 500 Children formerly associated with armed forces and groups and those at risk with inclusive community-based reintegration support. WOCCA,s Child protection caseworkers will Trace and reunify 1000 unaccompanied and separated children with their families.The CP caseworkers will also train 3500 duty bearers and actors on child protection and children rights.1000 children living with disability who have access to community-based prevention and response MHPSS are provided with child protection services,case management and psychosocial support.

Mitigation Measures

-Risk that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA,WOCCA will mitigate this risk by providing clear guidance from HQ to field offices on engaging communities and the affected population to raise awareness on PSEA. WOCCA will also have in place PSEA and Child Safeguarding focal points.-Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) as mitigation measure to this risk WOCCA will focus on strengthening of gender inclusive community-based protection mechanisms and robust information campaigns to ensure impartial and timely access to protection services, also in situations of displacement -Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities as mitigation measure WOCCA will conduct training on gender equality in the targeted communities focusing on opinion leaders, Imams, women and girls, Recruit women in decision making roles, conduct community action research /assessment to understand causes of marginalization and community solutions to address them. All beneficiary lists include a category of marginalized groups, people with disabilities, the elderly, women and girls. Use focused radio programmes and community meetings to mobilise women and girls to take on leadership roles and participate in activities affecting their lives.-Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms as mitigation measure to this risk WOCCA with the help of community support workers will train the community members on how to use and access the complaint and feedback mechanisms so as to get feedback from the beneficiaries.
