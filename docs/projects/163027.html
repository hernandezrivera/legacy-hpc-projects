Project Summary

The economic crisis in Zimbabwe is affecting household economies, with disproportionate impacts on the poorest and most vulnerable, who resort to harmful coping mechanisms. This crisis combined with climate shocks (extreme rainfall and prolonged drought) and epidemics (cholera and endemic typhoid) continues to deepen the humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe. Tropical Cyclone Idai affected the population in the Provinces of Manicaland, Masvingo and Mashonaland East, who continue to require humanitarian assistance, mainly the internally displaced persons in temporary camps or living with host families. Continuing drought conditions is impacting nearly 5.5 million people (including ~2.6 million children) in rural Zimbabwe who are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance and protection especially during the critical period January to March 2020 as food and water security are threatened. A total of 2.2 million people in urban areas are cereal insecure, and about 1.5 million households are living below the Food Poverty Line and are unable to meet their basic needs.
In rural areas, of the 55,593 water sources tracked by the rural water information management system (RWIMS), only 30% are with water, functional and protected. According to ZIMVAC 2019 report, sixteen percent (16%) of households have to travel more than 1km to fetch water from the nearest primary water source and 32% over 500m. This means access to safe water and practice of good sanitation and hygiene is compromised increasing the risk of diarrheal diseases
Women and girls face a heightened risk of protection concerns and loss of education due to socio-economic roles, including supporting vulnerable family members and migrating to cities. In Zimbabwe, it is the role of the female members to ensure that the family is fed and that there is sufficient water for the family. In times of scarcity, women and girls travel long distances to fetch water and may be predisposed to dangers of wild animals or rape. Nationally about 83.4% of women bear the responsibility of fetching water for the household. Furthermore, time taken to fetch water may compromise the children’s participation in school.
In urban areas electricity and chemical supply challenges have led to a significant decrease in piped water supply leading populations to higher reliance on unsafe sources and increased vulnerability for girls and women. Water production for Harare had reduced by half in September 2019, compared to the same period last year. This has increased reliance on alternative water sources, many of which are unprotected and pose a risk to diarrheal diseases. Diarrheal diseases have been increasing in Harare (September - November) with significant concerns in cholera hotspot suburbs. Thus, the risk of water-borne disease outbreaks is high.
Based on the above triggers, UNICEF will carry out the following interventions to achieve the three Sector Objectives:
• Rehabilitation/upgrading/ construction of strategic water points with a focus of maximizing use of perennial water sources to cover needs of institutions (schools, health centres) and vulnerable communities
• Improving safe hygiene and sanitation practices, with a focus on participatory health and hygiene education in institutions (health facilities, schools) and communities with high malnutrition rates. This will include training of Environmental Health Technicians (EHTs), Village Health Workers (VHWs) and Community Health Workers (CHWs) supporting community-based counselling of caregivers on infant and young child feeding practices on critical lifesaving WASH messages / hygiene practices. May also entail provision of temporary latrines/ mobile toilets where appropriate
• Distribution of WASH hygiene kits targeting care givers of severe and moderate acute malnourished children
• Strengthening coordination and surveillance mechanisms at national and sub-national levels

1 - Activities

UNICEF will implement the following project activities with partners and relevant government line ministries to ensure ownership and sustainability;• Strengthening effective coordination and surveillance mechanisms at National and sub-national level, with links to other cluster coordination arrangements on critical inter-sectoral issues• Restoring access to sufficient water of appropriate quality and quantity to fulfil the basic needs of children and women. Based on the information from the Rural WASH Information Management System (RWIMS) and other sectoral databases, followed by a confirmation on the actual site conditions at time of implementation, the project will restore the functionality of existing water sources by undertaking critical repairs and / or rehabilitation of the deteriorated infrastructure and or construction of new water sources. When possible, focus will be given to the rehabilitation of piped water schemes to maximize the impact of the project in terms of people benefited and livelihood such as livestock, however the choice of technology can be switched over to cater for ground realities with an aim to maintain or increase the number of beneficiaries. This will also include distribution of key items for household water treatment and storage, including storage containers, water treatment products, together with key information on their use• Improving awareness among children and women on safe hygiene, proper solid waste management and sanitation practices, with a focus on community engagement, participatory health and hygiene education (PHHE) and water conservation.• Providing access to critical WASH-related hygiene kits, with a focus on the most vulnerable families in the targeted areas, including pre-positioning of emergency WASH supplies in critical provinces. • Strengthening water quality monitoring at the local level during the peak-period so that appropriate action can be taken early before an outbreak. • To support the Health system to reduce the risk of hospital-associated infections and enhance infection, prevention and control (IPC) WASH related support will be given to health care facilities.• Mainstreaming Accountability to Affected Populations

2 - Indicators

The following indicators will be monitored through the response;• Number of people reached with safe water• Number of people reached with key sanitation and hygiene messages• Number of households reached with WASH hygiene kits
