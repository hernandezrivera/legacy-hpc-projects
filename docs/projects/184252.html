Project Summary

The proposed project aims at providing integrated Nutrition treatment and prevention services for both host communities and internally displaced under five boys, girls and caregivers in 15 districts in 6 regions in south central somalia (Banadir region: Kahda, Waaberi, Abdiaziz, Hodan, Wartanabada and Deynile districts, Bakool region: Rabdhure, Elberde, Wajid, and Hudur districts, LowerShabelle region: Afgoye and Barawe, Nugaal region: Garowe, Bay region: Baidoa district and Gedo region: Elwak district) for a period of 12 months. The project will contribute to reduction of nutrition related morbidity and mortality rates through Scale up of nutrition program for treatment of acute malnutrition cases through admission to OTP, SC and TSFP programmes, as well as establishing referral linkages with other nutrition actors in the operational areas. Action Against Hunger will continue strengthening Family MUAC approach to promote early identification of under five children with malnutrition as well coupling this initiative with community screening by community health workers in the target locations. Besides Nutrition treatment services the project will offer MIYCN promotion through group and individual counselling sessions, mother to mother and father to father support groups to sensitize pregnant and lactating mothers on optimal MIYCN practices. Moreover, Action Against Hunger will provide integrated health, hygiene and nutrition promotion at both community and facility level and will work with community health workers to support community nutrition activities such as routine and continuous screening, active case finding, health hygiene and Nutrition promotion and follow up of program beneficiaries. Action Against Hunger is planning to scale up the CMAM surge approach to respond to the persistently high GAM rates in Somalia through lessons learnt from the current CMAM surge pilot being conducted in Khada district. COVID-19 preventive measures will be adhered during implementation. Nutrition surveys will be conducted to assess the malnutrition rates, coverage and barriers to access in the supported facilities. Action Against Hunger will further ensure supported health facilities are disabled friendly to promote inclusivity of the PLWD. Reporting to the cluster ONA, DHIS2 and other platforms will be done as required to monitor and update on nutrition interventions across supported facilities/sites. Overall, the project envisages to reach 77,366 individuals (30,665 B, 31,846 G and 14855 PLWs) including 1,161 people living with disabilities for a period of 12 months. The project will integrate nutrition services in 20 health centers, 2 referral health centers and 3 hospital across the 6 regions. To expand coverage, the project will ensure service provision for people living in hard to reach areas through deployment of 10 mobile teams to provide nutrition sensitive program.

1. - Projects activities

The treatment of acute malnutrition will follow the Somalia national guidelines for Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM). Children with MUAC <11.5cm, weight for height <-3 z-scores and/or bilateral pitting oedema will be treated through outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP) and monitored through weekly follow up visits (and household visits, if necessary). Any cases of SAM with complications, such as lack of appetite or medical problems in addition to the malnutrition, will be admitted/ referred to inpatient treatment services. Feeding needs for caretakers of SAM with medical complications admitted in the SC will be met through this project to enhance optimum care. Caretakers of SC admitted children will also benefit from IYCF sessions whilst in the SC. To ensure continuum of care, children who have recovered from SAM will be referred to TSFP services. Additionally, <5 children and PLWs with MAM will be linked to the TSFP and BSFP programs in the target locations. To put caregivers at the center of tackling malnutrition, Action Against Hunger will strengthen the Family MUAC approach in the target locations to ensure early detection at household level. Furthermore, to promote good nutrition practices Action Against Hunger will link MIYCN promotion at nutrition sites to the community through establishment of Mother-to-Mother Support Groups (MtMSGs) and father to father support groups (FtFSGs) in the project target locations. Participatory cooking demonstrations will be conducted on a quarterly basis within established MtMSGs to educate mothers on optimal complementary feeding practices. In view of COVID-19, continuous sensitization and capacity building targeting CHWs, HWs and beneficiaries at community and facility level on appropriate IPC practices will be maintained. Nutrition coverage assessment will be planned to be conducted at the end of the project in Bakool and Banadir regions. The findings from the coverage assessment will be used to evaluate coverage of the Programme as well as monitor programme performance, identify boosters and barriers to programme access with a view to formulating recommendations to improve programme quality and impact. An effective CMAM programme will tend to achieve good coverage and a programme with good coverage is expected to be an effective programme. Comparison will also be made with coverage estimates for SQUEAC assessment conducted in 2021. The coverage findings will be shared with the nutrition cluster and other partners implementing activities in the target location. Refresher training on MIYCN and IMAM will be conducted for HCWs and CHWs supported by the action. To improve quality of services conducted in the nutrition sites, follow up will be done through on job training sessions within health facilities and nutrition centers Action Against Hunger technical staff. A joint supportive supervision will also be organized on a quarterly basis whereby Ministry of health at federal and state levels will be involved. This will form a basis of addressing any challenges identified in the implementation sites. Additionally, Action Against Hunger will engage communities through community sensitizations held at community level by community volunteers. Through initial community awareness sessions, Action Against Hunger will encourage both men and women to attend the sessions and explain the importance of the program including the impact of involving both women and men in treatment as well as mainstreaming gender in all target locations. Additionally, the program will improve the Micronutrient status of children 6-59 months and women of reproductive age, through continuous supplementation and support MOH in the bi-annual micronutrient and deworming supplementations.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: ACF have strong global policies in place to prevent Fraud, Conflict of Interest, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), along with a whistleblowing policy to receive serious complaints. ACF already maintains an active Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project areas, including contact numbers. Each complaint or request for assistance is responded to in a timely manner, and serious complaints are raised to ACF regional office to ensure accountable management. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc: ACF will provide medical care within 72 hours for the SGBV cases reported. ACF will refer survivors who need additional support such psychosocial care to the GBV partners. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: ACF will engage District local authorities, Health facility committees and Village structures including opinion leaders at the kick planning stage and during implementation to allow them participate in project implementation decisions.
