Project Summary

IOM’s Protection Project for the 2025 JRP is designed to address the needs of the Rohingya community and the surrounding host community, with particular attention to vulnerable groups, including older persons, widows, single-headed households, persons with disabilities, and individuals with diverse sexual orientations, gender identities, and expressions. By purposeful engagement of authorities and partners, IOM aims to enhance the effectiveness of our activities and support local systems, fostering an approach that upholds a commitment to do no harm, assists based on assessed needs, and prioritizes respect for human rights, safety, and dignity.
IOM will continue to work, in coordination with the Protection Sector and in-line with the Sector’s rationalization efforts, on improving protection, safety and security and enhanced access to justice. This includes IOM’s commitment to continue serving as the Protection Focal Point in seven camps (9, 18, 19, 20, 20 ext, 24, and 25), where we directly implementing protection activities. IOM will strengthen community-based protection through community engagement, awareness raising, sensitization, case management, and specialized Protection services for individuals in need. IOM’s Protection activities include a disaster risk reduction component; we work with sectors, community protection networks, and authorities to identify Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs) needing disaster preparation. IOM verifies needs, coordinates services, supports harm mitigation, and leads relevant the Protection Emergency Response Units (PERU).
IOM actively contributing to the Peace and Security Campaign and will serve as one of the co-leads for the Rule of Law Working Group (ROL WG). These initiatives respond to the increase in violent incidents and the pervasive environment of fear in the camps. The Peace and Security Campaign and ROL WG aim to support peace and security through targeted, coordinated communications, assistance to victims, advocacy against violence, and fostering dialogue to rebuild trust, enhance the rule of law, and restore safety. Central to these efforts include engagement with security actors to advocate for more effective, human rights-aligned responses and to support capacity-building for victim-centered, protection sensitive approaches. Complementary protection monitoring and research will further inform these actions, enabling evidence-based strategies to respond to emerging risks and improve safety and dignity for affected communities.
IOM co-chairs the Sector’s Anti-Trafficking Working Group (ATWG) and implements
counter-trafficking activities, including prevention, protection, capacity building and partnership in 17 camps as well as 10 host community unions within Cox’s Bazar district. These activities are intended to reduce the trafficking incidents and risks in the camps and host communities through comprehensive activities that follow the governmental National Plan of Action. The Counter Trafficking activities will foster collaboration and increased capacity among stakeholders who respond to human trafficking cases. The integrated approach to Protection strives to have sustainable impact and improve peaceful coexistence between various communities. IOM’s protection mainstreaming activities promote a holistic lens, incorporating the age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population in Cox’s Bazar into programming through capacity building, training, as well as supporting appropriate activity monitoring and modification.
IOM is an active member of the SAGs for the Protection Sector and GBV and CP Sub-Sectors. IOM is a technical member of the Age and Disability Technical Working Group and participates in the Gender Diverse Population Working Group.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The year 2025 marks eight years of the Rohingya refugee response. The consequences of prolonged deprivation have been multifaceted and dire. The Rohingya refugee camps face significant protection challenges, exacerbated by limited self-reliance opportunities. The security situation within the camps is deteriorating, with the Protection Sector raising concerns in 2024 about different organized groups fighting for influence and recruiting men and boys for combat in Myanmar. This raises concerns about criminal activities such as extortion, physical abuse, and abductions. According to the Inter Sector Needs Assessment (ISNA) of October 2024, 48% of respondents reported feeling scared due to increased gunshots and confrontations between organized groups, and 38% of households expressed concern about incidents of abduction and kidnapping. The Protection Sector Joint Protection Monitoring Report documented 157 abduction cases in Q3 2024, typically involving ransoms. During national protests in July and August, the Sector reported that a reduced presence of the Armed Police Battalion (APBn) led to increased impunity for organized groups, resulting in more violent incidents. Although APBn presence has returned to normal levels, the security situation remains volatile, with ongoing impacts from that period. The lack of economic and educational opportunities, coupled with reduced humanitarian assistance, has heightened the vulnerability of the Rohingya, particularly youth, women, and persons with disabilities. This has led to harmful coping strategies, creating fertile ground for trafficking, exploitation, and abuse. Most trafficking victims identified by IOM cited the lack of cash-for-work and livelihood opportunities as reasons for leaving the camps. IOM’s case data for Q3 shows that 46% of the victims were Rohingya women, girls, and boys, while 54% were Bangladeshi men, women, and children, trafficked mainly for forced labor. Addressing human trafficking requires self-reliance initiatives through skills development, income generation for trafficked victims, community awareness sessions, and capacity-building training for stakeholders. Gender-diverse individuals in the camps face exclusion, discrimination, and violence due to entrenched social norms and laws. They frequently experience gender-based violence (GBV), including physical, sexual, psychosocial, and economic abuse. Stigma and discrimination hinder their participation in daily activities and access to healthcare, increasing mental health risks like anxiety and depression. The security situation, worsened by organized groups recruiting members, exacerbates these issues, indicating heightened protection risks from criminal activities such as extortion, rape, and physical abuse. As displacement persists, the loss of income and limited opportunities have led to rising frustration and the adoption of negative coping mechanisms, resulting in an alarming increase in protection risks. The situation is exacerbated by the camps' acute vulnerability to climate shocks; cyclical floods and landslides have left a trail of devastation and dread. Given the protracted nature of the Rohingya refugee crisis, maintaining protection as a central focus in the overall response remains imperative.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Incorporating a protection lens is fundamental to IOM’s operations, ensuring that all programs are designed and implemented with careful attention to protection risks and potential violations. IOM follows four core protection mainstreaming principles that are crucial in crisis and post-crisis responses. In Cox’s Bazar, IOM prioritizes protection as a key component of its humanitarian response, delivering integrated activities that safeguard the rights of crisis-affected individuals. The focus is on empowering communities, building resilience, ensuring safety, and preserving dignity. Protection is approached as a shared responsibility, with efforts aimed at reducing risks and enhancing the safety, resilience, and participation of all affected individuals. Specialized services, including general protection, counter-trafficking, child protection, and GBV support, are delivered by integrated protection specialists.IOM conducts ongoing protection monitoring, community consultations, safety audits, and risk mapping to address the needs of vulnerable groups, including people with disabilities and those with diverse SOGIESC. As part of its protection mainstreaming approach, IOM actively participates in technical working groups related to age, disability, and gender diversity, ensuring that everyone receives the support they need. The 'Do No Harm' principle remains central to IOM’s community engagement, guiding all activities.Capacity-building sessions for community volunteers, advocates, and support groups—within and beyond protection sectors—improve access to information and aid decision-making. Collaboration with non-protection sectors ensures that protection mainstreaming is integrated across all initiatives, such as targeted Cash for Work programs for vulnerable families, accessible shelters, and safe spaces for gender-diverse individuals in livelihood programs. IOM remains committed to safeguarding the well-being and rights of those affected by the crisis.

2.3 - Age & Disability

IOM Protection programming addresses diverse age groups, needs, and abilities by applying an AGD approach in case management, protection monitoring, awareness-raising, and research activities. Annually, IOM conducts Extremely Vulnerable Individual (EVI) assessments to develop data on individuals in vulnerable categories, including older persons and persons with disabilities, considering their limited social support. This informs potential response plans during emergencies.Staff are trained to use the Washington Group Questionnaire (short set) to identify persons with disabilities. IOM aligns its work with the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, advocating for the inclusion of persons with disabilities. As a technical partner in the Age and Disability Technical Working Group (ADTWG), IOM promotes age and disability inclusion through regular engagement and capacity-building for staff and community volunteers, addressing barriers to participation for persons with disabilities.IOM facilities, including protection spaces, are progressively designed to be disability-friendly through accessibility audits. Protection Focal Points (PFPs) support the Disability Inclusion Support Committee (DISC) and facilitate Community Protection Committee meetings to ensure meaningful participation of persons with disabilities. Collaboration with the Gender Diverse Population Working Group ensures inclusivity for individuals with diverse SOGIESC, while planned engagement with the Youth Working Group aims to enhance youth participation.Although comprehensive disability prevalence data is unavailable, IOM uses REACH’s 2021 estimate of 12% and UNHCR’s 3% prevalence as benchmarks for targeting beneficiaries. IOM remains committed to inclusive, age-sensitive programming, advocating for the rights and well-being of all individuals in its response.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Information-sharing on humanitarian aid and programs, is ensured through the design, translation, testing, and dissemination of messages for awareness-raising and sensitization—empowering affected populations to make informed decisions. Multimedia resources and IEC materials are contextualized for understanding and accessibility. Two-way communication is maintained through regular, participatory community engagement and routine activities that protect the right to information and promote meaningful decision-making inclusive of gender, diversity, and vulnerable, marginalized, and at-risk groups. IOM’s Protection program prioritizes its community-based and participatory approaches, which aim to be responsive to a diversity of needs and ensure inclusive participationA standardized, multi-sectoral CFM is equally available across all IOM camps for refugees to ask questions and request assistance while safeguarding data protection and privacy and complementing referral pathways for sensitive complaints. This activates appropriate processes and procedures for internal and external referral and reply in a timely manner to foster trust, while also giving communities the opportunity to share satisfaction and resolution. CFM reports, disaggregated to capture and respond accordingly, are published for transparency and to support the response to strengthen interventions. IOM actively collaborates with peers and participates in the AAP TWG to support technical principles and inter-agency responsibilities. Aid effectiveness is prioritized through programming that is rights-based, people-centered, and community-based and led to uphold dignity and respect. Consultative evidence-generation allows for the tracking of rumors and identification of timely issues of importance to affected persons to raise voices and contribute to advocacy with all stakeholders.A dedicated CwC team supports all IOM units, including Protection, for a harmonized AAP strategy.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM has in place a Zero Tolerance Policy toward sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) which binds staff members, implementing partners, third party contractors, vendors and service providers to standards of behavior at all times. PSEA of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and is grounds for disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution. IOM will continue to roll out PSEA training packages and offer Training of Trainers sessions for PSEA Champions in the coming year to ensure that IOM staff, implementing partner staff, and volunteers adhere to the PSEA guiding principles and mandatory reporting systems.In the initial phase of the project implementation, SEA project related risks will be screened, and mitigation measures identified to enhance safer programming for the affected population. IEC materials will be developed to raise awareness among affected populations about their rights to free assistance, the availability of different reporting mechanisms as well as available referral and assistance services. The PSEA officer, in collaboration with the program team, will conduct regular consultations and focus group discussions with beneficiaries to enhance their awareness of PSEA and address any concerns they may have. IOM has a robust online reporting mechanism which is WEAREALLIN.IOM.INT, allowing anyone to report SEA incidents. Recognizing the communities we work with might not be able to access and use the WAAI platform, IOM has established complaint and feedback mechanisms in place to receive complaints including concerns related to SEA from beneficiaries and community members. IOM ensures follow-up of SEA complaints through its specialized investigation team and ensures access to protection and assistance of potential survivors in coordination with its MPHSS-Protection team and the PSEA network, and in line with the existing GBV and Child Protection referral pathways.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

IOM delivers its Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Climate Change Adaptation (CCA) activities in line with the UN Plan of Action on DRR for Resilience, reinforcing local and national initiatives to prevent and prepare for disasters and related population movements, providing emergency assistance and protection where displacement cannot be avoided, and fostering post-disaster recovery solutions and resilience-building. IOM works in support of governments and communities to: i) Enhance disaster preparedness measures in vulnerable locations, early warning systems, physical risk mitigation, community-based disaster risk reduction capacities and related activities and ii) Integrate disaster risk reduction into recovery and reconstruction programming, related to shelter and housing support following disasters, support in the construction of disaster-resilient infrastructure and livelihoods, and approaches to increase resilience and reduce the likelihood of future disasters.IOM prioritizes environmentally friendly approaches, reducing paper and plastic waste, and minimizing usage in camps. IOM’s Protection facilities use solar panels. This environmentally friendly approach is replicated wherever appropriate to ensure long-term environmental responsibility.IOM identifies EVIs in need of additional support because of disaster-related risks and conduct outreach to develop and reaffirm individual and household preparedness and response plans, prior to disasters. After a disaster, IOM assists beneficiaries with re-establishing meaningful access to humanitarian services, monitor protection trends, including any changes to the human trafficking context, for new or emerging threats and risks, and apply mitigation measures. IOM's awareness module tailors messaging to seasonal and camp contexts, such as cyclone risks during monsoons or fire hazards in winter, guiding protection staff to adapt messages to climate concerns.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The IOM Protection team works on early warning and preparedness systems to mitigate risks and enable more effective responses. IOM also raises awareness about Protection Key Messages, helping communities mitigate the impact of disasters before, during, and after they occur. This includes house-to-house visits by trained volunteers to ensure that children, older persons, female-headed households, individuals with disabilities, and other vulnerable populations receive necessary support.IOM, alongside its implementing partners, is an integral part of the Rapid Protection Assessment team, Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU), and Mobile Medical Team (MMT), ensuring a swift response to crises and disasters. Close coordination is maintained with Site Management, Camp-in-Charge (CiC) offices, and the Disaster Management Unit (DMU) volunteers, who are trained in DRR, safe referrals for gender-based violence (GBV), and child protection, including response to child/family separation during disasters.IOM maintains a Rohingya Response Business Continuity Plan (BCP), and contingency stocks to support overall EPR efforts for host communities and refugees via relevant Sectors and ISCG.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Engaging with youth is a priority for IOM as whole, and in particular for IOM’s Protection Unit. The Organization is committed to developing activities targeting youth and young people on the move, addressing their needs and vulnerabilities and promoting meaningful youth engagement. The Child Protection project applies IASC Guidelines on working with and for Young People in Humanitarian and Protracted Crises. IOM Child Protection has dedicated support groups for adolescents and coordinates programming with the General Protection, GBV, and Counter-Trafficking teams look at ensure that all Youth between the ages or 15 to 24 are supported, regardless of gender and ability.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

IOM’s protection programming promotes self-reliance in refugee populations by implementing a comprehensive set of strategies that enhance individual resilience and strengthen community support networks. These strategies include: Psychosocial Support, Community Participation, Protection against explotation, Strengthening Family and Community Structures, Linkages to Essential Services, Capacity Building for Local Partners and Security Sector.By integrating these elements, IOM’s general protection programming not only addresses immediate safety, and well-being needs but also lays the foundation for refugees' long-term ability to sustain themselves and contribute positively to their host communities.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

IOM, including the IOM Protection Unit, actively engages in various projects within the host community through initiatives focused on GBV, counter-trafficking, and general protection. This includes providing specialized expertise in security sector activities, particularly through training programs aimed at law enforcement actors such as the APBn and the District Police. This training is crucial for equipping officers with the necessary skills for victim-centered investigations of cases and supporting victims of crimes in a protection-sensitive manner. The inclusion of such trainings underscores IOM’s commitment to strengthening the rule of law, which is essential for fostering safer environments and supporting sustainable development initiatives in host communities.
