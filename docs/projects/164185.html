Project Summary

The aim of the project is to support self-resilience of HHs in Rural Damascus, Hama and Dar'a through distribution of agro-kits and poultry kits. The Action will strengthen resilience of the affected HHs and reduce the dependency on aid of the most vulnerable Syrian population living in these areas. The Project will contribute to the Sector Objective 2 “Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives” in order to reach 1.2 million people in 2020.
To decrease the dependency on food assistance and to promote the self-resilience and consequently the dignity of beneficiaries, the Project will provide agro inputs (seeds) and poultry for family consumption to a total of 10,000 HHs. Agro-inputs will be delivered to those families who have an access to their land, ensuring that lands are free of UXOs, while in the areas still UXOs affected or for the families who do not possess land, poultry kits will be delivered.
In the process of beneficiary’s selection specific focus will be on returnees and local population (host communities). Special attention will be given to female headed HHs, elderly, HHs with People with Disabilities, unaccompanied children and to those groups who have only limited access to income to cover the family food needs and are forced to adopt negative copying mechanisms, unskilled or seasonal workers for instance.
The most common negative coping mechanism detected by COOPI during the implementation of the current projects and through last post harvesting assessment (September 2019) are: not buying clothes, not buying uniforms for children attending school, taking loans, requested support from relatives, waiting for aid coming from INGOs and SARC, families sent their children to work.
During the preparation and implementation of the activities, COOPI will ensure the coordination with local authorities and robust participation of the communities giving them a voice to express desired modalities of implementation (the design of similar projects have been already successfully tested by COOPI in Dar’a and Nashabiyeh). Coordination will be ensured with the FSA Sector and its members but also with international and national actors active in the same villages in order to harmonized the response, avoid overlapping and to promote synergies. Synergies will also be promoted by COOPI through other projects submitted to HRP 2020 taking place in the governorates of this Action.
Additionally, the same approach will be implemented in NES and NW if access is granted to INGOs.

4 - Needs

After 9 years of crisis, the food security situation in Syria has further deteriorated. According to HNO, estimation is that number of people in need of better access to food (PiN) increased for 8% compared to last year, with a total of 9.8 million PiN out of which 569,477 people are estimated to be severely food insecure, 7.3 million estimated to be moderately food insecure. In the 3 proposed areas number of PiN, according to Food security and Livelihood assessment is in total 2.1 million. The sector is continuously highlighting relationship between poverty and food security. According to WFP Market Price Watch Bulletin for December 2019, the cost of the food basket has increased by 17% m-o-m and 43% y-o-y. An estimated 70 percent of Syrians are dedicating over 65% of their expenditure on food with 37% of assessed households reporting outstanding debts and 57% adopting stress, emergency or crisis negative coping strategies (HNO 2020). The economic crisis – impacting a population where more than 80% live under the poverty line - is resulting in deepening humanitarian needs (OCHA report 15-30 January 2020). Shortages of essential items such as rice, vegetable oil and sugar have been noted across the country. As a result, significant price increases have been recorded in essential items with some areas witnessing a 47% increase in the price of bread and a 10% increase in price of medicines. Even still under finalization, the MEB shows that 26% of the food expenditure is used for purchase of the vegetables and 11% for poultry. In COOPI targeted governorates situation is alarming: in Dar’a 77% of the people is buying food while only 0.6% is producing it. In Rural Damascus 87% people are buying food, while only 0.7% is producing it. In Hama situation is slightly better, 69% of people buying food, while 2.2% is producing it. With this Project we will support families to produce food and enable them to utilize their income for satisfying other basic needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Project will be implemented in Rural Damascus, Dar'a and Hama. In the 3 governorates out of 5.5M inhabitants, 2.1M are in need of different level of support to reach an adequate access to food. COOPI aims to provide 5.600 HHs with agro and poultry kits. Appropriate agro inputs or poultry kits will be provided to food insecure, vulnerable returnees and host communities to protect and restore their livelihood and to allow them to decrease their dependence on food aid. Composition of both kits will be discussed and agreed with SARC and MAAR. This support will also allow HHs to protect their assets (negative copying mechanism). HHs will be selected based on technical and vulnerability criteria agreed with SARC and MAAR. Technical criteria for agro-kits are: HHs have land free of UXOs, HHs have access to water for daily watering, have basic technical skills and knowledge in Home gardening. Technical criteria for poultry kits are: HHs do not have access to land, HHs have previous experience in poultry rising, HHs have space for poultry raising. Vulnerability criteria will be the same for both types of assistance: vulnerable returnees and host communities, woman-headed HHs, high dependent ratio (one breadwinner or no breadwinner).Distribution points in each village will be established with dedicated line for people with disabilities, female and elderly people. In each distribution point a feedback mechanism will be in place. The procurement will preferably source supplier and goods from local market to support the local economy. COOPI will be present at the distribution to ensure the correct provision of aid to the selected HHs. Before the distribution of agro kits field visits will be carried out to ensure HHs have access to uncontaminated land and to water for irrigation. The project will provide technical farming/veterinary training for HHs to ensure they adopt proper techniques.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

COOPI staff and beneficiaries could be exposed to protection risks during the implementation of the Action. The assessed risks are the following: sex exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers, violence from HHs against aid workers or among themselves, interference in the HHs targeting, HHs list not correctly updated by local authorities, aid diversion, hostilities in new accessible areas, tension among the community due to the HHs selection and diversion of input/assets. By prior experience in similar projects COOPI evaluate this risk as “low” and “sporadic”To mitigate these risks, the project foresees the following measures: establishment of feedback mechanism, balanced trained staff between men and women, training and follow up on code of conduct, distributions with strong planning and logistic attention, training to staff on humanitarian principles and targeting modalities and criteria, periodical verification of list of HHs before and during the project implementation, FGDs before, during and after the distribution, appropriate locations for distribution points in line with security situation in new accessible areas in consultation with relevant stakeholders, preparation of contingency plans, clear, public and transparent criteria for the selection of HHs, House to house assessment in order to properly select HHs who meet the criteria and who have capacities to utilize delivered assistance. Moreover, before the distribution of agro inputs, project staff will check with local authorities to verify the status and ownership of the land to mitigate the risk to secondary or no rights holders.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK: Direct access to the target areas and HHs is not permitted or becomes unsafe for humanitarian workers. Access to new areas is granted and later withdrawn by the authorities.ASSUMPTION: Escalation of the hostilities in the targeted areas. MITIGATION MEASURES: COOPI will maintain constant communication with agencies, government and in-country security monitoring networks including SARC. All staff will receive security on the field trainings in order to gain knowledge how to act if face problems on the field RISK: Selected beneficiaries cannot access their land. ASSUMPTION: The presence of UXOs may hamper access to agricultural fields and rehabilitation of food production MITIGATION MEASURES: in this case COOPI will organize awareness raising sessions for staff, volunteers and communities in UXOs recognition and will coordinate with SARC for information on progress of UXOs clearance operations and obtain clearance over agricultural fields prior distribution of kits and repair of irrigation networks.
