Project Summary

PIB CPiE will focus on vulnerable and at-risk children, survivors of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation, adolescents and youth through two interlinking components – qualitative case management and community-based child protection (CBCP). The services will ensure boys, girls and adolescents in the Rohingya camps and host communities are safe and protected from violence, abuse and exploitation.
Age appropriate, gender responsive and timely individual Case Management Services will be provided for vulnerable CAYs targeting unaccompanied and separated children, child-headed households, children in psychosocial distress, children with specific needs, and child survivors of GBV. PIB will mainstream with education team by ensuring safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoid causing harm to the children, and will build the capacity of EIE staff on referrals.
CBCP activities will be structured through feedback from community consultation and participation to ensure inclusion and ownership by both the host and the refugee community. The interventions will ensure inclusion of CAYs, persons with disability and the youths. Community-level interventions will include promoting social cohesion and strengthening self-protection of children. Addressing harmful practices and attitudes, Plan will invest in active community participation and positive parenting.
PIB is accountable and will ensure that child friendly feedback mechanisms are in place with strong emphasis on PSEA and Child Safeguarding. PIB will ensure its partners have PSEA policies and the same is implemented.
PIB is committed to coordinating the response through active participation in sectors such as Protection, Education, GBV and Child Protection Sub-Sector and other working groups to ensure meaningful access, prioritization of safety and integration of services. The services offered will be in line with CPMS, interagency tools and SOPs, and will be ensured through structured training enhancing competencies in providing quality gender-responsive and timely services.
Plan will undertake several assessments such as baseline, midterm, impact assessment, and will collect disaggregated data including people with disability. Plan will also use Washington Questionnaire to better understand the need and requirement of people with disability.

1 - Identified Needs

Since August 2017 approximately 862,277 Rohingyas are being supported in Bangladesh, and among them, girls and boys represent 55% of all refugees. There is an urgent need for gender analysis focusing on identifying existing social norms and gender related barriers which holds women and girls to access their rights. Children are in high risk when gender equality is not present and disable children are not included in program planning as well as other excluded and marginalized groups. It is an imminent need to collect and analyze sex, age and ability-disaggregated data to plan and include activities that explicitly target and support the identified needs and capacities of women and girls.Based on observations from ongoing projects, there is a specific need to prevent and respond to abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation and to ensure immediate access to appropriate services. There is a strong and justifiable need to provide and ensure gender sensitive and responsive case management and referral services for the identified vulnerable girls and boys who are at risk and who have urgent protection need and who are harmed or at risk of harm. Raising awareness and building capacity of all staff and volunteers will undoubtedly ensure equal access to and benefit from project services for women and girls in all interventions. Children are at very high risk of child labor, and adolescent girls are at risk of early marriage as well as boys for child trafficking. Due to school closure in host community, children are also going to be victims and unprotected which could lead them to trauma and other related anxiety or depression issues. The increasing rate in early, forced and child marriages was attributed to financial pressures faced by families, as well as a desire to keep girls safe. Adolescent girls can no longer access women and girls’ friendly spaces and learning centers where they previously received support and capacity-building opportunities.Parents are worried that their children can fall victim to diseases and kidnapping. Children and adolescents are likely to experience the negative impacts on their mental health due to the loss of educational opportunities and closures. There is a strong and urgent need to implement targeted community engagement approaches to reach high-risk adolescents, particularly adolescent girls and their families, to highlight concerns around early, forced and child marriage, child kidnapping, child labour, drug abuse and the short- and long-term implications of adolescent pregnancies. There is a strong need to scale up structured psychosocial support remains urgent. Additionally, unawareness of gender and SRHR issues among the communities are also high because of religious and cultural conservatism.COVID 19 pandemic has caused a lot of stress and uncertainty. There is an ongoing need to strengthen community engagement and risk communication efforts to ensure timely access of COVID-19 risk-prevention messages for children, including through providing guidance to parents/caregivers. Among the Rohingya communities, there is a noticeable change in children’s behaviour since the onset of the COVID pandemic, including mood swings and anger. PIB recognises longer-term measures focusing on children and adolescents’ needs in host communities, which increasingly face economic and social tensions, are increasingly important. Like refugees, host communities living in Cox’s Bazar District have unaddressed basic needs and are also exposed to protection risks due to a combination of factors, including poverty, limited access to livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services and inadequate infrastructure and markets. Recreational space for children in host communities is limited and the pressure on limited space attributed by some host community members to the large number of refugees has been reported as a factor contributing to inter-community tensions.

2 - Activities or outputs

Ensured gender sensitive and responsive case management and referral services for the identified vulnerable girls and boys who are at risk Parents, caregivers and Community level CP actors are capacitated to improve their relationship with the children through capacity building of CBCP committees (positive parentings, MHPSS sessions (PSS and PFA), and Capacity building of CM staff, volunteers and non-CP actors (case management foundation training, basic MHPSS (PFA, PSS), communication with children, safe identification and referrals, safeguarding and PSEA) Case management staffs are capacitated on Case Management, Technical supervision, CPIMS+, caring for child survivors through training, mentoring and refresher training. CBCP and CM staffs, volunteers from PLAN and Partner organization received training and refreshers on CPiE, Positive parenting, Gender Equality and Gender Norms, Community led initiatives for preventing and protecting CP risk in community, Form and functioning Women Led Organization and Disability and inclusion. Capacity building initiatives are in place for police, health, camp authority personnel on CP and child friendly interviewing and communication skills Capacity building of key male/female stakeholders in the community on coordination and age-/gender sensitive response in supporting CM staff and increasing their knowledge about CP principles, gender equality and inclusionYouth club members (Girls, boys, female and male youth) are aware of Gender and SRHR issues, have access to PEER to PEER self-protection like life-skill, PSS, Community Based Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CBCFM) and counselling sessions in camp and host communities. This CBCFM will ensure that complaints are addressed properly, and accountability to affected population is ensured.CBCPC Members are trained on child rights and child protection, Gender equality and inclusion, child development and gender norms and Community knowledge and awareness of the rights and contributions of diverse groups are increased and the community people play a pro-active role to mitigate and prevent the Child Protection Risk in any types of emergency (like, natural disaster and man-made disasterPrevention on COVID 19 through awareness sessions, community-led cultural campaign, SBCC, IEC materials and distribution of health and hygiene kits. Adolescent youth including persons with disabilities participate in livelihood development initiatives and have access to various essential services, provided by different service providers in both camp and host community. Awareness, community mobilization, beneficiary selection for Cash based intervention and distribution of cash to selected vulnerable Different authorities and child protection (like police, local administration, relevant Government department, Camp in Charge and camp authorities actors) were addressed with capacity building and advocacy initiatives such as dialogue, workshop for strengthening child protection systems in both camp and host community. Establish, train and support women-led organizations and mobilize community people including persons with disability to raise awareness on social cohesion, Gender equality and peace building. A participatory research is in place on social cohesion and mutual trust building between refugees and host community. Facilitate and support dialogue and trust-building activities in pilot locations in host communities and camp locations. Workshop with duty bearers (government officials and local authorities), community leaders, parents, teachers and religious leaders within both community groups on developing a basic understanding of everyday peace, social cohesion, social trust and public accountability. Undertake capacity-strengthening and training activities for national and local institutions, actors and partners on conflict sensitivity, social cohesion and peace-building, particularly targeting civil society organizations (CSOs), women and youth
