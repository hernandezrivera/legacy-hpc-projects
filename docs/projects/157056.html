Project Summary

Using its rapid response mechanism in NW Syria, NGO X proposes an action of tents distribution either for newly displaced HHs or for replacements of the old tents. The proposed action will target approximately 393 unique households (2,358 unique vulnerable displaced individuals) throughout 393 tents distributions.
This project is part of NGO X’s integrated programming approach that caters for a range of life-saving needs including by providing tents, food and NFI distribution, using pre-positioned in-kind and cash assistance modalities; shelter repairs/tents, and protection referrals to respond to these needs.
This submission is only focusing on tents and CCCM Cluster related activities during 2019. Please see below the relevant activities form CCCM Cluster Results Framework 2019.

Needs

NGO X conducted two rapid needs assessments (RNA): 1) 8-12 October 2018 in North Aleppo IDP camps: Ahel al-Sham Camp, Al Harameen Camp, Iğde-Ikda Camp, Kaiba Camp, Mukavema Camp, Risale Camp, and Yazibag Camp in the A’zaz district; 2) 14-18 December 2018 in Idleb: Ariha, Idleb, Harim, and Jisr-ash-Shugur Districts. NGO X conducted RNA in North Aleppo on 8-12 October to ascertain the major local needs and gaps. This RNA used two main tools for data collection: 12 KIIs with local councils and camp managers; and 39 FGDs with IDPs in four camps. The data collection was carried out by NGO X team members who managed to secure permission to cross the Turkey border to Syria, as well as by Syria-based enumerators. The assessment outcomes and findings represent the following:Shelter issues:• Tents are the type of shelters that families live in for the majority of the HHs living in the visited camps.• Participants reported that tents do not have weather resistance, and the majority of the participants said that the tents are very old and very poor regarding protection from the cold, heat, wind, rain, snow.• Tents are overcrowded and have no privacy.• Participants indicated that there is no covered space for essential household activities (shower, cooking etc.),• They mentioned that they improve their shelter conditions by putting old clothes and blankets (which they buy using their own money) on the tent to insulate from the summer heat and the cold weather.• Assessment team encountered HHs who are staying/accommodated in the toilets because they do not have a tent or place to stay.Form the RNA Tents came one of the urgent needs of the affected populations.The assessment report has been shared with relevant respected clusters including CCCM cluster. NGO X conducted RNA in Idleb on 14-18 December in Idleb to ascertain the emergency, shelter and protection needs and risks (4 FGDs and 250 interviews with beneficiaries: 37% women and 33% men; 45% IDPs and 55% resident community members). NGO X sampled HHs reported living in owned property, housing without rent payment, housing through (in-)formal rental agreements and Communal Centres (CC) as the main shelter solutions. The majority of respondents owning houses were from the host community showing that IDPs are more vulnerable to shelter related vulnerabilities. Damage to shelter, lack of privacy and corresponding concerns regarding the shelter not offering adequate climatic protection were the most frequently cited shelter issue, referenced by 53% of survey respondents. High rent payments were among the highest concerns for households living in rented accommodation. Other issues cited relate to water storage wastewater management and other plumbing issues in addition to insufficient bathroom / toilet facilities for all household members. These pose risks to health and raise protection concerns (e.g. lack of gender-separation or privacy, lighting, etc.). Concerns over shelter and WASH conditions were also cited in assessed IDP camps.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:Create pull-factor and expose IDPs to physical safety risks when/(if the camps and settlements are located in unsafe areas) Mitigation Measures:Tents provision is the beginning of any potential camps regardless if formal or informal. Any distribution of tent should follow CCCM guidelines to minimize this risk.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, supported by continued adherence to NGO X's acceptance strategy and the joint operating procedures; and security remains sufficiently stable;- The Turkish Armed Forces continue to provide stability in N Aleppo via their presence, their support for opposition factions under the banner of the “National Army” and their provision of security services;- NGO X maintains a level of cooperation with the Turkish authorities to secure safe access to the region;- Local markets continue to function with an adequate supply / diversity of items and retain sufficient capacity to support cash-based interventions; Risks:- Continued military offensives by GoS and inter-factional fighting presents the risk of death or injury to staff; damage to or destruction of NGO X premises or assets; reduced access to programme areas for NGO X staff or beneficiaries; border closure;- Continued targeting of certain opposition factions by international actors presents the risk of those factions directing their suspicions at NGO X;- Risks associated with operating in camp settings specifically in terms of GBV, child protection, armed recruitment;- Travel risks for NGO X staff and beneficiaries in N Aleppo due to insecurity, check points, roaming armed groups;- Remote control management from Turkey and Jordan presents the risk of international and Syrian staff losing legal status; sudden border closures disrupting operations; other regulatory changes potentially affecting the programme;- There is a risk of fraud including in banking, cash management, procurement, transport, and programming;- There is a risk that cash and market-based programmes in a conflict setting raises security and acceptance risks Contingency:- Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and SOPs in place, and an experienced security team which will continue to monitor trends, conduct actor mapping, and share information to enable rapid and appropriate changes to implementation plans;- Ongoing communication with actors operational across NGO X's areas; continued adherence to Acceptance Strategies: working closely with Local Councils to identify shocks and affected HHs, plan and conduct distributions, as well as maintaining open communications with communities;- Pre-emptive actions will be taken in camps to mitigate risks through mainstreaming protection, incl. GBV, child protection, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse;- Travel and other risks have been included in NGO X’s SOPs for risk assessment in N Aleppo;- To minimise the risk of fraud and its potential impact, NGO X has updated and strengthened its controls and assurances framework;- NGO X has registered as an INGO in Jordan and established an office in Amman. Should registration in Turkey be revoked, NGO X will transfer operations to Amman as its primary hub;- Should the situation require it, NGO X will suspend programming in a phased manner.
