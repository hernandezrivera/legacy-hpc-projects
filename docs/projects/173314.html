Project Summary

Oxfam will be supporting WASH activities in affected areas including restoration of 60 boreholes, 4 piped water schemes ,30 latrines for vulnerable households and other ancillary such as handwashing stations using build back better principles where appropriate. The project will also assist 8 000 vulnerable individuals with WASH NFIs to meet their minimum WASH needs in an emergency and improve their water and sanitation conditions in Harare,Matobo, Bulilima and Mangwe districts. The project will reach 66 677 people with hygiene messages and 30 latrines will be constructed for vulnerable households in rural districts.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Oxfam in Zimbabwe subscribes to the global Oxfam position of being accountable to the people it serves, stakeholders and donors. Oxfam ensures active participation of beneficiary communities and stakeholders including government through different mechanisms that included review meetings, through help desk and tollfree channels in all its programming. Communities and stakeholders shall be engaged during the various project activities such as joint assessment, planning, implementation, review and monitoring. The programme team will develop a tailored accountability system with partners and beneficiary communities to ensure transparency and dissemination of all key information regarding the programme; participation by stakeholders in all programme phases from design to exit; feedback and complaint mechanisms; and sharing of learning with stakeholders. The accountability mechanisms will be safe, accessible and effective and the mechanisms under consideration include help desks, suggestion boxes, joint monitoring visits, community meetings and a telephone hotline dedicated to disseminating programme information, receive and capture feedback through voice calls or short messaging. The hotline number will be displayed at all community gatherings. The project will make specific and key efforts to ensure the long-term sustainability of the interventions supported under this cooperation through building upon the existing structures such as the RDC, PWSSC, DWSSC, WPCs and village pump mechanics, which have the primary responsibility of managing water supply services within communities, clinics and schools. The project will strengthen and monitor the capacity of community structures to respond to water supply management issues and challenges using the Group Maturity Index

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will develop and implement an Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP) during project implementation period. The plan will serve as an "Umbrella Document" that integrates the findings of all impact studies carried out during the design phase, the plans and other provisions for complying with the requirements of the National and areal environmental and social legislation and practise. Implementation shall be in line with various acts such as EMA, Water Act as well as regional and town planning act. The project shall minimise any negative impact to the environment during construction of piped water schemes and rehabilitation of handpumps.
