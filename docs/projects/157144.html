Project Summary

Under Sudan’s MYHS 2017-2019 and HRP 2019, UNICEF will endeavor to support the most vulnerable and marginalized particularly, women and children.
UNICEF positions itself to work in the deprived communities with the highest level of vulnerability aiming to address the underlying immediate and long-term causes. Based on the comparative advantage of UNICEF’s programming and hands-on experience of 60 years in Sudan, it is evident that a meaningful impact and results only be attained by adopting inter-sectoral approaches given they are fully integrated.
UNICEF aims to deliver an integrated package for children by adopting a “whole child” approach wherein, the UNICEF led sectors would work together by identifying the geographic convergence, improving programmatic complementary under a common result framework thus, realizing rights of the most vulnerable children in the areas of health, education, WASH and protection.
This will allow UNICEF to enhance sustainability within the projects and build resilience of the affected population and thus, bridging the humanitarian and development.
In the spirit of partnership and the new way of working, UNICEF submits an inter-sectoral proposal – a progressive step in the direction of achieving integration.

Needs

A range of humanitarian crises - protracted conflicts and related displacement, natural disasters, epidemics, malnutrition and food insecurity have affected the people of Sudan. These have been further exacerbated by the economic crisis of 2018, along with the ongoing violence in many States resulting in multi-pronged impacts ranging from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) to children dropping out of Schools and heightened protection risks and vulnerabilities for women, girls, boys and men and strained health services. Food insecurity and malnutrition are widespread, with 11 out of 18 states experiencing global acute malnutrition rates(GAM) above the emergency threshold, affecting 2.4 million children. An estimated 700,000 children U5 years suffer from SAM. This displacement has contributed also to depriving 3 million children at primary school age (5-13 years), from their right to education, safety and protection. Universal Primary Education remains a challenge across the country with the national enrolment rate ranging between 49% to 72.5%. 1,465,840 conflict affected children (597,515 IDPs, 110,767 Returnees and 756,800 vulnerable residents), need Education in Emergencies. Over 7,300 active cases of unaccompanied, separated, and missing children were recorded in 2018. Mobilization of children including, their recruitment and use in armed conflict continues to be a substantial risk. Gender based Violence against children a serious violation that needs addressing specially in the newly accessible areas of Darfur while lifesaving protection measures are required for children in newly accessible areas in East Jebal Marra, South Kordofan and Blue Nile. The weakening of the national health system, poor services’ coverage, shortage of essential health supplies and qualified health personnel is inadequate to serve more than 3.6 million people needing health assistance (mostly women and children under five years). The under-five mortality hovers at 30% due to preventable diseases that continue to be among the major public health threats. Recently, a chikungunya virus (CHIKV) outbreak started in the eastern zone of Sudan, affecting more than 30,000 people. This susceptibility is linked to weak health systems coupled with poor emergency preparedness and inefficient response at all levels. Health awareness among conflict affected population is also one of the major challenges. According to the MICS, only 29% of mothers have adequate knowledge on childhood illnesses’ danger signs and less than 10% of women know prevention of HIV. AWD and malnutrition are directly linked to lack of basic WASH services. Some 3.3 million people urgently require water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) support, including the need to prevent future disease outbreaks. The 2019 HRP identifies this population affected by WASH-related epidemics in 106 localities of Sudan. These need urgent WASH support services as a key response to their immediate survival and protection requirements.

Activities

Treat 290,467 SAM children with 348,560 cartons of Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) while supporting operations and expansion of Outpatient Treatment Program (OTP) sites, community mobilization and awareness. Early case finding and referral, provision of community-based counselling on optimal IYCF, hygiene and care practices to 688,628 caregivers. Capacity building, construction/rehabilitation of gender and disability inclusive basic WASH services. Operation of water supply services, community empowerment on management of WASH services with focus on women/adolescent’s roles; latrines, using Community Led Total Sanitation approaches. Coordination and delivery of life-saving protection services to reach 110,245 children with PSS and specialized protection services including case management, 3,189 children with family tracing, reunification (FTR) or alternative care arrangements, 120,244 women and children with GBV prevention and response interventions, capacitate 50 community-based networks, support 100 children previously associated with armed forces and armed groups and the respective communities and strengthen MRM on grave child rights violations. Establish and rehabilitate classrooms, provide gender sensitive WASH facilities, to support 73,599 children. Essential learning materials for 256,701 children. Increase school enrollment, capacitate 4,278 teachers, 2,208 PTA’s members; children and adolescents. consult women, men, boys and girls in the design, implementation and evaluation of the project, this will be done through perception surveys, FGD's during assessments, establishment of feedback mechanisms and community engagement.

Impact on environment/natural resources

UNICEF conscientiously works towards ensuring that interventions undertaken contribute positively to impacting the environment. Through the implementation of the activities herein embedded, UNICEF will ensure at the very minimum the following: 1. Use of digital technology to replace paper-based assessments and reporting.2. Increase the usage of solar energy through use of equipment for cold chain, water supply 3. Gardening and Tree planting around the school as part of the education related activities 4. Waste disposal including waste management as part of the menstrual hygiene activities5. Environmentally friendly disposal of medical supplies and potential waste in the Health facilities 6. Introduction of sustainable ways of transporting water supply including assistance through cash-based interventions7. Sustainable community based Disaster Risk management activitiesSpecifically related to WASH, UNICEF will strive to undertake construction of new water supply lines on sites that will be intentionally selected taking in consideration low environmental impact of water pumping through IWRM and climate change adaptation measures for balanced and rationalized utilization of water resources. Besides, awareness and capacity on IWRM and climate change adaptation among decision makers, sector practioners and communities will be enhanced. Geohydrological information management and monitoring system will be established with focus on the groundwater vulnerable areas. Environment friendly water supply motorized solar systems, hand pumps and concrete instead of wooden latrine slabs will be widely adopted.With regards to Nutrition interventions, impact through waste generated from packaging materials of RUTF and nutrition supplements will be checked by working alongside the GoS’s health services in implementing their responsible waste disposal policy that includes, incinerators and/or waste disposal facilities at health centres.As for Health, natural disasters are increasing the vulnerability of affected population and this project therefore considers supporting health emergency preparedness, timely response and building community resilience as one of its many objectives. Locaties at high-risk of the natural disasters (mainly floods and drought) are fully considered and targeted by this project. Building the community capacity and supporting services' provision through community workers- including community midwives- are among the activities planned for under this project to serve as mitigation measures.As for Protection and Education, the service providers, teachers and students will be made aware of the negative impacts of project implementation keeping in view the accountability to the affected population and the dangers of exploiting scarce natural resources and enabled to understand strategies that could conserve and preserve resources for future generation.
