Project Summary

Now in its eleven-year, the conflict inside Syria shows all the signs of a complex protracted humanitarian crisis. Not only have the humanitarian needs increased in spite of the considerable efforts of the international community, but drivers of instability and conflict are growing due to the political situation. While opportunities for people to seek refuge in neighboring countries are reducing, IDPs, who are frequently subjected to multiple displacements, are mostly concentrated in a few (urban) areas, affecting the socio-economic stability of the host communities and increasing pressure on resources (such as water or land) and services. This has the potential to contribute to localized crises or conflicts, further increasing tensions, and/or leading IDPs and host communities to resort to negative coping strategies.
To support the current urgent needs of stabilization in communities that have maintained minimum security stability, and show the potential of progress toward civil recovery; to resolve some of the lack the opportunity and improve the economy for people in Idleb and Aleppo, Mercy-USA seeks through this project to support vulnerable IDPs population and host community members to contribute towards increased resilience of vulnerable households through sustainable and restorative socio-economic interventions, targeting 300 beneficiaries (150 males and 150 female, 50% HC & 50% IDPs), with the following activities:
• Deliver Economic Recovery and Market Systems (ERMS) Livelihood assistance in the forms of In-Kind grants, business development services, and vocational training to IDPs and Host Community members in selected communities in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates.
• Build community capacity in local governance and management of community supporting projects.
ERMS activities will be supported in coordination with ERL cluster will target males and females between the ages of 18-50, who are currently residing in the target community and camps supported by Mercy projects. Mercy-USA proposes to shift the current project BNFs who have previous experience in livelihood activities by supporting them to restore their livelihoods.
Each HH will receive a start-up value of USD$300 as first tranche, then USD$125 cash every month for a total of 4 payments (total: USD$800).
Beneficiaries would begin generating income 4 months from the initial project start and would generate over USD$800 in an 8-month period.
The client should prioritize two trainings, and at the end of the registration, he will be informed of the training he/she can be enrolled in.

4 - Needs

Pre-crisis, Syria’s economy was based on agriculture, industry, oil, trade and tourism, with the state providing substantial subsidies, especially to the agricultural sector.More than eleven years of crisis have had a devastating impact on Syria’s economy. Deep economic recession, fluctuating national currency, sanctions, vastly increased food and fuel prices, and disruption of markets have impacted all economic sectors and contributed to extreme vulnerability. A lack of purchasing power has been identified as the main barrier to accessing basic items. 79 per cent of the labour force has lost their jobs. Overall, livelihood opportunities are becoming extremely scarce and some households depend primarily on unsustainable livelihood strategies (selling of property, use of savings etc.). Some households also go for extreme coping strategies, including child labour, early marriage of girls to reduce the economic burden on families, joining armed groups and other conflict-related activities. Child labour and early marriage are not new phenomena in the Syrian context.Due to widespread erosion of previous livelihoods and massive displacements, many Syrians are in a situation where their skills are no longer relevant. Many who were skilled to work in livelihoods now find themselves in new areas seeking new employment; other displaced persons come from farther afield and do not always have skills that suit the local economy.

Small business support will focus on HHs already having some previous experience of developing small enterprise activities. These HHs will be reliant on petty trade, and/or maybe embarking on new ventures based on their existing experience but lack capital to start, or they may have lost assets or resources through displacement and are therefore unable to restart their enterprise without an injection of capital or resources. 1.5 Distribution of cash based on a business plan, and monitoring If conditional cash transfers are used as the modality for the business grants, this can be split into three transfers, the second and third transfer being released upon successful implementation of the agreed-upon steps in the business plan. The applicants will also provide a basic package of business training and coaching/mentoring for the successful applicants. 1.6 Following up and monitoring the selected subsidized projects.
