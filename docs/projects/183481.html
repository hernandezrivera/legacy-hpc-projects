Project Summary

Physicians for Human Rights Israel (PHRI) will contribute to Health and Nutrition Cluster objective 1, as its purpose is to provide quality lifesaving and essential services to the most vulnerable Palestinian communities, while strengthening the local health system though emergency preparedness and other types of trainings in specialties lacking in the Occupied Palestinian Territory (oPt).
Through specialist mobile clinics to the West Bank and medical delegations to Gaza, PHRI’s volunteer physicians will give direct medical assistance to 10,000 beneficiaries in both areas; will provide free medication; and will issue or fund referrals to hospitals in East Jerusalem and Israel for over 100 cases. The specialist mobile clinics will visit West Bank villages and areas with limited access to specialized care once a week, conducting health screenings in some of the clinics. Approximately once a month, PHRI will deploy medical delegations to the Gaza Strip, consisting of volunteer surgeons and specialists. At least 60% of the overall patients who visit the specialist mobile clinic and are served by the Gaza delegations will be women.
As part of the medical delegations to the Gaza Strip, PHRI doctors will also carry out 100 surgeries. Patients will be selected from the Ministry of Health’s waiting list and in coordination with local surgeons. PHRI surgeons will bring necessary surgical equipment, disposables and tools lacking in the Gaza Strip- both for use during surgeries and in order to increase the capacity of local hospitals.
While providing medical aid, the intervention also aims to enhance preparedness within the health sector and reinforce the capacity of the Palestinian health system. Health professionals will be trained as per their own priorities, based on academic and practical gaps identified in collaboration with the Ministry of Health and local NGOs. In Gaza, trainings will take place through hands-on surgical coaching, workshops and conferences. The surgery coaching in the key fields of orthopaedics, vascular and chest surgery (among others) will benefit around 30 Palestinian surgeons and health staff in Gaza. In between visits, PHRI’s surgeons continue to provide advice and support to their colleagues in Gaza via calls and emails. PHRI will also facilitate 5 medical conferences in Gaza with over 660 participants in total, bringing up-to-date medical knowledge to meet the needs of medical professionals.
The intervention will also address the Health and Nutrition Cluster strategic objective 2, carrying out advocacy to protect the right to health of the most vulnerable. For patients and companions who require treatment unavailable in the oPt and whose Israel-issued exit permits have been denied, as well as for health staff needing to exit the oPt for training, PHRI will intervene to reverse denials in 200 cases. PHRI will sometimes use legal action combined with a range of advocacy tools to tackle individual cases and will also employ strategic litigation to challenge general criteria according to which permits are denied. Particular focus will also be given to persons with disabilities and children in PHRI’s advocacy work. PHRI will advocate for the right to health of orthopedic and cancer patients from Gaza, who require life-changing interventions such as spinal surgeries and chemotherapy and whose permits have been consistently denied on grounds that the expertise for these surgeries is available in Gaza. PHRI will focus on advocacy surrounding long-term permits, which Israel rarely issues and in turn impacts patients who must frequently exit the Gaza Strip for treatment.

1 - Needs

As indicated in the Health Cluster draft HNO 2022 narrative, the oPt’s humanitarian health crisis is driven by cross-cutting challenges, which have placed a greater burden on an already burdened healthcare system; namely the COVID-19 pandemic, flare-ups of military hostilities between Israel and Palestine. Moreover, the decades-long occupation of the oPt and Israel’s blockade on the Gaza Strip have further strained the Palestinian healthcare system by imposing access restrictions, and disallowing the Palestinian healthcare system to function as one unit. In the West Bank, the local health system is particularly challenged to respond to patient needs in numerous fields, and access to health services are challenging in some vulnerable communities due to movement restrictions, especially for people with disabilities. As the humanitarian situation deteriorates, women’s needs for specialized services, including reproductive health screenings, has increased. Chronic depletion of essential medical supplies and drugs must be addressed through medication and equipment deliveries. As per the MoH’s latest report (September 2021), 204 items out of the essential medications list were at zero stock in Gaza, which amounts to 39%. Israeli approval rates of exit permits for treatment unavailable in Gaza and the West Bank have been plummeting, and PHRI receives many more requests than it can handle. Specific challenges are identified for cancer patients – in particular women. Israel rejects permit applications on grounds that such expertise is present in Gaza, without considering the patient load. Palestinian physicians face pronounced challenges in their pursuit of studies, continuing education, and specialized training. To travel from Gaza or the West Bank to trainings in other parts of the occupied territory, Israel or other neighbouring countries, they must obtain a valid travel permit, yet often fall victim to Israel's non-transparent movement and access restrictions, preventing them from reaching residencies, trainings and jobs.PHRI’s activities on the ground allow us to track developing and existing trends on the community level. PHRI’s needs assessment mechanism involves both continued cooperation and contact with stakeholders such as village councils and Ministry of Health officials, as well as an analysis of the data gathered on the ground through our medical delegation of specialist doctors work. PHRI has observed growing needs for referrals in the fields of Internal and family medicine, orthopedics, physical medicine and rehabilitation, gynecology, nephrology, neurology, cardiology, among others. PHRI’s needs assessment is based on a variety of sources: WHO situation reports, the Humanitarian Response Plan for the oPt (HRP), the Health Cluster’s Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), reports from other organizations, and its own practical assessments. PHRI is in constant dialogue with local partners, the Ministry of Health and physicians themselves. PHRI has been handling permit denials for 25 years. PHRI’s local partners are in contact with the beneficiaries and report to us on the needs of our patients.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Mobile clinics and medical delegations will offer specialized care to 10,000 patients (over 60% of them women and girls) in both the West Bank and Gaza, in the most vulnerable communities. The needs of people with disabilities will be addressed by making all clinics and medical services accessible. Female health-staffed clinics will address special women’s health needs. Local women grassroots organizations will help ensure high patient turnout and that marginalized beneficiaries will not get overlooked. Specialized women’s mobile clinics will be conducted under this activity. Activity 2: 100 mobile clinic patients in need of treatment unavailable either in the West Bank or Gaza will be supported with referrals to hospitals or appointments in East Jerusalem or Israel. Activity 3: Chronic depletion of essential medical supplies and drugs will be addressed through medication and equipment deliveries based on Ministry of Health lists. The mobile clinic will also distribute free medication during consultations, while PHRI surgeons will bring equipment needed for surgeries. Activity 4: Through its team of highly skilled surgeons, 100 essential elective surgeries will be conducted. Some cases may include emergency surgery in cases where our delegation happens to be present in the operating theater and may be able to assist. A focus will be given to orthopedics and cardiovascular surgery. Activity 5: The project will enhance the skills and knowledge of over 690 Palestinian medical staff to improve health services. workshops will bring volunteer experts from Israel to the oPt to present recent advancements in their fields, including mental health and women's health. 30 of the trainees will be trained during surgeries. Activity 6: PHRI will support patients whose permits to exit Gaza and the West Bank for treatment have been denied. Particular attention will be given to female patients, as well as parents who need to accompany their children. Permit restrictions affecting orthopedic patients will be addressed. New restrictive regulations issued by Israel will be examined from a gender perspective, highlighting how they affect women’s access to health. PHRI may take legal action to appeal refusals and use strategic litigation when it becomes aware of a restriction affecting a grouping of patients. Activity 7: A percentage of the women in mobile clinics will be asked a questionnaire to further examine the impact of the conflict on the health of women in Gaza in underserved communities. The questionnaire will help shed light on specific health issues affecting women. The data which will be presented in a position paper. Activity 8: The project will protect the right to health of Palestinians through advocacy activities targeting Israeli dutybearers, including security officials and MKs, combined with media work to increase pressure. International advocacy activities will challenge Israel's policies and their adverse effect on Palestinians' health.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The direct inquiries, feedback, and complaints raised by our beneficiaries and partners constitute the primary focus of projects’ needs assessment, implementation, M&E, and future project design. The feedback mechanism is integrated into all aspects of the project. PHRI’s feedback mechanism operates through two channels. First, feedback from partners aids PHRI in project assessment, modification of implementation, M&E and future project design. PHRI’s partners and local physicians are in constant dialogue with the Director of the Medical Delegation who is in charge of the referrals mechanism. The Ministry of Health is a key partner in all activities. Palestinian organisations, most of which are grassroots NGOs well embedded in the host communities, play an important role in pointing at patient needs and providing feedback. The second channel for PHRI’s feedback mechanism is receiving feedback directly from beneficiaries through questionnaires, which will be distributed to a certain percentage of beneficiaries- depending on the activity. A questionnaire will be distributed, scored, and analyzed. Further, complaints will be received by PHRI’s volunteer doctors and partners, who will self-report on any complaints received. Regarding freedom of movement cases, PHRI plans to conduct two in-depth interviews with beneficiaries every month (one successful and one unsuccessful case), to get a better understanding of its working modalities and how they are perceived. Appropriate measures will be taken when relevant. PHRI receives feedback and cooperates with other organisations working on Palestinian prisoner issues.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

During all interactions with beneficiaries, PHRI ensures COVID-19 precautionary safety measures, such as physical distancing, surgical mask wearing and hygiene promotion are adhered to. Further, PHRI ensures that secondary negative effects of COVID-19 are identified and responded to in a timely manner. Do No Harm principles are considered through all PHRI’s projects. All staff and volunteers have experience working in the oPt and have a well-integrated conflict-sensitivity. The physicians are all volunteers working in their free time in solidarity of their Palestinian colleagues and patients and have great professional and personal integrity. PHRI’s work is also guided by our safeguarding policy document. It inter alia describes how PHRI is committed to preventing and responding appropriately to harm to children and vulnerable adults; how PHRI openly communicates a lack of tolerance towards abuse; commits to preventing a person from working with children and/or vulnerable people if he/she poses an unacceptable risk; places clear responsibilities and expectations on PHRI’s staff and associates and supports them in understanding and acting in line with the safeguarding policy. The policy is implemented by providing training for personnel, contractors, volunteers and board members. Furthermore, PHRI has a robust recruitment screening process and employment contracts containing provisions for dismissal, suspension or transfer to other duties for any employee who breaches the safeguarding code of conduct. PHRI prioritizes the safety and dignity of its patients. Information shared by beneficiaries is always kept confidential, unless otherwise instructed. PHRI’s activities support the development of self-protection capacities and help empower beneficiaries, allowing them to claim their right to health. Patients who approach PHRI needing referrals and appointments outside the West Bank, who ask PHRI to try to obtain an exit permit after their application has been turned down, must claim their rights themselves – this is particularly true of female patients which are empowered to own and defend their cases. Indeed, each permit request requires quite a commitment on the part of the patient, who needs to go back to her doctor for more information in her file, understanding fully her case, etc.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PHRI strives to remove all access barriers to our activities, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups. PHRI provides assistance in accordance with basic humanitarian principles, based on identified needs (impartiality), without any adverse distinction, as indicated by the different categories of patients that are treated by our clinics and medical delegations, have undergone surgeries, or received referrals or permits in the past decade. Our beneficiaries have included women, men and children, with a special focus on the most vulnerable people. Vulnerable groups are identified and targeted through our network of well-recognized partners on the ground: women grassroots organizations, rehabilitation organizations, cancer organizations, and others. Acceptance levels within the target population are high: PHRI is well known and trusted in the oPt, after over three decades of regular activities and ongoing cooperation with partner organisations on the ground. PHRI – an Israeli organization - is perceived as a professional, trustworthy and neutral. Among the vulnerable groups targeted by our services are women, who are especially targeted by our women's-only mobile clinics in the West Bank and our focus on women's health needs in Gaza. People with disabilities are also targeted by PHRI's mobile clinics, all of which are made wheelchair accessible, and which strive to provide specialist healthcare to people with disabilities, including by helping them to obtain medical equipment (such as wheelchairs) which is otherwise unavailable to them.
