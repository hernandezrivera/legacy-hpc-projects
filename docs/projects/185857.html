Project Summary

"Under this JRP in 2022, Caritas Bangladesh will cover Total 3980 HHs (19,037 individuals) with WASH related different kinds of activities in the caritas working camps 2000 HHs at Camp-17 (Block A & B) , 990 HHs at Camp-4 (Block-F) and 990 HHs at ward#1,2,3 under Jaliapalong union of the Host communities . As per the ISCG situation report of the Rohingya Refugee Crisis, 19% of the people in camps still do not have access functional latrines and 16 % do not have access to safe drinking water. As per ISCG/WASH-Sector reports 54 % of households reporting tap stand as main source of water used for drinking at the time of data collection. It was expressed that non-functionality of tube-wells stems from declining water level, having no head and broken pipes.
Further, they informed that of bathing facilities are out of order due to unavailability of water facilities, lack of cleanliness, distance from the shelter, no privacy, and broken door/fence and drainage problems. A good number of people use communal bathing facilities. Most of the people do not have designated bathing facilities, and thus use tube-well platforms. On the other hand, around 35% of latrines are non-functional and not in use due to a lack of privacy/no separation between men and women, toilets being full, not being safe (no door, no lock, etc.), and being too far away. These challenges around latrines and bathing places especially affect women because they did not feel safe using the facilities at night and there was no segregation of facilities for men and women, a problem due to cultural norms. Additionally, females’ s menstrual hygiene needs are largely unmet.
In a joint agency gender analysis on the response, children and adolescents also raised concerns about safety when accessing WASH facilities. Girls were prevented from using latrines that were being used by both men and women, due to the lack of segregation and privacy. All girls surveyed felt that fetching water from tube wells was unsafe because of the distance from home. It was alarming that 43% of HHs with persons with disabilities not reporting having used an improved water source.
The projects aim to mainstream protection for ensuring that beneficiaries safely access their services, without causing harm, and for promoting meaningful access, accountability, and participation of beneficiaries in the provision of WASH assistance and it will maximize the positive impacts of WASH programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoy their rights and strengthen participation and empowerment.
The program includes DRR components integrated into WASH activities will have special attention for upholding the environmental sustainability. CB will integrate site improvement and construction will be implemented for the improvement of the WASH facilities. The community people will be motivated to protect the soil erosion through roadside plantation. The community people will be motivated not to do open defecation. They will also be made knowledgeable and motivated not to cut trees indiscriminately and to plant trees at their homesteads. Besides. All DRR activities will employ an Environmental Stewardship tool which is being developed under DRR Coordination group comprised by CRS, Caritas Australia, Cafod and Trocaire.
Finally, the project will support social cohesion through developed proactive communication strategies, including challenging negative stereotyping within and between communities. Through community engagement across all WASH components activities, sports events, outings, and welcome events, as part of wider strategies to promote community cohesion and community engagement"

1 - Identified Needs

Improved Water Services. Beneficiaries living in areas not yet covered by piped water networks continue to access water from tube-wells fitted with handpumps with quality surveillance showing that 90% of handpumps had zero coliforms. Although between 45% and 77% of the population reported accessing the piped networks, 19% reported having used negative coping mechanisms for water needs, with fetching water from a further source sighted as the main issue. Handpumps are still a major source of water for domestic use and bathing. Access to Sanitation. Latrine coverage at a camp level provides access at 1:20 in line with sphere standards, but there is a gap due to the high population density with a camp wide estimated at 8,500 latrines. Current functionality has an average of 92% and is a continual process for partners. Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence with 95% preferring private bathing spaces. Solid Waste produce daily in the targeted area, SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51 per cent of the refugee households are disposing of waste in the designated area and 16% reported disposing of waste direct to open space. Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement. Hygiene knowledge and access to soap remains high although behavior change remains an issue that requires further research to assess the impact of knowledge on hygiene habits. Access to MHM remains a barrier for many females through stigma, taboo and restrictive practices but also from a lack of MHM kit individuality due to common practices of one size fits all or variations of inter sector kit and insufficient coordination. Needs of host communities In August 2021 heavier than usual seasonal rains caused widespread flooding to the host community, resulting in a request for support from DPHE to assist in the assessment and the response. Approximately 23% of the HC population was affected by the floods, affecting both water and sanitation facilities. Data sets do not currently enable accurate evaluation of the overall WASH infrastructure within HC. Improved Water Services. Most households in the host communities (88 percent)2 access water from tube wells/handpumps, but only 77%2 of households are reporting having enough water for their domestic need (only 74%2 in Teknaf where groundwater is scarce.Access to Sanitation. In 2020, 63%2 of households have at least basic access to sanitation with an additional 7,000 constructed in 2021., however, 20% of the population reported using an unimproved sanitation facility. Regarding waste management, 44% reported not having a bin and 69% throwing household waste into the open.
