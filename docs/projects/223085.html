Project Summary

OCHA has been present in Mozambique with a Humanitarian Advisory Team (HAT) since. March 2019, following the landfall of and humanitarian response to cyclones Idai and Kenneth. Subsequently, OCHA HAT supported the coordination and response to a number of crises in the country, including the conflict and displacement in northern Mozambique, climatic shocks (tropical storms and cyclones, floods, etc.), etc. Over the past years, OCHA HAT continued to expand its presence in Mozambique and, effective January 2022 transitioned into a fully-fledged OCHA Mozambique Country Office (CO). In 2024, OCHA will be strengthening its pivotal role in galvanizing, coordinating, and supporting the overall humanitarian action in Mozambique, focusing on OCHA's main functions, i.e. coordination, advocacy, humanitarian financing and information management.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

1. Increased insecurity and the difficult operating environment may affect OCHA's ability to support coordination in locations where humanitarian needs are highest, hindering humanitarian access and protection of affected people. 2. Inadequate and delayed funding may undermine effective humanitarian response activities and attainment of the HRP's objectives. 3. The Government of Mozambique does not issue humanitarian visas for aid workers (UN agencies and INGOs) in a timely manner. OCHA will continue to monitor the humanitarian and security situation in the country, advocate for full humanitarian access throughout northern Mozambique and other parts of the country with partners, stakeholders, Government of Mozambique, etc. OCHA will advocate with key authorities for the timely issuance of visa for UN agencies and INGOs, to have a fit-for-purpose humanitarian team OCHA will support resource mobilization efforts to help ensure adequate funding of the 2025 HRP. Finally, OCHA will ensure that proper contingency planning is in place to enable a swift response by the humanitarian community to changes in the context and needs of affected people.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

OCHA has been placing women, adolescent girls and children in the communities at the center of the response, in accordance with collective commitments on Accountability to Affected People (AAP) and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). This effort contributes to the implementation of the IASC GEEWG policy and its accountability framework and will follow the guidance provided at the IASCGender Handbook for Humanitarian Action to integrate gender equality programming in humanitarian action. This includes ensuring effective, inclusive, adapted and transparent communication to enable informed decisions by affected communities; using feedback mechanisms to strengthen accountability and inform adjustments in the response; and providing meaningful opportunities for meaningful community participation in humanitarian action. The Linha Verde hotline, run by WFP on behalf of the humanitarian community, has been available as an inter-agency complaint and feedback mechanism, including on sensitive issues. The Mozambique PSEA Network has been serving as the primary body for coordination and oversight of activities related to PSEA mainstreaming by humanitarian personnel and will provide technical support to the clusters, agencies and partners involved in the response.
