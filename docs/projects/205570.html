Project Summary

In several regions of South Sudan, severe food insecurity has persisted, severely impacting the livelihoods of the local population. This crisis is most acute in areas classified as Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and it has worsened over the years in various counties, including Jonglei state (specifically Bor more specially Canal/Pigi, Ayod and Fangak), Lake's state (Cuiebet, and Rumbek North), and Central Equatorial (terekeka) Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East and North). The primary focus is on assisting vulnerable households, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and returnee communities with severe food and nutrition needs in these regions.
This ongoing crisis is a result of various factors, including Intensified sub-national violence has continued to result in the loss of life, destruction of property, population displacement, deliberate disruption of livelihoods, the loss of livestock, and has restricted access to food sources. Additionally, consecutive years of severe flooding have caused the loss of livestock, submerged human settlements, farmlands, and pasturelands, disrupting markets and the delivery of humanitarian aid.
The continuing macro-economic crisis caused by the depreciation of the local currency and the global impacts of the conflict in Ukraine, climatic shocks (floods and dry spells), conflict and insecurity, climate- and conflict-induced population displacements, persistent low agricultural production levels, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion that continue to erode households’ coping capacities, and the loss of livelihoods. The situation has been further exacerbated by infestations of fall armyworm and desert locusts, leading to reduced crop yields. The projected increase in conflict in pastoral areas poses a threat to food security and livelihoods.
In response to this ongoing emergency, Community Organization for Development and Empowerment (CODE), aims to improve food availability and enhance the resilience of vulnerable communities to future threats associated with large-scale displacements. Our project includes Promoting climate-smart agriculture which involves using agricultural practices that help to mitigate climate change and adapt to its impacts through, Agroforestry can help to improve soil health, reduce erosion, and sequester carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, intercropping as a practice of growing two or more crops in the same field at the same time. This can help to reduce the risk of crop failure if one crop is affected by pests, diseases, or extreme weather events. It can also help to improve soil fertility and reduce weed pressure, capacity-building for contact farmers and farmers' groups in conservation agriculture, and value chain addition to increase crop production coverage while minimizing costs. Farmers' groups will be organized and supported in the development of community food banks, gunny bag gardening, and kitchen gardens to enhance food security for vulnerable households. This will empower them to process farm produce and identify potential markets for their products.
We will collaborate with strategic partners to provide early maturing vegetable seeds and tools to farmers in the targeted areas, with the goal of restoring food security and expanding nutritional diversity. We will also support fishing activities to increase production capacity. Additionally, we plan to distribute fuel-efficient stoves and briquettes, support animal health in pastoral communities, and provide food assistance to reduce household food expenses and alleviate burdens on vulnerable groups, including the elderly, women, girls, people with disabilities, and child-headed households. We are committed to providing psychosocial support and protection against gender-based violence and abuse.
Throughout the project's implementation, CODE's Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) teams will conduct field visits and produce results.

1. Needs

In accordance with IPC (September 2023 - July 2024 projection). The most severely acute food insecure populations are in locations that have been significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation, high food prices), conflict and insecurity, low agricultural production, reduction in humanitarian assistance, and the effects of the ongoing conflict in Sudan. Between September and November 2023, 10 counties across the country and an estimated 56,000 (60%) returnees are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, 61 counties are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 8 counties are classified in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity. In the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2023 to March 2024, 18 counties and an estimated 84,000 (70%) returnees will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, 53 counties will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 8 counties will likely be in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity. In the lean-season projection period of April to July 2024, 38 counties and an estimated 98,000 returnees (75%) will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, 38 counties will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 3 counties will likely be in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity. In the current analysis period of September to November 2023, an estimated 5.83 million people (46% of the population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 1.64 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The levels of severe acute food insecurity in the country remain high, necessitating urgent scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent the total collapse of livelihoods in the affected counties, especially those with a high share of populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity as well as for South Sudanese returnees. The marginal improvements in the food security situation are mainly driven by relative calm and reduced intensity of climatic shocks, particularly flooding, in some locations across the country.In response to this crisis, Community Organization for Development and Empowerment (CODE) aims to implement the project to improve food availability and enhance community resilience. The project will include promoting climate-smart agriculture practices, such as agroforestry, intercropping, and conservation agriculture. Community Organization for Development and Empowerment will also work on value chain addition to increase crop production efficiently. Farmers' groups are being organized to develop community food banks, gunny bag gardening, and kitchen gardens to enhance food security.Community Organization for Development and Empowerment will collaborate with partners to provide inputs like early maturing vegetable seeds and tools to farmers, aiming to restore food security and nutritional diversification. Additionally, CODE will support the pastoral communities with fuel-efficient stoves, animal health, and food assistance to alleviate food expenses.The organization will also address the needs of vulnerable groups, including the elderly, women, girls, people with disabilities, and child-headed households, by providing psychosocial support and protection against gender-based violence.During project implementation, CODE’S Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) teams will conduct field visits and progress reports in various locations to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of their interventions. These areas include Kapoeta East, Lafon, Cuibet, Pibor, Fangak, Ayod, Canal/Pigi, and Leer.

2. Project activities

• Identification, registration, and Verification of 20,000 vulnerable HHs amongst IDPs, returnees and host communities as primary beneficiaries of the project in the project locations in consultation with other stakeholders like the community leaders and the local authorities. • Distribution of vegetable kits to 2,000 HH vulnerable (1 standard Kits - 180g/HH) -based on FAO technical standards in the areas of Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East and Lafon Counties).; Rapid post distribution monitoring conducted for the household receiving kits.• Rapid training of 2,000 beneficiaries on vegetable farming in Jonglei, Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria states; Support Contact farmer’s feed disseminate key production information and submit reports.• Training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) and community-based extension service providers (CBESP).• Provision of water pumps, Irrigation training/support.• Ox Ploughs & training.• Maintain support to small scale subsistence producers in locations with less agricultural potential and include animal health support. This should also include emergency nutrition, especially during the lean season.• 150 Self-selected producer groups formed as a platform for transferring improved agricultural technologies and making stronger market linkages. Agricultural training modules include social cohesion, gender, and conflict and nutrition sensitivity.• Recruitment of 20 Field Extension Agents (FEAs) equipped with modern agricultural education and skills.• Training of Community Based trainers (CBTs) as TOT on VSLAs.• Cascade training of community beneficiary groups on VSLAs.• Provide flood adaptive seeds and approaches (rice, floating gardens), tools and fishing inputs to farmers. • Pre-position grain and seed protection kits (hermetic bags, tilted buckets, grain bag etc.).• Monitoring of small enterprises and support for small startups to strengthen markets.• Support sensitization for 500 Pregnant, breastfeeding mothers and caregivers living with under 5 to address barriers to good nutrition during the monthly feedback meetings. • The project will support vulnerable households with food supplies and severely malnourished will be linked for/supported with food by prescription.• Setting up of 10 mobile Growth Monitoring out post, cooking demonstration and education on complementary feeding using commonly available foods. • Sensitize and Link youth, women and adult GBV survivors on Development of IGA’s. • Scale-up provision of humanitarian assistance (food and Non-food items) to counties in Crisis (IPC Phase 4 and 5). • Adopt revised global guidance on population-based assessments so that real time data is available on the nutrition situation.• Conduct assessment and review existing early action protocol (EAP) • Provision of water pumps, Irrigation training/support.• Good Agronomic practices (e.g. farmer field schools, Demo plots, post-harvest management etc). • Monitoring and Evaluation, this activity will be undertaken throughout the project. The MEAL team will conduct field visits throughout the project to evaluate the progress.Cross-cutting Activities In this project, CODE will work closely with local community leaders, authorities, women, people with disabilities, and displaced community leaders to conduct assessments. The aim is to gather valuable insights and ensure that the voices of these community members are heard and considered.Additionally, the project places a strong emphasis on mainstreaming Gender-Based Violence (GBV) prevention and implementing the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) measures. This is essential to protect vulnerable individuals from all forms of abuse at every stage of the response. The project aims to expand and enhance access to essential services, including clean water, sanitation, and healthcare services, all year round. This will contribute to the well-being and overall quality of life for the communities in need.
