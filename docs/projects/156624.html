Project Summary

The project aims at supporting students with non-formal education to bridge the gap that was formed during the crisis, support children under 5years with early childhood education, support teachers and education staff through providing them with capacity building training, in addition to raise awareness among children and parents about the importance of education
Activities will be implemented in 3 static centers in East Ghota (Saqba, Hamouria, Ein Tarma) which are under the sub district Kafar batna.
Activities will target students (boys and girls), parents, in addition to encouraging students with disability to be part of the program.
Monitoring mechanism will include pre and post assessments in addition to calculating beneficiaries disaggregated by age and gender.

Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria, now in its eighth year, has forced an estimated 2.1 million, over one third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (including more than 100,000 Palestine refugee children) and over 120,000 teachers are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 per cent of whom are in acute and immediate need (WoS Education Severity Needs Analysis, 2018). Teaching capacity remains overstretched. Over 140,000 teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts. ( Education Management Information System data, Ministry of Education, 2017-18 academic year). Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor. More than 40 per cent of the out-of-school children in Syria are between 15 and 17 years of age and have differentiated learning, skills development and livelihoods needs (No Lost Generation Initiative, 2018). The escalation of violence has led to increasing war related psychological trauma for children, their families and education personnel. Exposure to violence decreases attendance, leads to drop out, and affects learning outcomes by limiting concentration. In some areas, over half of children with a disability have an unmet need for education, and over two thirds require specialized health services which are not available in their area (Physical Rehabilitation Working Group, 2018). The changes in control in central and south Syria in early and mid-2018 caused new waves of civilian displacement into Rural Damascus. In public schools in locations in Rural Damascus, Quneitra and Daraa, classroom sizes reached 150 students per teacher in September 2018 with an average of 48:1 pupil teacher ratio ( Ministry of Education, 2018). Teachers face diverse and complex learning needs within their classrooms as children’s age and level of education attainment greatly varies. During AlTamayoz work in East Ghota- Rural Damascus (Kafarbatna, Saqba, Hamouria, Ein tarma) from June to December 2018, many needs have been addressed in education perspective, children in Elementary education need more support as 60% of students in this stage are out of school comparing with 28% in the primary stage. The need to re-enroll students in school is important for both boys and girls (dropping out of school ratio is 74% among boys and 36% among girls) this high ratio among boys is due to poverty that forced boys to work in hard conditions to support their families with basic needs especially food, and sometimes due to the disability of the child because of some injuries. The percentage of elderly has noticeably increased in Rural Damascus with the increase also in the number of women house-headed families which increase the need to depend on children as a resource for income. Children not in school are at higher risk of sexual harassment, exploitation and exposure to violence, child labor, and early marriage. (AlTamayoz observation) Many schools in Rural Damascus deficit the adequate number of teachers especially in languages and information technology subjects (AlTamayoz observation). Children are often learning without textbooks and/or learning materials, in very poor learning spaces. Teachers and students suffer from stress and psychosocial disorders and require support to address their own and their students’ psychosocial support needs. Non-formal education opportunities and pathways back to inclusive education at age appropriate levels are increasingly required to address the complex learning needs of children, including early childhood education, and youth who have had years of disrupted education, and children with specific physical and psychological needs. (HRP2019). It is critical to improve the access to quality education services for returnees and over-burdened community in Eastern Ghota and to expand access to PSS services
