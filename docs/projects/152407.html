Project Summary

Nasir and Ulang counties in Upper Nile state are among the most affected by the war since 2013. Continuous clashes by warring parties in the area, and in Jonglei have led the two counties to play temporary home to a huge population of displaced persons. Their geographical place of being the route to Ethiopia camps make them the vulnerable counties in this war. Recent displacement from fighting in Nasir and subsequent displacement from fighting by warrying parties in Uror and Nyirol Counties, Jonglei state has further worsened the situation of IDPs in these areas.
An IRNI assessment, conducted in May 2018 in Ulang and Nasir, by humanitarian teams that included TADO/SSUDA/IOM and other non-NFI partners exposed a huge need in the population assessed. The needs found among the population included: a)Lack of shelter for newly arrived returnees and IDPs b) Dilapidated shelter for IDPs, Returnees and host community C) Lack of blankets e) Lack of mosquito nets f) Lack of Water cans/pots to fetch water g) Lack of cooking utensils and h) Lack of dignity kits. An intervention conducted by SSUDA/IOM and TADO was effective in mitigating the immediate situation then but there is still a huge unmet need. The signing of the latest peace agreement in August 2018, has brought about hope among refugees and IDPs. This is likely to lead to an upsurge in population in the two counties in form of returnees.

With these real needs, this project would support provision of and rehabilitation of emergency shelter and life-saving Non-Food-Items (NFIs) to vulnerable populations in Ulang & Nasir Counties in Upper Nile State. The core of this intervention is to improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs. The project also responds to urgent, demonstrated needs among the target 6000 beneficiaries. The intervention will involve continuous assessment and monitoring of the situation and will adopt an integrated approach, informed by and collaborating with other clusters’ interventions. This will improve protection for displaced persons as their access to emergency shelter protects them from harsh weather conditions and other protection risks. Further, the intervention supports communities in a dignified, integrated and coordinated manner by using local materials to support livelihoods through skills training, using skilled and unskilled labor. TADO will work closely with SSUDA the state focal point organization on S/NFIs.
TADO will pursue the following cluster objectives:
1) Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced or vulnerable populations in secondary displacement, and
2) Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs.
The project will employ the following strategies/activities in order to contribute to the foregoing objectives:
a) Provide access to emergency shelter (in-kind)
Target IDPs and returnees that have either newly arrived or missed out completely on past interventions
b) Provide access to life-saving NFIs (in-kind)
Beneficiaries will be identified and mapped, priority to pregnant and lactating mothers, the elderly and the disabled. Households with young children or child-led households and unaccompanied minors will be prioritized.
c) Shelter upgrades (in kind)
TADO in collaboration with local project implementation committees will identify beneficiaries whose shelters are dilapidated and/or inadequate due to emerging needs and prioritize upgrades to ensure that they continue being protected from harsh weather and other protection risks.
d) Replenish essentials NFI and emergency shelter
Beneficiaries whose NFI supplies are depleted or otherwise missing will be targeted for replenishment to ensure that they have access to a life of dignity and protection.
e) Engage the community in a way that stimulates active participation
Programming will continuously seek to engage beneficiaries and other stakeholders in order to build resilience to recurrent shocks. Beneficiaries will be given skills to enable them to cope and help each other and promote peaceful co-existence.
f) Mainstream HLP and conflict sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response
TADO will ensure that HLP and peacebuilding messages are integrated into the interventions. Participants will be given capacity through training and dialogue meetings to take an active role on the matters that affect them
g) Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response
Beneficiaries will be aided and given training and to ensure that they participate in the protection process. Community-led initiatives will be encouraged and supported so that the beneficiaries can embrace gender equality and put in place mitigation measures against GBV.
TADO has over 5 years of hands-on programming experience in the Upper Nile State in the field of Education in Emergency and Peacebuilding. The organization has an intimate knowledge and understanding of the needs of the region and has thus far established excellent working relationships with the local community and the county government authorities. With a field office in Ulang, TADO is a formidable partner in the region with a vast network and infrastructure to support project implementation. TADO has 7 national staffs including S/NFI team leader and focal point based in Ulang field office with a good understanding of S/NFIs implementation and well experienced and always involved during the S/NFIs needs assessment in collaboration with ROSS and other partners in the field.
The unstable security context of Upper Nile State necessitates flexible responses; and in Ulang and Nasir counties, TADO has adapted to deliver quick implementation projects to continue to provide lifesaving interventions while limiting the time staff spends away from a permanent field base. The targeted counties continue to face high levels of displacement and intermittent conflict, with limited access to key services and infrastructure. Simultaneously, skyrocketing inflation has negatively influenced the ability of households to purchase basic goods.

Needs

Upper Nile State (UNS) is one of the three states most severely affected by the ongoing conflict in South Sudan. Sustained fighting among the warrying parties in February 2018 in areas around Nyatot, Manding and Nor in Nasir have caused huge displacement in the County with population either scattered to interior villages within the state, e.g Bitutgoon and wechbuoni in Ulang and many relatively safe villages in Nasir county such as Jikmir, Kier and some as far as kiechkuon. Many decided to stay back because they did not want to subject their children to dire situation in the camps in Ethiopia. The March 2018 fighting in Uror, Niyirol and Akobo Counties also forced huge populations, mainly women, children and elderly to look for safety in relatively safe villages in Ulang and Nasir counties. The life threatening threats from conflict of gunfire coupled with destruction of shelter and food by the criminals from communal feuds exposes huge displaced population that is mainly of young children, elderly and mothers.These threats and incidences became a major concern for the humanitarian actors in Ulang and Nasir counties. On 12th April 2018, the Education/NFIs partners in Ulang county (CMD, SSUDA, IOM, TADO, ADRA and UNICEF) conducted a Rapid assessment in consultation with National Education and S/NFIs Clusters to assess the condition of the crisis and its impact especially on education, but not leaving out S/NFI sections as the first and urgent priority for IDPs. Key county institutions such as the County Education Department, Relief Organization of South Sudan (ROSS—the relief wing for the SPLM-IO) participated in the needs assessment. Huge gaps exist in the provision of shelter and Non-food items to vulnerable communities in both Ulang and Nasir counties. According to REACH Assessment Report January-March 2018 and South Sudan Humanitarian Dashboard Report of 31 August 2018, Ulang and Nasir counties of Upper Nile State are characterized by episodes of insecurity which led to displacement of over a huge number of people into Ulang and Nasir counties due to fighting in areas around Waat between the SPLA Government and SPLA –IO Forces, Inter-community conflict following revenge attacks between Lou Nuer community from Nyirol county of Jonglei state and Dome of Ulang county from Upper Nile state. Besides that, an attack in Wichbuoni and Nyatot villages of Nasir by government in February 2018 led to a significant displacement of people to Kewer, Mandeng and Jikmir payams where they settled temporarily with the host communities. The hope to return to their homes is high fueled by expectations from the recently signed Peace Accord by South Sudan warring parties in Addis Ababa Ethiopia on 12 August 2018. Huge need in term of provision of shelter to support reintegration of returnees, WASH, Health and other lifesaving services are real.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

TADO has in place a complaints/feedback handling mechanism that recognizes the importance of the process and the confidentiality requiredComplaints are categorized in terms of severity and handled appropriately.Minor complaints are acknowledged and easily and promptly resolved. Most of these pose minimal risk to the organization and beneficiaries.Most significant complaints are acknowledged, an initial assessment is done, an investigation follows and the appropriate remedial action taken. TADO adheres to set standards as follows: TADO’s handling of complaints will meet the following minimum standards: 1. All complaints will be acknowledged as soon as possible, and ideally within 5 working days by the recipient. All complainants will receive a copy of our policy. 2. All complainants will receive a response giving the outcome of their complaint as soon as possible and ideally within thirty working days of receipt. If the matter is more complex a further acknowledgement will be sent explaining what further investigation is required to resolve the complaint and the likely timeframe. 3. All complaints should be recorded on a Complaints Record Form. 4. All complainants will be treated respectfully, whether it is felt the complaint is justifiedor not. A beneficiary committee is in place that collaborates with project staff and local stakeholdersto receive and handle complaints. Beneficiaries can also send their complaints or feedback directly to the Complaints Handling focal person, who also visits beneficiaries to listen and receive feedback Specific proceduresTADO also ensures the participation of beneficiary representative at the project review stageTADO has Feedback/complaint mechanisms tools to measure and collect information on the project progress from beneficiaries and community stakeholders

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project targets each segment of the population differently depending on the needs identified. Women and girls are generally affected disproportionately to men and boys and this is taken into account when designing protection interventions. For instance, Women & young girls walking long distances to access water or forage for food are exposed to greater risk of abduction and SGBV compared to men and boys. Protection interventions must be alive to such realities. Further, the aged and disabled are more vulnerable. Interventions are informed by risk analysis that takes into account gender, age and cultural practices that increase or reduce vulnerability to risk This project is design to consider protection of the beneficiaries from all forms of risks associated with its implementation. This would be and ensuring information acquired from a beneficiary would be kept confidential and could only be released to the third party at the consent of the originator. Parties would be acquired to adhere to rule Before any intervention carried out, TADO in collaboration with Protection clusters/partners on the ground and the local authority will make sure the areas are safe from any danger to the populations/beneficiaries and environment for successful implementation of the project

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is aware that certain cultural practices and customs may pose a barrier to access and participation of beneficiaries. The project ensures that there is a buy-in process that involves consultations with community gatekeepers such as elders, religious leaders and local government authorities. Consent for access is sought for and agreed upon prior to project implementation. It is part and parcel of the entry strategies that the beneficiary project committee develops and implements. Further, the participation and collaboration of community stakeholders who are opinion leaders lends credibility and promotes ownership of the project interventions. Further, hard to reach people such as the elderly, people with disabilities and pastoralists will be targeted with flexible activities that can easily adapt to their specific situations, the building of technical capacity is targeted at local actors/service providers further building the resource base of the community to respond and make access easier. Beneficiaries are selected by need and need only. All stakeholders are represented at the decision-making body Some locations/villages are far from town centres hence walking is necessary, but since TADO is a community organization and employees are from the local area, it is relatively acceptable to walk on foot with help of local authority in the areas to bridge the barriers for access
