Project Summary

The project seeks to deliver quality life saving and life sustaining, inclusive and gender responsive interventions to 1110,000 IDPs Returnees and populations whom affected by ongoing Conflict , across Khartiom White Nile ,Aljazirah and East Darfur State. This will be achieved through the following thematic areas;
• Provide crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to basic water services.
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to basic/limited sanitation services
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to hygiene services
• Provide Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) affected population access to minimum WASH for Nutrition package
• Provide outbreaks affected and ‘at-risk’ population access to minimum WASH for Health package
The response targets five localities in East Darfur ( Aubjabra , Bahr Al Arab, Alsalaya, Aublarinka Sharia , Yassin ,Khartoum Aljaziarh and White Nile ),

1 - Needs

The Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene situation (WASH) is deteriorating due to worsening economic crisis, political instability, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, a five-fold increase in operation and maintenance cost of water infrastructures, insufficient revenue collections, poor budget allocation by the Government, and limited external investments. Most affected WASH population groups are IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable populations battling food insecurity and extreme poverty who cannot afford WASH services and practices As per the HNO 2023, 10.9 million people (excluding Refugees) who fall under the above categories need humanitarian WASH. Floods and flash floods continue to impact various states in Sudan, resulting in an increasing number of casualties and damage. According to the authorities and humanitarian organisations, as of 15 August, 52 people have died, more than 8,900 houses destroyed and another 20,600 damaged in 12 states. About 136,000 people have been affected since May 2022. The floods have also affected 238 health facilities, more than 1,500 water sources and damaged or washed away 1,500 latrines. conflict especially in the Darfur region led to displacement of people from their homes to rescind in IDPs. . rain and flooding were forced to shelter with relatives and move to government buildings. This put more pressure in an already bad WASH situation in those areas. These multiple shocks severely impacted livelihoods, escalating the vulnerabilities of the crisis affected populations. This response project targets 4 localities with a severity ranking of mainly 4-5 in East Darfur state where the bulk of the people in need rescind. The state was in protracted conflict for about 17 years and therefore also host majority of IDPs in Sudan. According to 2023 HNO Around 74% of the population access basic water services from improved water sources and with a collection time less than 30 minutes. However, MSNA 2022 results indicate that 77% of the population have access to improved water services from improved water sources without factoring the collection time. About 28% of the water sources do not have sufficient water, and 25% of the water sources are not functioning. The increase (5 times especially the time those water yards were constructed, and community contribution were determine) in fuel prices means that fuel reliant water yards cannot be maintained as it is beyond the affordability of the affected people. Sanitation coverage in Sudan continues to be amongst the worst in the MENA region. Over 30 percent of the population have access to basic sanitation with prevalent open defecation practices at 33.3%. which is especially concerning for women and girls. Hand washing with soap (as the main indicator for hygienic practices) is estimated at 14.3%. There is an increase in the number of people with access to soap and water, because of the COVID 19 campaigns in the last two years According to the HNO 2022 vulnerability mapping, less than 7.5% of households in majority of the targeted localities in these four states have access to hand washing with soap and water. Similarly, less than 7.5% of households in targeted areas of East Darfur .Access to basic water services as better but still low ranging between 35-65% across the targeted localities in the state. Status of WASH in schools was also found to be stressed or severe in these localities according to a study done by the WASH cluster. Access to dignity kits among adolescent girls in IDPs and other remote and hard to reach areas is a major challenge which often forces to use old pieces of cloth, sometimes leading to infections

2 - Activities/Outputs

Through providing timely, sustainable, and equitable access to safe WASH services, and through pairing WASH programming with Health and Nutrition interventions, WASH sector activities aims to end the cycle of diarrheal diseases and control health outbreaks The proposed WASH interventions will target affected populations comprising of IDPs, refugees, returnees as well as host communities. Sphere standards will be applied in the design and implementation of the proposed interventions to ensure that the target program participants receive assistance that meets the set humanitarian standards. Sudan WASH sector standards will also be followed to the extent possible, within the context of emergency response. Water safety will be a high priority for this project. Detailed water quality analysis will be conducted for all sources identified for rehabilitation as well as the newly developed sources to ensure that the water availed to beneficiary meets WHO drinking water standards or at the minimum the country standards as may be applicable. A specific WASH Needs assessment was not done for this proposal but recent secondary data was relied upon, like the 2023 Humanitarians Needs Overview data and this was collaborated with Sudan’s 2018 S3M11 data and the country’s SDG 6 plan of 2019. Robust Free Residual Chlorine monitoring will be maintained from the source, drawing points, health care facilities and household level. Below is the FRC monitoring plan for the various contaminants at different supply points.Close supervision of water supply infrastructure will be done by ARCO Engineers in collaboration with Government Engineers to ensure strict adherence to specifications to achieve the expected standard of workmanship. WASH committees will be established or reactivated and given a proper orientation on operation and maintenance of the established/rehabilitated/upgraded water supply infrastructure to ensure safety of users and sustainable operation and management of the facilities.Proposed water points will be located in secure places to ensure they are safely accessible, especially for women, children, the elderly, and those living with disabilities. Hand pumps are management by WASH committees while the mechanized boreholes are largely under the management of the state water corporation/WES in collaboration with WASH committees. African Relief Committee will support in replacement of damaged parts like submersible pump, diesel generator for power back up, and pipe fittings. Detailed Water quality analysis will be done before rehabilitation of targeted water projects. Once rehabilitated, monitoring of Free Residual Chlorine will be maintained at key supply points. This has been incorporated in the Technical design section.The Government of Sudan declared CLTS as the recommended approach for improving access to sanitation in the country and discouraged partners from facilitating any form of subsidies except for rapid onset emergency response situations. This is in line with The Ministry of Health CLTS road map and National Sanitation Strategic Framework that outlines the Government’s plan towards an open defecation free Sudan. ARCO will therefore use CLTS as one of the approaches to help reverse the low sanitation coverage and the associated negative impacts in health status within the target communities. African Relief Committee will also work with the local administrative authorities to improve solid waste management, which if not properly handled contributes to pollution of water sources and general environmental degradation. The project intends to take advantage of the current environment where people are more alert and receptive to hygiene messaging, to facilitate a mix of approaches aimed at influencing mind set change towards good hygiene practices. The project plans to work with influential change agents like faith leaders, local opinion leaders and community health volunteers to trigger behaviour change through the Nurturing Care

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Uncoordinated use of natural resources can have detrimental impacts on the environment further contributing to climate change. Climate change has become one of the major issues threatening the sustainability of WASH Infrastructure development in the world. Changes to weather patterns, including the reliability and predictability of seasonal rainfall and the impacts of extreme events, now place unprecedented pressures on water resources, especially in flood- and drought-prone contexts like Sudan. Climate change threatens water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities and services, as these are intimately linked to the water cycle and are vulnerable to changes in the quantity and quality of available water resources. Floods and droughts, which pollute and reduce water delivery respectively, have now become a perennial issue to deal with in Sudan. We witnessed the destruction of WASH infrastructure when flash-floods were experienced across many states in Sudan in recent months. One of the Five Principles of Sustainable WASH is the element of environmental sustainability which entails placing WASH interventions in the wider context of the natural environment and implementing an approach of integrated and sustainable management of water resources. This is the approach proposed to be used in this response, to protect watersheds and over-exploitation of ground water. Through IWRM, ARCO will ensure harmonization of water use with land use to enhance the resilience of water and sanitation services to disasters as an effective strategy to reduce disaster impact, enhance ecosystem services, minimize pollution hazard, minimize over- pumping of groundwater aquifers, promote the sustainable use of natural resources for WASH infrastructure, and source materials as sustainably as possible. Borehole sitting will be done through geophysical surveys accompanied with Environmental and Social Impact assessments to ensure correct siting. Through a participatory approach with the target beneficiaries, potential environmental impacts from proposed interventions will be identified during ESIA processes and mitigation plans developed, implemented and monitored. Green energy solutions like solar pumping and gravity schemes will be optimized as much as possible. Ground recharge technologies like sand dams will be adopted where feasible. Use of locally available appropriate materials will also be promoted for sanitation facilities as opposed to transported brick and mortar

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be mainstreamed into planned activities. The project will ensure that each activity will not expose vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout all the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be linked to protection agencies (Government entities or other organizations dealing with protection issues) to prevent exposure from further harm. To possible extent, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults. The project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups (like minority groups, PLWD, among others) within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. Selection of villages will be done in consultation with line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNFPA, department of welfare in the Ministry of social welfare to ensure needs of people with special needs are taken care of. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and ARCO staffs is not negotiable and project will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, ARCO staf or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While the project will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. Discussion will be held to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including women and children. To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staff will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries. Sanitation facilities in schools will ensure gender segregation, be located in well lit safely accessible spaces especially for girls and be designed and constructed to offer safe and private dignified access to boys and girls. Care givers will be educated to ensure that children only carry age appropriate jerricans when they have to help in fetching water.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to beneficiaries will be two-fold; to direct beneficiary groups and accountability to host community leadership. Accountability to direct beneficiaries will be through transparent engagement, communication and dialogue sessions with group members and regular joint review of progress. Accountability to host community leadership will be through MoUs that ARCO represented by ‘Village Focal Points’ - will sign with community representatives. The MoUs will spell out the roles of the organization as well as the role and contribution of the communities. In addition to being accountability tools; MoUs enable ARCO to communicate decisions such as criteria for selection of beneficiaries and project locations. MoU are reference points for issues that arise during implementation. MoUs have enabled ARCO hold communities accountable for commitments they make at the onset of projects. Participation: This program will be designed in consultation with IDP and host communities leadership and government entities. In addition to regular consultation as part of ongoing interventions, IDP , host communities and government leaders were informed of this project and involved in selecting beneficiaries and prioritizing interventions. At the project onset, kickoff meetings at the village level will be facilitated by project staff to further publicize the program and communicate opportunities for participation. Feedback mechanisms: The program will institute feedback mechanisms such as ‘suggestion boxes’; quarterly feedback forums and ‘hotlines’ where feasible. The ‘hotline’ approach has been piloted successfully in Darfur. The project staff will be tasked with mobilizing communities – particularly women – to participate and will be responsible for investigating factors that hamper participation. These factors will have come through normal feedback mechanisms and / or through or discovered through interactions. The project staff will work towards ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements, ensuring that have timely access to relevant and clear information and they are satisfied with the opportunities they have to influence the response. The program staff will consult with beneficiary communities’ on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. Program staff Welcome and accept complaints, and communicate how the mechanism can be accessed and the scope of issues it can address. The complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages
