Project Summary

Decades of conflict and insecurity, drought and massive displacement in the Southern Somalia have placed women and girls in extreme vulnerability and increased risk of gender-based violence (GBV). In response to the growing humanitarian crisis in southern Somalia, SOS Children villages is proposing the following interventions to response to the extreme vulnerability that have placed girls and women in increased risk of gender based violence (GBV) in middle Shabelle, and Bay, and Bakool. While the humanitarian response are attending to the affected population, external shocks limits the population access to basic services increasing levels of vulnerability. As a result women and girls remain high vulnerable and are forced to adopt risky and sometimes harmful coping strategies, including collection and sale of firewood and early marriage of young girls for exchanges of money. In addition, Findings from multi-sectoral safety audits in 2019 indicate specific GBV risks for women and girls such as sexual harassment and rape are mainly caused by inadequate fencing of IDP camps, poor shelters constructed with inappropriate and light housing materials (e.g. dump cloths, plastic sheets and basic containers), poor lighting, lack of private spaces for sleeping, over-crowding, and faulty solar lanterns and torches. Further more in such situations, person with disabilities face extremely hurdles in being recognized and accessing resources and remain highly vulnerable with gender based violence particulary women and girls with disability.
Considering the complexity of the operating environment, SOS propose an intervention that will employ multifaceted approached that are tailored to each location. This include. Women and girls GBV survivors are provided with timely, confidential and comprehensive case management and medical response in target districts of Jowhar, Balcad and Hudur . The proposed project will also provide trauma counselling and PSS services to traumatized GBV survivors. In addition, the project will provide capacity building to health workers in case management and clinical management of rape survivors. The project will also Provide life saving material assistance (dignity kits, shelter kits and transport cost) to GBV survivors based on individual assessments by GBV case workers. Provision of life saving material assistance refers also to emergency food (rice, flour, sugar and oil) for GBV survivors (based on the IASC guidelines on MH and Psychosocial support in emergency settings).
SOSCV has the second largest referral hospital and this proposed intervention will establish referral pathway to GBV survivors for medial and health services. The proposed project will establish one stop center at target district hospital where GBV survivors would be provided PSS and counselling services.
Communities, authorities and humanitarian actors lack awareness of GBV risks and consequences and have limited practical involvement for GBV prevention and response. Therefore the project will also provide awareness creation Non-GBV humanitarian actors across all sectors, and the government authorities in the knowledge and practice of prevention and response of GBV.
Further more in such situations, person with disabilities face extremely hurdles in being recognized and accessing resources and remain highly vulnerable in with gender based violence. This is particularly true for children and women with disability. The project will ensure person with disabilities particularly women with disabilities are provided protections assistance and GBV support services to the survivors.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as aresult of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.Mitigation - Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiariessufficient time to travel home before nightfall. - Identify central locations - beneficiariesshould not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to accessdistribution points. - Avoid placing distribution points near military installations orthreats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers should notbe present inside distribution points. - Consider the frequency of distributions – thegreater the time between distributions the larger the quantities distributed, which putsbeneficiaries at risk of attacks and pillage. - Ensure the ration size is appropriate forthe beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financialburden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them. 2. Risk of women,girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse(SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Mitigation -Consider separate waitingareas for males and females. -Have a proportionate number of female health staff. Iffemale doctors/nurses are not available, consider a female doctor rotation betweenlocations. In this case, women must be adequately informed of which days a femaledoctor will be available. -Employ health staff with skills and experiences working withchildren - Install lights outside and on the access route to the facility 5. Risk thatmarginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do notparticipate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or areexcluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures -Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and preventdiscrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups -Identify individuals who lackdocumentation to prevent exclusion from services. -promote active participation andconsultation with target beneficiates including women, girls and disable personsduring project planning, implementing and monitoring. - Provide information aboutservices in both verbal and written form -Make facilities and infrastructure disability friendly.
