Project Summary

The spread of COVID-19 and associated preventive measures have led to increase in
vulnerability of IDPs, especially with regard exercising their HLP rights. Yet IDPs,
especially female-headed households are already exposed to evictions, poor housing
condition among other risks. The proposed intervention seeks to provide assistance
through: eviction monitoring, mitigation/response through cash for rent and negotiations,
and advocacy.

1 - Needs

The pattern of displacement in North-East of Nigeria shows that in the BAY states, 60% ofIDP’s reside in informal settlements, while 40 percent reside within the host communities.IDP’s in areas of displacement and return have continued to encounter obstacles limitingthem from exercising their HLP rights. The lack of fulfilment of HLP rights; particularly theright to adequate housing including security of tenure has exposed individuals especiallywomen to several protection risks. In 2019, the HRP identifies 50,000 individuals in needof Legal advice and services, including on housing, land and property. Only 6,256 wasreached in 2019. Theeviction monitoring exercises conducted by the sub sector between January to April 2020indicates about 1460 individuals spread across MMM, Jere, Mobbar, Gwoza LGA of Bornostate and Mubi, Yola in Adamawa States were at risk of eviction. More threats are anticipated on grounds of land-owners’intention to use land for personal use while others attributed to inability to pay rent.Although the current covid-19 situation has limited service delivery as access constraintsare also encountered, it’s negative financial impact has further aggravated the sufferingof the persons of concern who tend to rely on humanitarian interventions, thus makingthem self-reliant and resorting to negative coping mechanisms to fend for themselves inthe midst of limited resources and opportunities within their host communities. The subsector’sinteraction with target beneficiaries indicates that they are unable to carry outtheir daily labour/menial jobs because of the lock down and other Covid-19 preventivemeasures laid down by the authorities. This has resulted in their inability to generatemoney to enable them pay their rental sum on their own and thus making them morevulnerable to rely on humanitarian aid. With the financial requirements, the HLP subsector will provide timely HLP legal assistance services to enable vulnerable HH’S enjoyHLP rights and prevent risks of exploitation and abuse especially for female headedhousehold who are often more vulnerable to the negative effects of lack of adequatehousing. This will also aid in curtailing the chances of contacting and spreading the virusas the would have a roof over their heads to observe the preventive measures.

2 - Activities

In pursuit of that understanding, the sub-sector will implement the following measures: •Regular eviction monitoring exercise will be conducted to observe compliance withtenancy rights and obligations as well as monitoring of threats or actual evictions toprovide timely response to halt evictions. • Eviction mitigation measures will be takenthrough interest-based negotiations with land owners who are now demanding forpayment of rent, as well as incentives for the use of their land. Being able to respond tothis emerging crisis will require the HLP Sector to deploy additional resources in order toaddress the massive, unprecedented need of protecting HLP rights through the provisionof legal assistance, counselling cash-for-rent and rehabilitation-for-rent in a bid tostabilize the most vulnerable IDPs for a period of time. • Follow ups will be conductedwith traditional leaders within the informal settlements and the host communities toensure that HLP disputes are resolved amicably with preference for collaborative disputeresolution structures.
