Project Summary

Action Africa Help International (AAH-I) is a regional organization that supports livelihood-challenged communities in East and Southern Africa to sustainably improve their well-being and standard of living. With Country Programmes in South Sudan, Uganda, Zambia, Somalia, and Kenya, AAH-I has over 35 years' experience working in South Sudan during the civil war, peace transition years and in the new era of independent South Sudan. AAH’s project interventions include primary health care services, humanitarian logistics, food and income security interventions, education, water supply and sanitation as well as capacity building Programmes for civil society strengthening and peaceful reintegration.
AAH-I is credited for setting up the first County Health Departments in Greater Maridi, Greater Mundri West & Greater Yei counties during the liberation struggle for South Sudan. Over the years of AAH-I implemented numerous health projects funded by various donors including the following: The USAID funded Sudan Health Transformation Project (SHTP1 and 2); USAID funded Integrated Service Delivery Program (ISDP) through Jhpiego; the MDTF (Multi-Donor Trust Fund) funded Basic Package of Health Services project; the Health Pooled Fund (HPF 1 and 2) bridging project and UNICEF Emergency Response Project among others.
Currently AAH-I is implementing a two years (2020-2022) Regional Primary Healthcare Programme (RPHC) in Ibba, Maridi, Morobo & Yei Counties of Western & Central Equatoria State with funding from BftW-PADD (Bread for the World). The RPHC programs included MNCH, nutrition, WASH, Family Planning, and malaria prevention community-based health care services. The project also supports operations and incentives for staff of Ibba Primary Health Care Centre; Maridi Nurses and Midwives training school. The overall objective of the RPHC project is to improve the health status of communities in South Sudan.
The RPHC projects focused on improving health status of communities through: (a) Enhancing maternal, new-born and child health (MNCH) services in targeted communities. (b) Reduce morbidity and mortality associated to childhood preventable diseases; and (c) strengthening community health structures’ resilience, recovery and coping mechanisms. The RPHC project ends by May 2022. However, the strategic objectives (SO 1) and specific objectives 1.3; 1.4 3.3 and 3.4 of the SSHRP 2022 health cluster are in line with objectives of the RPHC. Therefore, the is project is envisioned to bridge gap and ensure health services in the targeted counties are not interrupted for the next 12 months (June 2022 – May 2023).

1 - Needs

The two years’ Regional Primary Health Care Project (2020–2022) funded by BftW ends by May 2022. The RPHC runs MNCH, nutrition, WASH, Family Planning, and malaria prevention community-based health care services in Yei River, Morobo, Maridi and Ibba counties. The activities under this project include: Medical camps and mobile clinics, basic training on MNCH, HIV/AIDS, STIs, and nutrition for Boma Health Committees (BHCs) and Boma Health Workers (BHWs); support to 35 primary and secondary school clubs with sexual, reproductive, and adolescent health commodities and awareness; provision of supplies, operation funds and incentives of Ibba Facility Theatre; strengthening referral systems from the community to health facilities at County to State Hospitals; conducting health education outreaches on MNCH and vitamin A supplementation; support health cluster monthly and quarterly performance review meetings; community awareness in water, sanitation, hygiene (WASH); production of behavior change communication; joint supportive supervision; and incentives for Boma Health Workers.As the Regional Primary Health Care Project ends by May 2022; 84 Boma Health Workers in Yei, Morobo, Maridi and Ibba incentivized through this project will be lost as they would prefer to look for other opportunities to survive. Never the less, with funding envelop for HPF3 shrining swiftly, SSUHA a HPF3 implementing partner in Central Equatoria State has the number of health facilities supported by them from 25 to 18. 07 Health facilities will not be supported and thus becomes non-functional. SSUHA also reduce the number of BHWs from 336 to 237. Meaning 99 BHWs again will be lost. This would create a serious negative impact on health services; social mobilization for immunization, defaulters tracing; health education and promotion on essential curative and preventive health services to the community. Therefore, this project is envisioning to close the gap and ensure previous gains in the health sector are maintained and strengthened. The Health Cluster estimated that 227,964 of the population of Yei River, Morobo, Maridi and Ibba Counties as People in Need (PIN) of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan in 2022. AAHI target 30% (68,389) of the estimated population for its SSHRP application for 2022-2023 of Children under-five constitutes 20.3% (46,233) of the target population. AAH will target also 30% (13,870) children under-five for provision of child health curative, preventive and promotional activities. While supporting operations Ibba hospital theatre; incentives for health workers and BHWs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from the beneficiaries will be used to redefine or align project objectives, and indicators, activities and strategies to achieve desired outcome; ensure value for money, efficiency and effectiveness in health services delivery to the community.AAHI will ensure beneficiaries participate in the different stages of the project implementation starting from planning, targeting, implementation, Monitoring and Evaluation. Community stakeholders shall also be informed during the performance review meeting of action points adopted and how such changes have contributed either positively or detrimentally to the success of the project. This would inculcate a sense of ownership and builds commitment on the part of the community toward realization of the project objectives.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During the launch of the project the management of AAH-I in South Sudan ensure women, men youths, girls, boys, and persons with disability as well as special interest groups like the elderly among others are represented. These representative are obliged to share the messages from the launch with their respective constituents. Where available, sign-language translators are invited to the meetings to provided translation to the deaf or take the message to them where ever they may be in the State, County or Community. IEC materials will be developed both in English and main local languages (simple Juba -Arabic, Bari/Kakwa, Keliko, Muru and Zande) to ensure illiterate women, men, boys and girls in the community have access to the life-saving information and services. AAHI shall also ensure that complaint feedback reporting mechanism committee is representative of Men, Women, girls, and girls as well as persons with disability. There shall be recruitment of local people who are conversant with the language dynamics of the different location, this will limit communication barriers within project locations.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AAH-I has institutional policy of PSEA and all staff, partners and communities we work with are protected from Sexual Exploitation through provision of training and signing a compliance document to follow PSEA policy. The health workers in the project areas are trained to create awareness, respond to cases of sexual exploitation and abuses in collaboration with relevant legal units such as the police, court and welfare department to bring perpetrators to justice in case of a breach of contract. Beneficiaries shall be made aware on what constitutes SEA so that are informed of it and its implications.AAHI will enlighten beneficiaries on circumstances that may expose them to protection risks, Activities shall be designed in the sense they end early in the day to avoid late movement of beneficiaries unless there are health emergencies, hence group movement shall be encouraged amongst beneficiaries so that they do not succumb to PSEA abuses.Issues of SEA and actions taken during the reporting quarter will be highlighted in the quarterly performance presentations, State, County and Health facility management committee meeting as deemed necessary. The donor shall be informed of any SEA case and its implication on success or failure of the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The State Ministries of Health and County Health Departments in Central and Western Equatoria States including the County Commissioners, Relief and Rehabilitation Commissions will be officially informed about the new project as a bridge funding to ensure health services and previous gains from the BftW project are not paralyzed. This will be followed by an official launch where the objectives and expected outcomes of the SSHF 2022 funding are shared.Quarterly performance review meeting will be conducted at State and County levels with participation of all community stakeholders and health partners. Performances, key lessons learnt, of the project will be presented to partners. Comments, recommendations/corrective measures from the meeting will be documented, shared with stakeholders and action points addressed. Performance feedback reports shall be shared with community through State and County Health Cluster meetings; County Coordination meeting; Health Facility Management Committee meetings and Boma Health Committee weekly and monthly meetings. AAHI shall establish a Complaint and Feedback reporting mechanism by having a Working Telephone number which shall be used by Community to report health emergency issues through the BHWs. AAHI shall ensure establishment of relevant structures from the local level to the County and States.AAHI shall also embrace the used of Complains drop boxes which shall be fixed in accessible sites at the health facility, and County Health Departments or State Ministry of Health to receive complains from beneficiaries. These drop boxes are opened by a committee on weekly basis to check, categories. Recorded Complaints shall be responded to appropriately with involvement of the concerned departments and project focal persons. AAHI will also encourage sensitive complaints to be directed to a specific staff taking into consideration confidentiality.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

It is during these meetings that the objectives, activities of the project; roles and responsibility of each community stakeholders as well as expected outcomes are clearly elaborated. AAHI shall also work hand in hand with relevant authorities like County Health Departments, RRC to ensure activities are well coordinated ensuring adherence to safety and security procedures that shall be issued by the authorities to avoid incidences of noncompliance. Similarly, the Monthly, quarterly and semi-annual performance review meetings at all level as some of the forums where project related issues and mismanagements can be mitigated. Regular grants oversight, financial and programmatic internal audits will also mitigate unethical conduct of business in the organization. There is also a whistle blowing system that tends to minimize issues related to fraud AAHI shall timely inform the funding partner in a situation do not allow continuity of the project implementation.
