Project Summary

As South Sudan grapples with an escalating humanitarian crisis, MEDAIR is implementing a multifaceted intervention program to bolster flood resilience, provide immediate relief, and facilitate long-term recovery. This comprehensive approach aims to safeguard the well-being of affected populations by addressing their immediate needs while simultaneously empowering them to adapt to future challenges.
With a focus on emergency and transitional shelter, the program prioritizes ensuring timely and adequate access to safe and dignified shelter and lifesaving NFIs for newly displaced people and populations with new vulnerabilities. This will be achieved through the distribution of durable shelter solutions, implementation of cash-for-voucher assistance (CVA) programs, and provision of emergency shelter and essential non-food items (NFIs). The target for this intervention is 40,500 beneficiaries in the following prioritized locations: Pibor, Leer, Aweil East, Malakal, Manyo, Melut, and Renk.
Beyond immediate relief, MEDAIR's intervention also emphasizes long-term recovery and resilience-building measures. To enhance the resilience of flood-prone communities, the program will equip them with flood-resilient construction techniques and establish early warning systems, empowering them to better anticipate and prepare for future flooding events. Additionally, cash-for-work programs will provide employment opportunities and support infrastructure rebuilding, fostering economic revitalization and community self-sufficiency.
In addition to these measures, MEDAIR's intervention will also integrate cross-sectoral strategies to address critical health, WASH, and nutrition needs. By promoting hygiene practices, providing access to clean water and sanitation facilities, and distributing essential nutrition supplements, the program aims to improve the overall health and well-being of vulnerable populations. Additional activities include rapid needs assessments, AAP development and implementation, distribution of essential items, psychosocial support, monitoring of the humanitarian situation, coordination with other humanitarian actors, advocacy for the rights and protection of affected populations, and facilitation of access to humanitarian assistance.
In essence, MEDAIR's intervention in South Sudan embodies a holistic approach that addresses both immediate humanitarian needs and the underlying factors contributing to vulnerability. By empowering communities to adapt to the challenges they face, the program lays the foundation for sustainable recovery and resilience.

1. Needs

South Sudan has been significantly impacted by the rise in flooding over the past few years due to its unique geographic location and terrain conditions, creating difficulty for numerous places throughout the country. For instance, according to the Greater Horn of Africa Climate Outlook Forum (GHACOF), South Sudan received above-average rains during the 2012 rainy season, resulting in flooding that in some areas of the country forced the eviction of thousands of households. Floods and ongoing conflict significantly influence the daily lives of the population in South Sudan, adversely affecting, among other things, dwellings, crops, livestock, roadways, and medical facilities. Communities have adopted a nomadic lifestyle in response to the yearly flooding and displacement. Periodic displacements, however, have a detrimental impact on the well-being of developing communities. [HNO 2023]The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for South Sudan 2023 estimates that 8.9 million people will require humanitarian assistance in 2023, an increase of 1.7 million people from 2022. The increase is due to a combination of factors, including ongoing conflict, climate shocks, and economic hardship. [ISNA] The Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre (IDMC) estimates that there are over 2 million internally displaced people (IDPs) in South Sudan, the majority of whom are displaced due to conflict. IDPs are often at risk of flooding, as they often live in informal settlements that are located in flood-prone areas. [DTM] The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) is a tool that is used to track displacement in South Sudan. According to the DTM, there were over 1.9 million IDPs in South Sudan as of June 2023. [IPC]Cluster assessments are conducted by humanitarian agencies to assess the needs of affected populations. According to the cluster assessments, there is a need for increased humanitarian assistance in South Sudan, particularly in the areas of food security, shelter, and water and sanitation.

2. Project activities

Medair's Emergency Response Teams (ERTs) prioritize sudden onset and protracted emergencies for response, focusing on particularly vulnerable communities and the most vulnerable households within those communities. Consultations with the affected communities guide the response, ensuring it aligns with assessed and identified needs. Distributions are carried out in a timely and conflict-sensitive manner. Medair conducts exit interviews after every distribution to gather feedback. During each intervention, Medair establishes an appropriate community feedback mechanism that gradually transitions towards becoming more community-led. This involves increased engagement at the community level and the development of a feedback mechanism owned by the community to address any concerns with Medair or the response. Medair aims to ensure that all beneficiaries have meaningful access to the services provided and that no intervention causes harm or increases personal risk due to the intervention. Medair teams are trained in protection mainstreaming. Within this capacity, Medair is planning to implement the following activities: A1: Durable shelter solutions and flood resilience (DRR) Objective: To ensure that affected populations have access to safe and more durable shelter solutions. Activities: - Design and develop flood-resilient shelter prototypes in collaboration with local communities and technical experts. - Conduct trainings on flood-resilient construction techniques for local builders and community members. - Provide support to households in accessing and constructing flood-resilient shelters. - Establish and maintain flood early warning systems to alert communities of impending floods. A2: Cash assistance (CVA) Objective: To provide affected populations with the financial resources they need to meet their basic needs and recover from flooding events. Activities: - Conduct needs assessments to determine the most effective and appropriate form of cash assistance. - Distribute CVA to affected households in a timely and efficient manner (e.g.: MPC, voucher assistance, etc) - Implement cash-for-work programs to provide employment opportunities and rebuild infrastructure. A3: Emergency Shelter/NFI distribution Objective: To provide affected populations with immediate shelter and essential non-food items (NFIs) in the aftermath of flooding events and/or conflict. Activities: - Preposition of emergency shelter kits and NFIs in strategic locations to facilitate rapid response. - Establish distribution points in affected areas to ensure equitable access to emergency supplies. - Conduct training on the proper use and maintenance of emergency shelters and NFIs. - Monitor the distribution of emergency supplies to ensure that they are reaching the intended beneficiaries. Additional mainstreamed activities:AAP activities:-Conduct rapid needs assessments to identify the most urgent needs of the affected population.- Develop and implement AAPs in coordination with local authorities and other humanitarian actors.- Distribute essential items such as food, water, shelter, and hygiene kits.- Provide psychosocial support to affected individuals and families. HLP activities:- Monitor the humanitarian situation and identify emerging needs.- Coordinate with other humanitarian actors to ensure a comprehensive and effective response.- Advocate for the rights and protection of affected populations.- Facilitate access to humanitarian assistance for all affected populations. Monitoring activities:- Conduct Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercises to assess the effectiveness of the distributions.- Collect feedback from beneficiaries to identify areas for improvement.- Use monitoring data to inform future interventions.
