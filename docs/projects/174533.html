Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring the that women, girls, boys and men are safe in their communities and receive support when they experience harm in the targeted locations in the Far North region. The project will provide gender-based violence (GBV) and protection programming to beneficiaries to help them be safer in their communities, and receive support when they experience harm

1 - Besoins / Needs

The FN has been facing incursions and attacks by non-state armed groups since 2014. Despite the presence of several state and regional armed forces, the security situation remains precarious and continues to severely impact the population. Out of the six Departments affected by the Boko Haram conflict in the FN Region, Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga departments remain the most affected by the on-going conflict. In the FN, in December 2019 over 461, 664 people were displaced (297,380 Internal Displaced Persons, 47, 305 Nigerian refugees, and 116, 979 returnees) while on-going violence and attacks continues to hinder the population’s access to basic services, driving protection risks and decimating livelihoods. Over the last six months, the trend has been for a deterioration of the security situation, with a near doubling of the monthly average of incidents compared to the previous six months, continuing to target civilians and driving displacement.In the FN, according to the HNO 2020, one million people are in need urgent assistance in the Far North region of Cameroon due to the conflict. Displacement trends do not appear to be abating; there was a 7% (33,375 people) increase in the displaced population between August 2019 and December 2019, and the 2020 HNO does not predict that the current level of displacement will decrease in the near future. The departments of Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga are among the most affected, hosting 91% of the displaced population. This situation is adding considerable pressure on the local host communities’ already limited capacity to access basic services. The on-going conflict has also negatively impacted the livelihoods and the local economy in a region already characterized by endemic poverty (74.3% of the region’s population is living below the poverty line, compared to 37.5% at the national level ), and lack of natural and financial resources and market opportunities.Output 1: Women, girls, boys and men have an improved access to protection assistance when they experience harm in the FN Region.1.1 Provide comprehensive protection services including case management and emergency crisis management1.2. Provide Case Management services – including PSS support for general protection concerns and PSN through a protection center1.3. Provide adapted emergency support to most vulnerable such as: safety cash for urgent/immediate matters; referral to basic services; and referral to IRC livelihood programming1.4. Conduct protection monitoring and rapid assessments in targeted locations to identify protection threats and individuals most at risks 1.5. Support or develop community-based protection committees to identify, prevent and mitigate conflict through an early warning early response mechanism, contributing to the improvement of the security/protection environment.1.6. Provide tailored information and improve awareness on legal aspects and fundamental rights and how to access them.1.7. Provide legal counselling and legal assistance to most at risk individuals 1.8. Train, coach and support women paralegals at community level1.9. Provide legal information and training to community leaders – on legal framework, equitable decision making and gender equality1.10. Strengthen the IRC’s internal referral mechanism – to enable the IRC’s protection program to refer beneficiaries to the Cash program when protection risks could qualify beneficiaries under the vulnerability criteria established for financial assistance.1.11. Distribute dignity kits to vulnerable women and adolescent girls.1.12. Conduct sessions for adolescent girls using the IRC’s Girl Shine curriculumOutput 2: Vulnerable households are able to meet their basic needs and avoid resorting to negative coping strategies in the Far North Region. 2.1 Distribute multipurpose unconditional cash transfers to most vulnerable households and ensure post-distribution monitoring.
