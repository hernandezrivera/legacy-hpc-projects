Project Summary

This project aims at providing integrated basic humanitarian WASH service deliveries for 33,333 (9,000 boys, 9,333 girls, 7000 men and 8,000 women ) most vulnerable populations with the following specific objectives;
Contribute to reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality through provision of basic water, sanitation, hygiene to the conflict and disasters communities
Provision of safe, equitable and dignified access to WASH services in underserved locations affected by disasters through static and mobile basic emergency WASH services deliveries.
Provision of durable access to WASH services in long term internally displaced persons locations and communities with repeated occurrences of disaster through sustainable access to safe WASH services for the community affected by flooding and the IDPs /former POCs sites.
The following are the breakdown of the target populations by the selected counties;
Terekeka 10,000 counties in Central Equatoria State, and Melut 10,000, and Malakal 13,333 in Upper Nile.

1. Needs

In 2023, the standard of living and well-being of an estimated 6.1 million people will be affected as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. WASH conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin states were identified as having the counties with the greatest WASH needs.The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting the resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes. In the areas hosting displaced people, above-capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water for the people, with the situation worsening when more people are displacedLimited access to WASH infrastructure coverage alone, such as water points and latrines, poses multi-sectoral impacts. Poor access to WASH services and goods, combined with high levels of food insecurity, has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as evidenced by the high prevalence of malnutrition in the country. At the national level, the GAM prevalence for children between 0 and 59 months is at 16 per cent, while SAM was at 5.9 per cent according to the preliminary FSNMS and 2022 SMART surveys. The presence of water-borne diseases such as diarrhea in children under age 5 is also indicative of poor WASH conditions.Increasing levels of insecurity, both ethnic and politically motivated conflict, localized violence and disputes over land are the underlying drivers of WASH conditions in almost all states, limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH services and restricting people’s access to existing WASH infrastructure. Insecurity has also disrupted livelihoods, trade and supply chains, further perpetuating the economic challenges and reducing the household income for WASH goods and services.In the coming three to six months, the WASH situation in certain locations is projected to worsen due to heavy flooding, which has caused further disruption to livelihoods, displacement and damage to WASH infrastructure while limiting humanitarian access. Limited sustainability of the WASH response in the IDP sites due to funding shortage will increase the likelihood of water-borne disease and outbreaks, including Hepatitis E, AWD and cholera. Certain rural areas such as Upper Nile State are particularly at risk, as they are unreachable during the wet season due to roads washed away and existing networks cut off. This is likely to force the displaced people into urban or already populated areas, causing a knock-on effect to the existing WASH infrastructure that are already operating over capacity.With regards to the abovementioned situations, Peace Winds Japan intend to provide not only accessible water supply and hygiene and sanitation facilities, but also flood-resilient facilities, such as improved drainage to control flood water. Peace Winds Japan will also provide self-sustained maintenance mechanisms and sustainable solutions through enhanced community engagement and ownership, such as facilities maintenance trainings and hygiene promotion activities. For sanitation and hygiene issues, those findings highlight the need for a comprehensive approach to improve sanitation and hygiene environment and to enhance people’s awareness, which is also a key approach to ensure ownership and capacity building of the beneficiaries. This project, therefore, will implement durable WASH assistance targeting IDPs and host communities to fill in the existing gaps emphasized above. By executing the installation of WASH facilities and the capacity building of beneficiaries, the project will contribute to resilience-building and will have a sustainable impact to the beneficiaries and the targeted communities.

2. Project activities

Community engagement in WASH in South Sudan is a crucial dynamic process connecting the community and stakeholders so that people affected by the crises have more control over the response and its impact on them. Therefore, Peace Winds Japan will conduct meetings with local authorities, community leaders, women representatives, beneficiaries and inform them about the project activities, the roles of Peace Winds Japan, local authorities and community leaders. Peace Winds Japan will develop checklist for explaining the mechanisms that must be in place to ensure that beneficiaries of all ages, sex and diversity background, affected by a crisis have equitable and meaningful access to appropriate, relevant and timely information before, during and after receiving assistance. Peace Winds Japan will make ensure that there are two-way communication channels that facilitate feedback/ complaints and provide redress for complaints, are committed to ensure affected population participate in decisions with fair and transparent systems of representation Peace Winds Japan will conduct a safe audit to find out the protection and GBV concerns prevalence due to inadequate WASH services in all the targeted locations before the actual implementations of the WASH services Installation of handwashing stations in IDPs camps in the targeted project locations. Rehabilitations and maintenance of existing water points/hand pumps in IDPs camps in Nutrition centers, health centers, schools) with flood resilience water points in areas prone to floods. Installations and maintenance of new Surface Water Treatment (SWAT) system for newly IDPs sites in Upper Nile State. Constructions of new water points/hand pumps with flood resistance designs in areas prone to floods) Upgrading/Rehabilitation of the existing hand pumps /motorized water yards to solar powered water supply system targeting health facilities, schools and nutrition centers and sharing safe water with the host community: Formation and training of water management committee membersincluding pump techniciansConducting periodic monitoring water quality for all for all the new and rehabilitated water points.Constructions of semi-permanent latrines stances and installation of solar security lightings at latrine sites for easy access and safety targeting health facilities and schools in IDPs/Refugees camps Constructions of semi-permanent shared household latrine stances in the existing IDPs/refugees (with flood resistance latrine designs in areas prone to floods.Constructions of permanent ventilated latrine stances in functional government health facilities, schools.Reconstruction of semi-permanent shared bathing stances and installation of solar security lightings at bathing shelter sites for easy access and safety by the IDPs in IDPs camps.Formation and training of community Hygiene promoters within the host community in the targeted counties. Promotion of good hygiene practices through dissemination of key health messages through home visits with attention at the homes with malnourished children by community Hygiene promoters in the targeted counties.• Formation and training of active school health clubs and promotion sanitation and hygiene activities at functional government schools by school pupils in the targeted counties.Conduct menstrual management sessions to adolescents’ school girls and distributions of dignity kits targeting adolescents’ girls in the targeted counties Distribution of WASH NFI kits targeting malnourished children with caretakers (1 bucket with lids and taps, 1 bucket with lid without tap, soap or 3 months) through Nutrition/Feeding centers in the targeted counties.
