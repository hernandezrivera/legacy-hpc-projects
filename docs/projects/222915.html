Project Summary

Aligned with GBVSS priorities for JRP 2025, this UN Women project aims to strengthen GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response for Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox's Bazar. The project implements an integrated approach through partnerships with WROs and WLOs to address root causes of GBV and transform harmful social norms. Through established Multi-Purpose Women's Centers (MPWCs), UN Women will expand service delivery to new arrivals through dignity kits and targeted GBV support, leveraging its expertise to strengthen community women's networks in prevention and referrals, and to strengthen capacities of local WROs operating MPWCs to deliver quality, rights-based services.
First, through survivor-centered GBV response, UN Women supported seven MPWCs in camps 3, 4, 4E, 18, 25, and the host communities of Whykong-Teknaf and Palong Khali-Ukhiya. These MPWCs provide safe spaces to deliver lifesaving GBV services, including confidential case management by trained GBV caseworkers, PSS counseling, PFA, and ethical referrals to health and legal services, with legal aid by UNHCR provided on location as of 2025. In addition to prioritizing trauma recovery, MPWCs integrate awareness raising of rights, life skills and livelihoods development to promote self-reliance for women and adolescent girls, including new arrivals. Trained community volunteers conduct outreach to ensure services reach the most vulnerable, including women with disabilities, elderly women, and those facing intersectional barriers. This integrated MPWC model, managed through local WROs and AAB, provides an integrated, multisectoral service point to empower survivors, building their resilience, agency, and well-being.
Second, to transform harmful gender norms, this project will implement EMAP across 9 locations, including camps 1E and 1W alongside MPWCs sites, to address coverage gaps identified in collaboration with the Prevention WG and key stakeholders. This intervention engages community members through a structured EMAP implementation framework involving direct participants and indirect beneficiaries in weekly dialogues on respectful relationships and positive masculinities, incorporating women's perspectives. Facilitated dialogues, involving influential community/religious leaders, promote sustained behavioral change. This transformative approach cultivates community ownership in challenging discriminatory norms and creating an environment of zero tolerance for GBV.
Third, to ensure adherence to GBViE minimum standards, this project strengthens capacities of MPWC staff, caseworkers, and volunteers engaged in GBV prevention and response through trainings on GBV components, equipping them with critical knowledge and skills. The skills of MPWC caseworkers in delivering survivor-centered case management will be strengthened to support survivors, including those with intersectional vulnerabilities. The project also equips volunteers and partners with PFA, trauma-informed care approaches, and stigma reduction techniques. These initiatives improve referral pathways, elevate service quality while strengthening local WROs' capacity to lead GBV response.
For awareness and risk mitigation, the project mobilizes trained volunteers to conduct household visits, gender assessments, and strengthen referral pathways through trust-building outreach. With IEC materials, recreational activities engage 2,500 community women and girls in peer support, fostering psychosocial well-being and challenging gender stereotypes. Campaigns, including 16 Days of Activism, amplify women's voices while promoting help-seeking behaviors. This integrated approach builds community resilience while transforming gender norms that perpetuate GBV. Through WRO partnership, strengthened women's networks and community-led solutions, the project contributes to sustainable GBV prevention and response in Cox's Bazar.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The worsening security context in Cox's Bazar in both the refugees’ camps and in the host communities, fuels critical GBV-related needs, demanding strengthened prevention and response. Evidence from 2024 indicates concerning trends. Reported intimate partner violence rose from 75% in Q1 to 76.4% in Q3, while physical assault consistently exceeded over half of the reported cases (Joint Protection Monitoring reports, 2024). By mid-2024, 64.6% of camps reported women and girls feeling unsafe or very unsafe (GBVIMS Factsheet, Q2 2024). Economic vulnerability significantly impacts women's safety and resilience. ISNA 2024 data show only 46.2% of female-headed households report income, compared to 75% of male-headed households. The disproportionate burden of unpaid care work further restricts women's choice, with 48% of women spending seven or more hours daily on caregiving versus 15.2% of men (ISNA 2024). In host communities, vulnerability increased by 13% since 2017 to reach 30% of households (REVA 7, 2024), while limited access to integrated GBV services remains a pressing concern (ISNA 2024). Service access and safety remain significant barriers, particularly for women with intersecting vulnerabilities. ISNA 2024 reveals that 31.77% of women feel unsafe using facilities at night, indicating a significant safety concern in the camps. Additionally, the awareness of formal complaint mechanisms is notably lower among women, with 65.9% of women being aware of how to provide feedback or make a complaint through formal mechanisms, compared to 78.5% of men. This disparity indicates a need for targeted efforts to increase awareness and accessibility of these mechanisms for women to ensure their safety and well-being. The ISCG Visioning Workshop (October 2024) identified increasing resistance to confidential GBV work, creating additional barriers for survivors and posing specific risks for already marginalized groups, women with disabilities and hijra community members seeking support. Limited male engagement in gender transformation is evident, with ISNA 2024 revealing that 38.1% of boys do not participate in domestic work, while girls carry a significantly higher burden of domestic responsibilities (ISNA 2024) Three critical service delivery gaps require immediate attention: the decline in GBV funding amidst rising cases, which strains response capacity; compromised confidentiality, which limits service utilization; and intersectional vulnerabilities, which demand more inclusive approaches. Strengthened partnerships with local actors, including women-led organizations and community actors, are essential to foster trust, expand outreach, and build sustainable GBV response capacities. These efforts will reinforce community resilience and promote women's leadership in GBV prevention (ISCG Visioning Workshop, 2024). With regards to GBV AoR commitments, there is a need to strengthen the role and capacities of WROs that are women-led for the provision of rights-based GBV services compliant with GBViE standards, which is critical to long-term sustainability as well.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project integrates core protection principles through four interconnected approaches: Safety, dignity, and Do No Harm: Safeguarding standards are implemented through MPWC operations and partner management. All implementing partners maintain PSEA Network membership, trained PSEA and Child Safeguarding Focal Points, and required government permits. MPWCs provide safe female only spaces with integrated services, including case management, psychosocial support, and economic empowerment while ensuring safe and confidential service delivery. Restricted male access and training for staff and volunteers on humanitarian principles and survivor-centered care emphasize harm prevention, amid rising insecurities. Meaningful access and non-discrimination: The WRO-managed MPWC model consolidates services at one stop, reducing barriers for women and survivors, especially those with intersectional vulnerabilities. Volunteers facilitate service access through regular outreach and referrals, while coordination with GBV sub-sector focal points ensures ethical referral pathways. This approach supports timely, inclusive access to services. Accountability and participation: Participatory approaches and WRO partnerships promote local ownership in program design and implementation. Periodic assessments by volunteers create feedback mechanisms for continuous improvement. The project builds sustainable local capacity through training on PSEA and safeguarding protocols. Transportation support and background checks for volunteers reinforce safety and accountability. Protection integration and joint responsibility: MPWCs serve as hubs offering holistic support while coordinating with Protection Sector partners. Collaboration with WROs and community actors, including religious leaders through EMAP, strengthens sustainable protection capacity. This ensures protection mainstreaming extends beyond service delivery, fostering women’s leadership and lasting community resilience.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UN Women is committed to “leaving no one behind” and focuses on vulnerable groups within camps and host communities, prioritizing gender-diverse persons, elderly women, and women and girls with disabilities. The project targets individuals aged 15 to 60+ across all gender groups, as reflected in the GAM report. UN Women staff are trained in disability and inclusion and act as focal points in the office. UN Women partners and volunteers engaged in the project are trained on disability inclusion, equipping them to address diverse needs. The project pursues an intersectional approach to address the specific challenges faced by elderly women and women with disabilities. Volunteers conduct outreach and household visits to reach those who face mobility barriers or challenges accessing MPWCs. The project aims to include 12% of refugee and host community women with disabilities or those with family members who have such needs in MPWC services. MPWCs are designed with safety and privacy in mind, providing secure access to GBV services. Dedicated spaces are included for nursing mothers; SRHR services for especially women of reproductive age are integrated with UNFPA support; and recreational and learning activities tailored for adolescent girls, including in collaboration with UNICEF, ensure their needs are addressed. Ongoing renovations are further enhancing MPWC accessibility for elderly women and individuals with disabilities. MPWC service staff receive training in accessible communication and practices, and accessible feedback and complaint mechanisms are in place to ensure usability for everyone, regardless of ability. The project emphasizes the collection of disaggregated data by age and disability to inform program design and implementation. This data-driven approach enables the identification of gaps and the tailoring of services to meet the specific needs of different groups, ensuring that interventions are both effective and inclusive.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project upholds AAP by integrating feedback mechanisms, ensuring meaningful participation and fostering adaptive programming. UN Women prioritizes crisis-affected women and girls in decision-making processes throughout the program cycle, aligning with the IASC commitments on AAP and PSEA. UN Women emphasizes incorporating a gender analysis that considers intersecting identities throughout all phases of humanitarian programming. This ensures programs are responsive and inclusive, addressing diverse needs related to SOGIESC, disabilities, and migratory status. MPWC Community Volunteers conduct household visits and community group meetings to garner support for access of women and girls to MPWCs and to gather feedback, ensuring services align with the needs of affected populations. This participatory approach reinforces accountability and enhances resilience. The EMAP programme engages men and boys in discussions on positive masculinity and respectful relationships, supporting an environment where men and boys are active allies in GBV prevention and gender equality, strengthening the community's overall accountability to all members. MPWCs are equipped with accessible feedback and complaint mechanisms, including suggestion boxes, direct reporting to trained staff, and anonymous options, ensuring safe, confidential reporting. Staff and volunteers are trained in PSEA and safeguarding protocols to handle feedback ethically, maintaining confidentiality. Information on services, rights, and complaint mechanisms is disseminated through culturally appropriate channels, tailored to varying literacy levels and language needs, ensuring inclusivity for individuals with disabilities and elderly women. UN Women collaborates with local women’s rights organizations (WROs) to build sustainable accountability practices and strengthen local actors’ ability to manage feedback and uphold service quality.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UN Women prioritizes protecting diverse individuals from SEA through robust governance frameworks and survivor-centered approaches aligned with IASC core principles. This ensures compliance with humanitarian standards, advances gender equality, and protects rights. As an active member of the PSEA network, Protection Sector, GBV SS, LSD Sector, AAP WG, and GiHA WG, UN Women remained informed of sector discussions. SEA allegations are reported to the UN Resident Coordinator and investigated per SEA complaint referral SOPs, in collaboration with the PSEA Network Coordinator to ensure actions post-investigation. Staff, partners, and volunteers are trained on PSEA principles, codes of conduct, and inter-agency standards. Designated PSEA focal points are linked to the PSEA network, with contact information displayed in office and MPWCs. During field activities, partners and volunteers assess SEA risks and escalate concerns following established reporting lines. UN Women also established PSEA feedback and complaint mechanisms at the MPWCs, available in written and verbal forms through the box or the hotline number and ensured client confidentiality and safety during case processing. ASK will be phased out by Dec 2024, Agrajattra by Mar 2025. New WRO partners will be assessed using the UN IP PSEA Capacity Assessment Tool before onboarding. Also, all partners' active participation in the PSEA Network will be ensured. UN Women ensures survivor-centered service assistance by providing MPWC-based Psychosocial and SRHR services for SEA survivors. It also ensures service linkages with rehabilitation/safe shelter for survivors through an inter-agency referral pathway. Additionally, UN Women emphasizes the role of leadership in mainstreaming PSEA in alignment with IASC guidance. Leaders and managers are trained on their PSEA roles and responsibilities, and these roles are outlined to enhance accountability. MPWC is the available economic empowerment hub for SEA survivors.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project integrates climate action by strengthening gender-responsive disaster preparedness and building resilience among women and girls with intersectional vulnerabilities in Cox's Bazar. MPWCs and the Women's Market function as designated cyclone shelters, equipped with electricity connectivity, emergency preparedness equipment, and essential relief materials to ensure safe spaces for women, children, and elderly during disasters. The project builds capacity at the community level by training Rohingya Volunteers in emergency preparedness and response, while supporting women's rights organizations with emergency preparedness resources. Based on needs assessment, emergency kits will be distributed to 100 refugee households, prioritizing female-headed households and those with specific vulnerabilities. Through WRO partnerships, the project ensures continuation of GBV services during environmental emergencies while promoting women's meaningful participation in disaster preparedness planning and decision-making processes. This integrated approach addresses the disproportionate impacts of climate-related disasters on women and girls while strengthening their leadership roles in building community resilience and sustainable response mechanisms.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project integrates EPR measures to ensure the continuity of essential GBV services during crises in Cox's Bazar. UN Women-supported MPWCs, designated as cyclone shelters, maintain updated business continuity plans for uninterrupted service delivery. Emergency response protocols, aligned with inter-agency standards, address natural disasters, security incidents, and health emergencies, with regularly updated referral pathways facilitating coordination. To strengthen community resilience, the project trains volunteers and WRO partners in emergency preparedness and response, enabling coordinated actions during emergencies. Pre-positioned emergency supplies, including dignity kits and essential materials, are strategically located at MPWCs to provide immediate support to women and girls with intersectional vulnerabilities. UN Women collaborates with women-led organizations and community women's networks, reinforcing gender-responsive decision-making in emergency preparedness and response. Formal linkages with Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) committees in Palong Khali and Whykong will ensure targeted host community engagement. Trained volunteers will act as community-based focal points to coordinate preparedness activities and foster collaboration with EPR management committees. These measures prioritize the safety and leadership of women and girls, ensuring inclusive and effective emergency responses in both Rohingya refugee and host communities.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project actively aligns youth with the IASC Guidelines on Working with and for Young People in Humanitarian and Protracted Crises and the UN Women Youth and Gender Equality Strategy, emphasizing the domains of voice, choice, and safety for young women in protracted settings. Integrating the EMAP approach, the project engages young men and boys and amplifies the meaningful participation of young women (voice), enabling them to advocate against GBV within their communities and strengthening their representation in decision-making forums. To enhance economic well-being and resilience (choice), the project provides skills training and livelihood programs through MPWCs, supporting young women in building economic security. Through volunteer networks and outreach initiatives, youth are engaged in promoting gender equality and raising awareness of GBV. These efforts align with the IASC’s focus on youth-sensitive program design and implementation, ensuring that youth are active contributors to community development. The project prioritizes young women’s safety by integrating their perspectives into GBV prevention and response strategies. By fostering environments where young women can participate in community decision-making platforms on issues they identify as priorities, the project strengthens their capacity to advocate for protections that address their specific needs. In all their diversity, this approach recognizes young women as meaningful agents of change to foster safer, more inclusive communities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project promotes refugee resilience through a holistic approach integrating GBV response with community-driven solutions. WRO-managed MPWCs serve as safe spaces where women and girls, including those with intersectional vulnerabilities, access integrated protection and empowerment services while building skills and social networks. MPWC services include Accelerated Adult Learning, non-formal technical training, and vocational trainings (included in UN Women’s LSDS project). In collaboration with UNICEF, education is offered to girls who have dropped out of school. Through structured engagement in EMAP and gender clubs, community members develop capacities to address harmful gender norms and prevent GBV. The project strengthens long term community resilience to prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV prevention through strengthening local women's leadership. All volunteers involved in the are trained in survivor-centered approaches, building sustainable response capacity.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

In host communities, this project aligns with the Cox's Bazar District Development and Growth Plan's priority on "Social Security, Safety and Gender Dimensions," by strengthening GBV prevention and response mechanisms. Partnership with local women's rights organizations strengthens institutional capacity for survivor-centered service delivery, supporting long-term development objectives. MPWCs in host communities function as safe hubs with integrated services, where GBV response connects with skills development, advancing district objectives for women's empowerment. Referrals are made to government-led services, including for legal aid. Women’s meaningful participation in local decision-making is encouraged through skills development. Through community-driven solutions and structured dialogues, the project enhances social cohesion and strengthens institutional mechanisms for women's protection, establishing sustainable foundations for district-wide resilience.
