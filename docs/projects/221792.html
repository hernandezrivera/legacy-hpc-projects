Project Summary

The malnutrition crisis in South Sudan will continue to deteriorate in 2025, driven by multiple converging humanitarian crises. Approximately 4.3 million children under five(U5), as well as 1.11 million pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, will need life-saving nutrition treatment and preventive care. According to the 2024 Integrated Food security Phase Classification-Acute Malnutrition Situation Report (IPC-AMN 2024), 0.65 million U5 are at risk of severe acute malnutrition, 1.4 million at risk of moderate acute malnutrition, and 1.1 million pregnant and lactating women (PLWs)requiring urgent treatment. In 49 counties, acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15 per cent, with Bailet County in Upper Nile projected to reach IPC-AMN phase 5. Economic challenges, climate impacts, ongoing conflicts, heightened GBV risks, and spillover effects from the Sudan crisis further aggravate the situation. In 2025, eight children out of every ten children (U5) are projected to be at risk of acute malnutrition; IPC- Acute Food Insecurity (AFI) results suggest that 7.69 million people, including 1.5 million U5, will be severely food insecure by July 2025.
The 2024 IPC data shows that more than 85 per cent of returnees and people on the move fleeing Sudan will be acutely food insecure and, hence, will be malnourished. In the addition, according to the Food Security and Nutrition (FSNWG) Update (October 2024), proxy malnutrition prevalence among refugees and returnees from Sudan, particularly at the transit centres in Renk were reportedly alarmingly high. Combined with disease outbreaks such as measles, this may result in increased mortality rates. Between January and February, 46,220 SAM admissions – the highest compared to the same period over the last five years – were recorded across the country, the majority (8,440) in Jonglei state. Furthermore, the returnees also make up almost half of those facing catastrophic hunger as they struggle to rebuild their lives amidst an unprecedented economic crisis, severe flooding, and prioritization of resources as needs outpace funding. Since the outbreak of conflict in Sudan, more than half a million people have fled to South Sudan, of whom around 80 per cent are South Sudanese returning to their country of origin. The returnees arrived at the point of entry (PoE) very tired and exhausted, while some especially the women, children and elderly displayed signs of poor health conditions, and malnourishment. According to the Intention surveys (October 2024) conducted by IOM Data Tracking Matrix Unit (DTM), more than 50 per cent of the returnees had no clear destination and spend weeks or even months in and around the transit centres, struggling to survive with a scarcity of humanitarian aid. The lack of nutrition services at the PoEs, and transit sites, further aggravated the malnutrition situation among the returnee population and people on the move.
To address the high rate of malnutrition among the returnees and the people on the move, IOM will ensure equitable access to quality lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition services to children and PLWs among returnee and the people on the move populations. IOM will conduct systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases, support curative and preventive nutrition interventions to treat U5, PLW with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). The nutrition services to be provided will include nutrition screening, a targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP) and an outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP). In addition to ensure effective nutrition referral pathways for returnees and people on the move, IOM will map nutrition and health sites in key destination locations, where identified SAM and MAM cases will be referred for continued treatment after being supported through IOM’s onward transport assistance (OTA).

2. Project activities

1. Conduct individual and group IYCF-E counselling among PLWs and caregivers of children aged 2 years and below at the POE sites to support the returnees and the people on the move. 2. Establish community-based Mother-to-Mother support groups for PLWs at POEs to increase health education on malnutrition. 3 . Scale up community-based malnutrition detection and identification of acute malnutrition through Family health workers (FHWs) and Mothers' Mid Upper arm circumference (MUAC) at POEs.4. Provide lifesaving treatment for SAM and MAM at POEs through established mobile sites. 5. Provide Deworming, Multiple micronutrients and Vitamin A supplementation as part of Outpatient management of acute malnutrition at the POEs. 6. Intensify breastfeeding counselling and re-lactation at the POE centre for acutely malnourished children under 6 months of Age. 7. Conduct community mobilization activities for screening and referral of malnourished children at the POEs for further treatment. 8 . Conduct Family MUAC screening at the POEs. 9 . Strengthen the capacity of health workers (such as midwives), Community Health Workers (CHWs), and FHWs to implement community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) through classroom and on-the-job mentoring and refresher training. 10. Referral of malnourished children/ pregnant and lactating women: IOM will screen and admit malnourished children, as well as pregnant and lactating women, at the Point of Entry (POE) centre. These individuals will continue to receive treatment until they reach their final destination. Prior to this, IOM will communicate and coordinate with the implementing partner responsible for nutrition services at the final destination. IOM will share the details of the children and pregnant/lactating women (PLW) with both the partner and the nutrition cluster to ensure they continue to receive nutritional treatment upon arrival.
