Project Summary

In 2020 the Nutrition intervention targets 808,537 women and children in BAY states, of which 539,045 are children below the age of 5 and 269,492 are women of reproductive age with infants below 2 years. Majority of those in need are in the host communities 405958 while approximately 223302 are IDPS and 179275 returnees while 0.2M still not accessed by humanitarian workers. The prevalence of acute malnutrition in the north east is further heightened by inadequate health services coverage, poor child care practices, inadequate hygiene and sanitation conditions and food insecurity.
Population arriving from the inaccessible areas have particularity high prevalence of acute malnutrition, a SMART survey conducted in Bama in August 2019 by save the children international indicated a very high prevalence of GAM among the host community (14% with SAM of 4.9% which is above the emergency threshold of 2.0% compared to those that were in the camp and had access to humanitarian services at 8.5% with SAM rate of 3.5% which is also above the emergency threshold. Similar trend was also recorded in the 8th round SMART joint survey conducted by UNICEF/WFP in the 3 in October 2019 indicate that Yobe has the highest prevalence with 11.5% with 2.8% SAM rate, Borno State has a GAM rate of 8.1 per cent and a SAM rate of 0.9 percent; while Adamawa State has a GAM rate of 7.2 per cent.
The health system is still contending with extensive damage and inadequate human resources as well as insufficient medical supplies to cater for the needs of the displaced population as well as those in the host communities to date the health system functionality is below 50%
Population movement within the northeast has been a key phenomenal with the majority arriving from the inaccessible areas has increased the burden on the existing nutrition services as this movements are associated with extreme needs as those IDPS arriving from these areas have not accessed humanitarian services. Movement have also been realized for those intending to move back to their LGA of origin to explore livelihood activities have also been reported. This movements have been sporadic and have continued to put pressure on existing humanitarian services mostly affected including the nutrition sector as services providing SAM with medical complication have been inadequate.
Acute malnutrition is often an outcome of poor food security, Health and poor child feeding and caring practices as well as other humanitarian services with chronic malnutrition thus the importance of involving multiple sectors in addressing malnutrition. The causes of malnutrition are complex and interconnected hence requiring intersectoral collaboration and integration with health agriculture social protection among others. An IRC Semi-Quantitative Evaluation of Access and Coverage survey (SQUEC) survey conducted by IRC between 5th to 23rd of March 2018 in 10 communities in MMC and Jere estimated single coverage at 47.7 percent of existing needs (95 percent confidence interval between 38.7 and 56.9 percent coverage). This coverage is below the 70 percent Sphere minimum standard for urban settings
IRC has been present in North east as health and Nutrition actor since 2012 and an active member and co-lead in both sectors in the 3 north east states.

1 - Needs

Establish and Maintain 5 stabilization centre (2 in MMC and Jere, 1 in Askira Uba,1 in BAMA and 1 in Gujba The IRC is currently running 5 (24 hour stabilization centres) in the BAY states (4 in Borno(2 in MMC and Jere.1 in Askira Uba and 1 in Gujba with a total bed capacity of 124-. The IRC will therefore increase the center’s capacity to admit more children and main the other 4 stabilization centres. The current SC’s have a bed occupancy above 120% and most of the time eligible patients are turn away due to lack of space. With NHS funding IRC will continue to provide 24 hour critical care services in the 5 SC’s currently managed. Additional Qualified health personnel will be recruited to support the MOH staffs operate the center 24 hours a day, providing patients clinical management and monitoring of feedings 3 hourly. Following a thorough needs assessment of the site, the IRC will supply the stabilization center with the required supplies and medical consumables, including basic furniture, equipment and anthropometric tools (scales, measuring board, and MUAC tapes), as well as medicine for the systematic treatment of malnourished children. The nutritional products (therapeutic milk and RUTF) will be supplied by UNICEF as part of the IRC’s Program Cooperation Agreement (PCA) and IRC will ensure delivery to the stabilization center. In addition IRC will provide meals to caregivers looking after their child with SAM for the duration of admission in the SC. Children who have recovered from medical complications will be transferred to the OTP. Engaging community leaders and men in awareness raising in CMAM and e-IYCF in hard to reach areas and transit centres. Mothers rarely act alone in making decisions about IYCF. They are guided by influential family members and perceptions of social norms, among other factors. Mobilizing community leaders’ support can help to create a supportive environment for improved practices in the communities. Community leaders are custodians of culture in their domains. They maintain law and order in their communities and also support members of the communities in their day to day activities. Community leaders. Lessons from project IRC is currently implementing in MMC on engaging on community leader’s participation show positive result in reducing barriers to practicing optimal IYCF and accessing services at the mobile clinics. In communities where the top leader (Lawan) and key leader (religious leader, women leader, youth leader and Traditional Birth Attendant representative, and CV leader) were informed and about IYCF CMAM practices an increase of referral to OTP was noticed For this grant, the IRC plans to extend its support to community leaders with inclusion of the leaders of sub communities (Bulamas) as a focal point in sub communities to ensure coordination between all community leaders in creating more awareness in IYCF/CMAM. A total of 5 Bulamas from each of the 10 LGA supported will be trained at the beginning of the project by IRC E-IYCF officer. They will then be in charge of organising mobilisation through community dialogue sessions with men from their communities to encourage referral to nutrition services and improvement of IYCF practices at home. Regular monthly meeting organised by Lawan with all key actors including as well Bulamas, will be maintained to encourage participation, sharing experience and solution findings. The meeting will also be a forum to receive feedback from the community leaders and stakeholders on the CMAM and IYCF programme and recommendations from them on how to improve the programmes. Protection action groups members and WPE case workers will also be involved to mainstream protection into the nutrition activities. In addition IRC will maintain 10 fixed OTP clinics in 10 communitie

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will integrate client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include sessions specifically with women, girls, and persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups. It will additionally make use of the existing suggestion boxes and community discussion forums to collect beneficiary feedback on quality emergency response actions. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who record and refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertised on IRC banners at program implementation sites. The IRC’s hotline, suggestion boxes and community engagement meetings/focus group discussions will ensure that community voices are inclusive of the implementation process and furthermore serve as reporting mechanisms in case of maltreatment or abuse. Client exit interviews will be conducted to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, which will allow the IRC to make rapid and informed adjustments to the project. Safety audits will also be conducted each month with women and girls in the IDP camp to understand the nutrition protection related risks. Advocacy strategies will be developed together with other partners, community structures, and female beneficiaries, to respond to identified needs.
