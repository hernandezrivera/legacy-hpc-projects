Project Summary

The nutrition response will focus on preventive and curative nutrition interventions in Kajo Keji and Aweil West Counties targeting the most vulnerable members of the community mainly boys and girls under five years of age as well as pregnant and lactating women. ARC is present in Kajo Keji County implementing integrated nutrition, Gender Based Violence (GBV) and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) programs whereas in Aweil West County, ARC is implementing nutrition program. The proposed interventions include management of acute malnutrition through outpatient therapeutic programme and targeted supplementary feeding programme; promotion of appropriate maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices in the nutrition sites and in the community; and vitamin A supplementation targeting children aged 6 to 59 months. The project aims to reduce morbidity and prevent mortality associated with acute malnutrition and subsequently improving maternal and child well-being. ARC will also work in coordination with other sectors such as WASH, Protection, Education, Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) and Health to strengthen integration and linkages of services with the goal of preventing acute malnutrition and enhance recovery of beneficiaries undergoing nutrition rehabilitation. The response will target the host community, internally displaced persons and returnees in the targeted locations. The project aims to reach 3,061 children aged 6 to 59 months with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications, 11,421 children aged 6 to 59 months with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and 4,942 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition. It is important to point out that ARC will target 40% of the SAM/MAM caseload in Aweil West County since there is another nutrition partner in the same County whereas in Kajo-Keji County, ARC will target the entire proposed SAM/MAM caseload as per the HNO targets for 2020. In addition, in terms of vitamin A supplementation, ARC will target all the children according to the 2020 HNO targets in both Aweil West and Kajo Keji Counties.

1 - Needs

The burden of acute malnutrition remains high in South Sudan. This could be attributed to the consequences of the perennial conflict resulting to high level of food insecurity due to unsustainable livelihoods, high inflation and climatic shocks; limited access to health care; poor hygiene practices; lack of adequate sanitation facilities; limited access to clean water, and sub-optimal maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices. The latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis report released in September 2019 that is based on Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) survey round 24 data collected between June and July 2019 indicates Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 16.2% in South Sudan, which is above World Health Organization (WHO) emergency threshold of 15%. The IPC analysis further predicts that between Jan and April 2020, the food security situation in South Sudan will deteriorate and it is estimated that 5.5 million people (47% of total population) are likely to face worse acute food insecurity. In addition, the IPC analysis estimates that 1.3 million children aged 6-59 months and 352,344 pregnant and lactating women will be affected by acute malnutrition in 2020. It is also estimated that approximately 84,523 children in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) and 94,504 children in Central Equatoria States will suffer acute malnutrition. This are within locations where ARC is implementing nutrition programs i.e. Aweil West and Kajo Keji Counties. The FSNMS round 24 also reported GAM rate of 15.2% in Aweil West County that is above WHO emergency threshold of 15% for acute malnutrition and GAM rate of 5.2% in Kajo Keji County classified as poor based on WHO emergency threshold for acute malnutrition. However, poor food security attributed to prolonged political instability that has led to displacement of the population coupled with other aggravating factors such as lack of adequate and safe water, poor sanitation facilities, lack of proper shelters, limited access to health care services common during displacements, exposes the population to a high risk of acute malnutrition. Vulnerable groups such as children, women, people with disabilities and older people suffer the most intense consequences of sustained displacement, violence and lack of access to basic services. Maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices in South Sudan continue to be poor especially complementary feeding practices among children aged 6 to 23 months years and has significantly contributed to the burden of acute malnutrition. This is consistent with the results of the FSNMS round 24 that reported introduction of complementary food at 6 to 8 months at 46%, minimum meal frequency at 23%, minimum dietary diversity at 13% and minimum acceptable diet at 7%. Malnutrition can have long-term negative effects particularly during the first 1,000 days of a child’s life i.e. from pregnancy through to two years of age, therefore, prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition remains a significant need in the targeted locations. Besides, protracted conflict has weakened the government infrastructure and systems and consequently ability to respond to high burden of acute malnutrition.

2 - Activities

ARC proposes to implement preventive and curative nutrition interventions in Aweil West and Kajo Keji Counties to prevent and treat cases of acute malnutrition among children under five years as well as pregnant and lactating women with the aim of improving their nutritional status. ARC will work in coordination with other sectors such as Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Protection, Education, Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) and Health to enhance integration and linkages of services with the goal of improving treatment of beneficiaries under nutrition rehabilitation and preventing new cases of acute malnutrition. It is important to point out that ARC will target 40% of the SAM/MAM caseload in Aweil West County since there is another nutrition partner in the same County whereas in Kajo-Keji County, ARC will target the entire proposed SAM/MAM caseload as per the HNO targets for 2020. In addition, in terms of vitamin A supplementation, ARC will target all the children according to the 2020 HNO targets in both Aweil West and Kajo Keji Counties.• Treat 3,061 children aged 6 to 59 months with severe acute malnutrition without medical complications through outpatient therapeutic programme as per the CMAM guidelines. ARC will facilitate transportation of severely malnourished children with medical complications to the stabilization center for inpatient management (Aweil West: 2,572 & Kajo Keji: 489).• Treat 11,421 children 6 to 59 months with moderate acute malnutrition through targeted supplementary feeding programme as per the CMAM guidelines. Targeted supplementary feeding programme will also ensure continuum of care for children discharged from outpatient therapeutic programme (Aweil West: 4,232 & Kajo Keji: 7,189). • Treat 4,942 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition through targeted supplementary feeding programme as per the CMAM guidelines (Aweil West: 4,762 & Kajo Keji: 180)• Conduct nutrition screening in nutrition sites and in the community to enable timely identification and treatment of children under five years as well as pregnant women and lactating mothers with acute malnutrition.• Conduct maternal, infant and young child nutrition counselling to 7,824 primary caregivers of children below 23 months in the nutrition sites and in the community (Aweil West: 5,742 & Kajo Keji: 2,082). • Conduct exit interviews to 75% of programme beneficiaries to establish their satisfaction, obtain feedback and areas for improvement on the quality of services provided in the nutrition sites. • Test 3061 children with severe acute malnutrition admitted in outpatient therapeutic programme for malaria and provide treatment to cases who test positive (Aweil West: 2,572 & Kajo Keji: 489). • Provide WASH kits to 3061 children with SAM in nutrition sites. The WASH kit will comprise of a water container, soap and aqua tab for water treatment at home (Aweil West: 2,572 & Kajo Keji: 489). • Establish 20 kitchen garden at nutrition sites to demonstrate to the programme beneficiaries production and utilization of nutrient-dense foods that will subsequently improve dietary diversity and quality of food consumed by children as well as pregnant and lactating women Aweil West: 15 & Kajo Keji: 5).• Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development by equipping 20 nutrition sites with child play materials. This will provide sensory stimulation for children with acute malnutrition (Aweil West: 15 & Kajo Keji: 5) • Conduct gender based violence safety audits in 20 nutrition sites to identify and address protection/safety needs (Aweil West: 15 & Kajo Keji: 5).• ARC in conjunction with the State Ministry of Health, will carry out Vitamin A supplementation campaign in Aweil West and Kajo Keji Counties targeting 45,681 children under five years of age (Aweil West: 44,634 & Kajo Keji: 1,047).

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ARC will adhere to the IASC international standards and guidelines while delivering the proposed humanitarian services. ARC will uphold the “Do No Harm” principle and as much as possible minimize any negative impact the proposed interventions may have on the beneficiaries and ensure their safety, dignity and respect is prioritized during service delivery. ARC will also uphold the principles of impartiality, neutrality and participation in delivery of services by giving equal opportunity without any biasness to the members of the community regardless of their status, age, gender or affiliations etc. ARC will regularly conduct gender based violence safety audits in nutrition sites to identify and address protection/safety needs. Additionally, ARC will sensitize the nutrition workers and volunteers on gender and gender based violence, and safe referral ways for gender based violence survivors so that they are able to identify and refer survivors for support. ARC will also work with all program beneficiaries to plan the schedules so that times are convenient and safe for women and children. ARC will provide services in a manner that reduces the time spent at, travelling to and returning from nutrition service points. For isolated beneficiaries who take over a day to travel to the nutrition site, an appropriate ration will be considered after verification with community nutrition volunteers. To mitigate gender-based violence, both men and women will be included in the process of selecting a safe and accessible distribution points where applicable for nutrition services.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ARC is committed to strengthening accountability to affected population by listening to feedback and addressing grievances from those who participate, support or are affected by program by giving both a voice, and the opportunity to influence key decisions. The aim is to build strong relationships with the community and to allow community feedback to shape and improve programming ensuring that interventions are relevant and appropriate. ARC has already established a relationship of two-way communication and transparency with target communities. Program plans will be communicated to the communities during inception and will continue throughout the implementation period through open forums and focus group discussions. In addition, ARC values complaints and feedback because it helps in improving programming. To avoid marginalization of vulnerable community members, more than one channel will be available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Examples include one-on-one meetings at nutrition sites and office and discussions with a staff member during community events. Focal points will be identified and oriented at all sites to lead on community complaints/accountability response mechanisms, including listening to and reporting feedback and complaints, ensuring timely response, and sensitizing communities on use and purpose of these mechanisms. Furthermore, ARC through the Project Management Committees in each nutrition site, will provide a means for beneficiaries to voice complaints and provide feedback on areas relevant to operations in a safe and dignified manner.
