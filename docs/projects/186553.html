Project Summary

this project designed to implement its integrated, inclusive and comprehensive response designed to heal while educating children affected by conflict and trauma in Adamawa. The starting point will be the re-education of teachers to
help pupils coming from traumatic experiences, supplemented with greater access to teaching material guidance, child friendly spaces, and training in trauma healing by psychological support. Then we will focus on creating and provide to pupils and teachers safe and protective learnings environments, gender and social inclusion will be the cornerstone of all interventions. In our comprehensive response, we will integrate gender responsive WASH in school while we care to these children’s and teachers living with disabilities and others specific needs. Moreover, we will appoint one Gender focal point by each targeted school to care specific needs of adolescent girls at school. She can act as a bridge with our Child Protection team for cases of SGBV, case management of girls during menstruation at school. These School Gender focal point will be trained on GBV and will also be able to train adolescent girls in school and create a positive change. Preventing grave child rights violations will be on our agenda by increasing local leaders and community awareness on the child safeguard and protection mechanism. Capacity-building for the community’s role in school management will be part of this intervention and Management School Committee (MSC) will be created/reinforced and trained to support education reconstruction. In our integrated approach, we will also focus on building positive relationships, fostering positive emotions through affirmative training, to enable the recovery of children who have suffered.
This project is to ensure that 6,500 emergency-affected school-age girls and boys (Including IDPs) continue to get access to safe and quality learning opportunities in emergency, to provide physical, psychological and cognitive protection that can sustain post conflict healing and resilience. It also aims at delivering a strengthening capacity of the education system and communities to deliver a timely and evidence-based education response for these girls and boys in needs of education especially these who are living with disabilities and these who are out-of-schools.

1 - Needs

Access to educational opportunities in North-East Nigeria is limited due to insurgency which has eventually led to huge numbers of children not attending or dropping out of school, increase in classroom sizes for communities hosting IDPs, and disruption of education services to the most vulnerable groups such as IDPs, girls and children with special needs. Conflict-affected children and adolescents also require support in accessing quality child protection services both in schools and communities wherein immediate mental health and psycho-social support (MHPSS) is provided. Further, due to inadequate protection measures, displaced children continue to miss out on critical support systems that are requisite to deal with life situations. In a protracted and complex crisis such as that of North-East Nigeria, this program will address both quality, access and education governance issues whilst integrating protection mechanism to design a comprehensive response to strengthen children and community’s resilience. To achieve the predicted impact of increasing access to quality and inclusive education, mental health and psychological support and vocational opportunities for crisis-affected girls and boys in safe learning environments in Adamawa, and in alignment with MYRP for North East Nigeria, APADE will implement following strategic activities:1. Renovation/construction of Temporary Learning Spaces: providing safe and healthy learning environments to the most vulnerable children – with focus on rehabilitation and renovation of safe and accessible learning spaces (procurement of renovation/construction materials, logistics and renovation/constructions of learning spaces)2. Conducting assessment and renovation/ construction of schools in conflict affected areas including gender responsive water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities in targeted schools. Construction will be safe and duly segregated between boys and girls. In addition, Menstrual hygiene education, facilities, and products will be provided in a safe, clean, clearly communicated, free and easily accessible manner. Each targeted school will be endowed by a school gender focal point.3. Conduct of a massive inclusive and gender responsive back to school campaign and enrolment/return to school initiative with more consideration of girls inclusion and children with disabilities.4. Provisioning appropriate child-friendly learning materials to reduce household cost on schooling5. Sensitizing 500 community members and local leaders on child rights to prevent child right violations6. Facilitating 500 most vulnerable children in accessing community-based case management service.7. Supporting vocational education and training for adolescents, with a focus on ensuring additional support and inclusion of girls. Consideration of children and teachers with disabilities, and their specific needs, will comprise a substantial focus of this work. Lack of opportunities for young people, especially girls and young people with disabilities, as well as limited pathways to alternative education and livelihoods available in many target areas underpin the prioritization of these activities.8. Providing professional development and mentoring support for Teaching at the Right Level to benefit 100,000 children.9. Promoting life skills to conflict affected children including girls by psychological support and Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) introducing.10. Reactivation and/or establishment of 50 SBMCs in conflict affected areas (mapping of SBMC and orientation of SBMCs), this activity enhances school governance and we expect that their capacities will be developed on gender equity and GBV, inclusive education especially for the benefit of disabled children to improve attendance, transition and continuing education their roles and responsibilities

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

All For Peace And Dignity’s methodology is to design an integration plan to ensure a coordinated, consistent, and vertically aligned program. The technical and financial monitoring of the implementation of this project is a continuous process and an integral activity of APADE. In this respect, monitoring starts in the community. APADE Community-based Monitor associated with School Community Based Management Committees, (SCBMC) will monitor at household level attendance to schools and learning performance weekly basis. Data will be collected and analyzed by our MEAL officer in order to track success and performance, to monitor gaps and anticipate corrective measures. Moreover, our MEAL officer and Project Manager will organize every month fields visit. Joint program monitoring evaluation will be carried out on quarterly basis with education authorities (SUBEB) and Save The Children Team to monitor and evaluate learner’s attendance and performance, teaching environment and performance, and addressing some gaps. For success stories and building sustainability of project APADE assess the beneficiaries’ feedbacks regarding the project impact and involve community to capitalize on success. Situation Report (SITREP) will be shared with Save The Children weekly basis and Activities Monthly reports as well. We value the success and we pursue our continuous improvement. This exercise includes programmatic monitoring, budget monitoring (spot check,) and regular internal audits through the Budget Tracking System by dedicated staff. Quarterly financial reports will be shared with Save The Children.
