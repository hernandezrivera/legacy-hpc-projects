Project Summary

n response to growing humanitarian needs in Lebanon caused by the socio-economic impacts of the economic crisis, the proposed intervention will provide life-saving assistance to 315 vulnerable Lebanese households living in Palestinian gatherings in Beirut (Said Ghawash, Daouk and Sabra) and South Lebanon (Kfarbadda, Jal el Bahar and Wasta). MCC together with its national implementing partner Popular Aid for Relief and Development (PARD) will distribute WASH kits (hygiene, infant and elderly kits) three times over a six month period as a complement to the monthly distribution of food vouchers. A higher proportion of beneficiary households will be from the targeted Beirut gatherings (64%) where there is higher reported incidence of food insecurity.
Households will receive standardized hygiene kits along with the food assistance every second month including infant kits (30%) and elderly kits (4%) to mitigate the spread of Covid-19. The food assistance and WASH kit distribution will be accompanied by nutrition and hygiene awareness sessions and distribution of multi-sectoral messaging on nutrition and hygiene, as well as mental health and reproductive health.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

1. WASH kits will be distributed to the same 900 households targeted, validated and selected for six months of food assistance.2. PARD will procure standardized hygiene, infant and elderly WASH kits for the selected 900 households every two months along with the first, third and fifth food voucher distributions. Procurement will follow PARD’s established policy and procedures, leveraging successful past experience with WASH kit distributions implemented most recently with UNDP and previously with MCC funding. The number of infant and elderly kits to be procured and distributed will depend on the actual numbers of infant and elderly household members selected. The budgeted number of each kit is according to percentages from a recent UNDP supported distribution. The kit composition will follow the WASH sector guidance for hygiene, infants (0-3, 3-6, 3-9 and 12-24 months) and elderly kits. Based on PARD’s previous experience with hygiene kit distribution, in the first distribution the kit will include all items with the second and third distributions omitting items that are not consumables such as the thermometers included in the infant kits. 3. Following the first distribution, the registration and distribution teams will conduct follow-up surveys to assess beneficiary satisfaction with the distribution process as well as gather feedback on the quality and quantity of the items included in the kits.4. Multi-sectoral messaging will be included with the distribution of kits.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

MCC's Code of Conduct requires staff to commit to preventing sexual violence and fostering an environment in which sexual violence is not tolerated. Strong complaints mechanisms are implemented in all MCC projects and a protection and safeguarding focal point is embedded in the MCC Lebanon office. The proposed project will enable equal access to assistance for the targeted beneficiary population on the basis of the established vulnerability criteria. During vulnerability validation, selection and follow up surveys and assessments, project staff will be able to identify specific risks and vulnerabilities of beneficiary household members and refer them though established mechanisms to trained social workers or to other agencies are required.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

All project implementation processes, and procedures have been designed with the safety and dignity of beneficiaries in mind. PARD’s processes for communication with beneficiaries and distribution of assistance draw on extensive experience in the distribution of food vouchers and non-food items, including hygiene kits, and feedback is collected regularly from beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will be invited to provide feedback through follow-up phone calls or home visits and satisfaction surveys conducted post-distribution. MCC and PARD have established feedback and complaints mechanisms in place for all beneficiaries and stakeholders though a hotline, whatsapp and complaints boxes at distribution sites. Feedback or complaints can be provided confidentially and anonymously, and are responded to and investigated by the relevant MCC or PARD program team.
