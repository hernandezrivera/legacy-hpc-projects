Project Summary

Ekklesiyar Yan’Uwa A Nigeria (EYN) through this project aims to provide lifesaving assistance to women, girls, boys and men whom have experienced violence and are currently experiencing violence as a result of normative role expectations associated with each gender, along with the unequal power relationships between these genders as a result of the humanly induced crises in the north –east of Nigeria.
We intend to provide GBV response and protection services in Hausari ward of Michika LGA and Damboa LGA. These two locations identified have limited access GBV services and limited awareness of GBV as a humanitarian assistance based on several assessments conducted which has contributed to negative coping mechanisms and sexual abuse and exploitations (SEA), including in temporary IDP sites in Damboa and in the host community of Hausari ward in Michika.
Agencies have noted the specific targeting of women and girls, the violence they experience under Boko Haram, and the dire need for specialized services for them. Yet there has been minimal effort to identify and address women and girls’ needs, much less target them as priority beneficiaries for any programming.
The services we aim to provide in these LGA’s will address the GBV humanitarian intervention that is urgently needed to address effects of serious human rights violations in particular for over-congested IDPs in Damboa especially for women and children who have witnessed murder, subjected to forced abductions, sexual abuse, forced marriages, forced religious conversions and also participation in insurgency activities. This service will also address the needs of new arrivals in Hausari ward of Michika.
The activities we aim to implement in this project includes the establishment of women and girl’s safe spaces (WGSS), case management for GBV survivors, Psychosocial support, first aid services and skills training and referrals to other service providers. We intend to implement Adolescent girls program for girls of 10 – 14 years and 15 – 18 Years. This we have successfully implemented in Askira, Chibok and MMC in partnership with an INGO.
Another component of this project will be the economic and social empowerment (EASE) for women and girls. A livelihood support kit will be provided for women and older girls based on the skills training provided. Women and girls will be trained on how to save through the Village savings and Loans scheme. Women and their spouses or intimate partners will be facilitated in a discussion group series(DGS) for 8 weeks to provide guidance and information on how to manage household income and expenditure.
Our project plan will also involve community education on GBV for community leaders, religious leaders, youths, women leaders and stakeholders in these communities with the aim of establishing a referral framework for GBV cases.
For this project, we are targeting 1,800 women and girls, boys and men in each location. 900 women and girls will be new arrivals in camps or host communities, 50 men and boys, 400 women and girls 25 young men and boys within proximity of our safe spaces (communities around 10km radius) and 400 women and girls, 25 young men and boys already in camps and host communities. A total of 3,600 women will benefit from this project. We aim to educate 400 men, youths and community leaders on our GBV program.
EYN is a faith based organization and our core principle is equality for all without discrimination of sex, gender or religion.
Our staff strength consists of well-trained GBV case workers, GBV community mobilization officers, GBV officers, Monitoring and Evaluations Officers and GBV community outreach workers.

Needs

GBV assessments in Damboa and Michika shows that women and girls who have been abducted have been exposed to forced marriages and sexual slavery, however, the stigma in northeastern Nigeria is so heavy that women and girls do not speak up about their abuse, and are forced to stay at home and indoors after release. There is huge distrust towards former captives and their children, born as a result of rape. This puts individuals at further risk of stigmatism, rejection, violence, abuse and abandonment by their own communities. The same assessment says that there are limited services available for GBV survivors in Damboa, Michika, and that survivors cannot access community leaders for assistance due to cultural practices. While Boko Haram is not controlling the area directly now, women and girl’s express fears of collecting water and firewood due to these items being in insecure, non-controlled areas, which puts them further at risk for multiple violations. In Borno state the available Psychosocial Support Services (PSS) have been focused around the women and girl survivors, leaving family members, husbands and community members out, which greatly decreases safe reintegration to the family and society by survivors. Similarly, the few available services will be utterly stretched as more women and girls are returning from captivity.
