Project Summary


Prevailing social norms, patriarchal gender roles,localized conflict, a highly militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different clans and tribes exacerbate the risk of GBV in South Sudan. Protection concerns such as generalized violence and insecurity, access to food, water and firewood, expose women to a high risk of GBV. There is, therefore, an urgent need to enhance GBV prevention efforts with additional strategies to mitigate risks. Furthermore, lack of recognition of GBV as violation of fundamental human rights continues a culture of impunity throughout the country.
Notwithstanding the advancing of the peace process, it is important to continue to raise awareness, reduce prevalent stigmas in the communities and increase identification and documentation of GBV cases as well as providing survivor’s access to psycho-social support and other services for their recovery and healing. SAADO's GBV intervention aims to ensure the provision of critical GBV related assistance and specialized services through an integrated/mainstreamed approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and prevent and mitigate GBV risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. SAADO will provide psycho-social support services, women and girls friendly spaces, CBP preparedness and protection risk mitigation. Where the people of concern will access psycho-social support services in form of peer-to-peer support, individual case management and recreational activities. All activities will focus on overcoming trauma and psycho-social distress by strengthening their individual coping mechanism, resilience.Cases will be identified through the community engagement groups, local authorities, community based protection networks and community mobilizers and will be referred to trained and skilled GBV Caseworkers. Furthermore, SAADO will continue to support the economic empowerment of women through income generating activities that support their livelihood through mainstreaming GBV in to FSL activities currently being implemented in Duk County. A selected group of women that is interested in starting their own businesses will attend regular business skills training. In order to confront social norms that drive GBV, SAADO will specifically focus on engaging men and boys through EMAP strategy in its programming to become change agents for shifting gender roles. The discussion around gender and social norms will be facilitated in regular workshops, including influential community leaders to reflect on masculinity and the reasons behind their violence towards women, gender, sexuality, and substance abuse. In addition, SAADO will conduct awareness campaigns addressing specific problems affecting girls such as early and forced marriage and the low inclusion of girls in school. There will be specific awareness campaigns in primary and secondary schools for the promotion of girls’ enrollment and to reduce the drop out from schools due to early marriage and sexual exploitation and abuse.
SAADO will play an important role in strengthening coordination among humanitarian actors present in Duk County and will link coordination with the GBV Working group, SAADO is working on establishing SGBV referral pathway in Duk with Support from the national GBV Sub-Cluster 2019. The coordination is deemed fundamental to elaborate a common strategy for the GBV sector, including partners’ joint activities and to prevent duplication or overlapping. SAADO's GBV interventions will be aligned with the GBV Sub-cluster Strategy. To effectively respond to GBV, SAADO will collaborate with partners to contribute to the provision of the basic package of multi-sectoral GBV services at community level and build capacity of service providers to deliver quality GBV services in line with best practices and minimum standards for humanitarian settings

1 - Needs

According to AVSI foundation research 2019,the signing of the Revitalized Agreement in September 2018 with its requirement of 35 per cent women in the composition of important transitional and Governmental structures, raised hope. Nonetheless, despite guarantees, the Executive Director of UN-Women pointed out that so far only three women have been included in key transitional bodies and insists that women must play leadership roles from the beginning of the peace process to ensure that institutions are reconstituted in a gender-sensitive way that focus on protection of civilians from all forms of violence.This statement buttress the fact that despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement on August 30th gender-based inequalities and social exclusion still remain key factors undermining vulnerable women,girls, men and boys’ capacities to cope, respond and recover from the conflict and economic crisis. Often times, women and girls face constraints that limit their capacity to respond to these 2 hazards based on traditional gender roles and the predominance of patriarchy in South Sudan. These traditional/societal norms have, for so long, led to the subordination of women, limited access to opportunities and information due to restricted mobility, limited control over economic, social and natural resources. According to the South Sudan seasonal flooding snapshot 21 Oct 2019,unusually heavy seasonal flooding is affecting large areas of South Sudan, with estimates that between 600,000 and 800,000 people have been affected especially in Greater Upper Nile including Duk County which is 80% submerged in water.This report corroborates the UNICEF South Sudan situation report June 2019,which cites lack of humanitarian access to Duk County and many other parts of the Greater Upper Nile states. In Duk County localized conflicts, violence coupled with floods have massively disrupted agricultural activities. As a result, food insecurity has increased dramatically across much of the state, with particularly severe effects among the communities according to the August 2019 IPC report.This insecurity and poverty are the major drivers of early marriage due to poverty with parents often allowing their daughters to get married for survival and also bridal wealth. Idleness and lack of proper school functioning to engage young girls in studies makes them very vulnerable to early marriage with child marriage being a key concern affecting adolescent girls living in the County according to the IDSR bulletin September 2019. From the observation by SAADO GBV team present in Duk using participant observation, nearly all girls are married at the age of 15 or much earlier. A focused group discussion with adolescent girls indicated that parents from these communities encourage their daughters to get married to men believed to be wealthier in terms of cows. It’s from the above back ground and need that SAADO will vigorously ensure proper community engagement and participation in prevention, mitigation and respond to GBV in the proposed targeted location.

2 - Activities

1.In the first instance, SAADO will carry out rapid GBV assessments (including risk assessments) to determine the factors that drive GBV and to enable proper planning of GBV interventions in each of the targeted locations. 2. SAADO will aim to provide comprehensive case management services for women and girls survivors of GBV and will pay special attention to manifestations of GBV across genders. 3. SAADO will also provide, through the Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS), a safe and confidential environment in which women, girls and people with Disability (PWDs) will be encouraged to access psycho-social support services in form of Individual psycho-social support, group psycho-social support and peer-to peer support, individual case management and recreational activities. All activities will focus on overcoming trauma and psycho-social distress by strengthening their individual coping mechanism, resilience and social network. 4. SAADO will ensure formation and strengthen of CBP preparedness and protection risk mitigation through deployment of Protection Monitoring Officers, and Community Mobilizers who will work with community-based protection mechanisms to identify cases, and refer them to SAADO trained GBV Caseworkers. 5. SAADO will establish and support referral mechanisms for provision of key services by local service providers. Furthermore, SAADO will continue to support the recovery and economic empowerment of women through IGA and livelihood for Protective purposes. 6. In order to confront social norms that drive GBV, SAADO will specifically focus on engaging men through its programming to become change agents for shifting gender roles through application of EMAP strategy. The discussion around gender and social norms will be facilitated in regular workshops, including influential community leaders to reflect on masculinity and the reasons behind their violence towards women and girls, gender, sexuality, and substance abuse.7. SAADO will also conduct awareness campaigns addressing specific problems affecting women and girls such as sexual violence (rape), physical violence, economic violence, psychological violence, forced marriage which includes early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse and the low inclusion of girls in school.There will be specific awareness campaigns in primary and secondary schools for the promotion of girls’ enrollment and to reduce the drop out from schools. 8. To effectively respond to GBV, SAADO will collaborate with strategic partners to contribute to the provision of the basic package of multi-sectoral GBV services at community level and build capacity of service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in line with best practices and minimum standards for humanitarian settings that promotes and protects gender equality. 9. SAADO will strengthen GBV prevention and risk mitigation across its interventions through mainstreaming GBV in Education, FSL and WASH sectors. 10. SAADO GBV interventions in DuK will be aligned with the GBV Sub cluster strategy.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

SAADO 's proposed emergency intervention will ensure that beneficiaries rights are legally recognized in all field sites.This will be done through having a robust Beneficiary Communication and Accountability Plan.This will include delivering initial orientation and ongoing information to communities on project activities,rationale behind beneficiary selection, and supporting the management and resolution of complaints throughout project planning and implementation.Established Project Management Committees will also be involved in supporting the implementation and management of project activities to ensure that these resources do not create a source of competition and dispute, the project will strengthen the capacity of community structures to develop and enforce context appropriate systems for ensuring the equitable access and utilization of communalassets and resources. 6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project will ensure that the protection environment is sufficiently safe through ensuring that the Women and Girl Friendly Spaces(WGFS) and training centres are safe spaces for both genders and those women with children have access to child spaces where they can play safely while their mothers attend training. In addition,during community meetings,dialogues and training sessions or any gatherings on this project, SAADO's gender and protection staff will address the people we serve/beneficiaries on issues around prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Where ever such issues are raised SAADO will link the survivors with protection implementing partners as part of strengthening the referral pathways.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

To ensure the sustainability of the project SAADO will work with Project Management Committees (PMC’s) members such as the community based protection networks(CBPN's) and male champions other key stakeholders in the host communities and will develop detailed plans relating to key tasks and persons responsible. The PMCs will own these plans, and be responsible for their implementation and adherence following project closure. Technical and soft skills training will be delivered through the project to ensure PMCs(CBPN's) have the capacity. The training approaches employed by the project will also encourage sustainability by training community members as trainers. This will ensure that technical knowledge and skills remain in communities following project closure.
