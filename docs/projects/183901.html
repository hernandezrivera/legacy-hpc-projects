Project Summary

Kuwaiti Patients Helping Fund (KPHF) proposes to implement integrated and life-saving health intervention to assist vulnerable population affected by displacement, conflict, natural emergencies, malnutrition and disease outbreaks by supporting 126 PHCCs distributed as follows: 32 PHCs in South Darfur State ( at the following localities: 3 in Al Radoum, 3 in As Sunta, 1 in Beliel, 3 in Ed El Fursan, 3 in Kubum, 2 in Mershing, 2 in Nyala Janoub , 3 in Nyala Shimal, 3 in Sharg Aj Jabal, 3 in Shattaya, 3 in Tulus and 3 in Um Dafoug), 17 PHCs in North Darfur State (at the following localities: 3 in El Fasher , 3 in Dar As Salam , 2 in Kutum , 2 in Saraf Omra, 4 in Tawila and 3 in Um Baru) and 25 PHCs in Kassala State (at the following localities: 5 in Reifi Hamashkureib, 5 in Reifi Kassala, 5 in Reifi Shamal Ad Delta, 5 in Reifi Telkok and 5 in Reifi Wad Elhilaiw), 16 PHCs in Red See State (at the following localities: 5 in Al Ganab, 5 in Dordieb, 3 in Haya and 3 in Sinkat ), 15 PHCs in Gedaref State (at the following localities: 3 in Al Fashaga, 5 in Al Galabat Al Gharbyah – Kassab, 5 in Al Mafaza and 2 in Basundah), 15 PHCs in Sennar State (at the following localities: 5 in Abu Hujar, 2 in Ad Dinde, 3 in As Suki and 5 in Sharg Sennar ) and 6 PHCs in Khartoum State (at the following localities: 2 in Jebel Awlia, 2 in Karrari and 2 in Sharg An Neel) . The proposed programming will support 998,000 direct beneficiaries including men (298,000), women (400,000), boys (150,000) and girls (150,000), comprising of IDPs, returnees, resident and host communities as well as 350,000 indirect beneficiaries. The project aims are to provide integrated primary health care services, contribute to Strengthen Health Sector capacity to prepare and deliver timely response to populations affected by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks as well as strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, reinforce community resilience, and enhance risk mitigation measures. This project will be implemented for a period of one year. According to SMoH gap analysis reports (health Map), HNO 2021 and HeRAM the current situation in South Darfur , North Darfur, Kassala, Red See, Gedaref and Sennar States is very bad due to low coverage of health and nutrition services, food shortage, and malnutrition, lack of clean water, poor hygiene & sanitation as well as epidemic disease. In addition to, covid-19 pandemic also affected on decreasing the uptake of primary health care and disrupted health care services Delivery State. The situation requires urgent intervention to fill this gap. KPHF will intervention through provision of integrated and comprehensive PHCs services packages which include these activities: Provide outpatient consultation, free essential drugs, EPI, disease surveillance focus on covid-19 and other epidemiological diseases, reproductive health services (including ANC, PNC, FP, vaccination against Tetanus and delivery care) and IMCI services. In addition to, provision of basic laboratory investigation, treatment of different injuries and wounds, support referrals of patients to the next level of health care as well as intensive health promotion at clinics and communities level. Response to covid-19 through different activities, which include provide PPE to health cadres, referral of suspected cases, regular disinfection and intensive health education against the pandemic in order to suppress transmission of disease. KPHF will provide support Ministry of Health in epidemic disease surveillance, outbreak response and immunization campaigns. During this proposed project, KPHF will conduct minor rehabilitation on targeted clinics to ensure quality, patient safety, infection prevention and continuity of services. KPHF also intends to continue to provide short-term and on the job training to health facility staff which focus on IMCI , EMoC , IPCs ,family planning ,COVID-19 protocol and other important topic for project staff.

1 - Needs

The worsening economy of Sudan and protracted health emergencies resulted in increase of number of people who can’t access basic health services and decrease in the health system capacity to deliver the required the needed essential healthcare to the population. In addition, Sudan has been affected by the global COVID-19 pandemic that further strained the capacity of healthcare providers and affected access to essential health services due to nationwide lockdowns, re-allocating of health resources, and disruption of global supply chains that impacted availability of medicines and medical supplies at national level (HNO). During 2020, the cost of healthcare increased by 90% due to inflation with availability of essential medicines ranging between 15 and 50%. By November, total COVID-19 confirmed cases reached 14,090 including 1,115 associated deaths. The high case fatality ratio CFR of 7.9% reflected the prevalent low capacity in case management, and availability of intensive care cadre and equipment. People living in North Darfur, South Darfur, Gadaref, Sinner, Red See and Kassala States are suffers from low coverage and access to health care, high disease prevalence &mortality rates & frequent disease outbreaks. According to HNO 2021the health system in North Darfur, South Darfur, Gadaref, Sinner, Red See and Kassala States are one of the areas remains weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic lifesaving health services and respond to outbreaks. There have been significant shortages of medicines even important and emergency kinds because effective distribution of drugs and medicine to clinics and hospitals is a major challenge. Also SMoH health map 2021 for North Darfur, South Darfur, Gadaref, Sinner, Red See and Kassala States shows that the States are still suffer from low coverage and access to PHC services due to limitation of functional PHCC. In addition to, HeRAM for Q4 2019 shows very serious health indicators as follows; only 45% less than half of the functional HFs in Darfur (included South Darfur) are providing MBHP; the remaining 55% do not provide the package. For South Darfur providing MBHP is very low, the HeRAM shows that only 40% of health facilities in Ed El Fersan and 62% in Nyala are Provision of Minimum Basic Health Package. Also HeRAM show that only 31% of HFs in Darfur provide BEOC, up to 69% of the HFs do not provide the service , while the South Darfur is highest number of HFs without BEmOC (83 HFs).

2 - Activities/Outputs

Activities: • KPHF in coordination with WHO, MOH and UNFPA will procure and distribute basic medical equipment’s, clean delivery kits, essential drugs including antibiotics, anti malarial, RH drugs ..etc. and medical supplies for targeted clinics • Rehabilitation of 55 PHCCs with fixed materials to maintain safe and favorable environment . • Provide outpatient consultation, basic laboratory investigation, treatment of different injuries and wound, provision of IMCI services, provide EPI services at clinics level and community level in low or no coverage areas, provide maternal and child health services as well as provide integration reproductive health services which include: ANC & PNC services, normal delivery, family planning , provide intensive health educations at community and clinics level , support referral of patients who need life-saving from targeted clinics to secondary care services. In addition to , KPHF will conduct intensive trainings workshops for project staff and SMoH staff on different topics which include: IMCI, EMoC, Waste management, Infection control, How to do safe and clean delivery, Family Planning and Management of postpartum hemorrhagic, also on the job trainings will be conducted. Also KPHF in collaboration with SMoH and UNFPA will Support training of midwifes students selected from targeted areas in midwifery schools. • In collaboration with SMoH KPHF will conduct intensive community awareness session campaigns on different topics. Strengthening surveillance and early warning systems in the targeted clinics and strengthen the capacity to prepare and deliver timely response to affected population by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks through 95 per cent of the alerts of outbreaks/emergencies are verified and response initiated within 72 hours from notification to protect communities from avoidable death and illnesses and prevent further spread.
