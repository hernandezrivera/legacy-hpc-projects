Project Summary

This proposal comes within the under of WASH Sector according to the following WASH cluster HRP objectives:
Objectives: Ensure acutely vulnerable Palestinians in area C in Zatara, and Zatara Al kurshan in Bethlehem has access to essential services by improving access to WASH services by providing an appropriate domestic water services response for vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in fragile, and critically underserved communities by ensuring resilience and access to clean drinking water in Zatara, and Zatara Al kurshan in east Bethlehem Governorate.
Country Strategic Objectives: S.O.2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
WASH Cluster Objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians
The assessment shows a great shortage in the water network outreach, as well as old parts of the network. The main needs the project will address, are listed below:
1- Provision of domestic water services. It is about an extension Installation 3000 meters 2- inches, 1000 meters 1" inches, and 1000 meters 0.5" inches in the Arab al Kursha area. And rehabilitation of 1000 meters 2 "inches, and 1000 M 1" of water networks pipeline in Zatara for the old line was installed before more than 30 years and suffers a loss of up to 45%, and this project is complementary of the project to rehabilitate internal lines in Zatara before 2 years to serve Arab Al-Karshan in Za'tara, In addition to providing service to the people of Za'tara who reside on the outskirts of the town and are not connected to the water network service. They will be serving 142 houses (687 individual beneficiaries) in a way that raises the level of healthy and social life in the region, especially in light of the spread of the Coronavirus, the high price of water, and poverty in the region.

1 - Needs

Zatara and Zatara Al Kurshan villages are located east of Bethlehem in the south of West Bank, 640 - 650 m above sea level with annual rainfall is about 324 mm; therefore residents are raising livestock as the main source of income. more than one-third of the two communities land is located in area C. In collaboration with Za'tara municipality, women club, and animal husbandry cooperative, the project will serve 1307 individual beneficiaries and address the community water needs, through extending and rehabilitate the Za’tara water network pipeline, to enhance water supply for human and animal used in the community. Moreover, the project will serve the Bedouin community in Kurshan Za'tara. This enhancement will decrease the water loss, cost, time, effort, and water-related diseases among people and animals, through guaranteeing better-quality water and consistent supply. The assessment shows a great shortage in the water network outreach, as well as old parts of the network. The main needs the project will address, are listed below:1- Installation 3000 meters 2- inches, 1000 meters 1" inches, and 1000 meters 0.5" inches in the Arab al Kurshan area.2- Installation 1000 meters 2 "inches, and 1000 M 1" in Zatara.3- Distributing 20 PE water tanks.4- Implementation of 10 awareness and guidance campaigns related to hygiene. The number of Houses in Za'tara who need the water network pipeline is 100 homes and 42 houses in Al-Kurshan which need the serves. Both communities are considered marginalized in terms of basic needs and services, especially the water and sewage facilities with taking into consideration that sites are classified within area C 100% and are subject to the Israeli occupation violations.They suffer from an old water network (part of the oldest lines was rehabilitated before 2 years), other lines still need rehabilitation, rust also appears clearly with part of the old Zatar water pipeline, and other areas need installation new lines, hence water loses in the old network is very high and reach up to 40%, and water supply capacity becomes very weak, in many cases, people in these sites receive water every 45 days, and during summer these households do not receive water at all. The residents of the two communities are supplied with water by the West Bank Water Service through the public water network. These housing units rely on the water of rainwater harvesting wells for their animal use, (Note that there is no sewage network and they depend on the absorption hole), and they rely on the purchase of water tanks, while others rely on the network and tanking. In theory, the average water supply per capita is average estimated at less than 30 liters per day, and the cost of 1 cube reaches more than 20 NIS.It should be noted here this amount of water does not reach the capita due to high losses of water, that opposite the World Health Organization (WHO) recommended the minimum is 100 liters per person per day, the received amount of water is no sufficient for human use, while the animal need is doubled the problem, as it increases the demand, and thus people go for tanking, which increase the cost of water. There are about 6500 sheep and goats in the area, sheep owners benefit from sheep milk in the form of cheese or Jamid. The owners buy feed in the summertime and graze their sheep in the eastern slopes in winter and spring to decrease the cost of production due to the high fodder prices. However, this has increased the cost of water tanking as the area is far from the town center.

2 - Activities

The Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) has provided a source of water to supply the area with water recently. The activities will be as the followings:- Rehabilitation and installation 7000 m of pipelines with different sizes 2",1" and 0.5", as the following: Arab al Kurshan (3000 M of 2", 1000 M of 1", and 1000 M 0.5"), and 1000 M 2 "inches, and 1000 M 1" in Zatara. Installing those pipelines will enhance water supply for human use and will improve their livelihoods. This will decrease the water loss, cost, time, effort, and water-related diseases, this will also guarantee better quality water. Hence AAA conducted several meetings with relevant stakeholders, in Oct. 2020 covered water supply and needs in the community around Arab AL kurshan to have a better vision about their needs and burdens. The project will decrease water loss (which is about 40% in the village nowadays). The municipality will be able to collect the price of water, connect the network with houses, and managing the necessary maintenance for the network. Output 1: 1307 individuals will benefit from access to safe and affordable drinking water through the pipelines in the targeted community. Output 2: No water losses in the carrier line and in the installed pipelines. Output 3: capacity building and improved awareness level for different groups within the community, especially for women. Output 4: Individual daily water use increased by 45% getting closer to the global average of water use by WHO. Output 5: No water physical contamination, thus high water pressure in the pipelines, so better water supply mainly to remote areas. This project will foster future expansion to serve the Walidah, Khalat Hamad, and Al Rawaen communities where they will be direct beneficiaries.- Installation 2000 meters 2&1 "inches in Zatara.- Distributing 20 PE water tanks.- Implementation of 10 awareness and guidance campaigns related to hygiene.

3 - Indicators

Monitoring and evaluation: • A baseline survey will be conducted at the initial phase of the project, which will be updated until the final evaluation. Information will be collected through semi-structured interviews, household visits, and field observations. Regular monitoring will allow AAA to document the impact of the activities, document challenges, and constraints on a monthly basis, and suggest the right solutions. Attention will be paid to the quality of implementation and will ensure close liaison with PWA as well as Zatara Municibility, community leaders, and local protection committees. AAA will consult prior to the implementation with the protection cluster and NRC to ensure legal advice, second legal opinion will be consulted to ensure minimum risks on the implementation is likely to happen. Accountability will be ensured through ongoing follow up by AAA with field teams and beneficiaries. The expected indicators and targets of the project are: - Installation of 7000 M of water pipeline network to serve 1307 direct beneficiaries (327 men, 314 women, 340 boys, and 327 girls) will benefit from enhanced access to drinking clear water.- Distribution of 20 PE water tank to serve 96 direct beneficiaries (24 men, 23 women, 25 boys, and 24girls) will benefit from storage water.- Distribution of 100 hygiene kits to 480 direct beneficiaries (120 men, 115 women, 125 boys, and 120 girls) beneficiaries will benefit by improving the hygiene level.- Implementation of 10 awareness and guidance campaigns related to hygiene. ِAn increase of 20% in the amount of water reaching the surrounding areas due to the high pressure of the pipes. -Expenditure on the drinking water will be decreased by 45%. - Reduced time and effort required by women and men by 50% in Arab Al kurshan zatara. - Reduced the lost water in the pipeline to 50%. - Decrease the community municipality water bill deficit by 10%. - Gender aspects in the project: The identified indicators will be disaggregated by gender to ensure the inclusion of women, and other groups as well as involve men, girls, and girls at schools, youth, PwDs, and the elderly. Women and girls have considerable responsibilities for water bringing and use at home, as well as ensure adequate hygiene is maintained for children. The intervention will ensure this role is respected and enhanced by reducing the pressure on women in the physical labor of bringing water on a daily basis. The implemented activities will take into consideration the needs of diverse groups of women and girls such as the hygienic aspects that comes more essential with better availability of drinking water that is close to the community, and that bringing water will be of much less burden on women who usually do this responsibility of work. Reports gathered on women's roles will be capitalized and used in AAA women empowerment as well as used to support local AAA initiatives on policy change towards marginalized areas. M&E Indicators will focus on 2 categories: 1 Women Health (women Access and Utilization of water, Disease and Prevention, Environmental Health, water and fertility, Health Care Management, Health Expenditures, Maternal, and Infant Health, Mental Health and Risk Behaviors, Nutrition and water), 2 Economic Contribution (Resource Equity) related to water availability.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

AAA will follow protection mainstreaming guides disseminated by the FSS Cluster: - All activities aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual in the good spirit of the relevant bodies of human rights law, international humanitarian law and local laws. - Key Protection principles AAA will ensure will include: Do no harm: by avoiding exposing people to further harm as a result of your actions, and ensure that: * The environment and way in which assistance is provided do not expose people and environment to further human rights abuses or violations; * Take all reasonable steps to ensure that the affected population are not subject to violent attack, or forced or induced into undertaking actions that may cause them harm or violate their rights; * Manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized. * Assistance and protection measures do not undermine local capacities for self-protection. Support the efforts of the affected population and local community to find security and restore dignity. * None- discrimination: ensure equitable and impartial access to assistance, without discrimination on any grounds: * Challenge any attempts to exclude parts of the target communities. * Promote respect for human rights, and assist and support affected people to claim their rights and access remedies from relevant authorities; to obtain information on their entitlements and secure the documentation needed to demonstrate their entitlements, and to recover by providing psychosocial and community support. * AAA will Ensure consultation with the target population at all stages, and the participation of all in the design and targeting of interventions, in particular vulnerable and marginalized groups. * Environment-building - AAA project will contribute to creating an environment conducive to respect for human rights and the rule of law, including the reduction of exposure or vulnerability to protection risks.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAA conducted the needs assessment in a very collaborative way, with the targeted municipalities, women club, cooperative, and community leaders. The needs assessment results then validated by an open workshop in the community which includes affected people, farmers, women, youth, and elderly and owners. Moreover, AAA agreed and set up the local committee which consists of the municipalities, women club, cooperative and community leaders. The committee will govern the implementation of the project throughout the project life. AAA is mandated to and work with rural communities and Farmers across the WB, AAA team will ensure the implementation of its feedback complaint mechanism utilizing the following actions: - Regular daily site visits by AAA field supervisors with on-site response to concerns from people in (East Jerusalem & Bethlehem). - Facilitation of information sessions between people and the councils & municipalities where all people's concerns are publicly heard and adjustments made accordingly to any activity. this implies both men and women are participating. - The communities will have access to a complaint box at the municipalities, also can reach up to the AAA office and team through phone calls and direct visits. - Updated AAA feedback/complaint database will be shared accordingly with Donor reporting. - Legal awareness sessions with people to be aware of security assumptions and legal status of the area. By including different stakeholders in the community and other actors from outside the community, including farmers and owners, AAA believes they have taken concrete steps in considering Do No Harm policy. AAA will keep an eye on any new issues in the area to avoid any negative impact as a result of the project activities.
