Project Summary

The people in NW SW continue to suffer severe consequences of the ongoing-armed conflict with the situation exacerbated by COVID-19 with NW 5th most affected with 18/19 Health Districts affected as at November 2020 (OCHA 2020). About 3M people are affected with 705,800 IDPs and 360,500 Returnees (Humanitarian Response Plan of 2021). The displacements and COVID 19 have led to many people losing their means of livelihood and a reduction in farming; a main means of survival. This explains the observed rise in cases of malnutrition and Under-nutrition in the NWSW. Children U5, adolescent girls, and pregnant/lactating women are most affected with the situation worst in hard-to-reach areas, among IDPs and households/communities hosting IDPs. With malnutrition being a serious public health issue because of the adverse consequences on children U5, women of reproductive age and their birth outcomes, a response aimed at improving the nutrition needs of these groups is mandatory. To respond to these observed critical needs, this project seeks to improve the access of children U5, adolescent girls, pregnant and lactating women to preventive and curative nutrition packages through outreaches, mobile clinics and facility base services in compliant with COVID 19. Activities include:
- Active mass screening of children 6-59 months for SAM using MUAC
- Community management of uncomplicated SAM as per national protocol
- Referral of complicated SAM for treatment.
- Vitamin A supplementation.
- IYCF-E counselling/awareness to mothers/caregivers,
- Hygiene promotion through community sensitization
- Weekly Iron/folic supplementation for adolescent girls/pregnant women

1 - Besoins / Needs

Reports on nutrition show that prior to the crisis, the NW and SW had high rates of stunting and poor indicators of infant and young child feeding practices. Complementary feeding was reportedly compromised as only 30% of children 6-23 months were reported to consume the minimum acceptable diet with over 40% of children, aged 6-59 months reported to have anaemia. In 2019, nutrition screenings integrated in EFSA revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 5.1% and 7.1% in NW and SW regions respectively. Furthermore, analysis from MUAC screening data showed the NW to be more affected with a GAM prevalence of 5.0%. Nutrition reports from CBCHS facilities show a steady increase in the number of children with malnutrition with a reported increase in the number of complicated cases brought to the facilities for management than was the case before the crisis. During the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) project supported by UNICEF and implemented by CBCHS in hard-to-reach areas in Kumbo West and East health districts between July 2019 and June 2020, 7,078 children were screened for malnutrition using MUAC and 142 SAM (2%) and 437 MAM cases were identified. Of this number screened, 1,954 were IDPs with 91 cases of SAM giving a prevalence of 4.66% SAM among the IDP population and 5,124 with 51 cases of SAM giving an approximate prevalence 1% SAM among the host population. Between September and December 2020, the CBCHS in another project supported by UNICEF in over 10 health districts of the NW and SW mostly in urban or peri-urban areas screened 9,796 children 6-59months for malnutrition using MUAC and had 107 SAM cases giving a percentage of 1.1% of SAM. Majority of the cases reported were among the IDP population. With the populations of the two regions still suffering from the consequences of a four year long armed conflict and the COVID 19 pandemic, this situation is most likely only going to worsen as all of 2020 has seen an increase in the drivers of malnutrition vis-a-vis high food insecurity, lack of access to health services, poor sanitary conditions, morbidity (malaria, respiratory infections, and cholera), poor sanitary conditions and repeated internal displacements. The health districts selected for intervention are based on Nutrition reports from the Regional Delegations of Public Health, community volunteers and mothers trained in at home MUAC activities during the implementation of the humanitarian response projects by CBCHS. Reports from the region and some field activities carried out shows Ako health district with many cases of SAM. Belo, Mejang and Konene are selected in Fundong health district, as Belo has been highest hit by arm battles leading to many IDPs moving down to Mejang and Konene, which is host to many IDPs from Buabua, Su-bum, Kimbi River and other parts of Boyo as there has not been any arm battles there so far. Ndop health district have also been chosen because it haa been hard hit with many pockets of very hard to reach areas where people have taken refuge as it is inaccessible to the SAGs by land and these areas are served by CHWs or the CBCHS Primary Healthcare staff thus making it accessible to CBCHS. Ndu and Nkambe have relatively been calm compared to other health districts and so serves as a safe haven for many IDPs especially in the hard to reach areas. Muyuka in the SW is selected and specifically Bafia Health area as CBCHS has a functional health unit there which serves the entire health area and reports from the SW regional delegation of public health shows that coverage of MCH services are very low in this health area. Mamfe, Kumba and Tiko have been selected as they have facilities with staff trained to manage SAM cases at all levels.
