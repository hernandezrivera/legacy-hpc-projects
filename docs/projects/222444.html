Project Summary

The conception of this project has been informed by the outcome of a critical analysis of gaps in the health sector by the Health Cluster which points to the dire need for the provision of essential lifesaving basic health services aggravated by the ongoing South Sudan crisis. It thus seeks to target 124,840 most vulnerable populations in conflict and flood-affected areas, prioritizing women, children, the elderly, and PWDs in Fangak, Nyirol, Uror and Ayod Counties of Jonglei State. The targeted population include; Host community, IDPs, refugees, and recent returnees from Sudan. Through this project, CMA seeks to scale up interventions to prevent and respond to disease outbreaks and other public health emergencies, integrate with nutrition, WASH, protection, FSL and strengthen community health services and engagement to bridge the gaps in accessing health care.
CMA will prioritize collaboration with key stakeholders to shift from emergency aid to sustainable, routine health services, aligning with the humanitarian-development-peace nexus framework which is aimed at building a resilient health system capable of meeting both immediate and long-term health needs in line with South Sudan National health policy 2016-2026 and 2023-2027 health sector strategic plan. The project will be aligned to 2025 Health Sector Response plan which highlights three objectives i.e. improving equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including MCH, adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations, reducing excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases, enhancing resilience and promoting humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
The design of this project is anchored on improving access to integrated essential life-saving health care services through viable clinical packages namely static, mobile and community health volunteers. The project will strengthen sub-national health delivery systems, quality improvements, timely detection of disease outbreaks, investigation and response in targeted Counties. High mortality rate from prevalent epidemic diseases will be addressed. In the volatile context of Jonglei, the project will target Sudan returnees, IDPs recently displaced by conflict and flooding, and host communities in areas with limited access to health services. Whereas the 2025 HC target is 41,782, 55,089, 87,455 and 97,529 in Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol and Uror respectively, this project targets 124,840 beneficiaries out of the HC target of 281,854.
Coordination will be strengthened at national and subnational levels through Technical Working Groups and a multi-sectoral approach, integrating efforts with other clusters like Nutrition, WASH, and FSL. Localization will be enhanced through capacity building of local organizations for a sustainable response. The key priorities for 2025 include expanding essential primary healthcare, improving immunization, prepositioning medical supplies, detecting and responding to disease outbreaks, enhancing referral services, implementing health cluster GBV action plan and supporting the South Sudan MHPSS Strategic Plan 2024–2028.
CMA will support county M&E to collect data from health facilities and BHWs and enter into DHIS2, IDSR, EWARS and 5Ws. Data will be analyzed and used for decision making. A successful implementation of these activities would enhance access and quality health care, enhance prevention and response to health threats, and strengthen the health system significantly resulting in the overall decrease in morbidity and mortality arising from the epidemic prone diseases (Measles, Cholera, HEV, EVD). It will further increase access to services for SGBV survivors, disabled individuals and people with mental health disorders.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan has deteriorated further, owing to rising food costs, a severe economic crisis, conflict, disease outbreaks, cross-border movement from Sudan, and flooding (OCHA, Oct 2024 Snapshot). Multiple and concurrent disease outbreaks, including measles, yellow fever, poliovirus, meningitis, hepatitis E, and cholera, have exacerbated the health situation. Sudan's continuous conflict, as well as reported outbreaks of diseases like cholera, put the country at risk of imported infections. The most recent WHO/UNICEF estimates for measles and Penta 3 coverage are 72% and 73%, respectively. Because of this inadequate coverage, multiple measles outbreaks have occurred across the country since 2022 (WHO 2023). Malaria remains prevalent and the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, with constant flooding exacerbating the situation. According to the 2024 ISNA, just 57% of the population can reach the nearest health facility in less than an hour, with 44% citing long distance as a barrier to getting health care. Women and girls in remote and crisis-affected areas face extra obstacles due to a lack of qualified professionals, recurring supply chain disruptions affecting family planning commodities and essential medications for clinical delivery assistance, and the treatment of sexually transmitted infections. Similarly, 38% reported that their children did not get vaccinated because there was no health facility nearby. According to the September 2024 IRNA report, the barriers to health system strengthening and delivery in Fangak county were: inadequate health facilities, the bulk of which were devastated by flooding, and insufficient medical supplies at health care facilities. Malaria and diarrhea are prevalent; stagnant water provide breeding grounds for mosquitos; and displaced persons unable to access health services. Moreover, there is very little referral support in the event of life-threatening conditions, with few skilled medical staff to serve the whole community, especially pregnant women. In spite of the effects of conflicts in the targeted counties, access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) remains inadequate. WHO prevalence estimates of mental disorders in conflict settings of South Sudan, is approx. 2.5 million cases, emphasizing the risk of mental health burden in the affected population (WHO 2019). A national prevalence study conducted in 2022 by The Ministry of Gender, Child, and Social Welfare (MoGCSW) and UNFPA revealed that GBV is widespread and severe in South Sudan. According to this study, more than one-third of South Sudanese women suffer from physical violence, about 14% of women face sexual violence, 30 per cent of women endure psychological violence, 30 per cent of women experience economic violence, and 30 per cent of women undergo forced marriage. These forms of violence have a negative impact on the mental health of women, as studies from post-conflict South Sudan report rates of depression as high as 50%. While the Health Sector Transformation Programme (HSTP) is currently supporting 50% of functional health facilities, 18% are non-functional. The July 2024 GBV health analysis revealed only 28 per cent of health facilities provide GBV care, with 43 per cent offering services. Lower-level facilities struggle with staffing and infrastructure, limiting early intervention to referrals. Inherent in the Health Cluster objectives for the HNRP for 2025, which is the ultimate goal of this project, is the need to address critical concerns such as timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases, strengthening health system recovery and coping mechanisms and ensuring equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services with a focus on maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations.

2. Project activities

The proposed activities for this project are consistent with the 2025 Health Sector Response Plan, South Sudan's National Health Policy 2016-2026, and the 2023-2027 Health Sector Strategic Plan. CMA's goal in implementing these activities is to improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services, such as maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, common illness treatment, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services, for crisis-affected populations. It also aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality through timely detection and coordinated intervention to epidemic-prone diseases. Enhance resilience and build humanitarian-development links to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms in Fangak, Nyirol, Ayod, and Uror counties. It is anticipated that scaling up the proposed activities will ensure that disease outbreaks are detected early, responded to promptly, and supported by adequate surveillance systems via the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS). In line with the HRP and health cluster objectives, the project has been planned around County Hospital, PHCC, PHCU, BHI, and mobile team clinical packages, with the following activities: 1.Equitable access to Health: Recruitment and placement of adequate staff in key cadres namely doctors, midwives, nurses and clinical officers to provide key services, Capacity building for health care workers , Provision of outpatient consultations ,Provision of BeMONC and CeMONC in key health facilities , Provision of MHPSS and CMR services in health facilities, Provision of rehabilitation services for the disabled, Capacity building of health personnel for effective provision of CMR and, MHPSS services following the standard treatment protocols, Referral of the survivors to safe houses. 2.Infrastructure: Renovation, rehabilitation and equipping the health facilities to provide quality health services including OPD Consultation, BeMONC, CeMONC, PMTCT, CMR and MHPSS. Initiate and sustain Infection prevention and control (IPC) measures meeting all WHO Minimum Requirements targets in health facilities. 3. Morbidity and mortality reduction: Emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic diseases control including Case management for selected diseases (Malaria, ARI, AWD, ABD, Measles), Carry out weekly surveillance through IDSR including case-finding medical outreaches by active household surveillance and reporting of disease trends, outbreak prevention, investigations and response. 4. EPI: Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases utilizing static, outreach and mobile modalities and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain. Repair and maintain the cold chain systems in the targeted Counties. 5. Resilience and promotion of humanitarian-development linkages: CMA will Provide technical and operational assistance to health facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population jointly with the CHD, Support targeted number of static integrated facilities, Establish mobile response units and deployment of medical teams. Collaborate with the SMOH to build the capacity of the CHDs to provide support to HF, support SMOH and CHD teams in designing and implementing State and County annual work plans, which will include SMOH quarterly supervision and CHD monthly HF supervision, Integration of the health project with key sectors such as WASH, nutrition, FSL and protection in the targeted Counties, Conduct community consultative meetings within the community, Promote humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian, peace and development actors together to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources and Establishment and maintenance of feedback mechanism at health facilities.
