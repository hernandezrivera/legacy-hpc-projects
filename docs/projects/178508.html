Project Summary

A decade of displacement and the recent COVID-19 related lockdowns have led to lost livelihoods and income for a large part of the population. Reduced public spending, damaged infrastructure and explosive hazard contamination affect people’s ability to restart or continue their learned livelihoods. Productive and financial assets have been lost and cannot be replaced without significant assistance. At the end of 2020, unemployment is estimated at 50% of the working-age population. Overall poverty is estimated close to 90%, with an increase of 3-4% points compared with 2019 (emergence of “working poor” category). Extreme poverty is estimated between 55 and 65%.
The objective of UNHCR's project is to increase resilience and livelihoods opportunities for vulnerable people affected by the Syrian crisis. This is to be achieved in collaboration with other UN agencies and local stakeholders through a range of livelihoods support interventions delivered in the underserved communities The project aims to assist a total of 145,280 IDPs, returnees and selected members of host communities across Syria. The project contributes to the Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) Sector outcomes as follows:
1. Strengthen access to livelihood by creating income generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery.
2. Improve access to basic and social services and infrastructure.

4 - Needs

A decade of displacement and the recent COVID-19 related lockdowns have led to lost livelihoods and income for a large part of the population. Reduced public spending, damaged infrastructure and explosive hazard contamination affect people’s ability to restart or continue their learned livelihoods. Productive and financial assets have been lost and cannot be replaced without significant assistance. At the end of 2020, unemployment is estimated at 50% of the working-age population. Overall poverty is estimated close to 90%, with an increase of 3-4% points compared with 2019 (emergence of “working poor” category). Extreme poverty is estimated between 55 and 65%.Daily paid labour is reportedly the most common source of households’ income. However, it is unreliable and low wage. As the result of the compounded impact of COVID-19 pandemic, unilateral coercive measures, the economic crisis in Lebanon and other factors, destitution of people of concern increased drastically. Food prices have increased dramatically in 2020 and WFP's national standard reference food basket price in November 2020 was 251% higher when compared to November 2019.Lack of income opportunities in combination with reduced purchasing power and increased indebtedness have left people preoccupied with daily survival rather than recovery or rebuilding their lives. As a result, there has been an increase in harmful coping strategies, which are likely to significantly worsen the socio-economic and livelihoods situation of people of concern.

5 - Implementation of activities

The following three main activities will be undertaken as part of the project: a) Rehabilitation of key local and community level economic and social infrastructure such as market places, vocational training classes/workshops, warehouses, irrigation channels; b) Provision of business advisory services (including 'counselling') regarding business startup processes, business management and pursuing business opportunities) provision of key agricultural inputs.In 2021, UNHCR will provide livelihood support to vulnerable IDPs by providing start-up grants for small business projects and to support agriculture activities. Basic essential assets and tools will be provided to returnees in rural and urban areas to help them resume former profession or previous means of living or to start a new business. Beneficiary selection and identification will be based on referrals made at community and satellite centres after vulnerability assessments have been done. Based on grant proposals, selected people of concern will receive in-kind assets to set up a small business such as clothes or grocery store or receive agriculture inputs and tools to resume agriculture production in rural areas.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk 2: Existing labour market opportunities limit the participation of youth, women and persons with disabilities. Mitigating measures: Timely assessment and analysis of socio-economic conditions and market opportunities to ensure livelihoods interventions are designed based on the market reality. Risk 3: Interference with beneficiary selection may lead to potential fraud and aid diversion resulting in financial or reputational risks. Mitigating measures: Capacity building of partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD. Advocate with relevant authorities and partners to apply shelter sector criteria implementation in beneficiary selection process. Emphasize importance of impartiality to partners and systematically monitor adherence and feedback from beneficiaries, with corresponding corrective measures. Establish an active and informed complaint mechanism.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms.
