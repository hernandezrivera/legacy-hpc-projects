Project Summary

This project aims to increase the purchasing power and the resilience of 3000 conflict affected people (1,150 households) residing in the 5km zone of the GCA, by providing them with multi-purpose cash assistance. Furthermore, Dorcas plans to provide these people with psycho-social support to increase their resilience and strengthen the community.
Selected vulnerable households receive four months worth cash assistance, divided over two cash transfers, which take place in two separate months. One month worth cash transfer per household member is 970 UAH, as agreed by the Cash Working Group.
The planned intervention will focus on the most vulnerable people in Eastern Ukraine. Groups that are
specifically targeted are IDPs, people living in the contact line, single parent households, lactating or pregnant women, child-headed households, households with small children, elderly people, low income households, disabled or chronically ill adults and disabled or chronically ill children.
In 2019, the multi-purpose cash assistance component of the joint response with SOS CV, will focus on providing cash assistance to 3000 people (1150 households). By providing cash assistance to the most vulnerable conflict affected people, Dorcas will enable them to provide for their basic needs, such as shelter, food and healthcare.
In 2018, Dorcas is the lead of the Ukraine Joint Response 4, a consortium program funded by the Dutch government, consisting of partner organisations Tdh and SOS CV. Dorcas focuses on multi-purpose cash grants, livelihood grants, distribution of NFI (winterisation) and Psycho-social support in Donetsk region.
Dorcas has 4 years of experience in implementing cash assistance projects in conflict affected areas of Ukraine.

Needs

According to REACH’s winter assessment in the GCA, an average HH spending is on food, heating, health care and utilities. Cash assistance gives the power of choice to people to decide for themselves what their priorities are, and which of their basic needs they would like to cover.Cash assistance is needed because of:- New displacements due to continued conflict.- IDPs unable to ensure their livelihoods- Local conflict-affected people (non-IDPs) suffering from local economic downturn..Dorcas’ own data collection and interventions:In 2018 Dorcas collected data of over 5,600 individuals screened for the possibility to receive various kinds of assistance. Dorcas data collection system uses over 20 criteria, including sex, age, displacement, marital status, unemployment, health conditions, income, etc. The data is gender- and age disaggregated to help establish individual vulnerability and target the most vulnerable for the cash intervention within the HRP project. Vulnerable (gender and age) groups:The vulnerability assessment conducted by social workers of the MPC team showed that the most vulnerable groups remain single headed households with children, elders (60+) (mostly those living alone), chronically ill and disabled people, female-headed households and households with no active employment, especially those headed by unemployed people aged 40-60. Working age people also face difficulties in finding a job that provides insufficient income for them to provide for their families, due to high rent and utilities expenses, especially in the winter.Elderly: People have exhausted their savings due to the protracted conflict and the economical difficulties. For pensioners it may take up to 3 months to start receiving a pension in a new place, assuming they have the right documentation, without which, it can take even longer to reinstate their pensions. Age group 40-60: Elders (60+) have traditionally been the most vulnerable age group, however in 2018, an increasing proportion of the households headed by people aged 40-60 report even bigger difficulties in providing for their basic needs. This is mainly a reflection of the high proportion of unemployed people within this age group who are heads of household. For male-headed households in this age group unemployment frequently goes together with addictions. Female-headed households: Women continue experiencing greater vulnerability which is also reflected in the vulnerability criteria used. The vulnerability of women increases even more when women are single head of a household, widow, young or very old of age. It is estimated that 30% of female-headed households have poor and borderline levels of food consumption and therefore continue to be more vulnerable than male-headed households at 11.4% in GCA. Women-headed households, especially those with children, are among the most likely to adopt negative coping strategies and suffer from food insecurity, which may be linked with lower income, less employment options and higher dependency rates. According to Dorcas’ assessment, 98% of beneficiaries were satisfied with the delivery mechanism (Ukr Poshta services). For those unsatisfied, it was due to distance to the nearest Ukr Poshta and unavailability of public transport. The issue was addressed by door-to-door delivery. Taking all of this into account, it can be concluded that Ukr Poshta as a delivery mechanism is the best in terms of safety and time spent in comparison to bank branches with long lines or absence of ATM in general in many locations. Dorcas’ MPC prpgramme caters to different needs of different people. Beneficiaries’ specific needs are taken into account by engaging people from various groups in project design, adaptation and implementation. Despite insecurity, various assessments confirmed the functioning of markets in the GCA along and further from the ‘contact line.’

Activities & outcomes

Activity: Provision of MPC to 3000 beneficiaries covering 4 months of assistance (970 UAH/person/per month).1. Focus Group Discussion: Dorcas organizes Fcus Group Discussions (FGD) and invites different people from the community to seek their views on the vulnerability criteria, vulnerability assessment, house visits, beneficiary selection and other cash transfer programming procedures. All this information is taken into account when designing and implementing the project.1. Vulnerability assessment: based on the lists of vulnerable population provided by the local administrations, people will be consulted (meeting or home visit) and provided with project information. Their vulnerability gets assessed by Dorcas Social Workers, using a vulnerability assessment questionnaire. Other possible sources of referrals of beneficiaries are the UN and OSCE.2. First beneficiary selection: based on the set vulnerability assessment, and the pre-set scores connected to different vulnerabilities, people who score most are selected for a home visit. Households to be selected need to qualify for all the following criteria:a. Household income is less than 1.853UAH per person per month.b. Vulnerability score is above the predetermined minimum score (e.g. distance to the contact line; IDP and family status; health, accommodation and income conditions; number of children; and age).c. Households do not receive the same type of support (multipurpose cash) from other organisations in the same month(s) (cross check mechanism is well developed to address risks of duplications).d. Exceptions are permitted in maximum 10% of the individuals assisted. The motivation for making an exception should be based on needs alone and should also be registered.3. Home visits and document collection: Social workers visit the people in their home, confirm their vulnerabilities, and collect their documents.4. Final beneficiary selection based on vulnerability assessment and house visits.5. Cash Transfer: Dorcas Ukraine transfers via Ukr Poshta the total amount of money to beneficiaries. Each household receives the amount of money according to the family composition (number of HH members * 970 UAH * 4 months). On average it is 2.62 persons in each family . 6. Confirmation of payment: Dorcas employees call to inform the beneficiaries that their money has been transferred. Then, beneficiaries call Dorcas upon receiving the money to confirm the reception.7. Beneficiary committee: after the 1st transfer, Dorcas invites its beneficiaries to discuss different project procedures such as the modality of cash provision, and other CTP procedures and make adjustment accordingly for the 2nd transfer.8. Post-Distribution Monitoring: Dorcas has created a PDM questionnaire, based on that of the CWG, which assesses the impact of the program on the lives of the beneficiaries, their spending priorities and dynamics. The PDM also includes questions which assess the decrease of negative coping mechanisms. The PDM is done after the two cash transfer have taken place. Dorcas ensures a gender-sensitive approach in needs assessments, activities, outcomes and monitoring of its programs. MPC will be distributed through existing postal services to avoid unnecessary, dangerous or unfeasible travel of single mothers and elderly women. Gender balance is assured among staff (social workers and community focal points) interacting with beneficiaries in the project's activities. Outcome: Increase the purchasing power and the resilience of the most vulnerable people in Donetsk region to cover their basic needs. Dorcas complements its MPC and agricultural livelihood grants with other types of support, including psycho-social support to strengthens coping capacity. Referral pathway has also been put in place.
