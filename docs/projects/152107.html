Project Summary

The main objective is to repair damaged houses and basic communal systems to facilitate safe return for returnees and minimun standards for IDPs. NRC will focus on vulnerable families living in substandard shelter conditions in urban and rural collective sites of Benghazi. Beneficiaries will be vulnerable families (mainly women head of households, returnees and IDPs) living in heavy damaged or unfinished buildings. Those families could own/rent or temporary occupying the dwelling they are living in. Housing, Land and Property component is integrated as part of the Shelter project ensuring due diligence and security of tenure.

Needs

The conflict in Libya has led to widespread displacement and caused significant damage to housing and infrastructure. These two factors, destruction and displacement, have resulted in many people living in inadequate shelter. Multiple displacement also occurs because of disruption of basic services in the area of displacement or high housing rental prices. Up to 800,000 Libyans have been displaced and the UN currently estimates that there are still 180,937 IDPs (DTM round 16). In general, most Libyans manage to live in adequate shelters despite some difficulties to access public services because of power cuts or water shortages. However, the situation for IDPs is much more difficult. 21.5% of IDPs are living in unfinished buildings and 2.2% collective centres compared to respectively 3.2% and 0.2% for non-displaced Libyans, and 62% rent their dwelling vs less than 10% for the non-displaced. Recent fires in December 2017 in two settlements in Benghazi and in Tripoli illustrate the precarious living conditions. 334,662 individuals managed to return to their area of origin since 2016. For some who have returned, the situation remains difficult as their house have been seriously damaged. According to the REACH MSNA, 17% of the returnees are living in damaged buildings that would need repairs to be liveable. Benghazi is by far the area with the largest population of IDPs and returnees, respectively 38,100 IDPs and 160,000 returnees (DTM round 16). Benghazi suffered from multiple heavy clashes in many neighbourhoods. In April 2016, Impact initiatives has identified in 13 areas, 3344 structures as destroyed, severely damaged or moderately damaged, with an additional 1152 identified as possibly damaged. Those 13 areas do not include Garyounes and Qnfodah that were also very much damaged as well meaning that number of damaged building will be much higher. 80,6% of IDPS and 52.5% of returnees living in Benghazi mentioned shelter as a top priority.

Activities

Output 1: Vulnerable families benefit from shelter repairs in urban areas-Communicate and coordinate with local leaders, local authorities and relevant stakeholders -Conduct social survey for the families living in damaged buildings information necessary to rate them on the agreed vulnerability selection criteria. -Sign MoUs (beneficiary cooperation agreement) with the selected beneficiaries.-Select one or several contractors that will rehabilitate the damaged shelters -Set up complain mechanisms -Perform the construction works and provide supervision and monitoring. -Handover the completed shelters to the beneficiaries. -Conduct surveys to check the occupancy rate after 3 months of completion. Output 2: IDPs living in collective sites benefit from safer and improved Shelter/WASH conditions -Communicate with the camp authorities, IDP leaders and representatives (women, youth representatives, etc.) to explain the project and gain their support. -Conduct an HLP survey to identify potential risk of eviction -Coordinate with the relevant stakeholders and agree on repair work priorities -Sign NRC MoUs with the owner of the camp and authorities.-Through tender process, select s contractors that will implement the rehabilitation works-Provide tools and spare parts so the IDPs car maintain the infrastructures themselves-Perform the rehabilitation works and provide supervision and monitoring. -Handover the completed works to the IDP representatives/beneficiaries-Conduct surveys to check the capacity of the IDPs to maintain the facilities 2 months after the intervention.
