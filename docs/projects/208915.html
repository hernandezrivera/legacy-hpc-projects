Project Summary

There is significant vulnerability of the overall population IDPs & Returnees due to lack of access by government services, and ongoing insecurity. SMoH presence is thin due to low capacity and proximity to rebel-held areas in ED and rural, hard to reach are prior to ARCO intervention, only 20% of health facilities were functional and most of these were providing only limited services. Through the support of other donors, ARCO has in place seconded staff from SMoH, however, these staff need regular refresher training (including on IMCI, reproductive health and SGBV, and facility management) to enable them to effectively provide health services in these localities. ARCO requests staff to be seconded by Ministry of Health in the health facilities that it supports, to ensure qualified staff, build long-term capacity, and promote sustainability. The health status of the populations of East Darfur remains critical with diarrhea, malaria, and acute respiratory infection (ARI) regularly affecting large numbers of people. ARCO proposes to provide Primary health care and treatment services through a total of 27 PHC facilities to covering a catchment population of 257,200 vulnerable people (with all gender categories, especially Children and Women), while also supporting the locality health department (LHD) in the localities and training for health Health Information System. Also provides community health through Care Groups, community health promotion, defaulter tracing for RH and OTP services staff, clinic running costs, provision of drugs, medical supplies and equipment, and support for the Localities Health Department, including the Health Information System. Also provides community health through Care Groups, community health promotion, defaulter tracing for RH and OTP services
For the nutrition ARCO will Coverage 17 centers targeting 48,220 support the provision of comprehensive nutrition services in 17 with in the 8 Localities refer the ongoing conflict in Sudan between SRF and SAF-East Darfur is Third biggest state hosted IDPs whom are arrived from Khartoum –South Darfur ,Central ,West Darfur ,North Darfur and Kordafan as a result of the conflict and most of IDPs are women and children with in the host community and Returnees need support for Stabilization Centers with high numbers of conflict-affected households (new IDPs and long-term one’s) vulnerable, host communities, and some returnees. ARCO will continue support to rural and hard-to-reach facilities in these Localities. Services include Reproductive Health, EPI, early warning and outbreak response, support The Nutrition Impact and Positive Practice (NIPP) approach to prevent and treat Moderate Acute malnutrition for the 17 centers targeting 48,220 in East Darfur to ensure comprehensive care in supported facilities, continued with coordination through Ministry of Health and the Health and Nutrition Sectors in ED, and will also engage in ongoing coordination with other partners in each locality to ensure no overlap to provide a package of lifesaving primary healthcare services in all supported facilities which includes curative consultation, provision and dispensing of essential drugs, managing injuries, EPI to increase the immunization rates in children and pregnant mothers, nutrition screening for children under 5, OTP for management of SAM, reproductive health services, disease surveillance, outbreak response, and health education. Our previous program has taught us that an integrated program works best, with linkages between health, RH, GBV, community health, and nutrition activities, especially at the community level and in defaulter tracing. Midwives play an especially critical role, both distributing Clean Delivery Kits which encourage skilled deliveries, and also playing a critical role in identifying and referring SGBV cases through ARCO’s existing RH referral pathway, which allows for anonymity and protection of survivors. 3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ARCO links beneficiaries with protection networks as needed, through referrals, and has expanded GBV treatment in supported facilities. We are working to further improve a full range of protection WASH ,Health and SGBV are integrated services in all facilities within in the specific area of intervention to have access to the Protection Analysis which identifies threats, vulnerable groups, and existing capacities. In addition, training will be provided to the child protection, women support committees and ARCO protection staff on protection mainstreaming. To create a proper understanding of identified threats, the most vulnerable groups, and the existing capacities of the affected populations as well as services and resources already available. This will help us to identify real and potential risks that may arise in protection programs. Will confirm the threats, vulnerabilities, and negative coping strategies identified in the Protection Analysis Report by consulting with the affected population of each area of intervention. In addition, through analysis, ARCO identifies with beneficiary’s preventive and mitigation measures with the objective of reducing the threats and the vulnerabilities and increasing the existing capacities. In addition, the community through the community-based protection structures will have full participation in the designing and implementation of the program. Furthermore, the community will have full participation including a decision-making role in the program. AGD approach will be used to ensure the inclusiveness of the program to all of the community in the area. Active and effective participation of affected people both in planning, response, and monitoring processes will be ensured through community discussion. ARCO will promote peace by ensuring that all community groups including Refugees ,IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups and hiring diverse staff. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations is mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area.
