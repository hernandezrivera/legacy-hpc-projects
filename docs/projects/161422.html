Project Summary

Displacements and returnee inﬂux situations affect families especially children .Boys and girls are greatly affected psychologically as they seek for safety, their daily routine are greatly disrupted, environment changed , human rights violated , generally exposing them to many child protection issues such as child abuse, violence, neglect child labour and violence due to their low ability to protect themselves. This project will work closely with GBV , FSL, Health ,S/NFI WASH, Education and Nutrition partners to ensure an integrated approach for a holistic service delivery and sustainability of the services rendered to the affected children and their care givers.The project will be targeting 14,200 vulnerable children from IDP and host communities from 2 counties (Cuiebet and Morobo ) of Lakes and Central Equatoria States respectively,with signiﬁcant impact aiming at helping children and young people affected by armed conﬂict. CIDO will work closely with all relevant stakeholders to provide lifesaving child protection prevention and response services through provision of Comprehensive case management services and referrals for 820 children with critical protection concerns; Psychosocial Support (PSS) activities; Child protection awareness raisings or campaigns and Strengthening of community-based child protection mechanisms to prevent and respond to child protection concerns targeting 14,200 most vulnerable individuals (45,50 girls, 4,270 boys, 2,850 women and 2,530 men). CIDO will also establish two community led Child Friendly Spaces–CFSs and equip them with recreational and learning materials. The project will target IDPs as well as host communities. CIDO will build upon existing efforts and target vulnerable children, while coordinating closely with the Child Protection SubCluster and other partners implementing this project.

1 - Needs

Children continue to suffer the brunt of conﬂict and economic pressures. The situation for children has deteriorated over 2018, with continued incidents of recruitment, abuse, exploitation and other grave violations having directly affected about 100,000 children since the beginning of the conﬂict. CIDOs ongoing projects and presence in Morobo(Kanji, Panyume,Yaribe,Bomas) and Cueibet(Waat ,Ngap and Duony Payams) bloomed the needs assessments in the locations using FGD's approach and, was able to estimate the CP needs. Through CIDO’s existing programming, and in line with Protection Cluster ﬁndings, CIDO has noted increasing and targeted attacks against women and girls with growing levels of sexual violence perpetrated by mostly armed persons. These are linked to the escalating insecurity, militarization of political leadership, mobilization of armed groups and instability caused by violent cattle raiding. In Cueibet people displacement is very intensive (merely 19% of HHs is composed of natives only) due to a combination of insecurity and lack of food security; some payams are visibly more resource-constrained than others and suffer more widespread conﬂicts over resources as a result; cattle raiding and revenge killings are, respectively, the main problem and criminal offence recorded in the county; GBV acts are very common: only 17% of HHs has not experienced GBV in the last 5 years; 3 out of 4 respondents claim that GBV acts that they suffered were related to cattle raiding; 12 to 18 years old adolescents is the most-at-risk group of GBV, and the school is considered the safest place by the community. Adolescent girls are particularly vulnerable to multiple forms of violence and have limited support systems and access to information. The conflicts have led to displacement, children being abandoned or encouraged to live alone. According to the HRP 2019.. In 2018, Lakes saw especially high numbers of individuals having moved due to communal violence with over 22,000 and 21,000 IDPs respectively. For the ﬁrst quarter of 2019, the proportion was especially high. About a number of UASC are particularly at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse or neglect in Morobo and Cueibet Counties. In total, it is estimated that 360 children will need Comprehensive Case Management and additionally 80 children including OVC’s, children with disability and children with mental health disorders will need individual protection assistance. Community social services provider including local authorities and community leaders (Teachers, social workers, clergy, CPCs, etc) needs capacity strengthening to have the ability to protect, prevent and respond to the core rights violations of children especially those affected by the conﬂict and support their participation in peace building activities. About 2600 caregivers and foster-care parents will also need training on appropriate and culturally acceptable child care practices. By establishing 6 community child protection and support groups for prevention and support of children with longterm psychosocial needs, about 160 community child protection committee members will need training on age appropriate PSS. For successful implementation of planned activities, there is need for the establishment of 2 community child friendly spaces using local available materials in Morobo and Cueibet Counties. More so the proximity to conﬂict lines and the use of children to obtain food can amplify the risks, including eminent threats to their lives through recruitment into armed forces, abduction, rape and other assault. . Social structures and family bonds are breaking; children are separated from their families, and they have very limited access to basic services, which hinders their physical and mental development. Boys are at high risk of recruitment into armed groups and girls are exposed to sexual abuse.

2 - Activities

Identiﬁy and documentation of children with critical protection concerns: Provision of case management services and referrals to boys and girls with critical protection concerns (UASC, child marriage, CAFAAG, sexual violence) - Provision focused and non-focused PSS activities for children in schools and communities. - Build capacity of frontline services providers (caseworkers and CP staff) on Child Protection case management and child protection: - Training of caregivers on positive parenting skills and PFA, establish peer-to-peer support groups for the caregivers.. - Establish or strengthen CBCPMs (community based child protection committees, youth groups) and train them on child protection: - Establish 2 child friendly spaces (CFS). - Conduct child protection awareness raising or campaigns on prevention and response to child protection issues and on referral mechanisms. Conduct 2 child protection risks assessments in target locations with other sectoral partners (WASH, Nutrition, Education, FSL to identify child protection risks and concerns, regular monitoring visits and provide reports that will assist in informing child protection response and advocacy.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Contamination of targeted project areas with explosive hazards will be mitigated through collaboration with mine action actors inorder to ensure clearance of farming spaces of UXOs. CIDO will ensure communities are enlightened on risks surrounding UXOs and how to approach, handle cases of suspected ordinances. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas will be mitigated through collaboration with local authorities and host community leaders inorder to negotiate agreements especially for displaced persons inorder to access farming spaces. Forced taxation of humanitarian assistance will be mitigated through collaboration with OCHA, the South Sudan NGO forum, UNDSS and state inline ministries. Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services will be mitigated by availing humanitarian assistance closer to the beneficiaries to avoid long distance treks to access aid.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CIDO has standard feedback mechanism procedures that are entailed in our response across South Sudan. CIDO staff will periodically interact with beneficiaries to obtain views on project implementation, and suggestions for better service delivery. Feedback pathways will be clearly stipulated to beneficiaries in local dialects that can easily be understood by the communities. The project will design feedback forms, setup suggestion boxes, organise focus group discussions, setup call/sms lines , help desks,dedicated to feedback. Community perception on project deliverables will be sought through local community leaders and representatives of different groups including caregivers ( women, boys, girls, people with special needs and the elderly). Lessons learnt will be compiled and feedback into the project to improve service delivery.
