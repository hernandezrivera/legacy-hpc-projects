Project Summary

This intervention is inserted in the framework of the strategic objectives 1, 2 &3 of the HNO 2020 draft, and it is designed to complement the efforts of the Protection sector in Syria. This project aims to contribute to the psychological wellbeing of vulnerable groups particularly women, children, and PWDs, through Inclusive women & child-friendly spaces, women safe spaces, and mobile teams through structured and comprehensive PSS activities, capacity building, awareness campaigns, and robust Risk Education. The project targets mainly IDPs and Returnees in need of protection in eight governorates, for a total amount of 42280
direct beneﬁciaries. This intervention will be implemented in partnership with SARC. The project plans to conduct focused training for SARC volunteers to build their capacity, to make them able of providing semi-structured PSS, case management and referral, awareness-raising sessions, and MRE, in addition to providing support in-kind assistance to those in need.
TdH is accountable toward the beneficiaries starting from the selection criteria that discussed with the local communities- through formal and informal community meetings, in addition to information sharing with the communities regarding progress on activities to ensure that information is understood and useful- through informal meetings, and FGDs. TDH understands community dynamics and potentials for community tensions and further updates the protection risk matrix- through approaching "DO NO HARM" and COMMUNITY PARTICIPATORY "approach.

5 - Implementation of activities

The implementation will be on two-phases: • The ﬁrst phase will focus on series of technical trainings for the volunteers to enhance their capacity on MHPSS, MRE, PSS, CP, and disability inclusion during the ﬁrst 2 months of the project to enable them to conduct the activities with high quality, also the trainings will enable them to design activities that is including PWDs, with range of different disabilities (cognitive, intellectual and other developmental disabilities, mobility, visual, hearing, and mental health disabilities)• The second phase (implantation phase): This phase will be done through the trained SARC volunteers, and TDH will provide them with technical support, supervision, and will monitor the implementation on a daily bases by TDH field staff and monthly bases by TDH National staff (PM, M&E, Protection officer). The work structure in each location will include: a mobile team (6 SARC volunteer+ 1 TDH technical supervisor), and a stationed team inside safe space accessible to PWDs via soft-rehabilitation (8 SARC volunteers+ SARC Case Manager + TDH technical supervisor, TDH field Coordinator). • Activities will include:1-awareness- raising sessions on General protection including CP, GBV, topics will be (protection from violence, diversity acceptance, PWDs acceptance, child labor, early marriage, and Mine risk education…). all materials used will be from approved sources such as SARC, UNICEF, and UNMASS.2- Recreational and PSS activities for children, life skills activities for youths & women, these activities will aim to increase social skills and emotional wellbeing, improve coping mechanisms, will be designed and supervised by TDH PSS technical expert3- Case Management for children, this will be done via SARC Case Manager and under the supervision of TDH technical supervisor.4- In addition to focused assistance for victims affected by explosive hazards, by providing Medical in-kind assistance, MHPSS, and provision of assistive devices.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

PR1: Doing harm when providing project services. M1: first we have to ensure that no harm comes to children or their families because of case management conduct, decisions made, or actions taken on behalf of the child or family. Caution should also be taken to ensure that no harm comes to beneficiaries during secessions and activities, by avoiding bringing culture-sensitive subjects and ensure using materials and methods that are already tested and approved droning sessions.PR2: The targeted population may be under risk as a result of lack of consideration for accessibility; lack of consideration of contextual information and the local dynamics M1: Liaise with the community oﬃcials, TDH and SARC Branches in the area, to ensure that our activities are implemented in a Safe way with easy access for PWDs and it is compliant with the minimum standards for protection mainstreaming PR3: Discrimination of selecting groups in targeted communities M3: Engage key and respectful people (such as school director, …etc.) that are representing the community and have an influence on them, focusing on having 50% women representatives, in beneﬁciary’s selection, and with diverse members of the community we will ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered, also select staff for all activities (especially volunteers) form the local community, and also respecting balance in gender, age, social proﬁle. PR4: Men and Women may come into conﬂict due to the mix of men and woman in the same activities period, M4: Take into consideration the culture and norms in each location through divide the targeted group during activities, creating a separate queue for women and men during awareness with sensitive subjects. PR5: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services M5: Establish a speciﬁc complaint and feedback mechanism 2. Conduct sensitization activities to inform beneﬁciaries of the reporting mechanism.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assunmption1: TDH will be implementing the project under the umbrella of SARC, and SARC has access and approvals to operate in the project areas.R1: Local partners lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities. M1: SARC has displayed an outstanding ability to implement humanitarian projects despite constraints and limitations put onto them. Currently, SARC is already accessing the mentioned locations with funding from various entities for several months. However, if SARC lost access to project locations, Should SARC, TdH would either identify other suitable partners or, in coordination with them and other concerned parties, identify other locations with the same high needs, where the programming could continue. R2: TdH is unable to timely contract technically skilled employees. M2: TdH has solid recruitment processes and regulations to ensure the best-qualiﬁed candidates are hired in a timely manner. Vacancies will be publicly announced immediately after the approval is received. R3: bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities. M3: TdH already obtained approvals to work in Northern and southern area of Syria, in addition, TdH will advocate through the local partner to have the needed approvals based on the signed MOU. R4: delay in receiving the fund due to the sanctions on the banking system in Syria M4: TdH has different banking accounts in Syria with different pipelines to receive the fund from different sources. R5: Security risks: TdH and Local partner’s staff are exposed to threats, including kidnapping, detention, intimidation, and injury. M5: the partners are able to conduct their work in relative safety, moreover TdH Security person and Security Focal points in the project locations are regularly analyze the operating environment and have established safeguards to prevent and respond to risks of insecurity which may arise, addressed through location-speciﬁc safety and security management plans.
